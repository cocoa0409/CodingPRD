# Retailer Installation Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national home-improvement retailer sells more than flooring, windows, furnaces, roofs and cabinetry: it sells the promise that a licensed, insured, background-checked crew will arrive at a customer's house on an agreed day and leave the work finished. That promise is expensive to keep. The measurement taken at the sales desk is an estimate, the crew that quoted the job may not be licensed in the county where the house sits, the permit office may or may not care about the square footage involved, and the pallet of material may arrive two days after the installer does. Today each of these facts lives in a different spreadsheet, and the gap between them is absorbed by store associates making phone calls.

This document specifies the backend service that closes those gaps. The service owns the **service catalogue** (what the retailer will install, on what billing unit, at what labour rate, and whether an in-home measurement is mandatory), the **jurisdiction policy table** (who pulls permits, at what unit threshold, with what licensing, insurance and background-check floors, and how many inspections the county can absorb at once), the **installer network** (recruitment, per-jurisdiction trade licences, verification, activation, probation and removal), and the **project** itself — quoting, deposit, measurement, change orders, material delivery, install window, permit, inspection, quality punch list, closing, labour warranty and cancellation settlement.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is exact**: every monetary value is an integer count of cents, every rounding point is pinned, and the four deposit buckets on a project must reconcile to the amount the customer has actually paid after every single write. Second, **compliance is checked at the moment of commitment, in a fixed order**: an installer's licence, background check, insurance, calendar and the freshness of the site measurement are all re-examined when an install window is held, and the first failing check — not an arbitrary one — is the error the caller sees. Third, **time is a value the server owns**: no deadline, expiry, rolling window or decay band is measured against the wall clock. A logical clock, explicitly settable, drives every temporal rule so that the whole programme is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads, writes and the two logical-clock endpoints alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401. This is the very first thing the service checks, before any body parsing.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ProjectCoordinator`, `ServiceManager`, `Installer`, and `Customer`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those six strings, or is one of them but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` and HTTP 403. The six roles are **peers, not a hierarchy**. A `ServiceManager` is emphatically not a super-user: it cannot sell a project, book or complete a measurement, price a change order, plan a delivery or verify a trade licence. A `ProgramAdmin` owns configuration and the retailer-side permit escalation path, but cannot install, cannot record a quality inspection and cannot triage a warranty claim. Their remits are:

- `ProgramAdmin` owns the programme configuration — the service catalogue and the jurisdiction policy table — and is the retailer-side escalation path for permit filings, permit issuance, jurisdiction inspection results, project cancellation and reinstatement. It shares customer registration and project creation with `ProjectCoordinator`, and may perform every read.
- `NetworkManager` owns installer-network recruitment and verification: recruiting a firm, amending its contact and insurance details, filing its trade licences, recording verification decisions and background-check outcomes, and admitting a fully verified firm to the assignable network. It reads installers, credentials, scorecards, projects and assignments.
- `ProjectCoordinator` owns project execution: selling the project, booking and completing the in-home measurement, raising and pricing change orders, planning material deliveries and advancing their transit status, holding, confirming and releasing installer windows, reserving jurisdiction inspection slots, recording the quality inspection, escalating punch items and closing the project. It may perform every read.
- `ServiceManager` owns escalation: triaging labour-warranty claims, signing off `SAFETY` punch items (it is the only role permitted to do so), releasing an install window as a no-show (again, the only role permitted to do so), filing scorecard events alongside `ProjectCoordinator`, cancelling a project, and removing an installer from the network.
- `Installer` is the installer firm acting on its own work: starting an assignment, completing it, filing a permit application when the responsibility falls to the installer, and reporting a punch-list defect resolved.
- `Customer` is the purchasing household acting on its own project: paying the deposit, paying the balance, deciding a change order, cancelling the project, filing a permit application when the responsibility falls to the customer, and filing a labour-warranty claim.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ProjectCoordinator` or `ServiceManager`, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `escalated_to_user_id` on a `PunchListItem` that an operator escalates, or as `escalated_to_user_id` on a `WarrantyClaim` that a service manager escalates) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Installer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Installer` row; ownership checks compare the resource's installer foreign key to `X-User-ID` — the `installer_id` of the `InstallerAssignment` being started or completed, and, for a punch-list resolution, the `installer_id` of the `InstallerAssignment` on that project whose status is `COMPLETED`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks compare `Project.customer_id` to `X-User-ID` for the deposit payment, the balance payment, the change-order decision, a customer-initiated cancellation and a warranty-claim filing.

An identity mismatch is never an authorisation problem. When the role itself is permitted at the endpoint but the acting `X-User-ID` does not own the resource, the answer is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403 — never `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`. A separate 403, `ERR_WRONG_PERMIT_PARTY`, covers the one case where the permitted role set is deliberately wider than the party actually obliged to act: filing a permit application is open to `ProgramAdmin`, `ProjectCoordinator`, `Installer` and `Customer` at the routing level, but the acting role must match the project's determined `permit_responsibility` — `RETAILER` expects `ProjectCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin`, `INSTALLER` expects `Installer`, and `CUSTOMER` expects `Customer`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; the value shown above illustrates the shape only — the service emits `req_` followed by the 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits of a freshly minted UUID.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from host time, so it repeats whatever instant the clock has been set to.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources; the computed reads and the two logical-clock operations also answer "object", carrying their documented field set directly as `data`.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE` and HTTP 422, and so is either parameter supplied as anything other than a base-10 integer literal — `limit=abc`, `limit=2.5` and a bare `limit=` all answer `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`, never `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE`. Surrounding whitespace is trimmed before the parse, and an `offset` past the end of the matching rows simply yields an empty page with `has_more` false.

List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"`, place the rows in `data`, and populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows match the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four orderings deviate and are part of the contract: the service-category list is ordered by `created_at` descending with `id` ascending as the tie-break; a scorecard-event list — whether scoped to one installer or to one project — is ordered by `occurred_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; a project's change-order list and a project's punch-list are both ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is also the default. Because every timestamp is minted from the logical clock, rows written under one clock setting share a `created_at` exactly; wherever an ordering rule leaves such rows tied — the `created_at` default and the `occurred_at`-then-`created_at` scorecard ordering alike — the tie is broken by insertion order, the sequence in which the server actually stored the rows. Filter parameters offered by a list endpoint are additive: supplying several narrows the result to rows matching all of them. Filter values are themselves never validated: a filter supplied as an empty string is ignored altogether, a value matching no row yields an empty page rather than an error, and the boolean `removal_eligible` filter reads `true`, `1` and `yes` case-insensitively as true and treats every other value as false.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Version Counters and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted — like every other server-stamped instant in the system, it is read from the logical clock described below and never from host time.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations performed by a cascade the caller did not directly request. `ServiceCategory`, `Jurisdiction`, `Installer`, `InstallerCredential`, `Customer`, `Project`, `Measurement`, `ChangeOrder`, `MaterialDelivery`, `InstallerAssignment`, `PunchListItem` and `WarrantyClaim` all behave this way. `ScorecardEvent` is the one exception: it is an immutable ledger row that carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update endpoint; once written, its three frozen point deltas are never rewritten.

All temporal reasoning uses a **logical clock** that the server owns. On a freshly started server the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it must not fall back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. An explicit clock-setting endpoint accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it; a companion read endpoint returns the logical clock's `current_utc`. Every deadline, expiry check, rolling window, decay band, lead time and cooling-off comparison in this specification is measured against the logical clock, never against the host's real time.

Moving the clock is not inert. Setting it runs an epoch-advance sweep over trade licences in ascending credential `created_at` order, insertion order breaking ties: every credential whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `license_expires_on` is at or before the new clock value becomes `EXPIRED`. Each expiry recomputes the owning installer's `verified_credential_count`, and when that count falls to zero the installer is suspended with the full assignment-release cascade described in §2.9. The clock accepts any instant, later or earlier than the one it currently holds, but the sweep is one-directional: only `VERIFIED` licences are examined, so a credential already `EXPIRED` is never returned to `VERIFIED` by any subsequent clock setting. The sweep then recomputes both scorecard variants for every installer row in the store, whatever its network status, because both the rolling window and the decay bands moved; an installer whose recomputation leaves every score, counter, flag and network status exactly as it found them has not been mutated, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move. The clock response reports `current_utc` along with three counters — `credentials_expired_count`, `installers_suspended_count` and `installers_rescored_count`, the last being the total number of installer rows visited rather than a count of those whose figures actually changed — so a caller can see what the advance did.

### 2.5. Money, Units, Scores and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an **integer count of cents**, in both request bodies and responses. A money value sent as a JSON string or as a float is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` and HTTP 422, as is any instant that is not an ISO 8601 UTC string with exactly three fractional digits.

Wherever rounding is required, the method is **round half up**: round the decimal value to the nearest integer with ties resolved away from zero. Where a value is a percentage or a score rather than cents, the same method is applied to exactly two decimal places. Every such non-money numeric — percentages, multipliers, scores and unit quantities alike — travels as a JSON number in both directions and never as a string, so a two-decimal figure is carried as the plain number it equals: `79.6`, not `"79.60"`, and `250.0`, not `"250.00"`. Unit quantities — estimated units, measured units, minimum billable units, change-order line deltas — permit at most two decimal places on input.

The **order** in which rounding is applied is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, because a different order produces a different and wrong answer by one or more cents. Three places pin it explicitly. In the tier ladder that prices installation labour, each tier band is rounded to whole cents and the rounded bands are then added. In the jurisdiction multiplier step, the base amount and the surcharge amount are added first, the sum is multiplied once, and the product is rounded once. In change-order pricing, every line is rounded to whole cents before any line is added to another, so that summing the raw decimals and rounding once at the end — which can differ by a cent — is incorrect. Each of these is spelled out where the calculation lives in §3.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on the Four Program-Critical Writes

Four writes carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, because in each case the state machine alone would legitimately admit the request and only the caller's staleness makes it wrong.

Creating an installer assignment requires `expected_project_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Project`. If it does not equal that project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard is load-bearing: a project sitting in `MEASURED` legitimately accepts an assignment, and a project that took an assignment and then had it released is back in `MEASURED`, so the state precondition alone would let a client holding an out-of-date view create a second overlapping hold.

Creating a change order likewise requires `expected_project_version` matching the project's current `version`, on pain of `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new change order may name `supersedes_change_order_id` and legitimately drive an earlier one to `SUPERSEDED`, a client whose view of the project predates another accepted change order would otherwise price its lines against a scope that no longer exists.

Reinstating a cancelled project requires `expected_project_version` matching the project's current `version`, again on pain of `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Reinstatement reverses the settlement bucket movements computed at cancellation, so a client whose view predates a later settlement adjustment would reverse amounts that are no longer the amounts on record.

Removing an installer requires `expected_installer_version` matching the target `Installer`'s current `version`, on pain of `ERR_STALE_INSTALLER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. `removal_eligible` is a computed field that any newly filed scorecard event can flip back to false, and removal is reachable from more than one network state, so a client holding a stale view would remove a firm on the strength of a scorecard the network no longer has.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where an OCC field is required it is **not optional**: if it is absent or null, the answer is `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` (HTTP 422), and it must be a positive integer — a value that is present but is not one, whether zero, negative, a float, a boolean or a numeric string, answers that same `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`. The OCC field stands outside the endpoint's declared input for the purposes of ranks 3 to 5, so it is never reported as a type or a range violation and is examined only at rank 9, after every path identifier and body foreign key has already been resolved.

### 2.7. Flat Addressing, Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Reverse Discovery

Collections sit at the root of the URL space: projects, installers, customers, measurements, change orders, material deliveries, installer assignments, punch-list items, warranty claims, installer credentials, service categories and jurisdictions are each addressable at their own top-level collection and at their own `{id}`. Child records are linked to their parents by foreign keys carried in request bodies or in the creating path, never by deep nesting: no resource is addressed more than one segment below another resource's `{id}`.

When a path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved **left to right**, and the first one that matches no stored row decides the error. Each entity has its own not-found code — `ERR_SERVICE_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLER_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATERIAL_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLER_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` — each returned with HTTP 404. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after every path identifier, and among themselves in the field order the endpoint declares for its input.

Because several resources are created as a side effect of an operation rather than being returned in its response body — the punch items opened by a quality inspection, the change order auto-opened by a measurement variance — every foreign key is reverse-reachable through a filtered list. A project's measurements, change orders, material deliveries, punch-list items and scorecard events each have their own project-scoped list; change orders can be filtered across projects by `project_id`, `source_measurement_id` or `supersedes_change_order_id`; credentials can be filtered by `installer_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `trade_code` or `verification_status`; assignments by `project_id` or `installer_id`; customers by `jurisdiction_id`; and warranty claims by `project_id`, `status` or `claim_type`.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder: One Fixed Evaluation Order

Many requests in this system violate more than one rule at once. A request that creates an install window for an unlicensed installer whose insurance also lapsed, on a project whose deposit was never taken, violates three rules; exactly one answer is correct. Checks are therefore evaluated in a single, globally fixed order, and the **first** check that fails is the one that produces the response. Lower-numbered ranks below are evaluated strictly before higher-numbered ones.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401). A header holding nothing but whitespace counts as empty. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and is listed as authorised for the target endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every required field of the endpoint's input is present and non-null — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` (422). Applies to every write endpoint. A request carrying no body at all, or a body that is not parseable JSON, is treated as an empty object and therefore fails here on the endpoint's first required field; a body that parses to something other than a JSON object — an array, a string, a number — is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` (422) instead at every endpoint that reads a body at all, while the action endpoints that take no request body — assignment confirmation and start, installer activation, punch-item sign-off and project closing — never inspect it and so cannot fail on its shape. An optional field supplied as null is treated as absent rather than as a value, and a field the endpoint does not declare is ignored at every rank: an unknown key is never rejected and never stored, with exactly one exception anywhere in the programme — the shape rule of rank 28 rejects the key `source_measurement_id` outright on a client-raised change order, as §3.4 spells out.
4. Every supplied field has its declared type, every enum value is a member of its declared set, every money field is a JSON integer rather than a string or a float, and every instant is an ISO 8601 UTC string with exactly three fractional digits — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` (422). Applies to every write endpoint.
5. Numeric ranges and string lengths hold: unit quantities are greater than zero with at most two decimal places, cent amounts are at least zero, `csat_score` lies between 1 and 5, `workmanship_score` lies between 0 and 100, `limit` lies between 1 and 100, `offset` is at least 0, `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`, and `promised_window_end` is strictly after `promised_window_start` — otherwise `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to every endpoint, reads included. Ranks 3, 4 and 5 are each a complete pass over the endpoint's whole input before the next pass begins, and inside a pass the fields are examined in the order the endpoint declares them, so a missing second field outranks a mistyped first one. Within each pass the top-level fields are examined first and the entries of a declared array of objects — a change order's `lines`, a quality inspection's `defects` — only afterwards, in array order and then in the sub-field order declared for an entry; an entry that is not a JSON object at all is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` at rank 4. Every declared string field, identifier and foreign-key fields included, carries rank 5's length floor of one character, so an empty-string foreign key answers `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE` here rather than a not-found code at rank 7, while a foreign key sent as a number answers `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` at rank 4.
6. Every identifier appearing in the URL path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right — otherwise that entity's not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
7. Every foreign-key identifier in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the field order the endpoint declares — otherwise that entity's not-found code (404). This rank guards project creation, change-order creation, assignment creation, credential filing, customer registration and scorecard-event filing.
8. Uniqueness holds: `ServiceCategory.code` is unique across all service categories, `Jurisdiction.code` is unique across all jurisdictions, and a credential is unique on the combination of `installer_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `trade_code` among rows whose `verification_status` is anything other than `REJECTED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). This rank guards service-category creation, jurisdiction creation and credential filing.
9. Where the endpoint declares an optimistic-concurrency field, that field is present and is a positive integer — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` (422). This rank guards assignment creation, change-order creation, project reinstatement and installer removal.
10. `expected_project_version` equals the target project's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412), on assignment creation, change-order creation and project reinstatement.
11. `expected_installer_version` equals the target installer's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_INSTALLER_VERSION` (412), on installer removal.
12. The target entity's own status admits the requested transition per its state machine — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Four operations are deliberately carved out of this generic rank and governed by their own specific ranks instead: project cancellation and project closing are governed by rank 38, punch-item resolution and punch-item verification by rank 21, and warranty-claim filing by rank 31. Consequently an un-cancellable project answers `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Closing is only a *partial* carve-out: rank 38 owns its financial precondition, but its execution-dimension precondition — `INSTALL_COMPLETE` or `PUNCH_OPEN`, and `quality_result` exactly `PASS` when `INSTALL_COMPLETE` — does still answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and it is the very last check the close handler performs, after ranks 21, 22 and 38 have each passed. The balance payment is likewise outside this generic rank: its financial dimension is examined only by rank 36, so a balance payment never answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. One inner ordering is fixed wherever a write reads the owning project's `fulfillment_status`: the terminal guard of rank 13 and the cancellation guard of rank 14 are both applied ahead of this generic rank, so a `CLOSED` project answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and, on the five child writes rank 14 names, a `CANCELLED` project answers `ERR_PROJECT_CANCELLED`; where rank 14 does not reach the write — the deposit payment is the plain example — a `CANCELLED` project falls through to this generic rank and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
13. The target entity is not already in a terminal state for its machine — a `CLOSED` project, an `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `SUPERSEDED` change order, a `VERIFIED` punch item, an `UPHELD` or `DENIED` warranty claim, a `REMOVED` installer, a `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` measurement, a `RETURNED` material delivery, or a permit dimension already at `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
14. The owning project's `fulfillment_status` is not `CANCELLED` for any operation that writes one of its children — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_CANCELLED` (409). This rank guards measurement booking, change-order creation, assignment creation, material-delivery planning and quality inspection.
15. Every referenced row is eligible for the use being made of it: the service category's `status` is `ACTIVE` at project creation, the jurisdiction's `status` is `ACTIVE` at both project creation and assignment creation, and the project does not already hold a live assignment — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
16. The installer being assigned has network `status` of `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; `APPLIED`, `DOCS_PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REMOVED` firms cannot be assigned — otherwise `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ASSIGNABLE` (409).
17. Whenever the jurisdiction's `license_required` is true, the installer holds a credential whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` for that project's `jurisdiction_id` and for the service category's `trade_code` — otherwise `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_LICENSED` (409).
18. `background_check_result` is `PASS` and `background_check_completed_at` is no older than the jurisdiction's `background_check_recency_days` whole days measured from the logical clock, inclusive — otherwise `ERR_BACKGROUND_CHECK_STALE` (409).
19. `insurance_expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock and `liability_coverage_cents` is at least the jurisdiction's `min_liability_coverage_cents` — otherwise `ERR_INSURANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
20. When the service category has `measurement_required` true, the project has a measurement whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, and the assignment's `window_start` is no later than that measurement's `completed_at` plus the category's `measurement_validity_days` whole days, inclusive — otherwise `ERR_MEASUREMENT_STALE` (409).
21. The punch-list safety rules hold: a `SAFETY` punch item may be resolved only from `ESCALATED`, a `SAFETY` punch item may be verified only by a `ServiceManager`, and a project may not be closed while any of its punch items is still `OPEN`, `RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED` — otherwise `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED` (409). This rank guards punch-item resolution, punch-item verification and project closing.
22. A project whose jurisdiction has `inspection_required` true and whose `permit_responsibility` is not `NOT_REQUIRED` may not be closed until its `permit_status` is `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED` (409), on project closing.
23. The requested install window does not conflict with any live window of the same installer once the travel buffer is added — otherwise `ERR_INSTALLER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
24. The installer's `committed_minutes` plus the new assignment's `duration_minutes` does not exceed the programme commitment cap — otherwise `ERR_INSTALLER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
25. At confirmation time the project has a material delivery whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED`, and the delivery slack before the window meets the minimum — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_SLACK_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
26. A requested inspection slot begins at least the jurisdiction's `inspection_lead_days` whole days after the logical clock, inclusive, and `inspection_slot_end` is strictly after `inspection_slot_start` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_TIME` (422).
27. The number of *other* projects in the same jurisdiction holding an overlapping scheduled inspection is strictly less than the jurisdiction's `max_concurrent_inspections` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_CAPACITY_FULL` (409).
28. A change order's shape matches its `origin` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_SHAPE` (422), on change-order creation and change-order submission.
29. The acting `X-User-ID` owns the resource: a customer decision comes from the project's `customer_id`, and an installer's start, completion or punch resolution comes from the relevant assignment's `installer_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rank guards the change-order decision, assignment start, assignment completion, punch-item resolution, warranty-claim filing, deposit payment and balance payment.
30. A permit application is filed by the party named in `permit_responsibility` — otherwise `ERR_WRONG_PERMIT_PARTY` (403).
31. A warranty claim has `claim_type` of `LABOR_DEFECT` and is filed no later than the project's `closed_at` plus 365 whole days, inclusive — otherwise `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` (409).
32. A reinstatement is requested strictly less than 72 hours after the project's `cancelled_at` — otherwise `ERR_REINSTATE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
33. `removal_eligible` is true, the installer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, and the installer holds no assignment whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` — otherwise `ERR_REMOVAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409). Held and confirmed windows are legal at removal time and are released by the removal cascade.
34. A deposit payment's `amount_cents` equals the project's `deposit_required_cents` exactly — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Both underpayment and overpayment fail.
35. An assignment may be created only while the project's `financial_status` is `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `BALANCE_DUE` or `SETTLED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_HELD` (409).
36. A balance payment's `amount_cents` equals the project's `balance_due_cents` exactly, and `balance_due_cents` is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_BALANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). This rank is the only check that consults the project's financial dimension on a balance payment; the generic state precondition of rank 12 is not applied to that write.
37. A change order's computed `upcharge_cents` is not below the credit floor — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422), on change-order creation and change-order submission.
38. A project may be cancelled only from `DRAFT`, `MEASURE_PENDING`, `MEASURED`, `CHANGE_PENDING`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and may be closed only once its `financial_status` is `SETTLED` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (409).

Two consequences of this ladder deserve naming, because they are the two most commonly mis-implemented orderings. Every compliance check on assignment creation — installer assignability, licensing, background check, insurance and measurement freshness, ranks 16 through 20 — runs **before** the calendar and capacity checks of ranks 23 and 24, which in turn run **before** the fiscal check of rank 35. An unlicensed installer being booked into an already-occupied window on a project with no deposit therefore answers `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_LICENSED`. And the stale-version guards of ranks 10 and 11 run **before** any state precondition, so a client that is both stale and wrong about the state sees the stale-version error.

### 2.9. Three Independent Status Dimensions, and How Cascades Propagate

A `Project` does not have one status; it has three, and they advance on different triggers and are asserted independently. `fulfillment_status` is the execution dimension and takes one of `DRAFT`, `MEASURE_PENDING`, `MEASURED`, `CHANGE_PENDING`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `INSTALL_COMPLETE`, `PUNCH_OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, terminating at `CLOSED`. `DRAFT` is a declared value of that dimension that no stored project ever actually holds: the sale of §3.3 opens the execution dimension directly at `MEASURE_PENDING` or `MEASURED`, and no operation anywhere writes `DRAFT`, so the `DRAFT` entries in the cancellable set of §3.8 and in its forfeit table are unreachable in practice. `financial_status` is the money dimension and takes one of `QUOTED`, `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `BALANCE_DUE`, `SETTLED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED`, beginning at `QUOTED` and terminating at `SETTLED`. `permit_status` is the permitting dimension and takes one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_APPLICATION`, `APPLIED`, `ISSUED`, `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, `INSPECTION_PASSED`, `INSPECTION_FAILED` or `CLOSED`, and unlike the other two its initial value is computed rather than fixed. A deposit may be taken while execution is still at `DRAFT`, `MEASURE_PENDING`, `MEASURED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`; a permit may progress while the install has not started. Only the specific transitions described in §3 couple the dimensions to one another.

Cascades are the second structural fact. Several single operations mutate more than one entity, and every affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, even though the caller named only the primary target. The release sweep shared by all four cascades below versions each affected installer and each affected project exactly once, however many of that row's windows the same sweep released, so a firm that loses three held windows to one suspension moves by a single increment rather than by three. The response body carries only the primary resource; the secondary effects are found through the reverse-discovery lists of §2.7. Four cascades recur throughout §3, and each is stated in full where it lives: releasing held work when a scope change reopens (`SCOPE_CHANGE_PENDING`), releasing held work when an installer loses its last verified licence (`INSTALLER_SUSPENDED`), releasing held work when an installer is removed (`INSTALLER_REMOVED`), and the wide cancellation cascade that voids measurements, supersedes undecided change orders, returns the material and releases the window (`PROJECT_CANCELLED`).

### 2.10. Two Conservation Invariants

The service maintains two arithmetic invariants that must hold after **every** write, with no exception, and that a reviewer may check at any moment.

The first is the **deposit ledger**. A project carries four deposit buckets — `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` — alongside `deposit_paid_cents`, the cumulative amount the customer has handed over, which never decreases. At all times `deposit_paid_cents` equals the sum of the four buckets. Money never appears or disappears; it only moves between buckets. This holds before a cancellation, after a cancellation and after a reinstatement.

The second is **installer capacity**. An installer's `committed_minutes` always equals the sum of `duration_minutes` over that installer's assignments whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`. Holding a window increases it, releasing a window or recording a no-show decreases it by exactly the same amount, and completing a window leaves it unchanged because completed work stays inside the sum.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Programme Configuration: the Service Catalogue and the Jurisdiction Policy Table

Nothing can be sold until a `ProgramAdmin` has described what the retailer installs. A **service category** is that description. It carries a `code` — the retailer-facing catalogue key, between 1 and 32 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, unique across all service categories with uniqueness compared case-sensitively — and a customer-facing `name` between 1 and 120 characters. The uppercase-letter, digit and hyphen shape of a `code` is a retailer-side convention that the service does not police: the only thing rank 5 enforces on either string is its length bound, so a lowercase or punctuated `code` of legal length is accepted and stored verbatim. It names the `trade_code` an installer must be licensed in to work the category, one of `FLOORING`, `GLAZING`, `HVAC`, `ROOFING` or `MILLWORK`, and the `unit_of_measure` labour is billed in, one of `SQFT`, `LINEAR_FT` or `EACH`. Pricing starts from `base_labor_rate_cents_per_unit`, the first-tier labour rate in cents per unit, which must be at least 1, and from `minimum_billable_units`, a floor of at least zero with at most two decimal places that is applied to the billable quantity before the tier ladder runs — note that this floor never influences permit thresholds, which look at the units actually expected on site.

Four policy switches complete the category. `measurement_required` decides whether an in-home visit is mandatory: when it is true, an install window cannot be held until a completed measurement exists. `measurement_validity_days` is how long a completed measurement remains usable for scheduling, counted from the measurement's `completed_at`; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 45 when the caller omits it. `permit_policy` decides how permit necessity is settled and is one of `NEVER`, `ALWAYS` or `BY_JURISDICTION`, defaulting to `BY_JURISDICTION`. `deposit_pct` is the fraction of the frozen labour quote the household must deposit before work can be scheduled; it must be greater than 0 and at most 1 with at most two decimal places, and defaults to 0.30 — thirty per cent. Finally `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; a `RETIRED` category is refused at project creation with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A category may later be amended in its `name`, `base_labor_rate_cents_per_unit`, `minimum_billable_units`, `measurement_validity_days`, `permit_policy`, `deposit_pct` and `status`; each amendment increments the category's `version`, and no amendment ever reaches back into a project whose quote has already been frozen. The programme has exactly three amendment endpoints — service category, jurisdiction and installer firm — and each treats the call itself as the mutation: a `PATCH` that merely repeats the values already on the row, or that supplies no amendable field at all, still refreshes `updated_at` and still increments `version`. Categories may be listed and filtered by `status` and by `trade_code`.

A **jurisdiction** is the permitting authority for an install site, and it is where almost every compliance floor in the programme is configured. It carries its own `code`, between 1 and 16 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and unique across all jurisdictions — again compared case-sensitively, and again with only the length bound actually enforced — plus a `name` between 1 and 120 characters. `permit_required_trades` lists the trade codes that need a permit here — drawn from the same five trades, and permitted to be empty; the list is stored exactly as supplied, in the order supplied and with any repeated entry kept. `permit_unit_threshold` is the unit quantity at or above which a permit becomes necessary; it must be at least zero, defaults to 0, is an **inclusive** lower bound, and is the one unit quantity in the programme that carries no two-decimal ceiling — a threshold of `250.125` is accepted. `permit_responsible_party` names who normally pulls the permit and is one of `RETAILER`, `INSTALLER` or `CUSTOMER`. `inspection_required` says whether an issued permit must reach a passed inspection before the project may close. `inspection_lead_days` is the minimum number of whole days between the logical clock and a booked inspection start, must be at least zero and defaults to 3. `max_concurrent_inspections` caps how many projects in this jurisdiction may hold overlapping inspection slots, must be at least 1 and defaults to 2.

Three more jurisdiction settings gate the installer network rather than the permit. `background_check_recency_days` is the maximum accepted age of an installer's completed background check for work here; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 365 — one calendar year. `min_liability_coverage_cents` is the minimum general-liability limit accepted here, at least zero. `license_required`, which defaults to true, decides whether a verified trade licence is demanded at all. Money is shaped by `labor_complexity_multiplier`, applied to the sum of the base and surcharge amounts in the labour calculation; it must be greater than zero with at most two decimal places and defaults to 1.00. A jurisdiction's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` jurisdiction accepts neither new projects nor new assignments, again answering `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Every one of these settings except `code` may be amended later, each amendment incrementing the jurisdiction's `version`, and jurisdictions may be listed filtered by `status` and by `permit_responsible_party`.

One consequence of amendability deserves emphasis because it is a common misreading: raising or lowering `permit_unit_threshold` does **not** re-run the permit determination on projects that already exist. The only event that re-runs a determination is the completion of a measurement on that project.

Duplicate configuration keys are rejected rather than merged. A second service category with an existing `code`, a second jurisdiction with an existing `code`, and a second live trade licence for a combination of installer, jurisdiction and trade all answer `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` with HTTP 409. Where the rank-3 to rank-5 passes of §2.8 need a declared field order, every write endpoint of this section and of §3.2 declares its required fields first, in the order this document introduces them, and then its optional fields in that same introduction order — so a jurisdiction registration that omits both `permit_required_trades` and `min_liability_coverage_cents` reports the former, while one whose `permit_unit_threshold` and `permit_responsible_party` are both mistyped reports the latter.

### 3.2. Building the Installer Network: Recruitment, Licences, Verification and Activation

A `NetworkManager` recruits an installer firm by recording its `legal_name` (1 to 160 characters), its `contact_email` (up to 320 characters, the at sign being a convention the service does not verify), the `primary_trade_code` it was recruited for, its general-liability limit as `liability_coverage_cents` (at least zero) and `insurance_expires_on`, the instant the liability policy lapses. A newly recruited firm lands in network status `APPLIED` with `background_check_result` of `NONE`, `background_check_completed_at` null, `committed_minutes`, `verified_credential_count`, `rolling_event_count`, `rolling_no_show_count` and `completed_project_count` all zero, `quality_score`, `timeliness_score`, `satisfaction_score`, `composite_score` and `lifetime_composite_score` all at the programme baseline of 85.00, `removal_eligible` false, and `probation_started_at`, `removed_at` and `removal_reason` all null. A firm's `contact_email`, `liability_coverage_cents` and `insurance_expires_on` may be amended later; each amendment bumps the firm's `version` and does **not** re-validate assignments already on the books, because the insurance test is applied at the moment a window is held and not retroactively. Firms may be listed filtered by `status`, by `primary_trade_code` and by `removal_eligible`.

Licensing is per jurisdiction and per trade. Filing a trade licence for a firm records the `jurisdiction_id` it is valid in — the jurisdiction must exist, though its `status` is deliberately not examined here, so a licence may be filed in a `SUSPENDED` jurisdiction — the `trade_code` it covers, the `license_number` as filed with the authority (1 to 40 characters), the `license_expires_on` instant, and `permit_pulling_authorized`, a flag defaulting to false that says whether this licence lets the firm pull permits in that jurisdiction. Every filed licence begins with `verification_status` of `PENDING`, `verified_at` null and `rejection_reason` null. Filing the **first** licence for a firm advances the firm `APPLIED → DOCS_PENDING` and increments the firm's `version`; second and later licences create their row and leave the firm's network status exactly where it already was. Filing is refused only for a firm already `REMOVED`, with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and that refusal sits at rank 13 — behind the duplicate-licence test of rank 8, so a duplicate combination filed against a removed firm answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. A licence list scoped to one firm accepts a `verification_status` filter, exactly as the network-wide licence list does.

Verification is recorded with a `verification_decision` of either `VERIFY` or `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by a `rejection_reason`. A `VERIFY` is admitted only from `PENDING`: it moves the licence `PENDING → VERIFIED` provided its `license_expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock, stamps `verified_at` with the clock, and recomputes the firm's `verified_credential_count`. A `REJECT` moves the licence to `REJECTED` from either `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`, recording the supplied reason in `rejection_reason` — a `REJECT` that carries none writes null there — and leaving whatever `verified_at` the licence already held untouched rather than clearing it. `ERR_INVALID_STATE` covers every decision the licence's own status or expiry does not admit: a `VERIFY` of a licence that is already `VERIFIED`, `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED`, a `VERIFY` of a `PENDING` licence whose `license_expires_on` is at or before the clock, and a `REJECT` of a licence that is `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED`. This endpoint is also the one place where a firm's `verified_credential_count` may move without the firm being versioned: when the recomputation changes only that counter and drives no network-status transition — a second licence verified for an already-`ACTIVE` firm, or a rejection that suspends nobody — the firm's `updated_at` and `version` stay exactly where they were and only the licence row is versioned.

Separately, a background-check outcome is filed as a `result` — `PASS` or `FAIL` — together with the `completed_at` instant the check finished, which are written to `background_check_result` and `background_check_completed_at` and bump the firm's `version`. The outcome may be refiled as often as an operator likes, in any network status short of `REMOVED` (which answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`), each filing overwriting both fields and incrementing `version` even when it changes neither. A `FAIL` is purely a record: it never demotes a firm that has already reached `VERIFIED`, `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, and it never sends a promoted firm back to `DOCS_PENDING`. It only bars the next admission attempt and the next window from being held.

Those two administrative acts jointly promote the firm, and **either one may be the act that does it**. A firm sitting in `DOCS_PENDING` advances `DOCS_PENDING → VERIFIED` as soon as, counting the effect of the call currently being handled, its `verified_credential_count` is at least 1, its `background_check_result` is `PASS`, and at least one of its `VERIFIED` licences has a `trade_code` equal to the firm's `primary_trade_code`. The service evaluates that rule at the end of both the licence-verification handler and the background-check handler, so whichever call completes the pair performs the promotion, and the other call simply leaves the firm in `DOCS_PENDING`.

Admitting a firm to the assignable network is a deliberate second act. It moves the firm `VERIFIED → ACTIVE`, and requires that `insurance_expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock and that `background_check_result` is `PASS`. Admission also runs the scorecard in both of its variants, so that `quality_score`, `timeliness_score`, `satisfaction_score`, `composite_score`, `lifetime_composite_score` and `removal_eligible` are all written rather than left at their creation defaults. Attempting to admit a firm that is not in `VERIFIED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and so does either of the other two admission conditions failing: lapsed insurance and a `background_check_result` other than `PASS` are both `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at admission, because `ERR_INSURANCE_INSUFFICIENT` and `ERR_BACKGROUND_CHECK_STALE` are reserved for the assignment gate of ranks 19 and 18. A firm already `REMOVED` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` instead. Admission writes the scores but does **not** apply the probation transition of §3.9: a firm whose freshly computed rolling composite would otherwise justify probation still lands in `ACTIVE` with `probation_started_at` null, until the next event-appending request or clock move re-evaluates it.

Losing licences works in the other direction, and it is a cascade. When a rejection — or the clock-driven expiry sweep of §2.4 — leaves a firm with a recomputed `verified_credential_count` of zero, the firm moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`. That suspension is hop three of a four-hop chain: the licence transition is hop one, the firm's suspension is hop two, hop three transitions every assignment of that firm whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `INSTALLER_SUSPENDED`, and hop four — caused by hop three — reduces the firm's `committed_minutes` by each released assignment's `duration_minutes` and reverts each affected project `SCHEDULED → MEASURED`. The firm's `version` increments, as does that of every row the cascade touched. Recovery is symmetrical and needs no separate endpoint: verifying any `PENDING` licence for a `SUSPENDED` firm moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, provided the recomputed `verified_credential_count` is at least 1 and `insurance_expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock, and clears `probation_started_at` to null.

`EXPIRED` is a status no operator can write. It is produced only by the epoch-advance sweep when the logical clock moves, applied in ascending credential `created_at` order to every `VERIFIED` licence whose `license_expires_on` is at or before the new clock value. Because the sweep is a pure function of the new clock value and the licence's expiry instant, expiry is fully reproducible. A firm whose licence expired this way will be refused at the next attempt to hold a window for it, with `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_LICENSED`.

### 3.3. Selling the Project: the Frozen Provisional Quote and the Deposit

A household enters the programme as a **customer** record carrying `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), `email` (up to 320 characters, the at sign again being a convention the service does not verify) and `jurisdiction_id`, the household's billing jurisdiction, which must name a stored jurisdiction but need not name an `ACTIVE` one. A newly registered customer starts with `project_count` at zero. Customers may be listed filtered by `jurisdiction_id`, and a single customer read exposes the `project_count` that later feeds the goodwill waiver tier of §3.8.

Selling a project records `customer_id`, `service_category_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, the `site_address` (1 to 200 characters), the sales-desk `estimated_units` (greater than zero, at most two decimal places) and `requested_install_date`, the household's preferred start. That last field is **advisory only**: it is stored and returned, and it never gates any transition. Note that the install-site `jurisdiction_id` on the project is the permitting jurisdiction and may legitimately differ from the customer's own billing `jurisdiction_id`; every compliance floor is read from the project's jurisdiction, not the customer's.

Project creation does five things in one request. It sets `effective_units` — the quantity the system currently believes will be installed — equal to `estimated_units`. It runs the labour calculation in its provisional variant and freezes the result into `quoted_labor_cents`, which is **never** recomputed afterwards, not by a measurement, not by an accepted change order, and not by a later amendment to the category's rate. It sets `deposit_required_cents` to the frozen quote multiplied by the category's `deposit_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and freezes that alongside. It runs the permit determination of §3.5, writing `permit_responsibility` and the project's initial `permit_status`. And it opens the two remaining dimensions: `financial_status` at `QUOTED` with all four deposit buckets and `deposit_paid_cents` at zero, and `fulfillment_status` at `MEASURE_PENDING` when the category has `measurement_required` true, or straight at `MEASURED` when it does not. Every remaining field of the new project row opens at its neutral value, and the creation response carries them all: `final_labor_cents` null and `change_order_upcharge_cents`, `permit_fee_cents_billed`, `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents` all zero; `permit_application_reference`, `permit_issued_on`, `inspection_slot_start`, `inspection_slot_end`, `workmanship_score`, `install_completed_at`, `cancelled_at`, `cancel_reason` and `closed_at` all null; `inspection_result` and `quality_result` both the string `NONE`; `inspection_attempt_count` and `escalated_punch_count` zero; and `service_manager_review_required` false. The purchasing customer's `project_count` is then recomputed as the number of projects now on file against that household — an increase of exactly one — and the customer row is itself mutated by the sale, so its `updated_at` and `version` both move even though the caller named only the project. Projects may be listed filtered by `customer_id`, `service_category_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `fulfillment_status`, `financial_status` and `permit_status`, in any combination.

**How labour is priced.** The labour charge is an integer number of cents and is built in six steps, in this order. First, a unit quantity is chosen: the provisional variant uses the project's `estimated_units`, while the final variant uses the `measured_units` of the project's most recent measurement whose `status` is `COMPLETED` — most recent by `completed_at` descending, with `created_at` ascending as the only permitted tie-break — falling back to `estimated_units` when the category does not require a measurement and none has been completed. Second, the billable quantity is the greater of that unit quantity and the category's `minimum_billable_units`. Third, the billable quantity is spread across three marginal tiers: the first 200.00 units are charged at the category's `base_labor_rate_cents_per_unit`; the next 300.00 units — that is, the portion above 200.00 and up to 500.00 — are charged at the base rate multiplied by 0.92, rounded half up to a whole number of cents before it is used; and every unit beyond 500.00 is charged at the base rate multiplied by 0.85, likewise rounded half up to whole cents before use. Each of the three band amounts is computed as its unit count multiplied by its band rate and **rounded half up to whole cents individually**, and only then are the three rounded band amounts added together to give the base amount. Fourth, site-condition surcharges are added — but only in the final variant, since the provisional quote is struck before anyone has visited the house, and its surcharge amount is therefore exactly zero. In the final variant the surcharge is the sum of three parts: a subfloor remediation charge of nothing when the measurement recorded `NONE`, the base amount multiplied by 0.04 and rounded half up when it recorded `MINOR`, and the base amount multiplied by 0.09 and rounded half up when it recorded `MAJOR`; plus 1850 cents (eighteen dollars fifty) for each obstruction counted in `obstruction_count`; plus 4200 cents (forty-two dollars) for each flight of stairs counted in `stair_flight_count`. Fifth, the base amount and the surcharge amount are **added first**, the sum is multiplied **once** by the jurisdiction's `labor_complexity_multiplier`, and that product is rounded half up **once** to whole cents. Sixth and last, the answer is the greater of that figure and the programme minimum labour charge of 15000 cents — one hundred and fifty dollars.

The provisional variant runs exactly once per project, inside the sale, and produces `quoted_labor_cents`. The final variant runs when the installer reports the work finished and produces `final_labor_cents`; it also runs, for disclosure purposes only, when a measurement completes with a material variance. Accepted change orders never feed the final variant — their money lives in `change_order_upcharge_cents` — so the measured units are charged exactly once.

**Taking the deposit.** The household pays the deposit by supplying `amount_cents`, which must equal `deposit_required_cents` **exactly**: both underpayment and overpayment are rejected with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the project's `customer_id`, or the answer is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A successful payment moves `financial_status` from `QUOTED` to `DEPOSIT_HELD`, sets `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_held_cents` both to the paid amount, re-checks the conservation identity of §2.10, and increments the project's `version`. Because the money dimension is independent of the execution dimension, a deposit may be taken while `fulfillment_status` is still `DRAFT`, `MEASURE_PENDING`, `MEASURED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`; the only bar is that a project already `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED` takes no deposit. A `CLOSED` project is stopped by the terminal guard of rank 13 with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; a `CANCELLED` one — the deposit payment not being among the five child writes rank 14 guards — answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, exactly as a project whose `financial_status` has already left `QUOTED` does, since the handler tests the cancellation and the financial dimension together in one generic state check.

### 3.4. The Measurement Hinge and the Change-Order Loop

**Booking the visit.** A coordinator books the in-home appointment against a project whose `fulfillment_status` is `MEASURE_PENDING`, supplying `scheduled_at` (an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits) and `technician_name` (1 to 120 characters). The appointment is created at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `completed_at`, `measured_units`, `subfloor_condition`, `obstruction_count`, `stair_flight_count`, `access_notes`, `variance_pct` and `voided_reason` all null and `change_order_triggered` false. Booking does not itself move the project off `MEASURE_PENDING`, so a project may legitimately hold more than one `SCHEDULED` appointment at a time; because completing any one of them drives the project off `MEASURE_PENDING`, the remaining appointments can then never be completed and answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Measurements can be listed per project or across projects, filtered by `project_id` and `status`, and read individually.

**Completing the visit is the hinge of the entire programme.** The technician's report supplies `measured_units` (greater than zero, at most two decimal places), `subfloor_condition` (`NONE`, `MINOR` or `MAJOR`), `obstruction_count` and `stair_flight_count` (both integers, zero or greater) and optionally `access_notes` (up to 500 characters). It requires the appointment to be `SCHEDULED` and the project to be `MEASURE_PENDING`. Completion performs, in one request: the appointment moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and `completed_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock value, which is the instant from which measurement freshness is later counted; `variance_pct` is computed as the absolute difference between `measured_units` and the project's `estimated_units`, divided by `estimated_units`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places; `change_order_triggered` is set true when `variance_pct` is 10.00 or greater and false otherwise; the project's `effective_units` is overwritten with `measured_units` — note that `estimated_units` itself is never overwritten, because `variance_pct` must remain reproducible; the permit determination of §3.5 is re-run against the new `effective_units`, so `permit_status` may flip in either direction between `NOT_REQUIRED` and `PENDING_APPLICATION`; and the project's `version` increments.

The project's `fulfillment_status` then takes one of two exits. When `change_order_triggered` is false, it moves `MEASURE_PENDING → MEASURED` and the project is ready to schedule. When `change_order_triggered` is true, the system itself raises a change order — `origin` `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE`, `source_measurement_id` pointing at this appointment, an empty `lines` array, `status` opened directly at `PENDING_CUSTOMER` and `upcharge_cents` priced by the variance-disclosure variant — and the project moves `MEASURE_PENDING → CHANGE_PENDING`, which blocks scheduling until the household answers. That reopening of the scope is where the `SCOPE_CHANGE_PENDING` cascade named in §2.9 lives: every assignment of this project still `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` is driven to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `SCOPE_CHANGE_PENDING`, each release recomputing its installer's `committed_minutes` and moving that installer's `version` once, while the project itself is left at `CHANGE_PENDING` rather than being reverted to `MEASURED`. In practice that release set is always empty, because a window can be held only from `MEASURED` while a measurement can be completed only from `MEASURE_PENDING`: like the `DRAFT` fulfillment value of §2.9, the cascade is declared but unreachable. That auto-created change order appears in **no** response body; it is discovered through the project's own change-order listing or through the cross-project listing filtered by `source_measurement_id`. It opens with `net_primary_units_delta` zero, `revision_count` zero and `revision_note` and `decided_at` null. The completion call's own response body carries the completed appointment row and nothing else — never the project — so the project's new `fulfillment_status`, `effective_units` and `permit_status` are read back through a project fetch.

A `SCHEDULED` appointment that never happens ends at `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` set to `PROJECT_CANCELLED`, written only by a cancellation cascade — either an explicit cancellation or the decline of a variance change order. Operators cannot set `VOIDED` directly, and a `COMPLETED` appointment is never voided.

**What a change order costs.** A coordinator raises a line-itemised change order supplying `project_id`, `origin`, `lines`, the concurrency token `expected_project_version`, and optionally `supersedes_change_order_id`. The `origin` must be `CUSTOMER_REQUEST` or `SITE_CONDITION`: a client that submits `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_SHAPE`, because only the measurement cascade may mint that origin. Symmetrically, a `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` change order must carry an empty `lines` array while every client-raised change order must carry between one and ten entries; either violation is the same `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_SHAPE`. Each entry of `lines` is an object with `unit_kind` — one of `PRIMARY_UNITS`, `TRIM_LINEAR_FT` or `FIXTURE_EACH` — and `units_delta`, a number with at most two decimal places that **may be negative**, because change orders also remove scope. The new change order opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `upcharge_cents` and `net_primary_units_delta` computed, `revision_count` zero, and `revision_note` and `decided_at` null; the project's `version` increments; and if `supersedes_change_order_id` was supplied, that earlier change order is driven from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CUSTOMER` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `SUPERSEDED` — it must belong to this very project and must hold one of exactly those three statuses, and a target failing either test is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_SHAPE`. Two further shape facts live here. Supplying the key `source_measurement_id` on this endpoint at all — even carrying null — is itself `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_SHAPE`, because that field belongs to the measurement cascade alone; it is the single undeclared key in the whole programme that is rejected rather than quietly ignored. And creation imposes no execution-dimension precondition of its own beyond the terminal and cancellation guards: a `DRAFT` change order may be raised against a project sitting anywhere from `MEASURE_PENDING` through `PUNCH_OPEN`, and it is submission, not creation, that demands `MEASURED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`.

The line-itemised price is built one line at a time, in array order. Each line's unit rate is the category's `base_labor_rate_cents_per_unit` for `PRIMARY_UNITS`, 296 cents for `TRIM_LINEAR_FT`, or 7350 cents for `FIXTURE_EACH`. The line's raw amount is its `units_delta` multiplied by that unit rate, multiplied by the jurisdiction's `labor_complexity_multiplier`, multiplied by the administration factor 1.06. **The rounding order is pinned: every line is rounded half up to whole cents first, and only the rounded line amounts are added.** Summing the raw decimal amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and differs observably by one or more cents. To make the requirement concrete: with a category rate of 425 cents, a jurisdiction multiplier of 1.15 and lines of 6.00 primary units, another 6.00 primary units and 12.00 linear feet of trim, the three raw amounts are 3108.45, 3108.45 and 4329.888, which round to 3108, 3108 and 4330 and sum to **10546** cents; summing first would give 10546.788, which rounds to 10547 and is **not** the required answer. Alongside the price, `net_primary_units_delta` is the plain sum of `units_delta` over the entries whose `unit_kind` is `PRIMARY_UNITS` — 12.00 in that example. It is informational only and never feeds the labour calculation.

The variance-disclosure price is not built from lines at all. It is the final-variant labour charge evaluated against the triggering measurement, minus the project's frozen `quoted_labor_cents`, and it may be negative. It is a disclosure and nothing more: accepting such a change order adds **nothing** to `change_order_upcharge_cents`, because those same measured units are already charged by the final labour variant when the installer reports the work complete. Booking it as well would charge the household twice.

**The credit cap.** Because `units_delta` may be negative, `upcharge_cents` may be negative. A change order whose `upcharge_cents` is below the negative of the project's `quoted_labor_cents` multiplied by 0.50 and rounded half up — that is, a credit larger than half the frozen quote — is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CAP`; the bound is strict, so an `upcharge_cents` exactly equal to that floor is admitted. The cap is tested on both of the writes that price lines, creation and submission, and on neither of them does it ever reach the `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` order the measurement cascade mints: that order is not line-priced, is never re-priced, and its disclosure figure may therefore be a credit of any size whatever. The cap is measured against the frozen quote, not against the current balance, so it does not move as the project accumulates upcharges.

**The revision loop.** Submission sends a change order to the household. From `DRAFT` it requires the project's `fulfillment_status` to be `MEASURED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` and the `lines` array to be non-empty; the status moves `DRAFT → PENDING_CUSTOMER`, the price is recomputed over the lines already stored on the change order — a `lines` array supplied on a `DRAFT` submission is ignored outright, neither stored nor priced — and a project sitting at `MEASURED` is driven to `CHANGE_PENDING`. From `REVISION_REQUESTED` a replacement `lines` array is **required**, not optional, and omitting it answers `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`; because that requirement is conditional on the change order's own status rather than declared unconditionally, it is examined after the terminal guard of rank 13 instead of inside the rank-3 pass. The array is replaced, `upcharge_cents` and `net_primary_units_delta` are recomputed, `revision_count` increments by one, and the status returns to `PENDING_CUSTOMER`. The cycle `PENDING_CUSTOMER → REVISION_REQUESTED → PENDING_CUSTOMER` may repeat without limit, and every pass through it re-prices from scratch, so a change of jurisdiction multiplier or category rate between passes is reflected in the new figure.

**The household's decision.** Only the project's own customer may decide, supplying `decision` — `ACCEPT`, `DECLINE` or `REQUEST_REVISION` — and, for a revision request, a `revision_note` of 1 to 300 characters, which stays **optional** even there: a revision request that carries none records null. An `ACCEPT` sets `status` to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `decided_at` with the clock, and moves the project `CHANGE_PENDING → MEASURED`; for `CUSTOMER_REQUEST` and `SITE_CONDITION` origins it adds `upcharge_cents` to the project's `change_order_upcharge_cents` and recomputes `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, while for `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` no money field changes at all. A `DECLINE` sets `status` to `DECLINED` and stamps `decided_at`; on a `CUSTOMER_REQUEST` or `SITE_CONDITION` change order it simply returns the project to `MEASURED` with no money change, but on a `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` change order it means the household has refused the re-measured scope, and the project is driven `CHANGE_PENDING → CANCELLED` with the full cancellation cascade of §3.8 — settlement computed, scheduled measurement voided, delivery returned, assignment released. A `REQUEST_REVISION` sets `status` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` with the supplied `revision_note` and deliberately **leaves** the project at `CHANGE_PENDING`; it is available only on `CUSTOMER_REQUEST` and `SITE_CONDITION` change orders, since a variance disclosure reports what was measured and cannot be renegotiated, so requesting revision on one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every decision increments both the change order's `version` and the project's `version`. Change orders reached from a terminal `status` — `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `SUPERSEDED` — are refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.5. Permitting and the Jurisdiction Inspection

**Who must pull the permit, and whether one is needed at all.** The determination reads the project's `effective_units`, its category's `permit_policy` and `trade_code`, its jurisdiction's `permit_required_trades`, `permit_unit_threshold` and `permit_responsible_party`, and the credential rows filed for that jurisdiction and trade. It runs in three steps. First it decides whether a permit is needed: a category whose `permit_policy` is `NEVER` never needs one; a category whose `permit_policy` is `ALWAYS` always needs one; and a category whose `permit_policy` is `BY_JURISDICTION` needs one exactly when the category's `trade_code` appears in the jurisdiction's `permit_required_trades` **and** the project's `effective_units` is greater than or equal to the jurisdiction's `permit_unit_threshold`. That unit test is an **inclusive** lower bound — `effective_units` exactly equal to `permit_unit_threshold` does require a permit. Second, when no permit is needed, both `permit_responsibility` and `permit_status` are set to `NOT_REQUIRED`. Third, when a permit is needed, the candidate party is the jurisdiction's `permit_responsible_party`, subject to one fallback: when that candidate is `INSTALLER` but **no** credential row exists with `jurisdiction_id` equal to the project's jurisdiction, `trade_code` equal to the category's trade, `verification_status` `VERIFIED` and `permit_pulling_authorized` true, the candidate becomes `RETAILER` instead — the retailer cannot delegate a permit to a network that holds no authorised licence there. The determined party is written to `permit_responsibility` and `permit_status` becomes `PENDING_APPLICATION`.

The determination runs twice at most: once inside the sale, and once more when a measurement completes, because `effective_units` switches from the estimated figure to the measured one. The second run may cross the threshold in **either** direction: a project sold at 249.00 units against a threshold of 250.00 starts `NOT_REQUIRED` and flips up to `PENDING_APPLICATION` when the measurement comes in at 250.00, and a project sold at 250.00 flips down to `NOT_REQUIRED` when the measurement comes in at 249.00. One thing the re-determination never does is rewind real-world progress: when the current `permit_status` is something other than `NOT_REQUIRED` or `PENDING_APPLICATION` — that is, an application has already been filed — only `permit_responsibility` is refreshed and `permit_status` is left exactly as it stands. And as noted in §3.1, amending a jurisdiction's `permit_unit_threshold` does not re-run the determination for any existing project; only a completed measurement does.

**Filing the application.** The party named in `permit_responsibility` files it, supplying `permit_application_reference` (1 to 64 characters, required on every call to this endpoint including a resubmission, where the value supplied is then thrown away) and optionally `resubmit`. The match is on the acting **role** alone, and no identity is ever compared: `RETAILER` admits `ProjectCoordinator` and `ProgramAdmin` alike, `INSTALLER` admits any caller presenting the `Installer` role whether or not that firm holds this project's assignment, and `CUSTOMER` admits any caller presenting the `Customer` role whether or not it is this project's own household — the permit filing is not one of the writes rank 29 guards, so it can answer `ERR_WRONG_PERMIT_PARTY` but never `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A caller whose role is permitted to reach the operation at all but does not match the recorded responsibility is refused with `ERR_WRONG_PERMIT_PARTY`. A filing from `PENDING_APPLICATION` records the reference and moves `permit_status` to `APPLIED`. A jurisdiction that rejects the paperwork is handled by filing again from `APPLIED` **with `resubmit` true**, which returns `permit_status` to `PENDING_APPLICATION` and clears `permit_application_reference` so the fresh reference can be filed cleanly on the next call; the flag exists precisely so that an accidental double-apply, which omits it, is caught and refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Issuing the permit and passing the fee through.** Recording the issue supplies `permit_fee_cents` (a non-negative integer) and `permit_issued_on`, requires `permit_status` to be `APPLIED`, and moves it to `ISSUED`. The supplied fee is copied to `permit_fee_cents_billed` and the project's `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are recomputed, so the jurisdiction's charge is passed straight through to the household with no markup.

**Booking the inspection.** Reserving a slot supplies `inspection_slot_start` and `inspection_slot_end`, requires `permit_status` to be `ISSUED` or `INSPECTION_FAILED`, and requires the jurisdiction's `inspection_required` to be true. Two independent limits apply. The lead-time floor requires `inspection_slot_start` to be at or after the current logical-clock value advanced by the jurisdiction's `inspection_lead_days` whole days; a nearer slot is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_TIME`. The capacity limit counts **other** projects in the same jurisdiction whose `permit_status` is `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` and whose recorded slot overlaps the requested one; when that count has already reached the jurisdiction's `max_concurrent_inspections`, the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_CAPACITY_FULL`. The overlap test treats each slot as a **closed, inclusive** interval, so two slots that merely touch end-to-start do count as overlapping. A successful reservation records both instants and moves `permit_status` to `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`.

**The inspector's verdict.** Recording the outcome supplies `result` — `PASSED` or `FAILED` — and optionally `inspector_note` (up to 300 characters, validated on the way in and then discarded — the project carries no field to hold it), and requires `permit_status` to be `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`. Either way the supplied `result` is written to `inspection_result` — a field a freshly sold project carries as `NONE` — and `inspection_attempt_count` increments by one. A `FAILED` result moves `permit_status` to `INSPECTION_FAILED` and clears `inspection_slot_start` and `inspection_slot_end` back to null, which releases the jurisdiction capacity for the next project in the queue. A `PASSED` result moves `permit_status` to `INSPECTION_PASSED` and then, **in the very same request**, on to `CLOSED`; there is no separate close-permit operation anywhere in the programme, and a project's permit dimension can only be observed at `INSPECTION_PASSED` transiently inside that handler, never in a stored row. The recorded slot instants are left in place on a pass, and no capacity is thereby held, because only projects still sitting at `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` are counted by the capacity test.

A failed inspection is not fatal. The cycle `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED → INSPECTION_FAILED → INSPECTION_SCHEDULED` may repeat without limit, each new booking re-testing the lead time against the clock as it stands then and the capacity as it stands then, with `inspection_attempt_count` accumulating across the whole history. `CLOSED` is the only terminal permit state.

### 3.6. Material Delivery, the Install Window and the Invoice

**Planning the delivery.** A coordinator plans the material drop against a project by supplying `promised_window_start`, `promised_window_end` (strictly after the start), `sku_count` (one or more) and `material_cost_cents` (a non-negative integer, the landed cost from which a restocking fee is later computed). The delivery opens at `status` `PLANNED` with `actual_delivered_at` null and `delay_days` zero. Planning imposes no execution-dimension precondition of its own beyond the terminal guard of rank 13 and the cancellation guard of rank 14: a drop may be planned against a project sitting anywhere from `MEASURE_PENDING` through `PUNCH_OPEN`, and any number of deliveries may be planned against the same project — which is precisely why the confirmation gate below and the restocking fee of §3.8 each have to say which row they read. Deliveries are listable per project and across projects, filtered by `project_id` and `status`.

**Moving the delivery.** The transit cycle is driven by supplying `new_status` and, for an arrival, `actual_delivered_at`. `PLANNED` or `DELAYED` may move to `IN_TRANSIT`; `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT` may move to `DELAYED`; and the pair `IN_TRANSIT ↔ DELAYED` therefore forms a cycle that may repeat without limit as the supplier's promises slip and recover. Either `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELAYED` may move to `DELIVERED`, and that hop **requires** `actual_delivered_at`; omitting it is a missing required field — a conditional requirement the handler tests before it resolves the delivery id at all, so a `DELIVERED` request that omits the instant answers `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` even when the path names no stored delivery. Supplied on any other hop, `actual_delivered_at` is validated on the way in and then discarded. On arrival, `delay_days` is computed as the arrival instant minus `promised_window_end`, floored at zero seconds, divided by 86400 seconds and rounded **up** to a whole day: arriving at or before the promised end gives zero, one second late gives one, exactly twenty-four hours late gives one, and twenty-four hours and one second late gives two. `new_status` is typed against all five delivery statuses, but only the hops just named are admitted: a request naming `PLANNED` or `RETURNED`, or naming a hop the cycle does not offer — `DELIVERED → DELAYED`, `DELIVERED → IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED → DELIVERED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, except that a delivery already `RETURNED` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` first, `RETURNED` being the transit machine's only terminal state and `DELIVERED` deliberately not being one. A successful hop touches the delivery row alone: no project field and no installer field moves with it. `RETURNED` is written only by a cancellation cascade, never by an operator, and it is reachable from `PLANNED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELAYED` or `DELIVERED`; the restocking component of the settlement is computed from the status the delivery held **before** it was marked `RETURNED`.

**Holding the install window: the compliance gate.** Creating an assignment is the single most heavily guarded write in the programme. The coordinator supplies `project_id`, `installer_id`, `window_start`, `window_end` (strictly after the start) and `expected_project_version`. The checks run in the fixed order of §2.8, and the ordering itself is part of the contract: after the stale-version guard, the compliance floors are tested before the calendar, and the calendar before the money. The project's own `fulfillment_status` must be exactly `MEASURED`: a project at `MEASURE_PENDING`, `CHANGE_PENDING`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `INSTALL_COMPLETE` or `PUNCH_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rank 12, which the handler tests after the terminal guard of rank 13 and the cancellation guard of rank 14 and before every floor below. Rank 15 then takes the rest of the project side together: the jurisdiction's `status` must still be `ACTIVE` and the project must not already hold an assignment whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, either failing answering `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — so a window since released, completed or recorded as a no-show leaves the project free to be scheduled again. In detail, the installer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` — a firm still `APPLIED`, `DOCS_PENDING` or `VERIFIED`, or one `SUSPENDED` or `REMOVED`, is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`, so a firm on probation may still be given work while a firm that has never been admitted may not. Where the project's jurisdiction has `license_required` true, the firm must hold a credential with `verification_status` `VERIFIED` for that `jurisdiction_id` and the category's `trade_code`, or the answer is `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_LICENSED`. The firm's `background_check_result` must be `PASS` and its `background_check_completed_at` no older than the jurisdiction's `background_check_recency_days` whole days measured from the logical clock, the age bound being **inclusive**, or the answer is `ERR_BACKGROUND_CHECK_STALE`. The firm's `insurance_expires_on` must be **strictly** after the logical clock and its `liability_coverage_cents` at least the jurisdiction's `min_liability_coverage_cents`, or the answer is `ERR_INSURANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Where the category has `measurement_required` true, the project must have a `COMPLETED` measurement and the assignment's `window_start` must be no later than that measurement's `completed_at` plus the category's `measurement_validity_days` whole days, an **inclusive** upper bound, or the answer is `ERR_MEASUREMENT_STALE` — measurements go stale, and a house measured months ago must be measured again.

Only then does the calendar speak. `duration_minutes` is the window length in whole minutes, **floored**. Two live assignments of the same installer conflict when the new window starts before the existing window's end extended by 90 minutes of travel buffer **and** the existing window starts before the new window's end extended by the same 90 minutes; both comparisons are **strict**, so a new window beginning exactly 90 minutes after an existing window's end is allowed while one beginning 89 minutes after it is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Windows are half-open intervals — start inclusive, end exclusive — and the buffer extends each window at its **end only**. Only assignments whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS` take part in the overlap test; `RELEASED`, `COMPLETED` and `NO_SHOW` rows are invisible to it, so a released window frees the calendar immediately. Next, the firm's `committed_minutes` plus the new `duration_minutes` must not exceed 4800 minutes, or the answer is `ERR_INSTALLER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; note that `committed_minutes` counts `COMPLETED` rows as well as live ones, so the cap is a lifetime workload ceiling rather than a rolling one. Last of all comes the money: the project's `financial_status` must be `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `BALANCE_DUE` or `SETTLED`, or the answer is `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_HELD` — the retailer does not dispatch a crew against an unpaid deposit.

A successful hold creates the assignment at `status` `HELD`, carrying the computed `duration_minutes` and opening `started_at`, `completed_at`, `on_time` and `release_reason` all at null, increases the installer's `committed_minutes` by `duration_minutes`, drives the project `MEASURED → SCHEDULED`, and increments both the project's and the installer's `version`. Assignments are listable filtered by `project_id`, `installer_id` and `status`.

**Confirming the window.** Confirmation takes no request body. It requires the assignment to be `HELD`, requires the project to have at least one material delivery whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED`, and requires at least 24 hours of slack between the material and the crew. Where several deliveries have been planned against the project, only rows holding one of those three statuses are candidates, and the **most recently created candidate** is the single row the slack is measured against: a `DELAYED` or `RETURNED` row is not merely ignored but skipped over in favour of an eligible older one, so such a row blocks confirmation only when no eligible row exists at all. That is deliberately not the rule §3.8 uses for the restocking fee, which reads the most recently created delivery whatever its status. The slack is the assignment's `window_start` minus the delivery's `promised_window_end`, divided by 3600 seconds and **floored** to a whole number of hours, and it must be 24 or greater. The comparison is on the floored integer, which makes the boundary sharp: a window starting exactly 86400 seconds after the promised end floors to 24 and is confirmed, while one starting 86340 seconds after it floors to 23 and is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_SLACK_INSUFFICIENT`. A negative difference floors toward negative infinity and is likewise refused. Confirmation moves the assignment `HELD → CONFIRMED` and touches nothing else.

**Starting work.** Only the assigned firm itself may start, and only from `CONFIRMED`. The permit dimension gates it: the project's `permit_status` must be one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `ISSUED`, `INSPECTION_SCHEDULED`, `INSPECTION_PASSED` or `CLOSED`. A project still at `PENDING_APPLICATION`, `APPLIED` or `INSPECTION_FAILED` is refused, because nobody may cut into a house on an unfiled, unissued or failed permit. Starting moves the assignment `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `started_at` with the logical clock, and drives the project `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`.

**Reporting the work finished.** The assigned firm supplies `completed_at`. This one call does eight things. The assignment moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and records `completed_at`. `on_time` is computed as `completed_at` being at or before `window_end`, **or** **any** material delivery on the project having `delay_days` of one or more — every delivery row is consulted, not just the single one the restocking fee of §3.8 would read, and whatever `status` it now holds, `RETURNED` included, since `delay_days` once computed is never cleared. The second clause is a deliberate excuse, so a crew that ran late behind late material is still credited as punctual. The final labour variant of §3.3 runs and its result is written to `final_labor_cents`. The invoice is then assembled: `change_order_upcharge_cents` is the sum of `upcharge_cents` over this project's `ACCEPTED` change orders whose `origin` is `CUSTOMER_REQUEST` or `SITE_CONDITION`, with `MEASUREMENT_VARIANCE` change orders excluded; `total_due_cents` is `final_labor_cents` plus `change_order_upcharge_cents` plus `permit_fee_cents_billed`, and is zero whenever `final_labor_cents` is still null. The deposit waterfall runs **once**, at the moment `final_labor_cents` is first written: the applied amount is the lesser of `deposit_held_cents` and `total_due_cents`, which is added to `deposit_applied_cents` and subtracted from `deposit_held_cents`; any surplus still held — which can only arise when the deposit exceeds the entire invoice — is moved into `deposit_refunded_cents`; and `deposit_held_cents` finishes at zero. `balance_due_cents` is then `total_due_cents` minus `deposit_applied_cents`, floored at zero. The project moves `IN_PROGRESS → INSTALL_COMPLETE` and its `install_completed_at` is stamped with the supplied `completed_at`. The money dimension moves `DEPOSIT_HELD → BALANCE_DUE` when `balance_due_cents` is above zero, or straight `DEPOSIT_HELD → SETTLED` when it is exactly zero. A system scorecard event is appended — `event_type` `ON_TIME_COMPLETION` when `on_time` is true and `LATE_COMPLETION` when it is false, with `occurred_at` set to the supplied `completed_at` — and the installer's composite score is recomputed on both variants. The installer's `completed_project_count` is recomputed, and its `committed_minutes` is recomputed but does not move, because `COMPLETED` assignments remain inside that sum. Every one of the three touched rows increments its `version`. To make the invoice concrete: a final labour charge of 253942 cents, one accepted customer-request change order of 10546 cents and a billed permit fee of 18500 cents give `total_due_cents` 282988; against a deposit of 59002 cents all still held, the waterfall applies the whole 59002, leaves `deposit_held_cents` and `deposit_refunded_cents` at zero, and gives `balance_due_cents` 223986, with the conservation identity reading 59002 equals 0 plus 59002 plus 0 plus 0.

**Giving a window back.** Release supplies `release_reason`, a string of 1 to 120 characters — a tighter bound than the 300 the programme's other reason and note fields carry — and is available from `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`. It sets `status` to `RELEASED`, records the reason, reduces the installer's `committed_minutes` by exactly `duration_minutes` — which is what keeps the capacity identity of §2.10 true before the hold, after the hold and after the release — and drives the project `SCHEDULED → MEASURED` so it can be rescheduled, unless the project is itself being cancelled in the same request. An ordinary release moves three `version` counters of its own: the assignment's, the installer's — whose `committed_minutes` always falls, so the row is always mutated — and, wherever the project was in fact driven `SCHEDULED → MEASURED`, the project's. The same operation carries one special path: when the acting role is `ServiceManager`, the assignment is `CONFIRMED`, the supplied `release_reason` is exactly `NO_SHOW`, and the logical clock is **strictly** past `window_end`, the assignment ends at `NO_SHOW` instead of `RELEASED`. That path additionally appends a system scorecard event of `event_type` `NO_SHOW` with `occurred_at` at the clock and recomputes the score, and it increments the assignment's, the project's and the installer's `version`. A `ProjectCoordinator` cannot record a no-show, and no-one can record one before the window has closed; either way the release degrades to an ordinary `RELEASED`.

**Settling the balance.** The household pays the remainder by supplying `amount_cents`, which must equal `balance_due_cents` **exactly** and which requires `balance_due_cents` to be greater than zero; any other figure, or a payment against a zero balance, is `ERR_BALANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Only the project's own customer may pay. That amount-and-balance test is the whole of the financial precondition: no separate `financial_status` gate guards this write. Because a project whose `financial_status` is anything other than `BALANCE_DUE` carries a `balance_due_cents` of zero, a payment attempted while the project is still `QUOTED` or `DEPOSIT_HELD`, is already `SETTLED`, or sits in one of the post-cancellation statuses `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED` — including on a project whose `fulfillment_status` is `CANCELLED`, which is not among the child writes guarded by rank 14 — is refused by that same rank-36 test with `ERR_BALANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, never with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `CLOSED` project is stopped earlier still, by the terminal guard of rank 13, and answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The payment moves `financial_status` `BALANCE_DUE → SETTLED` and drives `balance_due_cents` to zero. A balance payment leaves **every** deposit bucket untouched — it is not a deposit — so the conservation identity reads exactly as it did before the payment; in the worked figures above it still reads 59002 equals 0 plus 59002 plus 0 plus 0. Once `financial_status` is `SETTLED`, `balance_due_cents` stays at zero and no later recomputation of `total_due_cents` reopens it.

### 3.7. Quality, the Punch List, Closing and the Labour Warranty

**The retailer's own inspection.** After the crew reports the work finished, a coordinator records the retailer's quality inspection, supplying `quality_result`, `workmanship_score` (an integer from 0 to 100) and `defects`. It requires the project's `fulfillment_status` to be `INSTALL_COMPLETE` and its `quality_result` to still be `NONE`, so the inspection is recorded exactly once. Each entry of `defects` is an object with `severity` (`MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `SAFETY`), `description` (1 to 300 characters) and `due_at`, the instant by which the crew must put it right.

When `defects` is empty the supplied `quality_result` is recorded as given, `workmanship_score` is recorded, and the project stays at `INSTALL_COMPLETE` — a clean inspection recorded as `PASS` leaves the project ready to close. When `defects` is non-empty the recorded `quality_result` is **derived**, not taken from the request: `PASS_WITH_PUNCH` when `workmanship_score` is 70 or above and `FAIL` when it is below 70. One punch item is created per entry of `defects`, in the order supplied, each at `status` `OPEN` with `resolution_note`, `resolved_at`, `verified_at`, `escalated_at`, `escalation_reason` and `escalated_to_user_id` all null; one system scorecard event is appended per created item — `PUNCH_MINOR`, `PUNCH_MAJOR` or `PUNCH_SAFETY` matching that item's `severity`, with `occurred_at` at the logical clock — and the installer's score is recomputed **once**, after all the events have been appended, not once per event; and the project moves `INSTALL_COMPLETE → PUNCH_OPEN`. The created punch items appear in no response body: the response carries the project, and the items are found only through the project's punch-list listing or the cross-project listing, both filterable by `status` and `severity`.

**Working the punch list.** The crew reports a defect fixed by supplying `resolution_note` (1 to 300 characters). Only the firm that did the work may do so — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `installer_id` of this project's `COMPLETED` assignment. Resolution is available from `OPEN` or from `ESCALATED`, and it sets `status` to `RESOLVED`, records the note and stamps `resolved_at` with the clock; coming from `ESCALATED` it also decrements the project's `escalated_punch_count` by one, which is the only case in which the project's `version` moves. There is one hard exception: a `SAFETY` defect may **not** be resolved from `OPEN`. It must be escalated first, and only from `ESCALATED` may the crew report it fixed; attempting to resolve a `SAFETY` item straight from `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED`. Because rank 21 rather than the generic rank 12 governs this write, an item already `RESOLVED` answers that same `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED` and never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while a `VERIFIED` item is stopped earlier by rank 13 with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Sign-off takes no request body, requires `status` to be `RESOLVED` — an item still `OPEN` or still `ESCALATED` answering `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED`, again by rank 21 rather than rank 12 — and moves it to `VERIFIED` with `verified_at` stamped from the clock. A `MINOR` or `MAJOR` item may be signed off by a `ProjectCoordinator` or a `ServiceManager`, but a `SAFETY` item may be signed off **only** by a `ServiceManager`; a coordinator who tries is refused with `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED`. Sign-off moves no row but the punch item itself: the project is not touched and `escalated_punch_count` is not adjusted here at all, any decrement due having been applied by the resolution that preceded the sign-off. `VERIFIED` is the only terminal punch state.

**Escalation reaches all the way to the network.** A coordinator escalates a defect by supplying `escalation_reason` (1 to 300 characters), from either `OPEN` or `RESOLVED` — an item already `ESCALATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, escalation being none of the three writes rank 21 governs — and the consequences travel five hops. The item's `status` becomes `ESCALATED`, `escalated_at` is stamped from the clock, the reason is recorded, and `escalated_to_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` — an opaque operator handle that is never compared to any entity id. Caused by that, the project's `escalated_punch_count` increases by one and, when the item's `severity` is `SAFETY`, its `service_manager_review_required` becomes true. Caused by that, and **only** for a `SAFETY` item, a system scorecard event of `event_type` `PUNCH_SAFETY` is appended against the `installer_id` of this project's `COMPLETED` assignment, with `project_id` set to this project and `occurred_at` at the clock. Caused by that, the installer's score is recomputed, rewriting `quality_score`, `satisfaction_score`, `composite_score`, `lifetime_composite_score` and `removal_eligible`. And caused by that, when the recomputed `composite_score` is 78.00 or below and `rolling_event_count` is 3 or more, the firm moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` with `probation_started_at` stamped from the clock. The punch item's, the project's and the installer's `version` all increment. Escalating a `MINOR` or `MAJOR` item stops after the second hop: no scorecard event, no rescore, no probation. Because an item may be escalated again from `RESOLVED`, the cycle `OPEN → ESCALATED → RESOLVED → ESCALATED` may repeat, and `escalated_punch_count` rises and falls with it.

**Closing the project.** Closing takes no request body and is available from `INSTALL_COMPLETE` or `PUNCH_OPEN`. From `INSTALL_COMPLETE` it requires `quality_result` to be exactly `PASS`. From `PUNCH_OPEN` it requires **every** punch item on the project to have `status` `VERIFIED` — an item still `OPEN`, still `RESOLVED` but unverified, or still `ESCALATED` blocks the close with `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_NOT_CLEARED`. Both paths additionally carry the permit gate of rank 22, which bites only where the project's jurisdiction has `inspection_required` true **and** the project's `permit_responsibility` is something other than `NOT_REQUIRED`: there and only there must `permit_status` have reached `CLOSED`, on pain of `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED`. Where the jurisdiction demands no inspection, or where the determination concluded that no permit was needed at all, the permit dimension does not gate the close whatsoever, and a project may close with `permit_status` still standing at `PENDING_APPLICATION`, `APPLIED` or `ISSUED`. Both paths also require `financial_status` to be exactly `SETTLED`, on pain of `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE` — a check the handler runs before it ever consults the execution dimension, so a project that is both in the wrong `fulfillment_status` and unsettled answers `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, not `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Closing sets `fulfillment_status` to `CLOSED` and stamps `closed_at` with the logical clock. `CLOSED` is terminal on the execution dimension, and `closed_at` starts the labour warranty clock.

**The labour warranty.** The household files a claim against a closed project, supplying `project_id`, `claim_type` and `description` (1 to 500 characters). Three conditions apply: the project's `fulfillment_status` must be `CLOSED`; the `claim_type` must be `LABOR_DEFECT`, because the installation labour warranty covers workmanship and a `MATERIAL_DEFECT` claim belongs to the manufacturer; and the clock must be no later than the project's `closed_at` plus 365 whole days, an **inclusive** bound. A claim failing **any** of those three tests — the `CLOSED` test just as much as the type and date tests, rank 31 having taken this write out of the generic state rank — is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` and never with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The claim opens at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` stamped from the clock, `escalation_level` zero, and `triaged_at`, `decision_note`, `denial_reason` and `escalated_to_user_id` null. Claims are listable filtered by `project_id`, `status` and `claim_type`.

**Triage.** A service manager records the decision, supplying `decision` — `UPHOLD`, `DENY` or `ESCALATE` — and optionally `decision_note` (1 to 300 characters) or `denial_reason` (1 to 300 characters as well). `UPHOLD` is available from `SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`: it sets `status` to `UPHELD`, stamps `triaged_at`, records the note, appends a system scorecard event of `event_type` `WARRANTY_UPHELD` against the `installer_id` of the project's `COMPLETED` assignment with `occurred_at` at the clock, and recomputes the score, which may in turn drive the firm `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. `DENY` is likewise available from `SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`, **requires** `denial_reason` — a conditional requirement tested before the claim named in the path is resolved, so a `DENY` omitting it answers `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` even when no such claim is stored — sets `status` to `DENIED` and stamps `triaged_at` — and deliberately appends **no** scorecard event, because a denied claim is not evidence against the crew. `ESCALATE` is available only from `SUBMITTED`: it sets `status` to `ESCALATED`, increments `escalation_level` by one and records `escalated_to_user_id` as the acting opaque handle, leaving the claim open for a second look. It leaves `triaged_at` null and discards any `decision_note` sent alongside it, and because it is reachable only from `SUBMITTED`, `escalation_level` never rises above one: a second `ESCALATE` against an already-`ESCALATED` claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every triage increments the claim's `version`. `UPHELD` and `DENIED` are terminal, and a further triage of either is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.8. Cancellation, the Settlement Ladder and Reinstatement

**Who may cancel, and from where.** Cancellation supplies `cancel_reason` (1 to 300 characters, and always required) and is available to `ProgramAdmin`, `ProjectCoordinator`, `ServiceManager` and the project's own `Customer` — and a `Customer` caller must own the project. A project may be cancelled only from `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT`, `MEASURE_PENDING`, `MEASURED`, `CHANGE_PENDING`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; a project already `INSTALL_COMPLETE`, `PUNCH_OPEN` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, while a `CLOSED` one never reaches that test at all — the terminal guard of rank 13 stops it first, so it answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and it does so even for a `Customer` caller who does not own it, the ownership check of rank 29 sitting between the two. Once the work is finished, the invoice, not the cancellation ladder, is the instrument that settles it.

**How the settlement is computed.** The calculation splits `deposit_held_cents` into a retained part and a refunded part in eight steps. Step one picks a labour forfeit percentage keyed on the `fulfillment_status` the project held **immediately before** this cancellation: `DRAFT` and `MEASURE_PENDING` forfeit nothing at all; `MEASURED` and `CHANGE_PENDING` forfeit 0.10 of the held deposit; `SCHEDULED` forfeits 0.25; and `IN_PROGRESS` forfeits 0.50. Step two computes `labor_forfeit_cents` as `deposit_held_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up. Step three computes `restocking_fee_cents` keyed on the status the project's material delivery held **before** the return cascade fired — and where more than one delivery has been planned against the project, the single row consulted is the **most recently created** one, the others being ignored entirely, whatever their status or `material_cost_cents`: no delivery at all or a delivery still `PLANNED` costs nothing; `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELAYED` costs 0.06 of the delivery's `material_cost_cents`, rounded half up; `DELIVERED` costs 0.12 of it, rounded half up; and a delivery already `RETURNED` by some earlier cascade costs nothing. Step four adds the two together into `gross_retained_cents`.

Step five is the **waiver ladder**, and its order is part of the contract. The five tiers are evaluated strictly top to bottom, and the **first** tier whose condition holds decides the answer; **no later tier is consulted, ever**. Tier one, `RETAILER_FAULT`, holds when the acting role is `ProgramAdmin` or `ServiceManager` — the retailer cancelling its own sale keeps nothing — and retains zero. Tier two, `COOLING_OFF`, holds when the logical clock is within 72 hours of the project's `created_at`, an **inclusive** bound, and retains zero. Tier three, `PERMIT_BLOCKED`, holds when `permit_status` is `PENDING_APPLICATION` and `permit_responsibility` is `RETAILER` — the retailer's own unfiled paperwork is blocking the job — and retains only `restocking_fee_cents`, waiving the labour forfeit. Tier four, `GOODWILL`, holds when the project's customer has a `project_count` of 3 or more, and retains `gross_retained_cents` multiplied by 0.50, rounded half up. Tier five, `NONE`, always holds and retains `gross_retained_cents` in full. Step six clamps the result: `retained_cents` is the lesser of the tier's figure and `deposit_held_cents`, and `refund_cents` is `deposit_held_cents` minus `retained_cents`. Step seven moves the money: `retained_cents` is added to `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `refund_cents` is added to `deposit_refunded_cents`, and `deposit_held_cents` is left at zero, which keeps the conservation identity of §2.10 exactly true. Step eight sets the money dimension: `REFUNDED` when `retained_cents` is zero, `FORFEITED` when `refund_cents` is zero and `retained_cents` is above zero, and `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` otherwise.

Three worked cases pin the arithmetic down, all with `deposit_held_cents` 59002 and `material_cost_cents` 250000. A project cancelled by its customer while `IN_PROGRESS`, ten days after the sale, with the material `DELIVERED` and a customer whose `project_count` is 1: the labour forfeit is 59002 times 0.50, which is 29501.0 and rounds to 29501; the restocking fee is 250000 times 0.12, which is 30000; the gross is 59501; tiers one through four all fail, tier five retains 59501; the clamp reduces it to 59002, the refund is zero, and the dimension lands in `FORFEITED`. The same project cancelled while `SCHEDULED` with the material `IN_TRANSIT`: the labour forfeit is 59002 times 0.25, which is 14750.5 and rounds **up** to 14751; the restocking fee is 15000; the gross is 29751; tier five retains all of it; the refund is 29251; and the dimension lands in `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. Now the same `SCHEDULED` case, but cancelled 48 hours after creation by a customer whose `project_count` is 4, so that **both** tier two and tier four hold: the ladder stops at tier two, retains zero, refunds the whole 59002, and lands in `REFUNDED`. Consulting the goodwill tier instead would have retained 14876 and refunded 44126, landing in `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` — the wrong answer, produced purely by evaluating the ladder out of order.

**What else a cancellation drags with it.** Beyond the settlement, a cancellation sets `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, stamps `cancelled_at` from the clock, records `cancel_reason`, and then sweeps the project's children: every measurement still `SCHEDULED` becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `PROJECT_CANCELLED`; every change order still `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CUSTOMER` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`; every assignment still `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `PROJECT_CANCELLED`, each one reducing its installer's `committed_minutes` by that assignment's `duration_minutes` and moving that installer's `version` once, though no released assignment's project is reverted `SCHEDULED → MEASURED` on this path, this project being on its way to `CANCELLED` instead; and **every** material delivery on the project — not merely the most recently created one the restocking fee was computed from — becomes `RETURNED` unless it already was. The project's `version` increments. The ordering matters for the money: the settlement is computed from the pre-cancellation `fulfillment_status` and the pre-cascade delivery status, so a delivery that is `DELIVERED` when the customer calls attracts the 0.12 restocking fee even though it reads `RETURNED` by the end of the same request. The declining of a variance change order runs this identical cascade with `cancel_reason` recorded as `VARIANCE_DECLINED`, and with the acting role taken to be the deciding `Customer` — so tier one, `RETAILER_FAULT`, can never match on that path, no matter who else might have raised the order. One further edge is deliberate: a project cancelled at `financial_status` `QUOTED` with `deposit_paid_cents` zero runs the settlement anyway, retains zero against a zero balance, leaves all four buckets at zero, and lands in `REFUNDED` — precisely so the reinstatement path can be uniform. And while a project is `CANCELLED`, any attempt to write one of its children — booking a measurement, raising a change order, holding an assignment, planning a delivery or recording a quality inspection — is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_CANCELLED`.

**Looking before leaping.** A computed read returns what the settlement *would* produce if the project were cancelled at this instant by the calling role, without moving a single bucket or changing a single status. It returns nine values: `deposit_held_cents`, `labor_forfeit_pct`, `labor_forfeit_cents`, `restocking_fee_cents`, `gross_retained_cents`, `matched_waiver_tier` (the name of the tier that decided it — `RETAILER_FAULT`, `COOLING_OFF`, `PERMIT_BLOCKED`, `GOODWILL` or `NONE`), `retained_cents`, `refund_cents` and `resulting_financial_status`. Because tier one keys on the acting role, the same project previewed by a `ServiceManager` and by its `Customer` legitimately returns two different answers. The preview imposes **no** precondition of its own: it is not gated on the execution dimension, so it answers for a project that could not in fact be cancelled — one already `INSTALL_COMPLETE`, `PUNCH_OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` — rather than raising `ERR_NOT_CANCELLABLE`; nor is it one of the writes rank 29 guards, so a `Customer` caller may preview any project on file, not only its own household's. Outside the six cancellable statuses `labor_forfeit_pct` simply falls back to 0.00 and `labor_forfeit_cents` to zero, while `restocking_fee_cents` and hence `gross_retained_cents` are still computed from the delivery exactly as usual and may well be non-zero; since every one of those four statuses has already emptied `deposit_held_cents`, the step-six clamp then drives `retained_cents` and `refund_cents` both to zero and `resulting_financial_status` to `REFUNDED`, whichever tier `matched_waiver_tier` reports. `labor_forfeit_pct` is reported as a two-decimal figure.

**Undoing a cancellation.** Reinstatement is available to `ProgramAdmin` and `ProjectCoordinator` and supplies `expected_project_version` together with an optional `reinstate_note` of up to 300 characters — a field validated on the way in and then discarded, the project carrying nothing to hold it. Three conditions apply: the project's `fulfillment_status` must be `CANCELLED`; the supplied version must match the project's current `version`, or the answer is `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`; and the logical clock must be **strictly** less than 72 hours after `cancelled_at`, or the answer is `ERR_REINSTATE_WINDOW_CLOSED` — exactly 72 hours is too late. The project's `financial_status` must be one of `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED`, which after a cancellation it always is. Reinstatement moves the whole of `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` back into `deposit_held_cents`, zeroes both, leaves `deposit_paid_cents` untouched, and sets `financial_status` to `DEPOSIT_HELD`, restoring the pre-cancellation bucket split exactly and with no drift — which is why the conservation identity holds before the cancellation, after the cancellation and after the reinstatement alike. It sets `fulfillment_status` to `MEASURED`, or to `MEASURE_PENDING` when no `COMPLETED` measurement survives on the project, and clears `cancelled_at` and `cancel_reason` back to null.

Reinstatement restores money and schedulability; it does not restore history. A `VOIDED` measurement is **not** un-voided, a `SUPERSEDED` change order is **not** un-superseded, and a `RETURNED` delivery is **not** un-returned. Those cascades are one-way, and the work they represent must be redone: a fresh appointment booked, a fresh change order raised, a fresh delivery planned.

### 3.9. The Scorecard, Probation and Removal

**The ledger.** Every judgement the programme forms about an installer is one immutable ledger row. A scorecard event carries `installer_id`, an optional `project_id` (null for network-level observations), an `event_type` from `ON_TIME_COMPLETION`, `LATE_COMPLETION`, `NO_SHOW`, `PUNCH_MINOR`, `PUNCH_MAJOR`, `PUNCH_SAFETY`, `WARRANTY_UPHELD` and `CSAT_SURVEY`, an optional `csat_score` from 1 to 5, the three frozen deltas `quality_delta`, `timeliness_delta` and `satisfaction_delta`, the `occurred_at` instant from which every window and decay band is measured, and a `source` of `MANUAL` or `SYSTEM`. As noted in §2.4 these rows carry only `id` and `created_at` — no `updated_at`, no `version` — and there is no operation anywhere that updates or deletes one. Operators file them with `event_type` and `occurred_at`, plus `project_id` and `csat_score` where relevant; `csat_score` is **required** when `event_type` is `CSAT_SURVEY` and **forbidden** otherwise, and either violation is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE`. That cross-field test belongs to the rank-4 pass, so it is answered before the installer named in the path is resolved: a filing that both names an unknown installer and gets `csat_score` wrong answers `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE`, not `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_FOUND`. An event may be filed against a firm in any network status — `APPLIED`, `VERIFIED` and `SUSPENDED` firms included — save one already `REMOVED`, which answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; and the optional `project_id` need only name a stored project, not one this particular firm ever worked. Operator-filed events get `source` `MANUAL`; the events appended by assignment completion, no-show release, punch escalation, quality inspection and warranty upholding get `source` `SYSTEM`. Events are listable per installer and per project, filterable by `event_type` and, per installer, by `source`, always ordered by `occurred_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

**The three deltas.** Each `event_type` fixes its contribution to the three dimensions once and for all. `ON_TIME_COMPLETION` contributes quality +2.00, timeliness +6.00, satisfaction +1.00. `LATE_COMPLETION` contributes 0.00, -8.00, -3.00. `NO_SHOW` contributes -4.00, -20.00, -10.00. `PUNCH_MINOR` contributes -3.00, 0.00, -1.00. `PUNCH_MAJOR` contributes -8.00, -2.00, -4.00. `PUNCH_SAFETY` contributes -15.00, -3.00, -8.00. `WARRANTY_UPHELD` contributes -10.00, 0.00, -6.00. `CSAT_SURVEY` contributes 0.00 to quality and 0.00 to timeliness, and to satisfaction the quantity `csat_score` minus 3, multiplied by a step of 5.00 — so a rating of 1 gives -10.00, 2 gives -5.00, 3 gives exactly 0.00, 4 gives +5.00 and 5 gives +10.00. These three numbers are written onto the ledger row when it is created and are **never** rewritten afterwards, even if the tariff itself were to change.

**Two scores over the same ledger.** The rolling variant considers only the events whose `occurred_at` is at or after the logical clock minus 90 days, an **inclusive** lower bound, so an event exactly 90 days old is still inside the window; every event inside it counts at full weight 1.00 and every event outside it counts for nothing at all. The lifetime variant considers **every** event ever recorded, with no cut-off, attenuated by age: an age of 90 whole days or fewer weighs 1.00, more than 90 and up to 270 weighs 0.60, more than 270 and up to 540 weighs 0.30, and beyond 540 weighs 0.10, the age being the whole days between `occurred_at` and the clock, floored.

Each variant computes its three dimension scores in a pinned order: multiply every event's delta by that event's weight, sum the products exactly, add the base score of 85.00, and only **then** round half up to two decimal places, finally clamping the result into the range 0.00 to 100.00. The composite is likewise pinned: it uses the **already-rounded and already-clamped** dimension scores, multiplying quality by 0.45, timeliness by 0.30 and satisfaction by 0.25, summing, and rounding half up once to two decimals — so where a dimension has been railed at 0.00 or at 100.00 it is the railed figure, not the unclamped sum behind it, that the weighting consumes. Rounding earlier or later than these two points yields different figures and is wrong. The rolling variant persists `quality_score`, `timeliness_score`, `satisfaction_score`, `composite_score`, `rolling_event_count` and `rolling_no_show_count`, the last two being counts of the events **inside the 90-day window only** and never of the whole ledger; the lifetime variant persists `lifetime_composite_score` and nothing else, its own three dimension scores being computed, clamped and then discarded.

A single example shows how far the two answers can diverge. With the clock at the programme default of `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, the rolling window opens inclusively at `2026-05-03T00:00:00.000Z`. Suppose six events: an `ON_TIME_COMPLETION` 31 days old, a `LATE_COMPLETION` 47 days old, a `PUNCH_MAJOR` exactly 90 days old, a `CSAT_SURVEY` with `csat_score` 2 that is 91 days old, a `WARRANTY_UPHELD` 198 days old and a `NO_SHOW` 509 days old. The rolling variant sees only the first three: quality is 85.00 plus 2.00 plus 0.00 minus 8.00, which is 79.00; timeliness is 85.00 plus 6.00 minus 8.00 minus 2.00, which is 81.00; satisfaction is 85.00 plus 1.00 minus 3.00 minus 4.00, which is 79.00; and the composite is 35.55 plus 24.30 plus 19.75, which is 79.60, with `rolling_event_count` 3 and `rolling_no_show_count` 0. The lifetime variant sees all six, weighting the 91-day and 198-day events at 0.60 and the 509-day event at 0.30: quality becomes 71.80, timeliness 75.00, satisfaction 69.40, and the composite 32.31 plus 22.50 plus 17.35, which is 72.16. The same six rows therefore yield 79.60 on the governing score and 72.16 on the reporting score, and the firm stays `ACTIVE` because it is the **rolling** figure that is compared with the threshold.

**Thresholds, probation and eligibility.** Every threshold is evaluated against the rolling composite; `lifetime_composite_score` gates nothing, ever. `removal_eligible` becomes true when the rolling `composite_score` is 70.00 or below **and** `rolling_event_count` is 3 or more, **or** when `rolling_no_show_count` is 3 or more, **or** when the number of `PUNCH_SAFETY` events inside the rolling window is 2 or more — three independent routes, any one of which suffices. Separately, a firm at `ACTIVE` is moved to `PROBATION`, with `probation_started_at` stamped from the clock, when its rolling `composite_score` is 78.00 or below and `rolling_event_count` is 3 or more; and a firm at `PROBATION` returns to `ACTIVE`, with `probation_started_at` cleared to null, as soon as its rolling `composite_score` rises **strictly** above 78.00. That recovery needs no operator action at all: the cycle `ACTIVE ↔ PROBATION` is driven entirely by the arithmetic, and a firm may pass around it any number of times. The minimum-event floor of 3 protects a new firm from being condemned by one bad day, but note that it guards only the composite routes: three no-shows or two safety escalations make a firm removal-eligible regardless of how few events it has.

Both variants are recomputed, and all of these flags re-evaluated, at the end of **every** request that appends an event — an operator filing, an assignment completion, a no-show release, a punch escalation, a quality inspection or an upheld warranty claim — and also inside the clock-setting operation, because moving the clock moves both the rolling window and the decay bands underneath a ledger that has not itself changed. That is why setting the clock reports `installers_rescored_count`.

**Reading the scorecard.** A computed read returns both variants side by side so that no caller has to infer either: `rolling_quality_score`, `rolling_timeliness_score`, `rolling_satisfaction_score`, `rolling_composite_score`, `rolling_event_count`, `rolling_no_show_count`, `lifetime_quality_score`, `lifetime_timeliness_score`, `lifetime_satisfaction_score`, `lifetime_composite_score`, `lifetime_event_count`, `removal_eligible` and `probation_active`. The last is true exactly when the firm's `status` is `PROBATION`. Note that the lifetime dimension scores and `lifetime_event_count` are available **only** through this read; the installer row itself persists only the lifetime composite.

**Removal.** Only a `ServiceManager` may remove a firm, supplying `removal_reason` (1 to 300 characters) and `expected_installer_version`. The conditions apply in the order of the ladder of §2.8: the supplied version must match the firm's current `version`, or the answer is `ERR_STALE_INSTALLER_VERSION`; a firm already `REMOVED` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; and rank 33 then takes the remaining three together — the firm's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, its `removal_eligible` must be true, and it must hold no assignment whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` — any one of the three failing answering `ERR_REMOVAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Removal is a judgement the arithmetic has to license — a service manager cannot remove a firm whose record does not yet justify it. A successful removal sets `status` to `REMOVED`, stamps `removed_at` from the clock and records `removal_reason`; releases **every** assignment of that firm still `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` with `release_reason` `INSTALLER_REMOVED`; reduces the firm's `committed_minutes` by each released assignment's `duration_minutes`; reverts each affected project from `SCHEDULED` back to `MEASURED` so it can be given to somebody else; and increments the firm's `version`. `REMOVED` is terminal: there is no reinstatement path for an installer, and every later act on the firm's own state machine — filing another licence, filing a background check, admitting it, removing it again, filing a scorecard event — is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Three things are deliberately not refused: the firm and its licences, events and scorecard stay fully readable; an amendment of its `contact_email`, `liability_coverage_cents` or `insurance_expires_on` still succeeds and still bumps its `version`; and a verification decision may still be recorded against one of its licences, which recomputes its `verified_credential_count` but can never restore its network status. Offering a removed firm a window is not a terminal-state answer either — it is `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ASSIGNABLE` at rank 16.
