# Pressure Vessel Fabrication API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A code welding fabrication shop lives or dies by its paperwork. The vessels and heat exchangers it builds are pressure-retaining equipment, and the shop's licence to stamp them with a code symbol rests on a Certificate of Authorization that a third-party inspection agency can suspend on the strength of a single quality-system audit. Between the purchase order and the nameplate sit half a dozen disciplines that must each leave an auditable trail: a design engineer sizes the wall from the boiler and pressure vessel code and has that arithmetic independently reviewed; a receiving inspector matches mill test reports to heat numbers and refuses material that misses its certified strength; a welding engineer owns the procedure specifications, the welders' performance qualifications and the joint-by-joint weld map; a quality-control inspector plans radiography, ultrasonics and surface examinations by joint category and interprets the film; furnace charges are soaked and charted; and an Authorized Inspector witnesses the hydrostatic or pneumatic test and signs the manufacturer's data report before anyone is allowed to touch a nameplate stamp.

Today that trail is a filing cabinet. The people who most need to know whether a vessel is stampable — the quality manager, the shop's Authorized Inspector, the customer — cannot answer the question without walking the shop floor. This API replaces the cabinet with a single system of record whose derived fields answer the question continuously: what fraction of the weld map is truly finished across all three of its dimensions, which data-package sections are still short, whether any nonconformance is still open, and whether the governing certificate still confers stamping authority at this instant on the shop's logical clock.

The design goal is that no operation can quietly succeed out of order. Every gate that a code inspector would apply on paper is a server-side precondition here, every computed value is recomputed inside the request that changes any of its inputs, and every step that a code requires two people to perform is refused when one person tries to perform both halves. The system is deliberately unforgiving: it is easier to explain a rejected request than to explain an unstamped vessel.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts and cross-cutting rules that hold for every endpoint in the system. Collections are mounted as flat, peer top-level paths — `/vessel-jobs`, `/design-calculations`, `/material-heats`, `/parts`, `/wps`, `/welder-qualifications`, `/production-welds`, `/nde-exams`, `/nonconformances`, `/pwht-batches`, `/pressure-tests`, `/data-reports`, `/certificates`, `/shop-audits` and `/personnel` — with no shared prefix segment.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the person or operator acting, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity they act in. Five roles exist and their spelling is exact: `ShopAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `WeldingEngineer`, `QCInspector` and `AuthorizedInspector`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of those five strings, or whose `X-User-ID` is absent or blank where the role is backed by a person record, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A request whose declared role is well formed but does not appear in the permitted-role list for the endpoint it targets is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403); §4 lists those permissions endpoint by endpoint.

`ShopAdmin` is the quality manager and shop administration capacity: it maintains the Certificate of Authorization, schedules and closes quality-system audits, opens and cancels jobs, registers people, applies the code nameplate and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `ShopAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may accept and log it, but it never resolves it against a `Personnel` row and never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The other four roles are person-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `DesignEngineer`, `WeldingEngineer`, `QCInspector` or `AuthorizedInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Personnel` row, and ownership and segregation checks on any record that references a person compare that record's person-reference field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a calculation sheet's `prepared_by_personnel_id` and `reviewed_by_personnel_id`, a furnace charge's `scheduled_by_personnel_id` and `chart_reviewed_by_personnel_id`, an examination's `examiner_personnel_id`, a nonconformance's `ai_concurrence_personnel_id`, a pressure test's `witnessed_by_personnel_id`, and a data report's `assembled_by_personnel_id` and `ai_personnel_id` are all populated from, and compared against, the acting `X-User-ID`.

For those four person-backed roles the `X-User-ID` must resolve to an existing `Personnel` row whose `role_code` matches the declared `X-User-Role` — `DESIGN_ENGINEER` for `DesignEngineer`, `WELDING_ENGINEER` for `WeldingEngineer`, `QC_INSPECTOR` for `QCInspector` and `AUTHORIZED_INSPECTOR` for `AuthorizedInspector`. A mismatch, or an `X-User-ID` that names no person at all, is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403). This identity resolution is applied on every endpoint except the two that necessarily precede or stand outside the person registry: registering a person at `POST /personnel` and moving the clock at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, both of which are `ShopAdmin`-only anyway.

Segregation of duties is a first-class principle rather than a convention. Four operations require the acting person to differ from the person who performed the paired earlier step: approving a calculation sheet, rejecting a calculation sheet, completing a furnace charge against its chart, and signing a manufacturer's data report. In all four, an attempt by the same person is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Every `GET` endpoint in the system is readable by all five roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when the caller omits it, it defaults to 10, and an explicitly supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; a supplied value must be zero or greater. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` counts the records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` states whether any matching record remains beyond the returned window.

Every list endpoint also accepts the domain filters named in §3 alongside `limit` and `offset`; filters combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Version Counters

Every record the API mints carries an `id`: a server-generated UUID, with no type prefix and no shop-specific formatting. Callers never supply an `id`. Alongside it, records carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never rewritten.

Most records additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, rewritten on every change to the record — including changes that only touch a derived field. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, whether the mutation was requested directly by a client or applied by the server as a cascade from somewhere else. Certificates, shop audits, vessel jobs, design calculation sheets, material heats, parts, procedure specifications, welder qualifications, production welds, examinations, nonconformances, furnace charges, pressure tests and data reports all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

`Personnel` is the single exception. A person record is immutable once registered, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and no endpoint mutates it.

### 2.5. The Logical Shop Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All expiry, continuity, scheduling-window and timestamp behaviour is measured against a server-held **logical UTC clock**. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Wherever this document says "the current logical UTC date", it means the calendar date of that clock reading, taken as a UTC date; "the current logical UTC instant" means the reading itself.

A `ShopAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which answers 200 and returns a body containing `current_utc`, `welder_qualifications_recomputed` and `certificates_recomputed` rather than a domain resource. Moving the clock is not a passive act. It re-evaluates the continuity posture of every welder qualification, which may move rows across the inactivity boundary in either direction, and it re-evaluates the posture of every Certificate of Authorization, which may push a certificate past its `expires_on` into `EXPIRED`. The two counts in the response body report how many welder qualification rows and how many certificate rows actually changed. Any certificate that loses stamping authority as a result cascades onto the data reports built under it, as §2.10 and §3.9 describe.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Every rounding step in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every rounding step is applied at the scale the owning rule declares, never at a looser scale "for display".

- Joint efficiency is carried at 2 decimal places.
- Wall thicknesses, corrosion allowances and procedure thickness limits are carried at 4 decimal places: `thickness_nominal_in`, `corrosion_allowance_in`, `thickness_circumferential_in`, `thickness_longitudinal_in`, `thickness_required_in`, `thickness_in`, `thickness_min_in`, `thickness_max_in` and `thickness_range_max_in`.
- Examination coverage is an integer percentage, and the thickness comparisons that select coverage and method are made at 3 decimal places.
- Test pressure is carried at 1 decimal place, as is the observed peak pressure. Hold times and medium temperatures are whole numbers of minutes and whole degrees Fahrenheit.
- Soak time is a whole number of minutes; the computed soak demand is rounded to whole minutes before the minimum-soak floor is applied.
- Pounds of material — received, issued and remaining — are carried at 2 decimal places, as are the data-package section points, the total completeness score, the weld completion percentage and the examined coverage percentage.
- Finding counts, renewal counts, repair generations, weld counts and day counts are integers.
- `stress_ratio` is supplied at 2 decimal places.

Values that a caller supplies at a finer scale than the field declares are a shape violation and draw `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than being silently truncated.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Protection on Job and Certificate Writes

Three write operations can silently destroy work a client never saw, and each is therefore guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency field naming the client's last observed `version` of the **parent** record. The field is required, not optional; omitting it is a shape violation.

Preparing a design calculation sheet — `POST /vessel-jobs/{job_id}/design-calculations` — must carry `expected_job_version` matching the current `version` of the `VesselJob` named in the path. A sheet created for a component that already carries one legitimately supersedes the prior sheet and pushes the job back to `DRAFT`, so a writer holding a stale job version would supersede engineering work it has never observed. When `expected_job_version` differs from the job's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JOB_VERSION` (412) and nothing is written.

Assembling a manufacturer's data report — `POST /vessel-jobs/{job_id}/data-report` — must likewise carry `expected_job_version` matching the job's current `version`, because a second assembly voids the first. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JOB_VERSION` (412).

Renewing a certificate — `POST /certificates/{certificate_id}/renewals` — must carry `expected_certificate_version` matching the current `version` of the `CertificateOfAuthorization` named in the path. A renewal rewrites the expiry date and clears the accumulated finding counters, so a writer holding a stale certificate version would renew on the strength of an audit posture that no longer holds. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION` (412).

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a stale-version check.

### 2.8. Validation Order: How a Rejected Request Is Diagnosed

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run strictly in the ascending order below; the **first** violated check produces the response and no later check runs. This ordering is part of the contract, because it decides which of two true diagnoses the caller is told.

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and well formed — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Identity resolution.** `X-User-ID` resolves to a `Personnel` row whose `role_code` matches the declared role, on every endpoint except `POST /personnel` and `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403).
3. **Role permission.** The declared role appears in the endpoint's permitted-role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. **Request shape.** The body parses as JSON, every required field is present and non-null, every field carries its declared type, enum fields carry a declared value, decimal fields respect their declared scale, instant fields are ISO 8601 UTC instants and date fields are ISO 8601 calendar dates, quantities declared strictly positive are strictly positive, `limit` sits in the range 1 to 100 and `offset` is zero or greater, and `weld_ids` is an array whose entries are distinct. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Note that an **empty** `weld_ids` array passes this gate and is caught much later at step 23 by `ERR_EMPTY_WELD_SET`.
5. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves to a live row, validated left to right (see §2.9) — otherwise the matching 404: `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CALCULATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WPS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NONCONFORMANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Body-reference existence.** Every identifier carried in the request body resolves to a live row, validated in the field order the endpoint declares — `certificate_id`, then `heat_id`, then `wps_id`, then `welder_qualification_id`, then `part_a_id`, then `part_b_id`, then `repair_of_weld_id`, then the entries of `weld_ids` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WPS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WELD_NOT_FOUND` (404). This step guards job creation, part issue, welder-qualification creation, weld-map entry creation, weld completion, furnace-charge scheduling and repair-joint creation.
7. **Stale version.** The optimistic-concurrency field matches the parent's current `version` on the three endpoints of §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_JOB_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION` (412).
8. **Target state.** The record named in the path is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the matching 409: `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CALCULATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_HEAT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PART_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WPS_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WELD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXAM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_NONCONFORMANCE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`.
9. **Related-record state.** The parent job — and, where relevant, the parent furnace charge or nonconformance — is in the state the operation requires, drawing `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_NONCONFORMANCE_STATE` (409). The required parent job state, gathered in one place: calculation submit needs the job in `DRAFT` or `DESIGN_REVIEW`; calculation revise needs `DRAFT`; part issue and part scrap need `FAB_PLANNING`, `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`; weld-map entry creation needs `FAB_PLANNING` or `FABRICATION`; weld completion needs `FABRICATION`, or `NDE_HOLD` when the joint is a repair joint whose covering nonconformance is dispositioned `REPAIR`; running the examination plan needs `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`; scheduling, starting and completing a furnace charge each need `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`; scheduling a pressure test needs `FABRICATION` or `TESTING`; recording a test result needs `TESTING`; dispositioning a nonconformance and creating its repair joint each need `NDE_HOLD`; and assembling a data report needs only that the job is neither `STAMPED` nor `CANCELLED`, so a partial package may be assembled and scored at any point during fabrication. Beyond the job, a chart review requires its furnace charge to be `IN_PROGRESS`, and a repair joint requires its nonconformance to be dispositioned `REPAIR`.
10. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409) on calculation approve, calculation reject, furnace-charge complete and data-report signature.
11. **Certificate expiry.** The governing certificate has not passed `expires_on` relative to the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` (409). This guards job creation, pressure-test witness, data-report signature and nameplate stamping. Expiry is deliberately checked **before** stamping authority, because an expired certificate also reads `stamping_authorized` as false and the expiry diagnosis is the more specific of the two.
12. **Stamping authority.** The governing certificate still confers stamping authority — otherwise `ERR_STAMPING_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (403). This guards pressure-test witness, data-report signature and nameplate stamping, and it is reachable independently of the previous step whenever the certificate is unexpired but sits on `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`.
13. **Welding qualification adequacy.** Guards weld-map entry creation, weld completion and repair-joint creation, drawing `ERR_WPS_NOT_QUALIFIED`, `ERR_THICKNESS_OUTSIDE_WPS_RANGE`, `ERR_WELDER_NOT_QUALIFIED`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WPS_MISMATCH` or `ERR_THICKNESS_OUTSIDE_QUALIFICATION` (409).
14. **Material acceptance.** Guards receiving inspection and part issue, drawing `ERR_MTR_BELOW_SPEC_MINIMUM`, `ERR_HEAT_NOT_ACCEPTED` or `ERR_MATERIAL_FORM_MISMATCH` (409).
15. **Design adequacy.** Guards calculation submit: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALL_THICKNESS` (409).
16. **Weld-map integrity at release.** Guards the weld-map release, drawing `ERR_EMPTY_WELD_MAP`, `ERR_PART_SCRAPPED` or `ERR_JOINT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (409).
17. **Examination adequacy.** Guards the weld-map release and pressure-test scheduling: `ERR_NDE_INCOMPLETE` (409).
18. **Heat-treatment adequacy.** Guards furnace-charge completion and pressure-test scheduling, drawing `ERR_PWHT_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_SOAK_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_CHART_RECORD_MISSING` (409).
19. **Test adequacy.** Guards recording a pressure-test result, drawing `ERR_AI_WITNESS_REQUIRED`, `ERR_TEST_PRESSURE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_HOLD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_MEDIUM_TEMP_TOO_LOW` (409).
20. **Open-nonconformance and concurrence gating.** Guards pressure-test scheduling, pressure-test result, data-report assembly, data-report submission to the Authorized Inspector, nonconformance disposition and nonconformance close, drawing `ERR_OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE` or `ERR_AI_CONCURRENCE_REQUIRED` (409).
21. **Aggregate membership.** Referenced rows belong to the aggregate that owns them, drawing `ERR_WELD_NOT_IN_JOB`, `ERR_PART_NOT_IN_JOB` or `ERR_TRACEABILITY_INCOMPLETE` (409). This guards receiving inspection, weld-map entry creation, furnace-charge scheduling, repair-joint creation and data-report assembly.
22. **Scoped uniqueness.** Guards every create endpoint, drawing `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STAMP_INITIALS`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_JOB_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_HEAT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WPS_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WELDER_QUALIFICATION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WELD_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHART_RECORD_NUMBER` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAMEPLATE_SERIAL` (409).
23. **Domain limits and applicability.** Guards nonconformance disposition, repair-joint creation, furnace-charge scheduling, pressure-test scheduling, data-report submission to the Authorized Inspector and certificate renewal, drawing `ERR_REPAIR_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_EMPTY_WELD_SET`, `ERR_TEST_SIDE_NOT_APPLICABLE`, `ERR_PNEUMATIC_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DATA_PACKAGE_INCOMPLETE` or `ERR_RENEWAL_AUDIT_NOT_SATISFACTORY` (409).
24. **Material mass ledger.** Guards part issue: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_HEAT_QUANTITY` (409).
25. **Resource scheduling.** Guards furnace-charge scheduling and pressure-test scheduling, drawing `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` or `ERR_TEST_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). This is the last gate in the ladder, so a request that is both out of state and in conflict with a booked window always reports the state problem.

Step 22's uniqueness scopes are worth restating because they are not all global: `cert_number`, `job_number`, `wps_number`, `chart_record_number` and `nameplate_serial` are unique across the whole system; `stamp_initials` is unique across `Personnel`; `heat_number` is unique per material specification, so the same mill heat number may legitimately appear twice under two different specifications; `weld_number` is unique within a job; and a welder qualification is unique on the combination of `welder_stamp` with `wps_id`, so one welder may hold qualifications against several procedures.

### 2.9. Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Several endpoints address a record nested beneath a parent, and one — the data-report assembly and the design-calculation preparation — reaches a child collection through its parent's identifier. Wherever a path carries more than one identifier, the server resolves them strictly left to right and reports the **first** identifier that names nothing, with the 404 belonging to that segment's resource type. A weld-map entry requested under a job that does not exist reports `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND` and never looks for the weld.

The path identifier names used across the API are `personnel_id`, `certificate_id`, `audit_id`, `job_id`, `calculation_id`, `heat_id`, `part_id`, `wps_id`, `qualification_id`, `weld_id`, `exam_id`, `nonconformance_id`, `batch_id`, `test_id` and `report_id`. Existence resolution always precedes state checks, so acting on a live child of a cancelled parent reports the parent's state, while acting on a non-existent child of a cancelled parent reports the child's absence only after the parent has resolved.

### 2.10. Derived Fields, Cascades and Immediate Consistency

No derived value in this system is a cached snapshot. Every computed field is recomputed inside the request that changes any of its inputs, and re-evaluated whenever a read returns the record that owns it. A certificate read reports the posture the logical clock implies right now; a welder qualification read reports the continuity gap the logical clock implies right now; a data report read reports the section scores and outstanding items the job's current children imply right now.

Cascades run in the same request as the operation that triggers them, and each record a cascade touches is itself mutated: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1. A single examination rejection therefore mutates the examination, the joint, the job and every data report of that job — four or more version increments from one call. Callers holding a job `version` for optimistic-concurrency purposes must expect it to advance whenever any of the job's roll-ups move.

Five cascade chains recur throughout §3 and are worth naming once here. First, closing a quality-system audit pushes its findings up into the parent certificate, which re-derives its posture. Second, an examination rejection drives the joint's examination dimension to `REJECTED` and opens a nonconformance. Third, a nonconformance opening or a certificate losing stamping authority pushes every `AI_SIGNED` data report of the affected job into `STAMP_HOLD`. Fourth, the job's open-nonconformance count drives the job between `FABRICATION` and `NDE_HOLD`, and every data report of the job re-derives its outstanding items when that count moves. Fifth, cancelling a job unwinds every child record it owns, including returning issued pounds to their heats.

Compensating transitions exist wherever an accounting entry can be reversed: a depleted heat becomes issuable again when a part is scrapped, a suspended or expired certificate becomes active again on renewal, a held data report returns to signed when the hold's cause clears, and a job on examination hold returns to fabrication when its last nonconformance closes.

### 2.11. Furnace and Test-Bay Scheduling

Two shop resources are booked by time window, and each has its own conflict geometry.

The **post-weld heat-treatment furnaces** — `FURNACE_A` and `FURNACE_B` — are treated as closed intervals surrounded by a three-hour cooldown buffer of 180 minutes on each side. A requested window conflicts with an existing charge in the **same** `furnace_code` when the requested `scheduled_start` falls earlier than 180 minutes after that charge's `scheduled_end`, and simultaneously the requested `scheduled_end` falls later than 180 minutes before that charge's `scheduled_start`. Only charges that are `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` are considered; `COMPLETE` and `ABORTED` charges never conflict, and a charge in the other furnace never conflicts. The boundary is exact: a window beginning precisely 180 minutes after an existing charge ends is admitted, while one beginning 179 minutes after it ends is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The **pressure-test bays** — `BAY_1` and `BAY_2` — are treated as half-open intervals with no buffer. Two windows overlap when the new `scheduled_start` is earlier than the existing `scheduled_end` and the new `scheduled_end` is later than the existing `scheduled_start`; a window that begins exactly when another ends therefore does not overlap. Only tests whose `result` is still `PENDING` occupy a bay. Within a single `test_bay_code` the shop tolerates two concurrent hydrostatic tests but no concurrent pneumatic test at all: a new `HYDROSTATIC` test is admitted only when it overlaps at most one existing pending `HYDROSTATIC` test in that bay, and a new `PNEUMATIC` test is admitted only when it overlaps nothing whatsoever in that bay — neither another pneumatic test nor any hydrostatic one. A pneumatic test already holding a bay likewise blocks any new test overlapping it. Violations are refused with `ERR_TEST_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

In both cases `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start`; an inverted or zero-length window is a shape violation.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Code Stamp Authority: The Certificate, Its Audits and Its Renewal

Everything downstream hangs from a **Certificate of Authorization**. A `ShopAdmin` records one by supplying the `cert_number` printed on the paper certificate — non-blank, and unique across the whole system, so a second attempt at the same number is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERT_NUMBER` — together with `scope_codes`, `issued_on` and `expires_on`. `scope_codes` is the list of code stamp scope symbols the shop may apply; it must be a non-empty array whose members are drawn from `U`, `U2`, `S` and `R` with no repeats. `issued_on` and `expires_on` are ISO 8601 calendar dates, and `expires_on` — which is the *inclusive* last valid day — must fall strictly after `issued_on`.

A fresh certificate opens at `status` `ACTIVE` with `open_major_findings_count` and `open_minor_findings_count` at 0, `renewal_count` at 0, `last_audit_closed_on` null and `stamping_authorized` true. Those four fields make up the shop's licence posture, and the posture is a pure function of them rather than something an operator sets. `status` is drawn from `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED`, and it is resolved in this priority: the certificate is `EXPIRED` whenever `expires_on` falls before the current logical UTC date; otherwise it is `SUSPENDED` whenever `open_major_findings_count` has reached 3 or more; otherwise it is `PROBATION` whenever `open_major_findings_count` is 1 or more or `open_minor_findings_count` is more than 5; otherwise it is `ACTIVE`. Expiry outranks findings, so an expired certificate reads `EXPIRED` however clean its audit record. `stamping_authorized` is true only when `status` is `ACTIVE`, `expires_on` is on or after the current logical UTC date, and `open_major_findings_count` is exactly 0 — it is the single flag every witness, signature and nameplate attempt consults. Certificates can be listed and filtered by `status` and `cert_number`, and a single-certificate read is how a client obtains the `version` it will later need for a renewal.

The finding counters are **running totals, not snapshots of the latest audit**. A `ShopAdmin` schedules a quality-system audit against a certificate by supplying that certificate's `certificate_id`, an `audit_type` of `INTERNAL_QMS`, `JOINT_REVIEW` or `TRIENNIAL_RENEWAL`, and a `scheduled_date`; the audit opens at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `closed_on` null, `findings` an empty array, `major_finding_count` and `minor_finding_count` at 0 and `satisfactory` false. Audits are listed with filters on `certificate_id`, `status` and `audit_type`.

Closing an audit moves it `SCHEDULED → CLOSED` and is the shop's only mechanism for recording findings. The closing `ShopAdmin` supplies `closed_on` and a `findings` array whose members each carry a `finding_code`, a `severity` of `MAJOR`, `MINOR` or `OBSERVATION`, and a `clause_reference`; an empty array means a clean audit. `closed_on` must be on or after `scheduled_date`, and an audit that is not `SCHEDULED` — or a `closed_on` that precedes `scheduled_date` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`. On close, `major_finding_count` becomes the number of findings whose `severity` is `MAJOR` and `minor_finding_count` the number whose `severity` is `MINOR`; findings recorded as `OBSERVATION` are counted in neither. `satisfactory` becomes true only when the audit is `CLOSED`, its `major_finding_count` is 0 and its `minor_finding_count` is 5 or fewer, and `CLOSED` is terminal for an audit.

The close then reaches up into the parent certificate, which is the first of the system's cascade chains. The certificate adds this audit's `major_finding_count` to its own `open_major_findings_count` and this audit's `minor_finding_count` to its own `open_minor_findings_count`, sets `last_audit_closed_on` to the audit's `closed_on`, re-derives `status` and `stamping_authorized` from the new counters, and increments its `version`. Where `last_audit_closed_on` is concerned, "most recent" means the greatest `closed_on` among the certificate's closed audits, with an earlier `created_at` losing the tie-break; the field stays null while no audit of the certificate has closed. The posture consequences follow the counters exactly: an `ACTIVE` certificate moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` when the closing audit contributes 1 or 2 majors, or contributes more than 5 minors; it moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` when the closing audit contributes 3 or more majors; and a certificate already on `PROBATION` moves `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` when the close pushes its cumulative `open_major_findings_count` to 3 or more. The boundary is exact — two majors put a shop on probation, three suspend it. In the `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` case `stamping_authorized` was already false and stays false. In both the `ACTIVE → PROBATION` and `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` cases `stamping_authorized` is re-derived to false, and the third cascade chain fires: every data report whose job was built under this certificate and which currently sits at `AI_SIGNED` is pushed to `STAMP_HOLD`.

Because the counters accumulate, a finding stays open until a **renewal** clears it. A `ShopAdmin` renews a certificate by supplying `expected_certificate_version`, a `renewal_audit_id` and a `new_expires_on`, and the call answers 200 with the refreshed certificate. The named audit must exist — a `renewal_audit_id` that names nothing draws `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` — must belong to this certificate, and must be `CLOSED` with `satisfactory` true; anything else is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_AUDIT_NOT_SATISFACTORY`. Renewal is declared out of three postures and out of no others: `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `EXPIRED → ACTIVE`. Renewing a certificate that is already `ACTIVE` is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`. On the `PROBATION → ACTIVE` and `EXPIRED → ACTIVE` paths, `new_expires_on` must additionally fall strictly after the current logical UTC date; the `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` path declares only the version match and the satisfactory-audit requirement.

All three renewal paths apply the same effects. `expires_on` is replaced by `new_expires_on`, both `open_major_findings_count` and `open_minor_findings_count` are reset to 0, `renewal_count` increases by 1, `status` and `stamping_authorized` are re-derived — which lands the certificate at `ACTIVE` with `stamping_authorized` true — and `version` increments. The renewal also runs the restoring half of the third cascade chain: every data report under this certificate sitting at `STAMP_HOLD` returns to `AI_SIGNED`, provided its own job carries no open nonconformance at that moment.

The clock is the fourth way a posture can move. When a `ShopAdmin` advances the logical clock past a certificate's `expires_on`, that certificate moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `PROBATION → EXPIRED`. In the `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` case `stamping_authorized` is re-derived to false and every `AI_SIGNED` data report under the certificate is pushed to `STAMP_HOLD`; in the `PROBATION → EXPIRED` case `stamping_authorized` was already false and stays false. Both increment the certificate's `version`, and both are counted in the `certificates_recomputed` figure the clock call returns.

### 3.2. Opening a Job: The Person Registry, the Vessel Envelope and Cancellation

Before anyone can act in a person-backed capacity, a `ShopAdmin` must register them. A person record is created from a `full_name`, which must be non-blank; a `role_code` of `DESIGN_ENGINEER`, `WELDING_ENGINEER`, `QC_INSPECTOR` or `AUTHORIZED_INSPECTOR`, which is the single functional capacity that person may ever act in; `stamp_initials`, being 2 to 4 uppercase letters and unique across all people, so a collision draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_STAMP_INITIALS`; and `ai_agency`, the authorized-inspection agency employing them. `ai_agency` is required when `role_code` is `AUTHORIZED_INSPECTOR` and must otherwise be omitted or null, in which case it defaults to null. The `id` the registry returns is exactly the value that person will put in `X-User-ID`, and people can be listed with an optional `role_code` filter.

A **vessel job** is the aggregate root for one piece of equipment. A `ShopAdmin` opens one by naming the `certificate_id` of the code stamp authority it is built under — and that certificate must not have passed `expires_on`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` — together with a `job_number` that is non-blank and unique system-wide (`ERR_DUPLICATE_JOB_NUMBER` otherwise), the `customer_name` that will be printed on the manufacturer's data report, and the vessel's design envelope. That envelope is `vessel_type`, either `PRESSURE_VESSEL` or `HEAT_EXCHANGER`; `design_code`, either `DIV1` or `DIV2`; `mawp_psi`, the maximum allowable working pressure in psi, strictly greater than 0 and at most 5000, which is the pressure term of every design and test-pressure formula; `design_temp_f`, the design temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, strictly greater than −60 and at most 1000; `mdmt_f`, the minimum design metal temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, at least −155 and strictly less than `design_temp_f`, which becomes the floor for pressure-test medium temperature; and `inside_radius_in`, the inside radius in inches, strictly greater than 0, which is the radius term of the wall thickness formulas.

Two envelope inputs are optional. `stress_ratio` is the ratio of allowable stress at test temperature to allowable stress at design temperature; it defaults to 1.00 when omitted and, when supplied, must be at least 0.50 and at most 1.00 at two decimal places. `pneumatic_justification` is the written justification for substituting a pneumatic test for a hydrostatic one; it defaults to null, and it must be non-blank before any pneumatic test may be scheduled for the job.

Opening the job derives its structural obligations from `vessel_type`. `required_components` — the design components that must each carry an approved calculation sheet before fabrication can be released — is `SHELL` and `HEAD` for a `PRESSURE_VESSEL`, and `SHELL`, `HEAD` and `TUBESHEET` for a `HEAT_EXCHANGER`. `required_test_sides` — the pressure boundaries that must each reach a passing test — is `SHELL_SIDE` alone for a `PRESSURE_VESSEL`, and `SHELL_SIDE` together with `TUBE_SIDE` for a `HEAT_EXCHANGER`. The job opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `governing_joint_efficiency` null, `nonconformance_open_count` at 0, `weld_completion_pct` at 0.00 and `design_release_at` null. Jobs are listed with filters on `certificate_id`, `status` and `vessel_type`.

A job's `status` is the master gate for every child operation and moves through `DRAFT`, `DESIGN_REVIEW`, `FAB_PLANNING`, `FABRICATION`, `NDE_HOLD`, `TESTING`, `DATA_PACKAGE` and then terminally to either `STAMPED` or `CANCELLED`. `design_release_at` records the logical UTC instant the job entered `FAB_PLANNING` and is null before that ever happens. `governing_joint_efficiency` is the smallest `joint_efficiency` among the job's approved calculation sheets, and null while no sheet is approved. `nonconformance_open_count` counts the job's nonconformance reports whose `disposition` is `OPEN`, `REPAIR` or `USE_AS_IS`; reaching 0 is what releases an examination hold.

Cancellation is the escape hatch, and it is available to a `ShopAdmin` from every non-terminal posture: `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, `DESIGN_REVIEW → CANCELLED`, `FAB_PLANNING → CANCELLED`, `FABRICATION → CANCELLED`, `NDE_HOLD → CANCELLED`, `TESTING → CANCELLED` and `DATA_PACKAGE → CANCELLED`. The caller supplies a `cancel_reason`. A job already `STAMPED` or already `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled and draws `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`. The effect set is identical from every one of those seven origins, and it unwinds the whole aggregate in one request: every calculation sheet of the job in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`; every part of the job still `ISSUED` becomes `SCRAPPED` with its `scrap_reason` recorded as `JOB_CANCELLED`, returning its `issued_quantity_lb` to the heat it was cut from so that the heat's mass ledger is restored and a `DEPLETED` heat lifts back to `ACCEPTED`; every furnace charge of the job in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `ABORTED`, which clears `pwht_batch_id` on its joints and returns their `pwht_status` to `PENDING`; every nonconformance of the job whose `disposition` is `OPEN`, `REPAIR` or `USE_AS_IS` becomes `CLOSED` with `closed_at` set to the current logical UTC instant; every data report of the job in `ASSEMBLING`, `READY_FOR_AI`, `AI_SIGNED` or `STAMP_HOLD` becomes `VOIDED`; `nonconformance_open_count` is recomputed to 0; and the job's own `version` increments.

### 3.3. Code Calculations, Dual-Variant Wall Sizing and Independent Design Review

A `DesignEngineer` prepares one **calculation sheet** per pressure part. The sheet is created under a job — the call carries `expected_job_version` as described in §2.7 — and names a `component` of `SHELL`, `HEAD`, `TUBESHEET` or `NOZZLE`. The engineer declares three design inputs plus one elected examination extent: `allowable_stress_psi`, the maximum allowable stress for the base metal at design temperature in psi, strictly greater than 0 and at most 30000; `corrosion_allowance_in`, the corrosion allowance in inches, at least 0 and at most 0.5000 at four decimal places, which is added on top of the governing computed thickness; `thickness_nominal_in`, the nominal wall thickness the shop intends to use, strictly positive at four decimal places; and `rt_extent_declared`, being `FULL`, `SPOT` or `NONE`. Creation answers 201 with the sheet at `status` `DRAFT`, `prepared_by_personnel_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_by_personnel_id` null and `reject_reason` null. Sheets are listed with filters on `job_id`, `prepared_by_personnel_id`, `reviewed_by_personnel_id`, `component` and `status`.

`revision` is a system-maintained index scoped to the pairing of job and `component`: the first sheet for a component is revision 1, and each superseding sheet starts at one more than the revision of the sheet it supersedes. When a sheet is created for a component that already carries a sheet in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, that prior sheet is driven to `SUPERSEDED` and the new sheet opens at `DRAFT` with the incremented `revision`. This is why the call is version-guarded, and it has a job-level consequence: the job returns to `DRAFT`. Specifically, `DESIGN_REVIEW → DRAFT` when a superseding revision is created while the job was in review, and `FAB_PLANNING → DRAFT` when one is created after release, in which case `design_release_at` is additionally reset to null. In both cases `governing_joint_efficiency` is recomputed over whatever approved sheets remain, and the job's `version` increments. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a sheet.

Joint efficiency comes first in the evaluation order and comes from nothing but the declaration. `joint_efficiency` is resolved directly from `rt_extent_declared`: a `FULL` declaration yields 1.00, a `SPOT` declaration yields 0.85 and a `NONE` declaration yields 0.70, carried at two decimal places. The order is pinned and one-directional. Step one resolves the joint efficiency from the declared radiographic extent. Step two consumes that efficiency to size the wall. Step three consumes the declared extent and the sized thickness to decide how much of the weld map must be examined. Examination coverage produced in step three **never** feeds back into step one: a weld map that ends up radiographing every Category A seam does not promote a `SPOT` declaration to `FULL`, and cancelling examinations does not demote the efficiency. The dependency looks circular — the efficiency sizes the examinations, and the examinations *are* radiography extent — but it is resolved strictly in declaration order.

Wall sizing then runs **both** code stress variants, always, and reports both. Let the pressure be the parent job's `mawp_psi`, the radius be the parent job's `inside_radius_in`, the stress be the sheet's `allowable_stress_psi` and the efficiency be the sheet's `joint_efficiency`. The circumferential-stress variant — the one that governs a cylindrical shell's longitudinal seam — demands a thickness equal to the pressure multiplied by the radius, divided by the quantity formed by taking the stress multiplied by the efficiency and subtracting six tenths of the pressure. The longitudinal-stress variant — the circumferential-seam case — demands a thickness equal to the pressure multiplied by the radius, divided by the quantity formed by taking twice the stress multiplied by the efficiency and adding four tenths of the pressure. `thickness_circumferential_in` and `thickness_longitudinal_in` are each of those two raw values rounded half up to four decimal places. Both are report-only, both are always populated, and **neither of them ever includes the corrosion allowance**. For every physically valid input the circumferential figure is the larger of the pair — the longitudinal figure lands near half of it — so the circumferential variant is normally the one that governs.

`governing_variant` and `thickness_required_in` are derived from the *unrounded* pair. `governing_variant` is `CIRCUMFERENTIAL` whenever the unrounded circumferential value is greater than or equal to the unrounded longitudinal value, and `LONGITUDINAL` otherwise. `thickness_required_in` is the greater of the two unrounded values, plus `corrosion_allowance_in`, with the sum rounded half up to four decimal places **once**. Rounding a variant first and then adding the corrosion allowance produces a different answer and is wrong.

Submitting a sheet for independent review moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires three things: the sheet is currently `DRAFT` (a second submit draws `ERR_INVALID_CALCULATION_STATE`), the parent job is in `DRAFT` or `DESIGN_REVIEW`, and `thickness_nominal_in` is at least `thickness_required_in`. A sheet whose intended nominal wall is thinner than the code demands is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALL_THICKNESS`. A successful submit carries the job `DRAFT → DESIGN_REVIEW` and increments both records' versions.

Review is where segregation of duties bites. Approval moves the sheet `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the sheet's `prepared_by_personnel_id`; the same engineer approving their own arithmetic is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On approval the reviewer's identity is written to `reviewed_by_personnel_id`, the job's `governing_joint_efficiency` is recomputed as the smallest `joint_efficiency` across the job's approved sheets, and — once **every** component listed in the job's `required_components` carries exactly one `APPROVED` sheet — the job moves `DESIGN_REVIEW → FAB_PLANNING` with `design_release_at` stamped to the current logical UTC instant.

Rejection is the mirror image and carries the same specification density. It moves the sheet `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `prepared_by_personnel_id` under the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and additionally requires a non-blank `reject_reason` in the request. On rejection the reviewer's identity is written to `reviewed_by_personnel_id`, the `reject_reason` is stored on the sheet, the job moves `DESIGN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, and `governing_joint_efficiency` is recomputed.

The revision loop closes on the same row rather than by creating a new one. Revising a sheet moves it `REJECTED → DRAFT` and requires the sheet to be `REJECTED` and the parent job to be in `DRAFT`; revising a sheet in any other posture draws `ERR_INVALID_CALCULATION_STATE`. The revising engineer resupplies `rt_extent_declared`, `allowable_stress_psi`, `corrosion_allowance_in` and `thickness_nominal_in`, which replace the previous values wholesale. `revision` increases by 1, `reviewed_by_personnel_id` and `reject_reason` are both cleared back to null, and `joint_efficiency`, `thickness_circumferential_in`, `thickness_longitudinal_in`, `thickness_required_in` and `governing_variant` are all recomputed from the new inputs. The row therefore cycles `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT` as many times as review demands, with `revision` incrementing on each pass, and the job cycles `DRAFT → DESIGN_REVIEW → DRAFT` alongside it until every required component is approved.

### 3.4. Material Traceability: From Mill Test Report to Issued Piece

Traceability begins at the receiving dock. A `QCInspector` records a **heat** of material with its `heat_number` — the mill heat number stamped on the steel and printed on the mill test report, non-blank and unique per material specification, so a repeat under the same specification draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_HEAT_NUMBER` — a `spec` of `SA-516-70`, `SA-240-304L`, `SA-105` or `SA-179`, a `form` of `PLATE`, `FORGING`, `PIPE` or `TUBE`, an `mtr_received` flag stating whether the mill test report is on file, the certified strengths `mtr_certified_yield_ksi` and `mtr_certified_tensile_ksi` in ksi, and `quantity_received_lb`, the pounds taken into the shop, strictly positive at two decimal places. The certified strengths are null until the report is on file, and `mtr_received` defaults to false.

Two properties are derived from `spec` alone and drive decisions much later in the process. `material_class` is `FERRITIC` for `SA-516-70`, `SA-105` and `SA-179`, and `AUSTENITIC` for `SA-240-304L`; it is what selects magnetic-particle versus dye-penetrant examination on a joint. `p_number` — the base-metal P-Number — is 1 for `SA-516-70`, `SA-105` and `SA-179`, and 8 for `SA-240-304L`; it must equal the `base_metal_p_number` of the procedure cited by any joint consuming this heat. The heat opens at `status` `QUARANTINE`, with `quantity_issued_lb` at 0.00 and `quantity_remaining_lb` equal to `quantity_received_lb`. Heats are listed with filters on `spec`, `status` and `heat_number`.

Receiving inspection settles the heat. The reviewing `QCInspector` supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, plus an optional `reason`. The heat must be `QUARANTINE`, or the review is refused with `ERR_INVALID_HEAT_STATE`. An `ACCEPT` decision requires `mtr_received` to be true — accepting material whose mill test report has not arrived is refused with `ERR_TRACEABILITY_INCOMPLETE` — and requires both certified strengths to reach the minima for the heat's specification: at least 38 ksi yield and 70 ksi tensile for `SA-516-70`, at least 25 ksi yield and 70 ksi tensile for `SA-240-304L`, at least 36 ksi yield and 70 ksi tensile for `SA-105`, and at least 26 ksi yield and 47 ksi tensile for `SA-179`. Either strength falling short is refused with `ERR_MTR_BELOW_SPEC_MINIMUM`. A successful accept moves the heat `QUARANTINE → ACCEPTED`. A `REJECT` decision requires a non-blank `reason` and moves the heat `QUARANTINE → REJECTED`, which is terminal.

A **part** is a piece cut from a heat for a job, and it is the link that carries the heat number into fabrication. A `WeldingEngineer` issues one by naming the `heat_id` it is cut from, a `part_mark` — the hard stamp applied to the piece, non-blank and unique within its job — a `component_type` of `SHELL`, `HEAD`, `TUBESHEET`, `NOZZLE` or `FLANGE`, and `issued_quantity_lb`, the pounds drawn, strictly positive at two decimal places. Issue is permitted while the job is in `FAB_PLANNING`, `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`. The heat must be `ACCEPTED`: a heat still in `QUARANTINE`, one that was `REJECTED`, or one already `DEPLETED` draws `ERR_HEAT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The product form must suit the piece — `SHELL` and `HEAD` require a heat whose `form` is `PLATE`, `NOZZLE` requires `PIPE`, `TUBESHEET` accepts either `FORGING` or `PLATE`, and `FLANGE` requires `FORGING` — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_FORM_MISMATCH`. The piece opens at `status` `ISSUED` with `scrap_reason` null.

Each part also carries `heat_traceability_complete`, a derived flag that is true only when the referenced heat has `mtr_received` true **and** its `status` is either `ACCEPTED` or `DEPLETED`. That flag is what the weld-map release gate and the material section of the data-package score both read, so a piece cut from a heat whose paperwork never arrived poisons both. Parts are listed with filters on `job_id`, `heat_id`, `component_type` and `status`.

The **mass ledger** on each heat is maintained by aggregation rather than by decrement, and it is re-derived on every part issue, every part scrap, and the job-cancellation cascade. `quantity_issued_lb` is the sum of `issued_quantity_lb` across all parts referencing this heat whose `status` is still `ISSUED`; scrapped pieces are excluded from that sum. `quantity_remaining_lb` is `quantity_received_lb` minus that issued total, rounded half up to two decimal places. The invariant that the received pounds always equal the remaining pounds plus the issued pounds therefore holds after every one of the three triggers. An issue is admitted only when `issued_quantity_lb` is less than or equal to the heat's current `quantity_remaining_lb`, so remaining pounds never go negative; an over-draw is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_HEAT_QUANTITY`. When an issue brings `quantity_remaining_lb` to exactly 0.00 the heat moves `ACCEPTED → DEPLETED`; any positive remainder, however small, keeps it `ACCEPTED`.

Scrapping a piece is the compensating move. A `WeldingEngineer` scraps an `ISSUED` part by supplying a `scrap_reason`, and the call is permitted while the job is in `FAB_PLANNING`, `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`. A part that is already `SCRAPPED`, or one that is still referenced as `part_a_id` or `part_b_id` by a joint whose `fab_status` has reached `WELDED`, cannot be scrapped and draws `ERR_INVALID_PART_STATE`. On scrap the piece moves `ISSUED → SCRAPPED`, its `scrap_reason` is stored, the heat's `quantity_issued_lb` and `quantity_remaining_lb` are re-derived so the mass invariant holds again, and a heat that was `DEPLETED` moves `DEPLETED → ACCEPTED` as soon as its remaining pounds rise above 0.00 — the same restoration the cancellation cascade performs. The job's `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed as part of the same request. Joints still at `PLANNED` that reference the scrapped piece keep their rows; they are not deleted, and they will block the weld-map release with `ERR_PART_SCRAPPED` until they are dealt with. `SCRAPPED` is terminal for a part, and a scrapped piece also leaves the traceability denominator that the release gate walks.

### 3.5. Welding Procedures, Welder Continuity and the Weld Map

A **welding procedure specification** is the shop's qualified recipe for joining two pieces. A `WeldingEngineer` writes one by supplying a `wps_number`, its shop-unique designation — a repeat draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_WPS_NUMBER` — a `process` of `SMAW`, `GTAW`, `SAW` or `FCAW`, a `base_metal_p_number` which must be either 1 or 8 and must match the `p_number` of any heat whose pieces the procedure is used to join, a `filler_f_number` classifying the filler metal, at least 1 and at most 6, the qualified thickness window `thickness_min_in` and `thickness_max_in` in inches at four decimal places where the minimum is strictly positive and the maximum is strictly greater than the minimum, a `position` of `1G`, `2G`, `3G`, `4G`, `5G` or `6G`, a `pwht_required` flag stating whether joints made under this procedure must be stress-relieved, `preheat_min_f`, the minimum preheat temperature in degrees Fahrenheit at no less than 32, and `supporting_pqr_number`, the non-blank designation of the procedure qualification record backing it. The procedure opens at `status` `DRAFT`.

Qualifying a procedure moves it `DRAFT → QUALIFIED` and requires that it currently be `DRAFT` — a second qualification draws `ERR_INVALID_WPS_STATE` — and that `supporting_pqr_number` be non-blank. Only a `QUALIFIED` procedure may be cited by a weld-map entry; a joint naming a `DRAFT` procedure is refused with `ERR_WPS_NOT_QUALIFIED`. Procedures are listed with filters on `process` and `status`.

A **welder qualification** records that one welder has demonstrated competence under one procedure. A `WeldingEngineer` records it with the welder's `welder_stamp`, the non-blank hard-stamp identifier the welder impresses beside each completed joint, the welder's `welder_name`, the `wps_id` of the procedure qualified to, a `position_qualified` from `1G` through `6G`, `thickness_range_max_in` as the maximum base-metal thickness the qualification covers, strictly positive at four decimal places, and `qualified_on`, the ISO 8601 calendar date of the successful performance test. The pairing of `welder_stamp` and `wps_id` must be unique — one row per welder per procedure — and a repeat draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_WELDER_QUALIFICATION`. On creation `last_continuity_date` is initialised to `qualified_on`. Qualifications are listed with filters on `wps_id`, `welder_stamp` and `status`.

Qualifications **lapse with the clock**, which is why the shop clock in §2.5 exists. The continuity limit is 180 days. `days_since_continuity` is the whole number of days from `last_continuity_date` to the current logical UTC date. `status` is `LAPSED` when `days_since_continuity` is strictly greater than 180, and `QUALIFIED` otherwise: exactly 180 days is still `QUALIFIED`, and 181 days lapses. Both `days_since_continuity` and `status` are re-derived whenever a qualification is read and whenever the logical clock is advanced, so a qualification traverses `QUALIFIED → LAPSED` purely as a consequence of the clock being set forward past its 180-day mark, with no request ever touching the qualification row, and every joint that welder could have made turns into an `ERR_WELDER_NOT_QUALIFIED` refusal.

Continuity is maintained, and lapses are reversed, by logging work. The engineer supplies a `continuity_date`, the ISO 8601 date on which the welder last deposited weld metal under the process, and the `process` worked. Three preconditions apply, each refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when violated: `continuity_date` must be on or before the current logical UTC date, it must be strictly later than the existing `last_continuity_date`, and `process` must equal the `process` of the procedure the qualification cites. A successful log sets `last_continuity_date` to `continuity_date`, re-derives `days_since_continuity` and `status`, and increments the qualification's `version`. This is the reinstatement path: a `LAPSED` qualification returns `LAPSED → QUALIFIED` on a fresh continuity log without any new performance test, and a still-current one traverses `QUALIFIED → QUALIFIED`, resetting the 180-day window each time.

The **weld map** is the enumerated list of every joint the vessel needs. A `WeldingEngineer` plans one joint at a time while the job is in `FAB_PLANNING` or `FABRICATION`. The joint carries a `weld_number`, its non-blank designation on the weld map drawing, unique within its job — a repeat draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_WELD_NUMBER` — and used as the ascending lexicographic ordering key when examination selection has to pick a subset. It carries a `joint_category` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`: Category A covers longitudinal seams in the main shell and all seams in a formed head, Category B covers circumferential seams in the shell, Category C covers flange and tubesheet attachment welds, and Category D covers nozzle-to-shell attachment welds. It carries a `joint_type` of `BUTT`, `CORNER` or `FILLET`, a `thickness_in` giving the governing base-metal thickness at the joint, strictly positive at four decimal places, the `wps_id` of the procedure it is made under, and the pieces joined: `part_a_id` is required and `part_b_id` is optional, left null for an attachment weld made to a single piece.

Two adequacy checks run at planning time, before any state check on the job. The cited procedure must be `QUALIFIED`, or the request draws `ERR_WPS_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The joint's `thickness_in` must fall inside the cited procedure's qualified window, at least `thickness_min_in` and at most `thickness_max_in`; outside it the request draws `ERR_THICKNESS_OUTSIDE_WPS_RANGE`. Both named pieces must belong to the same job as the joint, or the request draws `ERR_PART_NOT_IN_JOB`.

A newly planned joint opens with `fab_status` `PLANNED`, `nde_status` `NOT_PLANNED`, `repair_generation` 0, `repair_of_weld_id` null, `pwht_batch_id` null, `welder_qualification_id` null and `welded_at` null. Its `pwht_status` opens at `PENDING` when the cited procedure has `pwht_required` true and at `NOT_REQUIRED` when it does not — the flag on the procedure, resolved once at planning time, is what decides whether the joint will ever need a furnace charge. The joint's examination requirements, `required_volumetric_pct`, `volumetric_method` and `required_surface_method`, are resolved by the rule in §3.6, and the job's `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed. Joints are listed with filters on `job_id`, `wps_id`, `part_a_id`, `part_b_id`, `welder_qualification_id`, `repair_of_weld_id`, `pwht_batch_id`, `joint_category`, `fab_status`, `nde_status` and `pwht_status`.

Completing a joint moves `fab_status` `PLANNED → WELDED` and is where the welder's competence is checked against the joint rather than against the procedure. The engineer supplies the `welder_qualification_id` of the welder who made it and `welded_at`, the instant deposition finished. The joint must be `PLANNED`. The job must be in `FABRICATION`, with one exception: a job sitting in `NDE_HOLD` still admits completion of a joint whose `repair_of_weld_id` is not null when the nonconformance covering the parent joint has been dispositioned `REPAIR` — that is how a repair gets welded without releasing the hold. The cited procedure must still be `QUALIFIED`. The cited qualification's `status` must be `QUALIFIED`, or the request draws `ERR_WELDER_NOT_QUALIFIED` — this is the lapse check, and it is evaluated against the qualification's continuity as of the current logical date, not as of `welded_at`. The qualification's `wps_id` must equal the joint's `wps_id`, or the request draws `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WPS_MISMATCH`: a welder qualified under a different procedure than the joint cites is not permitted to make it even when both procedures share a process. The joint's `thickness_in` must be at most the qualification's `thickness_range_max_in`, or the request draws `ERR_THICKNESS_OUTSIDE_QUALIFICATION` — note that the qualification carries only a ceiling, whereas the procedure carries a two-sided window, so a joint can clear the procedure's window and still exceed the welder's. Finally `part_a_id` and, when present, `part_b_id` must both still be `ISSUED`; welding to a scrapped piece is refused with `ERR_PART_SCRAPPED`. On success `fab_status` becomes `WELDED`, `welder_qualification_id` and `welded_at` are stored, and `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed. Neither `nde_status` nor `pwht_status` is touched by completion — the three dimensions advance independently.

**Releasing the weld map** is the aggregate gate that moves the job `FAB_PLANNING → FABRICATION`, and a `WeldingEngineer` invokes it. It walks the whole map and refuses on the first failure. The job must have at least one planned joint, or the release draws `ERR_EMPTY_WELD_MAP`. Every `ISSUED` part of the job must have `heat_traceability_complete` true, or the release draws `ERR_TRACEABILITY_INCOMPLETE`. No joint still at `PLANNED` may reference a piece whose `status` is `SCRAPPED`, or the release draws `ERR_PART_SCRAPPED`. When the job's `governing_joint_efficiency` equals 1.00 — that is, when every approved calculation sheet declared full radiography — every Category A joint must have `joint_type` `BUTT`, because a full-efficiency design cannot be built with a corner or fillet on a main seam; a violation draws `ERR_JOINT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Every joint's `thickness_in` must still lie inside its procedure's qualified window, drawing `ERR_THICKNESS_OUTSIDE_WPS_RANGE` if a procedure was rewritten underneath it. And every joint must carry a resolved `required_volumetric_pct`, or the release draws `ERR_NDE_INCOMPLETE`. Release therefore couples the design decision made in §3.3, the material paperwork of §3.4 and the joint geometry of the map into a single admission decision.

### 3.6. Examination: Coverage Resolution, Plan Selection and Interpretation

Every joint carries **three independent status dimensions**, and confusing them is the most common way to misread this specification. `fab_status`, being `PLANNED` or `WELDED`, says whether metal has been deposited. `nde_status`, being `NOT_PLANNED`, `PLANNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, says where the joint stands with non-destructive examination. `pwht_status`, being `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE`, says where it stands with post-weld heat treatment. The three advance on their own triggers and in any interleaving: a joint may be heat-treated to `COMPLETE` while still awaiting radiography, or examination-`ACCEPTED` while its `pwht_status` is still `PENDING`. Only the completion measure in this section, and the aggregate gate on pressure testing, read all three at once.

**Coverage resolution** decides, at planning time, how much of each joint must be examined and by what method. It is evaluated as an ordered cascade and the first matching clause wins. All thickness comparisons are made at three decimal places.

`required_volumetric_pct` is an integer percentage resolved as follows. A joint whose `repair_of_weld_id` is not null takes 100 unconditionally — every repair is fully radiographed regardless of what the original joint required or what the design declared. Otherwise, a joint whose parent job has `design_code` `DIV2` and whose `joint_category` is `A` or `B` takes 100, because Division 2 mandates full volumetric examination of all main seams irrespective of the declared extent. Otherwise the declared extent governs, read from the `rt_extent_declared` of the `APPROVED` calculation sheet whose `component` matches the `component_type` of the joint's `part_a`. For a Category A joint, a `FULL` declaration gives 100, a `SPOT` declaration gives 25 and a `NONE` declaration gives 0. For a Category B joint, a `FULL` declaration gives 100 when `thickness_in` is at least 1.500 and gives 25 when it is thinner, a `SPOT` declaration gives 25, and a `NONE` declaration gives 0. Category C and Category D joints take 0 — attachment welds get no volumetric requirement from this rule, only from the repair clause above.

`volumetric_method` is null whenever the resolved coverage is 0. Otherwise it is `UT` when `thickness_in` is at least 2.000 **and** `joint_type` is `BUTT`, since ultrasonic examination is the practical method on heavy butt joints, and `RT` in every other case.

`required_surface_method` is resolved independently of coverage, and the austenitic clause outranks the geometry clause. It is `PT` when either joined piece traces to a heat whose `material_class` is `AUSTENITIC` — magnetic-particle examination is not valid on non-magnetic stainless, so dye penetrant is substituted. Otherwise it is `MT` when the `joint_category` is `C` or `D`, or the `joint_type` is `FILLET` or `CORNER`. Otherwise it is null. A surface examination, when required, always carries a `required_coverage_pct` of 100; the percentage sampling notion applies only to volumetric examination.

**Plan selection** is invoked by a `QCInspector` on a job in `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`, and it manufactures the examination records. It answers 201 with the examinations created by that invocation, in creation order. The candidate set is every weld of the job whose `fab_status` is `WELDED` and whose `nde_status` is `NOT_PLANNED`; joints that are still `PLANNED`, and joints already past `NOT_PLANNED`, are skipped, which is what makes re-invocation safe and non-duplicating.

Candidates are grouped by `joint_category` and the groups are processed in the fixed order `A`, then `B`, then `C`, then `D`. Within a group, the group's coverage requirement decides the selection. When the requirement is 100, every candidate in the group is selected. When it is 25, the number selected is the group's candidate count multiplied by 0.25 and rounded **up** to the next whole joint, and the joints taken are that many from the front of the group ordered by `weld_number` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending; a group of one therefore still yields one examination, and a group of five yields two. When the requirement is neither, no volumetric examination is created for that group. Crucially, the group size used in that arithmetic counts only **this invocation's** candidates, not every joint of that category ever planned, so examining in two passes can select more joints in total than examining in one.

Each volumetric selection produces one examination whose `method` is the selected joint's `volumetric_method` and whose `required_coverage_pct` is that joint's `required_volumetric_pct`. After the volumetric pass over all four groups finishes, a second pass runs across the whole candidate set: every candidate whose `required_surface_method` is not null yields one further examination with that method and `required_coverage_pct` 100, appended in `weld_number` ascending order across the job irrespective of category. A joint can therefore emerge from one invocation carrying two examinations — one volumetric, one surface — or a surface examination alone. Every joint selected by either pass moves `nde_status` `NOT_PLANNED → PLANNED` and has its `version` incremented; a `WELDED` joint that neither pass touches stays at `NOT_PLANNED`, and that is a legitimate terminal posture for a joint that needs no examination at all.

An **examination** record names its `weld_id`, its `method` of `RT`, `UT`, `PT` or `MT`, and its `exam_class`, which is derived from the method: `VOLUMETRIC` for `RT` and `UT`, `SURFACE` for `PT` and `MT`. It carries `required_coverage_pct`, the percentage the plan demands; `examined_coverage_pct`, the percentage actually shot or scanned, at least 0 and at most 100 at two decimal places, null while the examination is pending; `indications_count`, the number of reportable indications found, at least 0 and null while pending; a `result` of `PENDING`, `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`; `examiner_personnel_id`, the interpreting inspector; and `exam_date`, the calendar date of examination. Examinations are listed with filters on `weld_id`, `examiner_personnel_id`, `method`, `exam_class` and `result`.

**Interpretation** is recorded by a `QCInspector` supplying a `result` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, together with `examined_coverage_pct`, `indications_count` and `exam_date`. Both branches share three preconditions: the examination must still be `PENDING`, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_EXAM_STATE`; the examined joint's `fab_status` must be `WELDED`; and `exam_date` must be on or after the calendar date of the joint's `welded_at`, since a joint cannot be examined before it exists. The `REJECT` branch additionally requires `indications_count` to be at least 1 — rejecting a joint on which nothing was found is refused. Both branches store `examiner_personnel_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` along with `exam_date`, `examined_coverage_pct` and `indications_count`. The examination record itself moves `PENDING → ACCEPT` on the first branch and `PENDING → REJECT` on the second, and both `ACCEPT` and `REJECT` are terminal for it — an interpretation once recorded is never revised.

The two branches then diverge sharply in what they cascade. An `ACCEPT` moves the joint `nde_status` `PLANNED → ACCEPTED`, but only once **every** examination attached to that joint reads `ACCEPT`; a joint carrying both a volumetric and a surface examination stays `PLANNED` until the second one lands. On that transition the job's `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed, and every data report of the job re-derives its `NDE` section score and its `completeness_score`.

A `REJECT` moves the joint `nde_status` `PLANNED → REJECTED` and triggers the full cascade: a nonconformance report is raised with `source` `NDE_REJECT` and `disposition` `OPEN`, the job's `nonconformance_open_count` increases by 1, the job moves `FABRICATION → NDE_HOLD`, every data report of the job re-derives `completeness_score` and `missing_items`, and every data report of the job sitting at `AI_SIGNED` is pushed to `STAMP_HOLD`. One rejected radiograph therefore reaches all the way into a signed data package.

A rejected joint is **never re-planned in place**. Its `nde_status` moves `REJECTED → ACCEPTED` only as a consequence of repair: when a repair joint whose `repair_of_weld_id` points at it reaches `fab_status` `WELDED` with `nde_status` `ACCEPTED`, the parent joint's examination status is satisfied through its replacement. Re-invoking plan selection does not pick up a `REJECTED` joint.

**`weld_completion_pct`** is the job's single progress measure and it is the one place all three joint dimensions are read together. The numerator counts the job's joints whose `fab_status` is `WELDED` **and** whose `nde_status` is `ACCEPTED` **and** whose `pwht_status` is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `COMPLETE`; a joint failing any one of the three is not counted. The denominator counts all of the job's joints, repair joints included, which is why raising a repair pushes the percentage down before it pulls it up. The value is 0.00 when the denominator is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. It is recomputed on weld completion, on examination interpretation, on furnace charge completion, on part scrap and on the nonconformance-close cascade.

### 3.7. Nonconformance, Disposition and the Repair Loop

Nonconformance reports are **never created by a client request**. They appear only as system consequences: a rejected examination raises one with `source` `NDE_REJECT`, and a failed pressure test raises one with `source` `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL`. Each report names its `job_id`, and its `weld_id`, which is the offending joint for an examination rejection and null for a report charged to a pressure test rather than to a specific joint.

The `ncr_number` is minted by the system as the literal `NCR-`, then the parent job's `job_number`, then a hyphen, then a per-job sequence number equal to the count of reports already existing for that job plus one, zero-padded to three digits. The first report on job `JOB-2024-0001` is therefore `NCR-JOB-2024-0001-001` and the eleventh is `NCR-JOB-2024-0001-011`. `severity` is derived, not supplied: it is `MAJOR` when `source` is `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL`, or when the failed joint's `joint_category` is `A` or `B`, and `MINOR` in every other case — a rejected nozzle attachment is minor, a rejected main seam is not. The report opens at `disposition` `OPEN`, with `disposition_notes` null, `ai_concurrence_personnel_id` null and `closed_at` null. Reports are listed with filters on `job_id`, `weld_id`, `ai_concurrence_personnel_id`, `source`, `severity` and `disposition`.

Three of the four dispositions count as **open**: `OPEN`, `REPAIR` and `USE_AS_IS` all keep the report inside the job's `nonconformance_open_count`. Only `CLOSED` leaves it. This is deliberate and it is what keeps a job in `NDE_HOLD` while a repair is being welded — deciding to repair does not resolve anything, it only commits to a course of action.

Dispositioning a report is permitted to a `QCInspector` or an `AuthorizedInspector` and requires `disposition_notes`. Both branches require the report to be `OPEN` and the job to be in `NDE_HOLD`. The `REPAIR` branch moves the report `OPEN → REPAIR` and additionally requires the referenced joint's `repair_generation` to be strictly below the shop's repair limit of 2; a joint already twice repaired cannot be repaired again and the request draws `ERR_REPAIR_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The `USE_AS_IS` branch moves the report `OPEN → USE_AS_IS` and requires that the acting `X-User-Role` be `AuthorizedInspector`, because accepting a known defect in the vessel is the Authorized Inspector's call alone and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `ai_concurrence_personnel_id`. A `QCInspector` who asks for `USE_AS_IS` is refused with `ERR_AI_CONCURRENCE_REQUIRED` even though the same role may freely ask for `REPAIR` on the same report — the two sibling branches of one endpoint carry deliberately different authority.

Raising a **repair joint** is a `WeldingEngineer` action taken against a report already dispositioned `REPAIR`; a report in any other posture draws `ERR_INVALID_NONCONFORMANCE_STATE`, and the job must be in `NDE_HOLD`. The engineer supplies only a fresh `weld_number` and the `wps_id` the repair will be made under — everything else is inherited. The new joint's `repair_of_weld_id` is set to the report's `weld_id`, its `repair_generation` is the parent joint's `repair_generation` plus 1, and its `joint_category`, `joint_type`, `thickness_in`, `part_a_id` and `part_b_id` are copied from the parent. Its `required_volumetric_pct` is 100 by the repair clause of §3.6, it opens at `fab_status` `PLANNED` and `nde_status` `NOT_PLANNED`, and the job's `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed to account for the enlarged denominator. The same welding-adequacy checks that govern a planned joint apply: the cited procedure must be `QUALIFIED` and the inherited `thickness_in` must fall inside its window, and the joint is subject to the same aggregate gates thereafter.

Closing a report is a `QCInspector` action, and the precondition depends on which open disposition it is closing from. `REPAIR → CLOSED` requires that a repair joint exist whose `repair_of_weld_id` equals the report's `weld_id`, whose `fab_status` is `WELDED` and whose `nde_status` is `ACCEPTED` — the repair must be made and passed, not merely planned. `USE_AS_IS → CLOSED` requires only that `ai_concurrence_personnel_id` be non-null, which the disposition step guaranteed. `OPEN → CLOSED` occurs as part of the job-cancellation cascade rather than by request. Either requested path stamps `closed_at` with the current logical UTC instant, decreases the job's `nonconformance_open_count` by 1, and moves the job `NDE_HOLD → FABRICATION` once that count reaches 0. Closing from `REPAIR` additionally returns every data report of the job sitting at `STAMP_HOLD` to `AI_SIGNED`, provided the governing certificate still authorizes stamping.

The loop closes in the other direction too. A report at `REPAIR` moves `REPAIR → OPEN` when an examination of the repair joint records `REJECT`; because both `REPAIR` and `OPEN` sit inside the open set, `nonconformance_open_count` is unchanged by that move and the job stays in `NDE_HOLD`. The cycle `OPEN → REPAIR → OPEN` is therefore traversable, once per generation, until `repair_generation` reaches the limit of 2, at which point the next disposition attempt fails with `ERR_REPAIR_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and the only remaining route out is `USE_AS_IS` with Authorized Inspector concurrence, or cancellation of the job. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Post-Weld Heat Treatment and Pressure Testing

A **furnace charge** stress-relieves a set of joints together. A `WeldingEngineer` schedules one by naming the `job_id`, a `furnace_code` of `FURNACE_A` or `FURNACE_B`, the reserved window `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` where the end must be strictly later than the start, a `soak_temp_f` giving the intended soak temperature in degrees Fahrenheit at no less than 500 and no more than 1600, and `weld_ids`, the array of joint identifiers loaded into the charge, whose members must be distinct. An empty array satisfies the request shape and is then refused by the domain check with `ERR_EMPTY_WELD_SET`. The job must be in `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`, and every named joint must belong to that job, or the request draws `ERR_WELD_NOT_IN_JOB`. Furnace capacity for the reserved window is checked as described in §2.11.

Creation answers 201 with the charge at `status` `SCHEDULED`, `scheduled_by_personnel_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `weld_count` equal to the number of entries in `weld_ids`, `chart_uploaded` false, `actual_soak_minutes` null, `chart_record_number` null and `chart_reviewed_by_personnel_id` null. Each named joint has its `pwht_batch_id` set to the new charge and its `version` incremented. Charges are listed with filters on `job_id`, `scheduled_by_personnel_id`, `chart_reviewed_by_personnel_id`, `furnace_code` and `status`.

Two figures are derived from the charge contents. `governing_thickness_in` is the greatest `thickness_in` among the batched joints — the thickest joint sets the hold for the whole charge. `required_soak_minutes` is the larger of 15 minutes and the product of `governing_thickness_in` and 60 minutes per inch, that product being rounded half up to a whole number of minutes. The 15-minute floor is therefore what binds for every joint thinner than 0.250 inch, and the per-inch rate binds above it. The soak temperature is additionally banded by base metal: for a charge whose joints are all made under procedures with `base_metal_p_number` 1, `soak_temp_f` must lie between 1100 and 1250 degrees Fahrenheit inclusive. A charge whose joints do not share a single base-metal P-Number cannot be given a coherent soak band at all and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`; outside a declared band only the field's own 500-to-1600 range applies.

Starting a charge moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. It requires the charge to be `SCHEDULED`, the job to be in `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`, every batched joint's `fab_status` to be `WELDED` — a charge cannot be run on joints that have not been made — and `soak_temp_f` to lie inside the applicable band, or the start draws `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, the same error a charge in the wrong posture or holding an unwelded joint draws. Every batched joint moves `pwht_status` `PENDING → IN_PROGRESS` in the same request.

Completing a charge moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE` and is where the chart is reviewed. The engineer supplies `actual_soak_minutes`, the soak time actually achieved at no less than 0; `chart_record_number`, the identifier of the time-temperature chart record, globally unique so a reused chart draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHART_RECORD_NUMBER`; and `chart_uploaded`, the flag stating the chart is on file. The charge must be `IN_PROGRESS` and the job in `FABRICATION` or `NDE_HOLD`. `chart_uploaded` must be true and `chart_record_number` non-blank, or the completion draws `ERR_CHART_RECORD_MISSING`. `actual_soak_minutes` must be at least `required_soak_minutes`, or it draws `ERR_SOAK_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `scheduled_by_personnel_id`, or it draws `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the person who scheduled the charge may not be the person who certifies its chart. On success `chart_reviewed_by_personnel_id` records the acting user, every batched joint moves `pwht_status` `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE`, the job's `weld_completion_pct` is recomputed, and every data report of the job re-derives its `PWHT` section score and its `completeness_score`. `COMPLETE` and `ABORTED` are both terminal for a charge. `ABORTED` is reached only through the job-cancellation cascade, as `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` for a charge never started and `IN_PROGRESS → ABORTED` for one already in the furnace; either way the cascade clears `pwht_batch_id` on each affected joint and returns each to `pwht_status` `PENDING`.

A **pressure test** proves the finished vessel. A `QCInspector` schedules one by naming the `job_id`, a `test_side` of `SHELL_SIDE` or `TUBE_SIDE`, a `test_type` of `HYDROSTATIC` or `PNEUMATIC`, a `test_bay_code` of `BAY_1` or `BAY_2`, and the window `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`. Bay capacity is checked as described in §2.11. The `test_side` must be a member of the job's `required_test_sides`, or the request draws `ERR_TEST_SIDE_NOT_APPLICABLE`: a single-chamber vessel has no tube side to test. A `PNEUMATIC` test requires the job's `pneumatic_justification` to be non-blank, since pneumatic testing is only permitted where a hydrostatic test is impracticable, and its absence draws `ERR_PNEUMATIC_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`.

Scheduling the first test of a job is the aggregate gate that moves it `FABRICATION → TESTING`, and it walks the entire weld map. Every joint's `fab_status` must be `WELDED`. Every joint's `nde_status` must be `ACCEPTED`, with one exemption: a joint still at `NOT_PLANNED` passes only when its `required_volumetric_pct` is 0 **and** its `required_surface_method` is null, meaning nothing was ever owed on it; any other posture draws `ERR_NDE_INCOMPLETE`. Every joint whose `pwht_status` is not `NOT_REQUIRED` must have reached `COMPLETE`, or the gate draws `ERR_PWHT_INCOMPLETE`. And the job's `nonconformance_open_count` must be 0, or it draws `ERR_OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE`. A heat exchanger owes a test per chamber, so a job already in `TESTING` admits further tests through a self-transition `TESTING → TESTING`, subject to there being no existing test of the job on the same `test_side` whose `result` is still `PENDING`.

Creation answers 201 with the test at `result` `PENDING`, `ai_witness_confirmed` false, `witnessed_by_personnel_id` null and the observed figures null. Tests are listed with filters on `job_id`, `witnessed_by_personnel_id`, `test_bay_code`, `test_side`, `test_type` and `result`.

The three test parameters are derived from `test_type`, and the two variants differ in every one of them. For a `HYDROSTATIC` test, `test_pressure_psi` is 1.3 multiplied by the job's `mawp_psi` multiplied by the job's `stress_ratio`, rounded half up to one decimal place; `min_hold_minutes` is 30; and `min_medium_temp_f` is the job's `mdmt_f` plus 30. For a `PNEUMATIC` test, `test_pressure_psi` is 1.1 multiplied by `mawp_psi` multiplied by `stress_ratio`, rounded half up to one decimal place; `min_hold_minutes` is 60; and `min_medium_temp_f` is `mdmt_f` plus 60. The pneumatic variant deliberately demands a **lower** pressure — compressed gas stores far more energy than water, so the code reduces the overpressure — while demanding a **longer** hold and a **warmer** medium to guard against brittle fracture. Reading the multipliers as "pneumatic is stricter everywhere" is wrong.

Witnessing is the Authorized Inspector's act and it is checked against the shop's certificate rather than against the vessel. Only an `AuthorizedInspector` may witness. The test must be `PENDING` and `ai_witness_confirmed` must still be false, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE`. The governing certificate must not be expired, or it draws `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`, and its `stamping_authorized` must be true, or it draws `ERR_STAMPING_NOT_AUTHORIZED` — an open audit finding at the shop level stops a witness from being recorded on an otherwise perfect vessel. On success `ai_witness_confirmed` becomes true and `witnessed_by_personnel_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. The test's own `result` is untouched: witnessing is a `PENDING → PENDING` self-transition that flips the witness flag and nothing else, and it may be performed exactly once because the second attempt finds the flag already true.

Recording the result is a `QCInspector` action supplying a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, together with `observed_pressure_psi`, the peak pressure held, at least 0 at one decimal place; `observed_hold_minutes`, the minutes held at that pressure, at least 0; and `test_medium_temp_f`, the temperature of the test medium. Both branches require the test to be `PENDING`, `ai_witness_confirmed` to be true — recording a result on an unwitnessed test draws `ERR_AI_WITNESS_REQUIRED` — and the job to be in `TESTING`. The test record moves `PENDING → PASS` on the first branch and `PENDING → FAIL` on the second, and both are terminal for it.

The `PASS` branch additionally requires the job's `nonconformance_open_count` to be 0, `observed_pressure_psi` to be at least `test_pressure_psi` or it draws `ERR_TEST_PRESSURE_INSUFFICIENT`, `observed_hold_minutes` to be at least `min_hold_minutes` or it draws `ERR_HOLD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`, and `test_medium_temp_f` to be at least `min_medium_temp_f` or it draws `ERR_MEDIUM_TEMP_TOO_LOW`. A pass moves the job `TESTING → DATA_PACKAGE`, but only once every member of the job's `required_test_sides` is satisfied — for each such side, the test on that side with the greatest `created_at` must read `PASS`. Recording the latest test on a side as `PASS` therefore does not carry the job forward while another required side is still untested or last read `FAIL`. Every data report of the job re-derives its `TEST` section score in the same request.

The `FAIL` branch raises a nonconformance report with `source` `PRESSURE_TEST_FAIL`, `severity` `MAJOR` and `disposition` `OPEN`, increases the job's `nonconformance_open_count`, and moves the job `TESTING → NDE_HOLD`. The vessel returns to the fabrication side of the process, and the way back out is the disposition-and-repair loop of §3.7 followed by a fresh test on the failed side.

### 3.9. The Data Package: Scoring, Signature and the Nameplate

The **manufacturer's data report** is the vessel's permanent record and the object the whole workflow converges on. A `QCInspector` or an `AuthorizedInspector` assembles one against a job by supplying `expected_job_version` — the call is version-guarded per §2.7 — and a `form_type` of `U-1A`, `U-1` or `U-2A`. The job must be neither `STAMPED` nor `CANCELLED`. Assembly is refused with `ERR_OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE` while the job's `nonconformance_open_count` is above 0, and with `ERR_TRACEABILITY_INCOMPLETE` when any part of the job has `heat_traceability_complete` false. Note that assembly is deliberately permitted from job states well before `DATA_PACKAGE`, so a partially complete package can be assembled and inspected for what it is still missing; it is *submission*, not assembly, that requires the job to have reached `DATA_PACKAGE`.

Creation answers 201 with the report at `status` `ASSEMBLING` and `assembled_by_personnel_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The `mdr_number` is minted by the system as the literal `MDR-`, then the job's `job_number`, then a hyphen, then a per-job sequence equal to the count of reports already existing for that job plus one, zero-padded to two digits — so `MDR-JOB-2024-0001-01` for the first and `MDR-JOB-2024-0001-02` for the second. `section_scores`, `completeness_score` and `missing_items` are derived immediately. Any prior report of the same job sitting at `ASSEMBLING` or `READY_FOR_AI` is moved to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, and the job's `version` increments. Reports already at `AI_SIGNED`, `STAMP_HOLD` or `STAMPED` are left alone. Reports are listed with filters on `job_id`, `assembled_by_personnel_id`, `ai_personnel_id`, `form_type` and `status`; a single-report read re-derives the scores before answering.

**Completeness scoring** is a weighted six-section roll-up. `section_scores` is a keyed structure carrying exactly the keys `DESIGN`, `MATERIAL`, `WELDING`, `NDE`, `PWHT` and `TEST`, each holding that section's earned points. The weights are 25 for `DESIGN`, 20 for `MATERIAL`, 20 for `WELDING`, 15 for `NDE`, 10 for `PWHT` and 10 for `TEST`, summing to 100.

Each section's fraction is a count over a count. `DESIGN` is the number of members of the job's `required_components` carrying an `APPROVED` calculation sheet, divided by the number of `required_components`. `MATERIAL` is the number of members of `required_components` for which at least one `ISSUED` part exists whose heat's `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `DEPLETED`, divided by the number of `required_components`. `WELDING` is the number of the job's joints whose `fab_status` is `WELDED`, divided by the number of the job's joints. `NDE` is the number of the job's examinations whose `result` is `ACCEPT`, divided by the number of the job's examinations. `PWHT` is the number of the job's furnace charges whose `status` is `COMPLETE` and whose `chart_uploaded` is true, divided by the number of the job's charges. `TEST` is the number of the job's pressure tests whose `result` is `PASS`, divided by the number of the job's tests. A section whose denominator is 0 takes a fraction of exactly 1.000 — a vessel that needs no heat treatment scores full marks for heat treatment rather than zero. `SUPERSEDED` calculation sheets, `SCRAPPED` parts and `ABORTED` furnace charges are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator of every section they touch.

The **order of rounding is pinned and load-bearing**. Each section's points are its weight multiplied by its fraction, rounded half up to two decimal places, and that rounded figure is what `section_scores` reports. `completeness_score` is then the sum of those already-rounded section points, rounded half up to two decimal places once more. Summing the unrounded products and rounding only at the end gives a different answer — a two-thirds `DESIGN` fraction alone separates the two orders by a hundredth, 66.66 against 66.67 — and only the per-section-first order is correct.

`missing_items` is an ordered list of strings. It contains, in the section order `DESIGN`, `MATERIAL`, `WELDING`, `NDE`, `PWHT`, `TEST`, the name of every section whose fraction is below 1.000. It then contains the literal `OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE` when the job's `nonconformance_open_count` is above 0, and then the literal `CERTIFICATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` when the governing certificate has `stamping_authorized` false. The last two entries are how a shop-level problem surfaces on a vessel-level document.

Submitting a report to the Authorized Inspector moves it `ASSEMBLING → READY_FOR_AI` and may be done by a `QCInspector` or an `AuthorizedInspector`. The report must be `ASSEMBLING`, the job must be in `DATA_PACKAGE`, the `completeness_score` must be exactly 100.00 — anything less draws `ERR_DATA_PACKAGE_INCOMPLETE` — and `nonconformance_open_count` must be 0 or the submission draws `ERR_OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE`. On success `missing_items` is recomputed and is necessarily empty.

Signing is reserved to an `AuthorizedInspector` and moves the report `READY_FOR_AI → AI_SIGNED`. The report must be `READY_FOR_AI`, or the request draws `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`. The governing certificate must have `stamping_authorized` true, its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and its `expires_on` must be on or after the current logical UTC date. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `assembled_by_personnel_id`, or the request draws `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the same `AuthorizedInspector` who assembled the package cannot also sign it, which is precisely why assembly is open to two roles. On success `ai_personnel_id` records the signer and `ai_signed_at` records the current logical UTC instant.

A signed report is **not final**, and the pairing of `AI_SIGNED` and `STAMP_HOLD` is a cycle traversable any number of times. A report moves `AI_SIGNED → STAMP_HOLD` whenever the governing certificate's `stamping_authorized` becomes false — which happens on a major audit finding, on suspension and on expiry — or whenever the job acquires an open nonconformance from a rejected examination or a failed test; the corresponding literal, `CERTIFICATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` or `OPEN_NONCONFORMANCE`, is added to `missing_items`. It moves `STAMP_HOLD → AI_SIGNED` when a certificate renewal, an audit closure or a nonconformance closure restores both conditions, and those literals are dropped from `missing_items`. The signature itself, along with `ai_personnel_id` and `ai_signed_at`, survives the round trip.

Stamping the nameplate is the last act and belongs to the `ShopAdmin`. The request supplies `nameplate_serial`, the serial impressed on the code nameplate, which must be unique across all data reports; a repeat draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAMEPLATE_SERIAL`. The report must be `AI_SIGNED` — a report at `STAMP_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE` — and the governing certificate must be unexpired with `stamping_authorized` true, drawing `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` or `ERR_STAMPING_NOT_AUTHORIZED` otherwise, since the authority is re-checked at the moment of stamping and not merely at the moment of signature. On success the report moves `AI_SIGNED → STAMPED` with `nameplate_serial` stored and `nameplate_stamped_at` set to the current logical UTC instant, and the job cascades `DATA_PACKAGE → STAMPED`. Both the report state `STAMPED` and the job state `STAMPED` are terminal, and no write to any child record of the job is admitted thereafter.
