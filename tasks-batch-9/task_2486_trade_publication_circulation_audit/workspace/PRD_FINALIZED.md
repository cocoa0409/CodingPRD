# Trade Publication Circulation Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A business-to-business trade publisher does not sell copies; it sells *proven access* to a named professional audience. An advertiser buying a page in a controlled-circulation title is not buying paper — it is buying the publisher's sworn assertion that a stated number of copies per issue reach people who hold a qualifying job function, work in the served industry, and carry purchasing authority. That assertion is periodically torn apart by an independent auditing bureau, which pulls a sample of recipient records, demands the signed request card or web-form capture behind each one, recounts the qualified circulation from primary evidence, and compares its own figure against the number the publisher certified. When the two numbers diverge materially, the certified statement is withdrawn and restated, and every rate-base guarantee riding on it is suspended until a replacement statement is filed.

The system specified here is the publisher's system of record for that entire chain. It holds the publication's qualification policy (which job functions, industries and purchasing-authority levels score points, and how many points are needed to qualify), the recipient file with its two independent status dimensions — whether the recipient is *qualified* and whether the recipient is *deliverable* — the source documentation retained behind every qualification, the periodic circulation statements with their paid, qualified-non-paid and controlled breakdowns, the digital audience snapshots offered alongside print circulation, the advertiser insertion contracts with their guaranteed rate bases, and the audit engagements that reconcile all of it. Six subsystems feed a single audited number, the statement's total qualified copies: qualification scoring, list hygiene, the controlled-copy cap, digital audience deduplication, rate-base make-good settlement, and restatement. Every one of them can move that number, and the specification's central obligation is that the number is always reproducible from the recorded population.

Three cross-cutting pressures shape the design. First, **separation of duties**: the operator who prepares a statement may never be the operator who certifies it, and the auditor who raises a finding may never be the auditor who accepts its resolution. Second, **evidentiary durability**: retained source documents expire on a retention schedule, and an audited recount must be able to distinguish "the evidence never existed" from "the evidence existed and was lawfully purged". Third, **reproducibility under a moving clock**: qualification windows, retention deadlines, reporting periods and measurement windows are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that a statement computed today yields the same breakdown when an auditor recomputes it next quarter. The API is mounted under `/circulation`, with a single test-driving clock facility outside that mount.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Identity, Roles and Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. When both headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The five roles are `CirculationManager`, `QualificationSpecialist`, `CirculationAuditor`, `PublisherExecutive` and `AdvertiserContact`, spelled exactly as written.

A `CirculationManager` is the publisher-side circulation operator. This role registers publications and their qualification policy, adds and reclassifies recipients, records undeliverable events, purges expired source documents, executes hygiene sweeps, prepares and recomputes statements, settles rate-base guarantees, offers make-good credits, registers advertiser contracts, records digital audience snapshots, and responds to audit findings. A `QualificationSpecialist` adjudicates recipient qualification exclusively: taking a request into review, requesting further information, verifying and rejecting. Attaching source documentation and opening or resubmitting a qualification request are shared between `CirculationManager` and `QualificationSpecialist`. A `CirculationAuditor` is the independent bureau: it alone opens audit engagements, records sample verifications, raises findings, accepts or rejects finding responses, issues audit reports, and adjudicates digital audience snapshots. A `PublisherExecutive` is the officer accountable for the certified statement; it shares certification with `CirculationManager` and holds sole authority to waive an offered make-good. An `AdvertiserContact` acts on the advertiser's side and may only accept an offered make-good obligation and read its own obligations.

Four of the five roles have **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `CirculationManager`, `QualificationSpecialist`, `CirculationAuditor` or `PublisherExecutive`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `prepared_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a circulation statement, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a qualification request, as `raised_by_user_id` on an audit finding, and as `validated_by_user_id` on a digital audience snapshot — and it compares recorded values against the acting `X-User-ID` where separation of duties requires it, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` against any entity's `id`.

The fifth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `AdvertiserContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AdvertiserContract`; the advertiser is represented in the system by its insertion contract and by nothing else. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `AdvertiserContract` therefore compare that resource's contract foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when an `AdvertiserContact` reads or accepts a make-good obligation, the obligation's `contract_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_OWNER` and HTTP 403. This ownership test is applied to make-good acceptance and to the single-obligation read, and to no other operation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the response, alongside `total_count` — the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific endpoint description states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Filters supplied as query parameters narrow the result set before pagination and before `total_count` is computed. A filter value that is supposed to be an enum member but is not — for instance a `qualification_status` filter naming a state that does not exist — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` and HTTP 422, exactly as a malformed enum in a request body would be.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`. Identifiers are UUIDs rendered in their canonical string form, with no type prefix and no embedded semantics — an identifier is never parsed, never sorted on, and never used as a decision input by any rule in this specification. Clients must not supply an `id` on creation; a supplied one is ignored.

Almost every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`; both are set from the logical clock described in §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time. `version` begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. These three properties are present on `Publication`, `QualificationRule`, `Subscriber`, `SourceDocument`, `QualificationRequest`, `CirculationStatement`, `AuditEngagement`, `AuditFinding`, `AdvertiserContract`, `MakeGoodObligation` and `DigitalAudienceSnapshot`.

A hygiene run is the one exception. A hygiene run is an immutable execution record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and there is no operation that modifies one once it has been written. A source document likewise has no general update operation — the only mutation defined for it is the purge action described in §3.2 — but it does carry the full complement of common properties, and the purge refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` like any other mutation.

Where an operation mutates several resources at once, each mutated resource's own `version` advances. The cascades in §3.9 are the extreme case: issuing an audit report can advance the version of a statement, a publication, several advertiser contracts and several make-good obligations inside a single request.

### 2.5. The Logical Circulation Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — qualification-window collision, qualification expiry, source-document retention, statement-period closure, digital measurement-window closure, fieldwork-window separation — is evaluated against a single server-held logical UTC clock, never against the host's wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is authoritative, and the server never falls back to real time.

An administrative test-driving facility moves the clock: a caller supplies a target instant as `to_iso8601`, and every subsequent request evaluates its temporal rules against the new instant. All four operator roles — `CirculationManager`, `QualificationSpecialist`, `CirculationAuditor` and `PublisherExecutive` — may drive the clock. Moving the clock is deliberately inert with respect to stored state: no entity is mutated and no sweep is triggered by the move itself. A recipient whose qualification window elapsed three months ago does not become `LAPSED` because the clock passed the expiry instant; it becomes `LAPSED` only when an expiry hygiene sweep is executed against its publication, as described in §3.5.

### 2.6. Nested Addressing and Resolution Order

Twelve peer collections make up the resource model, and all but three are addressed directly by their own identifier beneath the `/circulation` mount. Qualification rules and digital audience snapshots are created beneath their owning publication, and source documents are created beneath their owning subscriber, because none of the three can exist without its owner; each is addressed thereafter by its own identifier or discovered through the owner's list channel. Every other relationship is expressed as a foreign-key field carried in the request body — a subscriber names its `publication_id`, a qualification request names its `subscriber_id` and `source_document_id`, a statement names its `publication_id`, an engagement names its `statement_id`, a finding names its `engagement_id` and optionally its `subscriber_id`, a contract names its `publication_id`, and a make-good obligation names its `contract_id` and `statement_id`.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and a body foreign key is resolved only after every path segment ahead of it has resolved. A publication identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `subscriber_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND`; a `source_document_id` yields `ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `qualification_request_id` yields `ERR_QUALIFICATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; a `statement_id` — including a `restates_statement_id` that names nothing — yields `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an `engagement_id` yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `finding_id` yields `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`; a `contract_id` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; a `make_good_id` yields `ERR_MAKEGOOD_NOT_FOUND`; and a `snapshot_id` yields `ERR_SNAPSHOT_NOT_FOUND`. All ten carry HTTP 404. When a request would fail more than one existence check, the leftmost unresolved reference is the one reported, in the order publication, subscriber, source document, qualification request, statement, engagement, finding, contract, make-good obligation, snapshot.

Because engagements, snapshots and hygiene runs have no unfiltered collection of their own, they are reached only through their owner's list channel: engagements through the statement they audit, snapshots and hygiene runs through the publication they belong to.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Protection on Population-Sensitive Writes

Two write operations take a snapshot of a live population at the moment they run, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent whose population they read. In each case the guarding field is a mandatory, non-optional element of the request body.

Opening a circulation statement requires `expected_publication_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Publication`. If that value does not equal the publication's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Nothing about statement creation is blocked by a state precondition — a publication may legitimately hold many draft statements, and each computes its breakdown afresh from the live recipient file — so the version check is the only thing standing between a client and a statement computed over a population it never saw. A hygiene sweep, a verification, a recipient enrolment or reclassification, an earlier statement create, or a restatement cascade — any of the operations that advance the publication's version, as each of them is described in §3 — will therefore invalidate the create if it landed since the client last read the publication, rather than silently produce a breakdown from a different population.

Opening a qualification request requires `expected_subscriber_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Subscriber`. If that value does not equal the subscriber's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIBER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Creating a request legitimately supersedes any prior non-terminal request for that recipient — the older one is closed out, as §3.3 describes — so a second create would otherwise succeed against identical-looking state and quietly discard the attestation the first writer had in view. The version check rejects the writer whose view of the recipient predates the supersede.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field. In particular, certifying a statement, verifying a request, recording a sample verification, raising a finding, offering or accepting a make-good, and executing a hygiene run all proceed without any client-supplied expected version; they rely on state preconditions instead. The single-publication read is the source of `expected_publication_version`, and the single-subscriber read is the source of `expected_subscriber_version`.

### 2.8. Copy Counts, Percentages and Money Precision

Three numeric families appear throughout the domain, and each has one fixed treatment.

**Copy counts are integers and are never rounded.** Sums of copies are exact. Where a copy count is derived by division — the controlled-copy allowance of §3.7 and the bonus-insertion count of §3.9 — the result is truncated toward zero, that is, the largest whole number not greater than the quotient, so that the remainder always falls to the conservative side of the allowance.

**Percentages are carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded half up.** This applies to the publication's hygiene ratio, to a statement's discrepancy percentage, to a make-good's shortfall percentage, and to the snapshot of the hygiene ratio taken when a breakdown is computed. Half up means a value exactly at the midpoint of the last retained digit rounds away from zero. Percentage comparisons against policy thresholds are performed at two decimals and are inclusive on the threshold: a hygiene ratio of 5.00 against a restriction threshold of 5.00 restricts the publication, while 4.99 does not; a discrepancy of 2.00 against a materiality threshold of 2.00 forces a restatement, while 1.99 does not.

**Money is carried in whole cents as integers.** No currency amount in the system is fractional. Where a cent amount is derived from a percentage and a multiplier, the rounding step is placed explicitly in the formula (see §3.9) and is not left to the implementation's discretion: the per-insertion credit is rounded half up to a whole cent *before* it is multiplied by the insertion count, never after. That ordering is load-bearing and yields a different total from the reverse order.

Percentage inputs supplied by clients — the publication's restriction, materiality and controlled-cap thresholds, a contract's shortfall tolerance, a snapshot's overlap share — are all two-decimal values. Each has its own accepted range, stated where the field is introduced in §3, and a value outside its range is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400.

### 2.9. Audit Posture: The Publication-Level Gate

Every publication carries a computed `audit_posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It starts at `OPEN` when the publication is registered and is re-derived after any mutation that changes the publication's recipient population, the status of one of its statements, or the status of one of its findings — that is, after a recipient is created or reclassified, after a qualification request is verified, after a hygiene sweep, after a finding is raised or its resolution accepted, after an audit report is issued, and after a statement is certified.

The derivation is a five-clause ladder evaluated from top to bottom, and **the first matching clause wins**:

1. If the publication's count of open critical findings, `open_critical_findings_count`, is greater than zero, the posture is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if any circulation statement of the publication is in status `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if the publication's hygiene ratio `undeliverable_pct` is greater than or equal to its `undeliverable_restrict_pct` policy threshold, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if the publication's count of open material findings, `open_material_findings_count`, is greater than zero, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

`open_material_findings_count` is the number of audit findings of severity `MATERIAL` whose status is `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_REOPENED`, counted across every finding reachable from this publication — that is, through each engagement's `statement_id` to a statement whose `publication_id` is this publication. `open_critical_findings_count` is the same walk with severity `CRITICAL`. A finding that has reached `ACCEPTED_RESOLVED` counts toward neither.

Posture has two distinct consequences. `FROZEN` blocks certification of a statement outright, reported as `ERR_PUBLICATION_FROZEN` with HTTP 409 — with exactly one exception, described in §3.9: a statement that names the offending `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` statement as the predecessor it restates may be certified despite the freeze, because certifying it is the only way out of the freeze. `RESTRICTED` never blocks certification by itself. But *any* change of posture, in either direction and between any two values, marks every `DRAFT` statement of the publication as having a stale breakdown, which does block certification until the breakdown is recomputed.

### 2.10. The Copies-on-File Ledger

A publication maintains a live ledger of the copies on its recipient file, recomputed whenever a recipient is added, a recipient is reclassified, a hygiene sweep runs, or a qualification request is verified. No recipient row is ever deleted, so the ledger's grand total moves only when recipients are added.

`copies_on_file_total` is the sum of `copies` over every recipient of the publication, in any qualification status and any delivery status. Three class buckets partition the same population by circulation class: `paid_copies_on_file` sums `copies` over recipients whose `subscription_class` is `PAID`, `qualified_non_paid_copies_on_file` over those whose class is `QUALIFIED_NON_PAID`, and `controlled_copies_on_file` over those whose class is `CONTROLLED`. Three delivery buckets partition it again by deliverability: `deliverable_copies` sums `copies` over recipients whose `delivery_status` is `DELIVERABLE`, `undeliverable_copies` over those that are `UNDELIVERABLE`, and `removed_copies` over those that are `REMOVED`.

Two conserved quantities hold after every mutation and after every compensating mutation, and they are the integrity test for this ledger. On the class axis, `copies_on_file_total` always equals the sum of `paid_copies_on_file`, `qualified_non_paid_copies_on_file` and `controlled_copies_on_file`. On the delivery axis, `copies_on_file_total` always equals the sum of `deliverable_copies`, `undeliverable_copies` and `removed_copies`. Reclassifying a recipient moves its copies from one class bucket to another and leaves the grand total untouched; a hygiene sweep moves copies from the deliverable bucket to the undeliverable or removed bucket and likewise leaves the grand total untouched.

Riding on top is the hygiene ratio `undeliverable_pct`: the sum of `undeliverable_copies` and `removed_copies`, multiplied by 100, divided by `copies_on_file_total`, and rounded half up to two decimal places. When `copies_on_file_total` is zero the ratio is exactly 0.00 rather than undefined.

### 2.11. Make-Good Credit Conservation

Each advertiser contract carries a four-bucket credit ledger, re-derived whenever a make-good is created by guarantee settlement, offered, accepted, waived, or voided by the restatement cascade. `makegood_credit_pool_cents` is the ceiling on all credit the contract can ever carry and equals the contract's `insertion_count` multiplied by its `gross_rate_cents_per_insertion`. `makegood_credit_committed_cents` sums `credit_cents` across the contract's obligations whose status is `PENDING`, `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`. `makegood_credit_waived_cents` sums `credit_cents` across those whose status is `WAIVED`. `makegood_credit_available_cents` is the pool minus the committed sum minus the waived sum.

Obligations in status `VOID` contribute to no bucket at all, and that exclusion is exactly the mechanism by which a voided obligation returns its cents to the available bucket with zero drift. The money-axis conserved quantity — invariant after every forward action and after every compensating action — is that `makegood_credit_pool_cents` always equals the sum of `makegood_credit_committed_cents`, `makegood_credit_waived_cents` and `makegood_credit_available_cents`.

### 2.12. Rejection Precedence

Many operations are guarded by several checks at once, and the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract: a request that violates two rules must report the higher-precedence one. The precedence ladder, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Existence**, resolved left to right along the path and then across body foreign keys, in the order given in §2.6, yielding the corresponding not-found error.
4. **Ownership.** For an `AdvertiserContact` acting on a make-good obligation, `X-User-ID` must equal the obligation's `contract_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_OWNER`.
5. **Request shape.** Required fields present, integer minimums respected, percentage fields inside 0.00 through 100.00, timestamps parsing as ISO-8601 UTC — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. Applies to every write endpoint.
6. **Enum membership.** Every supplied enum value is a member of its declared set — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` with HTTP 422. Applies to every write endpoint and to every list endpoint that accepts an enum-valued filter.
7. **Stale-version rejection.** The two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.7, yielding `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIBER_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
8. **State preconditions**, in this internal order: the statement-must-be-`DRAFT` check (`ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`), then the request-must-be-`UNDER_REVIEW` check (`ERR_REQUEST_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`), then the general "this trigger is not declared from the entity's current state" check (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), then the "entity is already terminal" check (`ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`), then the sample-exhaustion check (`ERR_SAMPLE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), then the live-findings check on report issuance (`ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_UNRESOLVED`). All six carry HTTP 409.
9. **Separation-of-duties and compliance gates on certification**, in this order: preparer-may-not-certify (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), then frozen publication (`ERR_PUBLICATION_FROZEN`), then stale breakdown (`ERR_BREAKDOWN_STALE`). All three carry HTTP 409.
10. **Evidentiary referential integrity**: purged source document (`ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_PURGED`), then source document belonging to a different recipient (`ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_SUBSCRIBER_MISMATCH`), then a predecessor statement that is not awaiting restatement (`ERR_RESTATEMENT_NOT_REQUIRED`).
11. **Temporal constraints**, in this order: inverted or empty ranges (`ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`), qualification-window collision (`ERR_QUALIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), statement-period overlap (`ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), fieldwork-window conflict (`ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), a period or measurement window that has not closed (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`), and a retention deadline that has not elapsed (`ERR_RETENTION_NOT_ELAPSED`).
12. **Qualification and policy constraints**, in this order: incomplete rule set (`ERR_QUALIFICATION_RULES_INCOMPLETE`), then — and this ordering is deliberate and explained in §3.4 — the score threshold (`ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`) *before* the mandatory-criterion gate (`ERR_MANDATORY_CRITERION_NOT_MET`), then the information-request cap (`ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED`), then the one-rule-per-dimension uniqueness check (`ERR_RULE_DIMENSION_EXISTS`), then the paid-requires-payment check (`ERR_PAID_REQUIRES_PAYMENT`), then the certified-source check on digital validation (`ERR_DIGITAL_SOURCE_UNCERTIFIED`).
13. **Fiscal checks, last of all**: the make-good credit pool ceiling (`ERR_MAKEGOOD_POOL_EXCEEDED`), then the nothing-to-settle check (`ERR_NO_SHORTFALL_TO_SETTLE`).

A useful consequence of placing the fiscal family last: a settlement request that both exceeds a credit pool and targets a statement in the wrong state reports the state failure, not the fiscal one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Publication and Its Qualification Policy

A `CirculationManager` brings a title into the system by registering it. The registration carries the publication's `name` — between 1 and 200 characters, required — its issue `frequency`, which is one of `WEEKLY`, `MONTHLY` or `QUARTERLY`, and the `industry_code` naming the vertical the title serves, between 1 and 40 characters and likewise required. Everything else on the registration is policy, and every policy knob has a default so that a publisher can register a title and tune it later.

Six policy values govern the publication's whole life. `qualification_validity_months` is how long a verified qualification stays good before it lapses; it defaults to 36 — three years, the customary trade-audit horizon — and must be between 1 and 60. `renewal_grace_days` is the run-up window before a recipient's qualification expiry inside which a renewal verification is allowed to truncate the still-running window instead of being rejected as a collision; it defaults to 90, that is, the final quarter of the validity period, and must be between 0 and 365. `source_doc_retention_months` is how long a piece of source documentation must be kept before it may lawfully be purged; it defaults to 36 and must be between 1 and 120. `undeliverable_restrict_pct` is the hygiene ratio at or above which the publication's posture becomes `RESTRICTED`; it defaults to 5.00 — one bad address in twenty — and lies between 0.00 and 100.00 at two decimals. `materiality_pct` is the discrepancy at or above which an audit report forces a restatement; it defaults to 2.00, also two decimals in the 0.00 to 100.00 range. `controlled_cap_pct` is the largest share of a statement's qualified circulation that controlled copies may occupy; it defaults to 25.00 and, because a cap of 100 percent would make the allowance arithmetic of §3.7 meaningless, is restricted to the range 0.00 through 99.00.

Registration is a clean slate. Every copy counter — the grand total, the three class buckets and the three delivery buckets described in §2.10 — starts at 0, the hygiene ratio `undeliverable_pct` starts at exactly 0.00, both open-findings counters start at 0, `audit_posture` starts at `OPEN`, and `version` starts at 1. Registering a publication is a `CirculationManager`'s exclusive privilege; all four operator roles may read it afterwards, and the single-publication read is where a client obtains the `version` it will later have to echo back as `expected_publication_version`.

Qualified circulation is defined by exactly three criteria, and each is expressed as its own rule row beneath the publication. A rule names its `dimension` — one of `JOB_FUNCTION`, `INDUSTRY` or `PURCHASING_AUTHORITY` — together with `allowed_values`, a list of between 1 and 20 non-empty strings that score the rule's points, `points`, an integer between 0 and 100 awarded when the recipient's attestation on that dimension is a member of the allowed list, and `is_mandatory`, a boolean defaulting to false. Membership is exact, case-sensitive string equality: an attested value of `"engineering"` does not match an allowed value of `"ENGINEERING"`. A publication may hold at most one rule per dimension; a second attempt on a dimension the publication already defines is rejected with `ERR_RULE_DIMENSION_EXISTS` and HTTP 409. Rules are read forward only — a rule created or a `points` weight chosen today is read by every scoring evaluation that happens after it, and no already-stored score is retroactively recomputed. Rules can be listed for a publication and the listing narrowed with a `dimension` filter.

Mandatory dimensions are the publisher's veto. When a rule is marked mandatory, a qualification request that scores zero on that dimension cannot qualify the recipient no matter how high its total climbs — a title serving plant engineers can insist that the job-function attestation land inside its allowed list even if industry and purchasing authority together would clear the threshold.

### 3.2. The Recipient File: Enrolment, Classification and Evidence

Adding a recipient to a publication's file is a `CirculationManager` operation, and the payload carries the `publication_id` the recipient belongs to, the subscribing organisation's `company_name` and delivery `contact_email` (each 1 to 200 characters and required), the recipient's `job_function_code` and `industry_code` on file (each 1 to 40 characters and required), the recipient's `purchasing_authority_level` — one of `NONE`, `RECOMMENDS`, `SPECIFIES` or `APPROVES` — the `subscription_class` under which the recipient's copies will be reported, and `copies`, the number of copies served per issue, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. `paid_amount_cents` records what was actually collected; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative.

The circulation class is the reporting axis of the whole system: `PAID` for copies the recipient bought, `QUALIFIED_NON_PAID` for copies given free to a recipient who meets the qualification criteria, and `CONTROLLED` for copies the publisher directs to a recipient at its own discretion. A `PAID` classification is a factual claim about money, so it is enforced: a recipient created as `PAID`, or reclassified into `PAID`, must end up with `paid_amount_cents` of at least 1, counting either the value supplied on the request or the value already stored on the row. A `PAID` recipient with nothing collected is rejected with `ERR_PAID_REQUIRES_PAYMENT` and HTTP 422.

A newly enrolled recipient starts in the only combination the system considers neutral: `qualification_status` is `UNQUALIFIED`, `delivery_status` is `DELIVERABLE`, `undeliverable_count` is 0, `qualification_score` is 0, and both `qualified_at` and `qualification_expires_at` are null because nothing has been verified yet. `version` starts at 1. Enrolment immediately re-derives the publication's copies ledger — the grand total, the class bucket matching the recipient's class, the deliverable bucket and the hygiene ratio — re-derives `audit_posture`, and marks every `DRAFT` statement of the publication as having a stale breakdown, because a statement drafted before this recipient existed no longer reflects the file it claims to describe. The publication's own counters have moved, so by §2.4 the publication is one of the resources this operation mutates and its `version` advances by 1 as well — which is precisely why a client that enrolled a recipient after reading a publication must re-read it before quoting `expected_publication_version`.

Reclassification moves an existing recipient between the three classes. The payload carries the new `subscription_class` and, when the target is `PAID`, may carry `paid_amount_cents`. Reclassification is precisely compensating on the ledger: the old class bucket falls by the recipient's `copies` and the new class bucket rises by the same amount, so `copies_on_file_total` is untouched and the class-axis conserved quantity of §2.10 still holds. Every `DRAFT` statement of the publication is marked stale, and the recipient's `version` advances by 1. The publication's class buckets moved too, so its `version` also advances by 1, exactly as on enrolment.

Deliverability is tracked separately and deliberately lazily. Recording a returned or bounced copy against a recipient increments its `undeliverable_count` by 1 and does nothing else — the payload may carry an optional `reason_code` for the operator's own records. Neither `delivery_status` nor `qualification_status` moves, and the publication's delivery buckets do not move either. The counter is a signal, not an action; it becomes an action only when a hygiene sweep evaluates it against a threshold, as §3.5 describes. The recipient's `version` advances by 1 and the updated recipient is returned. This lazy design is what makes the two status dimensions genuinely independent: a recipient can be `QUALIFIED` and `UNDELIVERABLE` at the same time, and neither dimension's rules ever read the other's value except at the two explicit crossing points named in §3.4 and §3.5.

Evidence lives beneath the recipient. A source document is attached with a `doc_type` naming the evidence class — `SIGNED_REQUEST_CARD`, `WEB_FORM_CAPTURE`, `TELEPHONE_SCRIPT_LOG` or `EMAIL_CONFIRMATION` — a `captured_at` instant recording when the evidence was taken from the recipient, and a `storage_ref` between 1 and 120 characters locating the retained image or transcript in the archive. The `captured_at` instant may not be later than the current logical clock; a future capture instant is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On write, the server derives `retention_expires_at` as `captured_at` advanced by the owning recipient's publication's `source_doc_retention_months`, sets `purge_status` to `RETAINED`, and leaves `purged_at` null. Both `CirculationManager` and `QualificationSpecialist` may attach evidence; the documents of one recipient can be listed and filtered by `purge_status`.

Purging is the end of an evidence row's useful life, and it is guarded by the retention clock. A purge may only proceed when the current logical clock has reached or passed the document's `retention_expires_at`; an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_ELAPSED` and HTTP 409. A successful purge — a `CirculationManager` operation carrying an optional `purge_reason` — sets `purge_status` to `PURGED` and `purged_at` to the current logical clock. Crucially the row survives as a tombstone rather than disappearing, because an audited recount has to be able to say "the evidence existed and was lawfully destroyed" rather than "no evidence was ever recorded". The consequence bites in two places: a purged document can no longer back a qualification request or a verification, and any audit-verified breakdown recomputed after the purge silently drops the recipients that document was the evidence for.

### 3.3. Qualification Requests and the Review Cycle

A qualification request is the paperwork trail behind one recipient's claim to qualified status. Opening one — an operation shared by `CirculationManager` and `QualificationSpecialist` — carries the `subscriber_id` of the recipient, the `source_document_id` of the evidence that backs the claim, the `request_channel` the request arrived through (`DIRECT_MAIL`, `WEB`, `TELEPHONE` or `EMAIL`), the `signed_at` instant at which the recipient signed or confirmed it, the three attested values `attested_job_function_code`, `attested_industry_code` and `attested_purchasing_authority`, and the `expected_subscriber_version` demanded by §2.7. The two code attestations are 1 to 40 characters each and required; the authority attestation is one of `NONE`, `RECOMMENDS`, `SPECIFIES` or `APPROVES`. As with a source document's capture instant, `signed_at` may not lie in the future of the logical clock, and a future value is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The cited evidence must be the recipient's own and must still exist. If the named source document's `subscriber_id` is not the `subscriber_id` on the request, the create fails with `ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_SUBSCRIBER_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422; if the document's `purge_status` is `PURGED`, it fails with `ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_PURGED`, also HTTP 422. A recipient whose `delivery_status` is `REMOVED` cannot normally be the subject of a new request — the one exception being a recipient whose `qualification_status` is `REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`, which is precisely the state a removed recipient is put into so that requalification remains possible.

Opening a request moves the recipient's qualification dimension to `PENDING_QUALIFICATION`. All four non-pending qualification states are legal starting points, and they mean different things: `UNQUALIFIED → PENDING_QUALIFICATION` is a first-time claim, `LAPSED → PENDING_QUALIFICATION` is a revival after expiry, `REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED → PENDING_QUALIFICATION` is the mandated re-attestation after a delivery failure, and `QUALIFIED → PENDING_QUALIFICATION` is a renewal cycle. The renewal case is the delicate one, and the rule is explicit: `qualified_at` and `qualification_expires_at` are left exactly as they are. The recipient's running qualification window keeps counting toward every statement produced while the renewal is in flight, and only a completed verification replaces it. In every case the new request is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `info_request_count` 0, `computed_score` 0, and the recipient's `version` advances by 1.

Creation also cleans up after itself. Any earlier request for the same recipient still sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` is closed out on the spot: its `status` becomes `REJECTED`, its `decided_at` is set to the current logical clock, and its `decision_reason` is set to the literal string `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_REQUEST` rather than to any operator text. One recipient therefore never has two live requests, and the three supersede paths — `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and `INFO_REQUESTED → REJECTED` — are all system-driven, with no actor and no separate call.

Review is the `QualificationSpecialist`'s alone. Taking a `SUBMITTED` request into review moves it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and evaluates the scoring model of §3.4 at the current logical clock, storing the result in `computed_score`. An optional `review_note` may accompany the call. Attempting to review a request that is not `SUBMITTED` — one already `UNDER_REVIEW`, or sitting in `INFO_REQUESTED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and HTTP 409; attempting it on a request that has already reached `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, also HTTP 409.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the specialist has three exits, and one of them is a loop. Requesting further information moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`, requires an `info_request_note` explaining what is missing, and increments `info_request_count` by 1. The loop is bounded at two round trips: the increment is only permitted while `info_request_count` is strictly below 2, so a third information request on the same paperwork is rejected with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 409. Resubmission — shared again between `CirculationManager` and `QualificationSpecialist` — moves the request `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and may restate any of `signed_at`, `request_channel`, `attested_job_function_code`, `attested_industry_code` and `attested_purchasing_authority`; each supplied value overwrites the stored attestation, and omitted values are left alone. Resubmission resets `computed_score` to 0, because the stored score described paperwork that no longer necessarily exists; the score is only recomputed when the request next enters review. `info_request_count` is deliberately *not* reset, so it survives the whole cycle and the cap counts round trips rather than consecutive attempts. Because `computed_score` is refreshed at every entry into `UNDER_REVIEW` and the clock keeps moving, a request that loops `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` several times can bleed points to the staleness penalty of §3.4 and end up below the threshold it once cleared.

The third exit is refusal. Rejecting a request requires a `decision_reason` and is permitted only from `UNDER_REVIEW`; on any other status the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` and HTTP 409, and that same error guards information requests and verifications. A rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED`, `decided_at` to the current logical clock, and `decision_reason` from the body. What happens to the recipient depends entirely on whether it had a qualification window running, and there are exactly three outcomes. If `qualified_at` is null — the recipient had never been qualified — the recipient falls back `PENDING_QUALIFICATION → UNQUALIFIED`. If `qualified_at` is set and `qualification_expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock, the refused renewal changes nothing about the still-valid window: the recipient returns `PENDING_QUALIFICATION → QUALIFIED` with `qualified_at` and `qualification_expires_at` untouched. If `qualified_at` is set and `qualification_expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock, the window has already run out and the refusal is what finally ends it: the recipient moves `PENDING_QUALIFICATION → LAPSED`.

Requests are readable individually — that is where a client sees `computed_score`, `info_request_count`, `decided_at`, `decision_reason` and the effective window — and listable with `subscriber_id`, `source_document_id` and `status` filters, which is the only way to walk from a recipient or a source document back to the requests that cite them.

### 3.4. Scoring, Verification, Renewal Windows and Reinstatement

The qualification score is a weighted-criteria model over exactly one request and the three rule rows of that request's recipient's publication; no other request contributes to it, and no other publication's rules are consulted. It is evaluated in two places: when a request enters review, and again at verification time, re-evaluated against the current logical clock immediately before the threshold test is applied. That second evaluation is not a formality — verification of a request reviewed months earlier can and does produce a lower number.

The model is built in five steps. **First**, each of the three dimensions is matched against its rule: the recipient's publication is expected to hold one rule per dimension, and the request's `attested_job_function_code` is tested against the `JOB_FUNCTION` rule's `allowed_values`, its `attested_industry_code` against the `INDUSTRY` rule's list, and its `attested_purchasing_authority` against the `PURCHASING_AUTHORITY` rule's list. A dimension scores that rule's `points` when the attested value is an exact member of the list, and scores 0 otherwise. **Second**, the base score is the sum of the three dimension scores. **Third**, the age of the signature is measured as the number of *complete* calendar months from the request's `signed_at` to the current logical clock, discarding any partial month; a signature dated the first of July 2025 evaluated on the first of August 2026 is thirteen complete months old. **Fourth**, two clock-driven adjustments are applied. A channel bonus rewards fresh paperwork on the channels the auditing bureaus regard as strongest: a request whose `request_channel` is `DIRECT_MAIL` earns 10 bonus points, and one whose channel is `WEB` or `EMAIL` earns 5, but only while the signature is no more than twelve complete months old — inside one year. A `TELEPHONE` request earns no bonus at any age, and no channel earns a bonus once the signature is older than twelve months. Against that, a staleness penalty applies once the signature passes twelve complete months: it is 5 points for each complete month beyond the twelfth, and it is capped at 30 points, so it stops growing after the signature is eighteen complete months old. **Fifth**, the base score plus the channel bonus minus the staleness penalty is clamped into the range 0 through 100 — never below zero, never above one hundred — and the clamped value is `computed_score`.

Qualification then turns on two tests whose order is pinned. A request qualifies its recipient when `computed_score` is at least 70 **and** every rule marked `is_mandatory` scored more than zero points for this request's attestations. The threshold is checked first: a request that is both below 70 and short on a mandatory dimension reports `ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` with HTTP 422, not the mandatory-criterion error. Only a request that has cleared 70 can ever surface `ERR_MANDATORY_CRITERION_NOT_MET`, also HTTP 422 — which makes that error mean exactly one thing: "the arithmetic was good enough, but you missed a criterion the publisher will not compromise on".

Verification is a `QualificationSpecialist` operation, may carry an optional `decision_reason`, and enforces six preconditions. The request must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. The source document named by `source_document_id` must still have `purge_status` `RETAINED`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_SOURCE_DOCUMENT_PURGED` — a qualification cannot be created out of destroyed evidence. The publication must hold a rule row for all three of `JOB_FUNCTION`, `INDUSTRY` and `PURCHASING_AUTHORITY`; a missing dimension fails with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_RULES_INCOMPLETE` and HTTP 409, and this check runs before scoring, since a partial policy cannot produce a meaningful number. Then come the threshold and mandatory tests in the pinned order above. Finally the new qualification window must not collide with the window already running.

The collision rule reads on closed intervals. The window the verification would open runs from the current logical clock to that instant advanced by the publication's `qualification_validity_months`; the window already running on the recipient runs from `qualified_at` to `qualification_expires_at`. Both intervals include their endpoints, so touching at a single instant counts as intersecting. Any intersection is refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409 — unless the renewal grace period is open, meaning the current logical clock has reached or passed `qualification_expires_at` moved back by the publication's `renewal_grace_days`. Inside the grace period, the collision is not an error but the expected shape of a renewal, and the verification proceeds by *truncating* the outgoing window: the predecessor `VERIFIED` request's `effective_to` is pulled back to the current logical clock, so the two windows abut instead of overlapping and no copy is ever counted under two qualifications at once.

A successful verification writes on both sides. On the request: `status` becomes `VERIFIED`, `decided_at` is set to the current logical clock, `effective_from` is set to that same instant, and `effective_to` is set to `effective_from` advanced by the publication's `qualification_validity_months`. On the recipient: `qualification_status` moves `PENDING_QUALIFICATION → QUALIFIED`, `qualification_score` is copied from the request's `computed_score`, `qualified_at` is set to the current logical clock, and `qualification_expires_at` is set to `qualified_at` advanced by `qualification_validity_months`. The recipient's identity fields on file are overwritten with what was actually attested — `job_function_code` takes `attested_job_function_code`, `industry_code` takes `attested_industry_code`, and `purchasing_authority_level` takes `attested_purchasing_authority` — because the audited file must say what the recipient said, not what a data-entry operator once typed.

Verification is also the **only** path back to deliverability, and this is the single point where the qualification dimension writes the delivery dimension. When the recipient was `UNDELIVERABLE` at the moment of verification, `delivery_status` moves `UNDELIVERABLE → DELIVERABLE` and `undeliverable_count` is reset to 0; the publication's `undeliverable_copies` bucket falls by the recipient's `copies` and its `deliverable_copies` bucket rises by the same amount. When the recipient was `REMOVED`, `delivery_status` moves `REMOVED → DELIVERABLE`, `undeliverable_count` is likewise reset to 0, `removed_copies` falls by the recipient's `copies` and `deliverable_copies` rises by the same amount; in both cases `copies_on_file_total` is unchanged, and the hygiene ratio and `audit_posture` are re-derived. There is no other reinstatement mechanism anywhere in the system: a removed recipient comes back by re-attesting and being verified, or not at all.

Every verification finishes by re-deriving the publication's copies ledger, its hygiene ratio and its `audit_posture`, advancing the publication's `version` by 1, and marking every `DRAFT` statement of the publication as having a stale breakdown — a statement drafted before a recipient joined the qualified population must be recomputed before it can be certified.

### 3.5. List Hygiene: The Two Sweeps

Undeliverable counters accumulate quietly and change nothing on their own. A `CirculationManager` turns them into decisions by executing a hygiene sweep against a publication. The request names the `publication_id`, the `run_type` — either `UNDELIVERABLE_SWEEP` or `EXPIRY_SWEEP` — and, for the undeliverable variant, an optional `undeliverable_threshold`, the bounce count at or above which a recipient is dropped from the list; the threshold defaults to 2, meaning two returned copies are enough to remove an address, and must be at least 1. A sweep produces an immutable execution record: `executed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `posture_before` captures the publication's `audit_posture` immediately before the sweep and `posture_after` captures it immediately after, and five counters record what the sweep did. Sweep records are listed for a publication newest first, and the listing may be narrowed with a `run_type` filter.

An `UNDELIVERABLE_SWEEP` walks the publication's recipients and acts on the delivery dimension in two tiers. A recipient with at least one bounce but strictly fewer than the threshold is flagged rather than dropped: `delivery_status` moves `DELIVERABLE → UNDELIVERABLE` and `undeliverable_flagged_count` is incremented. The publication's `deliverable_copies` bucket falls by that recipient's `copies` and its `undeliverable_copies` bucket rises by the same amount, leaving `copies_on_file_total` unchanged. The recipient's `qualification_status` is untouched — a still-qualified recipient can be undeliverable, and that combination is exactly what the two-dimension design exists to represent.

A recipient whose `undeliverable_count` has reached or passed the threshold is dropped, and the drop has a different arithmetic signature depending on where the recipient started. From `DELIVERABLE`, `delivery_status` moves `DELIVERABLE → REMOVED`, `removed_count` is incremented, `deliverable_copies` falls by the recipient's `copies` and `removed_copies` rises by the same amount; because copies have crossed from the clean bucket into a bucket that counts against hygiene, the publication's `undeliverable_pct` and `audit_posture` are re-derived. From `UNDELIVERABLE`, `delivery_status` moves `UNDELIVERABLE → REMOVED`, `removed_count` is incremented, `undeliverable_copies` falls by the recipient's `copies` and `removed_copies` rises by the same amount — and here both `copies_on_file_total` *and* `undeliverable_pct` are unchanged, because the hygiene ratio counts undeliverable and removed copies together and the copies simply moved between two buckets that both sit in its numerator.

Dropping a recipient also drives the qualification dimension, and this is the second and last crossing point between the two dimensions. A recipient dropped by the threshold and standing in `QUALIFIED` moves `QUALIFIED → REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`; one standing in `LAPSED` moves `LAPSED → REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`; one standing in `PENDING_QUALIFICATION` moves `PENDING_QUALIFICATION → REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Each of the three increments `requalification_required_count` and sets the same row's `delivery_status` to `REMOVED`. The pending case has a deliberate wrinkle: the in-flight qualification request keeps its own status untouched, so it may still be reviewed and verified, and that verification will reinstate delivery exactly as §3.4 describes. A recipient standing in `UNQUALIFIED` when it is dropped keeps `qualification_status` `UNQUALIFIED` — there is nothing to requalify — while its `delivery_status` still becomes `REMOVED`.

An `EXPIRY_SWEEP` touches only the qualification dimension and only one transition: a recipient in `QUALIFIED` whose `qualification_expires_at` has been reached or passed by the current logical clock moves `QUALIFIED → LAPSED`, and `lapsed_count` is incremented. `delivery_status` is explicitly left alone; an expired qualification says nothing about whether the post office can still find the recipient. This is the mechanism that gives the clock its teeth — advancing the clock past an expiry instant does nothing at all until an expiry sweep is run.

Every sweep finishes by re-deriving the publication's copies ledger, hygiene ratio and `audit_posture`. When `posture_after` differs from `posture_before` in either direction, every `DRAFT` statement of the publication is marked as having a stale breakdown, and `statements_invalidated_count` reports how many drafts were marked. When the posture did not move, no draft is marked by the sweep and `statements_invalidated_count` is 0.

### 3.6. Digital Audience Measurement

Trade publishers sell print circulation and digital reach side by side, and auditors treat the two with very different suspicion. A `CirculationManager` records a closed measurement window against a publication, supplying `window_start` and `window_end`, the `website_unique_visitors` counted in the window, the `newsletter_sends` dispatched and the `newsletter_unique_opens` measured, an `overlap_pct` estimating what share of newsletter openers also appear among the website visitors, and a `source` naming the measurement's provenance — either `INTERNAL_ANALYTICS` or `THIRD_PARTY_CERTIFIED`. `window_start` must be strictly before `window_end`, or the record is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE` and HTTP 422. The window must also have closed: if `window_end` lies after the current logical clock, the record is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, also HTTP 422. All three counts must be at least 0, and `newsletter_unique_opens` may not exceed `newsletter_sends` — a violation of either is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `overlap_pct` defaults to 15.00 and is a two-decimal value between 0.00 and 100.00.

Two reach figures are computed and stored on every write, and the distinction between them is the whole reason the field exists twice. `combined_reach_gross` is simply `website_unique_visitors` plus `newsletter_unique_opens`, at face value — the "total digital touchpoints" number a sales team likes to quote, which double-counts every reader who both visited the site and opened the newsletter. `combined_reach_deduplicated` discounts the newsletter side first: the newsletter opens are multiplied by the complement of the overlap share — that is, by one hundred minus `overlap_pct`, divided by one hundred — and that product is rounded half up to a whole person *before* `website_unique_visitors` is added to it. With 48,000 website visitors, 12,500 unique newsletter opens and an overlap of 15.00 percent, the deduplicated figure is 58,625 while the gross figure is 60,500. Only the deduplicated figure is ever admissible into a circulation statement.

A recorded snapshot enters the world in status `RECORDED` and is adjudicated by a `CirculationAuditor`, who supplies a `decision` of either `VALIDATE` or `REJECT`. A `VALIDATE` decision moves the snapshot `RECORDED → VALIDATED` and stamps `validated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, making its `combined_reach_deduplicated` eligible for a statement's verified digital reach on the next breakdown computation. A `REJECT` decision moves it `RECORDED → REJECTED`, also stamping `validated_by_user_id`, and the snapshot never contributes to any statement. The provenance gate is absolute on the validate side only: a `VALIDATE` decision is permitted solely when `source` is `THIRD_PARTY_CERTIFIED`, and attempting to validate an `INTERNAL_ANALYTICS` snapshot is rejected with `ERR_DIGITAL_SOURCE_UNCERTIFIED` and HTTP 422. Uncertified analytics may be recorded and may be rejected, but never validated into audited circulation. Both `VALIDATED` and `REJECTED` are terminal: a second adjudication of either is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Snapshots are discovered only through their publication's listing, which accepts a `status` filter.

### 3.7. Producing, Recomputing and Certifying a Circulation Statement

A circulation statement is the publisher's periodic sworn account of who receives the title. A `CirculationManager` opens one for a closed reporting period, supplying the `publication_id`, the period boundaries `period_start` and `period_end`, an optional `basis`, an optional `restates_statement_id`, and the mandatory `expected_publication_version` of §2.7. The period is half-open in the sense that matters for overlap — `period_start` is inclusive and `period_end` is exclusive — and `period_start` must fall strictly before `period_end`, or the create is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`. The period must also be over: if `period_end` lies after the current logical clock, the create is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. No statement may be produced for a period that has not closed.

`basis` selects which of two counting doctrines produces the copy figures, and it defaults to `AS_OF_PERIOD_END`, the publisher's own view. The alternative, `AUDIT_VERIFIED`, is the evidence-gated view used for audited statements and restatements. `statement_type` is derived from the basis rather than supplied: it is `AUDITED` when `basis` is `AUDIT_VERIFIED` and `PUBLISHER_STATEMENT` otherwise, and it is re-derived whenever a recompute switches the basis. `restates_statement_id` is null on an original statement and is set only when this statement is the replacement for a predecessor the audit condemned; when supplied, it must name a statement of the *same* publication whose `status` is `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, and anything else — a statement of another publication, or one in any other status — is rejected with `ERR_RESTATEMENT_NOT_REQUIRED` and HTTP 409.

**Eligibility is judged against the reporting period, never against the current clock.** This is what makes a statement reproducible months later. A recipient contributes its `copies` to the statement when all of the following hold: it belongs to the statement's publication; its `delivery_status` is `DELIVERABLE`; its `qualified_at` is not null; its `qualified_at` falls strictly before `period_end`; and its `qualification_expires_at` is at or after `period_end`. Note carefully what is *not* consulted: the recipient's `qualification_status` enum plays no part whatsoever. The two date fields decide inclusion, which is exactly why a recipient that a later expiry sweep marked `LAPSED` still counts toward a statement whose period closed while the window was live.

Eligible recipients are then summed by class. `paid_copies` is the sum of `copies` over eligible recipients whose `subscription_class` is `PAID`; `qualified_non_paid_copies` is the same sum over those whose class is `QUALIFIED_NON_PAID`; and the raw controlled figure is the same sum over those whose class is `CONTROLLED`.

The raw controlled figure is not admitted wholesale. Audit bureaus cap the share of qualified circulation that a publisher may fill with copies it merely directs, and this publication expresses that cap as `controlled_cap_pct`. The published rule reads "controlled copies may not exceed `controlled_cap_pct` of total qualified copies" — and since total qualified copies itself includes the admitted controlled copies, the rule looks self-referential. **The evaluation order is pinned to the closed form below, and it is derived from the pre-controlled core only, never iteratively from a running total and never as a percentage of the core.** First, the core is the sum of `paid_copies` and `qualified_non_paid_copies`. Second, the controlled allowance is the core multiplied by `controlled_cap_pct`, divided by one hundred minus `controlled_cap_pct`, with the quotient truncated down to a whole number of copies. Third, `controlled_copies_included` is the smaller of the raw controlled figure and that allowance. Fourth, `controlled_copies_excess` is the raw controlled figure minus `controlled_copies_included`; those copies are real and are reported, but they sit outside qualified circulation. The consequence to check when the allowance binds is that `controlled_copies_included` divided by `total_qualified_copies` equals `controlled_cap_pct` exactly — a core of 900 copies under a 25.00 percent cap admits 300 controlled copies out of a 1,200-copy total, which is 25.00 percent on the nose. Truncating the allowance rather than rounding it means any remainder copy always lands in `controlled_copies_excess`.

`total_qualified_copies` is then `paid_copies` plus `qualified_non_paid_copies` plus `controlled_copies_included` — the single number the whole system exists to defend. Alongside it, `digital_reach_verified` sums `combined_reach_deduplicated` across every digital audience snapshot of the same publication whose `status` is `VALIDATED` and whose measurement window sits *fully inside* the reporting period, meaning its `window_start` is at or after `period_start` and its `window_end` is at or before `period_end`; a snapshot straddling a period boundary contributes to neither period. `undeliverable_pct_at_close` snapshots the publication's live `undeliverable_pct` as of this evaluation, so the statement records the hygiene of the file it was drawn from. Every evaluation of the breakdown clears the statement's stale flag: `breakdown_stale` is set to false.

The two bases differ in exactly one respect, an evidence gate stacked on top of the eligibility test above. Under `AS_OF_PERIOD_END` there is no gate: the publisher counts every deliverable recipient whose qualification window spans `period_end`, whether or not retained documentation still backs it. Under `AUDIT_VERIFIED`, a recipient additionally needs a qualification request of its own in status `VERIFIED` whose cited source document has `purge_status` `RETAINED` **and** whose `retention_expires_at` is at or after `period_end`. Recipients whose evidence was purged, or whose evidence would already have expired by the close of the period, drop out of the population. Because they drop out, the core shrinks — and because the controlled allowance is derived from the core, a smaller core re-derives a *smaller* allowance. The same recipient file therefore yields two genuinely different totals under the two bases, and the audit-verified total is not merely the publisher's total minus a few copies.

A statement is born in `DRAFT`, with `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, `breakdown_stale` false, and the publication's `version` advanced by 1. `declared_total_copies` is not written by creation or by any recompute; it holds its initial value of 0 until certification freezes it. A `CirculationManager` may recompute a `DRAFT` statement at any time, optionally supplying a new `basis` to switch doctrines; the recompute re-runs the whole breakdown including the controlled allowance, re-derives `statement_type`, and clears `breakdown_stale` back to false. Recompute is the prescribed cure for every staleness marker planted by §3.2, §3.4, §3.5 and §2.9. Both recompute and certification require the statement to still be in `DRAFT`; on a statement that has left `DRAFT` either is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DRAFT` and HTTP 409, and on one that has reached `AUDIT_CLEARED` or `SUPERSEDED` the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Certification is the moment the publisher's assertion becomes binding, and it is shared between `CirculationManager` and `PublisherExecutive` with an optional `certification_note`. Four gates stand in front of it, evaluated in this order. **First**, separation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the statement's `prepared_by_user_id`. The operator who assembled the numbers may never be the one who swears to them, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409. **Second**, the publication's `audit_posture` must not be `FROZEN` — unless this statement's `restates_statement_id` names a same-publication statement in `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, which is the sanctioned escape hatch; otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PUBLICATION_FROZEN`, HTTP 409. **Third**, `breakdown_stale` must be false; a statement whose underlying population or audit posture moved since the last breakdown computation must be recomputed first, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_BREAKDOWN_STALE`, HTTP 409. **Fourth**, periods may not overlap: the statement's half-open period must not intersect the period of any other statement of the same publication in `DRAFT`, `FILED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `FINDINGS_OPEN`, `AUDIT_CLEARED` or `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`. Exact adjacency is explicitly permitted — one period ending at the instant the next begins is not an overlap — and the statement named by `restates_statement_id` is excluded from the candidate set, since a restatement is meant to cover the same period as the statement it replaces. A genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409.

A successful certification moves the statement `DRAFT → FILED`, stamps `filed_at` with the current logical clock and `certified_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and freezes `declared_total_copies` at the current `total_qualified_copies`. That frozen figure, and not the live one, is the number the audit will reconcile against. The publication's `version` advances by 1. When `restates_statement_id` is set, certification also unwinds the freeze in three further hops, described in §3.9. Statements are readable individually and listable with `publication_id`, `status`, `basis` and `restates_statement_id` filters — the last of which is how a reader walks from a condemned statement to the restatement that replaced it.

### 3.8. The Audit Engagement: Sampling and Findings

Only a `CirculationAuditor` may open an engagement, and only against a statement the publisher has already certified. The request names the `statement_id`, the `auditor_org` performing the work (1 to 120 characters, required), the fieldwork boundaries `fieldwork_start` and `fieldwork_end`, and the `sample_size` — the number of recipient records the engagement will verify before it may reconcile, which must be at least 1. `fieldwork_start` must precede `fieldwork_end` strictly, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`. Unlike a statement period, the fieldwork interval is treated as **closed**: both endpoints are days on which the auditor is on the publisher's premises.

Auditors will not tolerate overlapping fieldwork on one title, and neither will this system. A new engagement's closed fieldwork interval must keep at least fourteen clear days — two full weeks — between itself and the interval of every other engagement of the same publication, reached through each engagement's `statement_id`, whose `status` is anything other than `REPORT_ISSUED`. The gap is measured end-to-start in either direction, so it does not matter whether the new window sits before or after the existing one; fourteen clear days is the smallest separation the system accepts, and anything tighter is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409. Engagements that have already issued their report impose no separation at all.

Opening an engagement requires the target statement to be in `FILED`; from any other status the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the engagement is created in `SCHEDULED` with `sample_verified_count` 0, `verified_qualified_copies` 0, `open_findings_count` 0 and `report_issued_at` null — that last field stays null until the report of §3.9 is issued against this engagement — and the statement moves `FILED → UNDER_AUDIT`. Engagements have no collection of their own and are discovered through the statement they audit, whose engagement listing accepts a `status` filter.

Sample verification is the engagement's substance. Each record names the sampled recipient's `subscriber_id`, an `evidence_ok` boolean recording whether the auditor found retained documentation that actually supports the recipient's qualification, and `verified_copies`, the copy count the auditor is prepared to certify for that recipient. `sample_verified_count` is incremented by 1 on every record. `verified_qualified_copies` accumulates `verified_copies` when `evidence_ok` is true and accumulates nothing at all — zero — when `evidence_ok` is false; a recipient whose evidence failed contributes no copies to the audited figure even though it still consumes a slot in the sample. Records may only be taken while the engagement is `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK`, and only while the statement under audit is in `UNDER_AUDIT` or `FINDINGS_OPEN`.

The first record moves the engagement `SCHEDULED → FIELDWORK`; subsequent records leave it in `FIELDWORK`, a self-transition that simply accumulates. At the end of *every* record the system evaluates one auto-advance: if `sample_verified_count` has reached or passed `sample_size` after the increment, the engagement moves `FIELDWORK → RECONCILIATION` inside the same request. When `sample_size` is 1, that auto-advance fires on the very first record, so the engagement moves `SCHEDULED → RECONCILIATION` in one request and the observable end status is `RECONCILIATION`, never `FIELDWORK`. Once in `RECONCILIATION` the engagement is frozen for further sampling: another record is rejected with `ERR_SAMPLE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409, and that same error also guards against double-counting — recording the same `subscriber_id` twice against one engagement is refused, because a sample of thirty distinct records is not a sample of one record thirty times.

Findings are raised while fieldwork or reconciliation is under way, by the `CirculationAuditor`, against the statement under audit. A finding names its `engagement_id`, optionally the `subscriber_id` of the recipient record it concerns — the field is null for exceptions that are not tied to one recipient, such as an unsupported digital metric — a `finding_type` drawn from `MISSING_SOURCE_DOC`, `EXPIRED_QUALIFICATION`, `UNDELIVERABLE_NOT_REMOVED`, `CLASSIFICATION_ERROR` and `DIGITAL_METRIC_UNSUPPORTED`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MATERIAL` or `CRITICAL`, and `affected_copies`, the number of copies the exception calls into question, which must be at least 0. Raising a finding requires the statement to be in `UNDER_AUDIT` and the engagement to be in `FIELDWORK` or `RECONCILIATION`.

Raising a finding has consequences that reach well past the finding row. The finding is created in `OPEN` with `response_count` 0 and `raised_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`. The statement moves `UNDER_AUDIT → FINDINGS_OPEN`. The engagement's `open_findings_count` is recomputed as the number of its findings in `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_REOPENED`. The publication's `open_material_findings_count` and `open_critical_findings_count` are recomputed and its `audit_posture` re-derived, and the publication's `version` advances by 1. Severity is what makes this bite: by the posture ladder of §2.9, a single open `CRITICAL` finding freezes the publication outright, while a single open `MATERIAL` finding restricts it. A `MINOR` finding moves neither counter and therefore leaves posture where it was.

Resolution is a genuine loop with no bound on the number of turns. A `CirculationManager` responds to a finding with a required `response_note`, moving it `OPEN → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` and incrementing `response_count` by 1. The auditor may refuse that response with a required `rejection_note`, moving the finding `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → REJECTED_REOPENED`; the finding stays counted in the engagement's `open_findings_count` and in both publication counters, so `audit_posture` does not relax one bit because a response was offered and refused. From `REJECTED_REOPENED` the manager may respond again — the same required `response_note`, which overwrites the previous note, and `response_count` increments again — moving the finding `REJECTED_REOPENED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. The cycle `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → REJECTED_REOPENED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` may turn indefinitely, and `response_count` is the record of how many turns it took.

Acceptance closes the finding for good. An auditor accepts a `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` finding with an optional `resolution_note`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `raised_by_user_id` — the auditor who raised an exception may not be the one who blesses its remedy, and a self-acceptance is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409, the same code the statement-preparer gate uses. On success the finding moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED_RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and `ACCEPTED_RESOLVED` is terminal: a further response, rejection or acceptance is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The engagement's `open_findings_count` is recomputed; the publication's two open-findings counters and `audit_posture` are recomputed and the publication's `version` advances by 1. And when the accepted finding was the engagement's last one standing in `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_REOPENED`, `open_findings_count` reaches 0 and the statement returns `FINDINGS_OPEN → UNDER_AUDIT` — from where a later finding can drive it back to `FINDINGS_OPEN`, as many times as the audit demands. Findings are readable individually and listable with `engagement_id`, `subscriber_id`, `status` and `severity` filters.

### 3.9. Rate-Base Guarantees, Make-Goods, and the Restatement Cascade

An advertiser insertion contract is the commercial promise that hangs off the circulation number. A `CirculationManager` registers one against a publication, supplying the `publication_id`, the `advertiser_name` the guarantee is owed to (1 to 200 characters, required), the `insertion_count` of paid insertions bought, the `gross_rate_cents_per_insertion` charged for each (both at least 1), the `guaranteed_rate_base` — the qualified-circulation copies promised to the advertiser per issue, also at least 1 — and an optional `shortfall_tolerance_pct`, the shortfall the advertiser has agreed to absorb without compensation, which defaults to 2.00 and is a two-decimal value between 0.00 and 100.00. On registration `guarantee_status` is `ACTIVE`, `makegood_credit_pool_cents` is computed as `insertion_count` multiplied by `gross_rate_cents_per_insertion`, `makegood_credit_committed_cents` and `makegood_credit_waived_cents` are 0, `makegood_credit_available_cents` is the whole pool, and `version` is 1. Contracts are readable individually — that is where the four ledger buckets are exposed — and listable with `publication_id` and `guarantee_status` filters.

Settling guarantees against a certified statement is a `CirculationManager` operation carrying an optional `settlement_note`, and it creates a batch of obligations in one shot, returning them as an array. The candidate set is every advertiser contract of the statement's publication whose `guarantee_status` is `ACTIVE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; the ordering is deterministic and no identifier is ever a decision input. Each candidate is evaluated in five steps. **First**, `shortfall_copies` is the contract's `guaranteed_rate_base` minus the statement's `total_qualified_copies`, floored at zero — a statement that met or beat the guarantee produces no shortfall. **Second**, `shortfall_pct` is `shortfall_copies` multiplied by one hundred, divided by `guaranteed_rate_base`, rounded half up to two decimal places. **Third**, the tolerance gate: when `shortfall_pct` is less than or equal to the contract's `shortfall_tolerance_pct`, no obligation is created for that contract at all, and the contract is simply skipped. **Fourth**, a severity multiplier `tier_factor` is selected from three bands: it is 0.50 when `shortfall_pct` is at most 5.00, it is 1.00 when `shortfall_pct` is greater than 5.00 and at most 10.00, and it is 1.50 when `shortfall_pct` is greater than 10.00 — so a publisher that misses badly pays three times the rate per point of shortfall that a publisher who misses slightly pays. **Fifth**, the money is computed, and **the rounding order here is pinned**: `per_insertion_credit_cents` is the contract's `gross_rate_cents_per_insertion` multiplied by `shortfall_pct` divided by one hundred, multiplied by `tier_factor`, and that product is rounded half up to a whole cent *first*; only then is `credit_cents` obtained by multiplying `per_insertion_credit_cents` by the contract's `insertion_count`. Rounding the whole product at the end instead would give a different answer: at a gross rate of 850,000 cents per insertion, a shortfall of 31.43 percent, a tier factor of 1.50 and twelve insertions, the pinned order gives a per-insertion credit of 400,733 cents and a total of 4,808,796 cents, whereas rounding after the multiplication would give 4,808,790. Finally `bonus_insertions` records the in-kind alternative offered to the advertiser: `credit_cents` divided by `gross_rate_cents_per_insertion`, truncated down to a whole number of free insertions.

Settlement is all-or-nothing on two counts. If the computed `credit_cents` for *any* qualifying contract exceeds that contract's `makegood_credit_available_cents`, the entire request fails with `ERR_MAKEGOOD_POOL_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422, and **no obligation is persisted for any contract in the batch** — a partially settled statement would leave the money-axis conserved quantity of §2.11 unverifiable. And if no `ACTIVE` contract of the publication has a `shortfall_pct` strictly greater than its own `shortfall_tolerance_pct`, there is nothing to settle and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_SHORTFALL_TO_SETTLE` and HTTP 409 rather than succeeding with an empty array. A successful settlement creates one obligation per qualifying contract in status `PENDING`, storing that contract's `contract_id` and the statement's `statement_id` alongside `shortfall_copies`, `shortfall_pct`, `tier_factor`, `per_insertion_credit_cents`, `credit_cents` and `bonus_insertions`; each contract's ledger is re-derived so that `makegood_credit_committed_cents` rises by `credit_cents` and `makegood_credit_available_cents` falls by the same amount; and `guarantees_settled_at` is stamped on the statement with the current logical clock. Settlement targets a statement that has been certified; a statement that has already reached `AUDIT_CLEARED` or `SUPERSEDED` is terminal and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

The obligation then walks a short branching settlement path. A `CirculationManager` offers a `PENDING` obligation to the advertiser with an optional `offer_note`, moving it `PENDING → OFFERED` and stamping `offered_at` with the current logical clock; the offer requires the owning contract's `guarantee_status` to still be `ACTIVE` and the underlying statement to be in `FILED` or `AUDIT_CLEARED`. The offer moves no money: the credit stays inside `makegood_credit_committed_cents`. An `AdvertiserContact` accepts an `OFFERED` obligation with an optional `acceptance_note`, moving it `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and stamping `accepted_at`; acceptance requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the obligation's `contract_id` (§2.1), the contract to still be `ACTIVE`, and the underlying statement not to have fallen into `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` or `SUPERSEDED`. Acceptance also moves no money — the credit remains committed, and both `makegood_credit_committed_cents` and `makegood_credit_available_cents` are unchanged. A `PublisherExecutive`, and only a `PublisherExecutive`, may waive an `OFFERED` obligation with a required `waiver_reason`, moving it `OFFERED → WAIVED`, recording `closed_reason` from `waiver_reason`, and performing the one compensating ledger move in this path: `credit_cents` leaves `makegood_credit_committed_cents` and enters `makegood_credit_waived_cents`, so `makegood_credit_available_cents` and `makegood_credit_pool_cents` are both unchanged and the pool identity still balances. Waiving advances the owning contract's `version` by 1. `ACCEPTED`, `WAIVED` and `VOID` are all terminal; a second action on any of them is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and an action declared only from another state — accepting an obligation still in `PENDING`, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Obligations are readable individually, subject to the ownership check when the reader is an `AdvertiserContact`, and listable with `contract_id`, `statement_id` and `status` filters.

Issuing the audit report is where the audited number finally meets the declared one. A `CirculationAuditor` issues it, optionally supplying a `report_note`; the engagement must be in `RECONCILIATION` — from any other status the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and from `REPORT_ISSUED` with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — and the statement must be in `UNDER_AUDIT` or `FINDINGS_OPEN`. The reconciliation is two lines of arithmetic. `discrepancy_copies` is the statement's frozen `declared_total_copies` minus the engagement's accumulated `verified_qualified_copies`; the sign is preserved on the stored field, so an audit that found *more* qualified copies than the publisher declared records a negative discrepancy. `discrepancy_pct` is the absolute value of `discrepancy_copies`, multiplied by one hundred, divided by `declared_total_copies`, rounded half up to two decimal places — and exactly 0.00 when `declared_total_copies` is 0. Findings do not enter this arithmetic at all; they only guard which branch may be taken. Both values are written onto the statement; `report_issued_at` is a field of the **engagement**, not of the statement, and it is stamped there with the current logical clock; and the engagement moves `RECONCILIATION → REPORT_ISSUED`. The engagement — carrying its new `status` and its freshly stamped `report_issued_at` — is the resource this call returns, on the clearing branch and on the condemning branch alike; a caller who wants to see what the report did to the statement, its contracts or its obligations re-reads those resources afterwards.

The branch is decided by comparing `discrepancy_pct` against the publication's `materiality_pct`, inclusively on the threshold. Three outcomes are possible. When `discrepancy_pct` is strictly below `materiality_pct` **and** the statement is in `UNDER_AUDIT`, the statement moves `UNDER_AUDIT → AUDIT_CLEARED` and **nothing downstream happens at all**: advertiser contracts keep their `guarantee_status`, no obligation changes status, and the publication's posture is unaffected by the report itself. When `discrepancy_pct` is strictly below `materiality_pct` but the statement is in `FINDINGS_OPEN` — meaning at least one finding of the engagement is still `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_REOPENED` — the report is refused with `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_UNRESOLVED` and HTTP 409: an immaterial variance may not be used to close an engagement that still has live exceptions. When `discrepancy_pct` is at or above `materiality_pct`, the statement is condemned, from either `UNDER_AUDIT` or `FINDINGS_OPEN`. The boundary is inclusive and the arithmetic is unforgiving: 1,200 declared copies against 1,176 verified copies is a 24-copy discrepancy, which is 2.00 percent, which against a materiality threshold of 2.00 forces a restatement rather than clearing.

A condemning report sets off a five-hop cascade, and every hop must land. **Hop 1** writes `discrepancy_copies` and `discrepancy_pct` onto the statement and moves the engagement to `REPORT_ISSUED`. **Hop 2** — because the discrepancy crossed materiality — moves the statement to `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, either `UNDER_AUDIT → RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` or `FINDINGS_OPEN → RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`. **Hop 3** — because the publication now holds a statement in `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, which is the second clause of the posture ladder in §2.9 — re-derives the publication's `audit_posture` to `FROZEN` and advances the publication's `version` by 1. **Hop 4** — because the posture became `FROZEN` — moves every advertiser contract of that publication whose `guarantee_status` is `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_RESTATEMENT`; a rate base cannot be enforced against a circulation figure the auditor has just withdrawn. **Hop 5** — because those contracts left `ACTIVE` — moves each of their make-good obligations standing in `PENDING` or `OFFERED` to `VOID`, recording `closed_reason` as the literal string `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED_CASCADE` rather than any operator text. Because `VOID` rows contribute to no ledger bucket, each voided obligation's `credit_cents` leaves `makegood_credit_committed_cents` and returns to `makegood_credit_available_cents` with zero drift, and the pool identity of §2.11 holds across the cascade. Obligations already in `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` are not touched; they are closed transactions, not live exposure. When the report was issued from `FINDINGS_OPEN`, the open findings deliberately stay open — they are carried forward into the restatement's audit trail rather than being closed by the report.

Escaping the freeze has exactly one route, and it is the mirror image of the cascade. The publisher prepares a fresh statement for the same publication whose `restates_statement_id` names the condemned one, recomputes it as needed — in practice under the `AUDIT_VERIFIED` basis, since the audit has just demonstrated that the publisher's own basis overstated the file — and certifies it. Certification of that successor is permitted despite `FROZEN` precisely because it names the offending statement, and it unwinds the freeze in three hops. **Hop 1**: the predecessor moves `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED → SUPERSEDED`, a terminal state driven entirely by the system with no separate call. **Hop 2**: because the publication no longer holds a statement in `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, `audit_posture` is re-derived and leaves `FROZEN` — settling on `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN` according to the remaining clauses of the ladder, so a publication still carrying an open `CRITICAL` finding stays `FROZEN` by the first clause even after the restatement is filed. **Hop 3**: because the posture left `FROZEN`, every advertiser contract of the publication whose `guarantee_status` is `SUSPENDED_PENDING_RESTATEMENT` returns to `ACTIVE`, and its rate-base guarantee is enforceable again against the restated figure. Obligations voided by the cascade are not resurrected; the restated statement is settled afresh if it too falls short of a guarantee.
