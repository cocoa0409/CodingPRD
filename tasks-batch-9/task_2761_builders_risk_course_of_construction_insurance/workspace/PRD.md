# Builders Risk Course Of Construction API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Builders risk — also traded as course-of-construction insurance — is the one line of property business where the insured object does not exist yet when the policy is written. The carrier prices a building that is still a hole in the ground, on the strength of a construction class, a completed value, a construction schedule and a natural-catastrophe exposure band, and then follows that building for twelve or eighteen months while its value grows, its materials travel between the yard, the truck and an off-site warehouse, and its completion date slips. The builders risk underwriting unit currently runs this line across a rating spreadsheet, a shared drive of loss-control reports, and an email chain of extension requests, which means nobody can answer the two questions that matter most: what premium have we actually written on this project, and how much of each sublimit is still available to pay a loss.

This document specifies the service that replaces that arrangement. The API is mounted under `/builders-risk` and covers the full arc of a builders risk account: intake and loss-control screening of a construction project, scheduling of coverage parts with their individual sublimits and coverage conditions, rating and referral, assembly of the named insured and loss-payee schedule, binding and inception, off-site storage commitments against shared warehouse capacity, testing and commissioning notices, project-extension endorsements with their premium adjustments and their reversals, loss adjustment through a pinned allocation waterfall, indemnity payments to named payees, and finally expiry or mid-term cancellation with a return of unearned premium.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, every monetary figure the service reports is reconcilable: premium is held in an append-only ledger of signed transactions rather than a mutable field, and coverage erosion is always recomputed from the surviving issued payments rather than incremented in place, so a compensating action (a rescinded endorsement, a voided payment) restores the prior figure exactly. Second, the service is deliberately conservative about who may act: the maker of a decision may never also be its checker, and a client whose view of a parent record has gone stale is refused rather than allowed to write against numbers it never saw.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Identity

Every request to the service carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); an unrecognised role string is treated as a failure to authenticate rather than as a recognised role that lacks permission. When both headers are well formed but the declared role is not listed among the target endpoint's authorised roles, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are:

- `Underwriter` — the builders risk underwriter. Submits construction projects, builds the coverage schedule and the named insured schedule, rates and binds policies, incepts coverage, files extension endorsement requests and resubmits returned ones, commits and releases off-site storage, and declares substantial completion on behalf of a project.
- `UnderwritingManager` — the referral and authority-limit authority. Dispositions referred submissions, returns extension requests for more information, approves large-premium extensions, rescinds approved extensions, cancels in-force policies, settles claims and voids issued payments. By design this role is never exercised by the same physical user who performed the corresponding maker step.
- `RiskEngineer` — loss-control engineering. Screens submitted projects and stamps their engineering grade, registers and suspends off-site storage facilities, schedules and dispositions testing and commissioning notices, and shares the storage-commitment operations with the underwriter.
- `ClaimAdjuster` — loss adjustment. Takes first notice of loss, chooses the valuation basis and supplies the loss items that drive the allocation waterfall, reopens closed or denied claims, and issues indemnity payments.
- `ProjectContact` — the insured project's own site contact.
- `LossPayee` — a named insured, contractor or lender named on a policy.

Four of these roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager`, `RiskEngineer` or `ClaimAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for an audit stamp — as `screened_by_user_id` on a screened project, `quoted_by_user_id` on a rated policy, `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an extension endorsement, `adjusted_by_user_id` on an adjusted claim, `created_by_user_id` and `voided_by_user_id` on a loss payment, and `decided_by_user_id` on a testing notice — and it compares that stamp against the acting `X-User-ID` for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The other two roles are entity-backed, and their `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ConstructionProject`: that contact may declare substantial completion only for the project whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, may schedule a testing or commissioning notice only when the `project_id` in the payload equals `X-User-ID`, and may read only that project, that project's notices, and individual notices belonging to it. When `X-User-Role` is `LossPayee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `NamedInsuredInterest`: that payee may acknowledge only the interest whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, may read only that interest, and when listing loss payments may only see rows whose `payee_interest_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any attempt by an entity-backed role to act on or read a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so a client walking a project's premium ledger or a facility's storage assignments sees them in the order the service recorded them.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching rows remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters are additive and all optional. Projects may be filtered by `status`, `cat_zone` and `project_kind`; policies by `project_id` and `status`; a policy's coverage schedule by `status` and `part_type`; a policy's insured schedule by `party_role` and `acknowledgment_status`; a policy's endorsements, storage assignments and a project's policies and notices by `status`; a policy's premium ledger by `txn_type`; claims by `policy_id`, `peril`, `adjustment_status` and `payment_status`; loss payments by `claim_id`, `payee_interest_id` and `status`; and storage facilities by `status` and `protection_class`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Audit Trail

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Construction projects, policies, coverage parts, named insured interests, extension endorsements, claims, loss payments, testing and commissioning notices, storage facilities and temporary storage assignments additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the client did not request directly, such as a derived total being recomputed because a child row changed.

`PremiumTransaction` is the one exception. Premium ledger entries are immutable once written, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and no operation edits or deletes a ledger row. The only way to correct the ledger is to append a reversing entry.

### 2.5. Cents, Days and Rounding Discipline

All money in this service is expressed as an integer number of cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents at any stage: wherever a rate, a load percentage or a proportion produces a non-integral figure, the figure is rounded half up to a whole number of cents — that is, to zero decimal places. Two conventions about *when* the rounding happens matter enough to state globally, because they are the difference between a correct premium and one that is off by a cent.

First, when a premium is assembled from several components, each component is rounded to whole cents independently and only the rounded components are added together. Summing the unrounded components and rounding the total once is not the rule. Second, when a loss is allocated across several coverage buckets, each line's valuation is rounded as it is computed and the rounded line values are then summed; rounding once at the end is not the rule.

Days are always whole days. Wherever a duration is derived from two dates the service takes the whole number of days between them, and wherever a date interval is described the interval is half-open unless this document explicitly says both bounds are inclusive: a policy term runs from `term_start_date` inclusive up to but not including `term_end_date`, and a storage or testing window runs from `window_start` inclusive up to but not including `window_end`. Datetimes are compared to whole-second precision.

Two ratio-valued fields are not money and do not follow the cents rule. `insurable_interest_pct` is a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive carrying at most two decimal places, and `referral_load_pct` is a proportion drawn from the set 0, 0.10 and 0.20. `slip_factor` is likewise a proportion, one of 1.00, 1.15 and 1.40.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

The service runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Every timestamp the service stamps itself — `bound_at` on a bound policy, `reported_at` on a claim, `acknowledged_at` on an acknowledged interest, `released_at` on a released storage assignment, and the `effective_date` on certain ledger entries — is taken from the logical clock at the moment of the operation, and every "is it too early / too late" comparison in this document is made against the clock, not against real time.

Setting the clock is an operator capability, available to `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager`, `RiskEngineer` and `ClaimAdjuster` alike; the caller supplies the new instant as `current_utc`. Advancing the clock is the only way a policy reaches the end of its term, so the capability is also the trigger for the expiry cascade described in §2.7. Its response reports the clock's new value in `current_utc` together with four counters describing what the advance swept up: `expired_policy_count`, `closed_coverage_part_count`, `released_assignment_count` and `lapsed_endorsement_count`.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this service mutate more than one record. Where they do, the cascade is specified hop by hop in §3 and is not optional: an implementation that performs the primary mutation but skips a downstream hop leaves the account unreconciled. Every record touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including records the client did not name in the request.

The largest cascade is the one that fires when a policy stops being `IN_FORCE`, whether because the logical clock reached its `term_end_date` or because an underwriting manager cancelled it. It runs in four hops. First the policy itself leaves `IN_FORCE`, becoming `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`. Second, because the policy left `IN_FORCE`, every `CoveragePart` of that policy still in `ACTIVE` becomes `CLOSED`, with its `eroded_cents` and `available_cents` frozen at their then-current values. Third, because the temporary-storage part closed, every `TemporaryStorageAssignment` of that policy still in `APPROVED` becomes `RELEASED`, with `released_at` set to the clock value at the moment of the sweep and `release_reason` set to `POLICY_EXPIRY` on expiry or `POLICY_CANCELLATION` on cancellation. Fourth, because those assignments stopped occupying their warehouses, `committed_value_cents` is recomputed on every `StorageFacility` they referenced. Running alongside those four hops is an independent branch: every `ExtensionEndorsement` of the policy sitting in `REQUESTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` becomes `LAPSED` without any ledger entry being written, and the project's `in_force_policy_count` is recomputed.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reverse Lookups

Entities in this service are peers. Each is addressed at its own top-level collection beneath `/builders-risk`, and parentage is expressed exclusively through foreign-key fields in the request body — `project_id`, `policy_id`, `claim_id`, `facility_id`, `payee_interest_id` — never by nesting one collection inside another for the purposes of writing. Sub-resource read paths do exist, but purely as reverse-reachability channels: they return the collection of children pointing at the named parent and own no write semantics of their own.

Identifiers in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error. Each collection has its own not-found code: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTEREST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404. The same codes govern foreign keys supplied in a request body: a `project_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, a `policy_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, a `facility_id` yields `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, a `claim_id` yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and a `payee_interest_id` yields `ERR_INTEREST_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign keys are resolved on project creation of a policy, on scheduling a coverage part, on adding a named insured interest, on filing an extension endorsement, on committing a storage assignment (both the policy and the facility), on first notice of loss, on scheduling a testing notice, and on issuing a loss payment (both the claim and the payee interest).

### 2.9. Stale-View Rejection on Parent Records

Three write operations in this service create a child row whose economics depend entirely on numbers held by a parent row, and in all three cases a competing writer may legitimately have moved the parent since the client last read it — so no state precondition can catch the problem. Those three writes therefore require the client to declare which version of the parent it believes it is writing against, and the service refuses the write if that belief is out of date.

Creating a policy requires `expected_project_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ConstructionProject` named by `project_id`. If it does not equal the project's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a fresh draft submission legitimately supersedes the project's prior draft policy, so the second writer is not blocked by any state rule — its view of the project is simply stale.

Filing an extension endorsement requires `expected_policy_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `BuildersRiskPolicy` named by `policy_id`. A mismatch with the policy's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newer request legitimately supersedes an open one on the same policy, and an approval silently moves `term_end_date` and `cumulative_extension_days`, so a stale policy view would price the slip factor and the additional premium off obsolete inputs.

Issuing a loss payment requires `expected_claim_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Claim` named by `claim_id`. A mismatch with the claim's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Successive partial payments against one claim are entirely legitimate, so nothing in the claim's state machine stops a second payer; this check is what forces a payer to re-read `paid_cents`, `outstanding_reserve_cents` and the eroded sublimits before drawing against them again.

No other operation takes a version field. Endpoints not named in the three paragraphs above accept no `expected_..._version` input and perform no version comparison.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Authority Limits

The underwriting unit operates on a maker–checker discipline: the user who performs a step may not be the user who checks it. Four pairings enforce this by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a stamp the service recorded at the maker step, and all four rejections carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A referral disposition is refused when the acting user equals the policy's `quoted_by_user_id`. An extension decision is refused when the acting user equals the endorsement's `requested_by_user_id`. A claim settlement is refused when the acting user equals the claim's `adjusted_by_user_id`. A payment void is refused when the acting user equals the payment's `created_by_user_id`.

Separately from the maker–checker rule, the unit runs a monetary authority limit on extension approvals. An `Underwriter` may approve an extension endorsement only while its stored `additional_premium_cents` is at most 500000 cents, that is $5,000; above that figure only an `UnderwritingManager` holds the authority, and an `Underwriter` attempting it is rejected with `ERR_MANAGER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). This check reads the premium figure that was frozen onto the endorsement at request or resubmission time, and it is evaluated early — before the endorsement's own state is examined — so an `Underwriter` reaching for a large-premium endorsement fails on authority even if that endorsement is no longer in `REQUESTED`.

### 2.11. Order of Evaluation

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure ends the request:

1. Header authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorisation against the target endpoint's role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. The extension-approval authority limit (`ERR_MANAGER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`), which as noted in §2.10 deliberately precedes every existence and state check on that endpoint.
4. Existence of every identifier in the request path, resolved left to right (the collection-specific 404 codes).
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body (the collection-specific 404 codes).
6. Ownership for entity-backed roles (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`).
7. Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
8. Request-body schema conformance (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`): every non-optional field present, every enum value within its declared set, money amounts non-negative where declared, `requested_extension_days` between 1 and 180 inclusive, `continuing_pm_weeks` between 0 and 12 inclusive, `insurable_interest_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, `loss_payee_priority` at least 1 when present, `loss_items` non-empty for every peril other than `DELAY`, and no unrecognised field values.
9. Facility site-code uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`).
10. Coherence of every date pair in the body (`ERR_DATE_RANGE_INVALID`): `planned_start_date` strictly before `projected_completion_date` on a project submission, `term_start_date` strictly before `term_end_date` on a policy creation, and `window_start` strictly before `window_end` on both testing-notice and storage-assignment creation.
11. Parent-version freshness (`ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION`).
12. The target record's own state (`ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ENDORSEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INTEREST_STATE`).
13. Loop counters, checked only after the state precondition has passed (`ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`).
14. Cross-entity state — the status of a related record rather than the target (`ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`).
15. Underwriting safety rules (`ERR_ACTIVE_POLICY_EXISTS`, `ERR_EXISTING_STRUCTURE_NOT_APPLICABLE`, `ERR_COVERAGE_PART_NOT_PERMITTED`).
16. Loss-control and schedule-completeness rules (`ERR_MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED`, `ERR_INTERESTS_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`).
17. Window conflicts and window containment (`ERR_TESTING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POLICY_TERM`), then loss-date containment (`ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`), then cancellation dating (`ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID`), then extension-date reconciliation (`ERR_REVISED_DATE_MISMATCH`), in that sequence.
18. Structural constraints on the schedules (`ERR_DUPLICATE_COVERAGE_PART`, `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`, `ERR_INTEREST_PCT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYEE_PRIORITY`, `ERR_FACILITY_TOO_DISTANT`), then the cumulative extension ceiling (`ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`).
19. Loss-item structure (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LOSS_ITEM`) and loss-item referential integrity (`ERR_COVERAGE_PART_MISSING`), then completion readiness (`ERR_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE`), then the coverage conditions (`ERR_TESTING_NOTICE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_STORAGE_NOT_APPROVED`).
20. Payment referential integrity (`ERR_PAYEE_NOT_ON_POLICY`, then `ERR_PAYMENT_PART_NOT_ALLOCATED`).
21. Fiscal ceilings last (`ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_ALLOCATION`, then `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE`).

### 2.12. The Premium Ledger and the Erosion Reconciliation

Two derived quantities in this service are never incremented in place; they are always recomputed from their underlying rows, which is what makes every compensating action exact.

The first is written premium. A policy's premium history lives in an append-only ledger of `PremiumTransaction` rows, each carrying the owning `policy_id`, a `sequence_no` that is its 1-based position within that policy's ledger assigned in `created_at` ascending order, a `txn_type` drawn from `INITIAL`, `EXTENSION_AP`, `EXTENSION_AP_REVERSAL` and `RETURN_PREMIUM`, a signed `amount_cents` that is positive when premium is charged and negative when premium is returned or reversed, an `effective_date` recording when the entry took effect, a `memo` describing its origin in human terms (for instance "bind", "extension approval", "extension rescission" or "cancellation return"), and, on reversing entries only, a `reverses_sequence_no` pointing at the `sequence_no` of an earlier row of the *same* policy that this row undoes. Original entries leave `reverses_sequence_no` null; a reversing entry must always point at a strictly smaller `sequence_no` than its own. The policy's `written_premium_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger row of that policy, with no status filter of any kind, and it is recomputed after each append. Because an approval's charge and its rescission's reversal sum to exactly zero, rescinding an approved extension restores `written_premium_cents` to precisely the value it held before the approval.

The second is coverage erosion. Whenever a payment is issued or voided, the service recomputes, for every `CoveragePart` of the affected policy, `eroded_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` over `LossPayment` rows whose `status` is `ISSUED`, whose `part_type` equals that part's `part_type`, and whose `claim_id` belongs to a claim of that same policy; `available_cents` as that part's `sublimit_cents` minus its `eroded_cents`; the policy's `eroded_total_cents` as the sum of `eroded_cents` over all its coverage parts; the claim's `paid_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` over that claim's `ISSUED` payments; and the claim's `outstanding_reserve_cents` as `indemnity_cents` minus `paid_cents`. Payments in `VOIDED` are retained for audit and contribute zero. Claims in every adjustment state participate in this recomputation — a later reopening does not un-erode anything already paid. Because the figures are recomputed rather than adjusted, issuing a payment and then voiding it returns `eroded_cents`, `available_cents`, `eroded_total_cents`, `paid_cents` and `outstanding_reserve_cents` to exactly their pre-payment values, and the identity that `total_incurred_cents` equals `indemnity_cents` holds at every intermediate step.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Project Intake and Loss-Control Screening

Every builders risk account begins with a **construction project**. An `Underwriter` or an `UnderwritingManager` submits one on behalf of the producing broker, supplying the project `name`, the `site_address` of the construction site, the ISO construction class as `construction_type` (one of `FRAME`, `JOISTED_MASONRY`, `NON_COMBUSTIBLE` or `FIRE_RESISTIVE`), the `project_kind` distinguishing `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` from `RENOVATION`, the completed value of the new work as `hard_cost_value_cents`, the natural-catastrophe exposure band as `cat_zone` (one of `INLAND`, `WIND_TIER_1`, `WIND_TIER_2`, `SEISMIC` or `FLOOD_A`), the scheduled `planned_start_date`, and the `projected_completion_date` the broker is prepared to stand behind. On a renovation the actual cash value of the building that is already standing is supplied as `existing_structure_value_cents`; the field is optional, defaults to 0 when omitted, may never be negative, and must be 0 on a `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` submission, which by definition has no pre-existing structure. `hard_cost_value_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, and `projected_completion_date` must be strictly later than `planned_start_date` — a submission failing the date test is refused with `ERR_DATE_RANGE_INVALID`.

A newly submitted project lands in `SUBMITTED` with `version` 1, no risk scores at all (`submission_risk_score` and `audit_risk_score` both null), `engineering_grade`, `loss_control_report_ref`, `screened_by_user_id` and `actual_substantial_completion_date` all null, and `in_force_policy_count` at 0. A project's `status` runs over `SUBMITTED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE`, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`, of which `INELIGIBLE` and `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` are terminal.

Nothing can be quoted until a `RiskEngineer` has screened the site. Screening records the loss-control grade as `engineering_grade` — one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D` — optionally attaches the report's reference as `loss_control_report_ref`, stamps the acting operator into `screened_by_user_id`, and computes and stores the project's `submission_risk_score`. Screening is available only out of `SUBMITTED`, and it is a one-shot act: there is no re-screening path, so a second screening attempt on a project that has already left `SUBMITTED` — including one that screened out as `INELIGIBLE` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`.

The submission score is a 100-point loss-control index, assembled from six components and then capped. Construction class contributes 30 points for `FRAME`, 20 for `JOISTED_MASONRY`, 12 for `NON_COMBUSTIBLE` and 6 for `FIRE_RESISTIVE`. Catastrophe zone contributes 0 points for `INLAND`, 12 for `WIND_TIER_2`, 18 for `SEISMIC`, 25 for `WIND_TIER_1` and 30 for `FLOOD_A`. Insured value contributes one point for each complete five million dollars — that is, for each complete 500000000 cents — of total insured value, rounded down to a whole number of points and capped at 20 points. Schedule length contributes two points for every whole month of term beyond the first twelve, where the month count is the project's duration in days divided by 30 and rounded *up* to a whole number, months beyond twelve are floored at zero, and the resulting points are capped at 15. A `RENOVATION` project contributes a flat 10 points and a `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` project contributes none. Claim history contributes five points per qualifying claim, capped at 15 points. The six components are added and the total is capped at 100.

Screening evaluates that index on the **submission basis**, which reads nothing but the project's own submitted values. On this basis the total insured value used for the value component is `hard_cost_value_cents` plus `existing_structure_value_cents` — the existing structure always counts at screening, regardless of what any later policy chooses to insure — the duration is the whole number of days from `planned_start_date` to `projected_completion_date`, and the claim count is zero by definition. Two thresholds hang off the resulting figure. A score of 90 or more, or an `engineering_grade` of `D`, makes the project `INELIGIBLE`; anything else makes it `ELIGIBLE`. Note that the score is computed and stored either way, so a declined submission remains auditable. The second threshold, 75, is not consulted at screening at all — it decides at rating time whether a quote may be offered directly or must be referred, as §3.4 describes.

Once coverage has incepted, the project moves to `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and `in_force_policy_count` rises to 1. The final act on the project record is the declaration of substantial completion, which either the site's own `ProjectContact` or the `Underwriter` may make. The declarant supplies `actual_substantial_completion_date`; the project must currently be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` (any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`), the declared date must not be later than the current logical clock, and it must be later than `planned_start_date`. When a `ProjectContact` makes the declaration, ownership applies: the project's `id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. On success the date is stored, the project becomes `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`, and its `version` increments by 1. This declaration is also the gate on delay coverage: until the actual completion date exists, no `DELAY` claim can be adjusted.

### 3.2. Assembling the Policy: the Coverage Schedule

A policy is drafted against an `ELIGIBLE` project. The `Underwriter` supplies the `project_id`, which is immutable thereafter, the parent-version declaration `expected_project_version` described in §2.9, the inception date `term_start_date`, the expiry date `term_end_date` (exclusive, and strictly later than the start), an `includes_existing_structure` flag defaulting to false, the occurrence deductible tier as `deductible_tier`, the policy aggregate `limit_cents`, and the agreed `daily_delay_rate_cents` for delay-in-completion soft costs. A project in any status other than `ELIGIBLE` — still `SUBMITTED`, screened out as `INELIGIBLE`, already `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, or `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` — cannot receive a new draft, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. `limit_cents` must be strictly greater than zero; `daily_delay_rate_cents` may be zero but never negative, and a zero value means the policy buys no delay coverage at all.

Setting `includes_existing_structure` to true is only meaningful on a renovation that actually has a standing building: it requires the project's `project_kind` to be `RENOVATION` and its `existing_structure_value_cents` to be strictly greater than zero. A true flag on a `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` project, or on a renovation whose existing structure is valued at zero, is refused with `ERR_EXISTING_STRUCTURE_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Creation derives four figures and freezes three of them. `original_term_days` is the whole number of days in the half-open interval from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` as submitted, and it is never changed by any endorsement — every ratio and per-diem in this service that speaks of the "original term" means this number. `insured_tiv_cents` is the project's `hard_cost_value_cents` plus, only when `includes_existing_structure` is true, the project's `existing_structure_value_cents`, and it too is frozen at creation. `occurrence_deductible_cents` is read straight off `deductible_tier`: `STANDARD_25K` gives 2500000 cents, `ELEVATED_100K` gives 10000000 cents and `HIGH_250K` gives 25000000 cents. `adjusted_completion_date` starts equal to the project's `projected_completion_date`, and `cumulative_extension_days` starts at 0. The new row lands in `DRAFT` with `quoted_premium_cents` null, `referral_load_pct` at 0, `written_premium_cents` at 0, `eroded_total_cents` at 0, `return_premium_cents` null, and `quoted_by_user_id`, `bound_at`, `cancel_reason` and `cancel_effective_date` all null. Two side effects reach outside the new row: any existing policy of the same `project_id` sitting in `DRAFT` is transitioned `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` (its `quoted_premium_cents`, if it had one, is retained for audit and nothing is cleared), and the parent project's `version` increments by 1. A policy's `status` spans `DRAFT`, `REFERRED`, `QUOTED`, `BOUND`, `IN_FORCE`, `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED`, `DECLINED` and `SUPERSEDED`; the last four are terminal and admit no trigger whatsoever.

Coverage on a builders risk policy is bought bucket by bucket. The `Underwriter` schedules each **coverage part** by naming the owning `policy_id`, the bucket as `part_type` — one of `MATERIALS_ON_SITE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `TEMPORARY_STORAGE`, `EXISTING_STRUCTURE`, `SOFT_COST`, `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION`, `WATER_DAMAGE` or `TESTING_COMMISSIONING` — the maximum payable from that bucket as `sublimit_cents`, and optionally a `condition_code`, which defaults to `NONE` and may otherwise be `APPROVED_NOTICE_REQUIRED` or `MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED`. A scheduled part lands `ACTIVE` with `eroded_cents` at 0 and `available_cents` equal to its `sublimit_cents`, and scheduling it increments the parent policy's `version` by 1. A part's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`; the closure is never requested directly but arrives as the cascade of §2.7 when the policy leaves `IN_FORCE`.

The coverage schedule is editable only while the policy is `DRAFT` or `REFERRED` — the schedule freezes the moment a quote is offered. Both scheduling a part and patching one require the parent policy to be in one of those two statuses, and an attempt against a `QUOTED`, `BOUND`, `IN_FORCE` or terminal policy is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. A patch may revise `sublimit_cents`, `condition_code`, or both; after a patch, `available_cents` is recomputed as `sublimit_cents` minus `eroded_cents` and the part's own `version` increments by 1.

Four rules police the schedule. Each `part_type` may appear at most once per policy, in any status, so a second part of the same type is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COVERAGE_PART`. Each individual `sublimit_cents` must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than the policy's `limit_cents`. Collectively, the sum of `sublimit_cents` over the policy's `ACTIVE` parts — counting the row being created, or the new value being patched — must not exceed `limit_cents`; each sublimit can be perfectly admissible on its own and still trip this rule together, in which case the answer is `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. Finally, two buckets are conditional on the policy's own terms: an `EXISTING_STRUCTURE` part may only be scheduled when the policy's `includes_existing_structure` is true, and a `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION` part may only be scheduled when the policy's `daily_delay_rate_cents` is strictly greater than zero. Either violation is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_PART_NOT_PERMITTED`.

### 3.3. The Named Insured and Loss-Payee Schedule

A builders risk policy rarely insures one party. The owner funds the work, the general contractor performs it, subcontractors hold interests in their own portions, and one or more lenders hold security over the finished asset. Each of these appears on the policy as a **named insured interest**, added by the `Underwriter` against the owning `policy_id` with the party's legal `party_name`, the capacity as `party_role` (one of `OWNER`, `GENERAL_CONTRACTOR`, `LENDER` or `SUBCONTRACTOR`), the party's `insurable_interest_pct` share of the completed work, and optionally a `loss_payee_priority`. Like the coverage schedule, the insured schedule is only editable while the policy is `DRAFT` or `REFERRED`; adding an interest to a policy in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`.

`insurable_interest_pct` is a percentage from 0 to 100 inclusive carrying at most two decimal places, and it must be 0 for a party named as `LENDER` — a mortgagee holds security, not an insurable share of the work. The sum of `insurable_interest_pct` across the policy's interests, counting the row being added, must not exceed 100; the attempt that would push it past 100 is refused with `ERR_INTEREST_PCT_EXCEEDED`. `loss_payee_priority` is the payment ordering of a mortgagee or other loss payee: it is required whenever `party_role` is `LENDER`, optional for `OWNER`, `GENERAL_CONTRACTOR` and `SUBCONTRACTOR` (any named party may also be designated a loss payee), must be at least 1 whenever it is present, is otherwise null, and must be unique within the policy wherever present — a second interest reusing a priority already taken on that policy is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYEE_PRIORITY`. Adding an interest lands it with `acknowledgment_status` at `PENDING` and `acknowledged_at` null, and increments the parent policy's `version` by 1.

Each named party must then confirm its own interest. Acknowledgement is exercised by the `LossPayee` role, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the interest's own `id`, so no party can acknowledge on another's behalf. The interest must currently be `PENDING`; acknowledging an already `ACKNOWLEDGED` interest is a rejection with `ERR_INVALID_INTEREST_STATE` rather than a silent no-op. The owning policy must still be in `DRAFT`, `REFERRED` or `QUOTED` — acknowledgement happens before the risk attaches, so a policy that has already bound is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. The transition `PENDING → ACKNOWLEDGED` stamps `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock and increments the interest's `version` by 1, and it is what unblocks the schedule-completeness gate on binding.

### 3.4. Rating, Referral, Binding and Inception

Rating a draft is the `Underwriter`'s act, and it is where the pricing engine runs. The draft must be in `DRAFT` (any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`), the parent project must be `ELIGIBLE` or already `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and must carry a non-null `submission_risk_score`, and the policy must already have a scheduled `MATERIALS_ON_SITE` coverage part — a builders risk policy with no materials cover is not a rateable object. Rating reads the project's submitted values together with every coverage part of the policy regardless of status (at rating time all parts are `ACTIVE` in any case); no claim or endorsement data participates.

The premium is assembled from five loaded components, a credit and a floor, and each component is rounded half up to whole cents on its own before anything is added. First, the number of rated units is the policy's frozen `insured_tiv_cents` divided by 100000 — one unit per $1,000 of insured value — kept as an exact fraction rather than truncated. The **base premium** is that unit count multiplied by the construction-class rate and rounded: 210 cents per $1,000 for `FRAME`, 150 for `JOISTED_MASONRY`, 110 for `NON_COMBUSTIBLE` and 80 for `FIRE_RESISTIVE`. Second, the **catastrophe load** is the base premium multiplied by the zone factor and rounded: nothing at all for `INLAND`, 18 percent for `WIND_TIER_2`, 25 percent for `SEISMIC`, 35 percent for `WIND_TIER_1` and 40 percent for `FLOOD_A`. Third, the **duration load** charges two percent of the base premium for every whole month of term beyond the first twelve: the month count is `original_term_days` divided by 30 and rounded up to a whole number, the excess over twelve months is floored at zero, and the product of base premium, two percent and that excess is rounded. Fourth, the **soft-cost load** is the sum of `sublimit_cents` over the policy's coverage parts whose `part_type` is `SOFT_COST` or `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION`, divided by 100000 to give an exact count of $1,000 units, multiplied by 60 cents per unit, and rounded. Fifth, the **renovation load** is twelve percent of the base premium, rounded, on a `RENOVATION` project and exactly zero on a `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` one.

Those five rounded components are then added to give the subtotal. The **deductible credit** is the subtotal multiplied by the tier's credit factor and rounded: nothing for `STANDARD_25K`, six percent for `ELEVATED_100K` and eleven percent for `HIGH_250K`. Subtracting the credit from the subtotal gives the pre-floor figure, and the quoted premium is the greater of that figure and the minimum premium of 250000 cents ($2,500); where the pre-floor figure equals the minimum exactly, the pre-floor figure stands. The result is stored as `quoted_premium_cents`.

Because the two project kinds route through the same arithmetic on different inputs, it is worth stating them side by side. On a `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` project the renovation load is zero and `includes_existing_structure` must be false, so `insured_tiv_cents` ignores `existing_structure_value_cents` entirely. On a `RENOVATION` project the renovation load adds twelve percent of the base premium, and when `includes_existing_structure` is true the pre-existing structure's value enters `insured_tiv_cents`, which lifts the base premium and therefore the catastrophe load, the duration load and the renovation load together.

Rating also branches the policy's status, and the branch is decided purely by the referral threshold of 75 points. When the project's `submission_risk_score` is below 75 the policy moves `DRAFT → QUOTED` and the premium may be offered. When the score is 75 or more the policy moves `DRAFT → REFERRED` instead: the premium is still computed and stored, but it is held as non-offerable pending a manager's disposition. Either way the acting operator is stamped into `quoted_by_user_id` and the policy's `version` increments by 1.

A referred submission is dispositioned by an `UnderwritingManager`, who supplies `decision` as either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. The policy must be in `REFERRED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the policy's `quoted_by_user_id` per §2.10. On `APPROVE` the manager may attach a `referral_load_pct` — 0, 0.10 or 0.20, defaulting to 0 when omitted — which is stored on the policy and immediately re-prices the stored premium: `quoted_premium_cents` becomes the existing `quoted_premium_cents` multiplied by one plus `referral_load_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The policy then moves `REFERRED → QUOTED`. On `DECLINE` the policy moves `REFERRED → DECLINED`, which is terminal, and `quoted_premium_cents` is left exactly as rated so the declined price remains on file. Either disposition increments the policy's `version` by 1.

Underwriters change their minds, and a quoted policy can be pulled back for revision. Reopening moves the policy `QUOTED → DRAFT`, clears `quoted_premium_cents` back to null and `quoted_by_user_id` back to null, and preserves any `referral_load_pct` already recorded on the policy; the coverage schedule and the insured schedule become editable again and the policy is rated from scratch on the next pass. The policy's `version` increments by 1.

Binding is the moment the premium becomes real. The policy must be `QUOTED`, and three completeness gates apply. The insured schedule must be complete and agreed: every `NamedInsuredInterest` on the policy must be `ACKNOWLEDGED`, at least one interest must carry `party_role` of `OWNER`, and the sum of `insurable_interest_pct` over the interests whose `party_role` is `OWNER` or `GENERAL_CONTRACTOR` must equal exactly 100. An unacknowledged payee, a missing owner and a schedule that does not add up to exactly 100 all produce the same refusal, `ERR_INTERESTS_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. The property schedule must also be coherent with the insured value: the sum of `sublimit_cents` over the parts whose `part_type` is `MATERIALS_ON_SITE` or `EXISTING_STRUCTURE` must not exceed the policy's `insured_tiv_cents`, and a bind that would breach that is refused. And a project may carry only one live policy: if any other policy of the same `project_id` is already `BOUND` or `IN_FORCE`, the bind is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_POLICY_EXISTS`. (Duplicate drafts never reach this check, because a new draft supersedes the old one at creation; the live-policy test exists for the bind step alone.)

A successful bind moves the policy `QUOTED → BOUND`, stamps `bound_at` from the logical clock, sets `written_premium_cents` equal to `quoted_premium_cents`, and opens the premium ledger by appending its first entry — `sequence_no` 1, `txn_type` `INITIAL`, `amount_cents` equal to `quoted_premium_cents`, `effective_date` equal to `term_start_date`, `memo` recording the bind — after which `written_premium_cents` is recomputed from the ledger as §2.12 requires. The policy's `version` increments by 1.

Inception attaches the risk. The `Underwriter` incepts a `BOUND` policy once the logical clock has reached or passed `term_start_date`; an inception attempted before the term begins is refused. The policy moves `BOUND → IN_FORCE` and its `version` increments by 1, and the change bubbles up to the project: the parent `ConstructionProject` moves `ELIGIBLE → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, its `in_force_policy_count` becomes 1, and its `version` increments by 1 as well.

Alongside the individual reads, the service exposes a reconciliation view of a policy's premium position for the `Underwriter` and the `UnderwritingManager`. It reports `quoted_premium_cents` and `written_premium_cents` as the policy holds them, `ledger_sum_cents` as the signed sum of every `PremiumTransaction` row of the policy, `transaction_count` as the number of those rows, `extension_ap_total_cents` as the signed sum of the `EXTENSION_AP` and `EXTENSION_AP_REVERSAL` rows only, `return_premium_cents` mirroring the policy's own `return_premium_cents` and reading 0 for as long as the policy is not `CANCELLED`, and `balanced` as the boolean statement that `ledger_sum_cents` equals `written_premium_cents`.

### 3.5. Site Exposures: Off-Site Storage and Testing Windows

Materials bought for a project do not sit on the site the whole time. They wait in warehouses, and those warehouses are shared: the same building may hold steel for three unrelated projects insured under three different policies. The service therefore models a warehouse as a **storage facility** in its own right, registered by a `RiskEngineer` with its `name`, a short `site_code`, the road `distance_from_site_miles` from the insured site, the `aggregate_capacity_cents` it may hold across all commitments at once, and its `protection_class`, either `SPRINKLERED` or `NON_SPRINKLERED`. Both `distance_from_site_miles` and `aggregate_capacity_cents` must be strictly greater than zero. `site_code` is unique across every facility on the books, and a registration reusing an existing code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`. A newly registered facility is `ACTIVE` with `committed_value_cents` at 0.

A `RiskEngineer` may take a facility off the market and put it back. Setting `status` to `SUSPENDED` requires the facility to be `ACTIVE`, and setting it to `ACTIVE` requires it to be `SUSPENDED`; the pair cycles indefinitely and neither value is terminal. Suspension is forward-looking only: existing commitments at the facility are left completely untouched, and only *new* commitments are refused, with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. Either change increments the facility's `version` by 1.

Committing materials creates a **temporary storage assignment**, which either the `Underwriter` or the `RiskEngineer` may raise. The payload names the covering `policy_id`, the holding `facility_id`, the `stored_value_cents` of the materials (strictly greater than zero), and the occupancy window as `window_start` and `window_end`, where the end must be strictly later than the start. The commitment is only admissible against a policy that is `BOUND` or `IN_FORCE` — material cannot be stored under a policy that has not yet been bound, and a `DRAFT`, `REFERRED`, `QUOTED` or terminal policy is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. Three further gates apply in the order §2.11 sets out. The facility must be `ACTIVE`, or the answer is `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`; a suspended warehouse accepts no new stored value even when it has spare capacity. The facility must be within 100 road miles of the site, so a `distance_from_site_miles` above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_TOO_DISTANT`. And the occupancy must sit inside the period the policy actually covers: `window_start` must be at or after the policy's `term_start_date` and `window_end` must be at or before the policy's `adjusted_completion_date`, with any breach refused as `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POLICY_TERM`.

The capacity test is the interesting one, because it is an exposure test rather than a running total. Two occupancy windows overlap when the existing window's start is strictly before the new window's end *and* the new window's start is strictly before the existing window's end; touching endpoints do not overlap, so a hand-off in which the new occupancy starts at the exact instant an existing one ends is always admissible no matter how large the two stored values are. The candidate set is every temporary storage assignment at the same `facility_id` whose `status` is `APPROVED`; released assignments are ignored no matter what their windows say, and assignments belonging to other policies and other projects count in full, because the warehouse is a genuinely shared resource. Peak exposure is the new `stored_value_cents` plus the sum of `stored_value_cents` over every overlapping candidate, and the commitment is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that peak is strictly greater than the facility's `aggregate_capacity_cents`; a peak exactly equal to capacity passes.

An accepted commitment lands `APPROVED` with `released_at` and `release_reason` both null, raises the facility's `committed_value_cents` by the assignment's `stored_value_cents`, and increments the facility's `version` by 1. `committed_value_cents` is always the sum of `stored_value_cents` over the facility's `APPROVED` assignments, so it is recomputed rather than tracked whenever an assignment's status moves.

Materials can leave storage early. Either the `Underwriter` or the `RiskEngineer` may release an assignment that is currently `APPROVED`; releasing one that is already `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, since `RELEASED` is terminal. A manual release moves `APPROVED → RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, sets `release_reason` to `MANUAL`, lowers the facility's `committed_value_cents` accordingly and increments both the facility's and the assignment's `version` by 1. The same transition also happens without anybody asking for it, as hop three of the cascade in §2.7, in which case `release_reason` is `POLICY_EXPIRY` or `POLICY_CANCELLATION` instead of `MANUAL`.

Testing and commissioning is the other site exposure the carrier wants advance sight of. Charging a sprinkler system with water, energising switchgear or firing a boiler for the first time are the moments builders risk losses actually happen, so each is notified in advance as a **testing and commissioning notice**. Either the site's own `ProjectContact` — for its own `project_id` only, on pain of `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — or a `RiskEngineer` schedules one, naming the `project_id`, the `system_type` under test (one of `SPRINKLER_HYDROSTATIC`, `HVAC`, `ELECTRICAL_ENERGIZATION`, `BOILER_STARTUP` or `ELEVATOR`), and the half-open window as `window_start` and `window_end`, the end strictly later than the start. Two boolean flags qualify the activity, both defaulting to false: `hot_work` states that the work involves welding, cutting or torch work, and `water_charged` states that piping will be charged with water during the window. A `mitigation_plan_ref` may accompany the notice.

Loss control ties the plan requirement to the project's engineering grade. When a notice declares either `water_charged` or `hot_work` as true and the project's `engineering_grade` is `C` or `D`, a `mitigation_plan_ref` must be supplied, and its absence is refused with `ERR_MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED`; projects graded `A` or `B` may schedule such work without one. A new notice lands in `SCHEDULED` with `decided_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` null.

Testing windows on one project must also be kept apart from each other, and the geometry here is deliberately *not* the geometry used for warehouse capacity: the buffer inflates both intervals, and there is no capacity dimension at all. The buffer is 24 hours whenever either the new notice or the existing notice declares `hot_work` as true, and zero hours otherwise. Two notices conflict when the new window's start is strictly before the existing window's end extended by the buffer, and the existing window's start is strictly before the new window's end extended by the buffer. The candidate set is every notice of the same `project_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `APPROVED` — a `REJECTED` notice never conflicts with anything, and the notice being created is of course not yet among the candidates. Any conflict refuses the create with `ERR_TESTING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. With a zero buffer the test degenerates to plain half-open overlap, so two back-to-back non-hot-work windows that meet exactly at an instant are admissible.

A `RiskEngineer` then dispositions the notice, supplying `decision` as `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and on rejection a `rejection_reason`. The notice must be `SCHEDULED`; both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal, and a decision on a notice that has already been dispositioned is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`. Approval carries one further condition: when the notice declares `water_charged` as true and the project's in-force policy carries a `WATER_DAMAGE` coverage part whose `condition_code` is `MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED`, the notice's `mitigation_plan_ref` must be present, and its absence refuses the approval with `ERR_MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED`. On `APPROVE` the notice moves `SCHEDULED → APPROVED`; on `REJECT` it moves `SCHEDULED → REJECTED` and the `rejection_reason` is stored while the coverage condition stays locked. Either way `decided_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting operator and the notice's `version` increments by 1. An approved notice is what unlocks testing and commissioning cover for a loss whose `loss_date` falls at or after its `window_start` and strictly before its `window_end`.

### 3.6. Schedule Slip: Extension Endorsements

Construction schedules slip, and when they do the policy term and the delay-coverage baseline have to move with them. That movement is bought as an **extension endorsement**, filed by the `Underwriter` against a policy that is currently `IN_FORCE` — an endorsement cannot be filed against a policy that has not incepted or has already left force, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. The filing names the `policy_id`, carries the parent-version declaration `expected_policy_version` of §2.9, states the `requested_extension_days` of slip (a whole number from 1 to 180 inclusive), records the cause as `slip_reason` (one of `WEATHER`, `LABOR_SHORTAGE`, `MATERIAL_DELAY`, `DESIGN_CHANGE` or `PERMIT_DELAY`), and asserts the `revised_completion_date` the project now expects to hit.

The asserted date must reconcile with the day count it is priced from: `revised_completion_date` must equal the policy's current `adjusted_completion_date` advanced by exactly `requested_extension_days` days. A date that does not reconcile is refused with `ERR_REVISED_DATE_MISMATCH`. There is also a hard ceiling on how far a project may be endorsed rather than re-underwritten: the policy's `cumulative_extension_days` plus the `requested_extension_days` now being asked for, divided by the policy's `original_term_days`, must not exceed one half. A project that has slipped more than half its original term needs a fresh underwriting decision, not another endorsement, and the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Both rules apply identically to a resubmission.

Filing prices the request immediately, and the evaluation order is pinned because the tier depends on a cumulative figure that already includes the days being priced, and because the daily rate is rounded *before* it is multiplied out. First, the cumulative-after figure is the policy's `cumulative_extension_days` plus the `requested_extension_days` of this request. Second, the slip ratio is that cumulative-after figure divided by the policy's `original_term_days`, kept as an exact fraction, and the `slip_factor` is 1.00 when the ratio is at most one tenth, 1.15 when the ratio is greater than one tenth but at most one quarter, and 1.40 when it is greater than one quarter. Third, the daily base rate is the policy's `quoted_premium_cents` divided by its `original_term_days`, rounded half up to whole cents at this point. Fourth, `additional_premium_cents` is that rounded daily rate multiplied by `requested_extension_days` and by the `slip_factor`, rounded half up to whole cents. Fifth, the project's risk index is consulted: the audit score is the project's `audit_risk_score` where one exists and its `submission_risk_score` otherwise, read as of the *start* of this request — so it excludes the days being asked for here even though the first step has already counted them — and when that figure is 85 or more, the additional premium is multiplied by 1.25 and rounded half up again as a catastrophe-reslip load.

The pricing inputs are worth stating exactly. Only `APPROVED` endorsements contribute to the policy's `cumulative_extension_days`; endorsements sitting in `SUPERSEDED`, `DECLINED`, `LAPSED` or `RESCINDED` contribute zero. `original_term_days` is always the term as originally submitted and is never re-derived from a `term_end_date` that endorsements have already moved. `quoted_premium_cents` is used as stored, including any referral load already applied to it. On a **first slip**, where `cumulative_extension_days` is still 0, the ratio is driven purely by the days being requested, so a request of at most a tenth of the original term prices at factor 1.00. On a **repeat slip**, where `cumulative_extension_days` is already above 0, the ratio includes the days approved previously, so an identical `requested_extension_days` can price a tier higher than the first request did.

A filed endorsement lands in `REQUESTED` with `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the acting operator, `resubmission_count` at 0, `decided_by_user_id` and `info_request_note` null, and `slip_factor` and `additional_premium_cents` carrying the figures just computed — quoted, not yet posted to the ledger. Filing also supersedes any open predecessor: an endorsement of the same `policy_id` sitting in `REQUESTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, keeping its own computed `additional_premium_cents` for audit but never posting a ledger entry. The parent policy's `version` increments by 1. An endorsement's `status` spans `REQUESTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, `APPROVED`, `DECLINED`, `LAPSED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `RESCINDED`, of which the last five are terminal.

An `UnderwritingManager` who wants better schedule evidence before deciding can send a request back, supplying an `info_request_note`. This requires the endorsement to be `REQUESTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENDORSEMENT_STATE` — and the acting user must not be the endorsement's `requested_by_user_id`. The endorsement moves `REQUESTED → RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, the note is stored, and `version` increments by 1.

The `Underwriter` may then resubmit, which closes the revision loop `REQUESTED → RETURNED_FOR_INFO → REQUESTED`. Resubmission requires the endorsement to be `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, and the loop counter is checked only after that state test passes: `resubmission_count` must be below 3, and the fourth resubmission is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. A resubmission overwrites `requested_extension_days` and `revised_completion_date` from the request, and `slip_reason` too when the request supplies one, increments `resubmission_count` by 1, recomputes `slip_factor` and `additional_premium_cents` from scratch against the policy's state as of this request, returns the endorsement to `REQUESTED`, and increments `version` by 1.

Deciding an endorsement is open to both the `Underwriter` and the `UnderwritingManager`, subject to the authority limit of §2.10, and the decider supplies `decision` as `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. Either way the endorsement must be `REQUESTED`, and the acting user must differ from `requested_by_user_id`. `DECLINE` moves the endorsement `REQUESTED → DECLINED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and does nothing else at all: no ledger row is written and the policy's term is untouched.

`APPROVE` moves the endorsement `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and then runs four hops. It additionally requires the endorsement's policy to be `IN_FORCE` — an expired or cancelled policy can neither take on nor give back endorsed days — and it re-checks the half-of-original-term ceiling on the cumulative figure. Hop one sets the endorsement to `APPROVED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and freezes `additional_premium_cents` at its computed value. Hop two, because the endorsement was approved, adds `requested_extension_days` to the policy's `cumulative_extension_days`, advances the policy's `term_end_date` by the same number of days, and moves the policy's `adjusted_completion_date` to the endorsement's `revised_completion_date` — which, by the reconciliation rule above, is identically the project's `projected_completion_date` advanced by the policy's new `cumulative_extension_days`. Hop three, because `cumulative_extension_days` changed, recomputes the project's `audit_risk_score`. Hop four appends a premium ledger entry with `txn_type` `EXTENSION_AP`, `amount_cents` equal to the endorsement's `additional_premium_cents`, `effective_date` equal to the policy's pre-extension `term_end_date` and a `memo` recording the extension approval, and, because that row was appended, recomputes the policy's `written_premium_cents`. The policy's `version` and the project's `version` each increment by 1.

The **audit basis** of the risk index deserves its own statement, because it is the same six-component arithmetic as §3.1 evaluated on realised rather than submitted inputs, and it therefore yields a different number from identical project inputs. On this basis the insured value is the policy's `insured_tiv_cents`, so an existing structure the policy chose not to insure drops out of the value component altogether. The duration is the whole number of days from the project's `planned_start_date` to the policy's `adjusted_completion_date`, so realised slip is included in the schedule component. And the claim count is the number of qualifying claims: the basis reads the project's single non-terminal policy, meaning the one whose status is `BOUND` or `IN_FORCE`, and counts that policy's claims whose `adjustment_status` is `ADJUSTED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED`. Claims sitting in `REPORTED`, `DENIED` or `REOPENED` are excluded, and claims belonging to a `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` policy are excluded. Five points are counted per qualifying claim, capped at 15, exactly as in §3.1. The audit score is recomputed after every approved extension, every rescission and every claim adjustment, and it is the figure the catastrophe-reslip load reads.

Because an approval moves dates and money, the unit needs a way to take one back. Rescission is the compensating action, reserved to the `UnderwritingManager`. It requires the endorsement to be `APPROVED`, the policy to still be `IN_FORCE`, and the logical clock to be strictly before the policy's `term_end_date`. The endorsement moves `APPROVED → RESCINDED` and every hop of the approval is undone: a ledger entry is appended with `txn_type` `EXTENSION_AP_REVERSAL`, `amount_cents` equal to the negative of the endorsement's `additional_premium_cents`, `reverses_sequence_no` pointing at the `sequence_no` of that endorsement's own `EXTENSION_AP` row, and a `memo` recording the rescission; `written_premium_cents` is recomputed and therefore returns to exactly its pre-approval value; the policy's `cumulative_extension_days` is reduced by `requested_extension_days`, and its `term_end_date` and `adjusted_completion_date` are each pulled back by the same number of days; and the project's `audit_risk_score` is recomputed on the restored dates. The policy's `version` and the project's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.7. Loss Adjustment: Valuation Basis and the Allocation Waterfall

First notice of loss is taken by a `ClaimAdjuster`, who records the `policy_id`, the cause of loss as `peril` (one of `WIND`, `FLOOD`, `FIRE`, `THEFT`, `WATER_DAMAGE`, `TESTING_FAILURE` or `DELAY`), the `loss_date`, and a free-text `description` of what happened. The policy must be `IN_FORCE` at the moment of notice, and a notice against a policy in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. The `loss_date` must fall within the policy term with both bounds inclusive — at or after `term_start_date` and at or before `term_end_date`, evaluated against the `term_end_date` as it currently stands after any approved endorsements — and a date outside that span is refused with `ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`. A new claim lands with `adjustment_status` at `REPORTED`, `payment_status` at `UNPAID`, `reported_at` stamped from the logical clock, `reopen_count` at 0, `adjustment_basis` and `adjusted_by_user_id` null, `continuing_pm_weeks` at 0, `allocation_lines` empty, and `indemnity_cents`, `uncovered_cents`, `delay_days_indemnified`, `paid_cents`, `outstanding_reserve_cents` and `total_incurred_cents` all at 0.

A claim carries **two independent state axes on one row**. The adjustment axis, `adjustment_status`, runs over `REPORTED`, `ADJUSTED`, `DENIED`, `SETTLED`, `CLOSED` and `REOPENED`. The payment axis, `payment_status`, runs over `UNPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` and `PAID`. Neither axis is terminal, and the two move independently: the payment axis moves as a consequence of payments being issued or voided without any adjustment-axis trigger, and the adjustment axis moves under its own triggers without the payment axis being touched. The only coupling between them is the closure rule in §3.8.

Adjustment is the `ClaimAdjuster`'s act. The adjuster supplies an `outcome` of either `ADJUST` or `DENY`, and on an `ADJUST` outcome also the valuation `adjustment_basis` (`RECONSTRUCTION_COST` or `ACTUAL_LOSS`), the `continuing_pm_weeks` of project-management supervision being claimed (a whole number from 0 to 12 inclusive, defaulting to 0), and the `loss_items`, each item naming a `part_type` and the `gross_loss_cents` claimed from that bucket. The claim's `adjustment_status` must be `REPORTED`, `REOPENED` or `ADJUSTED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Re-adjusting a claim that is already `ADJUSTED` is a self-loop that additionally requires the payment axis to still read `UNPAID`, because once any money has gone out the valuation is closed to recomputation. The policy must be `IN_FORCE` throughout. `loss_items` must be non-empty for every peril except `DELAY`, and no two items in one request may name the same `part_type` — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOSS_ITEM`.

Only `ACTIVE` coverage parts of the claim's policy can absorb an allocation. Every `part_type` named in `loss_items` must match an `ACTIVE` `CoveragePart` of that policy; a bucket that was never scheduled, or one that closed when the policy left force, is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_PART_MISSING`. Three coverage conditions sit on top of that. A `DELAY` peril requires the project to have declared its `actual_substantial_completion_date` — that is, to be `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` — and requires the policy to carry an `ACTIVE` `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION` part; either absence is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE`. A `TESTING_COMMISSIONING` loss item, or a claim whose `peril` is `TESTING_FAILURE`, requires an `APPROVED` testing and commissioning notice on the same project whose window contains the claim's `loss_date`; a notice still `SCHEDULED`, or one `REJECTED`, does not satisfy the condition, and the adjustment is refused with `ERR_TESTING_NOTICE_REQUIRED`. A `TEMPORARY_STORAGE` loss item requires a temporary storage assignment of the same policy whose window contains the `loss_date` and whose status was `APPROVED` at that date; goods released before the loss are not covered at the storage location, and the adjustment is refused with `ERR_STORAGE_NOT_APPROVED`. These conditions are what the coverage part's `condition_code` records: a part carrying `APPROVED_NOTICE_REQUIRED` may only indemnify a loss for which the corresponding approved notice exists, and a part carrying `MITIGATION_PLAN_REQUIRED` is the trigger for the mitigation-plan gate of §3.5, while a part carrying `NONE` imposes no additional condition.

The allocation itself is a **waterfall**, and its order is pinned. Buckets are always processed in this sequence: `EXISTING_STRUCTURE`, then `MATERIALS_ON_SITE`, then `IN_TRANSIT`, then `TEMPORARY_STORAGE`, then `TESTING_COMMISSIONING`, then `WATER_DAMAGE`, then `SOFT_COST`, then `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION`. Seven steps run in order.

**Step one — line assembly.** The submitted `loss_items` become allocation lines sorted by their `part_type`'s position in the pinned sequence, ascending. When the claim's `peril` is `DELAY` the request carries no loss items at all; instead a single synthetic line is used, with `part_type` `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION` and `gross_loss_cents` equal to the gross delay indemnity computed below.

**Step two — valuation basis, rounded per line.** Under `RECONSTRUCTION_COST` each line's `covered_basis_cents` is its `gross_loss_cents` multiplied by 1.08 — the rebuild-to-code uplift — and rounded half up to whole cents at once. Under `ACTUAL_LOSS` each line's `covered_basis_cents` is its `gross_loss_cents` unchanged, since only documented invoices are paid. A `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION` line never takes the uplift under either basis. The rounding here is strictly per line: rounding each line as it is computed and then summing is the rule, and summing unrounded values and rounding once can differ by a cent and is not the rule.

**Step three — continuing project management.** Under `RECONSTRUCTION_COST` only, the carrier also indemnifies the supervision cost of running the rebuild. The project-management amount is the sum of `covered_basis_cents` across all lines, multiplied by fifteen hundredths of one percent (0.0015) per week, multiplied by the lesser of `continuing_pm_weeks` and the twelve-week maximum, rounded half up to whole cents. That amount is added to the `SOFT_COST` line's `covered_basis_cents`. When the request carried no `SOFT_COST` line and the project-management amount is strictly greater than zero, a line is appended at `SOFT_COST`'s position in the pinned sequence with `gross_loss_cents` of 0 and `covered_basis_cents` equal to that amount. When the project-management amount is exactly zero no synthetic line is created at all, so a `RECONSTRUCTION_COST` adjustment with `continuing_pm_weeks` of 0 produces exactly as many allocation lines as it had loss items. Under `ACTUAL_LOSS` the project-management amount is forced to zero: `continuing_pm_weeks` is still stored on the claim but ignored, which is precisely why identical loss items indemnify less under `ACTUAL_LOSS` than under `RECONSTRUCTION_COST`.

**Step four — the occurrence deductible, applied exactly once.** Among the lines whose `part_type` is not `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION`, the target line is the one with the greatest `covered_basis_cents`; where two lines tie, the one earlier in the pinned sequence wins. That target line's `deductible_applied_cents` is the lesser of its own `covered_basis_cents` and the policy's `occurrence_deductible_cents`. Every other line has `deductible_applied_cents` of 0. Each line's net amount is its `covered_basis_cents` less its `deductible_applied_cents`, so the money deductible never bites a delay line.

**Step five — the sublimit cap.** Each line is capped against its own bucket: its `allocated_cents` becomes the lesser of the line's net amount and the matching coverage part's `sublimit_cents` less that part's `eroded_cents`, where `eroded_cents` reflects `ISSUED` payments only and voided payments contribute nothing.

**Step six — the policy aggregate cap.** The lines are then walked once more in the pinned sequence. The remaining aggregate at each line is the policy's `limit_cents` less its `eroded_total_cents` less the `allocated_cents` of the lines already walked in this pass, and the line's `allocated_cents` is reduced to the lesser of itself and that remaining aggregate, floored at zero.

**Step seven — the claim totals.** `indemnity_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` across the lines. Each line's `uncovered_cents` is its net amount less its `allocated_cents`. The claim's `uncovered_cents` is the sum of the lines' `uncovered_cents` plus the deductible retained by the insured on the target line. `outstanding_reserve_cents` is `indemnity_cents` less `paid_cents`, and `total_incurred_cents` is `paid_cents` plus `outstanding_reserve_cents` — which means `total_incurred_cents` must always equal `indemnity_cents`. The finished lines are stored in `allocation_lines`, each carrying `part_type`, the `coverage_part_id` of the bucket it drew on, `gross_loss_cents`, `covered_basis_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `allocated_cents` and `uncovered_cents`, held in the pinned sequence order. No rounding of any kind occurs after step three.

A delay loss is priced before the waterfall runs. The baseline is the policy's `adjusted_completion_date` and the actual is the project's `actual_substantial_completion_date`; if the latter is absent the adjustment is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE`. The raw delay is the whole number of days from baseline to actual, floored at zero. The indemnified delay is the raw delay less the five-day waiting period, again floored at zero, and the comparison is strict in the sense that a raw delay of exactly five days indemnifies zero days. The gross delay amount is the indemnified day count multiplied by the policy's `daily_delay_rate_cents`, and that figure becomes the single `DELAY_IN_COMPLETION` line handed to the waterfall, where it is capped at the delay bucket's sublimit and, as step four says, never reduced by the money deductible. The indemnified day count is stored on the claim as `delay_days_indemnified`, and remains 0 for every peril other than `DELAY`. Because approved extensions push the baseline forward, they shrink the raw delay: with `cumulative_extension_days` still at 0 the baseline is the project's `projected_completion_date` exactly as submitted, whereas once `cumulative_extension_days` is above 0 the baseline is pushed out by exactly that many days, so an identical `actual_substantial_completion_date` indemnifies fewer days. Endorsements that were `RESCINDED` or `DECLINED` move the baseline not at all.

The outcome of an adjustment depends on both the requested `outcome` and the money. An `ADJUST` outcome from `REPORTED` or `REOPENED` that produces a strictly positive `indemnity_cents` moves the adjustment axis to `ADJUSTED`, stores the allocation lines and every derived figure, sets `adjustment_basis`, `continuing_pm_weeks` and `adjusted_by_user_id`, and recomputes `outstanding_reserve_cents` and `total_incurred_cents`. An `ADJUST` outcome from `REPORTED` or `REOPENED` that computes to an `indemnity_cents` of exactly zero — a delay entirely inside the waiting period, for instance — moves the axis to `DENIED` instead, storing the zero-value allocation lines and `uncovered_cents` so the denial is auditable. A `DENY` outcome moves the axis to `DENIED` with `indemnity_cents` of 0 and `allocation_lines` empty. The self-loop `ADJUSTED → ADJUSTED` fully replaces `allocation_lines`, `indemnity_cents`, `uncovered_cents`, `adjustment_basis` and `continuing_pm_weeks`, and a re-adjustment that happens to recompute to zero indemnity stays `ADJUSTED` — the denial branch is reachable only out of `REPORTED` or `REOPENED`. Every adjustment, whichever branch it takes, also recomputes the project's `audit_risk_score` (the claim now counts toward the claim-history component) and increments the project's `version` by 1 alongside the claim's own `version`.

Settlement is what opens the door to payment, and it is a checker step: either a `ClaimAdjuster` other than the one who adjusted, or an `UnderwritingManager`, may settle. The claim must be `ADJUSTED` with `indemnity_cents` strictly greater than zero, and the acting user must differ from `adjusted_by_user_id`. The adjustment axis moves `ADJUSTED → SETTLED`, which freezes `allocation_lines` against further re-adjustment; the payment axis is untouched by this transition, and the claim's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Paying, Voiding and Reopening

Indemnity leaves the carrier as a **loss payment**, issued by a `ClaimAdjuster` against one coverage bucket and to one named payee. The payload carries the `claim_id`, the parent-version declaration `expected_claim_version` of §2.9, the `payee_interest_id` of the party being paid, the `part_type` of the bucket being drawn down, and the `amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero. The claim must be `ADJUSTED` or `SETTLED` — a claim still `REPORTED`, one `DENIED`, one `REOPENED` or one already `CLOSED` pays nothing — and its policy must be `IN_FORCE`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`, never `DRAFT`, `QUOTED` or `BOUND`, since a policy that never attached has no losses to pay. Both refusals carry `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Four further gates run in the order §2.11 fixes. The payee must belong to the right policy: `payee_interest_id` must reference a `NamedInsuredInterest` whose `policy_id` equals the claim's `policy_id`, and paying a loss payee attached to some other policy is refused with `ERR_PAYEE_NOT_ON_POLICY` before any money check runs at all. The bucket must have received an allocation: `part_type` must appear in the claim's `allocation_lines` with an `allocated_cents` strictly greater than zero, or the payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_PART_NOT_ALLOCATED`. The payment must fit within that bucket's allocation: `amount_cents` must not exceed the matching allocation line's `allocated_cents` less whatever `ISSUED` payments have already been made against that same `part_type` on this claim, and a breach is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_ALLOCATION`. Finally the payment must fit both the claim's outstanding reserve and the bucket's remaining availability: `amount_cents` must not exceed `indemnity_cents` less `paid_cents`, and must not exceed the target coverage part's `available_cents`, with either breach refused as `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE`.

An accepted payment lands with `status` `ISSUED`, `created_by_user_id` stamped from the acting operator, and `voided_by_user_id` null, and then four hops run. Hop one runs the erosion reconciliation of §2.12, raising the matching coverage part's `eroded_cents` and lowering its `available_cents`. Hop two, because that part's erosion changed, raises the policy's `eroded_total_cents` and the claim's `paid_cents` and lowers the claim's `outstanding_reserve_cents`. Hop three moves the payment axis: `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` when the resulting `paid_cents` is strictly between zero and `indemnity_cents`, and straight to `PAID` — from either `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` — when the resulting `paid_cents` equals `indemnity_cents` exactly. Hop four couples the axes in the one place they touch: when the payment axis becomes `PAID` and the adjustment axis is `SETTLED`, the adjustment axis moves `SETTLED → CLOSED`. The claim's `version` and the policy's `version` each increment by 1. A payment's `status` is either `ISSUED` or `VOIDED`, and `VOIDED` is terminal.

Voiding is the compensating action for a payment, reserved to the `UnderwritingManager` and refused when the acting user is the payment's own `created_by_user_id`. The payment must still be `ISSUED`; voiding an already `VOIDED` payment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`. Hop one moves the payment `ISSUED → VOIDED` and stamps `voided_by_user_id`. Hop two, because the payment was voided, drops the claim's `paid_cents` by the voided `amount_cents` and moves the payment axis accordingly — `PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` when some `ISSUED` payments remain, `PARTIALLY_PAID → UNPAID` when the resulting `paid_cents` reaches exactly zero, and `PAID → UNPAID` when the void was of the only payment on the claim — and where the adjustment axis had reached `CLOSED` it returns `CLOSED → SETTLED` as soon as `paid_cents` falls below `indemnity_cents`. Hop three, because the claim's paid figure dropped, reduces the matching coverage part's `eroded_cents` by the voided `amount_cents` and restores its `available_cents`. Hop four, because that part's erosion changed, recomputes the policy's `eroded_total_cents`. The claim's `version` and the policy's `version` each increment by 1, and because every one of these figures is recomputed from the surviving `ISSUED` rows rather than nudged, an issue followed by a void leaves the account bit-for-bit where it started.

Claims sometimes have to be revisited. A `ClaimAdjuster` may reopen a claim whose adjustment axis reads `CLOSED` or `DENIED`, provided the policy is `IN_FORCE` or `EXPIRED` and the loop counter allows it: `reopen_count` must be below 2, checked only after the state precondition passes, and a third reopening is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. Reopening moves the axis to `REOPENED`, increments `reopen_count` by 1, and increments the claim's `version` by 1; it deliberately touches neither `paid_cents` nor any coverage-part erosion, so money already out stays out. The reopen cycle is closed by a fresh adjustment: `CLOSED` or `DENIED` becomes `REOPENED`, and `REOPENED → ADJUSTED` re-runs the whole waterfall exactly as it does from `REPORTED`, with the policy required to be `IN_FORCE` for that re-adjustment to be admissible.

### 3.9. End of Term: Expiry and Cancellation

A policy that runs its course expires by itself. Because the service runs on a logical clock, expiry happens when an operator advances the clock to or past a policy's `term_end_date`: every policy that is `IN_FORCE` and whose `term_end_date` is at or before the new clock value moves `IN_FORCE → EXPIRED`, and the cascade of §2.7 then closes that policy's `ACTIVE` coverage parts, releases its `APPROVED` storage assignments with `release_reason` of `POLICY_EXPIRY`, recomputes `committed_value_cents` on every facility those assignments referenced, lapses every `REQUESTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` extension endorsement without writing any ledger entry, and recomputes the project's `in_force_policy_count`. Each entity the sweep touches has its `version` incremented by 1, and the sweep reports what it did through `expired_policy_count`, `closed_coverage_part_count`, `released_assignment_count` and `lapsed_endorsement_count`.

Cancelling mid-term is the `UnderwritingManager`'s decision, and it returns money. The manager supplies the `cancel_reason` — `INSURED_REQUEST`, `CARRIER_INITIATED` or `PROJECT_ABANDONED` — and the `cancel_effective_date` at which cover stops. The policy must be `IN_FORCE`. The effective date must be strictly later than `term_start_date`, at or before `term_end_date`, and at or after the current logical clock: a cancellation takes effect now or in the future, and a back-dated one is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID`.

The return premium is computed on the policy's `written_premium_cents` as of the cancellation request — which is to say, including every approved extension charge and every rescission reversal already posted, and reduced by nothing at all on account of claim payments. The total term is the policy's `original_term_days` plus its `cumulative_extension_days`. The earned days are the whole number of days from `term_start_date` to `cancel_effective_date`, and the unearned days are the total term less the earned days, floored at zero. The pro-rata figure is `written_premium_cents` multiplied by the unearned days and divided by the total term, rounded half up to whole cents — and the order here is pinned: the pro-rata figure is rounded *first*, and any retention and fee then apply to the rounded figure. Where the cancellation is at the carrier's initiative or because the project was abandoned, the insured receives that full pro-rata figure with no retention and no fee. Where the cancellation is at the insured's own request, a short-rate calculation applies instead: the carrier keeps a tenth of the unearned premium and charges a flat expense fee of 25000 cents ($250), so the return is ninety percent of the rounded pro-rata figure — itself rounded half up to whole cents — less 25000 cents, floored at zero. The same term and premium therefore return strictly less on an insured-request cancellation than on a carrier-initiated one. The result is stored as `return_premium_cents`, which stays null on any policy that is not `CANCELLED`.

Cancellation then does everything at once. `cancel_reason` and `cancel_effective_date` are stored and the policy moves `IN_FORCE → CANCELLED`. A premium ledger entry is appended with `txn_type` `RETURN_PREMIUM`, `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `return_premium_cents`, `effective_date` equal to `cancel_effective_date` and a `memo` recording the cancellation return, and, because that row was appended, `written_premium_cents` is recomputed and therefore falls by exactly the returned amount. The §2.7 cascade then runs in full: every `ACTIVE` coverage part of the policy becomes `CLOSED`; every `APPROVED` temporary storage assignment becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` of `POLICY_CANCELLATION`; each affected facility's `committed_value_cents` is recomputed; every extension endorsement sitting in `REQUESTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` becomes `LAPSED` with no ledger entry; and the project's `in_force_policy_count` returns to 0. The policy's `version` increments by 1, as does that of every record the cascade touched.
