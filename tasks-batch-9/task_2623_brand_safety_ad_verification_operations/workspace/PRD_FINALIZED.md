# Brand Safety & Ad Verification Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An independent ad-verification provider sits between two counterparties who do not trust each other's numbers. On the buy side, advertisers pay a CPM for impressions they believe were seen by a human on a page whose content they are willing to stand next to. On the supply side, publishers monetise inventory whose content is classified, and sometimes reclassified, by systems they do not control. This platform is the neutral record between them: it classifies pages into brand-suitability tiers rather than a binary safe/unsafe split, it blocks unwanted inventory before the bid through inclusion and exclusion segments pushed to the buying platform, it measures viewability and invalid traffic after the bid, it certifies or quarantines each measured delivery against an accredited methodology, and it adjudicates the money and the disputes that follow — refund claims filed by advertisers against publishers, and appeals filed by publishers against classification decisions.

Three pressures shape the design. First, **every number must be reproducible**: a viewability rate, a made-for-advertising score, or a credit amount that cannot be recomputed from stored inputs is worthless in a dispute, so thresholds live on a versioned, audited methodology record rather than in code, and every measured batch pins the methodology version it was scored under. Second, **the same underlying data must be allowed to produce different answers in different contexts** — a page can be blocked pre-bid while post-bid monitoring records it at a milder tier, and a claim's provisional credit at intake is deliberately computed on a broader basis than the final credit at approval. Third, **money moves in matched pairs**: every credit posted to an advertiser is simultaneously charged back to a publisher, and every reversal restores both sides to exactly their pre-approval values.

Because the domain is genuinely flat — no single business aggregate owns the surface — the API mounts every entity as its own top-level collection at the API root (`/publishers`, `/pages`, `/measurement-batches`, `/refund-claims`, and so on) and carries links between them as identifier fields in request bodies rather than as URL nesting. Two exceptions exist for reverse reachability: the entries of one inventory list and the invalid-traffic findings of one measurement batch are read through the parent's path.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Callers, Roles and Identity

Every request carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` names the capability set the caller is acting under. If `X-User-Role` is absent or holds a value outside the seven declared roles, or if `X-User-ID` is absent for a role whose identity is entity-backed, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The one exception is the logical-clock endpoint, which is exempt from the general authentication rule but still requires `X-User-Role` to be present and to name `PlatformAdmin`. Once authenticated, a caller whose role is not listed for the target endpoint in §4 is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `Advertiser`, `Publisher`, `ClassificationAnalyst`, `DisputeOfficer`, `AccreditationAuditor`, and `MeasurementOperator`.

`PlatformAdmin` is the verification provider's own operator: it registers both counterparties, changes publisher standing, drives the logical clock, and reverses settled credits. `ClassificationAnalyst` is the content-classification operator that creates and reviews page classifications. `DisputeOfficer` adjudicates both dispute channels — publisher appeals and advertiser refund claims. `AccreditationAuditor` authors methodology versions and concludes their accreditation audits. `MeasurementOperator` ingests, scores and certifies measurement batches and adjudicates invalid-traffic findings. For all five of these roles the identity is **opaque**: when `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ClassificationAnalyst`, `DisputeOfficer`, `AccreditationAuditor` or `MeasurementOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a transition writes it down — `classifier_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a page classification, `decided_by_user_id` on an appeal and on a refund claim, `authored_by_user_id` and `lead_auditor_user_id` on a methodology version — and those recorded values are later compared to a subsequent caller's `X-User-ID` for the segregation-of-duties rules described in §2.9.

The two remaining roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Advertiser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting advertiser; ownership checks on any resource that resolves to an advertiser compare that advertiser identifier to `X-User-ID` — a campaign's `advertiser_id`, an inventory list's `advertiser_id`, a pre-bid segment's campaign's `advertiser_id`, and a refund claim's campaign's `advertiser_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Publisher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting publisher; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to a page's `publisher_id`, to the `publisher_id` of the page behind an appealed classification, to a measurement batch's `publisher_id`, and to the publisher whose scorecard is being read. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `PlatformAdmin` caller reads every collection without any ownership restriction.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the beginning of the result set. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, so a stable page boundary is preserved as new records arrive at the tail.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current page. Filter query parameters are combined conjunctively: a request that supplies several filters returns only the records satisfying all of them.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. That rendering is canonical for every instant the API ever emits — `updated_at`, `published_at`, `synced_at`, `scored_at` and the clock setter's `current_utc` alongside `created_at` — and it governs instants the caller supplied just as much as the ones the server mints: a submitted instant (`effective_from`, `effective_to`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `to_iso8601`) is parsed into the UTC instant it denotes and is stored and echoed in that same three-fractional-digit rendering rather than kept as the caller's literal text, so a `window_start` or `effective_from` sent as `2026-06-01T00:00:00Z` reads back as `2026-06-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Almost every resource also carries `updated_at`, refreshed on each mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record — including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request. The single exception is the inventory list entry: a `ListEntry` is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

All time-dependent rules read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts, never falls back to wall-clock time, and is advanced only by an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Everything that depends on time reads that clock: the 14-day appeal filing window, the 30-day claim filing window, methodology effectivity windows, the check that a measurement window does not lie in the future, the 30-day trailing window of the pre-bid publisher rollup, and the `published_at`, `synced_at` and `scored_at` stamps.

Path parameters are resolved strictly from left to right. For a nested read such as the entries of one inventory list or the invalid-traffic findings of one batch, the parent identifier is resolved first: an unresolvable parent yields that parent's own not-found error (`ERR_INVENTORY_LIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEASUREMENT_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`) before any child filter or query parameter is examined. The same left-to-right discipline governs action endpoints: the `{id}` in the path is resolved before the request body is validated.

### 2.5. Suitability Tiers, Creative Formats and Content Categories

Brand suitability is graded on a five-rung ladder rather than a binary flag. From worst to best the tiers are `FLOOR`, `HIGH_RISK`, `MEDIUM_RISK`, `LOW_RISK` and `SAFE`, and they carry the ordinal ranks 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively. Comparisons throughout this document are made on that rank: a tier is *worse than* another when its rank is numerically smaller, and *at least as good as* another when its rank is greater than or equal. Post-bid measurement adds a sixth value, `UNRATED`, used when a page has no classification to speak of; `UNRATED` is never acceptable to any floor.

Creative format is one of `DISPLAY`, `LARGE_DISPLAY` (creatives of at least 242,500 pixels) or `VIDEO`, and it selects which of the methodology's in-view thresholds apply.

A classification assigns between zero and six content category codes drawn from this closed set: `ADULT`, `TERRORISM`, `ILLEGAL_DRUGS`, `ARMS`, `CRIME_VIOLENCE`, `HATE_SPEECH`, `PIRACY`, `DEATH_INJURY`, `TOBACCO_ALCOHOL`, `GAMBLING`, `DEBATED_SOCIAL`, `NEWS_HARD`, `SPORTS_NEWS`, `GENERAL`. Each code carries a risk weight in points: `ADULT`, `TERRORISM` and `ILLEGAL_DRUGS` weigh 100 points each; `ARMS`, `CRIME_VIOLENCE`, `HATE_SPEECH` and `PIRACY` weigh 40 points each; `DEATH_INJURY`, `TOBACCO_ALCOHOL`, `GAMBLING` and `DEBATED_SOCIAL` weigh 15 points each; `NEWS_HARD` and `SPORTS_NEWS` weigh 5 points each; `GENERAL` weighs 0 points.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Three precision regimes coexist, and mixing them produces wrong answers.

**Rates are carried to 4 decimal places, rounded half up.** This governs `coverage_rate`, `viewability_rate`, `ivt_rate`, the campaign coverage ratio, and the `ivt_rate_avg`, `viewability_rate_avg` and `overturn_rate` figures of the publisher scorecard.

**Scores are carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up.** This governs `mfa_score` — where each of the four components is rounded to two decimals individually and the sum is rounded to two decimals once more — as well as `adjusted_risk_points` and `quality_index`.

**Money is carried in whole cents, rounded half up.** Credit amounts are computed per cited measurement batch, each batch's amount rounded to whole cents *before* the amounts are summed. Summing unrounded amounts and rounding the total afterwards is forbidden and produces a different figure: with a `cpm_cents` of 875 and per-batch impression bases of 1501 and 2503, the pinned per-batch computation yields 1313 plus 2190 for a total of 3503, whereas rounding after summation would yield 3504.

Impression counters are always integers and are never rounded, because they are only ever added, subtracted, capped or compared.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Stale-View Rejection

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency on a parent record's `version`, and only those two carry a version field.

Creating a page classification requires `expected_page_version` in the request body as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Page`. If it does not equal the page's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard cannot be replaced by a state precondition, because a new classification legitimately supersedes the page's currently effective record: a client holding an outdated view of the page would silently supersede a classification it never saw. The current `version` of a page is exposed by the single-page read, which is the client's source for this token.

Creating a refund claim requires `expected_campaign_version` in the request body as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Campaign`. If it does not equal the campaign's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Claim creation is legitimately repeatable on the same campaign — each claim cites a different set of batches and each one raises `open_claim_count` — so the guard is not there to make the operation idempotent; it exists because the advertiser's view of the campaign's open-claim exposure and certified counters must be current before another claim is stacked on it. The single-campaign read is the client's source for this token.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence: The Order Checks Run In

Checks are evaluated in a strict ascending priority order. The **first** violated check returns its error and no later check is evaluated. This matters because many requests violate several rules at once, and the benchmark answer is the earliest violation, not the most severe one.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-Role` present and one of the seven roles; `X-User-ID` also required when the role is `Advertiser` or `Publisher`. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock setter, which still requires `X-User-Role`.
2. **Role authorisation** — the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Ownership** — an entity-backed caller may only reach its own aggregate, as described in §2.1. Violation: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose target resolves to an advertiser or a publisher, explicitly including the publisher scorecard read and the pre-bid decision read.
4. **Path existence** — every `{id}` path segment resolves to a stored entity of the expected type. Violation: the corresponding not-found error at HTTP 404. Applies to every path with an `{id}` segment.
5. **Body and query reference existence** — every foreign-key value in the request body resolves, including every element of `batch_ids` and the `segment_id` query parameter of the pre-bid decision read. Violation: the corresponding not-found error at 404. Applies to campaign creation, inventory-list creation, pre-bid-segment creation, page creation, classification creation, appeal filing, batch ingestion, finding creation, claim creation, and the pre-bid decision read.
6. **Request well-formedness** — required input fields present; enum fields inside their declared value sets; numbers inside their declared ranges (ratios and confidences between 0 and 1 inclusive, `originality_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, impression counters and `cpm_cents` non-negative integers, `category_codes` an array of known codes, `refresh_seconds` a non-negative integer, `batch_ids` a non-empty array of distinct identifiers). Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every write endpoint.
7. **State reachability** — the target entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Certification requires `scoring_status` `SCORED`; claim approval requires `claim_state` `UNDER_REVIEW`; publishing requires list `status` `DRAFT` or `STALE`; an appeal decision requires `appeal_state` `FILED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`; a finding confirmation requires `status` `PROPOSED`. Violation: the entity's own invalid-state error at HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine.
8. **Page version** — `expected_page_version` equals the current page `version`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_PAGE_VERSION` (412). Applies to classification creation.
9. **Campaign version** — `expected_campaign_version` equals the current campaign `version`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (412). Applies to refund-claim creation.
10. **Segregation of duties on appeals** — the deciding `DisputeOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` differs from both `reviewed_by_user_id` and `classifier_user_id` of the classification under appeal. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to the appeal decision.
11. **Segregation of duties on audits** — the concluding `AccreditationAuditor`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the methodology version's `authored_by_user_id`. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to the audit conclusion.
12. **Methodology accreditation** — the pinned methodology version's `accreditation_status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `ACCREDITED`. Violation: `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ACCREDITED` (409). Applies to batch scoring and to classification creation.
13. **Methodology effectivity** — the pinned methodology version is effective at the batch's `window_start`, meaning `effective_from` is at or before `window_start` and `window_start` is strictly before `effective_to`. Violation: `ERR_METHODOLOGY_LAPSED` (409). Applies to batch scoring.
14. **Revised tier discipline** — an `OVERTURNED` appeal decision supplies `revised_tier` and that tier is strictly better (a strictly greater rank) than the disputed classification's `assigned_tier`; an `UPHELD` decision must not supply `revised_tier`. Violation: `ERR_REVISED_TIER_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to the appeal decision.
15. **Batch scope** — every batch in `batch_ids` belongs both to the claim's `campaign_id` and to the claim's `publisher_id`. Violation: `ERR_BATCH_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to claim creation.
16. **Batch claimability** — every batch in `batch_ids` has `scoring_status` `QUARANTINED` or `DECERTIFIED`; an `INGESTED`, `SCORED` or `CERTIFIED` batch is not claimable. Violation: `ERR_BATCH_NOT_CLAIMABLE` (409). Applies to claim creation.
17. **Batch availability** — every batch in `batch_ids` has `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, that is, it is not already cited by a claim in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`. Violation: `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_CLAIMED` (409). Applies to claim creation.
18. **List slot and ownership match** — a supplied `inclusion_list_id` must name a list whose `list_type` is `INCLUSION`, a supplied `exclusion_list_id` one whose `list_type` is `EXCLUSION`, and every supplied list must belong to the same advertiser as the segment's campaign. Violation: `ERR_LIST_ADVERTISER_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to pre-bid-segment creation.
19. **Appeal target currency** — the appealed classification's `review_state` is `CONFIRMED` or `OVERRIDDEN` and its `id` equals the page's `current_classification_id`. Violation: `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_SUPERSEDED` (409). Applies to appeal filing.
20. **Single live appeal** — no other appeal on the same `classification_id` is in `FILED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`. Violation: `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING` (409). Applies to appeal filing.
21. **Format agreement** — a batch's `format` equals its campaign's `format`; a `VIDEO` campaign cannot receive `DISPLAY` measurement. Violation: `ERR_FORMAT_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to batch ingestion.
22. **Page ownership agreement** — a batch's `page_id` belongs to its `publisher_id`. Violation: `ERR_PAGE_PUBLISHER_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to batch ingestion.
23. **Counter consistency** — `measurable_impressions` is at most `measured_impressions`, `in_view_impressions` is at most `measurable_impressions`, and `measured_impressions` is greater than zero. Violation: `ERR_IMPRESSION_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT` (422). Applies to batch ingestion.
24. **Finding magnitude and confidence** — a finding's `flagged_impressions` does not exceed its batch's `measured_impressions`, and a confirmation requires `confidence` of at least 0.75. Violation: `ERR_IVT_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW` (409). Applies to finding creation and finding confirmation.
25. **Uniqueness of entries and page URLs** — the triple of `list_id`, `entry_type` and `entry_value` is unique within a list, and the pair of `publisher_id` and `url` is unique across pages. Violation: `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIST_ENTRY` (409) on entry creation, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAGE_URL` (409) on page creation.
26. **Appeal round ceiling** — an appeal may not open a fourth round: a `MORE_EVIDENCE` decision or an evidence submission when `round_no` is already 3 is refused, though a terminal `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` decision remains available at any round. Violation: `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to the appeal decision with outcome `MORE_EVIDENCE` and to evidence submission.
27. **Non-empty list and non-empty segment** — a list may not be published while its `entry_count` is 0, and a pre-bid segment must pin at least one of `inclusion_list_id` or `exclusion_list_id`. Violation: `ERR_EMPTY_LIST` (409) on publication, and `ERR_SEGMENT_REQUIRES_LIST` (409) on segment creation.
28. **No restricted allow entries** — a list may not be published while it holds an `ALLOW` entry naming a publisher whose `publisher_status` is `RESTRICTED` or `DELISTED`. Violation: `ERR_LIST_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PUBLISHER` (409). Applies to publication.
29. **Methodology window exclusivity** — effectivity windows are half-open, including `effective_from` and excluding `effective_to`, and must not overlap another version of the same `standard_family`; exactly abutting windows, where the previous version's `effective_to` equals the new version's `effective_from`, are legal. Violation: `ERR_METHODOLOGY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to methodology-version creation.
30. **Batch window separation** — batch windows are closed intervals including both `window_start` and `window_end`, and two batches sharing the same `campaign_id`, `page_id` and `format` must be separated by more than 60 seconds. A new batch is refused when its `window_start` is at or before the existing batch's `window_end` plus 60 seconds *and* the existing batch's `window_start` is at or before the new batch's `window_end` plus 60 seconds. Violation: `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_COLLISION` (409). Applies to batch ingestion.
31. **Batch window sanity** — `window_start` is strictly before `window_end`, and `window_end` is not after the current logical clock. Violation: `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE` (422). Applies to batch ingestion.
32. **Appeal filing window** — an appeal is filed within 14 days of the disputed classification's `created_at`, measured against the logical clock. Violation: `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to appeal filing.
33. **Claim filing window** — a claim is submitted within 30 days of the latest `window_end` among its cited batches. Violation: `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to claim creation.
34. **Credit cap** — an approval whose `net_credit_cents` would push the advertiser's `credit_received_cents_total` above `credit_cap_cents` is refused. Violation: `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to claim approval.
35. **Non-zero credit** — an approval whose recomputed `credited_impressions` is 0 is refused; the claim must be rejected instead. Violation: `ERR_NO_CREDITABLE_IMPRESSIONS` (409). Applies to claim approval.

Three precedence pairs are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. A rejected claim submitted for approval over an exhausted credit cap returns `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, because state reachability at priority 7 precedes the fiscal cap at priority 34. A batch whose counters are inconsistent *and* whose window collides with an existing batch returns `ERR_IMPRESSION_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT`, because counter consistency at priority 23 precedes window separation at priority 30. A self-review by the deciding officer on an appeal already at round 3 returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, because the duty check at priority 10 precedes the round ceiling at priority 26.

Two endpoint-local checks sit slightly outside this ladder and are stated where they belong. On classification creation, a `methodology_version_id` whose `standard_family` is not `SUITABILITY` is refused with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_FAMILY_MISMATCH` *before* the accreditation guard of priority 12 and the effectivity guard of priority 13 are consulted. On batch ingestion, a `methodology_version_id` whose `standard_family` is not `VIEWABILITY` is refused with the same `ERR_METHODOLOGY_FAMILY_MISMATCH`, evaluated together with that endpoint's other referential checks.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two adjudications are structurally protected against self-review, and in both cases the protection is enforced on `X-User-ID`, never on role — two different people holding the same role are perfectly acceptable counterparties.

An appeal decision may not be recorded by the `DisputeOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals either the `reviewed_by_user_id` or the `classifier_user_id` of the disputed classification. In practice this means the analyst who authored a classification, and the analyst who confirmed or overrode it, are both barred from deciding the appeal against it, and the platform therefore expects the analyst population and the officer population to be distinct identities even though the role names differ. An audit conclusion may not be recorded by the `AccreditationAuditor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the methodology version's `authored_by_user_id`. Both violations answer `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation

Several adjudications ripple outward through up to four hops. Cascades are executed immediately and synchronously inside the triggering request, so the response of that request already reflects every downstream effect. Every record a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — a client that pinned a version before the cascade will therefore find that token stale afterwards, which is exactly the serialisation signal the concurrency rules of §2.7 depend on.

Cascades never run backwards. When a publisher is reinstated to `ACTIVE`, the inventory lists that went `STALE` because of it are **not** automatically republished; the advertiser must publish them again. When a campaign leaves `UNDER_REMEDIATION` for `ACTIVE`, its `STALE` pre-bid segments are **not** automatically re-synced; the advertiser must sync them again. When a confirmed invalid-traffic finding is retracted, however, the batch's invalid-traffic figures are recomputed from the remaining confirmed findings and land exactly on their pre-confirmation values, with no residual drift — subject to the one nuance §3.8 records about a batch whose figures had never been computed before that confirmation.

### 2.11. The Double-Entry Credit Ledger and Impression Conservation

Two invariants hold at all times and are the platform's audit backbone.

**Credit is double-entered.** Every cent of `net_credit_cents` posted on an approved claim is added to the advertiser's `credit_received_cents_total` and, in the same operation, to the charged publisher's `credit_issued_cents_total`. A reversal subtracts the identical amount from both. The sum of `net_credit_cents` over all claims currently in `APPROVED` therefore always equals the total across advertisers' `credit_received_cents_total` and equally the total across publishers' `credit_issued_cents_total`. A reversal restores both figures to exactly their pre-approval values.

**Impressions are conserved.** For every scored batch, `measured_impressions` equals the sum of `qualified_in_view_impressions`, `non_viewable_impressions` and `unmeasurable_impressions`; before scoring those three counters still sit at the 0 defaults of §3.7, so the identity is not yet in force on an `INGESTED` batch. On the invalid-traffic side, `net_billable_impressions` plus `ivt_impressions` equals `measured_impressions` on every batch whose invalid-traffic figures have been computed at least once — that is, from the first confirmation, retraction or scoring onward — and it must hold before a confirmation, after a confirmation, and again after the matching retraction. A batch that has never been scored and has never had a finding confirmed or retracted is outside this second identity too: as §3.7 spells out, its `ivt_impressions` and `net_billable_impressions` are both still 0.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding Counterparties, Campaign Contracts and Supply-Side Standing

A `PlatformAdmin` opens the platform by registering the two counterparties. Registering an advertiser takes a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters and, optionally, a `credit_cap_cents` — the lifetime ceiling on credit this advertiser may ever receive from settled refund claims. When the caller omits it, the cap defaults to 1,000,000 cents, that is ten thousand dollars of lifetime credit; it must be zero or greater. The new advertiser starts with `credit_received_cents_total` at 0 and `settled_claim_count` at 0, and both of those are maintained by the platform rather than the client.

Registering a publisher takes a `name` under the same 1-to-120 non-blank rule, a `primary_domain` naming the registrable host of the publisher's flagship property in lowercase host form such as `news.example.com` with no scheme and no path, and optionally a `sourced_traffic_pct` between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive that declares the share of sessions the publisher buys from third-party traffic vendors. That share defaults to 0.0 and is more consequential than it looks: it is one of the four inputs to every made-for-advertising score computed against any page of this publisher. Two publishers may not share a `primary_domain`; a collision answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_PUBLISHER_DOMAIN`. A newly registered publisher stands at `publisher_status` `ACTIVE` with a null `status_reason`, and every quality counter — `mfa_page_count`, `quarantined_batch_count`, `certified_batch_count`, `decided_appeal_count`, `overturned_appeal_count` and `credit_issued_cents_total` — starts at 0.

Campaigns are the buy-side contract, and either a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `Advertiser` may create one. The payload names the owning `advertiser_id`, a `name`, a `cpm_cents` strictly greater than zero (the media cost per thousand impressions, and the basis on which every refund credit is later priced), a `min_suitability_tier` naming the lowest brand-suitability tier the campaign is willing to accept, a `format` that every one of this campaign's measurement batches must also declare, and optionally a `target_coverage_rate` between 0 exclusive and 1 inclusive that defaults to 0.75 — the contracted measurement coverage the campaign must sustain across its certified batches. A new campaign starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `coverage_status` `UNKNOWN`, `certified_measured_impressions` and `certified_measurable_impressions` at 0, and `open_claim_count` at 0. When an `Advertiser` creates the campaign, the `advertiser_id` in the payload must be the caller's own identifier. Campaigns can later be listed narrowed by `advertiser_id`, `status`, `coverage_status` and `format`.

Supply-side standing is the platform's main lever over a misbehaving publisher, and it moves along a three-value ladder. A publisher is `ACTIVE` until the platform itself restricts it, and `RESTRICTED` publishers can be reinstated or removed for good. Administrative standing changes carry a `new_status` and an optional free-text `reason`. Reinstatement moves `RESTRICTED` back to `ACTIVE` and writes `status_reason` `ADMIN_REINSTATEMENT`, but only while the conditions that justified the restriction have actually cleared: if `quarantined_batch_count` is 3 or more, or `mfa_page_count` is 3 or more, at the moment of reinstatement, the request is refused with `ERR_RESTRICTION_STILL_JUSTIFIED`. Delisting moves either `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED` to `DELISTED` and writes `status_reason` `ADMIN_ACTION`. `DELISTED` is terminal: any further standing change against a delisted publisher answers `ERR_PUBLISHER_DELISTED`, and a `new_status` that is not reachable from the publisher's current `publisher_status` — asking for `ACTIVE` on a publisher that is already `ACTIVE`, for instance — answers `ERR_INVALID_PUBLISHER_STATE`. Every standing change increments the publisher's `version` by 1.

Restriction itself is never requested by a client. The platform imposes it: the moment a certification quarantines a batch and drives `quarantined_batch_count` to 3, or a classification write drives `mfa_page_count` to 3, an `ACTIVE` publisher moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED` with `status_reason` set to `QUARANTINE_THRESHOLD` when `quarantined_batch_count` has reached 3 and to `MFA_THRESHOLD` otherwise, and the publisher's `version` increments by 1. The restriction then propagates, in the two further hops described in §3.5 and §3.6, into the advertiser lists and pre-bid segments that had explicitly allowed this publisher.

A publisher's own reputation is exposed as a computed scorecard, readable by a `PlatformAdmin`, a `DisputeOfficer`, or the publisher itself. The scorecard's evidence set is the publisher's **adjudicated** batches — those whose `scoring_status` is `CERTIFIED`, `QUARANTINED` or `DECERTIFIED`. Batches still `INGESTED` or `SCORED` have not been adjudicated and are excluded entirely, and the count of the evidence set is reported as `adjudicated_batch_count`. Over that set the platform sums `measured_impressions`, `ivt_impressions`, `measurable_impressions` and `qualified_in_view_impressions`. From those sums it derives three rates, each rounded half up to four decimals: `ivt_rate_avg` is the summed invalid-traffic impressions divided by the summed measured impressions, or exactly 0.0000 when no measured impressions exist; `viewability_rate_avg` is the summed qualified in-view impressions divided by the summed measurable impressions, or exactly 0.0000 when no measurable impressions exist; and `overturn_rate` is the publisher's `overturned_appeal_count` divided by its `decided_appeal_count`, or exactly 0.0000 when no appeal has been decided.

The index itself starts from 100 and is charged four penalties and granted one credit. The invalid-traffic penalty is `ivt_rate_avg` expressed as a percentage — that is, multiplied by 100 — and then multiplied by a weight of 2.0. The viewability penalty is exactly 0.00 when the publisher has no measurable impressions at all; otherwise it is the shortfall of `viewability_rate_avg` below a target of 0.70, floored at zero so that beating the target earns nothing extra, expressed as a percentage by multiplying by 100, and then multiplied by a weight of 1.5. The made-for-advertising penalty is 5 points for each page counted in `mfa_page_count`. The quarantine penalty is 3 points for each batch counted in `quarantined_batch_count`. The one credit is 10 points multiplied by `overturn_rate`, on the reasoning that a publisher which keeps winning its appeals was being classified too harshly. The raw figure is then clamped into the closed range from 0 to 100 and rounded half up to two decimals to give `quality_index`. Finally `tier_band` is `PREFERRED` when `quality_index` is at least 85.00, `STANDARD` when it is at least 60.00 but below 85.00, `WATCHLIST` when it is at least 40.00 but below 60.00, and `SUSPENDED_QUALITY` when it is below 40.00. Note the deliberate consequence of the empty-evidence rule: a publisher with no adjudicated batch has no measurable impressions, so the viewability penalty is 0.00 rather than the full 105.00 the shortfall arithmetic would otherwise charge, and its `quality_index` is exactly 100.00 in band `PREFERRED`. The scorecard also echoes back `publisher_id`, `mfa_page_count` and `quarantined_batch_count` alongside the computed figures.

### 3.2. Measurement Methodology and Accreditation Audits

Nothing on this platform is measured against a hard-coded threshold. An `AccreditationAuditor` authors a **methodology version**, and every classification and every measured batch pins the specific version it was evaluated under, so a later change of standard cannot silently rewrite history. A version belongs to a `standard_family`, one of `VIEWABILITY`, `IVT` or `SUITABILITY`, carries a `version_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique inside its family — a collision answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERSION_CODE` — and declares an effectivity window as `effective_from` and `effective_to`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, with `effective_to` strictly after `effective_from`. The window is half-open: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. Inside one `standard_family` windows may not overlap, and an attempt to author an overlapping version answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; windows that abut exactly, where the earlier version's `effective_to` equals the new version's `effective_from`, are legal and are the normal way to succeed a standard.

The thresholds a version carries are the substance of the standard. `display_in_view_seconds` defaults to 1.0 and `video_in_view_seconds` to 2.0; each must be greater than zero and states the minimum continuous in-view duration an impression of that format must reach to count as viewable. `display_pixel_pct` defaults to 0.5, `large_display_pixel_pct` to 0.3 and `video_pixel_pct` to 0.5; each must be greater than 0 and at most 1, and each states the minimum in-view pixel share for its format. `coverage_rate_floor` defaults to 0.7 and must be greater than 0 and at most 1: it is the minimum measurement coverage a batch must reach to be certified. `ivt_rate_ceiling` defaults to 0.02 and must be at least 0 and strictly below 1: it is the maximum invalid-traffic rate a batch may carry and still be certified. Every one of these is optional on creation and falls back to its default.

A freshly authored version stands at `accreditation_status` `PROVISIONAL`, and the platform stamps `authored_by_user_id` from the authoring auditor's `X-User-ID`. That stamp is the whole point of the audit workflow: it becomes the segregation-of-duties counterparty, so the person who wrote the standard can never be the person who signs off on it. `audit_outcome`, `audit_finding_rate`, `deficiency_note` and `lead_auditor_user_id` are all null until an audit concludes, and `decertified_batch_count` starts at 0. Versions can be listed narrowed by `standard_family` and `accreditation_status`.

Concluding an audit is the branching decision of this subsection. A second `AccreditationAuditor` supplies an `outcome`, an `audit_finding_rate` — the sampling error rate the auditor actually measured, which must lie between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive — and, where the outcome demands it, a `deficiency_note`. If the concluding auditor's `X-User-ID` equals the version's `authored_by_user_id`, the conclusion is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` before either branch is considered. A conclusion submitted against a version whose `accreditation_status` is already `SUSPENDED` or `LAPSED` answers `ERR_INVALID_METHODOLOGY_STATE`.

The benign branch is `PASSED`. A `PROVISIONAL` version moves `PROVISIONAL → ACCREDITED`, and the platform records `audit_outcome` `PASSED`, `lead_auditor_user_id` from the concluding auditor's `X-User-ID`, and the reported `audit_finding_rate`. The branch is admissible only while `audit_finding_rate` is at most 0.05; a passing conclusion reporting a higher error rate is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_RATE_TOO_HIGH`. The version's `version` increments by 1.

The severe branch is `DEFICIENT`, and it can be taken from either `PROVISIONAL` or `ACCREDITED` — the two transitions `PROVISIONAL → SUSPENDED` and `ACCREDITED → SUSPENDED` behave identically. A deficient conclusion must supply a non-blank `deficiency_note`; omitting it or supplying it blank answers `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOTE_REQUIRED`. On success the platform records `audit_outcome` `DEFICIENT`, `lead_auditor_user_id`, `audit_finding_rate` and `deficiency_note`, increments the version's `version` by 1, and then repudiates everything that version certified, in four hops.

In the first hop, every measurement batch pinning this version whose `scoring_status` is `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`, each such batch's `version` increments by 1, the batch's publisher has its `certified_batch_count` decremented by 1, and the version's `decertified_batch_count` is set to the number of batches moved. In the second hop, because those batches have left `CERTIFIED`, each affected campaign has the decertified batches' `measured_impressions` and `measurable_impressions` removed from its `certified_measured_impressions` and `certified_measurable_impressions`, and its `coverage_status` is recomputed. In the third hop, a campaign whose recomputed `coverage_status` is `BELOW_TARGET` moves `ACTIVE → UNDER_REMEDIATION`. In the fourth hop, because that campaign entered remediation, every pre-bid segment of it whose `sync_status` is `SYNCED` moves `SYNCED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `CAMPAIGN_UNDER_REMEDIATION`. Each record touched has its `version` incremented by 1. A decertified batch has one further consequence worth stating here even though it belongs to the money story: because the measurement itself has been repudiated, the batch becomes claimable — its `settlement_status` may now move from `UNBILLED` to `CLAIMED`.

Lapsing is the other way a version leaves service, and it is evaluated rather than requested. As the very first step of batch scoring, batch certification and classification creation, the platform checks the pinned version's effectivity against the logical clock: if the clock has reached or passed `effective_to`, a `PROVISIONAL` version moves `PROVISIONAL → LAPSED` and an `ACCREDITED` version moves `ACCREDITED → LAPSED`, that version's `version` increments by 1, and the triggering request is then rejected with `ERR_METHODOLOGY_LAPSED`. Both `SUSPENDED` and `LAPSED` are terminal for a methodology version.

### 3.3. Registering Pages and Classifying Content

Pages are registered either by the owning `Publisher` or by a `PlatformAdmin`, and the payload is deliberately thin: a `publisher_id` and a `url`. The URL must be 1 to 300 characters and must begin with `https://`, and no publisher may register the same `url` twice — a repeat answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAGE_URL`, while the same URL under a different publisher is permitted. From the URL the platform derives `domain`, the lowercase host, and stores it as a first-class field because it is the match key that domain-level list entries are compared against during pre-bid evaluation. A new page stands at `classification_state` `UNCLASSIFIED` with `current_tier` null, `current_classification_id` null, `latest_category_codes` empty, `mfa_score` null, `mfa_flag` false and `appeal_count` 0. Pages can be listed narrowed by `publisher_id`, by `current_classification_id`, by `classification_state`, and by `mfa_flag`.

Classification is the work of a `ClassificationAnalyst`, and each act of classification appends an immutable record rather than editing the page. The payload names the `page_id`, the `methodology_version_id` whose thresholds and standard the record is evaluated under, the `expected_page_version` token described in §2.7, and a `classifier_kind` of either `MODEL` for an automated verdict or `HUMAN` for an analyst-authored one. A third value, `APPEAL_REMEDY`, exists on the field but is never accepted from a client; only the platform writes it, and only when an appeal is overturned. The pinned methodology version must belong to the `SUITABILITY` family — a version from any other family answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_FAMILY_MISMATCH`, checked before the accreditation and effectivity guards — and its `accreditation_status` must be `PROVISIONAL` or `ACCREDITED`, failing which the write answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ACCREDITED`.

The rest of the payload is evidence. `category_codes` lists the content categories the classifier assigned, drawn from the closed set in §2.5 and holding at most six entries. `ad_density_ratio`, between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, is the share of the above-the-fold area occupied by ad slots. `refresh_seconds` is the page's auto-refresh interval in seconds and must be a non-negative integer, where 0 means the page does not auto-refresh at all. `originality_score` runs from 0.0 to 100.0 inclusive and rates editorial originality, with 100 meaning fully original content. `model_confidence`, between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, is optional and defaults to 1.0. Three of these — the ad density, the refresh interval and the originality score — plus the owning publisher's `sourced_traffic_pct` read at request time, are the made-for-advertising signals; `model_confidence` feeds the risk arithmetic instead.

**Evaluation order inside a classification write is pinned and must not be rearranged**, because each step consumes the previous one's output: first `mfa_score`, then `mfa_flag`, then `base_risk_points`, then `adjusted_risk_points`, then `assigned_tier`.

The made-for-advertising score is the sum of four independently computed components, each rounded half up to two decimals before it is added in, with the total rounded half up to two decimals once more. The **density component** takes `ad_density_ratio`, multiplies it by 100 to put it on a percentage scale, multiplies that by a density weight of 0.4, rounds the result half up to two decimals, and then caps it at 40.00 — a page that is entirely ad slots therefore contributes exactly 40.00 and no more. The **refresh component** is a step function of `refresh_seconds`: exactly 25.00 points when the interval is greater than 0 and at most 15 seconds, exactly 12.00 points when it is greater than 15 and at most 30 seconds, and exactly 0.00 points otherwise, which includes both slower refresh intervals and the no-refresh case where `refresh_seconds` is 0. The **sourced-traffic component** multiplies the publisher's `sourced_traffic_pct` by a maximum of 20.00 points and rounds half up to two decimals, so a publisher buying all of its sessions from vendors contributes the full 20.00. The **originality component** takes the distance of `originality_score` below 100, multiplies it by a weight of 0.15, and rounds half up to two decimals, so wholly unoriginal content contributes 15.00. The resulting `mfa_score` is stored on the classification record to two decimals, and `mfa_flag` is true exactly when `mfa_score` is greater than or equal to the flag threshold of 70.00 — at 70.00 precisely the flag is raised, not withheld. The flag that feeds the next step is the one this very request just computed; it must never be read from the page's previous classification.

Risk is then scored. `base_risk_points` is the sum of the per-category weights listed in §2.5 over the record's own `category_codes`, and it is 0.00 when the array is empty. Two multipliers are applied to it, in this pinned order: first the made-for-advertising multiplier, which is 1.5 when this record's `mfa_flag` is true and 1.0 otherwise; then the confidence multiplier, which is 1.25 when the `classifier_kind` is `MODEL` **and** `model_confidence` is strictly below the confidence floor of 0.80, and 1.0 in every other case. Rounding happens exactly once, half up to two decimals, after both multipliers have been applied, and the result is `adjusted_risk_points`. A `HUMAN` classification never attracts the confidence multiplier no matter what `model_confidence` the caller supplies.

`assigned_tier` follows from `adjusted_risk_points` on a four-boundary ladder: `FLOOR` when the points are 100.00 or more; `HIGH_RISK` when they are at least 40.00 but below 100.00; `MEDIUM_RISK` when they are at least 15.00 but below 40.00; `LOW_RISK` when they are at least 5.00 but below 15.00; and `SAFE` when they are below 5.00. The record is stored with `review_state` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `classifier_user_id` stamped from the calling analyst's `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_by_user_id` null and `review_note` null.

Where the page ends up depends on where it started, and four of its five lifecycle positions accept a new classification. From `UNCLASSIFIED` the page moves `UNCLASSIFIED → MODEL_CLASSIFIED`. From `MODEL_CLASSIFIED` a further write moves `MODEL_CLASSIFIED → MODEL_CLASSIFIED`, replacing an as-yet-unreviewed verdict. From `HUMAN_CONFIRMED` a write moves `HUMAN_CONFIRMED → MODEL_CLASSIFIED`, reopening review on a page that had already been attested. From `RECLASSIFIED` — the position a page holds after an appeal was overturned in its favour — a write moves `RECLASSIFIED → MODEL_CLASSIFIED`, and the sequence `RECLASSIFIED → MODEL_CLASSIFIED → HUMAN_CONFIRMED → APPEALED` is the page's ordinary revision cycle, which a page may run through repeatedly. The one position that refuses a classification write is `APPEALED`: while an appeal is live, further classification of that page answers `ERR_INVALID_PAGE_STATE`, and the page stays frozen until the appeal reaches `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED`.

Whichever position it came from, a successful write **supersedes** the record it displaces. The previously effective classification of that page has its `review_state` set to `SUPERSEDED` and its own `version` incremented by 1 — a `PENDING_REVIEW` record moves `PENDING_REVIEW → SUPERSEDED`, a `CONFIRMED` record moves `CONFIRMED → SUPERSEDED`, and an `OVERRIDDEN` record moves `OVERRIDDEN → SUPERSEDED`. Superseding an already-attested record additionally requires that the page carries no appeal in `FILED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`; the freeze on `APPEALED` pages is what enforces that. The page then repoints `current_classification_id` at the new record and republishes `current_tier` from its `assigned_tier`, `latest_category_codes` from its `category_codes`, and `mfa_score` and `mfa_flag` from the new record's own values. `SUPERSEDED` and `VACATED` are terminal for a classification record: nothing further happens to it.

One consequence of republishing the flag reaches all the way to supply-side standing. When the newly effective classification carries `mfa_flag` true where the page previously had it false, the owning publisher's `mfa_page_count` increments by 1; when the flag flips from true back to false, that counter decrements by 1. A first classification that arrives with the flag already raised counts as such an increment. If the increment brings `mfa_page_count` to 3, the publisher moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED` with `status_reason` `MFA_THRESHOLD`, and the list and segment staling hops described in §3.5 and §3.6 follow immediately inside the same request. Every classification write increments the page's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a client that wants to write twice must re-read the page between writes. Classification records can be listed narrowed by `page_id`, by `methodology_version_id`, by `review_state` and by `classifier_kind`.

### 3.4. Human Review, Publisher Appeals and the Overturn Remedy

A model verdict is provisional until a human looks at it. A `ClassificationAnalyst` reviews a record by supplying a `decision` of either `CONFIRM` or `OVERRIDE`, optionally a `revised_category_codes` array, and optionally a `review_note` that is stored verbatim as the analyst's narrative. Review is only available on a record whose `review_state` is `PENDING_REVIEW` and which is the page's `current_classification_id`; anything else answers `ERR_INVALID_CLASSIFICATION_STATE`. Confirming moves the record `PENDING_REVIEW → CONFIRMED`; overriding moves it `PENDING_REVIEW → OVERRIDDEN`. Either way the page moves `MODEL_CLASSIFIED → HUMAN_CONFIRMED`, `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped from the reviewing analyst's `X-User-ID`, and both the classification's `version` and the page's `version` increment by 1. That stamped reviewer identity is the segregation-of-duties counterparty for any appeal filed against this record.

`OVERRIDE` is the substantive branch and requires `revised_category_codes` to be present and to differ from the record's stored `category_codes`. The supplied array replaces `category_codes`, and the record is then re-scored end to end: `mfa_score` and `mfa_flag` are recomputed, `base_risk_points` is recomputed from the new categories, and `adjusted_risk_points` and `assigned_tier` follow — but with the `classifier_kind` treated as human for the purposes of the confidence multiplier, so an overridden record never carries the 1.25 inflation regardless of the `model_confidence` originally supplied. The page then republishes `current_tier` from the re-scored `assigned_tier` and `latest_category_codes` from the revised categories. `CONFIRM`, by contrast, changes no computed figure at all: it accepts the tier as scored, republishing nothing, and it neither takes nor honours `revised_category_codes`.

A publisher that disagrees with an attested classification appeals it. Appeals are filed only by the owning `Publisher`, and only against the page's currently effective record: the payload's `classification_id` must equal the page's `current_classification_id` and that record's `review_state` must be `CONFIRMED` or `OVERRIDDEN`. A target that is superseded, vacated or still unreviewed answers `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_SUPERSEDED`. The filing must reach the platform within 14 days of the disputed record's `created_at`, measured against the logical clock; later filings answer `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Only one appeal may be live against a given classification at a time: filing a second while one sits in `FILED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` answers `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. Filing against a page whose `classification_state` does not declare the move — a page still at `MODEL_CLASSIFIED`, for instance — answers `ERR_INVALID_PAGE_STATE`.

The payload names a `reason_code` of `CATEGORY_MISREAD`, `CONTEXT_IGNORED`, `STALE_CRAWL` or `MFA_SIGNAL_WRONG`, and an `evidence_note` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters carrying the publisher's argument for the current round. A filed appeal stands at `appeal_state` `FILED` with `round_no` 1, `revised_tier` null, `decision_note` null, `decided_by_user_id` null and `remedy_classification_id` null; the page moves `HUMAN_CONFIRMED → APPEALED`, its `appeal_count` increments by 1, and its `version` increments by 1. Appeals can be listed narrowed by `classification_id`, by `remedy_classification_id`, by `appeal_state` and by `reason_code`.

Adjudication is a `DisputeOfficer`'s job and admits three outcomes, supplied as `outcome` together with a `decision_note` and, where the outcome demands it, a `revised_tier`. Before any outcome is considered, the officer's `X-User-ID` is compared against both the `reviewed_by_user_id` and the `classifier_user_id` of the disputed classification; a match answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A decision against an appeal whose `appeal_state` is already `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` answers `ERR_INVALID_APPEAL_STATE`, and so does a decision from any state that does not declare the requested move.

Choosing `MORE_EVIDENCE` moves the appeal `FILED → MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, stores the `decision_note`, leaves the page at `APPEALED`, and increments the appeal's `version` by 1. It is only available while `round_no` is strictly below the ceiling of 3 rounds; at round 3 it answers `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The publisher answers by supplying a further evidence round, which moves the appeal `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED → FILED`, increments `round_no` by 1, replaces `evidence_note` with the new narrative, and increments the appeal's `version` by 1. That submission is available only to the `Publisher` that owns the appealed page — the caller's `X-User-ID` must be that publisher — and it too is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` when `round_no` is already 3. The `FILED → MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED → FILED` loop is the negotiation cycle, and it is bounded at three rounds; a terminal decision, however, remains available at any round, including round 3.

Choosing `UPHELD` closes the appeal against the publisher. The appeal moves `FILED → UPHELD`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` are stamped, the page moves `APPEALED → HUMAN_CONFIRMED` with `current_classification_id`, `current_tier` and `latest_category_codes` all left exactly as they were, the publisher's `decided_appeal_count` increments by 1, and both the appeal's `version` and the page's `version` increment by 1. An `UPHELD` decision must not supply `revised_tier`; doing so answers `ERR_REVISED_TIER_REQUIRED`.

Choosing `OVERTURNED` closes the appeal in the publisher's favour and is the most far-reaching single decision in the platform. It requires `revised_tier`, and that tier must be strictly better than the disputed record's `assigned_tier` — strictly greater in rank on the ladder of §2.5. An omitted `revised_tier`, or one no better than the tier under dispute, answers `ERR_REVISED_TIER_REQUIRED`. On success the appeal moves `FILED → OVERTURNED`, stamping `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note`, `revised_tier` and `remedy_classification_id`, the publisher's `decided_appeal_count` and `overturned_appeal_count` each increment by 1, and four hops follow.

In the first hop the disputed classification moves `CONFIRMED → VACATED` or `OVERRIDDEN → VACATED` according to how it had been reviewed, and the platform mints a fresh classification record with `classifier_kind` `APPEAL_REMEDY`, an `assigned_tier` equal to the awarded `revised_tier`, and `review_state` `CONFIRMED` — it needs no review because a `DisputeOfficer` has just attested it. The page repoints `current_classification_id` and `current_tier` at that remedy record, whose identifier is also written back to the appeal as `remedy_classification_id`, and the page moves `APPEALED → RECLASSIFIED`.

In the second hop, because the page's `current_tier` has changed, every measurement batch of that page whose `settlement_status` is `CLAIMED` recomputes its `assessed_tier` against the revised tier and re-derives its `suitability_breach_flag`; each such batch's `version` increments by 1. In the third hop, because those breach flags may have cleared, every refund claim citing those batches whose `claim_state` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` recomputes `claimed_impressions` and `provisional_credit_cents` on the gross basis described in §3.9. In the fourth hop, a claim whose recomputed `claimed_impressions` falls to 0 is rejected outright by the platform: it moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` with `reject_reason_code` set to `APPEAL_OVERTURNED`, every batch it cited moves `CLAIMED → CLOSED_NO_CREDIT`, and the campaign's `open_claim_count` decrements by 1. `APPEAL_OVERTURNED` is written only by this cascade and is never accepted from a client. Every record touched across the four hops has its `version` incremented by 1, the appeal's own `version` and the page's `version` included.

### 3.5. Curating Inclusion and Exclusion Lists

Inventory lists are the advertiser's own instrument, created and curated by the `Advertiser` role alone. A list declares its owning `advertiser_id`, a `list_type` of `INCLUSION` or `EXCLUSION`, and a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique among that advertiser's lists of the same type; a collision answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIST_NAME`. The two types are not interchangeable: an `INCLUSION` list carries entries that allow inventory, an `EXCLUSION` list carries entries that block it, and the type is what decides which. A new list stands at `status` `DRAFT` with `entry_count` 0, `published_version` null, `published_at` null and `stale_reason` null. Lists can be listed narrowed by `advertiser_id`, by `list_type` and by `status`.

Entries are appended one at a time under the parent list's path, so the entry's `list_id` is taken from the path rather than the body; the body carries only an `entry_type` and an `entry_value`. `entry_type` is one of `PAGE_URL`, `DOMAIN`, `PUBLISHER` or `CATEGORY`, and it decides both how `entry_value` is interpreted and how much authority the entry carries. `entry_value` is polymorphic and holds 1 to 300 characters: a full page URL when the type is `PAGE_URL`, a lowercase host when it is `DOMAIN`, the identifier of a publisher when it is `PUBLISHER`, and a content category code when it is `CATEGORY`. From the parent list the platform derives `action`, which is `ALLOW` for an entry of an `INCLUSION` list and `BLOCK` for an entry of an `EXCLUSION` list; from the type it derives `specificity_rank`, which is 4 for `PAGE_URL`, 3 for `DOMAIN`, 2 for `PUBLISHER` and 1 for `CATEGORY`. Neither is ever supplied by the caller. The combination of `list_id`, `entry_type` and `entry_value` must be unique inside the list; a repeat answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIST_ENTRY`. Each successful append increments the list's `entry_count` by 1 and the list's `version` by 1. Entries are append-only and immutable — as noted in §2.4 an entry carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` — and they are read back in ascending `created_at` order, optionally narrowed by `entry_type`. That ordering is not cosmetic: it is the tie-break order the pre-bid precedence rules fall back on.

Publication is what makes a list consumable. Publishing snapshots the list's `version` as it stands at that moment into `published_version`, stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, clears any `stale_reason`, and then increments the list's `version` by 1. A `DRAFT` list moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`; a list that had gone stale moves `STALE → PUBLISHED`, and the `STALE → PUBLISHED → STALE` cycle is the list's ordinary revision loop. Publishing a list whose `status` is already `PUBLISHED` answers `ERR_INVALID_LIST_STATE`. Two substantive guards apply. A list whose `entry_count` is 0 cannot be published and answers `ERR_EMPTY_LIST`. And a list may not be published while any of its entries has `action` `ALLOW`, `entry_type` `PUBLISHER`, and an `entry_value` naming a publisher whose `publisher_status` is `RESTRICTED` or `DELISTED`; that attempt answers `ERR_LIST_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PUBLISHER`. The second guard exists because an advertiser must not be able to keep buying a publisher the platform has taken out of good standing.

The same concern is what stales a list after the fact. When a publisher moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED`, every `PUBLISHED` list that owns an entry with `action` `ALLOW`, `entry_type` `PUBLISHER` and `entry_value` equal to that publisher's identifier moves `PUBLISHED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `RESTRICTED_PUBLISHER_ENTRY`, and each such list's `version` increments by 1. This is the second hop of the quarantine cascade, and it immediately triggers a third: every pre-bid segment in `sync_status` `SYNCED` that references one of those newly stale lists moves `SYNCED → STALE`. Reinstating the publisher does not undo any of it — as §2.10 states, stale lists are never auto-republished, and the advertiser must publish them again deliberately. Entries appended to an already-published list are likewise invisible to the buying platform until the list is republished and the consuming segment re-synced.

### 3.6. Pre-Bid Segments and the Placement Decision

A pre-bid segment is the object the buying platform actually consumes. An `Advertiser` creates one naming the `campaign_id` whose bidder will read it, a `suitability_floor_tier` that may be stricter than the campaign's own `min_suitability_tier`, and up to two pinned lists: an optional `inclusion_list_id` and an optional `exclusion_list_id`. At least one of the two must be supplied, and a segment created with neither answers `ERR_SEGMENT_REQUIRES_LIST`. A supplied `inclusion_list_id` must name a list whose `list_type` is `INCLUSION` and a supplied `exclusion_list_id` one whose `list_type` is `EXCLUSION`, and both must belong to the same advertiser as the segment's campaign; any of those mismatches answers `ERR_LIST_ADVERTISER_MISMATCH`. A new segment stands at `sync_status` `PENDING` with `synced_inclusion_version`, `synced_exclusion_version`, `synced_at` and `stale_reason` all null. Segments can be listed narrowed by `campaign_id`, by either pinned list identifier, and by `sync_status`.

Synchronising a segment pushes the current list snapshot to the buying platform. It moves the segment `PENDING → SYNCED` on its first use and `STALE → SYNCED` afterwards; a sync against a segment already in `SYNCED` answers `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`. Two preconditions guard it. Every list the segment pins must be in `status` `PUBLISHED` — a pinned list still in `DRAFT`, or one that has gone `STALE`, answers `ERR_LIST_NOT_PUBLISHED`. And the owning campaign's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a campaign in `UNDER_REMEDIATION` answers `ERR_CAMPAIGN_UNDER_REMEDIATION` and stays unsyncable until it returns to `ACTIVE`. On success the platform captures `synced_inclusion_version` and `synced_exclusion_version` from the `published_version` of the corresponding lists, leaving either one null when that list reference is null, stamps `synced_at` from the logical clock, clears `stale_reason`, and increments the segment's `version` by 1. Those two captured numbers are how a client can tell that a list has been revised since the bidder last saw it.

Segments go stale for two distinct reasons, and `stale_reason` records which. When a pinned list moves `PUBLISHED → STALE`, a `SYNCED` segment referencing it moves `SYNCED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `LIST_STALE`. When the owning campaign moves `ACTIVE → UNDER_REMEDIATION`, a `SYNCED` segment of that campaign moves `SYNCED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `CAMPAIGN_UNDER_REMEDIATION`. Either way the segment's `version` increments by 1, and — as noted below — the placement decision read stops answering for it until it is synced again.

The placement decision is the read that a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `Advertiser` performs against one page under one segment, naming the segment as a `segment_id` query parameter. **The gates are evaluated in a pinned order, and the first gate that resolves returns**; nothing after it is consulted.

Gate zero is readiness, and it produces an error rather than a decision: a segment whose `sync_status` is `PENDING` or `STALE` answers `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_SYNCED`. Gate one is publisher standing: when the page's publisher has `publisher_status` `RESTRICTED` or `DELISTED`, the answer is a `decision` of `BLOCK` with `reason_code` `PUBLISHER_RESTRICTED`, and `decisive_entry_id` and `decisive_specificity` both null. No list entry can override a publisher out of good standing.

Gate two is list precedence over the union of both pinned lists. The candidate set is every entry belonging to either the pinned inclusion list or the pinned exclusion list, restricted to entries of lists whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, that matches the page in one of four ways: an entry of type `PAGE_URL` whose `entry_value` equals the page's `url`; an entry of type `DOMAIN` whose `entry_value` equals the page's `domain`; an entry of type `PUBLISHER` whose `entry_value` equals the page's `publisher_id`; or an entry of type `CATEGORY` whose `entry_value` appears among the page's `latest_category_codes`. Candidates are ordered by `specificity_rank` from highest to lowest, so a page-level entry outranks a domain entry, which outranks a publisher entry, which outranks a category entry. At equal `specificity_rank`, an entry with `action` `BLOCK` is ordered before one with `action` `ALLOW` — blocking wins ties. If two candidates still tie, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins; the entry's own identifier is never consulted as a tie-break. If the candidate set is non-empty, the first entry under that ordering is decisive. A decisive `BLOCK` returns `decision` `BLOCK` with `reason_code` `LIST_EXCLUSION`; a decisive `ALLOW` returns `decision` `ALLOW_OVERRIDE` with `reason_code` `LIST_INCLUSION` and bypasses the tier gate entirely. In both cases `decisive_entry_id` carries the deciding entry's identifier and `decisive_specificity` carries its `entry_type`.

Gate three is the tier floor, reached only when the candidate set is empty, and it is the one place where the platform deliberately answers a *different* tier than post-bid monitoring would. Rather than reading the requested page's own classification, the gate rolls the publisher up. The candidate pages are the pages of the same `publisher_id` whose `classification_state` is one of `HUMAN_CONFIRMED`, `APPEALED` or `RECLASSIFIED` — that is, human-attested inventory only — and whose currently effective classification has a `created_at` at or after the logical clock minus 30 days. Among those candidate pages, the effective tier is the one with the **lowest** rank on the ladder of §2.5: the worst suitability in the trailing window wins. When the candidate set of pages is empty, the rollup yields `UNRATED`. A page classified only by a model therefore never lifts its publisher out of `UNRATED`, and a single badly classified sibling page drags every page of that publisher down — the same page with the same stored data can be blocked pre-bid at `HIGH_RISK` while post-bid monitoring records it at `MEDIUM_RISK`.

With the rolled-up `effective_tier` in hand, the gate resolves in three ways. An `effective_tier` of `UNRATED` returns `decision` `BLOCK` with `reason_code` `UNRATED_INVENTORY`. An `effective_tier` whose rank is strictly below the rank of the segment's `suitability_floor_tier` returns `decision` `BLOCK` with `reason_code` `BELOW_SUITABILITY_FLOOR`. Otherwise the answer is `decision` `ALLOW` with `reason_code` `MEETS_FLOOR`. Every response of this read echoes `page_id` and `segment_id`, reports the `decision` as one of `ALLOW`, `ALLOW_OVERRIDE` or `BLOCK`, the `reason_code` as one of `PUBLISHER_RESTRICTED`, `LIST_EXCLUSION`, `LIST_INCLUSION`, `UNRATED_INVENTORY`, `BELOW_SUITABILITY_FLOOR` or `MEETS_FLOOR`, the `decisive_entry_id` and `decisive_specificity` of the deciding entry where one existed and null otherwise, the resolved `effective_tier`, and the `floor_tier` the segment enforced.

### 3.7. Post-Bid Measurement: Ingestion, Scoring and Branching Certification

Post-bid monitoring is carried by the `MeasurementBatch`, which is the platform's cross-aggregate record: it names the buy side and the supply side of one measured delivery at once. A `MeasurementOperator` ingests a batch naming the `campaign_id`, the `publisher_id`, the `page_id` that was measured, the `methodology_version_id` pinned for scoring and certification, the `format` of the creative, and the delivery window as `window_start` and `window_end`. The window is a **closed** interval: both endpoints belong to it. Alongside the window come five sensor figures: `measured_impressions`, strictly greater than zero, is what the tag observed; `measurable_impressions`, at least zero and at most `measured_impressions`, is the subset for which viewability could be determined at all; `in_view_impressions`, at least zero and at most `measurable_impressions`, is what the sensor reported as meeting the in-view criteria; `avg_in_view_seconds`, at least zero, is the average continuous in-view duration of those in-view impressions; and `avg_in_view_pixel_pct`, between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, is their average in-view pixel share.

Ingestion applies four referential guards on top of the shape checks. The pinned methodology version must belong to the `VIEWABILITY` family; another family answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_FAMILY_MISMATCH`. The batch's `format` must equal the campaign's `format`, so a `VIDEO` campaign can never receive `DISPLAY` measurement; a mismatch answers `ERR_FORMAT_MISMATCH`. The `page_id` must belong to the `publisher_id` the batch declares; otherwise `ERR_PAGE_PUBLISHER_MISMATCH`. And two batches sharing the same `campaign_id`, `page_id` and `format` must be separated by more than 60 seconds of clear air at both ends, as spelled out in §2.8; an overlap or a too-close abutment answers `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_COLLISION`. A window whose `window_end` lies after the current logical clock, or whose `window_start` is not strictly before its `window_end`, answers `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE`. Recall from §2.8 that when the submitted counters are internally inconsistent *and* the window also collides, the counter error `ERR_IMPRESSION_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT` is the one that returns. A freshly ingested batch stands at `scoring_status` `INGESTED` and `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, with every computed figure at its default: `qualified_in_view_impressions`, `non_viewable_impressions`, `unmeasurable_impressions`, `ivt_impressions` and `net_billable_impressions` are 0, `ivt_rate` is 0.0000, `sophistication_flag` and `suitability_breach_flag` are false, and `coverage_rate`, `viewability_rate`, `assessed_tier` and `scored_at` are null. `net_billable_impressions` starts at 0 rather than at `measured_impressions` because the invalid-traffic figures are only ever written by the recomputation of §3.8, which a confirmation, a retraction and scoring each perform. Batches can be listed narrowed by `campaign_id`, `publisher_id`, `page_id`, `methodology_version_id`, `format`, and either status dimension — `scoring_status` or `settlement_status`.

Those two status dimensions are the reason this record deserves careful reading: **they evolve independently on the same row**. `scoring_status` tracks the measurement, `settlement_status` tracks the money, and neither constrains the other's transitions. A batch that has already been claimed may still be decertified afterwards, and a decertified batch may still be credited.

Scoring is requested on a batch whose `scoring_status` is `INGESTED`; anything else answers `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, and so does any scoring attempt on a `DECERTIFIED` batch. The very first thing scoring does is evaluate the pinned methodology version for lapse against the logical clock as described in §3.2, after which a version that is not `PROVISIONAL` or `ACCREDITED` yields `ERR_METHODOLOGY_LAPSED`. The version must also be effective at the batch's `window_start`, meaning `effective_from` is at or before `window_start` and `window_start` falls strictly before `effective_to`; a batch measured outside its pinned version's window is refused with the same `ERR_METHODOLOGY_LAPSED`. A version that has been suspended answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ACCREDITED` instead. Notably, an unclassified page is *not* an obstacle to scoring; it simply yields an `assessed_tier` of `UNRATED`.

The viewability arithmetic begins by selecting the two thresholds the format demands. The required duration is `video_in_view_seconds` for a `VIDEO` batch and `display_in_view_seconds` for both `DISPLAY` and `LARGE_DISPLAY`. The required pixel share is `video_pixel_pct` for `VIDEO`, `large_display_pixel_pct` for `LARGE_DISPLAY`, and `display_pixel_pct` for `DISPLAY`. The sensor's in-view claim is then accepted or rejected **wholesale**, not proportionally: `qualified_in_view_impressions` equals the full `in_view_impressions` when `avg_in_view_seconds` is at least the required duration **and** `avg_in_view_pixel_pct` is at least the required pixel share, and is exactly 0 when either threshold is missed. From there, `unmeasurable_impressions` is `measured_impressions` less `measurable_impressions`; `non_viewable_impressions` is `measurable_impressions` less `qualified_in_view_impressions`; `coverage_rate` is `measurable_impressions` divided by `measured_impressions`, rounded half up to four decimals; and `viewability_rate` is `qualified_in_view_impressions` divided by `measurable_impressions`, rounded half up to four decimals, or exactly 0.0000 when `measurable_impressions` is 0. These four figures are what makes the impression-conservation invariant of §2.11 hold structurally on every scored batch.

Scoring also folds in invalid traffic and captures the suitability verdict. It recomputes `ivt_impressions`, `ivt_rate`, `sophistication_flag` and `net_billable_impressions` over the batch's confirmed findings by the deduplication rule of §3.8. It then captures `assessed_tier` as a snapshot of the page's post-bid suitability: the tier of the page's own currently effective classification, whatever the page's `classification_state` happens to be — a merely `MODEL_CLASSIFIED` page counts, unlike in the pre-bid rollup — and `UNRATED` only when the page is still `UNCLASSIFIED`. From that snapshot, `suitability_breach_flag` is set true when the rank of `assessed_tier` is strictly below the rank of the campaign's `min_suitability_tier`, and also whenever `assessed_tier` is `UNRATED`; otherwise false. Finally `scored_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the batch moves `INGESTED → SCORED`, and its `version` increments by 1.

Certification is a **branching** adjudication: one and the same request lands the batch in either `CERTIFIED` or `QUARANTINED` depending on what the scored figures say, and the two branches have disjoint consequences. It is available only on a batch whose `scoring_status` is `SCORED`; anything else answers `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. The pinned methodology version must still be `PROVISIONAL` or `ACCREDITED`, failing which the request answers `ERR_METHODOLOGY_NOT_ACCREDITED`; as with scoring, a version whose window has closed is lapsed first and then reported as `ERR_METHODOLOGY_LAPSED`.

The batch moves `SCORED → CERTIFIED` only when all four of the following hold: `coverage_rate` is at least the pinned version's `coverage_rate_floor`; `ivt_rate` is at most its `ivt_rate_ceiling`; `sophistication_flag` is false; and `suitability_breach_flag` is false. On that branch the publisher's `certified_batch_count` increments by 1, the batch's `measured_impressions` and `measurable_impressions` are added to the campaign's `certified_measured_impressions` and `certified_measurable_impressions`, the campaign's `coverage_status` is recomputed, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. Coverage status is derived from the campaign's own two lifetime counters, which are maintained incrementally by certification and decertification rather than recomputed from scratch, and which count only batches whose `scoring_status` is `CERTIFIED` — quarantined and decertified batches are excluded, and window boundaries are irrelevant. When `certified_measured_impressions` is 0 the status is `UNKNOWN`. Otherwise the ratio of `certified_measurable_impressions` to `certified_measured_impressions` is rounded half up to four decimals and compared to the campaign's `target_coverage_rate`: at or above it the status is `ON_TARGET`, below it the status is `BELOW_TARGET`. A campaign sitting in `UNDER_REMEDIATION` whose recomputed status reaches `ON_TARGET` moves `UNDER_REMEDIATION → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments by 1; its `STALE` pre-bid segments are not auto-resynced and must be synced again explicitly.

The batch moves `SCORED → QUARANTINED` when any one of the four conditions fails — that is, when `coverage_rate` is strictly below the `coverage_rate_floor`, or `ivt_rate` is strictly above the `ivt_rate_ceiling`, or `sophistication_flag` is true, or `suitability_breach_flag` is true. Sophisticated invalid traffic alone forces quarantine no matter how good the other figures are. On this branch the campaign's certified counters are left completely untouched, and instead a four-hop cascade runs: the publisher's `quarantined_batch_count` increments by 1; if that brings it to 3, the publisher moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED` with `status_reason` `QUARANTINE_THRESHOLD`; because the publisher is now restricted, every `PUBLISHED` inventory list holding an `ALLOW` entry of type `PUBLISHER` naming it moves `PUBLISHED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `RESTRICTED_PUBLISHER_ENTRY`; and every `SYNCED` pre-bid segment referencing one of those lists moves `SYNCED → STALE` with `stale_reason` `LIST_STALE`. Every record touched has its `version` incremented by 1. A quarantined batch is also the point at which the money side opens: it becomes claimable, so its `settlement_status` may now move from `UNBILLED` to `CLAIMED`.

The one remaining scoring transition is not client-driven at all. When the methodology version a certified batch pinned is suspended, that batch moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED` with the counter reversals described in §3.2, and `DECERTIFIED` is terminal for the measurement dimension — neither scoring nor certification may be requested against it again. Like quarantine, decertification makes the batch claimable.

### 3.8. Invalid Traffic: Detection, Confirmation and Retraction

Invalid traffic is recorded as a set of detector findings against a batch rather than as a single number, because different detectors see overlapping slices of the same traffic. A `MeasurementOperator` records a finding naming the `batch_id`, an `ivt_class`, the `flagged_impressions` the detector attributes to that class, the detector's `confidence`, and optionally a `detector_note`. `ivt_class` is one of `GENERAL_BOT`, `DATACENTER`, `SIVT_HIJACKED_DEVICE`, `SIVT_AD_FARM` or `SIVT_INCENTIVIZED`, and from it the platform derives `ivt_family`: `GIVT` — general invalid traffic — for `GENERAL_BOT` and `DATACENTER`, and `SIVT` — sophisticated invalid traffic — for the three `SIVT_` classes. The derived family is the grouping key the deduplication depends on and is never supplied by the caller. `flagged_impressions` must be strictly greater than zero and must not exceed the parent batch's `measured_impressions`; a finding that claims more invalid impressions than the batch measured answers `ERR_IVT_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`. `confidence` lies between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive. A new finding stands at `status` `PROPOSED` with `retract_reason` null, and a proposed finding contributes nothing at all to the batch's figures. Findings of one batch are read through the batch's path, optionally narrowed by `status` and by `ivt_family`.

Confirming a finding moves it `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED` and is the act that makes it count. It requires a `confidence` of at least 0.75; a weaker detector signal answers `ERR_IVT_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`. It also requires that the parent batch has not yet been adjudicated: the batch's `scoring_status` must be `INGESTED` or `SCORED`, and a batch already `CERTIFIED`, `QUARANTINED` or `DECERTIFIED` answers `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. Confirming a finding whose `status` is not `PROPOSED` answers `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. On success the batch's invalid-traffic figures are recomputed, both the finding's `version` and the batch's `version` increment by 1.

The deduplication rule is the substance of this subsection, and it is deliberately not a sum. **Inside one family the contribution is the maximum, because detectors of the same family observe overlapping impression sets; across the two families the contributions are additive, because they observe genuinely different phenomena.** Concretely: the `GIVT` component is the largest `flagged_impressions` among this batch's findings whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `ivt_family` is `GIVT`, and 0 when that set is empty. The `SIVT` component is the largest `flagged_impressions` among this batch's confirmed `SIVT` findings, and 0 when that set is empty. `ivt_impressions` is the sum of the two components, capped at `measured_impressions` so that overlapping detectors can never claim more traffic than the batch delivered. `ivt_rate` is `ivt_impressions` divided by `measured_impressions`, rounded half up to four decimals. `sophistication_flag` is true exactly when the `SIVT` component is greater than zero — a single confirmed sophisticated finding raises it. `net_billable_impressions` is `measured_impressions` less `ivt_impressions`. Only `CONFIRMED` findings enter this computation; `PROPOSED` and `RETRACTED` findings are excluded entirely. Ties inside a family need no tie-break because the aggregate is the maximum *value*, not a chosen row.

Retraction is the compensating operation and is available from either non-terminal position. It requires a non-blank `retract_reason`; a missing or blank reason answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A finding that had never been confirmed moves `PROPOSED → RETRACTED` and changes no batch counter whatsoever, because it never entered `ivt_impressions` in the first place; only the finding's own `version` increments by 1. A confirmed finding moves `CONFIRMED → RETRACTED`, and the batch's `ivt_impressions`, `ivt_rate`, `sophistication_flag` and `net_billable_impressions` are recomputed over the findings that remain confirmed, which restores the figures to exactly the values they held before that confirmation with no residual drift — the impression-conservation invariant of §2.11 holds, within the scope that section sets out, before the confirmation, after it, and again after the matching retraction. This is a re-derivation rather than a stored rollback: `net_billable_impressions` is always recomputed as `measured_impressions` less the deduplicated total, so when no confirmed finding remains it lands on the full `measured_impressions`, which is the pre-confirmation value in every case except the first-ever confirmation of a batch that had not been scored — there it lands on `measured_impressions` rather than on the 0 default the batch carried at ingestion. In that case both the finding's `version` and the batch's `version` increment by 1. Retracting a finding whose `status` is already `RETRACTED` answers `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`, and retraction is subject to the same adjudication guard as confirmation: once the batch has left `INGESTED` or `SCORED`, retraction answers `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`.

### 3.9. Refund Claims: Intake, Adjudication, Settlement and Reversal

When measurement goes against a publisher, the advertiser asks for money back. A `RefundClaim` is filed by the `Advertiser` and names the claiming `campaign_id`, the `publisher_id` being charged back, the `batch_ids` it cites — between 1 and 20 identifiers with no duplicates — and the `expected_campaign_version` token described in §2.7. Four referential guards apply in the priority order of §2.8: every cited batch must belong both to the claim's campaign and to the claim's publisher (`ERR_BATCH_SCOPE_MISMATCH`); every cited batch must have `scoring_status` `QUARANTINED` or `DECERTIFIED`, since a batch that was certified, merely scored or merely ingested has nothing to refund (`ERR_BATCH_NOT_CLAIMABLE`); every cited batch must have `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, so a batch already cited by a live or settled claim cannot be cited twice (`ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_CLAIMED`); and the filing must arrive within 30 days of the latest `window_end` among the cited batches, measured against the logical clock (`ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`).

On acceptance the claim stands at `claim_state` `SUBMITTED`, every cited batch moves `settlement_status` `UNBILLED → CLAIMED` with its `version` incremented by 1, the campaign's `open_claim_count` increments by 1 — once per claim, not once per cited batch — and the campaign's `version` increments by 1. `credited_impressions`, `gross_credit_cents`, `admin_fee_rate`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_credit_cents` are all null at this stage; they exist only from approval onward. Claims can be listed narrowed by `campaign_id`, by the charged `publisher_id`, by any cited batch identifier through `batch_ids`, by `claim_state` and by `reject_reason_code`.

Whether a cited batch actually supports a credit is decided per batch by a single **defect test**, and that test is shared by both stages of the money arithmetic. A cited batch is defective when any one of these holds: its `suitability_breach_flag` is true; or its `ivt_impressions` is greater than zero; or its `coverage_rate` is strictly below the `coverage_rate_floor` of the methodology version it pinned; or its `sophistication_flag` is true; or its `scoring_status` is `DECERTIFIED`, the measurement itself having been repudiated. A cited batch that fails the defect test contributes zero at whatever moment the figures are being computed, which matters because the figures are recomputed more than once over the claim's life.

Two deliberately different impression bases exist, and the platform switches from one to the other at exactly one moment.

The **gross basis** governs the provisional stage — claim creation, claim review, and every cascade recomputation while `claim_state` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`. It credits the *entire* measured delivery of every defective batch, on the reasoning that at intake the advertiser is disputing the whole placement: `claimed_impressions` is the sum of `measured_impressions` over the cited batches that pass the defect test. A batch defective only through invalid traffic still contributes all of its `measured_impressions` under this basis.

The **net basis** governs the final stage — claim approval only — and its result is frozen into `credited_impressions`. It credits only the demonstrably defective subset, with one exception. For a batch whose `suitability_breach_flag` is true, the whole `measured_impressions` is credited, because standing next to unsuitable content spoils the entire placement. For any other defective batch, the credited quantity is `ivt_impressions` plus the non-viewable shortfall — that is, `measurable_impressions` less `qualified_in_view_impressions`, floored at zero — and that sum is then capped at the batch's `measured_impressions` so that overlapping detectors cannot inflate the credit above the delivery. Unmeasurable impressions are never credited under the net basis, because non-measurement is not itself a delivery defect. `credited_impressions` is the sum of those per-batch quantities over the cited batches that pass the defect test. For a batch that is defective through both invalid traffic and poor viewability, the two bases therefore return different impression counts from identical stored data — that divergence is intended, not a rounding artefact.

Money is priced from whichever basis is active, one batch at a time. For each cited defective batch the platform multiplies that batch's basis impressions by the campaign's `cpm_cents`, divides by 1,000 because a CPM is quoted per thousand impressions, and rounds half up to whole cents. Those per-batch amounts are then summed, in ascending `created_at` order of the batches — an order that cannot change the total, since each term is already an integer. As §2.6 insists, rounding the per-batch amounts before summing them is mandatory and rounding the summed product afterwards is forbidden. Under the gross basis the resulting figure is written to `provisional_credit_cents`; under the net basis it is written to `gross_credit_cents`.

Opening adjudication is a `DisputeOfficer` action that moves the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and refreshes `claimed_impressions` and `provisional_credit_cents` on the gross basis, incrementing the claim's `version` by 1. It carries one precondition of its own: no page reached through the cited `batch_ids` may carry an appeal in `appeal_state` `FILED` or `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, because the tier that decides the defect test is exactly what the appeal is disputing. A live appeal on a cited page answers `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. A review requested from any other `claim_state` answers `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Approval moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and is the only operation that moves money. It accepts an optional `decision_note`. The evaluation order is pinned and runs in a single pass: first `credited_impressions` on the net basis; then `gross_credit_cents` priced from it per batch; then `admin_fee_rate`, selected from `gross_credit_cents` and never from the net amount; then `admin_fee_cents`; then `net_credit_cents`. The administration fee is tiered on one break at 500,000 cents — five thousand dollars of gross credit: a `gross_credit_cents` of at least that break attracts the high-volume rate of 0.10, and anything below it attracts the standard rate of 0.15. `admin_fee_cents` is `gross_credit_cents` multiplied by `admin_fee_rate` and rounded half up to whole cents, and `net_credit_cents` is `gross_credit_cents` less `admin_fee_cents` as an exact integer subtraction. The dependency only looks circular: keying the tier on the payable amount would be ill-defined, since a gross of 555,000 at the 0.10 rate yields a net of 499,500, which sits below the break and would demand 0.15, which in turn yields 471,750 — a second fixed point. Because the tier is pinned to `gross_credit_cents`, a gross of 555,000 unambiguously yields a rate of 0.10, a fee of 55,500 and a net of 499,500.

Two fiscal guards decide whether the approval is allowed at all. A recomputed `credited_impressions` of 0 — and therefore a `net_credit_cents` that is not greater than zero — is refused with `ERR_NO_CREDITABLE_IMPRESSIONS`; such a claim must be rejected instead, not approved for nothing. And an approval whose `net_credit_cents` would push the advertiser's `credit_received_cents_total` above that advertiser's `credit_cap_cents` is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`; the sum of the existing total and the new net credit must remain at or below the cap. Remember from §2.8 that a claim in a state that does not permit approval fails on state reachability first: a claim already `REJECTED` submitted for approval over an exhausted cap answers `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, not the cap error.

A successful approval freezes `credited_impressions`, `gross_credit_cents`, `admin_fee_rate`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_credit_cents` on the claim; adds `net_credit_cents` to the advertiser's `credit_received_cents_total` and, in the same operation, to the publisher's `credit_issued_cents_total`, honouring the double-entry invariant of §2.11; increments the advertiser's `settled_claim_count` by 1; moves every cited batch `settlement_status` `CLAIMED → CREDITED`; decrements the campaign's `open_claim_count` by 1; stamps `decided_by_user_id` from the officer's `X-User-ID` along with the `decision_note`; and increments the claim's `version` by 1, as well as the `version` of every record it touched.

Rejection is the other officer-driven terminal outcome and moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`. It requires a `reject_reason_code` of `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`, `BATCH_RESCORED` or `DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, and accepts an optional `decision_note`; the fourth value `APPEAL_OVERTURNED` is written only by the appeal-overturn cascade and is refused from a client. Every cited batch moves `settlement_status` `CLAIMED → CLOSED_NO_CREDIT`, which is terminal for the money dimension — such a batch can never be cited by another claim — the campaign's `open_claim_count` decrements by 1, both credit ledgers are left completely untouched, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, and the claim's `version` increments by 1.

Reversal is the compensating operation on a settled claim and belongs to `PlatformAdmin` alone; note that `APPROVED` is settled but not terminal precisely so that this remains possible, whereas `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` are both terminal. Reversal requires a non-blank `reversal_reason`, and a blank or missing one answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It moves the claim `APPROVED → REVERSED`, subtracts `net_credit_cents` from both the advertiser's `credit_received_cents_total` and the publisher's `credit_issued_cents_total`, restoring both to exactly their pre-approval values with no drift; decrements the advertiser's `settled_claim_count` by 1; moves every cited batch `settlement_status` `CREDITED → UNBILLED`, which makes those batches citable again by a future claim; and leaves the campaign's `open_claim_count` unchanged, because it was already decremented at approval. The frozen money triple — `credited_impressions`, `gross_credit_cents`, `admin_fee_rate`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_credit_cents` — is deliberately retained as the audit record of what was reversed rather than nulled out, and the claim's `version` increments by 1. Any of review, approval, rejection or reversal requested from a `claim_state` that does not declare the transition, including any call against a terminal `REJECTED` or `REVERSED` claim, answers `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.
