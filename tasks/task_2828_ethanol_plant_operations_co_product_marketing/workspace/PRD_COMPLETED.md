# Ethanol Plant Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A corn ethanol plant is three businesses wearing one hat. It is a grain merchandiser that buys bushels on basis contracts and pays for them net of quality discounts; it is a chemical process operation that converts starch into denatured fuel ethanol at a yield nobody controls perfectly; and it is a marketer of everything the still leaves behind — distillers grains, corn oil, renewable fuel credits and low-carbon-fuel credits — each priced off a different market. The plant this API serves runs all three simultaneously, on one shared corn inventory, one shared tank farm, one shared rail spur and one regulatory record that the state and federal agencies can subpoena at any time.

Today those three businesses are reconciled by hand. The scale house grades a truck on a paper ticket; grain accounting keys the discount schedule into a spreadsheet a day later; the control room decides how much to grind from a whiteboard number that nobody re-derives when an air permit goes into exceedance; logistics discovers that a tank went off spec only when the crew arrives at the track. Every one of those handoffs is a place where money and compliance evidence leak. This document specifies a single operational system of record that closes them: every state change is an explicit, authorised, auditable action; every derived quantity — corn inventory, tank balance, committed railcars, carbon intensity, crush margin — is recomputed from primary records rather than transcribed; and every rejection carries a stable machine-readable reason so that an operator, an auditor and a downstream integration all see the same story.

The system is mounted under the URL prefix `/ethanol-ops`. It is deliberately append-only in shape: all writes are creations or named actions, nothing is edited in place and nothing is deleted. Time is not read from the host wall clock but from a server-held **logical clock**, so that a scheduling window, a rolling permit average and a quote expiry can all be exercised deterministically by an operator or a test harness.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Authority and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the human or service account making the call, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operating role that call is being made under. If either header is absent, empty or whitespace-only, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`; both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before the role is matched, so ` PlantManager ` is the role `PlantManager`. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight roles below, the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`. If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being invoked (see the table in §4), the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The eight roles are `PlantManager`, `GrainBuyer`, `ScaleOperator`, `GrainAccountant`, `ProcessOperator`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `RiskManager`.

- A `PlantManager` is the plant's general manager. It stands up the facility, posts the market marks that every mark-to-market figure reads, and is the certifying authority on carbon projects. It reads every collection but runs no production or logistics operation directly.
- A `GrainBuyer` is the corn merchandiser and co-product marketer: it owns basis contracting on the buy side and quoting and buyer responses on the co-product sell side. It cannot grade, settle or ship.
- A `ScaleOperator` staffs the inbound scale house, booking trucks against contracts and recording the grading factors that drive discounts. It deliberately cannot settle what it graded.
- A `GrainAccountant` settles graded deliveries into money and inventory.
- A `ProcessOperator` runs the control room: milling and fermentation, distillation, tank service and denaturant blending, and logging of environmental monitoring readings.
- A `LogisticsCoordinator` owns rail: track commissioning, unit-train scheduling and loading, the bill of lading, invoicing, and co-product shipment.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` owns regulatory affairs: renewable credit retirement, carbon project registration and submission, and environmental permit conditions and their deviation closures.
- A `RiskManager` runs the commodity risk desk: it posts marks alongside the plant manager and owns the futures book across corn, ethanol and natural gas, while reading the physical book without touching it.

All eight roles are **opaque identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the same holds for `GrainBuyer`, for `ScaleOperator`, for `GrainAccountant`, for `ProcessOperator`, for `LogisticsCoordinator`, for `ComplianceOfficer` and for `RiskManager`. The server may record such an identifier on a resource — it stores the grading operator's `X-User-ID` as `graded_by_user_id` on a corn delivery and the settling operator's as `settled_by_user_id` — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, and no resource is "owned" by a user in the ownership-check sense.

Two segregations of duty are enforced on top of the role split, and both are business rules rather than mere role checks. First, the operator who graded an inbound load may not be the operator who settles it: even though `GrainAccountant` is the authorised role for settlement, a settlement whose `X-User-ID` equals the delivery's stored `graded_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, a `ComplianceOfficer` submits a carbon project for third-party verification but may never certify it — reviewing a submitted project is exclusive to `PlantManager`, so the two halves of certification always land on different roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly sixteen characters drawn at random from the upper-case Latin letters and the digits — twenty characters in total.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Like every other instant the server writes, it is read from the **logical clock** of §2.4 and not from the host wall clock, so two responses issued without an intervening clock advance carry the identical timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`. The same error envelope, with the same two `meta` fields, is used for every non-2xx response the server can produce, including the 404 and 405 responses of §2.5 and any unexpected 5xx.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set, defaults to 0, and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound — an offset past the end of the result set yields an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false. A value outside those bounds is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Both parameters must be plain base-ten integer literals. The supplied text is first trimmed of surrounding whitespace, and it is then rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` unless it is identical to the canonical decimal rendering of the integer it denotes — so `10` is accepted but `010`, `+10`, `10.0` and `ten` are all refused. A parameter that is absent altogether, or present with an empty value, falls back to its default.

Unless a list endpoint's own description in §4 names a different ordering, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock can leave many rows sharing one `created_at`, every ordering in this specification — the default and the named ones alike — takes the server's internal insertion sequence as its **final tiebreak**, so rows written earlier always sort earlier among ties. Every filter query parameter on a list endpoint is optional, and one supplied with an empty value is treated as not supplied at all rather than as a filter matching the empty string; an enum-valued filter carrying an unrecognised member is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Four pagination fields appear in the `meta` object of every list response — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; supplying one is an unknown field and therefore a request-shape failure. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row is first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused indirectly by an action on some other resource. Two entities are exceptions because they are strictly append-only: a **fermentation health reading** and a **permit reading** are immutable once written, are never updated and are never deleted, so both carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` at all, and there is no endpoint that modifies either.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host wall clock. Before the clock is ever set, it reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An operator in any of the eight roles may advance it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the new instant must be strictly later than the instant currently in force, and re-posting the instant already in force is itself a regression and is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`. Every rolling window, every quote expiry, every occupancy comparison and every system-recorded instant in this specification reads that logical clock. A clock advance is not itself a resource: it answers 200 with a `data` object carrying the single field `current_utc`, the newly installed instant rendered to three fractional digits.

Every instant-valued field anywhere in this API is parsed the same way. The canonical form is ISO 8601 with a `Z` suffix, but any ISO 8601 date-and-time string carrying an explicit UTC offset is accepted and normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared — `2026-08-02T12:00:00+02:00` is the same instant as `2026-08-02T10:00:00Z` — and a string with no offset at all is read as already being UTC. Fractional seconds are optional on input and may carry any number of digits; on output every instant is rendered as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` with the sub-millisecond remainder **truncated** rather than rounded. A date-only string such as `2026-08-02` is accepted and read as midnight UTC on that date. Anything that is not a parseable ISO 8601 date or date-and-time is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reference Checking

Paths in this API nest at most one level: a collection, a resource id, and optionally a sub-resource or an action. A trailing slash on any path is insignificant, so `/ethanol-ops/plants` and `/ethanol-ops/plants/` reach the same endpoint. A path that matches no route at all answers HTTP 404, and a path that matches a route under a method that route does not accept answers HTTP 405; both use the error envelope of §2.2 with `error_id` `ERR_VALIDATION`, which is the one place that identifier appears with a status other than 400. Both are settled by routing before the precedence ladder of §2.9 is entered, so an unknown path answers 404 even when the request carries no authentication headers at all. Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, so the outer resource must exist before the inner one is looked at, and the outermost missing reference is the one reported. Each entity has its own not-found code, and they are checked in a fixed order: plant (`ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`), corn purchase contract (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`), corn delivery (`ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`), fermentation batch (`ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`), product tank (`ERR_TANK_NOT_FOUND`), denaturant blend (`ERR_BLEND_NOT_FOUND`), loading track (`ERR_TRACK_NOT_FOUND`), unit train shipment (`ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`), co-product sale (`ERR_SALE_NOT_FOUND`), RIN batch (`ERR_RIN_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`), carbon project (`ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`), hedge position (`ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`) and permit condition (`ERR_PERMIT_CONDITION_NOT_FOUND`).

Existence checking does not stop at the path. Identifiers supplied in a request body or as a list filter are checked with the same codes: a `rolled_from_contract_id` filter resolves against contracts and yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; a `fermentation_batch_id` filter yields `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`; a `separated_shipment_id` filter or reference yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `track_id` in a body or filter yields `ERR_TRACK_NOT_FOUND`; a `predecessor_project_id` in a create body or a filter yields `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`. Where a single body references several tanks, they are resolved in the order `destination_tank_id`, `neat_source_tank_id`, `denaturant_source_tank_id`, `source_tank_id`, and any miss reports `ERR_TANK_NOT_FOUND`. Path identifiers always resolve before body identifiers.

Reverse navigation is provided by five sub-resource lists — a contract's deliveries, a batch's health readings, a tank's batches, a tank's denaturant blends and a condition's readings — and by filtered collection lists over corn purchase contracts, unit train shipments, co-product sales, RIN batches, carbon projects, hedge positions and permit conditions. The tank-blend list accepts a `tank_role` filter, because a blend can reference the same tank in three different capacities.

### 2.6. Units, Precision and Rounding

Money crosses the API boundary as **integer cents** wherever a field name ends in `_cents`, and the fields named `settlement_amount_cents`, `invoice_amount_cents`, `capital_cost_cents`, `realized_pnl_cents` and `unrealized_pnl_cents` are all whole numbers of cents that may legitimately be negative in the two profit-and-loss cases. Prices quoted per unit — `basis_cents_per_bushel`, `futures_price_cents_per_bushel`, `net_price_cents_per_bushel`, `discount_cents_per_bushel`, `price_cents_per_gallon`, `entry_price`, `exit_price` and the four `_discount_cents` components — are decimals carried to two places.

Every decimal width stated below is also an **input gate**, and it is a gate on the literal as written rather than on its value: a supplied number carrying more fractional digits than the field permits is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` even when the extra digits are zeros, so a two-place field accepts `12`, `12.5` and `12.50` but refuses `12.500`. Numbers must be finite — no infinity and no not-a-number. A field declared as a whole number accepts any numeric literal whose fractional part is zero, so `5` and `5.0` are the same integer 5, while a boolean is never a number and a numeric string is never a number. Where a field is declared with a range, the precision gate is applied before the range gate, and both report `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Every rounding operation in this specification, without exception, is **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Decimal quantities cross the wire as **unquoted JSON numbers**, trailing zeros included and never in exponent form — a two-decimal figure of fifty thousand is emitted as `50000.00`, not as `"50000.00"`, `50000` or `5.0E+4`. A decimal is rendered at exactly the scale at which the server holds it, which for almost every field is its **full declared width** below: every figure the server computes, and every declared input the server normalises on storage, is emitted padded to that width. The exceptions are the handful of fields the server deliberately stores without rescaling — the five market marks of §3.2, a co-product sale's `quote_reference_mark`, and `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu`, `hours_elapsed` and `temperature_c` — which echo the client's literal at exactly the width it was supplied, as noted at each. Apart from those, the decimal widths are fixed per quantity and are not negotiable, because the tests compare exact strings:

- Bushel quantities (`gross_wet_bushels`, `net_dry_bushels`, `bushels_contracted`, `bushels_delivered`, `corn_bushels_charged`, `corn_inventory_bushels`, `open_contract_coverage_bushels`) carry 2 decimals.
- Gallons (`capacity_gallons`, `opening_gallons`, `current_gallons`, `available_gallons`, `neat_gallons`, `denaturant_gallons`, `output_gallons`, `gallons_per_railcar`, `planned_gallons`, `loaded_gallons`, `ethanol_gallons_produced`, `rin_eligible_gallons`, `corn_oil_lbs_produced` and `corn_oil_inventory_lbs`) carry 2 decimals.
- Short tons (`ddgs_tons_produced`, `ddgs_inventory_tons`) and the sale `quantity` carry 3 decimals.
- Yield figures (`starch_bonus_gal_per_bushel`, `health_penalty_gal_per_bushel`, `intervention_penalty_gal_per_bushel`, `effective_yield_gal_per_bushel`, `rolling_yield_gal_per_bushel`) carry 3 decimals.
- Carbon intensity figures (`ci_score_g_per_mj`, `credited_reduction_g_per_mj`, `lcfs_credit_tonnes_per_million_gal`) carry 3 decimals, while the declared inputs `baseline_ci_g_per_mj`, `claimed_reduction_g_per_mj` and `verified_reduction_g_per_mj` carry 1 decimal.
- Permit figures (`limit_value`, `value`, `rolling_average_value`) carry 3 decimals.
- The fermentation health figures (`reading_score`, `fermentation_health_index`) carry 2 decimals, as do `moisture_pct`, `foreign_material_pct` and `damaged_kernels_pct`; `test_weight_lb_per_bu`, `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu`, `hours_elapsed`, `temperature_c` and `loading_rate_cars_per_hour` carry 1 decimal.
- `denaturant_pct` carries 6 decimals; `index_pct` carries 4 decimals, as does `freight_rate_per_unit`.

Where a multi-step formula in §3 pins an intermediate rounding, that pinned order is normative: rounding earlier or later than stated produces a different final value and is wrong even when the difference is a single hundredth.

### 2.7. Reservation Version Guards on Tanks and Tracks

Two creations in this system reserve part of a shared parent resource, and both require the caller to prove it has seen the parent's current state.

Scheduling a denaturant blend requires a non-optional `expected_destination_tank_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the destination product tank named by `destination_tank_id`. If that value does not equal the tank's current `version` — because some other writer has since moved liquid, taken the tank off service or reserved another window on it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TANK_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The caller's occupancy picture is stale, and a blend legitimately reserves a closed window on that tank, so the create must not proceed on an outdated view.

Scheduling a unit train shipment requires a non-optional `expected_track_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the loading track named by `track_id`. If it does not equal the track's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRACK_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the caller's view of `committed_railcars` no longer reflects the track and a shipment legitimately commits a slice of the track's aggregate spot capacity.

Both version fields are whole numbers with a floor of 1, and that floor is a request-shape gate rather than a guard failure: a supplied 0 or a negative value is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` at step 2 of the ladder, never with `ERR_STALE_TANK_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_TRACK_VERSION`. Any other integer is compared for exact equality with the parent's current `version`, so a value above the current one is just as stale as a value below it. Each guard value is also persisted on and echoed back by the reservation it created, as `expected_destination_tank_version` on the denaturant blend and `expected_track_version` on the unit train shipment.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Every other write is evaluated purely against current server state.

### 2.8. Plant Posture: Compliance, Procurement and the Grind Cap

The plant row is a singleton: exactly one may exist, every operational entity belongs to it implicitly, and no operational entity carries a plant foreign key. Two derived postures on that row gate production and shipping across the whole system, and both are recomputed immediately whenever any of their inputs change.

`compliance_posture` is a strictly **worst-of** aggregation, never additive, over exactly two input sets: every permit condition currently in `EXCEEDANCE`, and every product tank currently `ON_HOLD` whose `hold_reason` is `OFF_SPEC`. Rank the four values as `OPEN` lowest, then `RESTRICTED`, then `CURTAILED`, then `SUSPENDED` highest. Starting from `OPEN`, each permit condition in exceedance raises the posture to at least `RESTRICTED` when its `severity` is `MINOR`, at least `CURTAILED` when its severity is `MAJOR`, and `SUSPENDED` when its severity is `CRITICAL`; each off-spec tank hold raises it to at least `RESTRICTED`. The final posture is the highest rank reached. A tank held for `MECHANICAL` reasons contributes nothing to the posture, and neither does a resolved exceedance — the historical `exceedance_count` is irrelevant to the posture, only the current status matters. Two `MINOR` exceedances and an off-spec hold together still give `RESTRICTED`, not something worse.

`procurement_posture` is `ADEQUATE` when `open_contract_coverage_bushels` is greater than or equal to `coverage_floor_bushels`, and `SHORT` otherwise; the comparison is inclusive, so coverage exactly equal to the floor is still `ADEQUATE`. Because a brand-new plant has an empty contract book, its coverage is zero and it therefore starts `SHORT` whenever `coverage_floor_bushels` is greater than zero.

`daily_grind_cap_bushels` is derived from those two postures and the plant's own `base_grind_cap_bushels`. The compliance multiplier is 1.00 when the compliance posture is `OPEN`, 0.90 when it is `RESTRICTED`, 0.60 when it is `CURTAILED` and 0.00 when it is `SUSPENDED`. The procurement multiplier is 1.00 when the procurement posture is `ADEQUATE` and 0.75 when it is `SHORT`. The base cap is multiplied by both multipliers **in a single expression** and the product is then floored to a whole number of bushels — never rounded, and never floored after each multiplier separately. With the default base cap of 120,000 bushels a day, an open and adequately covered plant may grind 120,000; open but short gives 90,000; restricted and adequate gives 108,000; curtailed and adequate gives 72,000; and a suspended plant's cap is 0 regardless of coverage.

### 2.9. Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined by a fixed precedence ladder. Checks are evaluated in this order and the first failure wins:

1. **Authentication and authorisation of the call itself** — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, then `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
2. **Request shape** — `ERR_VALIDATION`. The body must conform to the endpoint's input contract: every required field present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges and decimal precision respected, month strings well formed as four digits, a hyphen and a month between 01 and 12, instants well formed as ISO 8601 UTC, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, quantities and gallon and contract counts strictly positive — with the single exception of `contracts_to_close` on a hedge close, whose floor of 1 is deliberately a capacity check at step 11 rather than a shape gate, so a zero or negative close size reports `ERR_CLOSE_EXCEEDS_OPEN_CONTRACTS` — `index_pct` present exactly when `pricing_basis` is `INDEX` and absent when it is `FLAT`, and unknown fields rejected outright. Within this one step the sub-order is also fixed. The body must first parse as JSON and must be a JSON object; a body that is absent, empty, whitespace-only or the literal `null` is read as the empty object `{}`, while a body that is a JSON array, string or number is refused outright. **Unknown fields are then rejected before any declared field is examined**, so a request that both names a field the endpoint does not define and omits one it requires reports the unknown field. The declared fields are then validated in the order the endpoint's own contract lists them, and the first one that fails ends the request. Throughout, a field explicitly supplied as `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: `null` in a required field is a missing required field, and `null` in an optional field takes that field's documented default. An endpoint documented as taking no body accepts an absent or empty body and rejects any field whatsoever as an unknown field. Every string-valued field in this API is required to be non-empty unless stated otherwise, and a value that is only whitespace counts as empty and is refused; a string that survives the gate is stored and echoed exactly as supplied, with its interior and surrounding whitespace intact.
3. **Existence of every referenced resource**, path identifiers before body identifiers, in the entity order of §2.5.
4. **Segregation of duties** on delivery settlement — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This sits after existence because it needs the delivery loaded to read `graded_by_user_id`.
5. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_PLANT_ALREADY_EXISTS` for a second plant, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` for a reused business code.
6. **Reservation version guards** — `ERR_STALE_TANK_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_TRACK_VERSION`.
7. **State preconditions** on the target resource, in the entity order contract, delivery, batch, tank, blend, shipment loading dimension, shipment billing dimension, invoiced lock, sale, RIN batch, carbon project, hedge position, permit condition.
8. **Safety and compliance gates** — plant suspension first, then tank service status, then the denaturant specification band, then the grind cap.
9. **Referential coherence** — tank product-type role checks, contract pricing, predecessor certification, shipment invoicing, posted marks, then open deliveries on a contract being rolled.
10. **Temporal checks** — quote expiry, tank occupancy conflict, clock regression.
11. **Constraint and capacity checks** — track capacity, track activity, intervention limit, revision limit, close-size limit, then contract remainder.
12. **Fiscal and inventory checks, evaluated last** — source tank volume, receiving tank capacity, corn inventory, then co-product inventory.

Two precedence pairs are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. When a batch is asked to distil while it is not `FERMENTING` **and** its destination tank is out of service, the state check wins and the response is `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, not `ERR_TANK_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. When a train is asked to load while the plant is `SUSPENDED` **and** the source tank holds less liquid than the shipment plans to draw, the safety gate wins and the response is `ERR_PLANT_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`.

### 2.10. Cascade and Version Propagation

A single action in this system routinely rewrites rows the caller never named. Three propagation rules apply everywhere and are not repeated at every workflow below.

First, **every row a cascade touches has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed**, exactly as if it had been written directly. Completing a batch increments the batch and the destination tank; issuing a bill of lading increments the shipment and every RIN batch that separates onto it; a mechanical tank hold increments the tank and every blend and shipment it cancels, plus the loading track whose committed railcars fall.

Second, **plant-level aggregates are re-derived, not incremented in place**. `corn_inventory_bushels`, `ddgs_inventory_tons`, `corn_oil_inventory_lbs`, `open_contract_coverage_bushels`, `rolling_yield_gal_per_bushel`, `rin_generated_count`, `rin_separated_count` and `rin_retired_count` are each defined as a sum over primary records in a named state, and after any action that changes those records the aggregate is recomputed from scratch. The derived postures and the grind cap are then re-derived from the refreshed aggregates, in the order coverage, then `procurement_posture`, then `daily_grind_cap_bushels`.

Third, **compensating actions restore balances but never resurrect reservations**. Cancelling a loaded shipment returns its gallons to the source tank and its railcars to the track, and reverts any RIN separation it performed. Releasing a tank hold, by contrast, un-blocks shipments that were merely blocked, but a blend or a shipment that a mechanical hold cancelled stays cancelled forever — release is not a compensating action and never re-reserves a freed window or a freed railcar slot.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning the Facility and Posting the Market Marks

Before anything else can happen, a `PlantManager` stands up the facility. The registration payload names the plant (`name`, non-empty and at most 120 characters) and declares its annual nameplate denatured-ethanol capacity as `nameplate_capacity_gallons`, a positive integer. Four further inputs are optional and carry defaults chosen for a typical 120-million-gallon dry mill: `base_grind_cap_bushels`, the unrestricted daily corn grind ceiling before posture multipliers, defaults to 120,000 bushels and must be a positive whole number of bushels; `coverage_floor_bushels`, the minimum open contracted coverage below which procurement is considered short, defaults to 200,000 bushels and is likewise a whole number of bushels that may be zero but not negative; `baseline_ci_g_per_mj`, the pathway baseline carbon intensity in grams of carbon dioxide equivalent per megajoule before any certified reduction, defaults to 60.0, is carried to one decimal and must be positive, and is normalised to exactly one decimal place on storage so that a value supplied as `62` reads back as `62.0`; and `rin_generation_enabled`, defaulting to true, which when false suppresses renewable credit creation on batch completion entirely.

Registration initialises every derived aggregate to its zero value — no corn, no distillers grains, no corn oil, no contracted coverage, and all three credit counters at zero. `compliance_posture` starts `OPEN` because nothing is in exceedance and no tank is off spec. `ci_score_g_per_mj` starts equal to `baseline_ci_g_per_mj` because no project has been certified, and `lcfs_credit_tonnes_per_million_gal` starts at 0.000. `rolling_yield_gal_per_bushel` starts at 2.850 gallons per bushel, the figure that also stands in whenever no batch has yet completed. `procurement_posture`, however, does **not** start optimistic: the contract book is empty, so coverage is zero, and with any non-zero coverage floor the plant is born `SHORT`. Under the default floor that makes the opening `daily_grind_cap_bushels` three quarters of the base cap floored to whole bushels — 90,000 with the default base. Exactly one plant may exist; a second registration attempt is refused with `ERR_PLANT_ALREADY_EXISTS`, and any plant-level read or write attempted before the plant exists is refused with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`. Because the row is a singleton, the `{id}` segment on a plant path is checked against it: a plant path naming any identifier other than the registered plant's own `id` is refused with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND` even though a plant does exist.

Five market marks drive every mark-based figure in the system, and they are posted together for a named contract month. A `PlantManager` or a `RiskManager` supplies `marks_effective_month` as a year-and-month string, `mark_corn_cents_per_bushel` (the corn futures mark in cents per bushel), `mark_ethanol_cents_per_gallon`, `mark_natgas_cents_per_mmbtu` (natural gas in cents per million British thermal units), `mark_soybean_meal_usd_per_ton` (the distillers grains index reference, US dollars per short ton) and `mark_sbo_cents_per_lb` (the soybean oil mark in cents per pound, used as the corn oil index reference). All five are required on every post and all five are unconstrained decimals: a mark may be negative, may be zero, and may carry any number of decimal places, and it is stored and echoed back **exactly as supplied** with neither rescaling nor rounding, so a corn mark posted as `440.000` reads back as `440.000`. All five are null on a fresh plant, and posting replaces the previous set wholesale rather than patching individual legs; the instant of the post is recorded in `marks_updated_at`. The body is validated before the plant is looked up, so posting a malformed mark set at an unknown plant id reports `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as the ladder of §2.9 requires. A posting also re-prices the futures book: every hedge position whose `status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_CLOSED` has its `unrealized_pnl_cents` re-derived against the new mark for its commodity and its own `version` incremented, while positions already `CLOSED` are left untouched. The plant's own `version` increments as well.

Operators drive time forward explicitly. Any of the eight roles may advance the logical clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant strictly later than the one in force; an earlier or identical instant is a regression and is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`. This matters more than it sounds: a permit condition's rolling window, a co-product quote's expiry and a tank occupancy comparison all read that instant, so advancing the clock can push a reading out of a rolling window or expire a quote without any other write occurring.

### 3.2. Corn Procurement: Basis Contracts, Pricing and Rolls

A `GrainBuyer` opens a corn purchase contract by naming the counterparty in `supplier_name` (non-empty, with no length ceiling), the `delivery_month` as a year-and-month string, the `bushels_contracted` on a 15.0 percent moisture dry basis (positive, two decimals), and the `basis_cents_per_bushel` — the over-or-under against futures, in cents per bushel, which may legitimately be negative, zero being permitted as well. Both decimal figures are normalised to exactly two decimal places on storage, so bushels supplied as `50000` read back as `50000.00`. A new contract opens with `status` at `OPEN`, `pricing_status` at `UNPRICED`, `bushels_delivered` at 0 and both `futures_price_cents_per_bushel` and `priced_at` null. Because the contract's unfilled remainder immediately joins the plant's coverage set, opening a contract re-derives `open_contract_coverage_bushels`, then `procurement_posture`, then `daily_grind_cap_bushels` — which is how a single large purchase can lift the plant out of `SHORT` and restore a quarter of its grind capacity. The whole corn chain is nonetheless independent of the plant row itself: opening, pricing and rolling a contract, and booking, grading and settling a load, neither require a plant to have been commissioned nor consult `compliance_posture`, so none of them can be refused with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PLANT_SUSPENDED`. Those aggregates simply become visible the moment a plant is read.

Each contract carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The fill dimension, `status`, runs `OPEN` → `PARTIALLY_FILLED` → `FILLED`, with `ROLLED` as an alternative terminus; it is advanced only as a consequence of delivery settlement or terminated by a roll, never set directly. The pricing dimension, `pricing_status`, runs `UNPRICED` → `PRICED` and nothing else. The two are genuinely orthogonal: a `PRICED` contract may sit at `OPEN` indefinitely, and an `UNPRICED` contract can never reach `FILLED` because settlement requires pricing first.

Fixing the futures leg is a one-shot action by a `GrainBuyer`, carrying a single `futures_price_cents_per_bushel` value that must be greater than zero. The contract's fill status must be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` — a `FILLED` or `ROLLED` contract refuses pricing with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE` — and its pricing dimension must still be `UNPRICED`; a second attempt on an already-priced contract is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_PRICED`. On success the futures price is stored, `pricing_status` moves `UNPRICED → PRICED`, `priced_at` records the logical clock instant, and the contract's **gross price** — the futures leg plus the basis, in cents per bushel — becomes available to the settlement arithmetic of §3.3.

The unfilled remainder of a contract can be pushed forward into a successor rather than defaulted on. A roll by a `GrainBuyer` names a new `delivery_month` and a `roll_basis_adjustment_cents` figure, in cents per bushel, that reflects the cost of carrying the position. Three preconditions apply, and they are evaluated in the precedence order of §2.9 rather than in the order they are described. First, the source contract's fill status must be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. Second, no delivery against it may still be in flight: if any of its deliveries has a `grading_status` of `PENDING_GRADE` or `GRADED`, the roll is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_HAS_OPEN_DELIVERIES`, because those loads must be settled or rejected before the remainder is known with certainty. Third and last, its remainder — `bushels_contracted` less `bushels_delivered` — must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_FULLY_DELIVERED`; a fully delivered contract that also has a load in flight therefore reports the open deliveries. The successor's `delivery_month` is checked only for well-formedness: nothing requires it to fall later than the source's month, and a roll into the same month or an earlier one is accepted. A successful roll moves the source contract to `ROLLED` and creates a successor contract, which is itself the row returned in the `data` field — the roll answers 201 with the **successor**, never with the source, and the caller must re-read the source to observe its new `ROLLED` status and incremented `version`. The successor's `rolled_from_contract_id` points back at the source, its `bushels_contracted` equals the source's unfilled remainder, its `basis_cents_per_bushel` equals the source's basis plus `roll_basis_adjustment_cents` (which is stored on the successor as its own `roll_basis_adjustment_cents`), its `pricing_status` is `UNPRICED` and its fill `status` is `OPEN`. Coverage is then re-derived: the source leaves the coverage set and the successor joins it, so `procurement_posture` and `daily_grind_cap_bushels` are both recomputed even though the total bushel count barely moved.

Coverage itself is defined narrowly. `open_contract_coverage_bushels` is the sum, over contracts whose `status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, of each contract's `bushels_contracted` less its `bushels_delivered`, each remainder rounded to two decimals. Contracts at `FILLED` or `ROLLED` are excluded from the input set **entirely** — not counted as zero, but dropped — which is why a roll can leave a non-zero remainder on the source row that no longer counts anywhere, the successor carrying it instead. Contracts are discoverable through a filtered list that accepts `status`, `delivery_month`, `pricing_status` and `rolled_from_contract_id`, the last of which is what makes the self-referential roll chain navigable in both directions.

### 3.3. The Scale House: Inbound Loads, Grading and Settlement

A `ScaleOperator` books each inbound truck against a specific contract, supplying the scale `ticket_number` — non-empty and globally unique across the whole system, so a re-keyed ticket is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — together with `gross_weight_lbs`, the loaded truck weight in whole pounds, and `tare_weight_lbs`, the empty truck weight, which must be at least zero and strictly less than the gross weight. The delivery's `contract_id` is taken from the creation path and never from the body. Two contract-level gates apply at booking: a contract whose fill `status` is `ROLLED` accepts no further loads and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`, because its remainder now lives on its successor; and a contract whose settled bushels already meet or exceed `bushels_contracted` is refused with the more specific `ERR_CONTRACT_FULLY_DELIVERED`. A booked load lands at `grading_status` `PENDING_GRADE` with every quality reading, bushel figure, discount component and money field null.

Grading is the load's single moment of quality truth, and it is also where the discount schedule is computed. A `ScaleOperator` — which must still be a load at `PENDING_GRADE`, since a `REJECTED` or already `SETTLED` load accepts neither grading nor settlement and both are refused with `ERR_INVALID_DELIVERY_STATE` — records five readings: `moisture_pct` (between 0 and 40 inclusive, two decimals), `test_weight_lb_per_bu` (positive, one decimal), `foreign_material_pct` (between 0 and 20 inclusive, two decimals), `damaged_kernels_pct` (between 0 and 40 inclusive, two decimals) and `aflatoxin_ppb` (a non-negative whole number of parts per billion). The operator's `X-User-ID` is stored as `graded_by_user_id`.

Two inbound safety limits fork the outcome. A load whose `aflatoxin_ppb` exceeds 20 parts per billion, or whose `moisture_pct` exceeds 20.00 percent, is auto-rejected: `grading_status` moves `PENDING_GRADE → REJECTED`, `rejection_reason` is set, and `net_price_cents_per_bushel` and `settlement_amount_cents` are left null because a rejected load is never priced and contributes nothing to `bushels_delivered`. The aflatoxin test is evaluated **first**, so a load breaching both limits reports `AFLATOXIN_OVER_LIMIT` rather than `MOISTURE_OVER_LIMIT`. Crucially, auto-rejection is a **successful** response, not an error: the load was legitimately graded and the grade happens to be a rejection. A load inside both limits moves `PENDING_GRADE → GRADED` and carries its computed figures forward. Either outcome records the five readings and `graded_by_user_id` on the row, and either outcome computes and stores `gross_wet_bushels`, `net_dry_bushels` and all four discount components together with their `discount_cents_per_bushel` total: rejection suppresses only the two money figures, and a `REJECTED` load is therefore fully documented on quality and discount even though it will never be paid.

The bushel and discount arithmetic runs in five pinned steps. **First**, the net wet weight is the gross weight less the tare weight, and `gross_wet_bushels` is that net weight divided by 56.0 pounds per bushel, rounded half up to two decimals. **Second**, the load is shrunk to the 15.0 percent moisture dry basis: the shrink factor is 100 less the recorded moisture percentage, divided by 100 less 15.0, and `net_dry_bushels` is `gross_wet_bushels` multiplied by that factor, rounded half up to two decimals. At exactly 15.0 percent moisture the factor is 1 and no shrink applies, so the wet and dry bushel figures are identical. **Third**, the contract's gross price in cents per bushel is its `futures_price_cents_per_bushel` plus its `basis_cents_per_bushel`.

**Fourth**, four discount components are computed, each on **integer step counts taken over whole hundredths or tenths** rather than over floating-point division of the decimal readings. Convert `moisture_pct`, `foreign_material_pct` and `damaged_kernels_pct` each into whole hundredths by multiplying by 100 and rounding half up to a whole number, and convert `test_weight_lb_per_bu` into whole tenths by multiplying by 10 and rounding half up to a whole number. Then:

- **Moisture steps**: take the moisture hundredths less 1,500 (that is, the excess over 15.00 percent), treat any negative result as zero, divide by 50 — one step per half a percentage point — and round the quotient **up** to the next whole step. Each step costs 4.0 cents per bushel.
- **Test-weight steps**: take 540 less the test-weight tenths (that is, how many whole tenths of a pound the load falls below the 54.0 pound-per-bushel basis) and treat any negative result as zero. Each step costs 0.35 cents per bushel.
- **Foreign-material steps**: take the foreign-material hundredths less 300 (the excess over the 3.00 percent tolerance) and treat any negative result as zero. Each step costs 0.15 cents per bushel.
- **Damage steps**: take the damaged-kernel hundredths less 500 (the excess over the 5.00 percent tolerance) and treat any negative result as zero. Each step costs 0.08 cents per bushel.

Each component — `moisture_discount_cents`, `test_weight_discount_cents`, `foreign_material_discount_cents` and `damage_discount_cents` — is its own step count multiplied by its own rate, **rounded half up to two decimals independently**, before any of them are added together. **Fifth**, `discount_cents_per_bushel` is the sum of those four separately rounded components; `net_price_cents_per_bushel` is the contract gross price less that total discount, with a floor of 0.00 so a load can never carry a negative price; and at settlement `settlement_amount_cents` is `net_dry_bushels` multiplied by `net_price_cents_per_bushel`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The net price can only be formed once the parent contract's futures leg exists, and grading does not require pricing: grading a load against a contract that is already `PRICED` stores `net_price_cents_per_bushel` there and then, while grading against a still-`UNPRICED` contract leaves it null and defers it to settlement, where it is recomputed from the readings on file. `settlement_amount_cents` is null until settlement in either case. A load exactly on every basis — 15.00 percent moisture, 54.0 pound test weight, 3.00 percent foreign material, 5.00 percent damage — takes all four discounts at 0.00 and settles at the full gross price.

Settlement is performed by a `GrainAccountant` and takes no request body at all; every input is already on the record. Three preconditions guard it, in the order the ladder of §2.9 fixes: the settling operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `graded_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; then the load's `grading_status` must be `GRADED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DELIVERY_STATE`; then the parent contract's `pricing_status` must be `PRICED`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_PRICED`, since the futures leg is null while the contract is unpriced and the money cannot be computed. Segregation genuinely comes first, so the grading operator's own attempt to settle a load that is already `SETTLED` reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and not `ERR_INVALID_DELIVERY_STATE`; the comparison is vacuous on a load still at `PENDING_GRADE`, whose `graded_by_user_id` is null, and that request falls through to the state check. On success `grading_status` moves `GRADED → SETTLED`, `settlement_amount_cents` is computed, `settled_by_user_id` and `settled_at` are recorded, and the deepest cascade in the system fires.

That cascade runs in a fixed order. The contract's `bushels_delivered` is recomputed as the sum of `net_dry_bushels` over its own `SETTLED` deliveries — `REJECTED` and `GRADED` loads never count — and the contract's fill dimension advances accordingly: to `PARTIALLY_FILLED` when delivered bushels are now strictly greater than zero but still strictly less than `bushels_contracted`, and to `FILLED` when delivered bushels reach or exceed `bushels_contracted`; a contract already at `PARTIALLY_FILLED` advances to `FILLED` under the same condition. The delivered bushels are then added to the plant's `corn_inventory_bushels`. Finally `open_contract_coverage_bushels` is re-derived, then `procurement_posture`, then `daily_grind_cap_bushels`. The chain has a counter-intuitive consequence worth internalising: because a `FILLED` contract drops out of the coverage set entirely, settling a small load that happens to complete a contract can **lower** total coverage below `coverage_floor_bushels`, flip `procurement_posture` from `ADEQUATE` to `SHORT`, and cut the plant's daily grind cap by a quarter — a settlement that shrinks the plant's production ceiling. Both the delivery's and the contract's `version` increment.

A contract's deliveries are listable as a sub-resource, filterable by `grading_status`, which is the reverse path from a contract to the loads booked against it.

### 3.4. Fermentation: Charging, Health Watch, Intervention and Distillation

Charging a fermenter is the single largest consumer of corn and the single most heavily gated action in the plant. A `ProcessOperator` supplies a globally unique `batch_code`, the dry-basis `corn_bushels_charged` (positive, two decimals), the `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu` — the weighted test weight of the corn actually going into the fermenter, positive and carried to one decimal, which is what earns the starch bonus later — and a `destination_tank_id` naming the tank that will receive the distilled neat ethanol. Five gates fire in a fixed order. A plant whose `compliance_posture` is `SUSPENDED` may not charge at all, and the refusal is `ERR_PLANT_SUSPENDED`. The destination tank must have `service_status` `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_TANK_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. The charge may not exceed the plant's current `daily_grind_cap_bushels` — the cap already scaled by both postures — otherwise `ERR_GRIND_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The destination tank's `product_type` must be `ETHANOL_200_PROOF`, otherwise `ERR_TANK_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. And last, the plant's `corn_inventory_bushels` must be at least the bushels being charged, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CORN_INVENTORY`. Charging is also the one fermentation action that requires the plant row itself to exist: with no plant registered the charge is refused with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, and that lookup sits **after** the `batch_code` uniqueness gate rather than with the ordinary existence checks, so a duplicate batch code posted at an uncommissioned plant reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Appending a reading, intervening, distilling, completing and dumping never require the plant row and can never answer `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`; completion reads the plant only to consult `rin_generation_enabled`, and distillation only through the derived posture. `corn_bushels_charged` is normalised to exactly two decimals on storage, while `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu` is stored and echoed back **exactly as supplied** rather than padded to its one declared decimal, so a charge test weight posted as `54` reads back as `54` and one posted as `54.0` reads back as `54.0`.

A charged batch lands at `status` `CHARGED` with `charged_at` recorded from the logical clock, `intervention_count` at 0 and `fermentation_health_index` at 100.00 — the value that stands while no reading exists. Everything else on the row is null: all four yield components, all three product volumes (`ethanol_gallons_produced`, `ddgs_tons_produced`, `corn_oil_lbs_produced`), `distillation_started_at`, `completed_at`, `dumped_at` and `dump_reason`. The charged bushels are subtracted from `corn_inventory_bushels` immediately, and they are **never returned**: charged corn is consumed the moment it is ground, and no later outcome, dump included, restores it.

Fermentation health is tracked through append-only lab readings. Each reading carries `hours_elapsed` since the fermenter was charged (at least zero, one decimal), `lactic_acid_ppm` and `acetic_acid_ppm` as non-negative whole parts per million, and `temperature_c` between 0 and 60 inclusive with one decimal. Readings are immutable: each carries only its own `id`, its `created_at` and the `batch_id` taken from the creation path, and there is no way to edit or remove one. Like `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu`, `hours_elapsed` and `temperature_c` are stored and echoed back exactly as supplied rather than padded to their one declared decimal, so `12` reads back as `12` and `12.0` as `12.0`; the derived `reading_score` is always rendered at its full two decimals. Every append recomputes the batch's health index and may move the batch's state.

Each reading first earns its own `reading_score`, and the three penalty components are each rounded to two decimals and capped **before** they are subtracted. Lactic acid is free up to 800 parts per million; the excess above that threshold is multiplied by 0.047 penalty points per part per million, rounded half up to two decimals, and then capped at 40 points. Acetic acid is free up to 300 parts per million; the excess is multiplied by 0.081 points per part per million, rounded half up to two decimals, and capped at 30 points. Temperature is judged against a band from 30.0 to 35.0 degrees Celsius: the deviation is how far the reading sits above 35.0 or below 30.0, whichever applies, and is zero anywhere inside the band including exactly at either edge. When the deviation is zero the temperature penalty is zero and **no step charge is applied at all**; when the deviation is non-zero the penalty is a fixed 9.75-point out-of-band step plus 3.9 points per degree of excursion, rounded half up to two decimals and then capped at 20 points. The `reading_score` is 100 less the three capped penalties, clamped into the range 0 through 100, and rounded half up to two decimals. That rounded score is persisted on the reading row.

The batch's `fermentation_health_index` is then a weighted mean over the **three most recent readings**, ordered by `hours_elapsed` descending with ties broken by `created_at` descending and, where those tie as well, by the server's insertion sequence **descending** — the whole ordering key runs in reverse, so of two readings sharing an `hours_elapsed` and a `created_at` the one written later is treated as the more recent, which is the one exception to the earlier-rows-sort-earlier tiebreak of §2.3. Because the window is chosen by `hours_elapsed` rather than by arrival order, appending a reading whose `hours_elapsed` falls below the three already on file leaves the index exactly where it was — the batch row is nonetheless rewritten and its `version` increments on every append. The three weights are 0.5 for the most recent reading, 0.3 for the second and 0.2 for the third; when fewer than three readings exist only the leading weights actually consumed are used. Each weight is normalised by dividing it by the sum of the weights actually in play and rounding half up to six decimals, and the index is the sum of each normalised weight multiplied by its reading's **already-rounded** `reading_score`, rounded half up to two decimals. The rounding order here is pinned and load-bearing: weighting the unrounded raw scores and rounding only the final mean is a different and wrong answer that can differ by one hundredth.

The index drives a cyclic state machine. From `CHARGED`, an appended reading moves the batch to `FERMENTING` when the recomputed index is at least 70.00 and to `HEALTH_WATCH` when it is strictly below 70.00. From `FERMENTING`, an appended reading keeping the index at or above 70.00 is a `FERMENTING → FERMENTING` self-transition — not a no-op, because the appended reading changes the three-reading window and therefore the yield the batch will realise — while an index strictly below 70.00 moves it `FERMENTING → HEALTH_WATCH`. Exactly 70.00 is healthy. From `HEALTH_WATCH`, an appended reading is a `HEALTH_WATCH → HEALTH_WATCH` self-transition **regardless of the new index**: recovery is never automatic, and a later clean reading that lifts the index back above 70.00 does not release the batch. Appending a reading to a `DISTILLING`, `COMPLETED` or `DUMPED` batch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` — only `CHARGED`, `FERMENTING` and `HEALTH_WATCH` admit an append, so once a batch is on the still its health window is frozen — as is any action a batch's current state does not admit. A batch's readings are listable as a sub-resource in exactly the order the index calculation uses, reversed insertion tiebreak included — `hours_elapsed` descending, then `created_at` descending, then insertion sequence descending — and that list accepts `limit` and `offset`.

Leaving `HEALTH_WATCH` requires an explicit intervention. A `ProcessOperator` doses the batch, recording a free-text `intervention_note`, and the batch moves `HEALTH_WATCH → FERMENTING`. The intervention is not free: `intervention_count` rises by 1 and `intervention_penalty_gal_per_bushel` is recomputed, permanently lowering the yield the batch can ever realise. It is the one yield component written before the still is charged: `starch_bonus_gal_per_bushel`, `health_penalty_gal_per_bushel` and `effective_yield_gal_per_bushel` all stay null until distillation, so an intervened batch carries one populated yield figure and three nulls. At most two interventions are permitted per batch; a third is refused with `ERR_INTERVENTION_LIMIT_REACHED`, which means a batch that keeps degrading after two doses can only be dumped. Intervening on a batch that is not in `HEALTH_WATCH` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

Sending the batch to the still moves it `FERMENTING → DISTILLING` and takes no request body. It requires that the batch be `FERMENTING` — a batch on health watch may not be distilled — that the plant not be `SUSPENDED`, and that the destination tank be `IN_SERVICE`, evaluated in exactly that order per §2.9, so a suspended plant whose destination tank is also out of service reports `ERR_PLANT_SUSPENDED`. At that instant the yield is frozen from four components, and the index it reads is the index **as of the distill call**, so readings appended afterwards cannot change it. First, `starch_bonus_gal_per_bushel`: convert `charge_test_weight_lb_per_bu` into whole tenths of a pound by multiplying by 10 and rounding half up to a whole number, take the excess of that figure over 540 tenths (the 54.0 pound-per-bushel basis) treating any negative result as zero, multiply by 0.0015 gallons per tenth, round half up to three decimals, and cap the result at 0.090 gallons per bushel. Second, `health_penalty_gal_per_bushel` is a descending tier ladder on `fermentation_health_index`: 0.000 at an index of 90.00 or above, 0.050 at an index of at least 75.00 but below 90.00, 0.150 at an index of at least 60.00 but below 75.00, and 0.300 below 60.00. Third, `intervention_penalty_gal_per_bushel` is `intervention_count` multiplied by 0.040 gallons per bushel, rounded half up to three decimals and capped at 0.100. Fourth, `effective_yield_gal_per_bushel` is a base yield of 2.850 gallons per bushel plus the starch bonus less the health penalty less the intervention penalty, rounded half up to three decimals and then clamped into the range 2.550 through 3.050 gallons per bushel. `distillation_started_at` records the instant.

Completing the batch moves it `DISTILLING → COMPLETED`, takes no request body, and is where physical product appears. It requires the batch to be `DISTILLING`, the destination tank to be `IN_SERVICE`, and that tank's `available_gallons` to be at least the ethanol volume about to land — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_CAPACITY`. Three volumes are computed from the frozen yield: `ethanol_gallons_produced` is `corn_bushels_charged` multiplied by `effective_yield_gal_per_bushel`, rounded half up to two decimals; `ddgs_tons_produced` is `corn_bushels_charged` multiplied by 17.0 pounds of distillers grains per bushel, divided by 2,000 pounds per short ton, rounded half up to three decimals; and `corn_oil_lbs_produced` is `corn_bushels_charged` multiplied by 0.600 pounds of corn oil per bushel, rounded half up to two decimals. The ethanol gallons are credited to the destination tank's `current_gallons` and that tank's `version` increments. The distillers grains tons are added to `ddgs_inventory_tons` and the corn oil pounds to `corn_oil_inventory_lbs`. `rolling_yield_gal_per_bushel` is then re-derived across all completed batches as the sum of `ethanol_gallons_produced` divided by the sum of `corn_bushels_charged` over batches whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, rounded half up to three decimals, falling back to 2.850 when no completed batch exists. And when `rin_generation_enabled` is true on the plant, exactly one renewable credit batch is created at `GENERATED`, raising `rin_generated_count`; where no plant row exists at all the switch is read as enabled and the credit batch is minted anyway, since only an explicit `false` on a registered plant suppresses it. `completed_at` records the instant.

Dumping is the escape hatch, available from `CHARGED`, `FERMENTING` or `HEALTH_WATCH`, and it is the only exit for a batch stuck on health watch with both interventions spent. The operator supplies a free-text `dump_reason`; the batch moves to `DUMPED`, `dumped_at` and the reason are recorded, and the outcome is deliberately unforgiving: the charged corn stays consumed and `corn_inventory_bushels` is not restored, no ethanol, no distillers grains, no corn oil and no renewable credits are created. `COMPLETED` and `DUMPED` are both terminal and accept nothing further.

Corn inventory follows directly from those rules. `corn_inventory_bushels` is the sum of `net_dry_bushels` over deliveries whose `grading_status` is `SETTLED`, less the sum of `corn_bushels_charged` over batches whose `status` is any of `CHARGED`, `FERMENTING`, `HEALTH_WATCH`, `DISTILLING`, `COMPLETED` or `DUMPED` — every state a batch can occupy, dumped included. Likewise `ddgs_inventory_tons` is the sum of `ddgs_tons_produced` over `COMPLETED` batches less the `quantity` of every `SHIPPED` sale whose `product` is `DDGS_DRY` or `DDGS_WET`, and `corn_oil_inventory_lbs` is the sum of `corn_oil_lbs_produced` over `COMPLETED` batches less the `quantity` of every `SHIPPED` `CORN_OIL` sale. Batches destined for a given tank are listable as a sub-resource of that tank, filterable by `status`.

### 3.5. The Tank Farm: Service State, Denaturant Blending and the E100 Specification

Tanks are the plant's safety choke point, and a hold on one propagates outward into batches, blends and shipments. A `ProcessOperator` commissions a tank with a globally unique `tank_code` such as T-101, a `product_type` that is one of `ETHANOL_200_PROOF`, `DENATURANT` or `DENATURED_E100`, and a positive working `capacity_gallons` carried to two decimals. Two inputs are optional: `opening_gallons`, the volume already in the tank at commissioning, defaults to 0 and must be at least zero and no greater than the capacity, that ceiling being a request-shape gate reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` and evaluated before the `tank_code` uniqueness check; and `settling_buffer_minutes`, defaulting to 120, a non-negative whole number of minutes appended to the end of every blend window on this tank before its occupancy interval is compared with other reservations. A new tank is `IN_SERVICE`, its `current_gallons` equal to its opening gallons, its `hold_reason` and `held_at` null.

A tank's balance is never incremented in place; it is re-derived from movements. `current_gallons` is the `opening_gallons` plus the `ethanol_gallons_produced` of every `COMPLETED` batch destined for this tank, plus the `output_gallons` of every `EXECUTED` blend whose destination is this tank, less the `neat_gallons` of every `EXECUTED` blend drawing neat ethanol from this tank, less the `denaturant_gallons` of every `EXECUTED` blend drawing denaturant from this tank, less the `loaded_gallons` of every shipment whose `loading_status` is `LOADED` and whose source is this tank. Only terminal, effective movements count — a scheduled blend moves nothing, and a cancelled shipment has null loaded gallons and drops out of the debit set entirely, which is exactly how cancellation restores the plant-wide gallons invariant without any separate reversal record. `available_gallons` is `capacity_gallons` less `current_gallons`, floored at 0.00, and both figures carry two decimals. `current_gallons` may never go negative; an operation that would drive it below zero is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`.

Placing a hold is one action that **forks on `hold_reason`** into two very different outcomes, and understanding the difference matters more than any other tank rule. A hold may only be placed on a tank that is `IN_SERVICE`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TANK_STATE`. In both branches `hold_reason` and `held_at` are recorded and the tank's `version` increments.

- An **`OFF_SPEC`** hold is a quality event. `service_status` moves `IN_SERVICE → ON_HOLD`. Every shipment whose source is this tank and whose `loading_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → BLOCKED`, and each blocked shipment's `railcar_count` **remains** inside its track's `committed_railcars`, because the window is still reserved and the train is merely paused. The loading track row is therefore not rewritten at all and its `version` does not move. The shipment's `billing_status` is untouched. Scheduled blends referencing this tank are left completely alone. And because an off-spec hold is a compliance event, `compliance_posture` is re-derived — the hold contributes at least `RESTRICTED` — which in turn lowers `daily_grind_cap_bushels`.
- A **`MECHANICAL`** hold is an equipment event. `service_status` moves `IN_SERVICE → OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Every blend whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and which references this tank in **any** of its three roles — as `neat_source_tank_id`, as `denaturant_source_tank_id` or as `destination_tank_id` — moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, recording `cancelled_at`, moving no liquid and changing no tank balance, and releasing its occupancy interval on its destination tank so a later blend may reserve the same window. Every shipment whose source is this tank and whose `loading_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with its `billing_status` forced to `VOIDED`, and that cancellation **drops** its `railcar_count` out of the track's `committed_railcars`, freeing aggregate spot capacity for a later shipment and incrementing the track's `version`. Deliberately, a mechanical hold does **not** change `compliance_posture` at all: a broken pump is not a permit violation.

Releasing a hold requires the tank to be `ON_HOLD` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — releasing a tank already `IN_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TANK_STATE` — and takes no body. `hold_reason` and `held_at` are cleared, `service_status` returns to `IN_SERVICE` from either state, every shipment on this source tank whose `loading_status` is `BLOCKED` moves `BLOCKED → SCHEDULED` with its own `version` incremented, and `compliance_posture` and `daily_grind_cap_bushels` are re-derived. What release does **not** do is undo a mechanical hold's destruction: blends and shipments that were cancelled stay `CANCELLED`, their windows and railcars stay freed, and nothing is re-reserved.

Blending turns neat ethanol into finished, denatured E100. Scheduling a blend is a two-phase affair: the schedule reserves an occupancy window on the destination tank, and a later execution moves the liquid. A `ProcessOperator` supplies a globally unique `blend_code`, the three tank references in the body order `neat_source_tank_id`, `denaturant_source_tank_id` then `destination_tank_id` — which is the order a missing or malformed reference is reported in, even though existence itself is resolved destination-first per §2.5 — the `expected_destination_tank_version` version guard described in §2.7, the `neat_gallons` and `denaturant_gallons` to draw (each positive with two decimals), and the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start. Each referenced tank's `product_type` must match its role — `ETHANOL_200_PROOF` for the neat source, `DENATURANT` for the denaturant source, `DENATURED_E100` for the destination — and any mismatch is refused with `ERR_TANK_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, the three roles being tested in that same order. Scheduling consults no tank's service state and no plant posture: a blend may legitimately be scheduled onto a tank that is `ON_HOLD` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and onto an uncommissioned plant, because it is only execution that demands all three tanks be `IN_SERVICE`.

Occupancy on a destination tank is **exclusive**: one reservation at a time. The blend's `occupancy_end` is computed as `window_end` plus the destination tank's `settling_buffer_minutes`, and the reserved interval runs from `window_start` through `occupancy_end` as a **closed** interval on both ends. Two intervals conflict when the candidate's start is at or before an existing blend's occupancy end **and** that existing blend's start is at or before the candidate's occupancy end — which means **touching endpoints do conflict**. Only blends whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` on the same destination tank are considered; executed and cancelled blends have released their window. A conflict is refused with `ERR_TANK_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. Concretely, on a tank with a 120-minute settling buffer, a blend whose window ends at noon occupies the tank through 14:00, so a second blend starting at exactly 14:00 conflicts while one starting at 14:01 does not. Instants are compared at second resolution in UTC. A successful schedule lands the blend at `SCHEDULED`, with `output_gallons`, `denaturant_pct`, `executed_at` and `cancelled_at` all null, and increments the destination tank's `version`.

Executing a blend requires the blend to be `SCHEDULED` — an `EXECUTED` or `CANCELLED` blend is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BLEND_STATE` — and takes no request body. All three referenced tanks must be `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_TANK_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. The blend must then be on specification. `output_gallons` is `neat_gallons` plus `denaturant_gallons`, rounded half up to two decimals, and `denaturant_pct` is `denaturant_gallons` divided by `output_gallons`, rounded half up to six decimals. The finished-product band runs from 0.019800 to 0.029800 **inclusive on both bounds**, so a blend sitting exactly on either edge is on spec; anything outside is refused with `ERR_DENATURANT_SPEC_VIOLATION` and no liquid moves — and neither `output_gallons` nor `denaturant_pct` is written, both staying null on a blend that remains `SCHEDULED` and may be re-attempted. Execution is never gated on the clock or on the reserved window: a scheduled blend may be executed before, inside or long after its `window_start`–`occupancy_end` interval, which exists only to arbitrate scheduling conflicts. After the specification check, volume is checked: the neat source's `current_gallons` must be at least `neat_gallons` and the denaturant source's at least `denaturant_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`; and the destination's `available_gallons` must be at least the combined output, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_CAPACITY`. On success the blend moves `SCHEDULED → EXECUTED`, `executed_at` is recorded, the two sources are debited and the destination credited, and all three tanks have their `version` incremented alongside the blend's own. Blends referencing a tank in any capacity are listable as a sub-resource of that tank, filterable by `status` and by `tank_role`, ordered by `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending. Unlike every other enum filter in this API, `tank_role` is matched leniently: the supplied text is trimmed and upper-cased, and each of the three roles answers to three spellings — `NEAT_SOURCE`, `NEAT` or `NEAT_SOURCE_TANK` for the neat source, `DENATURANT_SOURCE`, `DENATURANT` or `DENATURANT_SOURCE_TANK` for the denaturant source, and `DESTINATION`, `DEST` or `DESTINATION_TANK` for the destination. Any other value is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; omitted or empty, the filter is not applied and the list returns every blend naming the tank in any of its three roles.

### 3.6. Rail Logistics: Track Capacity, Loading, Billing and Cancellation

Unlike tanks, loading tracks are **shared**. A `LogisticsCoordinator` commissions one with a globally unique `track_code` such as TRK-A and a positive whole `spot_capacity` giving the maximum railcars that may be committed to overlapping windows on that track. Two optional inputs follow: `loading_rate_cars_per_hour`, a positive one-decimal figure defaulting to 4.0 that is purely informational and gates nothing, and `is_active`, defaulting to true, which when false makes the track refuse new shipments. A new track opens with `committed_railcars` at 0, that figure being the sum of `railcar_count` over shipments on the track whose `loading_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `BLOCKED` or `LOADED` — cancelled shipments release their railcars entirely.

Scheduling a unit train commits a slice of that shared capacity. A `LogisticsCoordinator` supplies a globally unique `shipment_code`, the `track_id` and its `expected_track_version` guard, the `source_tank_id` naming the finished-product tank the train will draw from, the receiving `destination_terminal` as a non-empty name, the `railcar_count` (a positive whole number), the `gallons_per_railcar` (positive, two decimals), the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end` with the end strictly after the start, and the `price_cents_per_gallon` at which the denatured ethanol is sold (positive, two decimals). The source tank's `product_type` must be `DENATURED_E100`, otherwise `ERR_TANK_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH`; that is the only gate the source tank faces at scheduling time. Scheduling consults neither the plant row nor its `compliance_posture` nor the tank's service state, so a train may be scheduled at an uncommissioned or suspended plant and against a tank already on hold — and such a train lands at `SCHEDULED`, never at `BLOCKED`, because only a hold placed *after* the schedule blocks anything.

Track windows use the **opposite** interval arithmetic to tank occupancy, and the contrast is deliberate. A shipment window is **half-open**: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. Two windows overlap when the first starts strictly before the second ends **and** the second starts strictly before the first ends, which means adjacency does **not** overlap — a window beginning exactly when another ends is a separate spot. Capacity is then judged in the aggregate rather than exclusively: sum the `railcar_count` of every shipment on the same track whose `loading_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `BLOCKED` or `LOADED` and whose window overlaps the candidate's, add the candidate's own `railcar_count`, and accept the schedule when that total is **at most** the track's `spot_capacity` — the bound is inclusive, so landing exactly on capacity is accepted. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_TRACK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On a track with a spot capacity of 110 railcars, a 60-car shipment from 06:00 to 12:00 and a 50-car shipment from 08:00 to 14:00 total exactly 110 and both stand, while one further car in any overlapping window is rejected; a 110-car shipment beginning exactly at 14:00 overlaps nothing and is accepted. Instants are compared at second resolution in UTC. After capacity, the track's `is_active` flag is checked, and an inactive track refuses the schedule with `ERR_TRACK_INACTIVE`.

A scheduled shipment lands with `planned_gallons` computed as `railcar_count` multiplied by `gallons_per_railcar` (two decimals), its `loading_status` at `SCHEDULED` and its `billing_status` at `PENDING_BOL`. Every other outcome field starts null — `loaded_gallons`, `invoice_amount_cents`, `bol_number`, `bol_issued_at`, `invoiced_at`, `loaded_at` and `cancelled_at`. Its railcars are added to the track's `committed_railcars` and the track's `version` increments.

Every shipment then runs **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The physical dimension, `loading_status`, tracks the move through `SCHEDULED`, `BLOCKED`, `LOADED` and `CANCELLED`. The paperwork dimension, `billing_status`, tracks the documents through `PENDING_BOL`, `BOL_ISSUED`, `INVOICED` and `VOIDED`. They evolve independently: a shipment can be blocked and unblocked any number of times while its billing dimension sits untouched at `PENDING_BOL`, and it can reach `LOADED` while still `PENDING_BOL`.

Loading the train moves it `SCHEDULED → LOADED` and takes no body. A `BLOCKED` shipment cannot be loaded and a `CANCELLED` one accepts nothing; either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_LOADING_STATE`. The plant must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_PLANT_SUSPENDED`; the source tank must be `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_TANK_NOT_IN_SERVICE`; and that tank's `current_gallons` must be at least `planned_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`. The suspension test reads the derived posture of §2.8 rather than the plant row, so loading is permitted when no plant has been registered at all — an unregistered plant has nothing in exceedance and no off-spec hold, hence an `OPEN` posture. On success `loaded_gallons` is set equal to `planned_gallons`, `loaded_at` records the logical clock, the source tank is debited by the loaded gallons and that tank's `version` increments; the loading track is not rewritten, because a `LOADED` shipment's railcars were already inside `committed_railcars` and stay there.

Issuing the bill of lading is the moment renewable credits separate from the fuel. It requires `billing_status` to be `PENDING_BOL` — a re-issue is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_BILLING_STATE` — and `loading_status` to be `LOADED`, since a train that has not been loaded has nothing to document, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_LOADING_STATE`. The coordinator supplies the `bol_number`, which is stored alongside `bol_issued_at`, and the billing dimension moves `PENDING_BOL → BOL_ISSUED`. The credit separation that rides along is described in §3.8.

Invoicing moves the billing dimension `BOL_ISSUED → INVOICED` and takes no body; any other billing state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_BILLING_STATE`. `invoice_amount_cents` is computed as `loaded_gallons` multiplied by `price_cents_per_gallon`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `invoiced_at` is recorded. Invoicing is the **point of no return**: it locks the shipment against cancellation and brings its revenue into the realized crush-margin view of §3.9.

Cancellation is the system's most thorough compensating action. A `LogisticsCoordinator` supplies a `cancellation_reason`. The loading dimension must be `SCHEDULED`, `BLOCKED` or `LOADED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_LOADING_STATE`; and the billing dimension must not be `INVOICED`, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_INVOICED` — which is refused even though the loading dimension would still allow it. On success the loading dimension moves to `CANCELLED` from whichever of the three states it held, the billing dimension moves to `VOIDED` from either `PENDING_BOL` or `BOL_ISSUED`, and `cancelled_at` is recorded. The supplied `cancellation_reason` is required and is kept on the record, but it is deliberately **not** part of the shipment representation and therefore never appears in any response — unlike a dumped batch's `dump_reason`, which is echoed. Four reversals then fire. Where the shipment had reached `LOADED`, its `loaded_gallons` are credited back to the source tank, `loaded_gallons` is set back to null and the tank's `version` increments — which restores the tank to precisely its pre-load balance, the plant-wide denatured-gallons invariant this compensation exists to protect. The `railcar_count` drops out of the track's `committed_railcars` and the track's `version` increments. Any credit separation this shipment performed is reverted, as described in §3.8. And where the shipment had never reached `BOL_ISSUED`, `bol_number` simply stays null and there is no separation to revert. Only `loaded_gallons` is unwound: `loaded_at` is deliberately left standing, as are `bol_number` and `bol_issued_at` where a bill of lading had in fact been issued, so cancellation voids the billing dimension without erasing the paperwork trail.

Shipments are discoverable through a filtered list accepting `track_id`, `source_tank_id`, `loading_status` and `billing_status`, ordered by `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending, which is the reverse path from both a track and a source tank to the trains that reference them. Both identifier filters are existence-checked before any row is read, and `source_tank_id` is resolved first, so a request naming an unknown tank and an unknown track alike reports `ERR_TANK_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.7. Co-Product Marketing: Quoting, the Collar, Rebates and Shipment

Distillers grains and corn oil are sold forward against a quote. A `GrainBuyer` opens one with a globally unique `sale_code`, the `buyer_name`, the `product` as one of `DDGS_DRY`, `DDGS_WET` or `CORN_OIL`, the `pricing_basis` as either `FLAT` or `INDEX`, the `quantity` (positive, three decimals — short tons for either grade of distillers grains, pounds for corn oil), and the `quote_expires_at` instant after which the buyer may no longer accept. No ordering constraint ties `quote_expires_at` to the logical clock, so a quote may legitimately be opened already expired; the bound bites only when the buyer responds. Three optional inputs complete the quote: `quoted_price` (positive, two decimals), `index_pct` and `freight_rate_per_unit`. A new sale opens at `status` `QUOTED`, which advances to `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `SHIPPED`, with `DECLINED` and `SHIPPED` terminal.

The two pricing bases treat those optional inputs as **mirror images**, and getting either backwards is a validation failure. A `FLAT` sale requires `quoted_price` and requires `index_pct` to be null. An `INDEX` sale requires `index_pct` — a multiplier between 0.10 and 2.00 inclusive carried to four decimals — and **ignores any `quoted_price` the caller supplies**, because for an index sale the quoted price is struck by the system at quote time from the plant's published marks. `freight_rate_per_unit` is optional on both bases, defaults to 0, must not be negative, is carried to four decimals and is expressed in US dollars per unit of `quantity`. Both mirror-image checks are cross-field checks evaluated only once **every declared field has individually passed** its own type, range and precision gate, so a `FLAT` sale supplying an `index_pct` of `5.0000` reports the out-of-range failure rather than the must-be-null one; and on the `FLAT` side the `index_pct`-must-be-null check is evaluated **before** the `quoted_price`-is-required check, so a `FLAT` body carrying an `index_pct` and no `quoted_price` reports the stray `index_pct`.

Which mark an index sale reads depends on the product: both grades of distillers grains price off `mark_soybean_meal_usd_per_ton`, while corn oil prices off `mark_sbo_cents_per_lb`. At quote time the system multiplies that reference mark by `index_pct`, rounds half up to two decimals and stores the result as `quoted_price`, while also stamping the mark it used into `quote_reference_mark` so the later restrike can be audited. `quote_reference_mark` is a verbatim copy of the posted mark and inherits its unrescaled storage of §3.2, so it is echoed at exactly the width the mark was posted at — a soybean meal mark posted as `330` stamps `330` and one posted as `330.00` stamps `330.00` — while the `quoted_price` it produces is always at two decimals. If the required mark has never been posted the quote cannot be struck and the sale is refused with `ERR_MARKS_NOT_POSTED`. A `FLAT` sale leaves `quote_reference_mark` null.

Responding to a quote carries a single `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and the sale must still be at `QUOTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SALE_STATE`. The logical clock must be at or before `quote_expires_at` — the bound is **inclusive**, so a response landing on the expiry instant to the second is still in time, while one instant later is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. `responded_at` is stamped with the clock on **both** decisions, and it is stamped before either branch runs. A `DECLINE` moves the sale to `DECLINED`, records `responded_at` and leaves every money field null forever; nothing is ever computed for a declined quote.

An `ACCEPT` is where the **collar** is applied, and the arithmetic differs by basis. For a `FLAT` sale the `settlement_price` is simply the stored `quoted_price` and both `collar_floor` and `collar_ceiling` remain null. For an `INDEX` sale the system restrikes the price against the mark **as it stands at acceptance**: multiply the current reference mark by `index_pct` and round half up to two decimals. That restruck figure is then clamped into a symmetric band of seven and a half percent computed **from the original `quoted_price`, never from the restruck price** — `collar_floor` is `quoted_price` multiplied by 0.925 rounded half up to two decimals, and `collar_ceiling` is `quoted_price` multiplied by 1.075 rounded half up to two decimals. If the restruck price falls below the floor the settlement price becomes the floor; if it rises above the ceiling the settlement price becomes the ceiling; otherwise the settlement price is the restruck price itself. Acceptance of an index sale requires that the mark still be readable and that `quote_reference_mark` is not null, otherwise `ERR_MARKS_NOT_POSTED`. Because the collar is anchored to the quote, a mark that has doubled since quoting still settles at the ceiling: a quote struck at 340.00 collars between 314.50 and 365.50 regardless of how far the mark has run.

Acceptance also produces the invoice, and the **order of its four steps is fixed**. First the subtotal: the unit divisor is 1 for both grades of distillers grains, whose settlement price is already US dollars per short ton, and 100 for corn oil, whose settlement price is cents per pound and must be converted to dollars. `subtotal_usd` is the `quantity` multiplied by the `settlement_price`, divided by that unit divisor, rounded half up to two decimals.

Second the volume rebate, and here the pinning matters: **the rebate tier is selected on `subtotal_usd` before the rebate itself and before freight are deducted**, never on the net figure. The rate is 0.0150 when `subtotal_usd` is at least 50000.00; failing that, 0.0075 when it is at least 20000.00; otherwise 0.0000. Both thresholds are inclusive lower bounds, so a subtotal of exactly 50000.00 earns the higher tier and one of exactly 20000.00 earns the middle tier. `volume_rebate_usd` is `subtotal_usd` multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up to two decimals. A subtotal of 50000.00 therefore yields a 750.00 rebate; had the tier been chosen after deducting freight it would have dropped a tier, which is exactly the mistake this ordering forbids.

Third the freight: multiply `quantity` by `freight_rate_per_unit`, round half up to two decimals, and take the **lesser** of that figure and a cap of 2500.00. The cap is inclusive, meaning freight computing to exactly 2500.00 is charged in full at 2500.00 and only amounts above it are trimmed. The result is stored as `freight_usd`.

Fourth the settlement: subtract both `volume_rebate_usd` and `freight_usd` from `subtotal_usd`, round half up to two decimals to obtain the net invoice in dollars, then multiply by 100 and round half up to a whole number to store `invoice_amount_cents`.

Shipping an accepted sale is what actually relieves the plant's co-product inventory, and it is a `LogisticsCoordinator`'s action rather than the buyer's. It takes **no request body** and rejects any field at all as an unknown field. The sale must be `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SALE_STATE`. For either grade of distillers grains the plant's `ddgs_inventory_tons` must be at least the `quantity`; for corn oil the plant's `corn_oil_inventory_lbs` must be at least the `quantity`. A short cupboard is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COPRODUCT_INVENTORY`. On success the matching inventory pool is reduced by the `quantity`, `shipped_at` is recorded and the sale becomes `SHIPPED`, which is also the point at which its revenue becomes visible to the realized crush margin. Sales are discoverable through a filtered list accepting `product`, `status` and `pricing_basis`.

### 3.8. Renewable Credits and the Carbon-Intensity Waterfall

Every gallon of ethanol the plant completes mints renewable credits, and those credits then travel a lifecycle of their own that is stapled to the rail shipments of §3.6. A credit batch is **never created by a caller**. When a fermentation batch completes, the system mints exactly one `RinBatch` row recording the `fermentation_batch_id` it came from, a `d_code` that is always 6 because corn starch ethanol is a D6 renewable fuel, and a `rin_year` taken as the UTC calendar year of the generating batch's `completed_at`. The volume is grossed up for denaturant: `rin_eligible_gallons` is the batch's `ethanol_gallons_produced` multiplied by a denaturant gross-up of 1.02 and by a D6 equivalence value of 1.0, rounded half up to two decimals. The tradeable count, `rin_count`, is then the **floor** of that eligible-gallons figure and never a rounding, because a fraction of a credit does not exist — 306000.60 eligible gallons mint 306000 credits and the remainder is discarded. A dumped batch mints nothing at all.

A credit batch opens at `status` `GENERATED`, advances to `SEPARATED` when its gallons leave the plant and finally to `RETIRED`, which is terminal. Because credit batches are never returned by any creation response, the **only** way a caller discovers them is the filtered list, which accepts `fermentation_batch_id`, `separated_shipment_id`, `status` and `rin_year`. `rin_year` is matched as a whole number and must be supplied as a plain base-ten integer literal on the same terms as `limit` and `offset` in §2.3, so `2026` filters and `2026.0` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Separation happens automatically the moment a bill of lading is issued, and its allocation is deliberately **greedy with an early stop**. The allowance is the floor of the shipment's `loaded_gallons`. Candidates are every credit batch still at `GENERATED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending and, where those tie, by the generating batch's `batch_code` ascending. The system walks that ordered list accumulating a running total, and separates a candidate only while the running total plus that candidate's `rin_count` remains at or below the allowance. **At the first candidate that does not fit, the walk stops.** It does not skip that candidate and keep scanning for a smaller one that would fit — a credit batch is never split, and no later batch is examined once an earlier one has been passed over. With an allowance of 290000 and candidates of 150000, 145000 and 100000 credits in order, only the first separates: the second overshoots at 295000 and halts the walk, leaving the third untouched even though it would have fitted comfortably. Each separated batch records `separated_shipment_id` pointing at the shipment and `separated_at`, and moves to `SEPARATED`.

Cancelling a shipment that had issued a bill of lading reverses that separation. Every credit batch whose `separated_shipment_id` is the cancelled shipment and whose status is still `SEPARATED` returns to `GENERATED` with both `separated_shipment_id` and `separated_at` cleared, which lowers the plant's `rin_separated_count` while leaving `rin_generated_count` untouched. Batches that had already reached `RETIRED` are **not** reverted — a retired credit is surrendered to the regulator and cannot be recalled by a commercial cancellation.

Retirement is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s act. The batch must be at `SEPARATED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RIN_STATE`, and the shipment that separated it must have reached `billing_status` `INVOICED`, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_INVOICED` — credits may only be surrendered against fuel that has actually been billed. The officer supplies a `retirement_reference` which is stored alongside `retired_at`.

The plant's three credit counters are always re-derived rather than incremented, each over a widening set of statuses: `rin_generated_count` sums `rin_count` across `GENERATED`, `SEPARATED` and `RETIRED` batches; `rin_separated_count` sums across `SEPARATED` and `RETIRED`; and `rin_retired_count` sums across `RETIRED` alone. A credit therefore stays counted as generated for its whole life, and separation and retirement only widen the narrower tallies.

Carbon-intensity reduction is tracked separately, through projects that must be verified by an outside body before they may lower the plant's score. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts one with a globally unique `project_code`, a `project_name`, a `project_type` of `CARBON_CAPTURE`, `COMBINED_HEAT_POWER`, `CORN_OIL_EXTRACTION`, `LOW_TILL_FEEDSTOCK` or `PROCESS_HEAT_ELECTRIFICATION`, the `claimed_reduction_g_per_mj` the project is expected to deliver (positive, one decimal), and the `certification_tier` sought, either `FULL` or `PROVISIONAL`. Two optional inputs follow: `capital_cost_cents`, a non-negative whole number of cents that is recorded for reporting and gates nothing, and `predecessor_project_id`, naming an earlier project this one builds upon — which at draft time is merely existence-checked and need not yet be certified. A project opens at `verification_status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, its stated `certification_tier` recorded, and `verification_body`, `verified_reduction_g_per_mj`, `credited_reduction_g_per_mj`, `review_note`, `submitted_at`, `reviewed_at` and `certified_at` all null. Projects are discoverable through a filtered list whose three parameters are named `predecessor_project_id`, `verification_status` and `certification_tier` — the status filter carries the full field name and not a bare `status` — and the first of them is existence-checked before any row is read.

Submitting for verification moves the project `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED` on the resubmission path, and carries the `verification_body` performing the audit, which is recorded alongside `submitted_at`. Any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. A resubmission overwrites `verification_body` and `submitted_at` but clears nothing: the previous `review_note`, `reviewed_at` and `revision_count` all stay standing on the row, so the audit trail of the return survives the resubmit. Where a `predecessor_project_id` is set, that predecessor must itself have reached `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` — a stacked claim cannot be audited before the foundation it rests on has been.

Review is a `PlantManager`'s act, enforcing the second segregation of duties of §2.1: the officer who submits may not be the one who certifies. That segregation is discharged **entirely by the role split** and by nothing else — because submission is `ComplianceOfficer`-only and review is `PlantManager`-only, the two halves can never share a role, and the review endpoint therefore performs **no comparison at all** between the caller's `X-User-ID` and the identity that submitted. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` consequently never arises on a carbon project; the same `X-User-ID` may submit under one role and review under the other, and the identity recorded at submission is not part of the project representation. The manager supplies a `determination` of either `RETURN` or `CERTIFY`, and optionally a `verified_reduction_g_per_mj` and a `review_note`. The project must be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`.

A `RETURN` moves the project `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, increments `revision_count`, and records the `review_note` and `reviewed_at`. Revisions are capped at two: a return is only permitted while `revision_count` is **strictly below** 2, so the first and second returns are allowed and a third is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Resubmission is likewise only permitted while `revision_count` is at most 2.

A `CERTIFY` moves the project `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, which is terminal. The effective verified reduction is the `verified_reduction_g_per_mj` from the body when the manager supplied one, and otherwise falls back to the project's stored `claimed_reduction_g_per_mj`; either way the figure must be greater than zero. It is recorded as `verified_reduction_g_per_mj`, alongside the `certification_tier`, the `review_note`, `reviewed_at` and `certified_at`.

Certification then re-runs the whole reduction **waterfall** across every certified project, because the plant's total reduction is capped and a newly certified project can push earlier ones against that cap. Order all `CERTIFIED` projects by `certified_at` ascending and, where those tie, by `created_at` ascending, and begin with a remaining allowance of 12.0 grams of carbon dioxide equivalent per megajoule. For each project in that order, take a tier factor of 1.00 for `FULL` and 0.70 for `PROVISIONAL`, and a stacking factor of 0.50 where `predecessor_project_id` is set and 1.00 where it is null. The raw reduction is the project's `verified_reduction_g_per_mj` multiplied by both factors and rounded half up to three decimals. The project's `credited_reduction_g_per_mj` is the **lesser** of that raw figure and the remaining allowance, and the allowance is then reduced by the credited amount, rounded half up to three decimals. Only verified reductions ever enter the waterfall; a claimed reduction that was never verified contributes nothing. The project straddling the cap is truncated to whatever allowance is left, and **every project after it is credited exactly 0.000** — order, not merit, decides who is cut. Because the entire waterfall is recomputed on each certification, `credited_reduction_g_per_mj` is rewritten on every certified project, not just the newest, and each such row takes a fresh `version` and `updated_at`. The newly certified project is the one exception to that bump, and only because it has already been bumped by its own transition: its `credited_reduction_g_per_mj` is written by the same waterfall run, but the run does not increment it a second time, so a project's first certification advances its `version` by exactly 1 and not by 2. The waterfall order also breaks a `certified_at`/`created_at` tie by the insertion sequence of §2.3.

Suppose a `FULL` project with no predecessor is verified at 8.0: it is credited 8.000 and leaves 4.000 of allowance. A `PROVISIONAL` project verified at 6.0 raws to 4.200, is truncated to the remaining 4.000, and exhausts the allowance. A third certified project, however large, is credited 0.000. Total reduction is the sum of the credited figures rounded half up to three decimals — here 12.000.

Two plant figures follow. `ci_score_g_per_mj` is the plant's `baseline_ci_g_per_mj` less the total credited reduction, rounded half up to three decimals, and then floored at zero so the score is never negative. `lcfs_credit_tonnes_per_million_gal` takes the shortfall of the score beneath a benchmark of 79.75 grams per megajoule — that shortfall floored at zero — multiplies it by an energy density of 81.51 megajoules per gallon and by one million gallons, converts grams to tonnes by dividing by one million, and rounds half up to three decimals. With a baseline of 29.100 and the 12.000 total reduction above, the score is 17.100 and the credit figure is 5106.602. Both are re-derived on every certification. That derivation is the **only** source of the credit figure: while no project has been certified, `lcfs_credit_tonnes_per_million_gal` holds the opening 0.000 of §3.1 rather than the figure the formula would produce from an untouched baseline score, so a plant registered at the default baseline of 60.0 reports 0.000 and not 1609.823. The score, by contrast, is always the baseline less the total credited reduction, which is simply the baseline itself while that total is 0.000.

### 3.9. Permit Compliance, the Futures Book and Margin Reporting

The plant's environmental permits are expressed as conditions, each a limit on one measured parameter averaged over a rolling window. A `ComplianceOfficer` registers one with a globally unique `condition_code`, a `permit_type` of either `AIR_EMISSIONS` or `WATER_DISCHARGE`, the `parameter_name` being limited such as VOC or TSS, the `limit_value` itself (positive, three decimals), the `unit` in which it is expressed such as lb/hr or mg/L, the `averaging_period_hours` over which compliance is judged (a positive whole number of hours), and the `severity` classification of a breach as `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`. A condition opens at `status` `COMPLIANT` with `readings_in_window` at 0, `rolling_average_value` null, `exceedance_count` at 0 and both `last_exceedance_at` and `resolution_note` null. Unlike every other entity in this system, a permit condition has **no terminal state** — it may cycle between `COMPLIANT` and `EXCEEDANCE` for the life of the plant. Conditions are discoverable through a filtered list accepting `permit_type`, `status` and `severity`, and that list is ordered by `condition_code` ascending rather than by creation time, so the permit register reads in code order.

Readings are appended to a condition by either a `ProcessOperator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` — the control room logs the routine monitoring data and the officer files the laboratory results — each carrying the `value` observed (not negative, three decimals), the `measured_at` instant the sample was taken and an optional `sample_reference` naming the laboratory record, defaulting to null. Those three are also the order in which the endpoint's contract validates them, so `value` is examined before `measured_at`. A reading is an immutable observation rather than a managed resource: it carries only its own `id` and `created_at`, has no version and is never updated or deleted. The condition's own `condition_id` is taken from the path. Readings are listed under their condition ordered by `measured_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

Appending a reading recomputes the rolling average, and the window's boundary arithmetic is the **third** distinct convention in this system. Take the logical clock as the window's end and subtract `averaging_period_hours` to obtain its start. The window is then **start-exclusive and end-inclusive**: a reading whose `measured_at` falls strictly after the start and at or before the clock is inside; a reading whose `measured_at` equals the start instant exactly is **outside**. `readings_in_window` is the count of readings inside, and `rolling_average_value` is the sum of their values divided by that count, rounded half up to three decimals — or null when the window is empty, which is not the same as zero. Because readings are never deleted, the same row can drift out of the window purely because the clock advanced, so both figures may change on a condition that received no new data.

With a `limit_value` of 12.500 and an `averaging_period_hours` of 24, a clock at 2026-09-05T00:00:00Z gives a window running from just after 2026-09-04T00:00:00Z through 2026-09-05T00:00:00Z. Readings of 13.000 and 12.400 inside it average 12.700 and breach the limit. A third reading of 2.000 measured exactly at 2026-09-04T00:00:00Z sits on the excluded start boundary and is left out; wrongly admitting it would give 9.133 and mask the breach entirely.

A breach is judged **strictly**: the condition enters `EXCEEDANCE` only when the rolling average is not null and is strictly greater than `limit_value`, so an average landing exactly on the limit is compliant. Crossing into `EXCEEDANCE` from `COMPLIANT` increments `exceedance_count`, records `last_exceedance_at`, and re-derives both the plant's `compliance_posture` and its grind cap by the worst-of rule of §2.8, which is how a `CRITICAL` water-discharge breach halts corn grinding entirely. A reading that leaves the average at or under the limit while the condition is already `COMPLIANT` simply keeps it `COMPLIANT`. Either way the condition row itself is **always rewritten** by an append: every appended reading refreshes the condition's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1, whether or not the append changed the status or the exceedance tally, so a stream of unremarkable clean readings still walks the condition's `version` up one per reading.

Once in `EXCEEDANCE`, a condition **cannot be cleared by clean data alone**. Further readings recompute the average and leave the status at `EXCEEDANCE` — and crucially they do **not** increment `exceedance_count` a second time, since the count records episodes rather than samples. Returning to `COMPLIANT` requires an explicit deviation resolution by the `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `resolution_note`. The condition must actually be in `EXCEEDANCE`, otherwise `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_IN_EXCEEDANCE`, and the current `rolling_average_value` must then be not null and at or below `limit_value`, so an officer cannot sign off a deviation while the plant is still over the line — and that second failure carries the **same** `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_IN_EXCEEDANCE` identifier as the first, including the case where the window has emptied and the average has gone null. On success the status returns to `COMPLIANT` and the note is stored, while `exceedance_count` and `last_exceedance_at` are deliberately left standing as the historical record of the episode, and the plant's posture and grind cap are re-derived — which is the only path by which a suspended plant resumes grinding.

The futures book runs alongside all of this. A `RiskManager` opens a position with a globally unique `position_code`, the `commodity` hedged as `CORN`, `ETHANOL` or `NATGAS`, the `direction` as `LONG` or `SHORT`, the `contract_month` as a calendar month in year-and-month form such as 2026-12, the number of `contracts` (a positive whole number) and the `entry_price` at which they were struck (positive, two decimals — cents per bushel for corn, cents per gallon for ethanol, cents per million British thermal units for natural gas). The system stamps the contract `unit_size` from the commodity — 5000 bushels for corn, 29000 gallons for ethanol, 10000 million British thermal units for natural gas — and opens the position at `status` `OPEN` with `contracts_closed` at 0, `contracts_open` equal to `contracts`, both `realized_pnl_cents` and `unrealized_pnl_cents` at 0, and `opened_at` recorded. Positions are discoverable through a filtered list accepting `commodity`, `direction` and `status`.

Closing takes the number of `contracts_to_close` and the `exit_price` achieved. The position must be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_POSITION_STATE`, and `contracts_to_close` must be at least 1 and at most `contracts_open`, otherwise `ERR_CLOSE_EXCEEDS_OPEN_CONTRACTS`. Closing strictly fewer contracts than are open moves the position to `PARTIALLY_CLOSED` — from `OPEN` on the first partial close and from `PARTIALLY_CLOSED` to itself on each subsequent one. Closing exactly the number open moves it to `CLOSED`, which is terminal, sets `contracts_open` to 0, records `closed_at` and forces `unrealized_pnl_cents` to 0.

Profit and loss turns on a direction sign of positive one for a long position and negative one for a short. The realized slice from a close is that sign multiplied by the difference of `exit_price` less `entry_price`, multiplied by the `unit_size` and by the `contracts_to_close`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and it **accumulates into** `realized_pnl_cents` rather than replacing it, so a position closed in three slices carries the sum of all three. `unrealized_pnl_cents` is instead re-derived from scratch each time it is re-derived rather than accumulated: the re-derived value is 0 when `contracts_open` is 0 or when the commodity's current mark has never been posted, and otherwise the same sign multiplied by the difference of the current mark less `entry_price`, multiplied by the `unit_size` and by `contracts_open`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The mark is read from the plant at the moment of that re-derivation — `mark_corn_cents_per_bushel` for corn, `mark_ethanol_cents_per_gallon` for ethanol, `mark_natgas_cents_per_mmbtu` for natural gas. It is a **stored** figure re-derived on exactly two triggers: posting new marks re-prices every `OPEN` and `PARTIALLY_CLOSED` position and leaves `CLOSED` ones alone, and closing contracts re-prices the position closed — against the marks standing at that instant and against the contract count left open after the close, which is how a partial close re-prices the remainder and a full close falls to 0. Between those triggers the stored figure simply stands, and it is reported as stored; in particular a position opened while marks are already posted still carries the 0 it was opened with until the next mark posting or close touches it. Both figures may be negative; a hedge is meant to lose money when the physical business gains.

Twenty long corn contracts entered at 450.00 against a mark of 440.00 show an unrealized loss of 1000000 cents with realized still at 0. Ten short ethanol contracts entered at 182.00 against a mark of 178.00 show an unrealized gain of 1160000 cents; closing all ten at 176.50 realizes 1595000 cents and drops the unrealized figure to 0. Both unrealized figures are the ones a re-derivation trigger left behind — a mark posting that followed the opening of the position, or a close — and not a figure computed afresh at read time.

The crush margin is the plant's headline profitability figure and is computed on demand rather than stored, readable by a `PlantManager`, a `RiskManager` or a `GrainBuyer`. It is reported as **two variants side by side in one response**, named `MARK_TO_MARKET` for the forward view and `REALIZED` for the backward view over closed transactions, and every leg is expressed in cents per bushel of corn to two decimals, rounded half up. Two cost legs are shared by both variants: the natural-gas cost is `mark_natgas_cents_per_mmbtu` multiplied by 0.0800 million British thermal units per bushel, rounded half up to two decimals; and the conversion cost is a fixed 32.50 cents per bushel.

The `MARK_TO_MARKET` variant values everything at posted marks and is always returned, being the only view available before any batch has completed or any shipment has been invoiced. Its ethanol leg is `mark_ethanol_cents_per_gallon` multiplied by the plant's `rolling_yield_gal_per_bushel`. Its distillers-grains leg takes `mark_soybean_meal_usd_per_ton`, applies an index of 0.92, scales by 17.0 pounds of distillers grains per bushel, divides by 2000 pounds per short ton and multiplies by 100 to reach cents. Its corn-oil leg takes `mark_sbo_cents_per_lb`, applies an index of 0.80 and scales by 0.600 pounds of corn oil per bushel. Its corn cost is `mark_corn_cents_per_bushel` exactly as posted — the futures mark, ignoring what the plant actually paid. Its gross figure is the sum of the three revenue legs, and its net figure is that gross less the corn cost, the natural-gas cost and the conversion cost.

The `REALIZED` variant reads only settled and invoiced history. The bushels actually ground are the sum of `corn_bushels_charged` over `COMPLETED` fermentation batches. Its ethanol leg is the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over shipments whose `billing_status` is `INVOICED`, divided by those bushels. Its co-product leg is the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over sales whose `status` is `SHIPPED`, divided by the same bushels. Its corn cost is the **volume-weighted** actual purchase price, being the sum over `SETTLED` deliveries of `net_dry_bushels` multiplied by `net_price_cents_per_bushel`, divided by the sum of `net_dry_bushels` over those same deliveries — which carries the quality discounts of §3.3 that the futures marks know nothing about. Its net figure is the ethanol leg plus the co-product leg, less the corn cost, the natural-gas cost and the conversion cost. A voided shipment, a merely accepted sale and a graded-but-unsettled delivery are all invisible to this variant. Where the bushels ground are 0, every `REALIZED` leg is reported as 0.00 — the ethanol leg, the co-product leg, the corn cost and the net figure alike, so the two shared cost legs are **not** subtracted into that zero net. Where bushels have been ground but no delivery has yet settled, the corn cost leg alone falls to 0.00 while the ethanol and co-product legs are computed normally and the net figure is those two less a zero corn cost and the two shared costs. A missing mark never arises in this variant, because the read as a whole is refused before any leg is computed.

Reading the margin requires that the marks be readable: all five of `mark_corn_cents_per_bushel`, `mark_ethanol_cents_per_gallon`, `mark_natgas_cents_per_mmbtu`, `mark_soybean_meal_usd_per_ton` and `mark_sbo_cents_per_lb` must have been posted, otherwise the read is refused with `ERR_MARKS_NOT_POSTED`.

The response is a plain computed view rather than a stored resource: it carries no `id`, no `created_at` and no `version`, and the two variant names appear only as the two object keys below and never as a `variant` field of their own. It names the plant's `rolling_yield` to three decimals, the two shared cost legs as `natgas_cost_cents_per_bushel` and `conversion_cost_cents_per_bushel`, a `mark_to_market` object carrying `ethanol_cents_per_bushel`, `ddgs_cents_per_bushel`, `corn_oil_cents_per_bushel`, `corn_cost_cents_per_bushel`, `gross_cents_per_bushel` and `net_cents_per_bushel`, a `realized` object carrying `ethanol_cents_per_bushel`, `coproduct_cents_per_bushel`, `corn_cost_cents_per_bushel` and `net_cents_per_bushel`, and finally `variant_delta_cents_per_bushel`, which is the realized net figure less the mark-to-market net figure, rounded half up to two decimals — a positive delta meaning the plant has done better than the marks suggest.

With marks of 440.00 cents per bushel for corn, 178.00 cents per gallon for ethanol, 265.75 cents per million British thermal units for natural gas, 330.00 dollars per short ton for soybean meal and 46.80 cents per pound for soybean oil, against a rolling yield of 2.888, the mark-to-market legs come to 514.06 for ethanol, 258.06 for distillers grains and 22.46 for corn oil, grossing 794.58; less 440.00 of corn, 21.26 of natural gas and 32.50 of conversion, the net is 300.82. Over the same plant at the same instant, with 90000.00 bushels ground, one invoiced shipment of 48260850 cents, one shipped sale of 23866834 cents and settled deliveries averaging 411.20 cents per bushel, the realized legs come to 536.23 for ethanol and 265.19 for co-products; less 411.20 of corn and the same 21.26 and 32.50 of costs, the net is 336.46. The two views sit 35.64 cents per bushel apart on identical data, which is the whole point of reporting them together.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is mounted beneath the `/ethanol-ops` prefix. Status codes follow one rule: an operation that brings a **new** resource into existence answers 201 Created, and every other successful operation — reads, lists, and transitions that modify a resource already on file — answers 200 OK. That distinction is about creation and not about the HTTP verb, so posting a roll of a corn contract or a reading to a permit condition answers 201 because a fresh row appears, while posting a price, a grade, a settlement, a hold, an execution, a load, a bill of lading, an invoice, a cancellation, a response, a shipment, a retirement, a submission, a review, a close or a deviation resolution answers 200 because it advances a row that already existed. Unless stated otherwise every successful create or modify returns the affected resource in the `data` field of the envelope described in §2.2. Authorization is enforced exactly as in §2.1, and where several roles are listed any one of them suffices.
