# Federal Environmental Review API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A federal agency's environmental review office lives or dies by the defensibility of its paper trail. Every proposed federal action must be routed onto the correct review pathway, screened for extraordinary circumstances, scoped with the public and with sister agencies, analysed resource area by resource area, published for comment, answered comment by comment, and finally decided with mitigation commitments that the agency is legally bound to fund and monitor. When any one of those steps is skipped, mis-sequenced, or documented after the fact, the resulting decision is vulnerable on administrative objection and, later, in litigation. Today the office tracks these steps across spreadsheets, shared drives and e-mail threads, and reconstructing "what was known when" for a litigation record takes weeks.

This document specifies the API for a system that replaces those artefacts with a single append-only administrative record. The system is deliberately opinionated: it refuses to let a document be published while a significant scoping issue has no finalised analysis behind it, it refuses to let the same official both prepare and sign a decision, and it refuses to let the office obligate more mitigation money than its ceiling allows. Where the real-world process is genuinely cyclic — a screen returned for rework and resubmitted, an analysis reopened, a decision vacated on remand — the system models the cycle explicitly and guarantees that the compensating path restores every counter and every ledger to its exact prior value.

Two audiences shape the design. Review staff need the system to compute the arithmetic they currently do by hand: significance scores under both an unmitigated and a mitigated reading of the same analysis, cumulative effects aggregated over a defined radius, and the lifecycle cost of a mitigation package including escalated monitoring. Reviewing and deciding officials need the system to enforce the separation of duties and the statutory clocks that make their signatures meaningful. Everything in this specification exists to serve one of those two needs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across the whole API, independent of any particular workflow. All resource paths are mounted at the server root; there is no additional URL prefix.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — read or write — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or blank, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the target operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `PolicyAdmin`, `NepaCoordinator`, `ResourceSpecialist`, `ReviewingOfficial`, `AgencyLiaison`, `DecidingOfficial`, `ObjectionReviewingOfficer` and `PublicCommenter`. A `PolicyAdmin` configures review offices and agencies and drives the server's logical clock, but performs no case work. A `NepaCoordinator` prepares the review: it opens actions, submits and resubmits screens, classifies, drafts purpose and need statements, opens scoping periods and records issues, creates alternatives, opens consultations, creates and publishes documents, prepares responses to comments, and drafts decisions with their mitigation commitments. A `ResourceSpecialist` owns the analytical work — the cumulative baseline and the resource-effects analyses, including finalising and reopening them — and owns no document, decision or objection operation. A `ReviewingOfficial` is the independent reviewer: it adjudicates extraordinary-circumstance screens, adopts purpose and need statements, screens alternatives in or out, and certifies the litigation record; it never authors the artefacts it reviews. A `DecidingOfficial` signs decision documents. An `ObjectionReviewingOfficer` resolves administrative objections. A `PublicCommenter` is a member of the public who submits comments during an open comment period and may file an objection afterwards. An `AgencyLiaison` acts for a single cooperating or consulting agency.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` in two different ways, and the difference matters for every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's `agency_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when an `AgencyLiaison` responds to a consultation, the consultation's `agency_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is any of `PolicyAdmin`, `NepaCoordinator`, `ResourceSpecialist`, `ReviewingOfficial`, `DecidingOfficial`, `ObjectionReviewingOfficer` or `PublicCommenter`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `prepared_by_user_id` on a decision draft, as `signed_by_user_id` at signature, and as `actor_user_id` on an administrative record entry — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty randomly chosen uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is stamped from real wall-clock time, not from the logical clock of §2.7.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filters

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or that falls outside those bounds, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both values are echoed back in the `meta` object of the envelope alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the request before pagination, and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The administrative record listing is the single exception: its rows are returned in `sequence_no` ascending order.

Most list endpoints also accept optional equality filters, and every filter is named after the field it matches. Review offices accept no filter. Agencies may be filtered by `status`. Proposed actions may be filtered by `office_id`, `classification` and `review_stage`. Extraordinary-circumstance screens may be filtered by `status`; purpose and need statements by `status`; scoping periods by `scoping_kind` and `status`; issues by `resource_area`, `status`, `action_id` and `scoping_period_id`; alternatives by `action_id`, `pinned_pn_statement_id` and `screening_status`; cumulative baseline entries by `resource_area` and `inclusion_status`; resource analyses by `resource_area` and `status`; consultations by `action_id`, `agency_id`, `status` and `consultation_type`; documents by `document_type`, `publication_status` and `comment_status`; comments by `document_id`, `assigned_issue_id` and `disposition`; decisions by `action_id`, `selected_alternative_id`, `status` and `decision_type`; mitigation commitments by `decision_id`, `source_analysis_id`, `monitoring_status` and `status`; objections by `status`; and administrative record entries by `entry_code`. A query parameter that is neither `limit`, `offset` nor a filter named above is ignored rather than rejected, and every filter matches on the string form of the stored value.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Administrative Record

Every resource the API creates carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Nearly every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation. A mutation includes the recomputation of a derived field: when finalising an analysis refreshes an action's rollup counters, that is a mutation of the action, and the action's `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are stamped from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock, and a single request advances a given row's `version` by exactly one however many of that row's fields it touches — repeated writes to the same row within one request do not compound.

The administrative record entry is the one exception. Record entries are immutable by construction, so an entry carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Record entries are the litigation artefact, and they are append-only. Each entry names its `action_id`, carries a `sequence_no` that is 1-based, gap-free and unique within that action, an `entry_code` naming the recorded event, an `occurred_at` holding the logical-clock instant at which the event happened, an `actor_role` naming the role that performed it, an `actor_user_id` holding that principal's `X-User-ID`, a one-line human-readable `summary`, and a polymorphic pair — `related_entity_type` naming which kind of row the entry describes and `related_entity_ref` holding that row's identifier. Because the reference is polymorphic, no single-parent foreign key applies to it. The permitted values of `related_entity_type` are `ExtraordinaryCircumstanceScreen`, `PurposeNeedStatement`, `ScopingPeriod`, `ResourceAnalysis`, `NepaDocument`, `DecisionDocument`, `Objection` and `ProposedAction`. The permitted values of `actor_role` are the eight role names plus `SYSTEM`, which is used for entries generated by a cascade rather than by a direct request; `actor_user_id` is null on a `SYSTEM` entry. Exactly three entry codes are cascade-generated and therefore always carry `actor_role` `SYSTEM`: `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED`, `DOCUMENT_SUPERSEDED` and `DECISION_VACATED`; every other entry code carries the requesting role and that principal's `X-User-ID`. Each entry code also pairs with a fixed `related_entity_type` and reference: `EC_SCREEN_ADJUDICATED` with the `ExtraordinaryCircumstanceScreen` adjudicated; `PN_ADOPTED` with the `PurposeNeedStatement` adopted; `SCOPING_CLOSED` with the `ScopingPeriod` whose close completed the set; `ANALYSIS_FINALIZED` and `ANALYSIS_REOPENED` with the `ResourceAnalysis`; `DOCUMENT_PUBLISHED`, `DOCUMENT_SUPERSEDED`, `COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED` and `DOCUMENT_FINALIZED` with the `NepaDocument`; `DECISION_SIGNED` and `DECISION_VACATED` with the `DecisionDocument`; `OBJECTION_FILED` and `OBJECTION_RESOLVED` with the `Objection`; and `CLASSIFICATION_ASSIGNED`, `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED` and `RECORD_CERTIFIED` with the `ProposedAction` itself. The permitted values of `entry_code` are `EC_SCREEN_ADJUDICATED`, `CLASSIFICATION_ASSIGNED`, `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED`, `PN_ADOPTED`, `SCOPING_CLOSED`, `ANALYSIS_FINALIZED`, `ANALYSIS_REOPENED`, `DOCUMENT_PUBLISHED`, `DOCUMENT_SUPERSEDED`, `COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `DOCUMENT_FINALIZED`, `DECISION_SIGNED`, `DECISION_VACATED`, `OBJECTION_FILED`, `OBJECTION_RESOLVED` and `RECORD_CERTIFIED`.

A new entry takes as its `sequence_no` one more than the highest `sequence_no` already present among that action's entries, or 1 if the action has none. When a single request appends more than one entry, the entries are appended in the cascade order documented for that operation — the first-order effect before the effect it causes, and that before the effect the second causes — and `sequence_no` increases by one per entry in that order. Where two entries share an `occurred_at`, they are ordered by `sequence_no` ascending; an entity's `id` never participates in ordering. The action's `record_entry_count` is always the number of entries bearing that `action_id`, and it is monotonically non-decreasing: no operation, including a compensating one such as reopening an analysis or vacating a decision, ever deletes or renumbers an entry.

### 2.5. Rejection Precedence

Several checks can fail on the same request. The server evaluates them in a fixed order and returns the first failure it finds, so that a caller always receives the most fundamental reason for rejection rather than an incidental one. The order is as follows.

1. Header presence and role recognition, on every endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation for the target endpoint, on every endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request-body shape, on every write endpoint: every required field present, no unknown fields, correct types, valid enum members, and every numeric range and array constraint stated in this document satisfied — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An endpoint that takes no request body accepts none, so any field at all supplied to it is an unknown field; a body that is not a JSON object, or that is unparseable JSON, fails the same way.
4. Path-identifier resolution, on every endpoint with a path parameter, validated left to right — `ERR_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Foreign-key resolution for identifiers carried in the request body — `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Agency scoping, when an `AgencyLiaison` responds to a consultation — `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. Segregation of duties, at decision signature and at objection resolution — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. State admissibility of the target row and of every row named in the transition's preconditions — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
9. Optimistic concurrency, on the two endpoints that carry an expected-version field — `ERR_STALE_ACTION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DECISION_VERSION`.
10. Publication completeness at publish time: unaddressed significant issues, then required consultations.
11. Referential coherence between a row and the rows it names — `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`.
12. Uniqueness and other business constraints — `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION` and its specialisations.
13. Interval, duration and deadline rules — `ERR_TEMPORAL_CONFLICT` and its specialisations.
14. The office obligation ceiling at signature — `ERR_OBLIGATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Three of these bands are families rather than single codes. Wherever the error catalogue provides a dedicated code for a specific condition, that dedicated code is returned in preference to the family code, and the family code covers only those conditions for which no dedicated code exists. At band 10 the dedicated codes are `ERR_UNADDRESSED_ISSUES` and `ERR_REQUIRED_CONSULTATION_INCOMPLETE`, evaluated in that order. At band 12 the dedicated codes are `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FEASIBLE`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALTERNATIVES`, `ERR_OBJECTION_STANDING_MISSING` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_ANALYSIS`, leaving `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION` for the uniqueness rules on `office_code`, on `agency_code`, on an alternative's `label` within an action, and on a cumulative entry's `label` within an action and resource area. At band 13 the dedicated codes are `ERR_SCOPING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_SCOPING_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_AGENCY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` and `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, leaving `ERR_TEMPORAL_CONFLICT` for the rule that the record may not be certified before the objection window has closed.

Band 8 covers the state of every row a transition reads, not only the row addressed in the path: a screen's `status`, a statement's `status`, an alternative's `screening_status`, a scoping period's `status`, an analysis's `status`, a document's `publication_status` and `comment_status`, a decision's `status`, an objection's `status`, and the action's `review_stage`. Reopening an analysis additionally reads the absence of any published or final document and of any signed decision on the action, and that particular failure carries the dedicated code `ERR_ANALYSIS_LOCKED`.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Coherence

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. A request addressed to a nested collection under a proposed action fails on the action's `id` before the server looks at anything further right, and the failure is always `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Identifiers carried in a request body are a different matter: `office_id`, `action_id`, `agency_id`, `document_id`, `decision_id`, `selected_alternative_id`, `source_analysis_id`, `scoping_period_id` and `assigned_issue_id` must all resolve to existing rows, and a body identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Resolving is not the same as being coherent. A referenced row must also belong to the right aggregate and be in an admissible state, and a violation of that stronger requirement yields `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Specifically: a document's `document_type` must match the owning action's `classification`; a decision's `decision_type` must match that same `classification`, with `CE_DETERMINATION` permitted only for `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`, `FONSI` and `MITIGATED_FONSI` permitted only for `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT`, and `RECORD_OF_DECISION` permitted only for `ENVIRONMENTAL_IMPACT_STATEMENT`; a `selected_alternative_id` must name an alternative of the same action whose `screening_status` is `SCREENED_IN`; a `source_analysis_id` must name an analysis of the same action whose `status` is `FINAL`; an `assigned_issue_id` must name an issue belonging to the same action as the commented document; and an issue's `scoping_period_id` must belong to the same action as the issue itself.

### 2.7. The Logical Clock, Intervals and Whole-Day Arithmetic

Every deadline, window and duration in this system is evaluated against a server-side logical clock rather than against wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that is the server startup default, and the server never falls back to real time. A `PolicyAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and every subsequent evaluation of a deadline, window or duration uses the new value.

Date arithmetic is whole-day arithmetic throughout. Adding a whole number of days to an instant advances it by exactly that many 24-hour periods. Measuring a span in days takes the floor of the exact elapsed days, so there is no partial-day credit: a scoping period's `duration_days` is the floor of the elapsed days from `start_at` to `end_at`, and a document's `comment_period_days` is the floor of the elapsed days from `comment_period_start_at` to `comment_period_end_at`.

Two different interval conventions coexist, and confusing them is the most common source of subtle error. A scoping period occupies the half-open interval that begins at `start_at` inclusive and ends at `end_at` exclusive. Two scoping periods on the same action with the same `scoping_kind` collide when the new period's `start_at` is strictly earlier than the existing period's `end_at` and the existing period's `start_at` is strictly earlier than the new period's `end_at`; comparison is strictly pairwise against each existing period, and touching endpoints do not collide, so a period beginning exactly when another ends is accepted. Existing periods participate in this test whether their `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. A consultation, by contrast, occupies a closed interval on its agency that begins at `requested_start_at` and ends at `statutory_due_at` advanced by the agency's `handoff_buffer_days` whole days, with both ends inclusive. A candidate consultation collides with an existing one when the two closed intervals share any instant at all, including a single shared endpoint; only rows whose `status` is `PENDING` on the same `agency_id` participate; and the rule is an aggregate-capacity rule rather than a pairwise one, so the candidate is rejected when the number of colliding pending rows reaches or exceeds the agency's `max_concurrent_consultations`.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Pinned Evaluation Order

Scores, percentages and credit quantities are carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded half up. Money is carried in whole cents and likewise rounded half up. Comparisons against configured thresholds are inclusive in the direction the threshold is written, and the specific direction is stated wherever a threshold is introduced; hectare and kilometre comparisons involve no rounding at all.

Three calculations in this system have a pinned rounding order, and taking a different order produces a different stored value. First, in the cumulative-effects aggregation, each included baseline entry's contribution is rounded to two decimal places on its own before the contributions are summed; summing the raw products and rounding the total once is not permitted. Second, in the mitigation lifecycle cost, each commitment's escalated monitoring stream is rounded to whole cents on its own before the commitments are summed; summing the raw streams across commitments and rounding the total once is not permitted, and the two orders differ by whole cents whenever two or more streams land on a half cent. Third, the four monetary and credit quantities a decision pins at signature are evaluated in a single forward pass in a fixed order and are never iterated to a fixed point; the details appear with the signature workflow.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations mutate a parent row's meaning badly enough that they require the caller to prove it was working from a current view of that parent. Both use the same mechanism: the request carries a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the parent that the client last observed, and the server rejects the request if that value does not equal the parent's current `version`. A value that is not a non-negative integer fails body validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field.

Adopting a purpose and need statement requires `expected_action_version` to equal the current `version` of the statement's `ProposedAction`. Adoption legitimately supersedes whichever statement is currently `ADOPTED` and drags every alternative pinned to that statement into `STALE`, so a writer holding an outdated view of the action would silently discard an adoption it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Attaching a mitigation commitment to a decision requires `expected_decision_version` to equal the current `version` of the target `DecisionDocument`. Each commitment changes the credit and obligation basis the decision will pin at signature, so a writer working from an outdated view of the decision would compute its mitigation package against a superseded commitment set. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DECISION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.10. Office Policy Parameters

A `ReviewOffice` is the policy container for every review it conducts. Beyond its `office_code` and its human-readable `name`, an office carries twenty configurable parameters. Each may be supplied at creation; each omitted parameter takes the default below.

The two classification thresholds are `ce_threshold_disturbance_hectares`, the exclusive upper bound of ground disturbance still eligible for a categorical exclusion, defaulting to 5.0 hectares and required to be greater than zero and strictly below the second threshold; and `ea_threshold_disturbance_hectares`, the exclusive upper bound still eligible for an environmental assessment, defaulting to 100.0 hectares and required to be strictly greater than the first. The three duration minima are `min_scoping_days`, the shortest public scoping period the office will accept as closable, defaulting to 30 whole days and required to be at least 1; `min_ea_comment_days`, defaulting to 30 whole days and required to be at least 1; and `min_eis_comment_days`, defaulting to 45 whole days and required to be at least `min_ea_comment_days`. The administrative-objection window is set by `objection_window_days`, defaulting to 45 whole days and required to be at least 1.

Analytical policy is set by five parameters. `cumulative_radius_km` is the inclusion radius for cumulative baseline entries, defaulting to 25.0 kilometres and required to be greater than zero. `significance_threshold` is the score value at or above which an analysis is significant, defaulting to 60.0 and required to be greater than zero and no greater than 100. `intensity_weight` and `context_weight` weight the two inputs of the significance base term, defaulting to 0.6 and 0.4 respectively; each must be strictly between zero and one, and the two must sum to exactly 1.0. `min_alternative_feasibility` is the feasibility value at or above which a non-no-action alternative may be screened in, defaulting to 60.0 and required to lie between 0 and 100 inclusive.

Mitigation and fiscal policy is set by four parameters. `max_mitigated_reduction_points` caps the total credit a single decision may claim, defaulting to 18.0 points and required to be greater than zero. `cost_per_reduction_point_cents` is the amount of base obligation that must stand behind each claimed credit point, defaulting to 250,000 cents — that is, $2,500 of committed lifecycle cost per point — and required to be non-negative. `monitoring_escalation_factor` multiplies each commitment's undiscounted monitoring stream, defaulting to 1.035, a 3.5 percent uplift, and required to be at least 1.0. `max_binding_obligation_cents` is the office-wide ceiling on obligations carried by signed decisions, defaulting to 900,000,000 cents — nine million dollars — and required to be non-negative.

Screening policy is set by four further parameters. `extraordinary_circumstance_codes` is the ordered list of circumstance codes this office screens for, defaulting to `EC_HISTORIC`, `EC_ENDANGERED_SPECIES` and `EC_WETLAND` in that order; the list must be non-empty and each element must be one of `EC_HISTORIC`, `EC_ENDANGERED_SPECIES`, `EC_WETLAND`, `EC_ENVIRONMENTAL_JUSTICE` or `EC_FLOODPLAIN`. `ec_severity_trigger_threshold` is the finding severity at or above which a circumstance that is present counts as triggered, defaulting to 3 and required to lie between 1 and 5 inclusive. `min_screened_in_alternatives_ea` is the number of screened-in alternatives an environmental assessment must have behind it before publication, defaulting to 1 and required to be non-negative, and `min_screened_in_alternatives_eis` is the corresponding number for an environmental impact statement, defaulting to 2 and required to be at least `min_screened_in_alternatives_ea`. A categorical-exclusion memorandum is subject to neither an alternatives minimum nor a minimum comment-period length.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The office operates on a segregation-of-duties model: the principal who prepares an artefact may not be the principal who blesses it. Two checks enforce this, and both return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). At decision signature, the signing `DecidingOfficial`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the decision's `prepared_by_user_id`. At objection resolution, the resolving `ObjectionReviewingOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the decision's `signed_by_user_id`. These checks are evaluated before any state or business check on the same request, so an official attempting to sign their own draft learns that fact even if the draft is not otherwise signable.

### 2.12. The Office Obligation Ledger

Each office carries a computed field, `binding_obligation_cents`, that is the office's live exposure to mitigation obligations. It is initialised to 0 when the office is created and is maintained incrementally thereafter, but it is defined by a closed form: at all times it equals the sum of `binding_obligation_cents` over every `DecisionDocument` whose action belongs to this office and whose `status` is `SIGNED`. Draft and vacated decisions are excluded from that sum.

Two events move the ledger. Signing a decision adds that decision's `binding_obligation_cents` to the office total. The vacatur of a signed decision on remand subtracts the same amount. The pair is exactly compensating: signing a decision and later vacating it restores `binding_obligation_cents` to its exact pre-signature value with no drift, even though the vacated decision retains its historical cent fields as part of the record. Before a signature is allowed, the office total after the addition must not exceed `max_binding_obligation_cents`; that is, the sum of the office's current `binding_obligation_cents` and the decision's own `binding_obligation_cents` must be less than or equal to the ceiling. A signature that would push the total past the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_OBLIGATION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Office and Its Agency Partners

Before any review can begin, a `PolicyAdmin` registers the review office that will conduct it, supplying an `office_code` — a short identifier of between 2 and 16 characters that must be unique across all offices — and a `name`. Every policy parameter described in §2.10 may be supplied at the same time or left to its default. Creation initialises the office's `binding_obligation_cents` to 0. A second office claiming an `office_code` already in use is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION`.

The same administrator registers each cooperating or consulting body as an `Agency`, with an `agency_code` of between 2 and 16 characters that must be unique across all agencies, a `name`, and an `agency_role` declaring the capacity in which the body participates — `COOPERATING`, `CONSULTING`, or `BOTH`. Three scheduling parameters govern how much consultation work the agency can carry: `max_concurrent_consultations`, the number of overlapping pending consultations it will tolerate, defaulting to 2 and required to be at least 1; `handoff_buffer_days`, the mandatory separation appended to the end of each occupied consultation interval, defaulting to 3 whole days and required to be non-negative; and `statutory_response_days`, the number of whole days the agency is allowed for a response, defaulting to 135 — roughly the statutory four and a half months — and required to be at least 1. An agency's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; only an `ACTIVE` agency may receive new consultation requests. Its `active_consultation_count` is a computed field, initialised to 0, that always equals the number of that agency's consultation requests whose `status` is `PENDING`. A duplicate `agency_code` is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION`.

One further administrative capability exists purely to make the statutory clocks testable: a `PolicyAdmin`-only operation sets the server's logical clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, as described in §2.7. That operation is the one write in the API that returns no resource: it answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` object.

### 3.2. Opening a Review and Screening for Extraordinary Circumstances

A `NepaCoordinator` opens a review by creating a `ProposedAction` under a named `office_id`, with a `title`, an estimated ground-disturbance footprint in `disturbance_hectares` that must not be negative, and a `reference_cost_cents` that must be strictly positive and which later serves as the denominator when alternatives are scored for cost. Four boolean flags describe the action's setting and each defaults to false: `in_critical_habitat`, whether the action area intersects designated critical habitat; `affects_historic_property`, whether a historic property may be affected; `tribal_interest`, whether a federally recognised tribe has asserted an interest; and `floodplain_or_wetland`, whether the area includes floodplain or wetland.

A new action begins at `review_stage` `INTAKE` with `classification` null. Its nine stages, in the order the workflow visits them, are `INTAKE`, `SCREENED`, `CLASSIFIED`, `SCOPING`, `ANALYSIS`, `DRAFT_PUBLISHED`, `COMMENT_CLOSED`, `DECIDED` and `RECORD_CERTIFIED`, of which `RECORD_CERTIFIED` is terminal. Every derived field starts at its baseline: `ec_screen_outcome` and `record_certified_at` are null, `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` is false, `significant_issue_count`, `unaddressed_issue_count`, `alternatives_screened_in_count`, `finalized_analysis_count` and `record_entry_count` are 0, and `max_unmitigated_score` and `cumulative_intensity_index` are 0.00.

Screening for extraordinary circumstances is the first substantive step, and it may be performed while the action's `review_stage` is any of `INTAKE`, `SCREENED`, `CLASSIFIED`, `SCOPING`, `ANALYSIS` or `DRAFT_PUBLISHED` — a late-surfacing circumstance is exactly the case the escalation cascade exists to handle. The coordinator submits a screen carrying `findings`, a non-empty array with one object per screened circumstance. Each object holds a `code` that must be a member of the owning office's `extraordinary_circumstance_codes`, a boolean `present`, an integer `severity` between 0 and 5 inclusive, and a `narrative` that is required whenever `present` is true. The server computes two derived fields from the array. `triggered_codes` lists the codes of those findings whose `present` is true and whose `severity` is at least the office's `ec_severity_trigger_threshold`, ordered as those codes appear in the office's `extraordinary_circumstance_codes` list. `max_severity` is the largest `severity` among findings whose `present` is true, or 0 when no finding is present. Because the membership test on `code` needs the owning office, it is evaluated after the path identifier has resolved but before the action's `review_stage` is examined, and it fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the array may name the same `code` more than once, and `triggered_codes` still lists each triggered code exactly once. A new screen is created with `screen_round` equal to 1, `status` `SUBMITTED`, and `adjudicator_note` null. A fresh screen may be submitted even while an earlier screen of the same action is still `SUBMITTED` or has already reached `CLEARED` or `TRIGGERED`; the number of screens per action is unbounded.

A `ReviewingOfficial` adjudicates a `SUBMITTED` screen by supplying a `disposition` of `RETURNED`, `CLEARED` or `TRIGGERED` together with an `adjudicator_note`; the note is a required field on every disposition, and only `RETURNED` additionally requires it to be non-empty. A `RETURNED` disposition requires the note to be present and non-empty; it records the note on the screen and moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`. Critically, a return is not an adjudicated outcome: the action's `ec_screen_outcome` and `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` are left exactly as they were, the action's `review_stage` does not move, and no record entry is appended. A `CLEARED` disposition is admissible only when `triggered_codes` is empty, and a `TRIGGERED` disposition only when `triggered_codes` is non-empty; a disposition that contradicts the computed codes in either direction is rejected with `ERR_EC_SCREEN_NOT_TRIGGERED`, a check evaluated only after the screen's `status` has been confirmed to be `SUBMITTED`. Both `CLEARED` and `TRIGGERED` are terminal for the screen.

Adjudicating `SUBMITTED → CLEARED` sets the action's `ec_screen_outcome` to `CLEARED` and its `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` to false; if the action's `review_stage` was `INTAKE`, it advances `INTAKE → SCREENED`; and an entry with `entry_code` `EC_SCREEN_ADJUDICATED` is appended, incrementing `record_entry_count`. The action's `version` increments as part of the same request. Because `ec_screen_outcome` reflects the most recently adjudicated screen on the action — the last one in `created_at` ascending order among those that reached an outcome — a later screen can overwrite an earlier verdict.

Adjudicating `SUBMITTED → TRIGGERED` fires a three-hop cascade, and every hop must land. The first hop sets the action's `ec_screen_outcome` to `TRIGGERED` and its `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` to true. The second hop follows from the flag becoming true: if the action's `classification` is `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`, that `classification` becomes `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT`, the `review_stage` is set to `CLASSIFIED`, and an entry with `entry_code` `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED` is appended. The third hop follows from the classification having moved away from `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`: every `NepaDocument` of the action whose `document_type` is `CE_MEMORANDUM` and whose `publication_status` is either `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` has its `publication_status` set to `SUPERSEDED` and its `comment_status` set to `CLOSED`, with one `DOCUMENT_SUPERSEDED` entry appended per superseded document. Independently of the cascade, if the action's `review_stage` was `INTAKE` it advances `INTAKE → SCREENED`, an `EC_SCREEN_ADJUDICATED` entry is appended, `record_entry_count` is incremented once for every entry appended by the request, and the action's `version` increments once for the whole request. The `EC_SCREEN_ADJUDICATED` entry is appended first and therefore takes the lowest `sequence_no` of the request, followed by the `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED` entry and then by one `DOCUMENT_SUPERSEDED` entry per superseded document in `created_at` ascending order.

A screen that was returned can be reworked without limit. Resubmission by a `NepaCoordinator` moves the screen `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`, replaces `findings` with the resubmitted array subject to the same constraints — the office-membership test on every `code` is applied first, ahead of the check that the screen is `RETURNED` — increments `screen_round` by one, recomputes `triggered_codes` and `max_severity` from the new array, and clears `adjudicator_note` back to null. The `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` cycle is unbounded, and `screen_round` is the record of how many rounds it took. Resubmission neither reads nor moves the action's `review_stage` and appends no administrative-record entry.

### 3.3. Classification and the Escalation Cascade

Classification is the decision that determines which review pathway — and therefore which document, which comment period and which decision instrument — applies. A `NepaCoordinator` classifies an action whose `review_stage` is `SCREENED` and whose `ec_screen_outcome` is not null; either condition failing yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The pathway is derived from the action's own data and its office's thresholds, evaluated top to bottom with the first matching branch winning. If `disturbance_hectares` is greater than or equal to the office's `ea_threshold_disturbance_hectares`, the `classification` is `ENVIRONMENTAL_IMPACT_STATEMENT`. Otherwise, if `disturbance_hectares` is greater than or equal to the office's `ce_threshold_disturbance_hectares`, or `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` is true, or `in_critical_habitat` is true, the `classification` is `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT`. Otherwise the `classification` is `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`. Hectare comparisons are made directly against the configured thresholds with no rounding, and both comparisons are inclusive of the threshold value, so a footprint sitting exactly on the environmental-assessment threshold lands in the impact-statement pathway.

Classification advances the action `SCREENED → CLASSIFIED`, appends a `CLASSIFICATION_ASSIGNED` entry, increments `record_entry_count`, and increments the action's `version` by one.

Classification is re-evaluated later only in the escalation direction, and only as a consequence of an extraordinary circumstance being triggered. Whenever a `TRIGGERED` adjudication sets `extraordinary_circumstance_flag` true — whether or not the flag was already true, since the escalation keys on the disposition rather than on a transition of the flag — and the current `classification` is `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`, the `classification` becomes `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT` and a `CLASSIFICATION_ESCALATED` entry is appended. An action whose `classification` is still null escalates nothing and keeps its `review_stage`. A flag flip never lowers an existing `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT` or `ENVIRONMENTAL_IMPACT_STATEMENT`, and there is no downgrade path of any kind. Escalation also rewinds the workflow: if the action's `review_stage` had already advanced to `SCOPING`, `ANALYSIS` or `DRAFT_PUBLISHED`, it reverts to `CLASSIFIED`, so the review must be rebuilt on the heavier pathway. This is the system's only `CLASSIFIED → CLASSIFIED` self-transition, and it is driven by a system event rather than by a client request.

### 3.4. Purpose and Need, and the Range of Alternatives

The purpose and need statement is the instrument that constrains how wide the alternatives range may be, so every alternative in the system is anchored to a specific statement. A `NepaCoordinator` drafts a statement on an action with a narrative `purpose_text`, a narrative `need_text`, a non-empty ordered `goal_codes` array whose elements are each between 2 and 24 uppercase characters with no duplicates, and an optional ordered `constraint_codes` array, defaulting to empty, subject to the same element rules. The server assigns `version_no` as one more than the highest `version_no` among the action's existing statements, or 1 for the first, and the statement begins with `status` `DRAFT`.

Only a `ReviewingOfficial` may adopt a draft, and adoption carries `expected_action_version` under the concurrency rule of §2.9. Adoption is admissible when the statement's `status` is `DRAFT` and the action's `review_stage` is `CLASSIFIED`, `SCOPING` or `ANALYSIS`. Moving a statement `DRAFT → ADOPTED` sets off a chain: any other statement of the same action whose `status` is `ADOPTED` moves `ADOPTED → SUPERSEDED`; every `Alternative` of the action whose `pinned_pn_statement_id` names that newly superseded statement and whose `screening_status` is `PROPOSED` or `SCREENED_IN` moves to `STALE`, and for each such alternative that had been `SCREENED_IN` the action's `alternatives_screened_in_count` is decremented by one; the action's `version` increments by one; and a `PN_ADOPTED` entry is appended, incrementing `record_entry_count`. A `PROPOSED → STALE` move changes no counter, while a `SCREENED_IN → STALE` move decrements the screened-in counter; `STALE` and `SCREENED_OUT` are both terminal for an alternative.

An alternative is created by a `NepaCoordinator` with an `action_id`, a `label` that must be unique among that action's alternatives, an `alternative_kind` of `NO_ACTION`, `PROPOSED_ACTION` or `REASONABLE_RANGE`, an optional `goal_alignment_codes` array defaulting to empty whose elements must each be a member of the pinned statement's `goal_codes` with no duplicates, and an `estimated_cost_cents` that must not be negative. At creation the server sets `pinned_pn_statement_id` to whichever statement of the action currently has `status` `ADOPTED`; if the action has no adopted statement, the creation is rejected with `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_PURPOSE_NEED`. Because the `goal_alignment_codes` membership test needs that pinned statement, it is evaluated only after one has been found, so an action with no adopted statement yields `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_PURPOSE_NEED` even when the supplied codes are themselves invalid. A duplicate `label` within the action is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION`, tested after both of those checks. Creating an alternative neither constrains nor moves the action's `review_stage`.

Creation also computes `feasibility_score`, which balances how much of the adopted purpose an alternative delivers against how far over the reference budget it runs. The alignment ratio is the number of entries in `goal_alignment_codes` that also appear in the pinned statement's `goal_codes`, divided by the number of entries in that `goal_codes` list. The cost overrun ratio is the amount by which `estimated_cost_cents` exceeds the action's `reference_cost_cents`, taken as zero when the estimate does not exceed the reference, divided by `reference_cost_cents`. The cost factor is one minus that overrun ratio, floored at zero, so an alternative costing twice the reference contributes nothing on the cost axis. The `feasibility_score` is one hundred multiplied by the sum of sixty-five hundredths of the alignment ratio and thirty-five hundredths of the cost factor, rounded half up to two decimal places. The alternative begins with `screening_status` `PROPOSED`.

A `ReviewingOfficial` screens a `PROPOSED` alternative by supplying a `decision` of either `SCREENED_IN` or `SCREENED_OUT`. Screening is admissible only while `screening_status` is `PROPOSED`, and only while the statement named by `pinned_pn_statement_id` still has `status` `ADOPTED`; an alternative whose pinned statement has been superseded is rejected with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_STALE`, and the same code applies when such an alternative is later named as a decision's selected alternative. Screening in additionally requires that the alternative's `alternative_kind` be `NO_ACTION`, or that its `feasibility_score` be greater than or equal to the office's `min_alternative_feasibility`; a no-action alternative is always eligible regardless of its score, because the range must always include it. A non-no-action alternative below the office minimum is rejected with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FEASIBLE`. A successful `PROPOSED → SCREENED_IN` increments the action's `alternatives_screened_in_count` by one; a `PROPOSED → SCREENED_OUT` changes no counter.

### 3.5. Scoping Periods and Issue Identification

Scoping is where the public and sister agencies tell the office what the review must address. A `NepaCoordinator` opens a `ScopingPeriod` on an action whose `review_stage` is `CLASSIFIED` or already `SCOPING` and whose `classification` is not null, supplying a `scoping_kind` of `PUBLIC` or `AGENCY`, a `start_at`, and an `end_at` that must be strictly later than `start_at`. The server computes `duration_days` and sets `status` to `OPEN`. Several periods may run on one action; a new period must not overlap an existing period of the same `scoping_kind` under the half-open rule of §2.7, and an overlap is rejected with `ERR_SCOPING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Opening the first period advances the action `CLASSIFIED → SCOPING` and appends no record entry; opening an additional period while the stage is already `SCOPING` leaves `review_stage` untouched.

Issues are recorded against an action while a period is open. A `NepaCoordinator` records one with a `scoping_period_id` naming a period that belongs to the same action and whose `status` is `OPEN`, a `resource_area` of `BIOLOGICAL`, `CULTURAL`, `AIR`, `WATER` or `SOCIOECONOMIC`, an `origin` of `PUBLIC` or `AGENCY` recording whether the input came from public or agency scoping, a `significance` of `SIGNIFICANT` or `NON_SIGNIFICANT` recording whether the issue must be carried into analysis, and a one-line `summary`. The issue begins with `status` `OPEN`; its other states are `ANALYZED` and the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. The open period is the only gate: recording an issue neither constrains nor moves the action's `review_stage`.

Recording an issue refreshes two rollups on the action, and both are also refreshed whenever an analysis is finalised or reopened. The `significant_issue_count` is the number of that action's issues whose `significance` is `SIGNIFICANT` and whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `ANALYZED`. The `unaddressed_issue_count` is the number of that action's issues whose `significance` is `SIGNIFICANT`, whose `status` is `OPEN`, and for which the action has no `ResourceAnalysis` on the same `resource_area` whose `status` is `FINAL`. Withdrawn issues never count toward either figure, and non-significant issues never count toward either.

Closing a scoping period is what moves the review into analysis. A `NepaCoordinator` may close a period whose `status` is `OPEN`, provided the action already has a `PurposeNeedStatement` with `status` `ADOPTED` — a close attempted without one is rejected with `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_PURPOSE_NEED`. A period whose `scoping_kind` is `PUBLIC` must additionally have `duration_days` greater than or equal to the office's `min_scoping_days`, or the close is rejected with `ERR_SCOPING_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`; a period whose `scoping_kind` is `AGENCY` is exempt from that length rule entirely. The period moves `OPEN → CLOSED`. The action's stage advances `SCOPING → ANALYSIS` only once every `ScopingPeriod` of the action has `status` `CLOSED`; closing one of several still-open periods closes that period, leaves `review_stage` at `SCOPING`, and appends no record entry. When the close is the one that completes the set, a `SCOPING_CLOSED` entry is appended and `record_entry_count` is incremented.

### 3.6. Interagency Consultation and Agency Capacity

Concurrent consultation is how the office keeps a review on schedule while satisfying several statutes at once. A `NepaCoordinator` opens a `ConsultationRequest` naming an `action_id`, an `agency_id`, a `consultation_type` of `ESA_SECTION_7`, `NHPA_SECTION_106`, `CWA_SECTION_404` or `COOPERATING_AGENCY`, and a `requested_start_at`. Opening a consultation against an agency whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, tested ahead of the capacity rule below. Creation places no constraint on the action's `review_stage` and does not move it: a consultation may be opened at any stage, including `INTAKE`.

Two fields are derived at creation. The `statutory_due_at` is `requested_start_at` advanced by the agency's `statutory_response_days` whole days, and it is the inclusive end of the response obligation. The boolean `is_required` records whether the action's own circumstances make this statutory track mandatory: `ESA_SECTION_7` is required when the action's `in_critical_habitat` is true; `NHPA_SECTION_106` is required when either `affects_historic_property` or `tribal_interest` is true; `CWA_SECTION_404` is required when `floodplain_or_wetland` is true; and `COOPERATING_AGENCY` is never a required track. A request whose `consultation_type` is in the action's required set has `is_required` true, and any other request has it false. The request begins with `status` `PENDING`, `responded_at` and `conditions_text` null, and `late` false, and the agency's `active_consultation_count` is incremented.

Capacity is enforced at creation under the closed-interval rule of §2.7: the candidate's occupied interval runs from `requested_start_at` through `statutory_due_at` advanced by the agency's `handoff_buffer_days`, both ends inclusive, and the candidate is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when the number of the agency's `PENDING` consultations whose own occupied intervals share any instant with it — a shared endpoint counts — reaches or exceeds the agency's `max_concurrent_consultations`.

Responding closes the consultation. An `AgencyLiaison` supplies an `outcome` of `CONCURRED`, `CONDITIONAL_CONCURRENCE` or `NOT_CONCURRED`, and may only do so for a consultation whose `agency_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. A `NepaCoordinator` instead supplies an `outcome` of `WITHDRAWN`, which is the coordinator's own retraction and carries no agency-scope requirement. The pairing is exclusive: an `AgencyLiaison` supplying `WITHDRAWN`, or a `NepaCoordinator` supplying any of the three agency outcomes, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, evaluated after the path identifier resolves and ahead of both the agency-scope check and the state check. In every case the consultation must be `PENDING`. `PENDING → CONCURRED` and `PENDING → NOT_CONCURRED` set `responded_at` to the current logical time and set `late` to true exactly when `responded_at` is strictly later than `statutory_due_at`. `PENDING → CONDITIONAL_CONCURRENCE` does the same and additionally records the `conditions_text` supplied with the response, which must be present and non-empty for that outcome; `conditions_text` offered alongside any other outcome is accepted and ignored, leaving the field null. `PENDING → WITHDRAWN` sets `responded_at` to the current logical time and sets `late` to false regardless of the deadline. All four outcomes are terminal, all four decrement the agency's `active_consultation_count` by one, and all four free the agency's occupied interval so that it no longer participates in the capacity test.

### 3.7. The Cumulative Baseline and Resource-Effects Analysis

Cumulative effects are assembled before the analyses that consume them. A `ResourceSpecialist` adds each past, present or reasonably foreseeable contributing action to an action's baseline with a `resource_area`, a `label` that must be unique within that action and resource area, an `intensity_contribution` between 0 and 100 inclusive, a `footprint_hectares` that must not be negative, a `distance_km` that must not be negative, and a `temporal_scope` of `PAST`, `PRESENT` or `REASONABLY_FORESEEABLE`. The server sets `inclusion_status` to `INCLUDED` when `distance_km` is less than or equal to the office's `cumulative_radius_km` and to `EXCLUDED` otherwise; an entry sitting exactly on the radius is included. A duplicate `label` within the same action and resource area is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATION`.

An analysis is opened per resource area. The specialist supplies a `resource_area`, an `intensity_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive measuring the direct effect, a `context_sensitivity_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive measuring how sensitive the affected setting is, an `affected_area_hectares` that must not be negative, and an optional `mitigation_reduction_points` defaulting to 0.0 and required to be non-negative and no greater than 40. At most one analysis may exist per action per resource area; a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_ANALYSIS`. Neither adding a baseline entry nor opening an analysis constrains or moves the action's `review_stage`: both are admissible at any stage, including `INTAKE`. The analysis begins with `status` `DRAFT`, with `significance_score`, `significance_determination` and `variant_applied` all null, and with `cumulative_effect_score`, `unmitigated_score` and `mitigated_score` at 0.00 until finalisation computes them.

Finalisation is where the arithmetic happens. A `ResourceSpecialist` finalises a `DRAFT` analysis on an action whose `review_stage` is `ANALYSIS`, supplying a `basis` of either `UNMITIGATED` or `MITIGATED`. A `MITIGATED` basis is admissible only when the analysis's `mitigation_reduction_points` is strictly greater than zero; requesting it against a zero reduction is rejected with `ERR_MITIGATION_BASIS_REQUIRED`.

The first quantity recomputed is `cumulative_effect_score`, aggregated over the baseline entries belonging to the same action and the same `resource_area` whose `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`. Excluded entries contribute nothing and are not counted anywhere in the aggregation. For each included entry the server computes a footprint weight equal to one plus the entry's `footprint_hectares` divided by 200, with that added term capped at five tenths, so a footprint of 100 hectares or more contributes the full cap. It then computes a temporal factor from the entry's `temporal_scope`: three quarters for `PAST`, exactly one for `PRESENT`, and eighty-five hundredths for `REASONABLY_FORESEEABLE`. The entry's contribution is its `intensity_contribution` multiplied by its footprint weight and then by its temporal factor, rounded half up to two decimal places at that point — the per-entry rounding of §2.8 — and the subtotal is the sum of the already-rounded contributions. A crowding multiplier reflects how many contributing actions crowd the same resource area: it is one plus five hundredths for each included entry beyond the first three, so three or fewer included entries leave the multiplier at exactly one. The `cumulative_effect_score` is the subtotal multiplied by the crowding multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. An empty included set yields a subtotal of zero, a count of zero, a multiplier of one, and a score of 0.00.

The significance score is then built in one pass, and both variants are computed and stored on every finalisation. The base term is the office's `intensity_weight` multiplied by the analysis's `intensity_score` plus the office's `context_weight` multiplied by its `context_sensitivity_score`. An area tier adder is selected from `affected_area_hectares`: exactly zero hectares adds nothing; more than zero and up to and including 5 hectares adds 2.0; more than 5 and up to and including 25 adds 4.0; more than 25 and up to and including 100 adds 7.0; and anything above 100 adds 11.0. A cumulative uplift adds fifteen hundredths of the amount by which `cumulative_effect_score` exceeds 40.0, taken as zero when the score is 40.0 or below. The direct term is the sum of the base term, the area tier adder and the cumulative uplift, capped at 100.0. The `unmitigated_score` is the direct term rounded half up to two decimal places. The `mitigated_score` is the direct term reduced by the analysis's `mitigation_reduction_points`, floored at zero, then rounded half up to two decimal places.

Which of the two the office stands behind is recorded explicitly. The `variant_applied` is set to the requested `basis`, and `significance_score` is set to `unmitigated_score` when the basis is `UNMITIGATED` and to `mitigated_score` when it is `MITIGATED`. Under an `UNMITIGATED` basis, `mitigation_reduction_points` is ignored entirely in choosing the recorded score. Under a `MITIGATED` basis the recorded score is the reduced one, which on identical stored inputs can flip the determination the other way. The `significance_determination` is `SIGNIFICANT` when `significance_score` is greater than or equal to the office's `significance_threshold`, and `NOT_SIGNIFICANT` otherwise; a score exactly equal to the threshold is `SIGNIFICANT`.

Finalisation moves the analysis `DRAFT → FINAL` and propagates outward. Every `Issue` of the action on the same `resource_area` whose `status` is `OPEN` moves `OPEN → ANALYZED`. The action's `finalized_analysis_count` becomes the number of its analyses whose `status` is `FINAL`; its `max_unmitigated_score` becomes the largest `unmitigated_score` among those final analyses, or 0.00 when there are none; its `cumulative_intensity_index` becomes the mean of `cumulative_effect_score` over those final analyses, rounded half up to two decimal places, or exactly 0.00 when there are none; and `unaddressed_issue_count` is recomputed. Draft analyses are excluded from all three aggregates. An `ANALYSIS_FINALIZED` entry is appended and `record_entry_count` is incremented.

Reopening is the exact compensating move. A `ResourceSpecialist` may return a `FINAL` analysis `FINAL → DRAFT` only while no `NepaDocument` of the action has `publication_status` `PUBLISHED` or `FINAL` and no `DecisionDocument` of the action has `status` `SIGNED`; once either is true the analysis is frozen and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ANALYSIS_LOCKED`. Reopening clears `significance_score`, `significance_determination` and `variant_applied` back to null, leaves `cumulative_effect_score`, `unmitigated_score` and `mitigated_score` at the values the finalisation stored, moves every `Issue` of the action on the same `resource_area` whose `status` is `ANALYZED` back `ANALYZED → OPEN`, recomputes the same four action rollups, and appends an `ANALYSIS_REOPENED` entry with the corresponding increment to `record_entry_count`. Because the issue moves mirror each other, the action's `unaddressed_issue_count` returns to exactly its pre-finalisation value.

### 3.8. Publication, the Comment Period, and Response to Comments

A NEPA document carries two independent state dimensions, and keeping them independent is essential. The publication dimension, `publication_status`, runs through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `FINAL` and the terminal `SUPERSEDED`. The comment dimension, `comment_status`, runs through `NOT_OPEN`, `OPEN` and `CLOSED`. A `NepaCoordinator` creates a document with a `document_type` of `CE_MEMORANDUM`, `ENVIRONMENTAL_ASSESSMENT` or `ENVIRONMENTAL_IMPACT_STATEMENT` and a `title`; the type must match the action's `classification` per §2.6. A new document is `DRAFT` on the publication axis and `NOT_OPEN` on the comment axis, with `comment_period_start_at` and `comment_period_end_at` null, `comment_period_days` at 0, `substantive_comment_count`, `responded_substantive_count` and `unresolved_substantive_count` all 0 and `response_completeness_pct` at 100.00. Creation itself neither constrains nor moves the action's `review_stage`; the type match is its only coherence gate.

Publication is the only operation that advances both axes at once, and it is heavily gated. The coordinator supplies the planned `comment_period_end_at`, the exclusive end of the comment period. The document's `publication_status` must be `DRAFT` and the action's `review_stage` must be `ANALYSIS`; its `document_type` must still match the action's `classification`, but because that re-match is a band-11 coherence test it is evaluated after the two completeness checks that follow, not alongside the state checks. The action's `unaddressed_issue_count` must be exactly zero, or the request is rejected with `ERR_UNADDRESSED_ISSUES`. For every consultation track in the action's required set, there must be a `ConsultationRequest` of that `consultation_type` whose `status` is `CONCURRED` or `CONDITIONAL_CONCURRENCE`; a missing or unfinished required track is rejected with `ERR_REQUIRED_CONSULTATION_INCOMPLETE`. The action's `alternatives_screened_in_count` must be at least the office minimum for the document type — `min_screened_in_alternatives_ea` for an environmental assessment and `min_screened_in_alternatives_eis` for an environmental impact statement — or the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALTERNATIVES`. Finally, the number of whole days from the current logical time to `comment_period_end_at` must be at least the office minimum for the document type — `min_ea_comment_days` for an environmental assessment and `min_eis_comment_days` for an environmental impact statement — or the request is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. As noted in §2.10, a `CE_MEMORANDUM` is bound by neither the alternatives minimum nor the comment-length minimum; because it is length-free, its `comment_period_end_at` may even fall at or before the current logical time, in which case its `comment_period_days` lands at zero or below.

A successful publication sets `publication_status` to `PUBLISHED` and `comment_status` to `OPEN`, records `comment_period_start_at` as the current logical time, computes `comment_period_days` as the floor of the elapsed days from that start to `comment_period_end_at`, advances the action `ANALYSIS → DRAFT_PUBLISHED`, and appends a `DOCUMENT_PUBLISHED` entry with the corresponding `record_entry_count` increment. After publication the two axes move independently.

While a document's `comment_status` is `OPEN`, a `PublicCommenter` may submit a comment naming the `document_id`, an optional `assigned_issue_id` mapping the comment onto a tracked scoping issue and defaulting to null when the comment raises no tracked issue, a `commenter_name`, a `resource_area`, an optional `is_substantive` flag defaulting to false that records whether a written response is required, and the comment `body`. Submitting against a document whose `comment_status` is anything other than `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. A new comment has `disposition` `RECEIVED`, with `response_text` and `response_category` null.

Every comment submission and every response recomputes four counters on the document. The `substantive_comment_count` is the number of that document's comments whose `is_substantive` is true and whose `disposition` is `RECEIVED` or `RESPONDED`. The `responded_substantive_count` is the number whose `is_substantive` is true and whose `disposition` is `RESPONDED`. The `unresolved_substantive_count` is the first figure minus the second. The `response_completeness_pct` is exactly 100.00 when `substantive_comment_count` is zero, and otherwise one hundred multiplied by `responded_substantive_count` divided by `substantive_comment_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Comments dispositioned `DUPLICATE` drop out of both counters.

Closing the comment period touches only the comment axis. A `NepaCoordinator` may do so when the document's `comment_status` is `OPEN` and the current logical time is at or after `comment_period_end_at`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. The move is `OPEN → CLOSED` on the comment axis, and `publication_status` is explicitly not changed — it remains `PUBLISHED`. If the action's `review_stage` was `DRAFT_PUBLISHED` it advances `DRAFT_PUBLISHED → COMMENT_CLOSED`; the `COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED` entry is appended in either case, with the corresponding `record_entry_count` increment.

Response-to-comments preparation happens only after the period is closed. A `NepaCoordinator` responds to a comment whose `disposition` is `RECEIVED`, on a document whose `comment_status` is `CLOSED`, supplying a `response_text` and a `response_category` of `FACTUAL_CORRECTION`, `ANALYSIS_REVISED`, `NO_CHANGE_NEEDED` or `OUT_OF_SCOPE`. Any category other than `OUT_OF_SCOPE` records both fields and moves the comment `RECEIVED → RESPONDED`, which raises `responded_substantive_count`, lowers `unresolved_substantive_count` and lifts `response_completeness_pct`. An `OUT_OF_SCOPE` category records both fields, with `response_category` set to `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and moves the comment `RECEIVED → DUPLICATE`, which removes it from `substantive_comment_count` altogether and recomputes `response_completeness_pct` over the smaller denominator. Both `RESPONDED` and `DUPLICATE` are terminal.

Finalising the document is the last publication-axis step before a decision may be signed. A `NepaCoordinator` may move `publication_status` `PUBLISHED → FINAL` only when `comment_status` is `CLOSED` and `unresolved_substantive_count` is exactly zero; a residual unresolved substantive comment is rejected with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMENTS`. Finalisation appends a `DOCUMENT_FINALIZED` entry and increments `record_entry_count`.

### 3.9. Decision, Mitigation Obligations, Objection and Record Certification

A `NepaCoordinator` drafts the decision instrument naming an `action_id`, a `selected_alternative_id` and a `decision_type` of `CE_DETERMINATION`, `FONSI`, `MITIGATED_FONSI` or `RECORD_OF_DECISION`, subject to the type-to-classification and alternative-state coherence rules of §2.6. A `STALE` selected alternative is rejected with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_STALE` ahead of every `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` test, and among those tests the `decision_type`-versus-`classification` rule is evaluated first, then the same-action rule, then the `SCREENED_IN` rule. Drafting a decision neither constrains nor moves the action's `review_stage`. The server records `prepared_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and sets `status` to `DRAFT`, with `signed_by_user_id`, `signed_at`, `objection_window_opens_at` and `objection_window_closes_at` all null, `credit_points` and `mitigated_max_score` at 0.0, `objection_count` at 0, and `committed_obligation_cents`, `base_obligation_cents`, `contingency_fee_cents` and `binding_obligation_cents` all at 0.

Mitigation commitments are attached to a draft decision one at a time, each under the concurrency rule of §2.9 via `expected_decision_version`. A commitment names the `source_analysis_id` of the `FINAL` analysis whose effect it mitigates, a `measure_text` describing the measure, a `cost_estimate_cents` for one-time implementation that must not be negative, an optional `monitoring_years` defaulting to 0 and required to be between 0 and 20 inclusive, an optional `annual_monitoring_cost_cents` defaulting to 0 and required to be non-negative, and an optional `reduction_points` defaulting to 0.0 and required to be non-negative and no greater than 20. The commitment begins with `status` `PROPOSED` and `monitoring_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, and attaching it increments the decision's `version` by one.

Each commitment carries a `lifecycle_obligation_cents` that captures its full cost over time. The undiscounted monitoring stream is `monitoring_years` multiplied by `annual_monitoring_cost_cents` and then by the office's `monitoring_escalation_factor`; that product is rounded half up to whole cents on its own, and `lifecycle_obligation_cents` is `cost_estimate_cents` plus the rounded stream. Rounding once per commitment before any summation is the pinned order described in §2.8.

Signature is reserved to a `DecidingOfficial` and is the moment every fiscal quantity is fixed. The decision's `status` must be `DRAFT`; the signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id` per §2.11; the action's `review_stage` must be `COMMENT_CLOSED`; and unless the `decision_type` is `CE_DETERMINATION`, the action must have a `NepaDocument` whose `publication_status` is `FINAL`, with a missing final document rejected as `ERR_FINAL_DOCUMENT_REQUIRED`. The signature then identifies every `MitigationCommitment` of the decision whose `status` is `PROPOSED` as the set that is about to become `BINDING`, and every figure and check below reads that set; the rows themselves are written only once the whole signature is permitted, so each one moves to `BINDING` with its `monitoring_status` set to `SCHEDULED` when its `monitoring_years` is greater than zero and to `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise, while a signature rejected at any point — including at the obligation ceiling — leaves every commitment `PROPOSED`, the decision `DRAFT` and the office ledger untouched. A `MITIGATED_FONSI` must carry at least one such commitment; a mitigated finding of no significant impact with no commitment behind it is rejected with `ERR_MITIGATED_FONSI_WITHOUT_COMMITMENT`.

The fiscal quantities look mutually dependent — the fee is keyed on a credit tier, the credit is keyed on an obligation, and the obligation includes the fee — but they are evaluated in a single pinned forward pass, in the order given here, and are never iterated to a fixed point. First, `committed_obligation_cents` is the sum of `cost_estimate_cents` over the decision's commitments whose `status` is `BINDING`, with no monitoring escalation and no contingency fee; it is the narrower of the two obligation readings and is always at or below `binding_obligation_cents` on identical commitment rows. Second, `base_obligation_cents` is the sum of `lifecycle_obligation_cents` over those same binding commitments; commitments still `PROPOSED` and commitments already `RELEASED` are excluded from both sums. Third, `credit_points` is computed from that base alone: the declared total is the sum of `reduction_points` over the same binding commitments, the capacity total is `base_obligation_cents` divided by the office's `cost_per_reduction_point_cents` — unbounded, and so never the smallest of the three, when that parameter is configured at zero — and `credit_points` is the smallest of the declared total, the capacity total and the office's `max_mitigated_reduction_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fourth, a fee rate is selected from that credit figure: five hundredths when `credit_points` is 10.00 or more, thirty-five thousandths when it is 5.00 or more but below 10.00, and two hundredths otherwise. Fifth, `contingency_fee_cents` is `base_obligation_cents` multiplied by that fee rate, rounded half up to whole cents — the fee is charged on the base, not on the binding total. Sixth, `binding_obligation_cents` is `base_obligation_cents` plus `contingency_fee_cents`. At that point `credit_points` is frozen: recomputing the capacity total from the larger `binding_obligation_cents` would raise the credit and is not permitted, and the value persisted on the decision is the one computed in the third step. Seventh and last, `mitigated_max_score` is the action's `max_unmitigated_score` reduced by the frozen `credit_points`, floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places.

Only once those figures exist is the office ceiling tested, under the ledger rule of §2.12, and a signature that would breach it is rejected with `ERR_OBLIGATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A permitted signature sets `signed_by_user_id` to the signer's `X-User-ID` and `signed_at` to the current logical time, moves the decision `DRAFT → SIGNED`, adds the decision's `binding_obligation_cents` to the office's own `binding_obligation_cents`, sets `objection_window_opens_at` equal to `signed_at` and `objection_window_closes_at` to `signed_at` advanced by the office's `objection_window_days` whole days, advances the action `COMMENT_CLOSED → DECIDED`, and appends a `DECISION_SIGNED` entry with the corresponding `record_entry_count` increment.

The objection window is half-open. A `PublicCommenter` or an `AgencyLiaison` may file an `Objection` at or after `objection_window_opens_at` and strictly before `objection_window_closes_at`; filing outside that span is rejected with `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The decision's `status` must be `SIGNED`, and that state check runs ahead of both the standing check and the window check, so filing against a `DRAFT` or `VACATED` decision is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; an `AgencyLiaison` filing an objection is subject to no agency-scope check. An objection names an `objector_name`, an `objector_type` of `COMMENTER`, `AGENCY`, `TRIBE` or `OTHER`, a `basis_issue_area` naming the resource area it is grounded in, and a `standing_basis` of `SUBSTANTIVE_COMMENT`, `AGENCY_CONSULTATION` or `NONE`. A `standing_basis` of `NONE` means the objector did not participate during the comment period and is rejected with `ERR_OBJECTION_STANDING_MISSING`. A filed objection has `status` `FILED` and `resolution_note` null, refreshes the decision's `objection_count` — the number of that decision's objections whose `status` is `FILED`, `REMANDED` or `DENIED` — and appends an `OBJECTION_FILED` entry with the corresponding `record_entry_count` increment.

An `ObjectionReviewingOfficer` resolves a `FILED` objection with a `disposition` of `DENIED` or `REMANDED` and a `resolution_note` that must be present and non-empty, and whose own `X-User-ID` must differ from the decision's `signed_by_user_id` per §2.11. A denial records the `resolution_note`, moves the objection `FILED → DENIED`, and appends an `OBJECTION_RESOLVED` entry with the corresponding `record_entry_count` increment. A denial deliberately triggers no cascade: the decision's `status` stays `SIGNED`, every `BINDING` commitment stays `BINDING` with its `monitoring_status` unchanged, the office's `binding_obligation_cents` is untouched, every document keeps its `publication_status`, and the action stays at `review_stage` `DECIDED`. Only the decision's `objection_count` is affected, and only because a `DENIED` objection still counts toward it. A remand is admissible only while the decision's `status` is `SIGNED` and the action's `record_certified_at` is still null; attempting to remand after the record has been certified is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`. A remand records the `resolution_note`, moves the objection `FILED → REMANDED`, and appends an `OBJECTION_RESOLVED` entry — and then fires a cascade that unwinds the decision.

The remand cascade has three consequences, and each follows from the one before it. The decision itself moves `SIGNED → VACATED`. Because the decision is vacated, every `MitigationCommitment` of that decision whose `status` is `BINDING` moves `BINDING → RELEASED` with its `monitoring_status` set to `RELEASED`, and the decision's `binding_obligation_cents` is subtracted from the office's `binding_obligation_cents`, restoring the ledger to its exact pre-signature value. Because the decision is vacated, every `NepaDocument` of the action whose `publication_status` is `FINAL` reverts that axis `FINAL → DRAFT` while its `comment_status` stays `CLOSED`, and the action reverts `DECIDED → COMMENT_CLOSED`. A `DECISION_VACATED` entry is appended alongside the `OBJECTION_RESOLVED` entry, in the cascade order of §2.4, and `record_entry_count` rises for each. The vacated decision keeps `committed_obligation_cents`, `base_obligation_cents`, `credit_points`, `contingency_fee_cents` and `binding_obligation_cents` as its historical record; those figures are not zeroed, even though the office ledger no longer counts them. `RELEASED` is terminal for a commitment and `VACATED` is terminal for a decision, so the review must be re-decided on a fresh draft.

Certifying the litigation record is the terminal act, and it belongs to a `ReviewingOfficial` rather than to the office that prepared the review. Certification requires the action's `review_stage` to be `DECIDED`, the decision's `status` to be `SIGNED`, the current logical time to be at or after `objection_window_closes_at` — an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_TEMPORAL_CONFLICT` — and no objection on the decision to remain with `status` `FILED`, with a still-pending objection rejected as `ERR_OBJECTIONS_PENDING`. Certification sets the action's `record_certified_at` to the current logical time, advances the action `DECIDED → RECORD_CERTIFIED`, appends a `RECORD_CERTIFIED` entry and increments `record_entry_count`. Because `record_certified_at` is then non-null, a second certification, like a later remand, is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`; that already-certified test runs ahead of every other check on certification, so it is returned in preference to the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` the terminal `RECORD_CERTIFIED` stage would otherwise raise. `RECORD_CERTIFIED` is terminal for the action.
