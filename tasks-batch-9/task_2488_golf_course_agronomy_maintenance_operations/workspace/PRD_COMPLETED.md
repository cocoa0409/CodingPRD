# Golf Course Agronomy & Maintenance Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A golf course superintendent runs three businesses at once: an agricultural operation, a regulated chemical-handling operation, and a hospitality operation whose customers are standing on the field while the work happens. The annual agronomic plan commits fertility, aeration, topdressing and pest-control work by turf area a year ahead of time; irrigation draws against a permitted annual withdrawal through pump stations with hard flow and daily caps; every pesticide and fertiliser application must be traceable to a licensed applicator, a product label, a buffer distance and a posting; and none of it may happen while a group is playing the hole. This API is the system of record for all of that, and its central design commitment is that no single subsystem is allowed to be optimised in isolation — a water decision changes what irrigation may run, a weather declaration changes what crews may do, and a voided spray record reaches all the way back into last winter's nitrogen budget.

The system is built around a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, because every regulated interval in the domain is a deadline: a licence expiry, a restricted-entry interval, a 24-hour readiness window before a tournament round, a fiscal-year-end projection for capital replacement. Operators need to be able to drive those deadlines deterministically, and auditors need the record to reproduce exactly. For the same reason the system keeps **ledger fields** rather than recomputing history on demand: nitrogen committed against a plan budget, nitrogen applied per thousand square feet on a turf area, gallons withdrawn today on a pump station and gallons withdrawn this year on a course are all maintained continuously and are re-derived whenever any contributing record moves. The two turf-area nitrogen figures go one step further: as §3.3 pins, the values a read reports are re-derived from the records that exist at the moment of the read.

Two cross-cutting behaviours dominate the specification. The first is **posture**: a course carries a water posture and a play posture that are never written by a client, are derived entirely from the water ledger and from the set of active weather events, and act as gates on almost every child operation. The second is **compensation**: nearly every irreversible-looking action in the domain has a defined reversal — an irrigation run can be reversed, a crew window can be deferred and rescheduled, a submitted plan can be returned, an application record can be voided — and each reversal is required to unwind exactly the ledger movements its forward counterpart made. Readers implementing this specification should treat the reversal path as being just as load-bearing as the forward path.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where a rule is stated here it is not restated in full in §3; where §3 describes a workflow it assumes everything in this section already applies.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. Five roles exist and their spellings are exact: `Superintendent`, `Agronomist`, `Applicator`, `TournamentOfficial`, and `EquipmentTechnician`. A request that omits `X-User-Role`, or that presents a role value which is not one of those five, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). `X-User-ID` is likewise required on exactly the three endpoints where the acting identity is itself part of a rule — `POST /agronomic-plans/{id}/submit`, `POST /agronomic-plans/{id}/approve` and `POST /applications/{id}/verify` — and a missing or empty `X-User-ID` on one of those three is also `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; no other endpoint inspects the header's presence, so a request to any of the other fifty-eight endpoints succeeds with `X-User-ID` absent. Once authenticated, a caller whose role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); the missing-`X-User-ID` test sits inside the `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` band and therefore precedes that authorisation test.

The five roles divide the write surface along lines of professional responsibility. A `Superintendent` owns the course record and its water allocation, the applicator and product registries, the weather log, the capital fate of the fleet, and the integrity of the application record; it is the only role that may void an application, reverse an irrigation run, approve or return a plan, retire a machine, declare or clear weather, or drive the logical clock. An `Agronomist` authors the annual plan, submits it, adds and schedules its tasks, books and runs crew windows, and schedules and completes irrigation. An `Applicator` records applications, verifies them, and releases field postings. A `TournamentOfficial` posts rounds — including simultaneous-start shotgun events — and certifies playing conditions. An `EquipmentTechnician` runs the shop: meter hours, completed services, and taking pump stations offline for repair. Turf-area registration is shared between `Superintendent` and `Agronomist`; play-event posting between `TournamentOfficial` and `Superintendent`; playability measurement between `Agronomist` and `TournamentOfficial`; fleet intake between `Superintendent` and `EquipmentTechnician`; and pump-station status between `Superintendent` and `EquipmentTechnician`.

Identity semantics differ by role and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicator` record — the identifier returned when that applicator was registered — and ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references an applicator compare the resource's applicator reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, verification of an application resolves the verifying applicator by looking up `X-User-ID` as an `Applicator` id, so an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no applicator yields `ERR_APPLICATOR_NOT_FOUND`, and an `X-User-ID` equal to the record's own `applicator_id` yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. When `X-User-Role` is `Superintendent`, `Agronomist`, `TournamentOfficial`, or `EquipmentTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier — it stores one as `submitted_by_user_id` on an agronomic plan when that plan is submitted — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The one comparison it does make is between two opaque identifiers: the caller approving a plan must not be the caller recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`, and a match is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` bounds the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp ascending, with ties broken by ascending insertion order — the sequence in which the server first wrote the rows — so the ordering is always total and deterministic. Two endpoints state otherwise: the weather log for a course returns its events with the most recent `declared_at` first, and the playability readings for a turf area return the most recent `measured_at` first; each of those two breaks a tie by the later `created_at` and then by the later insertion order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Versioning

Every resource in the system carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Clients never supply either field.

Most resources additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, formatted identically to `created_at`, refreshed on every change to the resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative, such as a ledger recomputation or an automatic status change cascaded from another entity. The resources carrying `updated_at` and `version` are courses, turf areas, agronomic plans, plan tasks, applicators, applications, pump stations, irrigation runs, maintenance windows, play events, equipment units, and weather events.

Two resource types are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: **pesticide products**, which are immutable label records with no update endpoint, and **playability readings**, which are append-only measurement events with no update or delete endpoint. Neither type has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field; responses for these two types must not include those keys.

Because so many rules in this system are cross-entity, a single request routinely bumps the `version` of several resources. The rule is mechanical: each resource that is actually mutated by the request has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, and a resource that a request merely reads is not touched. A resource whose version was bumped only because a derived field was recomputed still counts as mutated.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a single logical clock, and every temporal decision in the system reads it rather than real wall-clock time: applicator licence expiry, restricted-entry interval expiry, the calendar year used by the nitrogen ledgers, the calendar day used by the pump station's daily withdrawal ledger, the 24-hour readiness window before a posted start, the remaining days used by the fiscal-year replacement projection, the `declared_at` and `cleared_at` stamps on weather events, and the `suspended_at` stamp on a suspended licence.

At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It never falls back to real time: before any caller has moved it, that instant is the clock. A `Superintendent` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, which becomes the new reading; the instant is required, may be any point in time — forwards or backwards relative to the present reading — and the endpoint answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object carrying the single key `current_utc`, holding the accepted instant rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Moving the clock mutates no entity and increments no `version`; it changes only what subsequent requests observe.

Two inputs are validated against the clock rather than against each other. An application's `applied_at` may not be after the current clock reading, and a playability reading's `measured_at` may not be after the current clock reading; a value in the future is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A weather event's `expected_clear_at` must be strictly after the current clock reading, which is also the event's `declared_at`, and a value at or before it is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every rounding operation in this specification is **round half up applied to the exact decimal value** of the quantity being rounded, never to a binary floating-point approximation of it. This distinction is normative, not advisory: rounding 4.675 to two decimal places must yield 4.68, and rounding 11.615 to two decimal places must yield 11.62. An implementation that produces 4.67 or 11.61 because it rounded a float is non-conforming. Implementations should perform monetary and agronomic arithmetic in exact decimal arithmetic throughout.

The decimal scale differs by quantity and each is fixed:

- Nitrogen figures at plan scope — a plan task's per-task commitment and a plan's committed total — carry **2 decimal places**.
- Nitrogen figures at turf-area scope, expressed in pounds per thousand square feet, carry **3 decimal places**.
- Applied product mass and applied nitrogen mass on an application record carry **2 decimal places**.
- An irrigation run's authorised gallons and its actually withdrawn gallons carry **1 decimal place**, as do a pump station's gallons withdrawn today and a course's gallons used this year; the run's flow rate carries **2 decimal places**.
- The water overage assessment's monetary figures carry **2 decimal places**; its one non-monetary figure, `billable_gal`, is reported rounded half up to **1 decimal place**, matching the scale of the two gallon ledgers it is derived from.
- Both equipment replacement scores carry **1 decimal place**, as do an equipment unit's `initial_engine_hours`, `engine_hours`, `engine_hours_at_last_service` and `hours_since_service`.
- A play event's readiness index carries **2 decimal places**.
- Frost pushback is always a whole number of minutes, even though the timestamps it produces carry three fractional digits.

Where a calculation both aggregates and rounds, **the order of the two operations is pinned** and is stated with the calculation in §3. In every case where the specification pins "round each contributor first, then add the already-rounded contributions", the alternative order — add the exact values and round once at the end — is non-conforming, because the two orders diverge whenever two or more contributors each carry a half-unit remainder. Similarly, where an intermediate result is rounded before feeding a subsequent multiplication, that intermediate rounding is part of the contract.

One comparison is deliberately **not** rounded: the ratio of gallons used to gallons allocated that drives the course water posture is compared as an exact decimal ratio with no intermediate rounding at all.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Cross-Aggregate Writes

Four write operations in this system commit a resource against headroom that lives on a different resource, and each of them therefore requires the client to pin the version of that other resource. The pinned field is a required, non-nullable input; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. If the value supplied does not equal the current `version` of the named parent resource, the write is refused rather than being applied to a state the client has not observed.

- Scheduling a plan task (`POST /plan-tasks/{id}/schedule`) requires `expected_plan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `AgronomicPlan` that owns the task. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because each schedule debits the plan's committed nitrogen against its budget, so a plan whose version has moved since the client read it no longer offers the budget headroom the client reasoned about.
- Approving an agronomic plan (`POST /agronomic-plans/{id}/approve`) requires `expected_plan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that plan. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A return that legitimately supersedes the submission, or a task addition that moved the committed ledger, both advance the plan's version, so a stale value means the approver is approving something other than what they read.
- Declaring a weather event (`POST /weather-events`) requires `expected_course_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Course` the event is declared on. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COURSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newly declared event supersedes the play posture derived from earlier events, so a stale course version means the declarer has not observed the posture, the frost pushback, or the out-of-play set that the new declaration would overwrite.
- Creating an irrigation run (`POST /irrigation-runs`) requires `expected_pump_station_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `PumpStation` supplying the run. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PUMP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The run's allotment is computed from the station's daily cap less what it has already withdrawn today, and is then checked against the station's flow and zone capacity, so a stale pump version means the headroom and capacity the client sized the run against are no longer the station's actual state.

No other endpoint accepts a pinned-version field. Endpoints not named above must not require or interpret one.

### 2.8. Request Evaluation Order

Validation in this system is a **single total order across all rules**, not a set of independent checks. When one request violates two or more rules, the rule with the numerically smallest priority determines the response and no later rule's error is reported. The order proceeds through bands: transport first, then existence of every referenced entity, then state legality, then version pinning, then business safety, then business referential integrity, then temporal geometry, then structural constraints, then fiscal limits.

The complete order, with the scope of each rule, is:

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — headers present and role recognised. Every endpoint.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — the acting role is authorised for the endpoint. Every endpoint.
3. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — the body parses; every required field is present and non-null; every field has its declared type; every enum value is a declared member; every id is a well-formed UUID; every number is inside its declared range; and every interval satisfies end strictly after start. Every endpoint, including endpoints whose only input is a path identifier: a path segment that is not a well-formed UUID is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) and never the corresponding 404, and this holds for the two sub-resource reads of §2.9 as well. A request body is optional wherever no field is declared — an absent, empty or `null` body is read as an empty object — and unrecognised extra keys are ignored rather than rejected.
4. `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — for `GET /courses/{id}`, `GET /courses/{id}/water-assessment`, `GET /courses/{id}/weather-events`, `PATCH /courses/{id}/water-allocation`, `POST /turf-areas`, `POST /agronomic-plans`, `POST /pump-stations`, `POST /maintenance-windows`, `POST /play-events`, `POST /equipment-units`, `POST /weather-events`.

### 2.9. Flat Collections, Reverse Lookups and Cross-Aggregate Containment

Every collection in this API is a top-level path. No resource identifier appears as a path prefix segment of another collection: a plan task is not addressed underneath its plan, and an irrigation run is not addressed underneath its pump station. Parent scoping travels instead in two places — as a foreign-key field in the request body when a child is created, and as a query-string filter on the child collection when children are read back. `GET /turf-areas` filtered by `course_id` is how the areas of a course are discovered; `GET /plan-tasks` filtered by `plan_id` or by `turf_area_id`; `GET /applications` filtered by any of `turf_area_id`, `product_id`, `applicator_id`, `plan_task_id` or `verified_by_applicator_id`; `GET /irrigation-runs` filtered by `pump_station_id` or `turf_area_id`; `GET /maintenance-windows` filtered by `course_id`, `turf_area_id` or `plan_task_id`; `GET /equipment-units` filtered by `course_id` or by the self-referential `replaced_by_unit_id`. Two reverse lookups are exposed as sub-resource reads instead: the weather log of a course, and the playability readings of a turf area. For those two the parent named in the path is resolved before any child row is inspected, so a request for the readings of a non-existent turf area reports `ERR_TURF_AREA_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty page — but the transport band still comes first, so a malformed path UUID, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset` or an undeclared filter value on the same request reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and the parent is never looked up.

Because parentage is expressed by foreign key rather than by path, the system enforces **cross-aggregate containment** explicitly, and every failure of containment is `ERR_TASK_TURF_AREA_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422):

- An application that cites a plan task must be on the same turf area as that plan task.
- Scheduling a plan task onto a crew window requires the window's turf area to be the task's turf area.
- A crew window's turf area must belong to the course named on the window.
- A plan task's turf area must belong to the course its plan governs.
- An irrigation run's turf area must belong to the same course as the pump station supplying the run.
- Every turf area named in a weather event's affected-area list must belong to the course the event is declared on.

### 2.10. Course Postures as System-Wide Gates

A course carries two derived posture values, and neither is ever writable by a client. Both are recomputed by the server whenever their inputs move, and both gate operations on the course's children.

The **water posture** (`water_posture`) takes one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, or `SUSPENDED`, and is derived from a single ratio: the course's gallons used this year divided by its annual allocation, multiplied by 100 to give a percentage. That percentage is compared, without any rounding, against two thresholds carried on the course itself. If it is greater than or equal to `water_suspended_threshold_pct` the posture is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if it is greater than or equal to `water_restricted_threshold_pct` the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Both boundaries are inclusive, so a course that has used exactly 80 percent of its allocation against a default restricted threshold of 80 is `RESTRICTED` and not `OPEN`: 6448 gallons used against an 8060 gallon allocation is exactly 80.0 percent, which is `RESTRICTED`. The starting value at course registration is `OPEN`. Water posture is re-derived on exactly three occasions: when an irrigation run is completed, when a completed irrigation run is reversed, and when the course's allocation or thresholds are revised.

The **play posture** (`play_posture`) takes one of `OPEN`, `FROST_DELAY`, `CART_PATH_ONLY`, or `CLOSED`, and is derived from the set of weather events on the course whose status is `ACTIVE`; cleared events are ignored entirely. Precedence is pinned, most restrictive first. If any active event is a lightning event or a storm-damage event, the posture is `CLOSED` and the course's `frost_delay_minutes` is 0. Otherwise, if any active event is a frost event, the posture is `FROST_DELAY` and `frost_delay_minutes` takes the delay of the **governing** frost event, which is the active frost event with the greatest `declared_at`, breaking a tie by the greatest `created_at`. Otherwise, if any active event is a heavy-rain event, the posture is `CART_PATH_ONLY` and `frost_delay_minutes` is 0. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN` and `frost_delay_minutes` is 0. Play posture is re-derived when a weather event is declared and when one is cleared, and the starting value at course registration is `OPEN` with a `frost_delay_minutes` of 0.

The gating effect of the postures is stated with the operations they gate, but the summary is this. A `CLOSED` play posture blocks recording an application, scheduling an irrigation run, starting a crew window, and certifying playing conditions, all with `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`; the single exemption is a crew window whose task class is `STORM_CLEANUP`, because storm cleanup is precisely the work a closed course needs. The water posture does not block irrigation outright — it scales it, and at `SUSPENDED` it scales it to nothing, which is then caught as a zero allotment. The water posture also mirrors onto pump station status, described in §3.5.

### 2.11. Interval Geometry

Three different overlap geometries appear in this system, and confusing them produces silently wrong conflict detection. Each is stated exactly once, here, and referred to by name afterwards.

**Crew-window against crew-window is closed-interval geometry.** Two maintenance windows on the same turf area conflict when the new window's start is at or before the other window's end **and** the other window's start is at or before the new window's end. Because both comparisons are inclusive, windows that merely touch at an endpoint do conflict. Only windows in status `SCHEDULED`, `DEFERRED`, or `IN_PROGRESS` are considered; `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` windows are invisible to this test. When the test runs on a reschedule, the window being rescheduled is excluded from its own comparison, so a window may always be moved to an interval that overlaps where it currently sits. A conflict is `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

**Crew-window against play is half-open geometry with a clearance buffer.** The crew window is first extended at its tail by a clearance buffer of 30 minutes, giving the interval that begins at the window's start, inclusive, and ends 30 minutes after the window's end, exclusive. That extended interval is tested against a play event's occupancy of the specific hole the window's turf area serves. A shotgun-start event occupies every hole from 1 up to its holes-in-play count for the whole of its interval, beginning at its effective start, inclusive, and ending at its posted end, exclusive. A sequential event — standard tee times or a tournament round — occupies hole *h* only during the interval that begins at its effective start plus (*h* minus 1) times the hole pace of 12 minutes, inclusive, and ends at its effective start plus *h* times 12 minutes, exclusive. For both geometries the hole must be inside the event's field of play: a hole number greater than the event's `holes_in_play` is unoccupied by that event, so a window on hole 14 never conflicts with a 9-hole event of either type. Only play events in status `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED` are considered. A turf area whose hole number is null — a practice green — shares no hole with any play event and therefore never conflicts. A conflict is `ERR_WINDOW_CONFLICTS_PLAY`.

**Irrigation run against irrigation run is half-open geometry with no buffer.** Two runs on the same pump station intersect when the half-open interval that begins at one run's start, inclusive, and ends at its end, exclusive, shares any instant with the other run's equally half-open interval. Adjacent runs that merely touch at an endpoint therefore do **not** intersect. Only runs in status `SCHEDULED` participate.

A fourth geometry appears in the weather cascade: a crew window is deferred by a weather event when the half-open interval from the window's start, inclusive, to its end, exclusive, shares an instant with the half-open interval from the event's `declared_at`, inclusive, to its `expected_clear_at`, exclusive.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Compensation

Many operations in this system mutate more than the resource they name, and the cascades run several levels deep. Three chains are worth naming up front because they recur throughout §3.

The **weather chain** runs from a declaration outward: declaring an event re-derives the course posture, then pushes back or cancels qualifying play events, then defers qualifying crew windows, then reverts any plan task each deferred window was carrying from `SCHEDULED` back to `PLANNED`, then recomputes that task's plan's committed nitrogen and its turf area's projected nitrogen load. A storm-damage declaration adds a parallel branch: it flags named turf areas out of play and cancels their non-cleanup windows, which in turn cancels the plan tasks those windows carried.

The **void chain** runs from an application record backwards into the plan: voiding an application recomputes the treated area's nitrogen ledgers and restricted-entry expiry, may suspend the recording applicator's licence, and — if the record satisfied a plan task that had already completed — reverts that task from `COMPLETED` back to `SCHEDULED`, recomputes its plan's committed nitrogen, and reopens the plan from `CLOSED` back to `ACTIVE` if it had closed.

The **water chain** runs from a run completion outward: completing a run books its volume, recomputes the station's withdrawal-today ledger, recomputes the course's used-this-year ledger, re-derives the water posture, and — if the posture moved — mirrors pump station statuses and possibly cancels every still-scheduled run on the course.

In all three chains, every resource actually mutated gets its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1, as described in §2.4. Every chain also has a compensating direction, and the compensating direction is required to unwind exactly the ledger movements the forward direction made — no more and no less. Clearing a weather event is the sole partial exception, and it is deliberate: postures and out-of-play flags are restored, but tee times that were already pushed back are **not** restored.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Course and Governing Its Water Allocation

A `Superintendent` opens the system by registering a course. The registration carries a `name` of at most 120 characters which may not be empty, a `region_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the regulatory region whose posting rules govern the property, and an `annual_water_allocation_gal` greater than zero stating the permitted annual withdrawal volume in gallons for non-exempt sources. Two optional posture thresholds may also be supplied: `water_restricted_threshold_pct`, which defaults to 80, and `water_suspended_threshold_pct`, which defaults to 100. The restricted threshold must be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than the suspended threshold, and the suspended threshold must be strictly greater than the restricted threshold. A newly registered course starts with `water_used_gal` at 0, `water_posture` at `OPEN`, `play_posture` at `OPEN`, `frost_delay_minutes` at 0, and `version` at 1.

Allocations are renegotiated with water authorities mid-season, so the `Superintendent` may revise the annual figure and either threshold afterwards; each of `annual_water_allocation_gal`, `water_restricted_threshold_pct` and `water_suspended_threshold_pct` is optional on that revision, and whatever is supplied replaces the stored value. The revision immediately re-derives the water posture using the rule in §2.10, and the posture may move in either direction — a course whose allocation is increased can relax from `SUSPENDED` back to `RESTRICTED` or all the way to `OPEN`, and a course whose allocation is cut can tighten from `OPEN` straight to `SUSPENDED`. Each posture movement carries its own consequences, which are the same consequences a run completion or reversal would produce, and are described in §3.5. The course's `version` is incremented by 1 by the revision.

Superintendents also need to know what an overage will cost. A computed read on the course returns a tiered overage assessment, and it returns no entity — the response body is the breakdown itself, comprising `billable_gal`, `base_charge`, `tier`, `surcharge_rate`, `surcharge`, and `total_charge`. **The evaluation order is pinned, step by step.** First, `billable_gal` is the amount by which the course's gallons used this year exceeds its annual allocation, or zero if it has not exceeded it — that is, the larger of zero and the difference, reported rounded half up to one decimal place while the exact difference is what feeds step five. Second, `base_charge` is the gallons used this year divided by 1000, multiplied by a base rate of 3.25 per thousand gallons, rounded half up to two decimal places. Third, the `tier` is selected **from `base_charge`, never from the final total**: a base charge below 25.00 is tier `T1`, a base charge below 100.00 but not below 25.00 is tier `T2`, and a base charge of 100.00 or more is tier `T3`. Keying the tier on the final total would be circular, because the total depends on the surcharge, which depends on the tier. Fourth, the `surcharge_rate` follows from the tier: 0.90 for `T1`, 1.40 for `T2`, and 2.10 for `T3`. Fifth, the `surcharge` is `billable_gal` divided by 1000, multiplied by the surcharge rate, rounded half up to two decimal places. Sixth, the `total_charge` is the base charge plus the surcharge, rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding happens at steps two, five and six, and nowhere else.

Consider a course that has used 7600.0 gallons against a 7000-gallon allocation. Billable gallons are 600.0. The base charge is 24.70, which is below 25.00, so the tier is `T1` and the surcharge rate is 0.90, giving a surcharge of 0.54 and a total charge of 25.24. Had the tier been keyed on the total instead, 25.24 would have selected `T2`, a rate of 1.40, a surcharge of 0.84, and a total of 25.54 — a different and non-conforming answer.

### 3.2. Mapping the Property: Turf Areas

Both a `Superintendent` and an `Agronomist` may register a maintained turf area, supplying the `course_id` it belongs to and its agronomic character. The `area_type` classification is one of `GREEN`, `TEE`, `FAIRWAY`, `ROUGH`, `APPROACH`, or `PRACTICE_GREEN`, and it drives both the mowing regime and the regulatory nutrient ceiling. A `hole_number` from 1 through 18 inclusive names the hole the area serves; it may be null only when the area type is `PRACTICE_GREEN`, and it must be null in that case, which is what makes a practice green immune to play conflicts. The `area_sq_ft` is the maintained square footage and must be greater than zero; it is simultaneously the denominator for all per-thousand-square-foot nutrient arithmetic and a multiplier in the irrigation demand calculation. The `mowing_height_mm` is the current bench setting height of cut in millimetres, greater than zero and at most 120. The `distance_to_water_ft` is the shortest distance from the area to the nearest surface water feature and must be at least zero; it is compared against product buffer requirements every time a spray is recorded. The `soil_type` rootzone classification is one of `SAND_USGA`, `NATIVE_LOAM`, or `PUSH_UP_CLAY`. Finally, an optional `target_moisture_pct` — greater than zero and strictly less than 60, defaulting to 18 — records the volumetric water content the superintendent is managing towards.

A newly registered area starts with `agronomic_status` at `ACTIVE`, both nitrogen ledgers at 0, `current_moisture_pct` null, `rei_expires_at` null, and `version` 1. The `agronomic_status` field is the play-and-renovation gate for the area and takes one of `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_RENOVATION`, or `OUT_OF_PLAY`. It is never set directly by a client: it becomes `UNDER_RENOVATION` while an aeration crew window on the area is in progress, becomes `OUT_OF_PLAY` when a storm-damage weather event names the area, and returns to `ACTIVE` when that aeration window completes or when the storm event clears. An `OUT_OF_PLAY` area refuses applications, irrigation runs, playability readings and new plan tasks with `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`, and the same refusal is re-tested when an irrigation run on the area is completed.

The `current_moisture_pct` field mirrors the latest measurement rather than an input: it is the moisture percentage of the most recent playability reading on the area, selected by the greatest `measured_at` and breaking a tie by the greatest `created_at`, and it stays null while the area has no readings at all. The `rei_expires_at` field is the area's restricted-entry gate: it is the latest per-application restricted-entry expiry across the application records on that area whose posting is still up, and it is null when no such record exists. Both nitrogen ledgers — `nitrogen_applied_lb_per_1000_ytd` and `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000` — carry three decimal places and are described in §3.3.

### 3.3. The Annual Agronomic Plan: Drafting, Task Commitment, Revision and Approval

An `Agronomist` opens an annual plan against a course by supplying the `course_id`, a `plan_year` from 2020 through 2100, and a `nitrogen_budget_lb` greater than zero — the total pounds of nitrogen the plan is permitted to commit across all of its tasks. A course may carry at most one plan for any given `plan_year`; a second attempt for the same course and year is refused as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), tested after the course's existence. A fresh plan is in `DRAFT` with `committed_nitrogen_lb` at 0, `return_count` at 0, `submitted_by_user_id` null, and `version` 1.

Tasks are added to the plan while it is in `DRAFT`, and only while it is in `DRAFT`; adding a task to a plan in any other state is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. A task carries the `plan_id` of its plan, the `turf_area_id` of the area it will be performed on, a `task_type` from `FERTILITY`, `AERATION`, `TOPDRESSING`, or `PEST_CONTROL`, and a planned execution window bounded by `planned_window_start`, which is the inclusive earliest instant the task may be executed, and `planned_window_end`, which is the exclusive latest instant and must be strictly after the start. A fertility task additionally carries `nitrogen_lb_per_1000`, the planned nitrogen rate in pounds per thousand square feet, which is required and must be greater than zero and at most 6 when the task type is `FERTILITY`, and which must be omitted or null for any other task type. Every new task starts in `PLANNED` with `version` 1. Creating a task recomputes the turf area's two nitrogen ledgers, but the area's `updated_at` and `version` move **only when one of the two figures actually changed** — so a new `FERTILITY` task bumps the area by 1 while a new `AERATION`, `TOPDRESSING` or `PEST_CONTROL` task leaves the area entirely untouched, since neither ledger admits a non-fertility contribution. The regulatory ceiling test likewise runs only for a `FERTILITY` task, being vacuous for the other three.

At creation the task's own `commitment_lb` is computed once and never recomputed. For a fertility task it is the planned per-thousand rate multiplied by the area's maintained square footage divided by 1000, rounded half up to two decimal places. For every other task type it is exactly 0.

The plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` ledger is the sum of the `commitment_lb` values of the plan's tasks whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, with the whole sum then rounded half up to two decimal places. Tasks in `PLANNED` and tasks in `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. **The rounding order is pinned: each task's commitment is rounded first, at task creation, and the plan ledger adds the already-rounded per-task values.** Summing the exact products and rounding once at the end is non-conforming and produces a different answer whenever two or more tasks each carry a half-cent remainder. To make the difference concrete: a task planning 0.85 pounds per thousand on 5500 square feet gives an exact product of 4.675, which rounds to 4.68; a task planning 0.75 pounds per thousand on 4300 square feet gives an exact product of 3.225, which rounds to 3.23; the plan's committed total is 7.91. Summing 4.675 and 3.225 first would give 7.900, which rounds to 7.90 — the wrong answer. Because every addend is already rounded to two decimals, the summation order is irrelevant and no tie-break is needed. The plan ledger is recomputed on every task status change that crosses the `SCHEDULED`-or-`COMPLETED` boundary, and also when the plan itself closes or reopens.

The turf area carries two nitrogen figures for the same regulatory quantity, and the difference between them is the difference between what is in the ground and what has been promised. **The applied-actual figure**, persisted as `nitrogen_applied_lb_per_1000_ytd` and reported when the area is read, counts only nitrogen already applied. For each application record on the area whose record status is `RECORDED` or `VERIFIED` and whose `applied_at` falls in the logical clock's calendar year, take the application's applied nitrogen mass divided by the area's square footage divided by 1000, and round that per-application figure half up to three decimal places; then add the already-rounded per-application figures and round the sum half up to three decimal places again. Voided records are excluded, which is why voiding a record moves this ledger. **The planned-commitment figure**, persisted as `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000`, starts from the applied-actual figure and adds the raw `nitrogen_lb_per_1000` rates of the area's fertility tasks whose status is `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED`, rounding the total half up to three decimal places. Completed fertility tasks are deliberately not added again, because the application records that satisfied them are already inside the applied-actual figure. The same database therefore yields a strictly larger planned figure than applied figure whenever any unexecuted fertility task exists — regulatory headroom is consumed at planning time, not at application time. Both figures are **re-derived by the two rules just given from the records that exist at the moment the area is read**, rather than being served from whatever value the last write left on the row: when the logical clock's calendar year moves, or when a fertility task on the area enters or leaves `PLANNED`-or-`SCHEDULED`, the next read of the area reports the recomputed figure, whether or not any write has touched the area in between. A read remains a read — it mutates nothing and increments no `version` — so the version increments prescribed for the writes and cascades described elsewhere are exactly as stated and are neither added to nor removed by this re-derivation.

That planned-commitment figure is the left-hand side of the regulatory nutrient ceiling. After adding a new task's or a new application's nitrogen, the area's planned-commitment figure must not exceed the ceiling for its area type: 4.00 pounds per thousand square feet for a `GREEN` and for a `PRACTICE_GREEN`, 5.00 for a `TEE` and for an `APPROACH`, 6.00 for a `FAIRWAY`, and 3.00 for a `ROUGH`. Exceeding it is `ERR_NITROGEN_LOAD_LIMIT` (HTTP 422), and the check guards both task creation and application recording.

Submission moves the plan `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. It is performed by an `Agronomist`, requires the plan to be in `DRAFT`, and requires at least one task on the plan to exist in status `PLANNED`. The caller's `X-User-ID` is captured into `submitted_by_user_id` and the plan's `version` is incremented by 1.

A `Superintendent` reviewing a `SUBMITTED` plan has two answers. Returning it moves the plan `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`, may carry an optional `return_note` of at most 2000 characters which is permitted to be empty and which the server validates but does not persist or echo, increments `return_count` by 1, clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, and increments `version`. The revision cycle is capped: `return_count` must be strictly less than 2 for a return to be permitted, so the first and second returns succeed and leave the count at 1 and then 2, while a third return attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409) and leaves the plan in `SUBMITTED`. Approving it moves the plan `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE` and increments `version`; approval requires the pinned `expected_plan_version` to equal the plan's current `version`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`, and it requires the approving caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded at submission, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Submit, return and approve each require the plan to be in the state their transition names — `DRAFT` for submit, `SUBMITTED` for return and for approve — and a plan in any other state yields `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`.

Once a plan is `ACTIVE`, its tasks may be bound to crew windows. Binding a task moves it `PLANNED → SCHEDULED` and is the point at which its nitrogen is actually committed. An `Agronomist` performs it by naming a `maintenance_window_id` and a pinned `expected_plan_version`. Seven conditions apply, and they are checked in the order the global priority list in §2.8 establishes, which is exactly the order they are listed in here: the named window must exist, else `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`; the plan must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`; the task must be in `PLANNED`, else `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` — so a task that is already `SCHEDULED` on a plan that has since `CLOSED` reports the plan error, not the task error; the named window must be in status `SCHEDULED` and must not already carry a task, else `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`; the pinned plan version must match, else `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`; the window's turf area must be the task's turf area, else `ERR_TASK_TURF_AREA_MISMATCH`; the window's interval must be contained inside the task's planned window, meaning the window starts at or after `planned_window_start` and ends at or before `planned_window_end`, else `ERR_APPLICATION_OUTSIDE_PLAN_WINDOW`; and the resulting committed total must not exceed the plan's `nitrogen_budget_lb`, else `ERR_NITROGEN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The budget comparison is inclusive, so a committed total exactly equal to the budget is allowed.

A successful binding produces four mutations. The task moves to `SCHEDULED` and its `version` increments by 1. The window records the task's identifier in its own `plan_task_id` and its `version` increments by 1; the binding is recorded on that side only — `maintenance_window_id` is a request input and not an attribute of a plan task, so the window bound to a task is discovered through `GET /maintenance-windows` filtered by `plan_task_id` as §2.9 describes. The plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` is recomputed and its `version` increments by 1. The turf area's `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000` is recomputed by the planned-commitment rule and that area's `version` increments by 1.

Tasks come back out of `SCHEDULED` by two routes and leave the lifecycle by two more. When the crew window carrying a task is deferred, the task reverts `SCHEDULED → PLANNED`, its `version` increments, its commitment leaves the plan ledger — which is recomputed with the plan's `version` incremented — and the turf area's projected load is recomputed with that area's `version` incremented. When the window carrying a task completes, the task advances `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, its `version` increments, the plan ledger is recomputed with the plan's `version` incremented (completed tasks stay in the ledger, so the figure does not change unless something else moved), and the plan's close condition is re-evaluated. When the window carrying a task is cancelled, the task moves to `CANCELLED` from either `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED`: from `PLANNED` the turf area's projected load is recomputed and the area's `version` increments, the task's `version` increments, and the plan's close condition is re-evaluated; from `SCHEDULED` the same happens and in addition the plan ledger is recomputed with the plan's `version` incremented, because the commitment leaves the ledger. And when an application record that satisfied a completed task is voided, the task reverts `COMPLETED → SCHEDULED`, its `version` increments, and the plan ledger is recomputed with the plan's `version` incremented.

The plan's own terminal state is derived, not commanded. A plan in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED` when at least one task exists on it and every one of its tasks is in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`; the close recomputes `committed_nitrogen_lb` and increments the plan's `version` by 1, and it is re-evaluated after any task of the plan reaches `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. The reverse also exists: a plan in `CLOSED` moves `CLOSED → ACTIVE` when at least one of its tasks has left the terminal set and now sits in `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED`; the reopen likewise recomputes `committed_nitrogen_lb` and increments the plan's `version` by 1. The reopen condition is re-evaluated after **every** route by which a task re-enters `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED` — an application void reverting a `COMPLETED` task, a crew-window deferral reverting a `SCHEDULED` task to `PLANNED`, and a reschedule advancing a `PLANNED` task back to `SCHEDULED` — not only after a void.

### 3.4. Licensing, Product Labels, and the Application Record

Before anything can be sprayed, two registries must be populated, and both are owned by the `Superintendent`.

An **applicator** is registered with a `full_name` of at most 120 characters which may not be empty, a `license_number` of at most 120 characters which may not be empty and which must be globally unique across all applicators, compared as an exact case-sensitive string — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_NUMBER` (HTTP 409) — a `license_category` of `CAT_3_TURF`, `CAT_7_AQUATIC`, or `CAT_3_AND_7`, and a `license_expires_on` calendar date in ISO 8601 date form. The licence remains usable through the whole of the expiry date, not up to its start. The identifier returned by registration is the value a caller presents in `X-User-ID` when acting as `Applicator`. A new applicator record carries `suspended_at` null and `version` 1, and its `license_status` is derived rather than supplied: it reads `ACTIVE` while the logical clock's calendar date is at or before `license_expires_on` and no suspension has been recorded, reads `EXPIRED` once the logical clock's calendar date is after `license_expires_on`, and reads `SUSPENDED` once any application record made by that applicator has been voided for a safety violation. `SUSPENDED` outranks `EXPIRED`: a suspended applicator whose licence has also lapsed reads `SUSPENDED`.

A **pesticide product** is a label transcription and is immutable once written. It carries a `trade_name` of at most 120 characters which may not be empty, an `epa_reg_no` registration number of at most 120 characters which may not be empty and which is *not* required to be unique, a `requires_license_category` of either `CAT_3_TURF` or `CAT_7_AQUATIC` naming the category an applicator must hold to apply it, a `rei_hours` restricted-entry interval from 0 through 336 hours during which the treated area is closed to maintenance entry, a `min_buffer_ft` minimum permitted distance from surface water of at least zero, a `max_wind_speed_mph` ceiling greater than zero, a `posting_required` boolean stating whether a re-entry notice must be posted at the treated area, and an optional `nitrogen_pct` nitrogen content by weight from 0 through 100 which defaults to 0. As noted in §2.4, a product record carries only `id` and `created_at` — no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no way to amend it.

Recording an application is the most heavily gated write in the system, and only an `Applicator` may do it. The payload names the `turf_area_id` of the treated area, the `product_id` of the product applied, the `applicator_id` of the licensed applicator who performed the work, an optional `plan_task_id` naming the plan task the application satisfies (null for unplanned work), the `applied_at` instant the application was made, the `rate_lb_per_acre` product rate greater than zero, the `wind_speed_mph` observed at the time of application which must be at least zero, and the `distance_to_water_ft` observed from the treated swath to surface water which must also be at least zero.

The gauntlet runs in the global priority order, and its first substantive rung is a state test rather than a licence test: if a `plan_task_id` is cited, that task must be in status `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — which, sitting at priority 18, is reported ahead of every licence, wind, buffer, nitrogen, posture and containment failure the same request may also carry. Then the recording applicator's licence must cover the work: the calendar date of `applied_at` must be at or before that applicator's `license_expires_on`, and the applicator must not be suspended; a failure of either is `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Because the licence is good through the whole of the expiry date, an `applied_at` falling on the expiry date passes and one falling on the following day fails. Next, the observed `wind_speed_mph` must not exceed the product's `max_wind_speed_mph`; the comparison is inclusive, so wind exactly at the label limit is permitted, and anything above it is `ERR_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Next comes the buffer zone: take the smaller of the application's observed `distance_to_water_ft` and the treated area's own recorded `distance_to_water_ft`, and require it to be greater than or equal to the product's `min_buffer_ft`; the comparison is inclusive, so a distance exactly equal to the buffer is permitted, and anything less is `ERR_BUFFER_ZONE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). Then the nutrient ceiling of §3.3 is applied to the area's planned-commitment nitrogen figure, yielding `ERR_NITROGEN_LOAD_LIMIT` if it would be breached. A `CLOSED` course play posture blocks the record with `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`, and an `OUT_OF_PLAY` turf area blocks it with `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`.

Licence **category** authority is then checked, and it is a distinct rule from licence validity: `CAT_3_AND_7` authorises both `CAT_3_TURF` and `CAT_7_AQUATIC` products, `CAT_3_TURF` authorises only `CAT_3_TURF` products, and `CAT_7_AQUATIC` authorises only `CAT_7_AQUATIC` products. A mismatch is `ERR_LICENSE_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Finally, if a `plan_task_id` is cited, the two remaining cited-task conditions apply — the task's status having already been settled at the head of the gauntlet: the task's turf area must be the application's turf area, else `ERR_TASK_TURF_AREA_MISMATCH`; and `applied_at` must fall inside the task's planned window — at or after `planned_window_start` and strictly before `planned_window_end` — and inside the plan's calendar year, else `ERR_APPLICATION_OUTSIDE_PLAN_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

A record that survives the gauntlet computes three derived values, **and the rounding order is pinned**. First, the treated acreage is the area's `area_sq_ft` divided by 43560 square feet per acre. Second, `total_lb` is the `rate_lb_per_acre` multiplied by that acreage, rounded half up to two decimal places. Third, `nitrogen_lb` is the **already-rounded** `total_lb` multiplied by the product's `nitrogen_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places; computing it from the exact unrounded product instead is non-conforming. Fourth, the record's own `rei_expires_at` is `applied_at` advanced by the product's `rei_hours` hours. For a worked illustration, a 5500-square-foot area sprayed at 200 pounds per acre gives an acreage of 0.126262626…, a `total_lb` of 25.25, and — with a product at 46 percent nitrogen — a `nitrogen_lb` of 11.62 obtained by rounding 11.615 half up. The per-thousand figure that reaches the turf area is derived from the pinned 11.62, not from an unrounded intermediate.

An application record carries two independent status dimensions, and neither ever gates the other. The **record dimension** `record_status` is one of `RECORDED`, `VERIFIED`, or `VOIDED`, and it starts at `RECORDED`. The **posting dimension** `posting_status` is one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `POSTED`, or `RELEASED`; at creation it is `NOT_REQUIRED` when the product's `posting_required` is false and `POSTED` when it is true. A record in `RECORDED` may have its posting released, and a record in `VERIFIED` may still be `POSTED` — the dimensions are orthogonal. A new record has `verified_by_applicator_id` null, `void_reason` null, and `version` 1.

Creation also touches the treated area: both of its nitrogen ledgers are recomputed by the rules in §3.3, its `rei_expires_at` is recomputed when the new record's posting is `POSTED`, and the area's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Verification** is a second-applicator control and moves the record `RECORDED → VERIFIED`. Only an `Applicator` may perform it, the record must be in `RECORDED` (a record already `VERIFIED` or `VOIDED` yields `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`), and the caller's `X-User-ID` must not equal the record's `applicator_id` — the person who sprayed may never be the person who signs it off, and an attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The verifying applicator, resolved from `X-User-ID`, is themselves subject to the licence tests: their `license_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`, and their `license_category` must authorise the product's `requires_license_category` by the same authority table, else `ERR_LICENSE_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. On success the record's `verified_by_applicator_id` is set to the verifying caller's `X-User-ID` and the record's `version` is incremented by 1. Verification mutates nothing else: because a `RECORDED` record and a `VERIFIED` record contribute identically to the applied-actual ledger, the treated area's nitrogen figures are recomputed and found unchanged, so the area's `updated_at` and `version` do **not** move.

**Releasing a posting** moves the posting dimension `POSTED → RELEASED` and is performed by an `Applicator`. The posting dimension must be `POSTED`, else `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`; and the logical clock must be at or after that record's own `rei_expires_at`, else `ERR_REI_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The comparison is inclusive, so releasing at exactly the expiry instant is permitted. On success the record's `version` is incremented by 1, and the treated area's `rei_expires_at` is recomputed over whatever application records on that area are still `POSTED`, incrementing that area's `version` by 1. The record dimension is untouched by a release.

**Voiding** is reserved to the `Superintendent` and moves the record dimension to `VOIDED` from either `RECORDED` or `VERIFIED` — `RECORDED → VOIDED` and `VERIFIED → VOIDED` are both permitted and behave identically; a record that is already `VOIDED` yields `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. The caller supplies a `void_reason` of either `DATA_ENTRY_ERROR` or `SAFETY_VIOLATION`, which is stored on the record. The side effects are identical from either origin state and they are the deepest cascade in the system. The record's `record_status` becomes `VOIDED`, its `posting_status` becomes `RELEASED` if it was `POSTED` (a `NOT_REQUIRED` or already-`RELEASED` posting is left alone), and its `version` is incremented by 1. The treated area's `nitrogen_applied_lb_per_1000_ytd` and `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000` are both recomputed — the voided record leaves the applied set — its `rei_expires_at` is recomputed, and that area's `version` is incremented by 1. When the reason is `SAFETY_VIOLATION`, the recording applicator's `license_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, their `suspended_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and that applicator's `version` is incremented by 1; the stamp is written unconditionally, so voiding a second record for safety re-stamps an already-suspended applicator with the current clock reading and bumps their `version` again. A `DATA_ENTRY_ERROR` void leaves the licence alone. And when the voided record cited a `plan_task_id` whose task had already reached `COMPLETED`, that task reverts `COMPLETED → SCHEDULED`, its plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` is recomputed, and a plan that had reached `CLOSED` reopens `CLOSED → ACTIVE`, with each of those resources' `version` incremented by 1 in turn.

The application collection is filterable on all five of its foreign keys — `turf_area_id`, `product_id`, `applicator_id`, `plan_task_id` and `verified_by_applicator_id` — and on both status dimensions via `record_status` and `posting_status`. The applicator collection is filterable on `license_category` and `license_status`, and the product collection on `requires_license_category` and `posting_required`.

### 3.5. Irrigation: Pump Stations, Allotment, and the Two Water Ledgers

A `Superintendent` registers a pump station against a `course_id` with a `source_type` of `WELL`, `POND`, or `RECLAIMED`, a `max_flow_gpm` maximum instantaneous output greater than zero, a `max_concurrent_zones` count of at least 1 bounding how many runs may occupy overlapping windows on the station, and a `daily_withdrawal_cap_gal` greater than zero bounding what the station may draw on a single calendar day. The source type matters for one reason: `RECLAIMED` sources are exempt from the annual allocation check. A new station has `withdrawn_today_gal` at 0 and `version` 1, and is created `ONLINE` — except that a station registered on a course whose water posture is already `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` is created `RESTRICTED` instead.

A station's `status` is one of `ONLINE`, `RESTRICTED`, or `OFFLINE`, and the three values reach it by different routes. `RESTRICTED` is never commanded: the server mirrors it automatically onto every station of a course whenever that course's water posture is `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`, and mirrors the station back to `ONLINE` when the posture returns to `OPEN`. `OFFLINE` is only ever set explicitly, by a `Superintendent` or an `EquipmentTechnician` supplying a `status` on the station-status operation, and it survives posture changes: a station taken offline for repair stays offline through any number of posture movements until somebody sets it back. That operation takes a required `status` whose declared members are all three of `ONLINE`, `RESTRICTED` and `OFFLINE`, and whatever is supplied is stored verbatim: no state-machine legality test guards it, so any value may follow any other — including setting a station back to `ONLINE` on a course whose water posture is still `RESTRICTED`, which the server does not re-mirror and does not refuse. The station's `version` is incremented by 1 even when the supplied status equals the one already stored. Taking a station `OFFLINE` immediately transitions every run on that station in status `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, zeroing each cancelled run's withdrawn volume and incrementing each run's `version` by 1; the station's own `version` is incremented by 1 by the status change.

An `Agronomist` schedules an irrigation run by naming the `pump_station_id` supplying it, the `turf_area_id` being irrigated, a `window_start` inclusive start and `window_end` exclusive end whose duration must be a whole number of minutes from 1 through 720, a `target_moisture_deficit_pct` volumetric deficit to replace from 0 through 60 percentage points, and the pinned `expected_pump_station_version`. A new run starts in `SCHEDULED` with `volume_gal` at 0 and `version` 1.

The run's authorised volume is a **constraint-propagation** result in which the binding constraint changes with state. The agronomic demand, in gallons, is the `target_moisture_deficit_pct` multiplied by the area's `area_sq_ft` divided by 1000, multiplied by 6.2 gallons per percentage point per thousand square feet. That demand is then scaled by a posture factor read off the course's water posture: 1.00 when the posture is `OPEN`, 0.60 when it is `RESTRICTED`, and 0.00 when it is `SUSPENDED`. Separately, the station's remaining daily headroom is its `daily_withdrawal_cap_gal` less its `withdrawn_today_gal`, floored at zero so it never goes negative. The run's `allotted_gal` is the **smaller** of the posture-scaled demand and the remaining daily headroom, rounded half up to one decimal place. Its `flow_gpm` is `allotted_gal` divided by the window duration in minutes, rounded half up to two decimal places. As an illustration, a 10-percentage-point deficit on a 130000-square-foot area gives a demand of 8060.0 gallons; at an `OPEN` posture the factor is 1.00; if the station's cap is 7600 with nothing withdrawn today, the headroom binds instead of the demand, so `allotted_gal` is 7600.0 and, over a 60-minute window, `flow_gpm` is the result of rounding 126.666… to 126.67.

The annual allocation is deliberately **not** part of that minimum. It is enforced as a separate check: for a station whose `source_type` is not `RECLAIMED`, the course's `water_used_gal` plus the new run's `allotted_gal` must not exceed the course's `annual_water_allocation_gal`, and a breach is `ERR_WATER_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). For a `RECLAIMED` station the check is skipped entirely.

Four more conditions guard run creation, in the global priority order. A `CLOSED` course play posture refuses the run with `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`, and an `OUT_OF_PLAY` turf area refuses it with `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`. The station must not be `OFFLINE`, else `ERR_PUMP_OFFLINE` (HTTP 409); both `ONLINE` and `RESTRICTED` stations admit runs, since a restricted station simply irrigates at the reduced posture factor. Zone capacity is next: count the station's runs in status `SCHEDULED` whose half-open intervals intersect the new run's half-open interval per §2.11, add one for the new run, and require the result not to exceed `max_concurrent_zones`, else `ERR_PUMP_ZONE_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409); adjacent runs that merely touch endpoints are not counted. Flow capacity uses exactly that same intersecting set: add the `flow_gpm` values of those intersecting scheduled runs to the new run's `flow_gpm` and require the total not to exceed the station's `max_flow_gpm`, else `ERR_PUMP_FLOW_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409). Last, the computed `allotted_gal` must be strictly greater than zero, else `ERR_ZERO_ALLOTMENT` (HTTP 422) — which is what a `SUSPENDED` posture, a zero deficit, or an exhausted daily headroom each collapse into.

**Completing** a run is an `Agronomist` action and moves it `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. The run must be in `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); then, in the global priority order, the irrigated area must not be `OUT_OF_PLAY`, else `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY` (rule 34); and only then must the supplying station not be `OFFLINE`, else `ERR_PUMP_OFFLINE` (rule 43) — so a run whose area is out of play on an offline station reports the out-of-play error. The caller may supply an optional `actual_minutes`, a whole number of at least 0 with no upper bound of its own; when it is omitted the planned duration in minutes is used, an `actual_minutes` of 0 books a volume of 0.0, and an `actual_minutes` larger than the planned duration is accepted and scales the volume above `allotted_gal`. The run's `volume_gal` becomes `allotted_gal` multiplied by `actual_minutes` divided by the planned duration in minutes, rounded half up to one decimal place, and the run's `version` is incremented by 1. Then the two ledgers move. The station's `withdrawn_today_gal` is recomputed as the sum of `volume_gal` over that station's runs in status `COMPLETED` whose `window_start` calendar date equals the logical clock's calendar date, and the station's `version` is incremented by 1. The course's `water_used_gal` is recomputed as the sum of `volume_gal` over every run in status exactly `COMPLETED` whose supplying station belongs to that course — scheduled, cancelled and reversed runs contribute nothing — the water posture is re-derived from the new figure, and the course's `version` is incremented by 1.

Re-derivation is where the chain continues, and the two consequences are keyed on the **destination** posture rather than on the particular pair of values, so they apply to every move into that destination. A posture whose new value is `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` sets every station of that course whose status is `ONLINE` to `RESTRICTED`, incrementing each of those stations' `version` by 1; stations already `RESTRICTED` or `OFFLINE` are left exactly as they are. A posture whose new value is `SUSPENDED` additionally transitions every run in status `SCHEDULED` on that course's stations to `CANCELLED`, zeroing each cancelled run's `volume_gal` and incrementing each run's `version` by 1. A posture whose new value is `OPEN` sets every station of that course whose status is `RESTRICTED` back to `ONLINE`, incrementing each of those stations' `version` by 1, and leaves `OFFLINE` stations offline. So `OPEN → RESTRICTED` mirrors only; `OPEN → SUSPENDED` and `RESTRICTED → SUSPENDED` both mirror and cancel; `RESTRICTED → OPEN` and `SUSPENDED → OPEN` restore; and `SUSPENDED → RESTRICTED` changes the posture, increments the course's `version` and mirrors any station that had somehow returned to `ONLINE`, cancelling nothing. Each posture value change increments the course's `version` by 1, and a re-derivation that lands on the value the course already carried is a no-op: no station and no run is touched, and the only `version` movement is the one the ledger recomputation itself already made. Because §2.4 counts each resource once per request, a station that was already version-bumped earlier in the same request — for instance the station supplying the run being completed — is not bumped a second time by the mirror.

**Reversing** a completed run is the compensating half of the water invariant and is reserved to the `Superintendent`. The run must be in `COMPLETED`, else `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. An optional `reversal_note` of at most 2000 characters, permitted to be empty, may accompany the request; the server validates it but neither persists nor echoes it. The run's `volume_gal` returns to 0, its status moves `COMPLETED → REVERSED`, and its `version` is incremented by 1; the station's `withdrawn_today_gal` is recomputed with the station's `version` incremented by 1; and the course's `water_used_gal` is recomputed and its water posture re-derived with the course's `version` incremented by 1. Because the recomputed figure is smaller, the re-derivation here typically relaxes rather than tightens — `SUSPENDED → RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED → OPEN`, or `RESTRICTED → OPEN` — restoring restricted stations to `ONLINE`. A run's status is therefore one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `REVERSED`, or `CANCELLED`, with the last two terminal.

### 3.6. Crew Windows: Booking Around Play, Deferring, and Getting Work Done

A **maintenance window** is a block of time during which a crew occupies a turf area. An `Agronomist` books one by naming the `course_id` the crew works on, the `turf_area_id` the crew occupies, a `task_type` from `FERTILITY`, `AERATION`, `TOPDRESSING`, `PEST_CONTROL`, `MOWING`, or `STORM_CLEANUP`, a `window_start` and `window_end` whose duration must be a whole number of minutes from 1 through 600, a `crew_size` from 1 through 40 technicians, and an optional `required_unit_type` naming the machine class the window depends on — one of `TRIPLEX_GREENS_MOWER`, `FAIRWAY_MOWER`, `SPRAYER`, `AERATOR`, or `TOPDRESSER` — which may be null when no specific machine is needed. `STORM_CLEANUP` is the one task class permitted on an out-of-play area or a closed course, and that exemption recurs throughout this subsection.

The booking payload additionally *accepts* an optional `plan_task_id`, whose well-formedness and existence are validated — a value naming no task is `ERR_PLAN_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, per rule 7 of §2.8 — but whose value is then discarded: a newly booked window is `SCHEDULED` with `plan_task_id` **null regardless of what was supplied**, `deferral_count` at 0, and `version` 1. Binding a task to a window happens only through `POST /plan-tasks/{id}/schedule`. Its `crew_hours` figure is derived: the `crew_size` multiplied by the window duration expressed in hours, rounded half up to two decimal places. A 90-minute window with a crew of 5 therefore carries 7.50 crew hours.

Booking runs one chemical and three geometric checks, and the global priority order of §2.8 puts them in this sequence. First, a restricted-entry interval still running on the area blocks the booking: unless the task class is `STORM_CLEANUP`, `window_start` must be at or after the area's `rei_expires_at`, and a violation is `ERR_REI_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The comparison is inclusive, and the whole rule is vacuous when the area's `rei_expires_at` is null. Second, the window's turf area must belong to the course named on the window, else `ERR_TASK_TURF_AREA_MISMATCH`. Third, the window must not collide with another live window on the same turf area, using the closed-interval geometry of §2.11 — touching endpoints conflict — against windows in `SCHEDULED`, `DEFERRED`, or `IN_PROGRESS`; a collision is `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Fourth, the window must not collide with play on its hole, using the half-open geometry with the 30-minute clearance buffer and the 12-minute hole pace of §2.11 against play events in `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED`; a collision is `ERR_WINDOW_CONFLICTS_PLAY`. Booking runs no posture check of any kind: neither a `CLOSED` play posture nor an `OUT_OF_PLAY` turf area prevents a window from being booked, because both are re-tested at the start instant instead.

**Starting** a window puts the crew on the ground and moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. An `Agronomist` performs it; the window must be in `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. Three conditions are re-tested at the actual start instant rather than at booking time, because the world moves between the two, and the global priority order runs them chemical gate first. The restricted-entry gate is applied again in its start-time form: either the area's `rei_expires_at` is null, or the logical clock is at or after it, else `ERR_REI_ACTIVE`; the comparison is again inclusive and `STORM_CLEANUP` is again exempt. Then the course's play posture must not be `CLOSED` unless the task class is `STORM_CLEANUP`, else `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`. Then the turf area's `agronomic_status` must not be `OUT_OF_PLAY` unless the task class is `STORM_CLEANUP`, else `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`. On success the window's `version` is incremented by 1, and when the task class is `AERATION` the turf area's `agronomic_status` becomes `UNDER_RENOVATION` with that area's `version` incremented by 1 — unless the area is already `UNDER_RENOVATION`, in which case nothing is written and the area is not touched or versioned at all.

**Completing** a window moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`; the window must be in `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. The window's `version` is incremented by 1. When the task class is `AERATION` and the area is currently `UNDER_RENOVATION`, the area's `agronomic_status` returns to `ACTIVE` and that area's `version` is incremented by 1. And when the window was carrying a plan task, that task advances `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, which recomputes its plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` and re-evaluates the plan's `ACTIVE → CLOSED` close condition, each mutated resource taking its own `version` increment.

**Deferral** is never requested by a client; it is how the system absorbs disruption. A window in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → DEFERRED` under two triggers. The weather trigger fires when a frost, lightning, or heavy-rain event is declared on the same course and the window's half-open interval intersects the event's half-open interval from `declared_at` to `expected_clear_at`. The equipment trigger fires when a unit is retired whose `unit_type` equals the window's `required_unit_type` and no other unit of that type on that course remains `IN_SERVICE`. Either way the cap applies: `deferral_count` must be strictly less than 2 for the window to defer, and a window whose `deferral_count` already equals 2 is **cancelled instead of deferred**. A successful deferral sets the status to `DEFERRED`, increments `deferral_count` by 1, and increments the window's `version` by 1; when the window was carrying a plan task whose status is `SCHEDULED`, that task reverts `SCHEDULED → PLANNED`, which recomputes the plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` and the turf area's `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000`; and when the trigger was a weather declaration, that event's `deferred_window_count` is incremented by 1 — and only then, so a window that was **cancelled instead of deferred** because its cap was exhausted contributes nothing to `deferred_window_count`, and neither does a window deferred by the equipment trigger, which has no event to count against. Only a window in `SCHEDULED` is a deferral candidate at all: a window already `DEFERRED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is passed over by both triggers, so a second weather declaration never re-defers and never cancels an already-deferred window.

**Rescheduling** is the compensating half of the deferral cycle and is performed by an `Agronomist`. It supplies a new `window_start` and `window_end` and may optionally supply a new `crew_size`. A window in `SCHEDULED` or `DEFERRED` may be rescheduled; a `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` window may not, and the attempt is `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. The new interval must pass the checks that govern a fresh booking and two posture checks besides, in the global priority order: restricted-entry interval (`ERR_REI_ACTIVE`, compared against the *new* `window_start`), closed course (`ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`), out-of-play area (`ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`), window overlap (`ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, with this window excluded from its own test), and play conflict (`ERR_WINDOW_CONFLICTS_PLAY`) — a `STORM_CLEANUP` window being exempt from the first three exactly as at booking and at start. Only after those does the deferral cap apply: for a reschedule out of `DEFERRED`, `deferral_count` must be strictly less than 2, and a window that has already exhausted its two deferrals is refused with `ERR_DEFERRAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409); a window still in `SCHEDULED` is not cap-tested at all, whatever its `deferral_count`. Last, when the window is carrying a plan task, the owning plan must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` — evaluated here, after the cap, rather than at its usual priority — and the ledger must still satisfy the plan's `nitrogen_budget_lb` inclusively, else `ERR_NITROGEN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`; the ledger tested is the plan's current `committed_nitrogen_lb` with that task's `commitment_lb` added back and the sum re-rounded to two decimals **only when the task currently sits in `PLANNED`**, and the plan's committed figure unchanged when the task is in any other status. On success the window's `window_start` and `window_end` are replaced, `crew_hours` is recomputed from the new duration and crew size, the status moves `DEFERRED → SCHEDULED` (or remains `SCHEDULED` when the window had never been deferred), and the window's `version` is incremented by 1; `deferral_count` is not decremented. When the window carries a plan task that is currently `PLANNED`, that task advances `PLANNED → SCHEDULED`, restoring its `commitment_lb` to both the plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` and the turf area's `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000`.

**Cancellation** of a window is likewise never client-requested. A storm-damage declaration cancels every window — `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and `DEFERRED → CANCELLED` are both reachable — whose turf area is named in the event's affected-area list and whose task class is not `STORM_CLEANUP`. The window's status becomes `CANCELLED`, its `plan_task_id` is cleared to null, and its `version` is incremented by 1; any plan task the window had been carrying transitions to `CANCELLED` from whichever of `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED` it occupied, so both `PLANNED → CANCELLED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` are reachable for a task. A window's status is therefore one of `SCHEDULED`, `DEFERRED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, or `CANCELLED`, with the last two terminal.

### 3.7. Play Events, Frost Release, and Certifying Playing Conditions

A `TournamentOfficial` or a `Superintendent` posts a round against a `course_id` with an `event_type` of `STANDARD_TEE_TIMES`, `SHOTGUN_START`, or `TOURNAMENT_ROUND`, a `start_at` posted first-tee instant, an `end_at` exclusive end of play strictly after the start, a `holes_in_play` count of exactly 9 or exactly 18 naming how many consecutive holes beginning at hole 1 the event occupies, and a `field_size` from 1 through 288 players. The event types differ in how they consume the golf course, and that difference drives everything else in this subsection: `STANDARD_TEE_TIMES` and `TOURNAMENT_ROUND` send groups off hole 1 in sequence, so they occupy each hole only for a pace-of-play slice; `SHOTGUN_START` starts every group on its own hole at the same posted instant, so it occupies every hole in play for the whole interval. A newly posted event is `SCHEDULED` on the schedule dimension and `PENDING` on the readiness dimension, with `delayed_minutes` at 0, `effective_start_at` equal to `start_at`, `readiness_index` null, and `version` 1.

The two dimensions are independent and never gate each other. The **schedule dimension** `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED`, or `CANCELLED`, and it is driven entirely by weather. The **readiness dimension** `readiness_status` is one of `PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, or `FAILED`, and it is driven entirely by certification; it is cyclic, because a failed certification may be re-attempted.

**Frost pushback** is the frost branch of a weather declaration and it is order-sensitive. The candidates are the play events on the declaring course whose schedule status is `SCHEDULED` and whose `start_at` is **strictly** before the event's `expected_clear_at`; an event whose `start_at` equals `expected_clear_at` is untouched, keeping `SCHEDULED` and a `delayed_minutes` of 0, and events already `DELAYED` or `CANCELLED` are never reprocessed. The candidates are then processed in a pinned order — ascending `start_at`, breaking ties by ascending `created_at` and any remaining tie by earlier insertion order; the random `id` never participates in the ordering. The processing carries one piece of state, the effective start of the most recent sequential event released so far, which begins as nothing. For each candidate, the naive release is its `start_at` advanced by the event's `delay_minutes`. If the candidate is a `SHOTGUN_START`, its `effective_start_at` is exactly that naive release: a simultaneous start puts every group on the course at one instant, so it needs no separation from the group ahead of it, **and it does not push the group behind it** — it leaves the carried effective-start state unchanged. Otherwise, its `effective_start_at` is the naive release when no sequential event has yet been released, and otherwise the later of the naive release and the previous sequential effective start advanced by a minimum separation of 10 minutes; the candidate then becomes the carried previous sequential effective start. In both cases the candidate's `delayed_minutes` becomes the whole number of minutes between its `start_at` and its `effective_start_at`, and its schedule status becomes `DELAYED` with its `version` incremented by 1.

Worked through: with a frost `delay_minutes` of 45 and three events at 13:00 (`STANDARD_TEE_TIMES`), 13:05 (`STANDARD_TEE_TIMES`), and 13:20 (`SHOTGUN_START`), the first releases at 13:45 and is delayed 45 minutes; the second's naive release of 13:50 is earlier than the separation floor of 13:55, so it releases at 13:55 and is delayed 50 minutes — the separation rule, not the frost, sets it; and the shotgun releases at 14:05, delayed 45 minutes, exempt from separation and leaving the carried previous sequential effective start at 13:55.

**Cancellation by weather** applies to lightning and storm-damage declarations. A play event in `SCHEDULED` on the declaring course whose `start_at` is strictly before the event's `expected_clear_at` transitions `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. A play event already in `DELAYED` transitions `DELAYED → CANCELLED` under the same declarations, but the comparison uses its pushed-back time: its `effective_start_at` must be strictly before the event's `expected_clear_at`. Either way the event's `version` is incremented by 1. `CANCELLED` is the schedule dimension's only terminal state.

**Certifying playing conditions** is exclusive to a `TournamentOfficial` and produces a composite score. Playability measurements come in first: an `Agronomist` or a `TournamentOfficial` records a reading against a `turf_area_id` with a `measured_at` instant that may not be after the current logical clock reading — a future measurement is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), settled before the area is even looked up — a `green_speed_ft` Stimpmeter reading greater than 0 and at most 16, a `firmness_gravities` surface firmness greater than 0 and at most 4, and a `moisture_pct` volumetric water content from 0 through 60. Readings are append-only and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `version`. Each reading computes a `within_tournament_spec` boolean, which is true when the absolute difference between `green_speed_ft` and the tournament target of 11.0 feet is at most the speed tolerance of 1.5 feet **and** the absolute difference between `firmness_gravities` and the tournament target of 1.35 gravities is at most the firmness tolerance of 0.30 gravities, both comparisons inclusive; moisture does not participate in that flag. Recording a reading also re-derives the turf area's `current_moisture_pct` from the newest reading the area holds — greatest `measured_at`, ties broken by greatest `created_at` and then by later insertion — and increments that area's `version` by 1. A back-dated reading therefore leaves `current_moisture_pct` alone because a newer measurement still wins, yet the area's `version` is bumped all the same: the bump is unconditional on a successful reading, not contingent on the moisture figure having moved. A reading may not be recorded on an `OUT_OF_PLAY` area, which is `ERR_TURF_AREA_OUT_OF_PLAY`.

Certification then reads those measurements through a **24-hour readiness window** that runs from 24 hours before the event's posted `start_at` up to the posted `start_at`, with both endpoints inclusive. The participating areas are the turf areas of the event's course whose `area_type` is `GREEN`, whose `agronomic_status` is `ACTIVE`, and which carry at least one reading inside that window; greens that are `UNDER_RENOVATION` or `OUT_OF_PLAY` are excluded from both the count and the average. At least 3 such greens must qualify, else the attempt is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_READINGS` (HTTP 422). For each qualifying green, exactly one reading enters the average: the one inside the window with the greatest `measured_at`, breaking a tie by the greatest `created_at` and any remaining tie by later insertion order.

Each participating green then yields three sub-scores, each of which starts at 100 and is reduced by the reading's proportional deviation from target, with the deviation capped so no sub-score falls below zero. The speed sub-score is 100 less the smaller of one and the absolute difference between `green_speed_ft` and 11.0 feet divided by 1.5 feet, that smaller value multiplied by 100. The firmness sub-score is 100 less the smaller of one and the absolute difference between `firmness_gravities` and 1.35 gravities divided by 0.30 gravities, that smaller value multiplied by 100. The moisture sub-score is 100 less the smaller of one and the absolute difference between `moisture_pct` and 18.0 percent divided by 6.0 percentage points, that smaller value multiplied by 100. The green's own index is the speed sub-score weighted at 0.50 plus the firmness sub-score weighted at 0.30 plus the moisture sub-score weighted at 0.20, rounded half up to two decimal places. **The per-green rounding happens first**; the event's `readiness_index` is then the sum of the already-rounded per-green indices divided by the number of participating greens, rounded half up to two decimal places.

A `readiness_index` of at least 82.00 sets `readiness_status` to `CERTIFIED`; anything below it sets `readiness_status` to `FAILED`. From a fresh event both `PENDING → CERTIFIED` and `PENDING → FAILED` are therefore reachable on the first attempt. Either outcome stores the index and increments the event's `version` by 1. Three greens reading (11.5 feet, 1.40 gravities, 19.0 percent), (11.0, 1.35, 18.0), and (10.4, 1.20, 24.0) yield per-green indices of 75.00, 100.00, and 45.00; the average of 220.00 over 3 is 73.33, which is below the threshold, so the event fails. The readiness dimension never gates certification: an attempt is admissible from `PENDING`, from `FAILED` **and from `CERTIFIED`**, which makes the dimension fully cyclic. After a failure, crews re-prepare the greens, fresh readings are recorded, and certification is re-attempted, moving `FAILED → CERTIFIED` if the recomputed index now reaches 82.00 and moving `FAILED → FAILED` if it does not; equally, a re-attempt on an already-`CERTIFIED` event recomputes from scratch and may move it `CERTIFIED → CERTIFIED` or demote it `CERTIFIED → FAILED`. Every admissible attempt replaces the stored `readiness_index` with the new value and increments `version` by 1, whichever outcome it lands on. Two conditions block the attempt outright: a play event whose schedule status is `CANCELLED` cannot be certified and yields `ERR_PLAY_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and a course whose play posture is `CLOSED` refuses certification with `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`. The play-event collection is filterable on `course_id`, `event_type`, `status`, and `readiness_status`.

### 3.8. The Equipment Fleet: Meter Hours, Service, Replacement Scoring, and Retirement

A `Superintendent` or an `EquipmentTechnician` adds a machine to the fleet against a `course_id`, with a `unit_type` from `TRIPLEX_GREENS_MOWER`, `FAIRWAY_MOWER`, `SPRAYER`, `AERATOR`, or `TOPDRESSER`, a `model_year` from 1990 through 2100, a `purchase_cost` in dollars greater than zero, an `expected_life_hours` design service life greater than zero, a `service_interval_hours` figure greater than zero giving the engine hours between scheduled services, an `avg_daily_hours` figure of at least zero giving the average engine hours accrued per calendar day, and an optional `initial_engine_hours` of at least zero recording what the meter read when the unit entered the fleet, defaulting to 0. Whatever value arrives is rounded half up to one decimal place before anything is stored, and that rounded figure — not the submitted one — is what seeds the meter and is echoed back as `initial_engine_hours`. A new unit is `IN_SERVICE` with `engine_hours` and `engine_hours_at_last_service` both equal to `initial_engine_hours`, `hours_since_service` at 0, `replaced_by_unit_id` null, and `version` 1; both replacement scores and the replacement flag are computed at intake.

`engine_hours` is `initial_engine_hours` plus the sum of every accepted hours entry on the unit, and `hours_since_service` is `engine_hours` less `engine_hours_at_last_service`; both carry one decimal place. `engine_hours_at_last_service` is the meter reading as of the most recent completed service and equals `initial_engine_hours` until the first service.

**Logging hours** is exclusive to an `EquipmentTechnician`, who supplies a `hours_added` figure strictly greater than zero. A unit that is `IN_SERVICE` or `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE` accepts the entry; a `RETIRED` unit refuses it with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The entry raises `engine_hours` and therefore `hours_since_service`, recomputes both replacement scores and the replacement flag, and increments the unit's `version` by 1. **In the same request**, an automatic transition is then evaluated: a unit whose status is `IN_SERVICE` and whose `hours_since_service` has reached or passed its `service_interval_hours` moves `IN_SERVICE → DOWN_FOR_SERVICE`. The threshold is inclusive — hours exactly equal to the interval takes the unit down — and the auto-transition increments the unit's `version` again.

**Completing a service** is also exclusive to an `EquipmentTechnician` and may carry an optional `service_note` of at most 2000 characters which may be the empty string; it is validated for type and length like any other field but is neither persisted on the unit nor echoed in the response. The unit must be `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE`, else `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The service sets `engine_hours_at_last_service` to the current `engine_hours`, drives `hours_since_service` to 0, recomputes both replacement scores and the replacement flag, moves the unit `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE → IN_SERVICE`, and increments its `version` by 1.

The **replacement score** is a weighted capital-planning score computed at intake, on every hours entry, and on every completed service, and it exists in two variants that differ in exactly one input. Three ratios feed it. The age ratio is the machine's age in years — the logical clock's calendar year less the `model_year` — divided by an expected age life of 10 years; a `model_year` ahead of the clock's year yields a negative difference, which is floored at 0 years before the division, so the age ratio is never negative. The overdue ratio is `hours_since_service` divided by `service_interval_hours`. The usage ratio is where the variants diverge. Each of the three ratios is capped at one before it is weighted, so a machine past its design life contributes exactly the full usage weight and never more. The score is 100 multiplied by the sum of the capped usage ratio weighted at 0.45, the capped age ratio weighted at 0.35, and the capped overdue ratio weighted at 0.20, and the whole weighted sum is rounded half up to one decimal place once, at the end.

The **as-of-now variant**, persisted as `replacement_score`, uses the usage ratio computed as `engine_hours` divided by `expected_life_hours` — the meter reading standing right now — and ignores `avg_daily_hours` entirely. The **fiscal-year-projection variant**, persisted as `replacement_score_projected`, uses the usage ratio computed as `engine_hours` plus `avg_daily_hours` multiplied by the whole number of days remaining from the logical clock's date to 31 December of the logical clock's year, all divided by `expected_life_hours`. That day count is itself floored at 0, so a clock standing on 31 December projects no additional hours at all and the projection collapses onto the as-of-now figure. The same row therefore carries two different scores at the same instant, and a unit can sit below the flag threshold on the as-of-now figure while sitting above it on the projection. `replacement_flag` is a boolean derived from the **projection only**: it is true when `replacement_score_projected` is at or above a flag threshold of 70, and false otherwise. Both scores carry one decimal place and both are bounded to the range 0 through 100, the bound being applied to the already-rounded figure rather than to the raw weighted sum. A `RETIRED` unit keeps whatever scores it carried at retirement and is never recomputed.

To work the projection through: at a logical clock of 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, a unit with `model_year` 2018, `expected_life_hours` 6000, `service_interval_hours` 250, `engine_hours` 4500, `hours_since_service` 300, and `avg_daily_hours` 3.5 has a usage ratio of 0.75, an age ratio of 0.8, and an overdue ratio of 1.2 which caps to 1.0; the as-of-now score is 100 multiplied by the sum of 0.45 times 0.75, 0.35 times 0.8, and 0.20 times 1.0, rounded to 81.8. There are 152 whole days remaining to 31 December, giving 532 projected additional hours and 5032 projected total hours, a projected usage ratio of 0.838666…, and a projected score of 85.7.

**Retirement** is a capital decision reserved to the `Superintendent`. A unit that is `IN_SERVICE` or `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE` may be retired; a unit already `RETIRED` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`. An optional `replacement_unit_id` may name the successor machine, and when it is supplied that unit must exist (else `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`), must be `IN_SERVICE` (else `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`), and must belong to the same course and be of the same `unit_type` as the retiring machine (else `ERR_REPLACEMENT_UNIT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422). Retirement moves the unit `IN_SERVICE → RETIRED` or `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE → RETIRED`, sets its self-referential `replaced_by_unit_id` to the named replacement or to null when none was named, and increments its `version` by 1.

Retirement's second effect is the fleet-to-schedule cascade. Every maintenance window on that course in status `SCHEDULED` whose `required_unit_type` equals the retired unit's `unit_type`, and for which no other unit of that type on that course remains `IN_SERVICE`, moves `SCHEDULED → DEFERRED` under the rules of §3.6 — which itself reverts any plan task the window was carrying `SCHEDULED → PLANNED`, which itself recomputes that plan's `committed_nitrogen_lb` and the turf area's `nitrogen_projected_lb_per_1000`. Retiring the last mower of a class therefore reaches, in one request, all the way into the plan's nitrogen ledger. A unit's status is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `DOWN_FOR_SERVICE`, or `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal, and the collection is filterable on `course_id`, `unit_type`, `status`, `replaced_by_unit_id`, and `replacement_flag`.

### 3.9. Weather: Declaration, Its Cascades, and Clearing

Weather declaration is reserved to the `Superintendent` and is the single most consequential write in the system. The declaration names the `course_id`, an `event_type` of `FROST`, `LIGHTNING`, `HEAVY_RAIN`, or `STORM_DAMAGE`, an optional `delay_minutes` frost pushback from 0 through 600 minutes, defaulting to 0 when omitted, which must be strictly greater than zero when the type is `FROST` and exactly 0 for every other type, an `expected_clear_at` instant at which the event is expected to end, an optional `affected_turf_area_ids` array, defaulting to the empty array when omitted, which must be non-empty when the type is `STORM_DAMAGE` and empty for every other type, and the pinned `expected_course_version`. The type-conditional consistency of `delay_minutes` and `affected_turf_area_ids`, and the requirement that `expected_clear_at` be strictly after the current logical clock reading, all belong to the field-validation band and are therefore settled as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) **before** any record is looked up — a declaration that is both back-dated and aimed at a non-existent course answers 400, not 404. Only then: the named course must exist, else `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`; every member of `affected_turf_area_ids` must exist, else `ERR_TURF_AREA_NOT_FOUND`; `expected_course_version` must equal the course's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_COURSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412); and every named area must belong to the declaring course, else `ERR_TASK_TURF_AREA_MISMATCH` — so a stale version beats an area that belongs to the wrong course.

A declared event is created with status `ACTIVE`, a `declared_at` stamped from the logical clock, a `cleared_at` of null, and a `deferred_window_count` set to the number of maintenance windows the declaration actually deferred. That counter is accumulated as the cascade runs and counts only windows that genuinely reached `DEFERRED` — a window the cascade found at its deferral cap and cancelled instead is not counted — and because it is part of the same creation the event's own `version` is **not** bumped for it: a freshly declared event always reports `version` 1 no matter how many windows it moved. The event's affected interval runs from `declared_at`, inclusive, to `expected_clear_at`, exclusive.

The declaration then fans out. The course's `play_posture` and `frost_delay_minutes` are re-derived by the precedence rule of §2.10 and the course's `version` is incremented by 1. A `FROST` declaration pushes back qualifying play events `SCHEDULED → DELAYED` by the ordered release schedule of §3.7. A `LIGHTNING` or `STORM_DAMAGE` declaration cancels qualifying play events instead, from `SCHEDULED` or from `DELAYED`. A `FROST`, `LIGHTNING`, or `HEAVY_RAIN` declaration defers qualifying maintenance windows `SCHEDULED → DEFERRED` — or cancels those that have exhausted their two deferrals — reverting any plan task each carried and recomputing the affected plans' committed nitrogen and the affected turf areas' projected nitrogen loads. A `STORM_DAMAGE` declaration additionally sets every turf area named in `affected_turf_area_ids` to `agronomic_status` `OUT_OF_PLAY`, incrementing each of those areas' `version` by 1, and cancels every non-`STORM_CLEANUP` maintenance window on those areas that stands in `SCHEDULED` or `DEFERRED`, which in turn cancels the plan tasks those windows carried. Note that a `HEAVY_RAIN` declaration touches no play event at all — it produces a `CART_PATH_ONLY` posture and defers crew windows, and nothing more.

**Clearing** an event is also a `Superintendent` action and moves it `ACTIVE → CLEARED`. The event must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_WEATHER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); an event that does not exist is `ERR_WEATHER_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. The clear stamps `cleared_at` from the logical clock and increments the event's `version` by 1. The course's `play_posture` and `frost_delay_minutes` are then re-derived over the events that remain `ACTIVE`, incrementing the course's `version` by 1 — so clearing one of two active frost events hands the posture to the other, and clearing a lightning event while a heavy-rain event remains active relaxes the posture from `CLOSED` to `CART_PATH_ONLY` rather than to `OPEN`. When the cleared event was a `STORM_DAMAGE` event, every turf area it named whose `agronomic_status` is `OUT_OF_PLAY` and which is named by no other still-`ACTIVE` storm-damage event returns to `agronomic_status` `ACTIVE`, with that area's `version` incremented by 1; an area covered by a second active storm event stays out of play.

One consequence of clearing is deliberately asymmetric and must not be "fixed": play events that were already pushed back keep their pushback. Clearing a frost does not restore the original tee times, does not reset `delayed_minutes` or `effective_start_at`, and does not return a `DELAYED` event to `SCHEDULED`. An event's status is `ACTIVE` or `CLEARED`, with `CLEARED` terminal, and the course's weather log is filterable on `status` and `event_type`.
