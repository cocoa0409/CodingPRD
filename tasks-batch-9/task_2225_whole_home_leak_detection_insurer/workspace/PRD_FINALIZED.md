# Whole-Home Leak Detection & Insurer IoT Discount API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Water is the most expensive thing that can go wrong in a house. A supply line that lets go behind a wall while the family is away for a week does not announce itself; it simply runs, and the first signal the carrier receives is a five-figure remediation invoice. This platform is the server side of a whole-home leak-detection program built around a main-line shutoff device: the hardware watches static pressure and flow at the point where water enters the building, learns what the household's ordinary consumption looks like, classifies departures from that norm as a drip, a running fixture, or a catastrophic burst, and — when the situation warrants it — closes the valve without waiting for a human.

The program is not only a safety product. Carriers underwrite the same houses, and they are willing to discount premium for a house whose device is demonstrably alive and armed. That willingness turns telemetry into money: the API measures **device health uptime** over insurer-defined attestation periods, converts uptime into a premium credit tier, accrues that credit period by period, trues it up once per policy year, and claws it back when a device leaves its armed posture mid-year. The same lineage of telemetry also decides how generously a water-damage claim pays out — a device that closed the valve within minutes of detection earns the homeowner a waived deductible, while a homeowner who overrode the shutoff and let the water run collects a scaled-down payout.

Three tensions dominate the design and should be kept in mind while reading the rest of this document. First, **trust in the baseline**: every classification decision is only as good as the learned profile behind it, so firmware pushes, ownership changes, and repeated false-positive reports all invalidate the baseline and force a relearn, during which the device cannot earn credit. Second, **the homeowner's right to override versus the insurer's right to price that override** — the platform never refuses to reopen a valve outright, but it records the override and lets it change the money. Third, **segregation of duties**: the technician who signed off on an installation may not later approve a warranty claim on that same hardware, and the adjuster who approves a payout may not be the one who pays it. Everything else in this specification is machinery in service of those three ideas.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads and including the operational and clock-control endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the principal's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds a string that is not one of the six recognised roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else the server does; a malformed body, a nonexistent path id, and a forbidden role are all irrelevant until authentication has succeeded.

The six roles are `Homeowner`, `InstallerTech`, `DeviceGateway`, `InsurerAnalyst`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, and `ProgramAdmin`. Their spellings are exact and case-sensitive.

- A `Homeowner` is the account holder. They own properties and devices, override the valve, set vacation and snooze modes, label alerts as false positives, acknowledge escalations, file reimbursement claims, and initiate or accept device transfers when a house changes hands. Two operations belong to a `Homeowner` alone: accepting a device transfer (and only when they are the named buyer) and supplying information back to an adjuster who has requested it.
- An `InstallerTech` is a field technician. Registering hardware and pushing firmware are shared with `ProgramAdmin`; performing the static-pressure hold test that verifies an installation is exclusively theirs.
- A `DeviceGateway` is the device-side ingest identity and the only principal in the system permitted to write telemetry. This exclusivity is deliberate: no human role can fabricate uptime coverage or manufacture an anomaly.
- An `InsurerAnalyst` is carrier-side. They write policies, open and finalize credit attestation periods, and settle payment on already-approved claims.
- A `ClaimsAdjuster` is also carrier-side and owns the review half of claim adjudication: beginning review, requesting information, approving, and denying. They may never pay what they approved.
- A `ProgramAdmin` is the program operator. They provision and close accounts, run the operational sweep, and set the logical clock. They may act on a homeowner's behalf for support operations, but they cannot write telemetry, cannot verify an install, and cannot make the carrier's policy or claim-decision calls.

Once authentication passes, the second gate is role permission: if the acting `X-User-Role` is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint in §4, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters. When `X-User-Role` is `Homeowner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Homeowner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a homeowner compare the resolved owning-homeowner identifier to `X-User-ID`. The resolution walk is transitive — a property resolves to its `homeowner_id`, a device resolves through its property, an anomaly event or telemetry reading resolves through its device, an escalation ticket through its event, a policy through its property, and a claim through the homeowner recorded as its submitter. A `Homeowner` acting on a resource whose resolved owner is a different homeowner is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

For the other five roles the header is not an entity reference. When `X-User-Role` is `InstallerTech`, `DeviceGateway`, `InsurerAnalyst`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, or `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to an entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded in several places and later compared to other recorded operator identifiers: an install verification stores the technician's identifier in `install_verified_by_user_id`, an acknowledgement stores the acting identifier in `acknowledged_by_user_id`, a transfer acceptance stores it in `accepted_by_user_id`, a claim decision stores it in `decided_by_user_id`, and a claim submission stores the resolved homeowner in `submitted_by_user_id`. Those recorded values are what the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.10 read.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and is a zero-based row skip. A `limit` outside its permitted range or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 names a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several endpoints do name a different ordering, and those overrides are binding: the emergency-contact roster is ordered by `priority_rank` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; irrigation windows are ordered by `start_at` ascending; a device's baseline profiles are ordered by `learning_cycle_no` ascending, so the live profile is the last row returned; a device's telemetry is ordered by `reading_at` ascending; anomaly events are ordered by `detected_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; a property's policies are returned newest first; a policy's credit attestations are ordered by `period_start_at` ascending.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page. Single-resource responses carry neither of the four.

Filters on list endpoints narrow the result set before pagination. The roster of emergency contacts and the irrigation-window and attestation lists accept `status`; the property list accepts `property_state`; the device list accepts `lifecycle_state`; the policy list accepts `credit_state`; the baseline-profile list accepts `learning_state`; the telemetry list accepts `classification_at_ingest` and `baseline_eligible`; the anomaly-event list accepts `device_id`, `telemetry_reading_id`, `classification`, and `event_state`; the escalation-ticket list accepts `anomaly_event_id`, `notified_contact_id`, and `stage`; the claim list accepts `policy_id`, `device_id`, `anomaly_event_id`, and `status`; the transfer list accepts `device_id`, `from_homeowner_id`, `to_homeowner_id`, and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — clients never supply an `id` and never derive one from a serial number, a policy number, or an email address. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with exactly three fractional digits, recording when the row first appeared.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant on every accepted mutation. One entity is deliberately excluded from both: a telemetry reading is immutable once accepted, so a `TelemetryReading` row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and nothing in the system ever rewrites a stored reading.

Time in this system is never taken from the host's wall clock. A single **logical clock** governs every deadline, SLA, window, expiry, and freshness comparison in this document. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by submitting a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-control endpoint, which responds with the resulting `current_utc` and performs no state transitions of its own — advancing the clock does not, by itself, escalate a ticket or expire a transfer. Those consequences happen only when the operational sweep runs.

Nested reads resolve their path segments strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request against a homeowner's contacts or properties resolves the homeowner first and answers `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) if that fails; a request against a property's devices, irrigation windows, or policies resolves the property first and answers `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`; a request against a device's baseline profiles, telemetry, or health summary resolves the device first and answers `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`; a request against a policy's attestations resolves the policy first and answers `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`. The equivalent codes for the remaining single-resource paths are `ERR_ANOMALY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCALATION_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`. Identifiers carried in a request *body* rather than the path resolve the same way and reuse the same codes: an unresolvable `homeowner_id` or `to_homeowner_id` yields `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `property_id` yields `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `device_id` yields `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `anomaly_event_id` yields `ERR_ANOMALY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `policy_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.5. Request Admission Order

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response must name the violation the server encountered first, and the ordering below is normative rather than advisory. It is written out in full because a great many operations in §3 are guarded by four or five checks simultaneously, and readers otherwise treat them as an unordered set.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present, role recognised: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation** — the acting role appears among the endpoint's authorised roles in §4: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence**, left to right, using the codes named in §2.4. Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
4. **Request shape** — every required body field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every numeric field non-negative and inside its declared bound, every timestamp parseable as ISO 8601 UTC, and every interval body satisfying a start strictly before its end (equal bounds and inverted bounds are both refused): `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
5. **Body foreign keys** resolve, using the codes named in §2.4.
6. **Ownership scoping** — a `Homeowner` may act only within their own account: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint a `Homeowner` may reach.
7. **Account liveness** — the homeowner resolved behind the request has `account_state` equal to `ACTIVE`. A deactivated account can neither act nor be the target of a write: `ERR_HOMEOWNER_DEACTIVATED` (409). Applies to every write endpoint that resolves a homeowner. Telemetry ingest is the one write that does not: the gateway resolves a device and never walks through to the household behind it, so a sample sent to a device whose account has been closed is answered by the posture check at step 14, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ACCEPTING_TELEMETRY`, rather than by this one.
8. **Device optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (412), on valve commands and transfer creation.
9. **Property optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (412), on policy creation.
10. **Device serial uniqueness** — `ERR_DEVICE_SERIAL_TAKEN` (409), on device registration.
11. **Policy number uniqueness** — `ERR_POLICY_NUMBER_TAKEN` (409), on policy creation.
12. **Contact rank uniqueness** — `ERR_CONTACT_RANK_TAKEN` (409), on emergency-contact creation.
13. **Install once only** — `ERR_INSTALL_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (409), on install verification.
14. **Telemetry-accepting posture** — `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ACCEPTING_TELEMETRY` (409), on telemetry ingest.
15. **Valve command is a real change** — `ERR_VALVE_ALREADY_IN_STATE` (409), on valve commands.
16. **Event actionability** — `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (409), on anomaly feedback and anomaly resolution.
17. **Ticket still pending** — `ERR_TICKET_NOT_PENDING` (409), on ticket acknowledgement.
18. **Attestation still open** — `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_OPEN` (409), on attestation finalize.
19. **Claim decision legality** — `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (409), on claim decisions.
20. **Transfer still pending** — `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_PENDING` (409), on transfer acceptance.
21. **Armed-device requirement** — `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` (409), on monitor-mode changes to `VACATION` or `SNOOZE`.
22. **Unresolved burst blocks reopen** — `ERR_UNRESOLVED_BURST_BLOCKS_REOPEN` (409), on a valve `OPEN` command.
23. **Transfer lock** — `ERR_DEVICE_TRANSFER_LOCKED` (409), on valve commands, monitor-mode changes, and firmware pushes.
24. **Mode transition legality** — `ERR_MODE_TRANSITION_FORBIDDEN` (409), on monitor-mode changes.
25. **Snooze cooldown** — `ERR_SNOOZE_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE` (409), on a monitor-mode change to `SNOOZE`.
26. **Install pressure test** — `ERR_INSTALL_PRESSURE_TEST_FAILED` (422), on install verification.
27. **Baseline completeness for attestation opening** — `ERR_BASELINE_NOT_COMPLETE` (409), on attestation creation. This is the *sole* armed-device gate on opening an attestation: a device whose profile is `COLLECTING` or `INVALIDATED` fails here, and never at step 21.
28. **Reading not in the future** — `ERR_READING_IN_FUTURE` (422), on telemetry ingest.
29. **Reading not too old** — `ERR_READING_TOO_OLD` (422), on telemetry ingest.
30. **Reading in order** — `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER` (409), on telemetry ingest.
31. **Irrigation window overlap** — `ERR_IRRIGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), on irrigation-window creation.
32. **Irrigation enabled on the property** — `ERR_IRRIGATION_NOT_ENABLED` (409), on irrigation-window creation.
33. **Attestation period overlap** — `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on attestation creation.
34. **Attestation period contiguity** — `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_NOT_CONTIGUOUS` (409), on attestation creation.
35. **Attestation period has ended** — `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (409), on attestation finalize.
36. **Policy not terminated** — `ERR_POLICY_TERMINATED` (409), on attestation creation, attestation finalize, claim submission, and claim decisions.
37. **Qualifying event for the claim** — `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_EVENT` (409), on claim submission.
38. **Claim filing window** — `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on claim submission.
39. **Duplicate claim** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409), on claim submission.
40. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on claim approval and claim payment.
41. **Open anomalies and a closed valve block a transfer** — `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EVENTS_BLOCK_TRANSFER` (409), on transfer creation *and* transfer acceptance.
42. **Transfer acceptance window** — `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on transfer acceptance.
43. **Transfer target validity** — `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID` (409), on transfer creation *and* transfer acceptance.
44. **Device transferability** — `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_TRANSFERABLE` (409), on transfer creation.
45. **Annual payout cap** — `ERR_ANNUAL_PAYOUT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422), on claim approval and claim payment.
46. **Approved-but-unpaid payout blocks closure** — `ERR_OPEN_PAYOUT_BLOCKS_DEACTIVATION` (409), on account deactivation.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out. Safety and business-referential checks (steps 22 through 27) are evaluated *before* the telemetry temporal checks and before every fiscal check, so a burst that is still open surfaces as `ERR_UNRESOLVED_BURST_BLOCKS_REOPEN` rather than being masked by a later rule. And the two fiscal checks sit last, so a payout that both exceeds the annual cap and violates segregation of duties reports the segregation failure.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency, because each of them is a legitimate operation in its own right — a second transfer really does supersede the first, a valve really can be reopened, a newer policy really does supersede its predecessor — and therefore no state precondition can distinguish an intentional action from one formed against a stale view.

Creating a device transfer (`POST /device-transfers`) requires `expected_device_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Device`. If that value does not equal the device's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). What the check catches is a seller acting on an out-of-date device snapshot: an auto-shutoff, a mode change, or another transfer has landed since they read the device.

A valve command (`POST /devices/{id}/valve-commands`) likewise requires `expected_device_version` as a non-optional body field. A command carrying a device version other than the device's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The homeowner must prove the command was formed against the newest device snapshot before the server will move water.

Creating an insurance policy (`POST /policies`) requires `expected_property_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Property`. A mismatch with the property's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), meaning the analyst never saw the policy that already superseded theirs.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Install verification, telemetry ingest, monitor-mode changes, firmware pushes, anomaly feedback and resolution, ticket acknowledgement, attestation creation and finalize, claim submission and claim decisions, transfer acceptance, the operational sweep, and account deactivation all take no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. Measurement Precision and Rounding

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, applied at the decimal place stated for the value in question, and applied once at the point the value is produced rather than repeatedly along the way.

- Pressure in pounds per square inch carries **1 decimal place**: `installed_static_pressure_psi`, `static_pressure_psi`, and the learned `baseline_static_pressure_psi`.
- Flow in gallons per minute carries **2 decimal places**: `flow_gpm`, `expected_max_flow_gpm`, `baseline_peak_flow_gpm`, and `drip_threshold_gpm`. Every classification gate derived from these values is rounded to 2 decimal places *before* the comparison is made.
- Volume in gallons carries **2 decimal places** (`total_gallons`), and flow duration in minutes carries **1 decimal place** (`duration_minutes`, `shutoff_latency_minutes`).
- A severity score carries **3 decimal places** and always lies between 0.000 and 1.000 inclusive.
- Uptime percentages carry **2 decimal places** (`uptime_pct`, `avg_uptime_pct`), and credit tier fractions carry **2 decimal places** (`tier_pct`, `current_credit_tier_pct`).
- All money is stored as an integer number of cents, and any scaling that produces a fractional cent is rounded half up to a whole cent. `annual_premium_cents`, `deductible_cents`, `credited_cents_total`, `clawback_cents_total`, `true_up_cents`, `paid_payout_cents_total`, `credit_cents_awarded`, `documented_loss_cents`, `approved_payout_cents`, and `deductible_applied_cents` are all integers.
- Coverage arithmetic is done in **whole minutes**: `period_minutes`, `covered_minutes`, and `coverage_minutes` are whole-minute quantities, never fractional.

Durations expressed in minutes, hours, or days are added to the logical clock as exact offsets with no timezone conversion of any kind.

### 2.8. Half-Open Intervals and Inclusive Boundaries

Two interval families exist in this system, and both are **half-open**: they include their start instant and exclude their end instant. An irrigation window occupies the span from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`. An attestation period occupies the span from `period_start_at` up to but not including `period_end_at`. The immediate consequence is that a new interval beginning at exactly the instant an existing interval ends does not overlap it and is admitted, while one beginning a single minute earlier does overlap and is refused.

Every threshold comparison elsewhere in this document is inclusive at its stated boundary unless the text says otherwise. A pressure reading exactly at a bound passes; a reading stamped exactly at the logical clock is accepted; an SLA deadline reached exactly escalates; a transfer accepted at exactly its expiry instant succeeds. The exceptions are explicitly strict and are called out where they occur: a telemetry sample must be strictly *after* the device's previous sample, a monitor-mode override grace period lapses at exactly its end instant rather than one instant later, an irrigation window's `end_at` must be strictly after its `start_at`, an attestation's `period_end_at` must be strictly after its `period_start_at`, and the sweep expires a transfer only once the clock is strictly past its expiry.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this system touch more than one row, and the secondary effects are as much a part of the contract as the primary one. Three cascade families recur.

**Bubble-up promotion.** A parent's posture can change because a child's did. A device does not arm because someone asked it to; it arms because its baseline profile reached completion during a telemetry ingest, and the profile's completion is what drives the parent forward.

**Demotion cascades.** Whenever a learned baseline stops being trustworthy, the invalidation propagates: the profile leaves `COMPLETE`, the device leaves `ARMED` for `LEARNING`, the device's `baseline_ready` flag returns to false, and any policy on the same property that was earning credit is suspended and clawed back. This chain fires identically for all three invalidation causes — a firmware push, an ownership transfer, and a false-positive threshold breach — and each cause is recorded in `invalidated_reason`.

**Compensating reversals.** Some effects are undone rather than progressed. Reopening a valve restores every irrigation window the closure suspended and repairs the valve counter parity invariant. Suspending an earning policy reverses the most recently awarded credit period through an explicit clawback rather than by rewriting the attestation rows that recorded it.

Every row touched by a cascade has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, not merely the row the request nominally targeted. A single telemetry ingest can therefore bump a device's `version`, mint a fresh anomaly event and escalation ticket, and move several irrigation windows in one atomic step.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties in the Carrier Workflow

Two operator identities are compared against previously recorded operator identities, and a match is a refusal rather than a warning. Approving a warranty claim requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `install_verified_by_user_id` recorded on the device the claim names — the technician who certified the installation cannot later bless a hardware-failure payout on it. Paying a claim requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `decided_by_user_id` recorded when the claim was approved — the adjuster who approved a payout cannot also disburse it. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Household Onboarding: Accounts, Escalation Roster and Properties

Enrolment begins with a `ProgramAdmin` registering the household. Account registration takes `full_name` (between 1 and 120 characters), a `email` address that must be lowercase and unique across all homeowner accounts, a `phone_e164` mobile number in E.164 form such as `+14155550100` — this is the number the platform texts or calls when a leak is first detected — and optionally a `notification_channel`, which selects how those first-stage alerts reach the homeowner and takes one of `SMS`, `EMAIL`, or `PUSH`, defaulting to `SMS` when omitted. The new account is created with `account_state` equal to `ACTIVE`, `deactivated_at` null, and `owned_property_count` at 0. That count is not a stored tally the client maintains; it is derived on read as the number of properties whose owner is this account and whose `property_state` is either `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFER_PENDING` — a property that has been archived stops counting.

The escalation roster is built next, and it is what makes the second rung of the alert ladder possible. Each emergency contact is created against a homeowner with a `full_name` between 1 and 120 characters, a `phone_e164` number that will be dialled at escalation stage 2, and a `priority_rank` between 1 and 5 where rank 1 is called first. Ranks must be unique among the homeowner's contacts whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a second `ACTIVE` contact at an already-taken rank is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_RANK_TAKEN`. A rank held only by a contact whose `status` is `REMOVED` is free to reuse, which is exactly why the escalation selector filters on `status` before it orders by rank. Contact creation also accepts the optional flag `verify_immediately`: when it is true the contact is created with `verification_state` equal to `VERIFIED`, standing in for a confirmed SMS opt-in; when it is false or omitted the contact is created `PENDING`. This distinction has teeth — only a `VERIFIED` contact is ever selectable at stage 2, so a household whose sole contact is `PENDING` will watch its alert ladder run out of rungs. Every new contact starts `ACTIVE` with `last_notified_at` null; the field is stamped only when an escalation sweep actually dials that contact.

Properties are created against a homeowner. The payload names the owning `homeowner_id` and describes the building: `address_line1` (1 to 160 characters), `city`, a two-uppercase-letter `state_code`, a five-digit `postal_code`, and `year_built`, which must fall between 1850 and 2026 and which the carrier's underwriting notes read. One further flag matters far more than it looks: `irrigation_present` declares whether the property has a sprinkler system, defaults to false, and gates whether irrigation exclusion windows may be declared at all. A new property begins with `property_state` equal to `ACTIVE` and `archived_at` null. Over its life the property may sit in `TRANSFER_PENDING` while a sale is in flight, return to `ACTIVE` when the sale completes or its transfer lapses, or be moved to `ARCHIVED` — a one-way move — when the owning account is closed.

### 3.2. Device Provisioning, the Pressure Hold Test and Baseline Learning

Hardware is registered by an `InstallerTech` (or a `ProgramAdmin`) against a property. Registration names the `property_id` whose main line the shutoff valve sits on, a `serial_number` between 6 and 32 characters that must be unique across every device the platform has ever seen — a collision is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_SERIAL_TAKEN` — a `model_code` of either `FLO_SMART_3_4` or `FLO_SMART_1_0`, and optionally `firmware_version` as a semantic-version string, which defaults to `1.0.0`. Because a device carries three independent posture dimensions rather than one status, a newly registered row lands in `lifecycle_state` `REGISTERED`, `valve_state` `OPEN`, and `monitor_mode` `NORMAL` simultaneously, with `close_count` and `reopen_count` both at 0 and `monitor_mode_updated_at` equal to `created_at`. Every other posture timestamp — `install_verified_at`, `last_shutoff_at`, `last_reading_at`, `vacation_started_at`, `vacation_ends_at`, `snooze_window_start_at`, `snooze_window_end_at`, `decommissioned_at` — begins null, `install_verified_by_user_id` and `installed_static_pressure_psi` begin null, and `baseline_ready` begins false.

Install verification is the technician's sign-off and the only operation in the system reserved exclusively to `InstallerTech`. The technician submits two measurements: `installed_static_pressure_psi`, the static line pressure held at the valve, and `pressure_drop_psi`, how much pressure bled off over the hold. The test passes only when the static pressure is at least 40.0 and at most 80.0 pounds per square inch **and** the pressure drop is at most 2.0 pounds per square inch; all three bounds are inclusive, so exactly 40.0, exactly 80.0, and a drop of exactly 2.0 all pass, while 39.9, 80.1, and a drop of 2.1 all fail with `ERR_INSTALL_PRESSURE_TEST_FAILED`. Verification is once only: a device whose `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `REGISTERED` refuses the operation with `ERR_INSTALL_ALREADY_VERIFIED`, and the device's property must be in `ACTIVE` state.

A passing test does several things at once. It records `install_verified_by_user_id` as the acting technician's `X-User-ID` and `install_verified_at` as the current logical clock, persists the submitted `installed_static_pressure_psi` onto the device, and advances `lifecycle_state` through `REGISTERED → INSTALL_VERIFIED → LEARNING` inside the single request — the intermediate state is real and enumerable, but no client action sits between the two hops. In the same step exactly one baseline profile row is created for the device, with `device_id` pointing back at it, `learning_state` `COLLECTING`, `learning_cycle_no` at 1, `learning_started_at` set to the clock, `sample_count` at 0, and `baseline_version` at 0. The three learned values — `baseline_static_pressure_psi`, `baseline_peak_flow_gpm`, and `drip_threshold_gpm` — are all null at this point, as are `completed_at`, `last_false_positive_at`, and `invalidated_reason`, and `false_positive_count` starts at 0. The device's `version` increments by 1.

Learning proceeds by accumulation, not by a command. `sample_count` is the number of telemetry readings for this device that are marked baseline-eligible and whose `reading_at` falls at or after the profile's current `learning_started_at`. On each accepted reading while the profile is still `COLLECTING`, three values are recomputed over exactly that eligible set: `baseline_static_pressure_psi` is the arithmetic mean of the readings' static pressures, rounded half up to one decimal place; `baseline_peak_flow_gpm` is the largest observed flow among them, carried to two decimal places; and `drip_threshold_gpm` is the greater of an absolute floor of 0.05 gallons per minute and two percent of `baseline_peak_flow_gpm`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The profile reaches `learning_state` `COMPLETE` on the reading that brings `sample_count` to **three or more** samples, and stays `COLLECTING` below that. While `sample_count` is 0, all three learned values remain null and the profile remains `COLLECTING`. If every eligible reading happens to show zero flow, `baseline_peak_flow_gpm` is 0.00 and the absolute floor binds, giving a `drip_threshold_gpm` of 0.05.

Reaching completion is the moment the device becomes useful. The transition `COLLECTING → COMPLETE` publishes the three learned values, increments `baseline_version` by 1, and stamps `completed_at` with the clock — and that publication bubbles up to promote the device from `LEARNING` to `ARMED`, setting `baseline_ready` to true. Only from `ARMED` does the classifier run at all, and only an armed device earns insurer credit. Note carefully what does *not* happen: a reading rejected by any validation is never persisted and therefore never advances `sample_count`, and readings belonging to an earlier `learning_cycle_no` are excluded from the aggregate entirely, so a relearn genuinely starts from zero rather than blending old household behaviour into new.

Baseline profiles are cyclic rather than terminal. A profile that has been invalidated re-enters `COLLECTING` on the very next accepted reading, at which point `learning_cycle_no` increments by 1, `learning_started_at` is reset to the clock, `baseline_static_pressure_psi`, `baseline_peak_flow_gpm`, and `drip_threshold_gpm` are all cleared, `false_positive_count` resets to 0, and `sample_count` restarts from the readings stamped at or after the new learning start. The cycle closes when `COLLECTING → COMPLETE` fires again with a higher `baseline_version` than before. A homeowner or technician reading the profile list for a device sees the whole history ordered by cycle number, and the live profile is the last row.

Firmware is the first of the three ways a baseline loses its standing. An `InstallerTech` or `ProgramAdmin` pushes new firmware by supplying `target_firmware_version`, which is written straight onto the device's `firmware_version` and increments the device's `version` by 1. The push is only meaningful if it changes something: the submitted `target_firmware_version` must differ from the device's current `firmware_version`. A firmware push is refused while the device's `lifecycle_state` is `TRANSFER_PENDING`, surfacing as `ERR_DEVICE_TRANSFER_LOCKED`. When the device was `ARMED` at the time of the push, the demotion cascade fires in three hops: the baseline profile moves `COMPLETE → INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` `FIRMWARE_UPDATE`, `completed_at` cleared to null, and `false_positive_count` reset to 0; that invalidation demotes the device `ARMED → LEARNING` and sets `baseline_ready` back to false; and leaving `ARMED` suspends any policy on the property that was earning credit, with `suspended_reason` `DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` and a clawback of the newest awarded period, as §3.8 describes. The cycle then closes normally: the next accepted reading reopens the same profile row as `COLLECTING` with `learning_cycle_no` incremented, and the device returns to `ARMED` once three eligible samples of the new cycle have landed. Pushing firmware to a device that was not `ARMED` simply updates the version string.

### 3.3. Telemetry Ingest, Classification and Severity

Only a `DeviceGateway` may write telemetry. Each ingest carries one immutable sample: `reading_at`, the instant the sample was taken; `static_pressure_psi`, the static line pressure at that instant, between 0.0 and 200.0 with one decimal place; `flow_gpm`, the observed flow in gallons per minute, never negative and carried to two decimal places; `duration_minutes`, the length of the continuous flow episode ending at `reading_at`, never negative and carried to one decimal place; `total_gallons`, the volume consumed across that episode, never negative and carried to two decimal places; and optionally `reading_source`, which is `DEVICE` for autonomous samples and `MANUAL_TEST` for an installer's bench test, defaulting to `DEVICE`. A bench test is never baseline-eligible, which is the point of distinguishing it — a technician exercising the valve on the workbench must not teach the house's baseline.

A device accepts telemetry only while its `lifecycle_state` is `LEARNING`, `ARMED`, or `TRANSFER_PENDING`. A `REGISTERED` device that has not yet passed its pressure test, and a `DECOMMISSIONED` device, both refuse the sample with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ACCEPTING_TELEMETRY`. Three temporal integrity rules then apply, in this order. The sample may not be stamped after the logical clock: a `reading_at` exactly equal to the clock is accepted, one minute later is refused with `ERR_READING_IN_FUTURE`. The sample may not be backdated by more than a full day: a `reading_at` exactly 24 hours before the clock is accepted, one minute earlier is refused with `ERR_READING_TOO_OLD`. And the stream must be strictly increasing: `reading_at` must be strictly later than the device's `last_reading_at`, so a repeat of the previous instant is refused with `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER`. A refused sample is never persisted at all — it leaves no trace, contributes nothing to learning, and, importantly, contributes nothing to the uptime coverage the insurer scores.

The order of work inside a single accepted ingest is fixed and resolves what would otherwise look like a circular dependency between classification and learning. **Step one**, the reading is persisted, with `device_id` set to the emitting device and `last_reading_at` on the device advanced to the new `reading_at`. **Step two**, classification runs against the baseline snapshot *as it stood before this reading was folded in* — that is, against the values published by earlier readings — and the resulting verdict is frozen in the reading's `classification_at_ingest` and, on any anomaly event minted, in `baseline_version_used`. **Step three**, the baseline aggregate is recomputed to include this reading and the new snapshot is published, incrementing `baseline_version` by 1. That third step is conditional: it runs only while the profile is `COLLECTING` — an invalidated profile having already re-entered `COLLECTING` at the top of this same ingest, as §3.2 describes — and only when the reading just stored is itself a member of the eligible set that §3.2 aggregates over. A reading that is not baseline-eligible, and any reading landing on a profile that is already `COMPLETE`, recomputes nothing at all and leaves `baseline_version`, the three learned values and `completed_at` exactly as they stood; a learning cycle that completes on its third eligible sample has therefore incremented `baseline_version` three times. Two consequences follow and are binding: the very reading that completes a learning cycle is itself never classified, and a stored reading's `classification_at_ingest` is permanent — later baseline recomputation never reclassifies history. While the profile is still `COLLECTING`, classification is skipped entirely, `classification_at_ingest` is `NONE`, and the reading is baseline-eligible.

Classification itself runs only when the device's profile is `COMPLETE` and its `lifecycle_state` is `ARMED`. It begins by resolving a sensitivity multiplier from the device's `monitor_mode`: in `VACATION` the multiplier is 0.50, which halves every flow and volume gate and thereby makes the device twice as suspicious; in `NORMAL` and in `SNOOZE` the multiplier is 1.00. Three gates are then derived, each rounded half up to two decimal places before any comparison is made. The **burst flow gate** is `baseline_peak_flow_gpm` multiplied by 1.50 and then by the multiplier. The **running-fixture flow gate** is `baseline_peak_flow_gpm` multiplied by 0.40 and then by the multiplier. The **burst volume gate** is 30 gallons multiplied by the multiplier. The **drip gate** is simply the published `drip_threshold_gpm`, deliberately *not* scaled by the multiplier, because the drip floor is absolute.

The verdict is then decided by an ordered, first-match-wins ladder evaluated top to bottom, so that a catastrophic burst outranks a running fixture, which in turn outranks a drip, and no reading ever yields two classifications.

1. If `flow_gpm` is at least the burst flow gate, **and** `duration_minutes` is at least 3 minutes, **and** `total_gallons` is at least the burst volume gate, the verdict is `CATASTROPHIC_BURST`.
2. Otherwise, if `flow_gpm` is at least the running-fixture flow gate **and** `duration_minutes` is at least 20 minutes, the verdict is `RUNNING_FIXTURE`.
3. Otherwise, if the device is in `VACATION` mode, `flow_gpm` is strictly greater than zero, **and** `total_gallons` is at least 15 gallons, the verdict is `RUNNING_FIXTURE`. This is the vacation-specific rung: nobody is home, so any sustained draw of fifteen gallons is a running fixture regardless of rate.
4. Otherwise, if `flow_gpm` is at least the drip gate **and** `duration_minutes` is at least a full hour, the verdict is `DRIP`.
5. Otherwise the verdict is `NONE`.

Every gate comparison is inclusive; the only strict comparison in the ladder is the vacation liveness test that flow be above zero. A reading sitting exactly on the burst flow gate with exactly 3 minutes of duration and exactly the burst volume gate classifies as `CATASTROPHIC_BURST`. A verdict of `NONE` writes no anomaly event at all, leaves `anomaly_event_created` false, and — provided the sample came from `DEVICE` rather than `MANUAL_TEST` and was not suppressed by irrigation — marks the reading `baseline_eligible`. Those three conditions together define eligibility: a reading is baseline-eligible exactly when its frozen classification is `NONE`, its `reading_source` is `DEVICE`, and its `suppressed_by_irrigation` flag is false.

Every persisted reading also carries `coverage_minutes`, fixed at 120, representing the two hours of monitoring uptime that one sample vouches for; §3.7 explains how those spans are merged into an insurer-facing uptime figure.

When the ladder returns anything other than `NONE`, an anomaly event is minted. It records `device_id`, `telemetry_reading_id` pointing at the sample that triggered it, the `classification` the ladder chose, `detected_at` equal to the current logical clock, and a frozen snapshot of the triggering measurements in `observed_flow_gpm`, `observed_duration_minutes`, and `observed_gallons`; the reading's `anomaly_event_created` flag flips to true. The event begins in `event_state` `DETECTED` and never remains there past the end of the request — it becomes either `ALERTED` or `SUPPRESSED` in the same ingest.

An event is created `ALERTED` when the device is `ARMED`, its `monitor_mode` is not `SNOOZE`, and no earlier event on the same device shares this classification with a `detected_at` inside the last hour. It is created `SUPPRESSED`, with a recorded `suppression_reason`, in exactly three circumstances: the device's `lifecycle_state` is `TRANSFER_PENDING`, giving `DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` — a branch the `ARMED`-only classification gate above leaves unreachable in practice, since a device that is not `ARMED` never reaches the ladder and so mints no event to suppress; the device's `monitor_mode` is `SNOOZE`, giving `SNOOZE_ACTIVE`; or an earlier event of the same classification on the same device has a `detected_at` strictly later than one hour before the clock, giving `DUPLICATE_WITHIN_COOLDOWN`. The two branches have disjoint consequences and the asymmetry is deliberate: an `ALERTED` event creates an escalation ticket and may close the valve, while a `SUPPRESSED` event creates no ticket whatsoever and leaves the valve exactly where it was.

Severity is scored on every minted event, whatever its classification, and lands in `severity_score` as a three-factor weighted figure between 0.000 and 1.000. Three reference denominators are computed first, each the corresponding burst gate doubled and rounded to four decimal places: the flow reference is twice the burst flow gate, the duration reference is twice the 3-minute burst duration threshold (so 6.0000 minutes), and the volume reference is twice the burst volume gate. Three components follow, each the observed value divided by its reference and then capped at 1.0 — the cap is applied to each component individually, before weighting, so no single enormous measurement can push the score beyond its own share. The components are combined with fixed weights: flow contributes 0.50, duration contributes 0.30, and volume contributes 0.20. The weights sum to exactly 1.00, so a reading that saturates all three components scores exactly 1.000 and can never exceed it. Rounding is applied **once**, to the weighted sum, half up to three decimal places; summing three separately rounded components is incorrect. As a worked illustration, a slow drip at 0.12 gallons per minute running 60 minutes for half a gallon, measured against a burst flow gate of 9.00, a 3-minute duration threshold and a 30-gallon volume gate, produces an unrounded flow component of 0.12 divided by 18.0000, a duration component of 60 divided by 6.0000 which saturates at 1.0, and a volume component of 0.5 divided by 60.0000, giving a final `severity_score` of 0.305.

### 3.4. Auto-Shutoff Policy, Valve Override and Irrigation Exclusion

Immediately after an anomaly event is minted, the auto-shutoff policy decides whether to stop the water. A shutoff is *required* in exactly two situations: the classification is `CATASTROPHIC_BURST`, in any mode; or the classification is `RUNNING_FIXTURE` and the device's `monitor_mode` is `VACATION`. In every other case no shutoff is required — notably, a `DRIP` never closes the valve in any mode, it only alerts, and a `RUNNING_FIXTURE` in `NORMAL` mode alerts without closing while the identical reading in `VACATION` mode closes.

Once a shutoff is required, three gates are consulted in this order and the first that applies aborts the closure. If the event was created `SUPPRESSED` — which is what happens to a burst arriving while the device is snoozed — the closure is skipped and the valve stays `OPEN` with `auto_shutoff_executed` false. Otherwise, if the device's `valve_state` is already `CLOSED`, the closure is skipped as redundant. Otherwise, if the device has any anomaly event whose `event_state` is `ALERTED` or `ESCALATED` and whose `override_reopen_at` is set, and the clock has not yet reached that instant plus two hours, the closure is skipped under the homeowner override grace: the household explicitly chose to restore water, and the platform honours that choice for two hours rather than fighting it reading by reading. The grace comparison is strict at its far end — while the clock is before the grace expiry the device is immune, and at exactly the expiry instant the grace has lapsed and the next qualifying reading will close the valve.

When no gate applies, the closure executes as a single atomic cascade: the device's `valve_state` moves `OPEN → CLOSED`, `close_count` increases by 1, `last_shutoff_at` is stamped with the clock, and the device's `version` increases by 1. On the event, `auto_shutoff_executed` becomes true and `shutoff_latency_minutes` is set to the elapsed minutes between the event's `detected_at` and the closure, rounded half up to one decimal place — which, because detection and closure happen inside the same ingest, is 0.0. And every irrigation window belonging to the device's property whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED_BY_SHUTOFF` with `suspended_at` stamped at the clock; there is no point excusing sprinkler flow on a line that has no water in it. On every skipped path `auto_shutoff_executed` stays false and `shutoff_latency_minutes` stays null, and that pair is precisely what the claims machinery in §3.8 reads.

Those two fields feed a derived verdict, `mitigation_credited`, which is true only when all three of the following hold: `auto_shutoff_executed` is true, `shutoff_latency_minutes` is at most 5 minutes, and `override_reopen_at` is still null. A latency of exactly 5.0 minutes keeps the credit; 5.1 minutes loses it. This single flag decides which payout algebra a water-damage claim uses.

Homeowners retain manual control of the valve, shared with `ProgramAdmin` for support purposes. A valve command carries the `command` itself — either `CLOSE` or `OPEN` — together with the mandatory `expected_device_version` described in §2.6, and optionally `override_reason` and `acknowledge_water_damage_risk`. A command that would not change anything is refused: `CLOSE` on an already-closed valve, or `OPEN` on an already-open valve, yields `ERR_VALVE_ALREADY_IN_STATE`. No valve command of either direction is accepted while the device's `lifecycle_state` is `TRANSFER_PENDING`; control is frozen for the duration of a pending sale and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_TRANSFER_LOCKED`. A manual `CLOSE` additionally requires the device's `lifecycle_state` to be `LEARNING` or `ARMED`.

A manual `CLOSE` behaves like the automatic one on the parts that matter: `close_count` increases by 1, `last_shutoff_at` is stamped, every `ACTIVE` irrigation window on the property moves to `SUSPENDED_BY_SHUTOFF`, and the device's `version` increases by 1.

Reopening is where the program's central tension lives. A reopen is refused outright — `ERR_UNRESOLVED_BURST_BLOCKS_REOPEN` — while the device still has an anomaly event whose `classification` is `CATASTROPHIC_BURST` and whose `event_state` is `ALERTED` or `ESCALATED`, *unless* the command carries `acknowledge_water_damage_risk` set to true together with a non-empty `override_reason`. In other words, the burst must be resolved, relabelled a false positive, or explicitly overridden with a written acknowledgement before water is restored; the platform will not silently move water back into a house it believes is flooding. When the reopen is accepted, `reopen_count` increases by 1 — restoring the parity invariant that `close_count` minus `reopen_count` equals 1 while the valve is `CLOSED` and 0 while it is `OPEN` — every irrigation window on the property whose `status` is `SUSPENDED_BY_SHUTOFF` returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspended_at` cleared back to null, and the device's `version` increases by 1. Every anomaly event on the device still sitting in `ALERTED` or `ESCALATED` has its `override_reopen_at` stamped at the clock, which both clears that event's `mitigation_credited` and starts the two-hour auto-shutoff immunity described above. A homeowner who overrides an otherwise perfectly mitigated burst therefore converts a waived deductible into a halved payout, and may find the adjuster denying the claim outright on that basis.

Irrigation exclusion windows are the platform's answer to the most common false alarm in the product: a sprinkler zone that draws steadily for forty minutes every Saturday morning. A homeowner (or `ProgramAdmin`) declares a window against a property with a human-readable `label` of 1 to 60 characters such as *Front zone Saturday*, a `start_at` instant, an `end_at` instant strictly after it, and an `expected_max_flow_gpm` strictly above zero and carried to two decimal places, which is the flow ceiling the platform will treat as ordinary irrigation. The property must have `irrigation_present` true or the declaration is refused with `ERR_IRRIGATION_NOT_ENABLED`, and the submitted half-open span must not overlap any window on the property whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or the declaration is refused with `ERR_IRRIGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the spans are half-open, a window beginning at exactly the instant an existing window ends is admitted while one beginning a minute earlier is not. New windows are created `ACTIVE` with `suspended_at` null and reference the owning `property_id`; a window may later be `SUSPENDED_BY_SHUTOFF` while the valve is closed, or `CANCELLED` when the owning account closes.

During ingest, a reading whose `reading_at` falls inside an `ACTIVE` window for the property and whose `flow_gpm` is at or below that window's `expected_max_flow_gpm` has `suppressed_by_irrigation` set to true, its classification forced to `NONE`, and no anomaly event written — the sprinklers are simply doing their job. Irrigation suppression also disqualifies the reading from baseline eligibility, so sprinkler flow never inflates the household's learned peak.

### 3.5. Monitor Modes: Vacation Sensitivity and Snooze Suppression

The third posture dimension on a device is `monitor_mode`, and it is genuinely independent of the other two: changing it neither moves the valve nor alters the lifecycle. A mode change carries `mode` — one of `NORMAL`, `VACATION`, or `SNOOZE` — plus `vacation_ends_at` when entering vacation and `snooze_minutes` when entering snooze. Any accepted change stamps `monitor_mode_updated_at` with the clock and increments the device's `version` by 1. Before any change has ever been made, `monitor_mode_updated_at` equals the device's `created_at`.

The mode machine is a star centred on `NORMAL`. The only legal transitions are `NORMAL → VACATION`, `VACATION → NORMAL`, `NORMAL → SNOOZE`, and `SNOOZE → NORMAL`. Going directly from `VACATION` to `SNOOZE`, or directly from `SNOOZE` to `VACATION`, is not a transition; the device must pass through `NORMAL` first, and an attempt to shortcut is refused with `ERR_MODE_TRANSITION_FORBIDDEN`. Entering either non-default mode requires the device to be `ARMED` — a device still learning, awaiting install verification, mid-transfer, or decommissioned is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` — while returning to `NORMAL` carries no such requirement. No mode change of any kind is accepted while the device's `lifecycle_state` is `TRANSFER_PENDING`, which surfaces as `ERR_DEVICE_TRANSFER_LOCKED`.

**Vacation mode** is the sensitivity setting for an empty house. The homeowner supplies `vacation_ends_at`, which must be strictly after the current clock and no more than 90 days beyond it. Acceptance stamps `vacation_started_at` at the clock and stores the submitted end instant in `vacation_ends_at`. For as long as the mode holds, the sensitivity multiplier described in §3.3 halves the burst flow gate, the running-fixture flow gate, and the burst volume gate, and the vacation-specific rung of the classification ladder becomes live so that any positive flow accumulating fifteen gallons is treated as a running fixture. Vacation mode also changes the shutoff policy: a `RUNNING_FIXTURE` that would merely alert at home will close the valve while the household is away. Returning to `NORMAL` — whether the homeowner does it early or the operational sweep does it once the clock reaches `vacation_ends_at` — clears both `vacation_started_at` and `vacation_ends_at` back to null and refreshes `monitor_mode_updated_at`.

**Snooze mode** is the opposite instrument: it is how a homeowner tells the platform to stop closing the valve while they fill a pool or flush a water heater. The request supplies `snooze_minutes`, which must be at least 30 and at most 240 — half an hour to four hours. Acceptance sets `snooze_window_start_at` to the clock and `snooze_window_end_at` to the clock plus the requested minutes. Classification continues to run unchanged during a snooze, with the multiplier at 1.00, but every anomaly event minted is created `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `SNOOZE_ACTIVE` rather than `ALERTED`, no escalation ticket is created, and auto-shutoff is disabled for the span. A catastrophic burst arriving mid-snooze therefore leaves the valve `OPEN` and `auto_shutoff_executed` false — a fact that later matters a great deal to the payout variant.

Snooze is rate-limited by a cooldown so that it cannot be chained indefinitely. Once a snooze window has ever existed on a device, a new snooze is admitted only when the clock has reached `snooze_window_end_at` plus a further hour; strictly before that instant the request is refused with `ERR_SNOOZE_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. The buffered boundary itself is available — a snooze requested at exactly 60 minutes after the previous window's end is admitted, one minute earlier is not. Crucially, reverting to `NORMAL` early does **not** clear the window bounds: `snooze_window_start_at` and `snooze_window_end_at` are retained after the mode reverts, whether the revert was manual or done by the sweep, precisely so that the cooldown still applies to somebody who tries to escape it by toggling back to `NORMAL`. This is the one asymmetry between the two modes — vacation bounds are cleared on revert, snooze bounds are not.

### 3.6. The Alert Escalation Ladder

Every anomaly event created in `ALERTED` mints exactly one escalation ticket, and that ticket is the two-rung ladder by which a leak reaches a human. The new ticket references its `anomaly_event_id`, begins in `stage` `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` with `notification_attempt_count` at 1 — the stage-1 notification to the homeowner's `phone_e164` over their chosen `notification_channel` counts as the first attempt — and carries `homeowner_deadline_at` set to its own `created_at` plus 15 minutes. `notified_contact_id`, `contact_deadline_at`, `escalated_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by_user_id`, and `expiry_reason` all begin null.

Deadlines are only enforced when the operational sweep runs. The sweep walks tickets whose `stage` is `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` or `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT` and whose underlying anomaly event is still `ALERTED` or `ESCALATED`, in chronological order by ticket `created_at` ascending, and applies the following to each.

A ticket in `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` whose `homeowner_deadline_at` the clock has reached — inclusively, so exactly fifteen minutes after creation escalates and one minute earlier does nothing — attempts to escalate to the emergency roster. The eligible pool is the owning homeowner's emergency contacts whose `status` is `ACTIVE` **and** whose `verification_state` is `VERIFIED`; contacts still `PENDING` verification are ineligible, and this is a real failure mode rather than a theoretical one. From the eligible pool the sweep selects the contact with the lowest `priority_rank`, breaking any tie by `created_at` ascending. If a contact is selected, the ticket moves `PENDING_HOMEOWNER → PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT`: `notified_contact_id` records the chosen contact, `escalated_at` is stamped at the clock, `contact_deadline_at` is set to the clock plus 30 minutes, `notification_attempt_count` becomes 2, and the chosen contact's own `last_notified_at` is stamped at the clock. The same hop mirrors onto the anomaly event, moving it `ALERTED → ESCALATED` and incrementing the event's `version` by 1. If no eligible contact exists, the ticket instead moves `PENDING_HOMEOWNER → EXPIRED` with `expiry_reason` `NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTACT`, and the anomaly event deliberately stays `ALERTED` — the leak has not been dealt with merely because nobody could be reached.

A ticket already in `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT` whose `contact_deadline_at` the clock has reached moves `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT → EXPIRED` with `expiry_reason` `CONTACT_SLA_BREACH`. The anomaly event stays `ESCALATED` and, importantly, becomes resolvable. A single sweep can move a given ticket at most one rung: a ticket that escalates during this sweep cannot also expire in it, because its `contact_deadline_at` was only just set to thirty minutes in the future.

Acknowledgement is the only exit from the ladder that does not involve the sweep. A homeowner (or a `ProgramAdmin` acting for them) may acknowledge a ticket in either `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` or `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT`, moving it to `ACKNOWLEDGED` with `acknowledged_at` stamped at the clock and `acknowledged_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`. An acknowledged ticket is skipped entirely by every future sweep. Acknowledgement on a ticket that is already `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_PENDING`, and a homeowner acknowledging a ticket belonging to another household's event is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The ladder also stops when the leak itself is closed out. If the underlying anomaly event reaches `RESOLVED` or `FALSE_POSITIVE` during a request, its still-pending ticket is expired in the same request with `expiry_reason` `EVENT_CLOSED`, from either `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` or `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT`. And when the owning account is closed, any still-pending ticket is expired with `expiry_reason` `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED`, again from either pending stage.

### 3.7. Closing Out Anomalies: Resolution, Feedback and False-Positive Tuning

An anomaly event accepts a closing action only while its `event_state` is `DETECTED`, `ALERTED`, `ESCALATED`, or `SUPPRESSED`; an event already `RESOLVED` or `FALSE_POSITIVE` is terminal and refuses further feedback or resolution with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE`.

**Resolution** is what a homeowner does after the plumber leaves. It carries `resolution_note`, a free-text closure note of 1 to 280 characters, and moves the event to `RESOLVED` with `resolved_at` stamped at the clock and the event's `version` incremented by 1. Resolution is available from `ALERTED` and from `ESCALATED`, but in both cases only once the event's escalation ticket has left the pending rungs — the ticket's `stage` must be `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `EXPIRED`. This is why an acknowledgement matters operationally: it unblocks resolution. Resolving also expires the event's pending ticket with `expiry_reason` `EVENT_CLOSED`, and, once the burst is resolved, the valve reopen described in §3.4 no longer needs an explicit risk acknowledgement.

**Feedback** is the tuning channel. It carries a single `label` of either `TRUE_POSITIVE` or `FALSE_POSITIVE` and is recorded on the event in `feedback_label`. A `TRUE_POSITIVE` label is the simple case: the event moves to `RESOLVED` with `resolved_at` stamped, no threshold anywhere is touched, and `false_positive_count` is not incremented.

A `FALSE_POSITIVE` label is the interesting one, and it is admitted from `ALERTED`, from `ESCALATED`, and from `SUPPRESSED` alike — an event that never alerted because the device was snoozed can still teach the platform that its drip gate is too tight. In all three cases the device's baseline profile must currently be `COMPLETE`. The tuning then proceeds in four steps.

First, the event moves to `FALSE_POSITIVE` with `feedback_label` set and `updated_at` refreshed to the clock, and any still-pending escalation ticket on the event is expired with `expiry_reason` `EVENT_CLOSED`. Second, the profile's `false_positive_count` is recomputed as the number of the device's anomaly events labelled `FALSE_POSITIVE` whose `updated_at` falls at or after 30 days before the clock, this event included — a rolling thirty-day window, which means three false positives spread over forty days never accumulate to a relearn. Third, the live baseline is widened: `drip_threshold_gpm` becomes the smaller of an absolute ceiling of 0.50 gallons per minute and the current threshold increased by twenty percent, rounded half up to two decimal places; `last_false_positive_at` is stamped at the clock; and `baseline_version` increments by 1. The chain therefore climbs and then stops: starting from 0.12 it walks 0.14, 0.17, 0.20, 0.24, 0.29, 0.35, 0.42, 0.50, and thereafter every further piece of feedback leaves it at 0.50. Widening the gate changes future verdicts and only future verdicts — a reading at 0.13 gallons per minute that would have classified as `DRIP` before the first feedback classifies as `NONE` afterwards, while already-stored readings keep their frozen `classification_at_ingest`.

Fourth and last, when the recomputed `false_positive_count` reaches **three or more**, the platform stops tuning and demands a fresh start. The profile moves `COMPLETE → INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` `FALSE_POSITIVE_THRESHOLD` and `completed_at` cleared to null — and unlike the firmware and ownership-transfer invalidations, this one leaves the `false_positive_count` that tripped it standing rather than zeroing it, so the tally stays visible until the next accepted reading reopens the cycle and clears it; the device is demoted `ARMED → LEARNING` with `baseline_ready` set back to false and its `version` incremented by 1; the next accepted reading reopens the same profile row as `COLLECTING` with `learning_cycle_no` incremented; and any policy on the property that was in `CREDIT_ACTIVE` is suspended with a clawback as described in §3.8. The relearn discards every tuning step taken: the next `COMPLETE` profile recomputes `drip_threshold_gpm` from scratch as the greater of the 0.05 floor and two percent of the newly learned `baseline_peak_flow_gpm`, with no memory of the widened value.

### 3.8. The Insurer Credit Program: Policies, Attestation Periods and Uptime

A carrier enters the program by writing a policy against a property, an operation reserved to `InsurerAnalyst`. The submission names the insured `property_id`, an `insurer_code` of 2 to 12 uppercase characters identifying the carrier — the check is a length bound plus the absence of any lowercase letter, not a match against an alphabet, so digits and separators are admitted and `AETNA_HOME` is a valid code while `Aetna` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `policy_number` of 4 to 32 characters that must be unique across every policy the platform holds — a collision is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NUMBER_TAKEN` — an `annual_premium_cents` figure of at least 10000 cents which is the base the IoT credit is computed from, a `policy_year_start_at` instant which the very first attestation period must begin at exactly, the mandatory `expected_property_version` described in §2.6, and optionally `deductible_cents`, the water-damage deductible, which must not be negative and defaults to 100000 cents when omitted.

Writing a policy supersedes its predecessors. The new row is created with `credit_state` `PENDING_ENROLLMENT`, `current_credit_tier_pct` at 0, `credited_cents_total`, `clawback_cents_total`, `true_up_cents`, and `paid_payout_cents_total` all at 0, `finalized_period_count` at 0, `avg_uptime_pct` and `last_attestation_end_at` and `suspended_reason` all null, and `version` at 1. Every prior policy on the same property that is not already `TERMINATED` is driven to `TERMINATED` with its own `version` incremented by 1, and the property's `version` is incremented by 1 as well — which is exactly what makes a second analyst's stale `expected_property_version` detectable. Termination is one-way: a `TERMINATED` policy freezes its `credited_cents_total` as it stands, with no clawback applied on termination, and any write that depends on it — opening an attestation, finalizing one, submitting a claim, or advancing a claim decision — is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERMINATED`. A policy leaving `CREDIT_ACTIVE` for `TERMINATED` also resets `current_credit_tier_pct` to 0.

Measurement happens in **attestation periods**, which an `InsurerAnalyst` opens one at a time against a policy. The submission carries `period_start_at` and `period_end_at`, occupying the half-open span between them, with the end strictly after the start. Three constraints govern the chain. The period must not overlap any existing period on the policy — refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, with half-open semantics meaning a period beginning exactly where a prior one ended is not an overlap. The period must be contiguous: `period_start_at` must equal the policy's `last_attestation_end_at` when the policy already has a finalized period, and must equal the policy's `policy_year_start_at` for the very first period; a gap in the chain is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`. And the property's device must have a baseline profile currently in `learning_state` `COMPLETE`, or the opening is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_NOT_COMPLETE` — this is the only place the armed-device requirement is enforced at attestation-opening time, so a device that is `LEARNING` or whose profile is `INVALIDATED` fails here specifically.

A newly opened attestation records its `policy_id`, begins in `status` `OPEN`, has `period_minutes` computed immediately as the whole number of minutes in its half-open span, and leaves `covered_minutes`, `uptime_pct`, `offline_gap_count`, and `tier_pct` null, `credit_cents_awarded` and `true_up_cents` at 0, `is_policy_year_close` false, and `device_snapshot_lifecycle_state`, `denial_reason`, and `finalized_at` all null. The device that will be scored is not pinned at opening; it is resolved at finalize time as the property's single device that is not `DECOMMISSIONED`.

Finalizing is also an `InsurerAnalyst` operation and takes no body at all. It requires the attestation to still be `OPEN` — a second finalize is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_OPEN` — and requires the clock to have reached `period_end_at`, inclusively, so the boundary instant itself is finalizable while anything earlier is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`.

**Uptime coverage** is computed first. Every accepted telemetry reading for the resolved device whose `reading_at` falls at or after `period_start_at` and strictly before `period_end_at` vouches for the two hours of monitoring that follow it: each contributes a coverage interval beginning at its `reading_at` and ending at the earlier of two hours later or `period_end_at`, so the tail of the last reading is clamped at the period boundary. Those intervals are sorted by start ascending and merged, with two intervals merging whenever the next one's start is at or before the current one's end — adjacency with a zero-minute gap merges, so readings taken exactly two hours apart produce one continuous block with no lost minute (samples at 06:40 and 08:40 cover a single 240-minute stretch). `covered_minutes` is the total length of the merged intervals in whole minutes; `uptime_pct` is `covered_minutes` multiplied by 100, divided by `period_minutes`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because of the clamp, `covered_minutes` can never exceed `period_minutes` and `uptime_pct` can never exceed 100.00. Coverage never carries across a period boundary: a reading taken before `period_start_at` contributes nothing to this period even though its two-hour window would overlap it. `offline_gap_count` is the number of maximal uncovered stretches inside the period whose length is at least a full hour; shorter holes are not counted. A period with no accepted readings at all scores `covered_minutes` 0, `uptime_pct` 0.00, and an `offline_gap_count` of 1 whenever `period_minutes` is at least 60. Consider the concrete case of a period running from 2026-08-02T00:00Z to 2026-08-02T06:40Z — 400 minutes — with readings at 00:00, 02:00, 04:00, and 06:20: the first three merge into a continuous stretch from 00:00 to 06:00 and the last contributes a clamped 20 minutes, giving 380 `covered_minutes` and a `uptime_pct` of exactly 95.00, while the single 20-minute hole is shorter than an hour and so `offline_gap_count` is 0. Readings that were rejected by any telemetry validation are absent from the input set entirely and therefore drag uptime down.

The same computation is available as a read: a device health summary returns `device_id`, `lifecycle_state`, `uptime_pct`, `covered_minutes`, `period_minutes`, `offline_gap_count`, `false_positive_count`, and `drip_threshold_gpm` for the half-open span between the supplied `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, and when the window is omitted it defaults to the period of the newest `OPEN` attestation. It writes nothing.

**Credit award** runs second. A tier is selected from `uptime_pct` by the first matching band, with every cut inclusive and no interpolation between bands: at least 99.00 percent earns a `tier_pct` of 0.12, at least 97.00 percent earns 0.08, at least 95.00 percent earns 0.05, and anything below 95.00 percent earns nothing. Exactly 95.00 therefore awards 0.05 while 94.99 denies; exactly 97.00 awards 0.08 and exactly 99.00 awards 0.12.

Two payout variants exist, and they disagree by a few cents on purpose. The **provisional period accrual** runs on every finalize: `credit_cents_awarded` is the policy's `annual_premium_cents` multiplied by `tier_pct`, divided by the two attestation periods that make up a policy year, and rounded half up to a whole cent. This is the amount the homeowner sees credited during the year. The **final annual true-up** runs only on the finalize that brings the policy's `finalized_period_count` up to those two periods, at which point `is_policy_year_close` becomes true: the tier fractions of all the year's finalized periods, this one included, are summed; that sum is multiplied by `annual_premium_cents`, divided by two, and rounded half up to a whole cent *once*; and `true_up_cents` is that annual figure minus the sum of the `credit_cents_awarded` already booked across those same periods. Because rounding happens once in the annual computation and twice in the provisional one, the two disagree and `true_up_cents` may be negative. A worked case: an `annual_premium_cents` of 149900 with both periods at tier 0.05 books 3748 cents per period (from 3747.5 rounded half up), for 7496 booked, while the annual close computes 7495 cents from 149900 multiplied by 0.10 and divided by 2, so `true_up_cents` is −1.

An award writes `covered_minutes`, `uptime_pct`, `offline_gap_count`, `tier_pct`, `credit_cents_awarded`, `device_snapshot_lifecycle_state` — the resolved device's `lifecycle_state` read at that instant — and `finalized_at`, and moves the attestation `OPEN → FINALIZED_AWARDED`. It requires the resolved device to be `ARMED` and `uptime_pct` to be at least 95.00. On the policy side it sets `current_credit_tier_pct` to the attestation's `tier_pct`, adds the attestation's `credit_cents_awarded` to `credited_cents_total`, sets `last_attestation_end_at` to the attestation's `period_end_at`, and increments the policy's `version` by 1. Two policy figures are derived rather than accumulated and are recomputed at this point: `finalized_period_count` is the number of the policy's attestations whose `status` is either `FINALIZED_AWARDED` or `FINALIZED_DENIED`, and `avg_uptime_pct` is the arithmetic mean of `uptime_pct` across those same rows, rounded half up to two decimal places, or null when the policy has no finalized period yet. On a year-closing finalize the policy's own `true_up_cents` mirrors the closing attestation's `true_up_cents`. If the policy was `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` it moves to `CREDIT_ACTIVE`; if it was `CREDIT_SUSPENDED` it moves back to `CREDIT_ACTIVE` and its `suspended_reason` is cleared — the credit posture genuinely cycles across periods.

A denial moves the attestation `OPEN → FINALIZED_DENIED` and writes `covered_minutes`, `uptime_pct`, `offline_gap_count`, `denial_reason`, and `finalized_at`, leaving `credit_cents_awarded` at 0 and `tier_pct` at 0. Three denial reasons exist and they are mutually exclusive: `UPTIME_BELOW_FLOOR` when `uptime_pct` is below 95.00; `DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` when the resolved device's `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `ARMED`; and `NO_ENROLLED_DEVICE` when no device resolves for the policy's property at all. A denial is still a finalize for every other purpose: if it is the finalize that brings `finalized_period_count` up to the two periods of a policy year, `is_policy_year_close` becomes true on the denied attestation and the annual true-up described below is computed there exactly as it would be on an award, this period contributing a tier of 0.00 to the annual sum, with the resulting `true_up_cents` written to both the attestation and the policy and folded into `credited_cents_total` — while the denied period itself books no provisional credit. A denial still advances the chain, setting the policy's `last_attestation_end_at` to the period end, and when the policy was `CREDIT_ACTIVE` and the reason was `UPTIME_BELOW_FLOOR` it drives the policy `CREDIT_ACTIVE → CREDIT_SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `UPTIME_BELOW_FLOOR` and `current_credit_tier_pct` reset to 0. No clawback accompanies a denied period — the period simply awards nothing.

Clawback is reserved for the other suspension path. Whenever a device leaves `ARMED` mid-year, whether because firmware was pushed, because the false-positive threshold forced a relearn, or because a sale was accepted, any policy on that device's property sitting in `CREDIT_ACTIVE` moves to `CREDIT_SUSPENDED`. `suspended_reason` records `DEVICE_NOT_ARMED` for the firmware and false-positive demotions and `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` for a transfer acceptance. The clawback amount is the `credit_cents_awarded` of the single most recently concluded awarded period — that is, among the policy's attestations whose `status` is `FINALIZED_AWARDED` and whose `period_end_at` is at or before the clock, the one with the latest `period_end_at`, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, or zero when no such period exists. That amount is added to `clawback_cents_total` and subtracted from `credited_cents_total`, `current_credit_tier_pct` is reset to 0, and the policy's `version` increments by 1. The attestation rows themselves are never rewritten: `credited_cents_total` is defined as the sum of `credit_cents_awarded` across awarded periods, plus the `true_up_cents` of any policy-year-closing period, minus `clawback_cents_total`. A denied period contributes a tier of 0.00 to the annual sum and nothing to the provisional total, so it drags the annual number down without ever producing a clawback.

### 3.9. Reimbursement Claims: Warranty Hardware and Water Damage

A claim is filed by the homeowner (or by a `ProgramAdmin` on their behalf) and covers two quite different things under one lifecycle. The submission names the `policy_id` the claim is filed against, the `device_id` whose warranty or mitigation performance is at issue, a `claim_type` of either `WARRANTY_DEVICE` for a hardware failure or `WATER_DAMAGE` for loss reimbursement, a `documented_loss_cents` figure of at least 1 cent representing the documented loss or repair cost, and optionally an `anomaly_event_id` naming the qualifying event.

That optionality is load-bearing. A `WATER_DAMAGE` claim **must** carry `anomaly_event_id`, and the referenced event must belong to the referenced device, which must in turn belong to the policy's property; any of those three failures is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_EVENT`. A `WARRANTY_DEVICE` claim may omit it, and when it does the payout takes the flat warranty form regardless of any mitigation history. Filing is also time-boxed: the clock must be no later than 30 days after the referenced event's `detected_at`, with the last instant of the window still accepted, or the claim is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. And a given event may support only one live claim per type: if another claim already exists for the same `anomaly_event_id` and the same `claim_type` in any status other than `DENIED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` — a denied claim leaves the slot free to try again.

A new claim is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `approved_payout_cents` and `deductible_applied_cents` at 0, `info_request_count` at 0, `payout_variant`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `denial_reason`, and `paid_at` all null, `submitted_by_user_id` set to the resolved homeowner's identifier, and `version` at 1.

Adjudication is a single `decision` verb applied repeatedly, and each verb is legal from exactly one status. `BEGIN_REVIEW` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and belongs to a `ClaimsAdjuster`. `REQUEST_INFO` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and increments `info_request_count` by 1. `SUPPLY_INFO` moves it `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and is the homeowner's own verb — the acting homeowner must be the one recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`. That pair may repeat any number of times, and `info_request_count` is the record of how many round trips the file took. `APPROVE` and `DENY` both act from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and `PAY` acts only from `APPROVED`. A verb submitted against a status it is not legal from is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, and every accepted decision increments the claim's `version` by 1. Beginning review and approving both additionally require the claim's policy not to be `TERMINATED`.

Denial writes the supplied `denial_reason`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `decided_at`, leaves `approved_payout_cents` at 0, and is terminal. The reason must be supplied and is one of `NO_QUALIFYING_EVENT`, `OVERRIDE_DEFEATED_MITIGATION` — the code an adjuster reaches for when the homeowner reopened the valve and let the damage grow — `INSUFFICIENT_DOCUMENTATION`, or `ACCOUNT_CLOSED`.

Approval is where the money is computed, and the algebra has three variants selected in this order. If `claim_type` is `WARRANTY_DEVICE`, the variant is `WARRANTY_FLAT`. Otherwise, if the referenced event's `mitigation_credited` is true — meaning the valve closed automatically within 5 minutes of detection and was never overridden — the variant is `MITIGATED`. Otherwise the variant is `UNMITIGATED`, which covers no shutoff at all, a shutoff later than 5 minutes, and a shutoff that a homeowner override subsequently defeated.

The waterfall then runs by variant:

- Under `WARRANTY_FLAT`, `deductible_applied_cents` is 0 and `approved_payout_cents` is the smaller of `documented_loss_cents` and a warranty cap of 150000 cents. The deductible is ignored entirely and so is mitigation history, so a warranty claim arising from a burst that a snooze suppressed still pays up to the warranty cap.
- Under `MITIGATED`, `deductible_applied_cents` is 0 — the deductible is waived outright as the reward for a device that did its job — and `approved_payout_cents` is the smaller of `documented_loss_cents` and a water-damage cap of 500000 cents.
- Under `UNMITIGATED`, `deductible_applied_cents` is the smaller of `documented_loss_cents` and the policy's `deductible_cents`; the net loss is `documented_loss_cents` less that applied deductible; and `approved_payout_cents` is the smaller of two independently rounded figures — the net loss halved, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the 500000-cent water-damage cap halved, rounded half up to a whole cent. Both the loss and the cap are scaled, and the cap is rounded on its own before the smaller of the two is taken.

The divergence between the variants is deliberately large. On a documented loss of 620000 cents against a `deductible_cents` of 100000, the `MITIGATED` path pays 500000 cents with `deductible_applied_cents` of 0, while the `UNMITIGATED` path applies the full 100000-cent deductible and pays 250000 cents — the smaller of 260000 (half of the 520000 net) and 250000 (half of the cap). A quarter of a million cents turns on whether the homeowner let the valve stay shut. Note also the degenerate case: an `UNMITIGATED` claim whose deductible exceeds the loss sets `deductible_applied_cents` equal to `documented_loss_cents`, leaves a net of zero, and produces an `approved_payout_cents` of 0 — the claim is still `APPROVED`, a zero-value approval rather than a denial.

Two guards sit on the money. Approval requires that the policy's `paid_payout_cents_total` plus the newly computed payout not exceed an annual policy payout cap of 600000 cents; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_ANNUAL_PAYOUT_CAP_EXCEEDED` rather than silently truncated. Because `paid_payout_cents_total` only moves at payment time, two claims can each individually clear the cap check at approval and the second can then fail the identical check when payment is attempted. Approval also enforces the segregation of duties from §2.10: for a `WARRANTY_DEVICE` claim the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the device's `install_verified_by_user_id`. A successful approval writes `payout_variant`, `approved_payout_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `decided_at`, and moves the claim to `APPROVED`.

Payment is the `InsurerAnalyst`'s verb and completes the file. It re-checks the annual cap against `approved_payout_cents`, refuses when the paying `X-User-ID` equals the recorded `decided_by_user_id`, and on success stamps `paid_at` at the clock, moves the claim `APPROVED → PAID`, and increases the policy's `paid_payout_cents_total` by `approved_payout_cents` along with the policy's `version`. `PAID` and `DENIED` are the two terminal claim statuses.

### 3.10. Property Sale, the Operational Sweep and Account Closure

When a house is sold, the device travels with it, and the platform treats that as a two-party handshake rather than an administrative edit. The seller initiates a transfer naming the `device_id` changing hands, the `to_homeowner_id` of the buyer account that must accept, the mandatory `expected_device_version` from §2.6, and optionally a `transfer_note` of 1 to 280 characters. The row is created in `status` `INITIATED` with `from_homeowner_id` recording the property's current owner, `initiated_at` stamped at the clock, and `expires_at` set to 168 hours — a full week — after initiation; `accepted_at`, `accepted_by_user_id`, and `cancel_reason` all begin null.

Initiation is fenced by four business rules. The device must be transferable, meaning its `lifecycle_state` is `ARMED` or `LEARNING`; a device that was never installed (`REGISTERED` or `INSTALL_VERIFIED`) or that has been `DECOMMISSIONED` is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_TRANSFERABLE`. The house must be quiet: the device must have no anomaly event whose `event_state` is `DETECTED`, `ALERTED`, or `ESCALATED`, and its `valve_state` must be `OPEN`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EVENTS_BLOCK_TRANSFER`. The buyer must be somebody else — a `to_homeowner_id` equal to the property's current `homeowner_id` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`. And no claim may be in flight on the device: a transfer is blocked while any reimbursement claim for it sits in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED`.

Accepting the initiation freezes both device and property. The device moves from `ARMED` or `LEARNING` to `TRANSFER_PENDING` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the property moves `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING` with its `version` incremented by 1. Any other transfer already `INITIATED` for the same device is cancelled with `cancel_reason` `SUPERSEDED`, and the device stays `TRANSFER_PENDING` under the newer transfer — this supersession is legitimate product behaviour, which is precisely why the staleness check on `expected_device_version` exists rather than a state precondition. While the device is `TRANSFER_PENDING` it still ingests telemetry, but the classification ladder does not run at all — §3.3 confines it to an `ARMED` device — so such a sample freezes `classification_at_ingest` as `NONE` and mints no anomaly event whatsoever; were an anomaly to be minted while the device was not armed it would be created `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `DEVICE_NOT_ARMED`. Valve commands, mode changes, and firmware pushes are all refused with `ERR_DEVICE_TRANSFER_LOCKED`.

Only the named buyer may accept, and acceptance is the one operation exclusive to a single specific `Homeowner`. The transfer must still be `INITIATED` — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_PENDING` — the clock must be no later than `expires_at`, with acceptance at exactly that instant still succeeding and anything later refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `to_homeowner_id` (otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`) and that buyer account must be `ACTIVE`, and the device must still have an `OPEN` valve and no anomaly event in `ALERTED` or `ESCALATED` (otherwise `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EVENTS_BLOCK_TRANSFER`).

Acceptance then runs the longest cascade in the system, and every hop is part of the contract. The transfer moves `INITIATED → ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` stamped at the clock and `accepted_by_user_id` recording the buyer. The property's `homeowner_id` is rewritten to `to_homeowner_id` and its `property_state` moves `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented by 1. That ownership rewrite invalidates the learned baseline — the profile moves `COMPLETE → INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER`, `completed_at` cleared, and `false_positive_count` reset to 0 — because the new household's water habits are not the old one's. The invalidation in turn demotes the device `TRANSFER_PENDING → LEARNING`, sets `baseline_ready` back to false, forces `monitor_mode` to `NORMAL` with `monitor_mode_updated_at` refreshed and both `vacation_started_at` and `vacation_ends_at` cleared, and increments the device's `version` by 1. The demotion out of `ARMED` then suspends any policy on the property that was `CREDIT_ACTIVE`, moving it to `CREDIT_SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` and clawing back the newest awarded period exactly as described in §3.8. The next accepted reading reopens the same profile row as `COLLECTING` with `learning_cycle_no` incremented. One thing deliberately does *not* change: every irrigation window on the property whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is retained as it stands, because sprinkler schedules belong to the garden rather than to the gardener.

The **operational sweep** is a `ProgramAdmin` capability that gives the logical clock its consequences, and it applies three families of work in one fixed order. First, lapsed monitor modes revert: a device in `VACATION` whose `vacation_ends_at` the clock has reached moves to `NORMAL` with both vacation timestamps cleared, and a device in `SNOOZE` whose `snooze_window_end_at` the clock has reached moves to `NORMAL` with its window bounds retained for the cooldown; each device changed has `monitor_mode_updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Second, escalation SLA hops run exactly as described in §3.6. Third, lapsed transfers expire: a transfer still `INITIATED` once the clock is strictly past its `expires_at` moves to `EXPIRED`, restoring the device out of `TRANSFER_PENDING` — back to `ARMED` when its baseline profile is still `COMPLETE`, otherwise to `LEARNING` — and restoring the property `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented by 1. The sweep reports what it did as `swept_at`, `modes_reverted`, `tickets_escalated`, `tickets_expired`, and `transfers_expired`.

**Account closure** is the widest cascade in the platform and is a `ProgramAdmin` operation. It is refused outright while any reimbursement claim on the account has `status` `APPROVED` — an approved but unpaid payout must be settled before the account can close — with `ERR_OPEN_PAYOUT_BLOCKS_DEACTIVATION`. Deactivating an account that is already `DEACTIVATED` is refused with `ERR_HOMEOWNER_DEACTIVATED`. On success the homeowner moves `ACTIVE → DEACTIVATED` with `deactivated_at` stamped at the clock and `version` incremented, and the following cascade runs in full:

- every emergency contact on the account moves `ACTIVE → REMOVED`;
- every property moves to `ARCHIVED` with `archived_at` stamped and `version` incremented;
- every device on those properties moves to `DECOMMISSIONED` from whichever lifecycle state it held — `ARMED`, `LEARNING`, or `TRANSFER_PENDING` — with `decommissioned_at` stamped, `valve_state` forced to `CLOSED`, `monitor_mode` forced to `NORMAL`, and `version` incremented;
- every baseline profile on those devices that was `COMPLETE` moves to `INVALIDATED`;
- every anomaly event in `DETECTED`, `ALERTED`, `ESCALATED`, or `SUPPRESSED` moves to `RESOLVED` with `resolution_note` recorded as `ACCOUNT_CLOSED`;
- every escalation ticket still in `PENDING_HOMEOWNER` or `PENDING_EMERGENCY_CONTACT` moves to `EXPIRED` with `expiry_reason` `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED`;
- every policy not already `TERMINATED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `version` incremented, from `PENDING_ENROLLMENT`, `CREDIT_ACTIVE`, or `CREDIT_SUSPENDED` alike;
- every `OPEN` attestation moves to `FINALIZED_DENIED` with `denial_reason` `ACCOUNT_CLOSED`;
- every claim in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `ACCOUNT_CLOSED` and `decided_at` stamped at the clock;
- every transfer still `INITIATED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED`;
- every irrigation window in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_BY_SHUTOFF` moves to `CANCELLED`.

After closure the account can neither act nor be the target of a write: any attempt resolves to `ERR_HOMEOWNER_DEACTIVATED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All resources are mounted at the top level of the API. A request that creates a new persisted resource responds with HTTP 201; a request that modifies, advances, or annotates an existing resource responds with HTTP 200, as do all reads and the operational and clock-control capabilities. Successful responses carry the created or updated resource in `data` under the envelope defined in §2.2.
