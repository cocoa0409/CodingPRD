# Marrow Donor Registry Search & Workup API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An unrelated haematopoietic stem-cell transplant is a race between two clocks that neither the transplant centre nor the donor registry fully controls. On one side, a patient with an acute haematological malignancy is committed to a conditioning regimen that destroys their own marrow; once that regimen starts, there is no way back and a graft must arrive inside a narrow, pre-computed window. On the other side, the only genetically suitable donor may be a volunteer in a partner registry on another continent, whose allele-level typing is not yet known, whose availability was last confirmed months ago, and whose own health and personal calendar can invalidate the plan at any moment. This API is the operational backbone that a national marrow donor programme uses to hold those two clocks together: it scores the registry pool, chases confirmatory typing against contractual deadlines, screens and defers donors, drives the clinical workup and the two-physician collection-method decision, books apheresis and harvest slots against real centre capacity, tracks the product through courier custody, and reconciles the money owed to every registry involved.

The system is deliberately built around explicit, machine-checkable dependencies rather than coordinator judgement. Every donor that enters a search is scored twice — once from ambiguous registry-level typing and once from the allele-level typing the partner registry reports back — so that the programme can see how much of a candidate's attractiveness was an artefact of missing data. Every search carries a ranked backup bench, and every way of losing the primary donor (an unanswered typing request, a health deferral, a withdrawal, a failed physical) triggers the same deterministic promotion cascade rather than an ad-hoc re-search. Every cascade that cancels a booking, releases a donor's calendar or voids a claim must return the programme's derived counters to exactly the value they held before, because those counters are what the finance and capacity teams reconcile against. And because a mis-timed collection wastes an irreplaceable product, the conditioning timeline, the collection date, the infectious-disease result validity and the product viability clock are all enforced as hard preconditions, not warnings.

Five operator populations share the system: the transplant centre's coordinators who own the patient case, the physicians who carry donor-safety authority, the liaisons who speak for each donor registry, the collection centres that run the apheresis and harvest suites, and the national programme's own operations team. Their authority is intentionally non-overlapping, and in one case — the collection-method decision — the system requires two distinct physicians on the same case. All business resources are mounted under the `/registry` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint: who may call, what a response looks like, how lists page, how numbers round, how references resolve, and in what order rules fire when a request violates more than one of them.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator acts. If either header is missing, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names the system recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The five roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in the header, are `TransplantCoordinator`, `DonorRegistryLiaison`, `MedicalDirector`, `CollectionCenterOps` and `RegistryOperations`.

A `TransplantCoordinator` owns a patient case end to end: it registers the patient, opens the registry search, raises confirmatory-typing requests, opens the donor workup, and shares the conditioning timeline with the `MedicalDirector`. A `DonorRegistryLiaison` speaks for exactly one donor registry: it registers that registry's donors, answers confirmatory-typing requests, records health screening outcomes, maintains donor calendars, and files follow-ups. A `MedicalDirector` carries physician authority for donor and recipient safety: it records clearance results, authorises the collection method, drives conditioning start, infusion and abort, shares booking cancellation with `CollectionCenterOps`, and shares follow-up filing with `DonorRegistryLiaison`. A `CollectionCenterOps` operator acts for exactly one collection centre: it creates, confirms and cancels bookings, records the collected product, and files courier events shared with `RegistryOperations`. A `RegistryOperations` operator is purely operational — it maintains registries and collection centres, runs the deadline sweep, finalises reimbursement claims and drives the logical clock — and is barred from every clinical write: it may not record a clearance result, decide a collection method, drive a conditioning action, or record a screening outcome.

Two of the roles are backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `DonorRegistryLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Registry`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Registry` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a donor write requires that the target `DonorProfile` carries a `registry_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and a typing response or screening write requires that the candidate's donor carries a `registry_id` equal to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `CollectionCenterOps`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CollectionCenter`; a booking or shipment write requires that the booking's `collection_center_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The other three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `TransplantCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it — as `coordinating_user_id` on the `Patient` at patient creation, and as `opened_by_user_id` on the `WorkupCase` at workup open — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Instead, ownership is enforced by comparing the target patient's recorded `coordinating_user_id` to `X-User-ID`; only the coordinator who created a patient may drive that patient's search, typing, workup and conditioning writes, and any other coordinator is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `MedicalDirector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `resulted_by_user_id` on a `ClearanceItem`, as `physical_cleared_by_user_id` on a `WorkupCase` when the physical examination passes, and as `method_decided_by_user_id` on a `WorkupCase` when the collection method is authorised, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `RegistryOperations`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server neither records nor compares it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints page through two optional query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100, a non-integer `limit` or `offset`, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list endpoint documents a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two lists override that default: match-candidate lists are ordered by `rank_position` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and collection-booking lists are ordered by `start_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before paging and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every deadline, expiry, viability, calendar and date comparison reads a server-held logical UTC clock, which before any call has been made reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to the host's real time. A `RegistryOperations` operator may move that clock forward or backward through a test-driving capability at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the newly effective instant as `current_utc` and the instant it replaced as `previous_utc`. Setting the clock changes no business state whatsoever — it only changes the instant that subsequent reads and writes observe.

Two temporal granularities coexist and must not be conflated. Instant-valued fields (`requested_at`, `deadline_at`, `responded_at`, `expired_by_sweep_at`, `resulted_at`, `expires_at`, `start_at`, `end_at`, `confirmed_at`, `collected_at`, `must_infuse_by`, `departed_at`, `delivered_at`, `released_at`, `reported_at`, `finalized_at` and `last_sweep_at`) are compared exactly, to the second. Date-valued fields (`conditioning_start_date`, `deferral_until`, `collection_date`, `date_from` and `date_to`) are whole UTC calendar days written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and every comparison involving them is a calendar-day comparison in UTC. When an instant-valued field must be compared against a calendar date — as when the infectious-disease panel's `expires_at` is checked against a booking's `collection_date` — the instant is reduced to its UTC calendar date first.

### 2.5. Common Record Properties

Almost every resource in the system carries the same four system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent modification of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative inside a cascade. Whenever a cascade changes a row — cancelling a booking, releasing a calendar window, voiding a claim, re-deriving a clearance dimension — that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented exactly as if a client had written it directly.

There is one deliberate exception. `DonorFollowUp` rows are immutable append-only records: they carry only `id` and `created_at`. A follow-up row has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field should be present in its response payload.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision: Scores, Cents, Dates and Intervals

Match scores are decimal numbers carried to exactly two decimal places, bounded to the closed range 0.00 to 100.00, and rounded half up. The rounding is applied exactly once, to the final raw value of the score computation; every intermediate weighted sum is kept exact and is never rounded on the way. Candidate ranking scores are carried to exactly three decimal places, likewise rounded half up exactly once at the end of the weighted sum, and likewise bounded to the closed range 0.000 to 100.000. When a ranking score consumes a match score, it consumes the already-rounded two-decimal value, not the exact pre-rounding value.

Money is integer cents throughout. Every claim line, every derived amount and every total is a whole number of United States cents; where a computation produces a fractional cent it is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the rounding happens per line and per derived amount, never once at the end of a sum. Percentages used as rates — the volume discount rate, the courier surcharge rate, the coordination fee rate, the cancellation fee rate — are rates, are stored as decimal fractions, and are never rounded. Foreign-exchange rates are decimal numbers carried to exactly four decimal places and must be strictly greater than zero.

Interval arithmetic uses two different and deliberately incompatible conventions, and mixing them up is the single most common source of wrong answers. Collection-centre slot intervals are half-open minute-precision instant intervals: a slot occupies the time from its start instant up to but not including its end instant, so a slot beginning exactly at the instant another interval ends does not overlap it. Donor calendar windows, by contrast, are closed inclusive whole-day intervals: a window spanning two dates blocks both of those dates and every date between them, and two windows conflict as soon as they share a single calendar day. Adjacency is legal on the calendar: a window ending the day before another window begins is not a conflict.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Case-Level Writes

Two write paths carry an explicit version guard because both of them legitimately overwrite a decision another operator may have made in the meantime.

A `POST /registry/collection-bookings` request must carry `expected_workup_case_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the `version` of the target `WorkupCase` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the workup case's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORKUP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because creating a booking is a supersede operation: it silently retires the case's prior held slot, so a client whose view of the case has fallen behind would otherwise discard a slot decision it never saw.

A `POST /registry/patients/{id}/conditioning` request must carry `expected_patient_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the `version` of the target `Patient` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the patient's current `version`, the action is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a re-schedule overwrites the previously recorded `conditioning_start_date` and cancels every booking that falls outside the newly derived window; a client acting on a stale view of the patient would be rewriting a timeline it has not read.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection accept no `expected_workup_case_version`, no `expected_patient_version` and no other version parameter, and perform no version comparison.

### 2.8. Path and Payload Reference Resolution

Business resources are flat top-level collections under `/registry`, linked by explicit foreign-key body fields rather than by URL nesting. Where a nested read path does exist, it exists purely as a reverse-reachability channel — a way to list the children a cascade created — and never as a statement of business identity.

Identifiers that appear in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the response with HTTP 404. An unknown registry identifier yields `ERR_REGISTRY_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown collection centre identifier yields `ERR_COLLECTION_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown patient identifier yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown search request identifier yields `ERR_SEARCH_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown donor identifier yields `ERR_DONOR_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown match candidate identifier yields `ERR_MATCH_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown confirmatory typing request identifier yields `ERR_TYPING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown workup case identifier yields `ERR_WORKUP_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown clearance item identifier yields `ERR_CLEARANCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown collection booking identifier yields `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown product shipment identifier yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown reimbursement claim identifier yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. The donor calendar window release path carries two identifiers: the donor is resolved first, and the window second, so a window identifier that does not exist — or that exists but belongs to a donor other than the one named earlier in the path — yields `ERR_UNAVAILABILITY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier, and in the order in which the endpoint declares them. Opening a search resolves `patient_id`, and an unknown value yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`. Creating a collection booking resolves `workup_case_id` first and `collection_center_id` second, so a request naming a non-existent case and a non-existent centre reports `ERR_WORKUP_CASE_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_COLLECTION_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`. Registering a donor resolves `registry_id`, and an unknown value yields `ERR_REGISTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. The Order in Which Rules Are Evaluated

A single malformed request commonly violates several rules at once. Rules are therefore evaluated in a strict ascending priority order, and the **first** violated rule alone decides the response; no later rule can mask or override it. The ladder runs as follows.

The first two rungs are authentication and authorisation: header presence and role recognition (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then role-versus-endpoint eligibility (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`). The third rung is body schema conformance on every write endpoint (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`): required fields present, types correct, enum values in range, numeric bounds respected, HLA typing objects carrying all five loci with exactly two strings each, and a non-empty consented-methods list. The fourth rung is pagination validity on every list endpoint (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`). The fifth rung resolves path identifiers left to right, and the sixth resolves body foreign keys in declaration order, both as described in §2.8.

Rungs seven through ten are the scope and duty checks, in this order: patient ownership for a `TransplantCoordinator` (`ERR_PATIENT_NOT_OWNED`), registry scope for a `DonorRegistryLiaison` (`ERR_REGISTRY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), collection-centre scope for a `CollectionCenterOps` operator (`ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), and finally the two-physician rule on the collection-method decision (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Because segregation of duties sits above every state and safety rule, a physician who tries to authorise the method on a case they personally cleared is told so even when the case would also have failed a later check.

Rungs eleven through thirty-two are the state-machine gates, in exactly this order: search not terminal and not stalled (`ERR_SEARCH_ALREADY_TERMINAL`); candidate status admits the operation (`ERR_INVALID_CANDIDATE_STATE`); candidate is the primary (`ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_PRIMARY`); typing request still open (`ERR_TYPING_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`); donor available for a passing screening (`ERR_DONOR_NOT_AVAILABLE`); workup case still open (`ERR_WORKUP_CASE_NOT_OPEN`); clearance item not yet resulted (`ERR_CLEARANCE_ITEM_ALREADY_RESULTED`); clearance items resulted in their pinned order (`ERR_CLEARANCE_ORDER_VIOLATION`); method not yet decided (`ERR_METHOD_ALREADY_DECIDED`); method already decided (`ERR_METHOD_NOT_DECIDED`); clearance complete (`ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_COMPLETE`); booking held (`ERR_BOOKING_NOT_HELD`); booking cancellable (`ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CANCELLABLE`); booking confirmed and unharvested (`ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`); conditioning already started (`ERR_CONDITIONING_NOT_STARTED`); conditioning action legal from the current conditioning state (`ERR_INVALID_CONDITIONING_ACTION`); conditioning cross-entity precondition met (`ERR_CONDITIONING_PRECONDITION_UNMET`); product delivered (`ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_DELIVERED`); shipment event legal from the shipment's status (`ERR_SHIPMENT_EVENT_NOT_ALLOWED`); claim still provisional (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PROVISIONAL`); claim finalizable (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FINALIZABLE`); and calendar window manually releasable (`ERR_WINDOW_NOT_RELEASABLE`).

The two version guards of §2.7 sit immediately below the state gates and above every safety, temporal and constraint rule: `ERR_STALE_WORKUP_VERSION` on booking creation, then `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` on a conditioning action. A booking request that is both stale and outside the legal collection window therefore reports the stale version, and a conditioning schedule that is both stale and dated in the past reports the stale version.

The remaining rungs are the safety, referential, temporal and constraint rules, in this exact order: infectious-disease panel validity at the requested collection date (`ERR_IDM_RESULT_EXPIRED`); donor consent for the requested method (`ERR_DONOR_METHOD_NOT_CONSENTED`); screening outcome and deferral class agreement (`ERR_SCREENING_OUTCOME_MISMATCH`); adverse-event grade plausibility (`ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_GRADE_INVALID`); delivered product dose adequacy (`ERR_PRODUCT_DOSE_INADEQUATE`); donor registry cooperation (`ERR_REGISTRY_NOT_COOPERATING`); reported typing being allele-level (`ERR_CT_TYPING_NOT_ALLELE_LEVEL`); typing response inside the contractual deadline (`ERR_CT_DEADLINE_PASSED`); conditioning start date not in the past (`ERR_CONDITIONING_DATE_IN_PAST`); calendar window date order (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`); collection date inside the conditioning-derived window (`ERR_COLLECTION_OUTSIDE_CONDITIONING_WINDOW`); donor calendar non-overlap (`ERR_DONOR_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`); product viability at infusion (`ERR_PRODUCT_VIABILITY_EXPIRED`); collection-centre buffered capacity (`ERR_CENTER_SLOT_CONFLICT`); confirmatory-typing attempt budget (`ERR_CT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`); no duplicate open workup for the candidate (`ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_WORKUP`); no second concurrent search for the patient (`ERR_SEARCH_ALREADY_ACTIVE`); patient medical-record-number uniqueness (`ERR_MRN_ALREADY_EXISTS`); registry code uniqueness (`ERR_REGISTRY_CODE_ALREADY_EXISTS`); donor code uniqueness within a registry (`ERR_DONOR_CODE_ALREADY_EXISTS`); and last of all, the patient's case budget (`ERR_PATIENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`).

Three orderings inside that tail are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. On booking creation, the infectious-disease validity check precedes the conditioning-window check, which in turn precedes the centre capacity check — so a booking that is simultaneously past the panel's validity, outside the legal window and into a full centre reports `ERR_IDM_RESULT_EXPIRED`. On a conditioning infusion, the product-dose check precedes the viability check, so a delivered but under-dosed product reports `ERR_PRODUCT_DOSE_INADEQUATE` even when its viability clock has also run out. And on opening a workup, the budget check is the very last rung, which is why a candidate that is not the primary, or that already has an open case, is reported as such rather than as a budget failure.

Finally, one deliberate exception to the state ladder: for the purpose of the candidate-status gate, a candidate already in `WORKUP_OPEN` is treated as permitted, so that a second attempt to open a workup for the same candidate falls all the way through to `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_WORKUP` instead of being masked as an invalid candidate state. Every other candidate status outside the permitted sets is rejected at the candidate-status rung.

### 2.10. Derived Counters and the Reversibility of Cascades

Six fields in this system are not stored decisions but pure re-derivations from child rows, recomputed on every write that touches one of those children. `CollectionCenter.committed_slot_minutes` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over that centre's bookings whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`. `DonorProfile.blocked_day_count` is the sum of `day_count` over that donor's calendar windows whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `Patient.committed_cost_cents` is the sum of `total_usd_cents` over that patient's claims whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINALIZED`. `Registry.donor_count` is the count of donor profiles whose `registry_id` is that registry, counted regardless of their `availability_status`. `SearchRequest.match_pool_size` is the count of match candidates belonging to that search. `SearchRequest.backup_candidate_count` is the count of that search's candidates whose `candidate_role` is `BACKUP` and whose `status` is one of `IDENTIFIED`, `TYPING_REQUESTED`, `TYPING_RESPONDED` or `SCREENING_PASSED`.

Because each of those numbers is a re-derivation and not an accumulator, rows in the excluded terminal states never contribute: a `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED` booking contributes no minutes, a `RELEASED` calendar window contributes no days, and a `VOID` claim contributes no cents. That property is what makes the system's cascades exactly compensating. A booking that is created and then cancelled must leave its centre's committed minutes at precisely the value they held before the booking existed; confirming a booking and then cancelling it must leave the donor's blocked day count at precisely its prior value; and opening a workup and then voiding its provisional claim must leave the patient's committed cost at precisely its prior value. An implementation that increments and decrements counters by hand, rather than re-deriving them, will drift on the deeper cascades and is incorrect even when the individual arithmetic looks right.

### 2.11. Two-Physician Donor Safety and Registry Scope

Donor safety in an unrelated-donor programme rests on a separation between the physician who judges the volunteer fit to donate and the physician who decides how the donation will be taken. The system enforces that separation mechanically: the `X-User-ID` recorded as `physical_cleared_by_user_id` when a workup case's physical examination passed is barred from authorising that same case's collection method. An attempt by that operator is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, regardless of how much else about the request is valid. The two identifiers are compared per case, so the same physician may clear one case's physical examination and decide another case's method without any conflict.

Registry scope is enforced on the same principle of accountability, and it guards exactly six writes: registering a donor, blocking a donor's calendar, releasing a donor calendar window, filing a donor follow-up, answering a confirmatory-typing request, and recording a screening outcome. In all six the rejection code is `ERR_REGISTRY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Collection-centre scope likewise guards exactly five writes: creating a booking, confirming a booking, cancelling a booking, recording the collected product against a booking, and filing a courier event against that booking's shipment. In all five the rejection code is `ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Neither scope check can be waived by an operations override; there is no administrative bypass in this system.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Programme: Partner Registries and Collection Centres

Before any patient can be searched for, the national programme records the registries it is willing to search. A `RegistryOperations` operator registers a registry with a `code` of one to sixteen characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen, which must be unique across all registries — a repeat is refused with `ERR_REGISTRY_CODE_ALREADY_EXISTS` — together with a legal `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters, a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `currency_code` of exactly three uppercase letters naming the currency the registry invoices in, and an `fx_rate_to_usd` giving the United States dollar value of one unit of that currency, expressed as a positive number with exactly four decimal places.

The single most consequential field on a registry is `is_domestic`. Exactly the national home registry carries the value true; every other registry is an international partner, and that one boolean drives four separate downstream behaviours: partner donors attract a courier surcharge and an international coordination fee on the reimbursement claim, partner registries are granted extra grace hours on their confirmatory-typing deadline, partner donors forgo the domestic bonus in the match score, and partner donors are ranked through a different logistics factor. A registry also carries `cooperation_status`, which is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`, and `cooperation_tier`, an integer from 1 to 3 recording the partner service tier that feeds the ranking calculation. Finally, `ct_response_deadline_hours` — an integer from 1 to 720 — is the contractual number of hours the registry has to answer a confirmatory-typing request. The read view of a registry also exposes `donor_count`, the number of donor profiles filed under it.

Registries are maintained rather than replaced. An operations update may adjust `cooperation_status`, `cooperation_tier`, `fx_rate_to_usd` or `ct_response_deadline_hours`, each optionally, and each such update increments the registry's `version` by one. Three consequences of that update are precisely bounded. A new `fx_rate_to_usd` affects only claim numbers computed after the change: a claim already sitting in `PROVISIONAL` keeps the `fx_rate_snapshot` it captured, and only picks up the new rate if and when it is finalised. Moving a registry to `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` blocks any new confirmatory-typing request against that registry's donors but leaves already-`REQUESTED` typing requests entirely untouched — they may still be answered, and they still expire on their original schedule. And a new `ct_response_deadline_hours` applies only to typing requests raised after the change; deadlines already computed are frozen.

Collection centres are registered by the same operations role, with a `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters, a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and a `concurrent_collection_capacity` from 1 to 5 recording how many collections the centre can have running over any single instant. A newly registered centre starts with `committed_slot_minutes` at zero.

### 3.2. The Donor Pool: Registration, Typing Ambiguity and Calendar Blocks

A `DonorRegistryLiaison` files each volunteer as a donor profile under its own registry, and the `registry_id` in the payload must equal the acting `X-User-ID` — a liaison cannot enrol a volunteer into someone else's registry. Within that registry the `donor_code` must be unique, one to twenty-four characters long; a repeat inside the same registry is refused with `ERR_DONOR_CODE_ALREADY_EXISTS`, while the same code under a different registry is perfectly legal. The clinical payload comprises `age_years` as a whole number, `sex` as either `M` or `F`, `weight_kg` as a positive number with one decimal place, and `cmv_status` as either `NEGATIVE` or `POSITIVE`. Registration accepts ages from 17 through 75 inclusive — registries do enrol volunteers slightly outside the collection age band, and rejecting them at enrolment would corrupt the registry's own records — but the search eligibility filter of §3.3 admits only volunteers aged 18 through 60 inclusive, so a 17-year-old or a 61-year-old is a perfectly valid profile that will never be materialised into a candidate.

Registry-level tissue typing arrives as `hla_typing_registry`, an object with exactly the five keys `A`, `B`, `C`, `DRB1` and `DQB1`, each holding an array of exactly two allele strings. Allele-level strings are written as a field and an allele separated by a colon, such as `07:02`. Real registries, however, frequently hold only antigen-level resolution at some loci, and the donor payload declares that honestly through `ambiguous_loci`, an array that must be a duplicate-free subset of `A`, `B`, `C`, `DRB1` and `DQB1` and that defaults to empty. For any locus named in `ambiguous_loci`, the two strings in `hla_typing_registry` may be antigen-level — the first field only, such as `07` — while every locus not named there must carry full allele-level strings. Ambiguity is not free: each ambiguous locus both coarsens the comparison and costs a fixed penalty in the preliminary score, as §3.3 describes. Missing loci, wrong array lengths and malformed strings are all rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two further registration fields shape how attractive a volunteer is. `consented_methods` is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `PBSC` and `MARROW` recording the collection methods the volunteer agreed to at enrolment; a volunteer who consented only to peripheral blood stem cell donation can never be harvested by marrow aspiration, and the collection-method decision of §3.6 enforces that. `prior_donation_count`, an integer from 0 to 5 defaulting to 0, counts completed prior donations, and each prior donation penalises the volunteer's domestic logistics factor in the ranking calculation, because the programme prefers to spread the donation burden. A donor profile also accepts `availability_status` at registration, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`; the four legal values are `AVAILABLE`, `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`, `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` and `WITHDRAWN`, and after registration this field is mutated only by system transitions — a non-availability answer to a typing request, a screening deferral, an adverse event, or the lapse of a deferral. The two deferred and withdrawn end states behave as follows: `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal, while `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` has exactly one route back to `AVAILABLE`, described below. Newly registered donors start with `blocked_day_count` and `adverse_event_count` at zero, with `deferral_until` null and `last_followup_at` null, and each registration raises the owning registry's `donor_count` by one.

A donor's calendar is a set of unavailability windows, each spanning a closed inclusive date range from `date_from` through `date_to`. A liaison may block its own donor's calendar by hand, supplying `date_from`, `date_to` and a `reason` of either `PERSONAL` or `MEDICAL_HOLD`. The other two reasons in the enumeration, `DEFERRAL` and `COLLECTION_WINDOW`, are system-created only and a client that supplies either of them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A manually created window leaves `source_booking_id` null; only windows auto-created by booking confirmation carry a `source_booking_id` pointing back at the booking that generated them. If `date_to` is earlier than `date_from`, the window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Otherwise the server computes `day_count` as the number of calendar days in the closed interval — the difference between the two dates plus one, so a window whose two dates are equal has a day count of one — and adds that count to the donor's `blocked_day_count`. A new window starts with `status` `ACTIVE` and `released_at` null.

Donor calendars have a capacity of exactly one, with no buffer: a requested window that shares even a single calendar day with any of the donor's other `ACTIVE` windows is rejected with `ERR_DONOR_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`. Two windows conflict precisely when the first begins on or before the second ends and the second begins on or before the first ends. Windows already in `RELEASED` status never participate in the comparison, so a released window's dates are freely reusable. Adjacency remains legal: a window that ends on the day immediately before another begins is not a conflict.

Releasing a window is deliberately restrictive, because some windows are not the liaison's to release. A window whose `reason` is `PERSONAL` or `MEDICAL_HOLD` may be released at any time while it is `ACTIVE`. A window whose `reason` is `DEFERRAL` may be released only once the current logical UTC date is strictly later than the donor's `deferral_until`. A window whose `reason` is `COLLECTION_WINDOW` may never be released by hand at all — it is released only by the booking cascades of §3.7 and §3.8. Any attempt outside those rules, including a release aimed at a window that is already `RELEASED`, is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_RELEASABLE`. A successful release sets the window's `status` to `RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, and lowers the donor's `blocked_day_count` by that window's `day_count`; the call returns no body. Releasing the last `ACTIVE` `DEFERRAL` window of a `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` donor also restores the donor's `availability_status` to `AVAILABLE` and clears `deferral_until` back to null. That lapse is the only path from `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` back to `AVAILABLE`, and it is what makes a deferred candidate's return to the workflow possible at all.

Donor profiles can be listed and filtered by home registry through `registry_id` and by availability through `availability_status`, and each donor's calendar windows can be listed with optional filters on `status` and on `reason`. The windows a particular booking created are separately reachable from that booking, which is how an operator confirms that a cascade really did release what it claimed to release.

### 3.3. The Patient Case and the Preliminary Registry Search

A `TransplantCoordinator` registers the recipient with an `mrn` of one to thirty-two characters that must be unique across all patients — a repeat is refused with `ERR_MRN_ALREADY_EXISTS` — a `transplant_center_code` of one to sixteen characters naming the owning centre as a free-form label with no entity behind it, a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and a `diagnosis_code` of one to sixteen characters. Clinical urgency is captured in `urgency_tier`, one of `STANDARD`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`. Recipient `weight_kg` is a positive number with one decimal place and matters later as the denominator of the cell-dose adequacy judgement. Recipient `cmv_status` is `NEGATIVE` or `POSITIVE` and is compared against each donor's serostatus for a match bonus. Recipient typing arrives as `hla_typing`, an object with exactly the five keys `A`, `B`, `C`, `DRB1` and `DQB1`, each an array of exactly two allele-level strings in the colon-separated form; recipient typing is never antigen-level, and anything malformed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Finally, `case_budget_cents` is the non-negative integer ceiling in United States cents that the transplant centre has authorised for this case. At creation the server stamps `coordinating_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, sets `conditioning_status` to `NOT_SCHEDULED` with `conditioning_start_date` null, `conditioning_aborted_reason` null and `committed_cost_cents` at zero.

Opening a search is the single most computationally involved call in the system, because the entire candidate pool is scored, ranked, sliced and frozen inside that one call. The coordinator supplies `patient_id`, a `search_scope` of either `DOMESTIC_ONLY` or `DOMESTIC_AND_INTERNATIONAL`, and optionally `min_preliminary_score` — a two-decimal number in the closed range 0.00 to 100.00 that defaults to 60.00 and acts as the floor a donor must reach to be worth materialising. A patient may have only one live search: if the patient already has a search in `SEARCHING`, `PRIMARY_ENGAGED`, `WORKUP_ACTIVE` or `BACKUP_REQUIRED`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEARCH_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. The seven legal search states are `SEARCHING`, `NO_MATCH_FOUND`, `PRIMARY_ENGAGED`, `WORKUP_ACTIVE`, `BACKUP_REQUIRED`, `STALLED` and `FULFILLED`; a new search starts in `SEARCHING` with `last_sweep_at` null, and `NO_MATCH_FOUND` and `FULFILLED` are terminal.

**Eligibility.** Every donor profile in the system, joined to its home registry, is tested against six conditions, and only a donor satisfying all six enters the pool. The donor's `availability_status` must be `AVAILABLE`. The donor's registry must have `cooperation_status` equal to `ACTIVE`. The donor's `age_years` must be at least 18 and at most 60. When `search_scope` is `DOMESTIC_ONLY`, the donor's registry must be the domestic one; when it is `DOMESTIC_AND_INTERNATIONAL`, registries of either kind qualify. The donor's preliminary match score must be greater than or equal to the search's `min_preliminary_score` — a donor whose score exactly equals the floor is admitted. And the donor must not already be tied up elsewhere: a donor holding a match candidate row in `WORKUP_OPEN` or `COLLECTED` status on any search, including a different patient's search, is excluded from the pool.

**The preliminary match score.** The match probability score is a weighted allele-matching score computed over the five loci in the fixed order `A`, `B`, `C`, `DRB1`, `DQB1` — that fixed order, never the order in which the keys happen to appear in the payload. At each locus the recipient carries two strings and the donor carries two strings, and the matched count at that locus is the size of the multiset intersection of those two two-element lists, yielding 0, 1 or 2. The comparison happens at one of two resolutions. In the preliminary variant, a locus listed in the donor's `ambiguous_loci` is compared at antigen level, meaning only the portion of each string before the colon participates; every other locus is compared at allele level, meaning the full strings must be identical. The weighted sum accumulates, locus by locus, the locus weight multiplied by that locus's matched count, using weights of 1.0 for `A`, 1.0 for `B`, 0.8 for `C`, 1.4 for `DRB1` and 0.6 for `DQB1`. The base score is one hundred multiplied by the weighted sum and divided by 9.6, where 9.6 is the weighted sum a perfect ten-of-ten match would produce, namely twice the sum of the five weights.

From that base, the preliminary variant subtracts an ambiguity penalty of 3.5 points for each locus listed in `ambiguous_loci` — two ambiguous loci cost seven points, three cost ten and a half — and then adds a bonus made of three independent parts. The first is an age bonus read from the donor's `age_years`: 4.0 points for a donor aged 18 through 25, 2.0 points for 26 through 35, 0.5 points for 36 through 44, and nothing at all for 45 through 60. The second is a serostatus bonus of 1.5 points, awarded when the donor's `cmv_status` is identical to the recipient's `cmv_status` and otherwise zero. The third is a domestic bonus of 1.0 point, awarded when the donor's registry has `is_domestic` true and otherwise zero. The raw score is the base minus the penalty plus the bonus; the stored value is that raw score rounded half up to two decimal places and then clamped into the closed range 0.00 to 100.00. There is exactly one rounding step, applied to the raw value at the very end — the base, the penalty and the bonus are all kept exact until then.

**Ranking.** Every eligible donor is then given a candidate ranking score, a weighted blend of clinical match, donor age and logistical practicality. The age factor is one hundred reduced by 2.5 points for every whole year the donor's `age_years` differs from the ideal donor age of 27, taking the absolute difference in either direction, and the result is then held to no less than zero and no more than one hundred. The logistics factor is chosen by a three-branch rule. A domestic donor who has never donated before — `prior_donation_count` of zero — scores a full 100. A domestic donor with prior donations scores one hundred reduced by 15 points per prior donation, but never dropping below 40. An international donor scores 55 plus 5 points per unit of its registry's `cooperation_tier`, but never rising above 70. The ranking score is then 0.55 multiplied by the donor's already-rounded two-decimal `preliminary_score`, plus 0.25 multiplied by the age factor, plus 0.20 multiplied by the logistics factor, with the resulting weighted sum rounded half up to three decimal places exactly once.

**Materialisation.** Eligible donors are ordered by `rank_score` descending, then — for donors whose ranking scores tie — by `preliminary_score` descending, and finally by the donor profile's `created_at` ascending, so that the volunteer enrolled earliest wins a genuine tie. The identifier is never a tie-breaker. Scores are compared at their stored precision, two decimal places for match scores and three for ranking scores, with no re-rounding during the ordering. The first five rows of that ordering are materialised as match candidates and everything beyond the fifth is discarded; the survivors receive `rank_position` values 1 through however many were kept. Roles follow directly from position: the candidate at position 1 becomes `PRIMARY`, positions 2 and 3 become `BACKUP` — two backup slots — and positions 4 and 5 become `ARCHIVED_POOL`. Each materialised candidate records its `search_request_id`, its `donor_id`, the computed `preliminary_score` and `rank_score`, its frozen `rank_position` and its `candidate_role`, opens in `status` `IDENTIFIED` with `ct_attempt_count` at zero, and carries `confirmed_score`, `confirmed_match_grade`, `screening_outcome`, `screening_deferral_type` and `promoted_from_rank` all null. The search's `match_pool_size` records how many candidates were written and `backup_candidate_count` records how many of them are backups.

If not a single donor survives eligibility, the search moves to `NO_MATCH_FOUND` inside the creation call itself and writes no candidate rows at all: `match_pool_size` stays at zero, and the row is never observable in `SEARCHING`. Because ranking is a one-time act, `rank_position` is frozen at materialisation and is never recomputed afterwards — not when a donor is deferred, not when a backup is promoted, not when the pool shrinks. The ten legal candidate states are `IDENTIFIED`, `TYPING_REQUESTED`, `TYPING_RESPONDED`, `TYPING_EXPIRED`, `SCREENING_PASSED`, `DEFERRED`, `EXCLUDED`, `WORKUP_OPEN`, `COLLECTED` and `CLOSED`, of which `EXCLUDED` and `CLOSED` are terminal.

Candidates can be listed and filtered by `search_request_id`, by `donor_id`, by `candidate_role` and by `status`, and a patient's searches are separately listable, which is how a coordinator reaches a search it did not record the identifier of.

### 3.4. Confirmatory Typing: Requests, Deadlines and the Expiry Sweep

Registry-level typing is not good enough to transplant on, so the coordinator asks the donor's registry for allele-level confirmation. Raising a confirmatory-typing request is a coordinator action on a specific candidate, and the only body field it accepts is an optional `priority_flag` defaulting to false, which marks the request as clinically urgent. The candidate must be in `IDENTIFIED` or `TYPING_EXPIRED` status, its search must not be terminal or stalled, the donor's registry must have `cooperation_status` of `ACTIVE` — a suspended or terminated partner is refused with `ERR_REGISTRY_NOT_COOPERATING` — and the candidate's `ct_attempt_count` must be strictly below the ceiling of two attempts. A third request against the same candidate is refused with `ERR_CT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`.

A successful request raises the candidate's `ct_attempt_count` by one and moves the candidate `IDENTIFIED → TYPING_REQUESTED`, or `TYPING_EXPIRED → TYPING_REQUESTED` on the second attempt, which is exactly why the candidate's history is cyclic: `TYPING_REQUESTED → TYPING_EXPIRED → TYPING_REQUESTED`, bounded to two attempts in total. The created request records `match_candidate_id`, an `attempt_number` equal to the candidate's `ct_attempt_count` after the increment — so 1 or 2 — a `requested_at` stamped from the logical clock, and a computed `deadline_at`. It opens in `status` `REQUESTED` with `responded_at`, `reported_hla_typing`, `reported_match_grade`, `donor_available_flag` and `expired_by_sweep_at` all null, and its four legal states are `REQUESTED`, `RESPONDED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED`, the last three all terminal. When the candidate carrying the request is the search's `PRIMARY`, the search itself advances `SEARCHING → PRIMARY_ENGAGED`; when the candidate is a newly promoted primary, the same act moves the search `BACKUP_REQUIRED → PRIMARY_ENGAGED`, and that cycle may repeat once for each available backup.

The deadline is computed once, at request time, and then frozen. It begins at `requested_at` plus the donor registry's contractual `ct_response_deadline_hours`. When the donor's registry has `is_domestic` false, a further 48 hours of grace — two full days — are added, in recognition of the time zones and translation involved in international coordination. When `priority_flag` is true, 24 hours are subtracted. All three adjustments are whole hours applied to the stored instant, and they combine: an urgent request against a partner registry gets the contractual hours plus 48 minus 24, a net addition of 24 hours.

The donor's registry answers through its liaison, supplying `reported_hla_typing` and `donor_available_flag`. The typing request must still be in `REQUESTED` status — a request that is already `RESPONDED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_TYPING_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN` — and the acting liaison must be the donor's own registry. `reported_hla_typing` is an object with exactly the five keys `A`, `B`, `C`, `DRB1` and `DQB1`, each an array of exactly two strings, and every one of those ten strings must be allele-level: if any string at any of the five loci is antigen-level, meaning it contains no colon, the response is refused with `ERR_CT_TYPING_NOT_ALLELE_LEVEL`. Timeliness is judged against the frozen deadline: a response filed strictly after `deadline_at` is refused with `ERR_CT_DEADLINE_PASSED`, while a response filed at exactly the deadline instant is accepted.

A valid response stamps `responded_at` from the logical clock, stores the reported typing and the supplied `donor_available_flag`, and moves the request `REQUESTED → RESPONDED`. Two very different things happen next, depending on the availability answer. When `donor_available_flag` is true, the confirmed variant of the match score is computed and stored: every one of the five loci is compared at allele level, the ambiguity penalty is zero because the reported typing carries no ambiguity, and the resulting score lands in the candidate's `confirmed_score`. Because the same donor data is now compared at full resolution and without penalty, a donor with ambiguous registry loci will produce a strictly different confirmed number than its preliminary one. Alongside the score, the response computes the allele-level matched count — the sum across the five loci of the allele-level matched counts, a whole number from 0 through 10 — and renders it as a grade string of that number followed by a slash and ten. That string is stored as the request's `reported_match_grade` and copied onto the candidate's `confirmed_match_grade`, and the candidate moves `TYPING_REQUESTED → TYPING_RESPONDED`.

The grade then gates admission. The programme will not transplant below nine matched alleles out of ten, so a candidate whose matched-allele count is lower than nine is moved straight to `EXCLUDED` inside the same response call — its `confirmed_score` is still stored for audit, but it never rests in `TYPING_RESPONDED` — and if that candidate was the search's primary, the promotion cascade runs. When `donor_available_flag` is false instead, the registry is telling the programme the volunteer is no longer available: the request still becomes `RESPONDED`, but no confirmed score is computed at all, the donor moves `AVAILABLE → WITHDRAWN`, the candidate is moved to `EXCLUDED`, and the promotion cascade runs when that candidate was the primary.

**The deadline sweep.** Requests do not expire on their own; expiry is an explicit operations act scoped to one search. A `RegistryOperations` operator sweeps a search, and the sweep walks that search's typing requests that are still in `REQUESTED` status in `created_at` ascending order. For each of them, the current logical instant is compared against `deadline_at` with a strict test: only a request whose deadline lies strictly before the current instant expires. A sweep that lands at exactly the deadline instant expires nothing; a sweep one second later expires it. An expiring request moves `REQUESTED → EXPIRED`, has `expired_by_sweep_at` stamped with the sweep instant, and drags its candidate `TYPING_REQUESTED → TYPING_EXPIRED`. If that candidate is the `PRIMARY` and its `ct_attempt_count` has already reached two, the candidate is additionally moved `TYPING_EXPIRED → EXCLUDED` and the promotion cascade runs. A `BACKUP` candidate at two attempts is deliberately *not* auto-excluded: it rests in `TYPING_EXPIRED`, which is precisely how a later third request against it becomes reachable and is refused with `ERR_CT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`.

Every sweep stamps the search's `last_sweep_at` with the sweep instant, whether or not anything expired. After processing, if the search has no candidate in the `PRIMARY` role sitting in a non-terminal status and no promotable backup remains — its `backup_candidate_count` having fallen to zero — the search moves to `STALLED`. Concretely, the search moves `BACKUP_REQUIRED → STALLED` when every one of its candidates is in `EXCLUDED`, `DEFERRED`, `CLOSED`, or in `TYPING_EXPIRED` with `ct_attempt_count` already at two. A stalled search accepts no further workflow operations: subsequent typing requests and workup opens against its candidates are refused with `ERR_SEARCH_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, exactly as they are for a `NO_MATCH_FOUND` or `FULFILLED` search. A candidate's typing requests are listable with an optional `status` filter, which is how an operator inspects both attempts of a twice-requested candidate.

### 3.5. Health Screening, Deferral and the Backup Promotion Cascade

Once a registry has confirmed the typing, its liaison screens the volunteer's health and records the outcome against the candidate. The submission carries `outcome`, `deferral_type`, an optional `deferral_until` and an optional `questionnaire_flags` payload capturing the screening questionnaire's answers. The candidate must be in `TYPING_RESPONDED` or `DEFERRED` status, and the acting liaison must own the donor's registry.

The three outcomes must agree with their deferral class, and a disagreement is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_OUTCOME_MISMATCH`. An `outcome` of `PASS` requires `deferral_type` `NONE` and no `deferral_until` at all. An `outcome` of `DEFER` requires `deferral_type` `TEMPORARY` together with a `deferral_until` that is strictly later than the current logical UTC date. An `outcome` of `FAIL` requires `deferral_type` `PERMANENT` and no `deferral_until`. The recorded outcome lands in the candidate's `screening_outcome` and the class in its `screening_deferral_type`.

A passing screening additionally requires that the donor's `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` at that moment; a `PASS` recorded against a donor who is `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`, `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_DONOR_NOT_AVAILABLE`. A clean pass moves the candidate `TYPING_RESPONDED → SCREENING_PASSED`, recording `screening_outcome` `PASS` and `screening_deferral_type` `NONE`.

A deferring screening moves the candidate `TYPING_RESPONDED → DEFERRED` and moves the donor `AVAILABLE → TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`. The supplied `deferral_until` is written onto the donor, and the system creates an `ACTIVE` calendar window for that donor with `reason` `DEFERRAL`, spanning the current logical UTC date through `deferral_until` inclusive, which raises the donor's `blocked_day_count` by that window's `day_count`. When the deferred candidate was the search's primary, the promotion cascade runs and the search moves `PRIMARY_ENGAGED → BACKUP_REQUIRED`.

A failing screening is permanent. The donor moves `AVAILABLE → PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED`, the candidate moves `TYPING_RESPONDED → EXCLUDED`, and the promotion cascade runs when that candidate was the primary. Permanent deferral removes the volunteer from every future eligible pool, and it also reaches sideways: every non-terminal match candidate anywhere in the system that names this donor is moved to `EXCLUDED`, and every one of their typing requests still in `REQUESTED` status is moved `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`, after which no response may ever be filed against them.

Deferral is not necessarily the end of a candidate's story. Once the deferral has lapsed — the current logical UTC date having passed `deferral_until`, and the `DEFERRAL` window having been released, restoring the donor to `AVAILABLE` — the same candidate may be screened again, and a `PASS` at that point moves it `DEFERRED → SCREENING_PASSED`, overwriting `screening_outcome` with `PASS` and `screening_deferral_type` with `NONE`. This re-entry needs no new typing request: the confirmed score and grade the candidate already carries remain valid.

**The promotion cascade.** Whenever a search loses its primary — through an exhausted typing budget, a withdrawal, an unacceptable match grade, a temporary or permanent screening deferral, or a failed clearance item in the workup — the same deterministic cascade runs, and it never re-ranks anything. Among that search's candidates whose `candidate_role` is `BACKUP`, the promotable ones are those in `IDENTIFIED` status and those in `TYPING_EXPIRED` status whose `ct_attempt_count` is still below two. Of the promotable ones, the candidate holding the smallest `rank_position` is lifted to `candidate_role` `PRIMARY`, and its `promoted_from_rank` records the `rank_position` it held when it was promoted. The outgoing primary's `candidate_role` becomes `ARCHIVED_POOL`. The search's `backup_candidate_count` therefore falls by one. Critically, the vacated backup slot is *not* refilled: the `ARCHIVED_POOL` candidate with the smallest `rank_position` is never auto-promoted into it, so a search's backup bench only ever shrinks. If no promotable backup exists when the cascade is invoked, no promotion occurs and the search is left for the sweep to move to `STALLED`.

### 3.6. The Donor Workup: Clearance Ladder and the Collection-Method Decision

With a screened donor in hand, the coordinator opens the donor workup against the candidate. The call takes no body fields whatsoever. The candidate's search must not be terminal or stalled — an attempt against a candidate of a `NO_MATCH_FOUND`, `FULFILLED` or `STALLED` search is refused with `ERR_SEARCH_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, exactly as a typing request would be. The candidate must be in `SCREENING_PASSED` status and its `candidate_role` must be `PRIMARY`; a backup or archived candidate is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_PRIMARY`. The candidate must not already hold another workup case in `case_status` `OPEN`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_WORKUP`. And the money must be there: the patient's current `committed_cost_cents` plus the total of the provisional claim about to be created must not exceed the patient's `case_budget_cents`, or the open is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

A successful open creates a great deal at once. The workup case records its `match_candidate_id`, stamps `opened_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and opens with `case_status` `OPEN`, `clearance_status` `PENDING`, `logistics_status` `UNSCHEDULED`, `collection_method` null, `method_decided_by_user_id` null, `physical_cleared_by_user_id` null, `idm_resulted_at` null and `abort_reason` null. Its `planned_window_days` starts at 6, which is the planning default used while no method has been chosen. Three clearance items are created together, distinguished by their `item_type` and written in a pinned order that never varies — first `PHYSICAL_EXAM`, then `IDM_PANEL`, then `CONSENT` — each recording its `workup_case_id`, each with `result` `PENDING`, `resulted_at` null, `resulted_by_user_id` null, `expires_at` null and no `note`. A provisional reimbursement claim is created in the same breath, raising the patient's `committed_cost_cents` by its total. The candidate moves `SCREENING_PASSED → WORKUP_OPEN` and the search moves `PRIMARY_ENGAGED → WORKUP_ACTIVE`.

The workup case is unusual in carrying **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `case_status` is the overall disposition and takes the values `OPEN`, `ABORTED` and `COMPLETED`, the latter two terminal. `clearance_status` is the clinical dimension, derived entirely from the three clearance items, and takes the values `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CLEARED` and `FAILED`, the latter two terminal. `logistics_status` is the operational dimension, driven by the method decision, the booking, the collection and the transport, and takes the values `UNSCHEDULED`, `METHOD_SET`, `BOOKED`, `COLLECTED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` and `PRODUCT_LOST`, of which `DELIVERED` and `PRODUCT_LOST` are terminal. The two dimensions evolve independently of one another until booking creation couples them by demanding that clearance be complete; a `MedicalDirector` can perfectly well decide the method while clearance is still `IN_PROGRESS`.

A `MedicalDirector` records each clearance result with a `result` of either `PASS` or `FAIL` and an optional free-text `note`. The case must be in `case_status` `OPEN`, or the write is refused with `ERR_WORKUP_CASE_NOT_OPEN`. The item must still carry `result` `PENDING`, or the write is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_ITEM_ALREADY_RESULTED`. And the three items must be resulted in their created order: `IDM_PANEL` may be resulted only once `PHYSICAL_EXAM` carries `PASS`, and `CONSENT` only once both `PHYSICAL_EXAM` and `IDM_PANEL` carry `PASS`. Any attempt out of order is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_ORDER_VIOLATION`. A recorded result stamps `resulted_at` from the logical clock and `resulted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`.

Two of the three items carry extra consequences on a `PASS`. A passing `PHYSICAL_EXAM` stamps the case's `physical_cleared_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the comparand the two-physician rule uses later. A passing `IDM_PANEL` sets that item's `expires_at` to its `resulted_at` plus thirty days — infectious-disease results are considered valid for one month and no longer — and surfaces the same instant as the case's `idm_resulted_at`, which stays null unless the panel actually passed. The `CONSENT` item carries no derived field, and its `expires_at` remains null, as does that of `PHYSICAL_EXAM`.

The parent's clearance dimension is then re-derived from its three children. The first passing result moves the case `PENDING → IN_PROGRESS`. A passing result on the last item still pending, with the other two already carrying `PASS`, moves the case `IN_PROGRESS → CLEARED`. Any failing result moves the dimension to `FAILED` — `PENDING → FAILED` when the failure is the very first result recorded, and `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED` when it follows an earlier pass — and once the dimension is `FAILED` the remaining items keep `result` `PENDING` forever and are never resultable again.

A failing clearance result is the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs in full whichever of the three items failed. The case moves `case_status` `OPEN → ABORTED` with `clearance_status` `FAILED` and `abort_reason` recorded. When the failure was on the `PHYSICAL_EXAM` or `IDM_PANEL` item, the donor is also moved `AVAILABLE → TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`: no client-supplied deferral date is accepted on this path, and `deferral_until` is instead derived as the current logical UTC date plus 90 days, with an `ACTIVE` `DEFERRAL` calendar window created over that same span, raising the donor's `blocked_day_count`. The candidate moves `WORKUP_OPEN → DEFERRED`. The case's provisional reimbursement claim moves `PROVISIONAL → VOID` with its `void_reason` recorded, and the patient's `committed_cost_cents` drops back by that claim's `total_usd_cents`, returning to exactly the value it held before the workup was opened. The promotion cascade runs, and the search moves `WORKUP_ACTIVE → BACKUP_REQUIRED`.

**The collection-method decision.** With the donor progressing, a `MedicalDirector` authorises how the product will be taken, supplying a `method` of `PBSC` or `MARROW` together with a mandatory `rationale` recording the clinical reasoning. The case must be `OPEN`. The method must not already have been decided: a case whose `collection_method` is already set — whose `logistics_status` has already left `UNSCHEDULED` — is refused with `ERR_METHOD_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The requested method must be contained in the donor's `consented_methods`, and a method the volunteer never consented to is refused with `ERR_DONOR_METHOD_NOT_CONSENTED`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `physical_cleared_by_user_id`; the physician who cleared the volunteer's physical examination is barred from this decision and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The decision stores `collection_method` and stamps `method_decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, moves the case `UNSCHEDULED → METHOD_SET` on its logistics dimension, sets `planned_window_days` to 6 for `PBSC` or 8 for `MARROW`, and increments the case's `version` by one — which matters immediately, because that version is what the next booking attempt must quote. The case's three clearance items are listable in their creation order, with the physical examination at index zero, the infectious-disease panel at index one and the consent at index two; a candidate's workup cases are listable with an optional `case_status` filter.

### 3.7. Booking the Collection, Harvesting the Product and Courier Transport

A collection slot is held by a `CollectionCenterOps` operator, and the request names `workup_case_id`, `collection_center_id`, the slot's `start_at` as a UTC instant at minute precision, and the mandatory `expected_workup_case_version`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `collection_center_id` being booked. Five substantive gates then apply, and they fire in the order §2.9 fixes: the case must be `OPEN`; its `collection_method` must already be set, or the request is refused with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_DECIDED`; its `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_COMPLETE`; the quoted case version must be current, or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_WORKUP_VERSION`; then the infectious-disease validity, conditioning-window and centre-capacity checks below.

The booking copies `method` from the case and freezes it, so a booking's method is immune to anything that happens to the case later. From the method it derives `duration_minutes` — 300 minutes for `PBSC`, 240 minutes for `MARROW` — then `end_at` as `start_at` advanced by that many minutes, and `collection_date` as the UTC calendar date of `start_at`. That derived date is the anchor of two separate rules. The booking opens with `status` `HELD`, `confirmed_at` null, `cancel_reason` null and `cancelled_after_confirmation` false; its five legal states are `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED`, the last three terminal. Creating it adds its `duration_minutes` to the centre's `committed_slot_minutes`, moves the case `METHOD_SET → BOOKED` on the logistics dimension, and increments the case's `version` by one.

**Infectious-disease validity.** The infectious-disease panel result must still be valid on the day of collection: the booking's `collection_date` must fall on or before the UTC calendar date of the `IDM_PANEL` clearance item's `expires_at`. A collection date after that day is refused with `ERR_IDM_RESULT_EXPIRED` — a booking sitting exactly on the expiry day is accepted.

**The conditioning window.** The recipient's conditioning regimen anchors the only legal collection dates. From the patient's `conditioning_start_date`, the legal window opens six calendar days later and closes eight calendar days later, inclusive on both ends — so exactly three candidate dates. A booking whose `collection_date` falls outside that span, or a booking attempted while the patient is not yet in `SCHEDULED` or `STARTED` conditioning at all, is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_OUTSIDE_CONDITIONING_WINDOW`.

**Centre capacity.** A collection centre's capacity is an aggregate, not a single-slot lock, and it is enforced with a turnaround buffer. The requested slot occupies the half-open interval from its `start_at` up to but not including its `end_at`. Every existing booking of the same centre whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` is expanded into a buffered half-open interval running from 90 minutes before its `start_at` up to but not including 90 minutes after its `end_at` — an hour and a half of cleaning and turnaround at each end. The requested slot overlaps such a buffered interval when the requested start is strictly before the buffered end and the buffered start is strictly before the requested end. Count the overlapping bookings; if that count has reached or passed the centre's `concurrent_collection_capacity`, the request is refused with `ERR_CENTER_SLOT_CONFLICT`, and only when the count is strictly below capacity does the booking succeed. Because the intervals are half-open, a slot beginning exactly at a buffered interval's end instant does not overlap it and is admitted. Bookings in `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED` status are excluded from the comparison entirely, and they likewise contribute nothing to `committed_slot_minutes`.

Booking creation is deliberately a supersede operation. If the case already holds a booking in `HELD` status, that earlier booking moves `HELD → SUPERSEDED`, records the reason `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_BOOKING` in its `cancel_reason`, and releases its `duration_minutes` back out of the centre's `committed_slot_minutes`. That is exactly why the quoted `expected_workup_case_version` is mandatory: without it, an operator working from a stale view of the case would silently discard a slot decision it never observed.

Confirming a held slot is the centre's own act, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `collection_center_id`. The booking must be in `HELD` status; a confirm aimed at anything else is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_HELD`. Confirmation stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock and creates the donor's collection window: an `ACTIVE` calendar window with `reason` `COLLECTION_WINDOW` and `source_booking_id` pointing back at this booking, spanning from a number of days before `collection_date` through a number of days after it. Those offsets are method-specific and asymmetric, reflecting how the two procedures actually burden a volunteer: a `PBSC` collection blocks the four days before the collection date and the one day after it, because mobilisation injections precede apheresis, while a `MARROW` harvest blocks the one day before and the six days after, because recovery from general anaesthesia and aspiration takes far longer. Either way the window's `day_count` is the inclusive number of days it spans, which comes to six for `PBSC` and eight for `MARROW` — precisely the case's `planned_window_days` — and it raises the donor's `blocked_day_count` accordingly. If that derived window overlaps any `ACTIVE` window already on the donor's calendar under the closed inclusive whole-day comparison of §3.2, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_DONOR_CALENDAR_CONFLICT` and nothing is created.

Cancelling a booking is shared between the collection centre and a `MedicalDirector`, and it requires a `reason`. The booking must be in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status and must have no product shipment recorded against it; anything else — a `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED` booking, or one that already produced a shipment — is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CANCELLABLE`. Cancellation records the supplied reason in `cancel_reason`, releases the booking's `duration_minutes` from the centre's `committed_slot_minutes`, and moves any `COLLECTION_WINDOW` window that this booking sourced to `RELEASED`, restoring the donor's `blocked_day_count` to exactly its pre-confirmation value. Whether the booking had been confirmed matters for the money: cancelling from `HELD → CANCELLED` leaves `cancelled_after_confirmation` false and costs the programme nothing, whereas cancelling from `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` sets `cancelled_after_confirmation` true, which adds a cancellation line to the case's final claim. Finally, if the case is left with no booking in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status, the case's logistics dimension reverts `BOOKED → METHOD_SET` while its clearance dimension is untouched — the case is bookable again without redoing any clinical work.

**Recording the collected product.** The collection centre records the harvest, supplying `collected_cd34_e6_per_kg` as a positive number with two decimal places, `collected_volume_ml` as a positive number with one decimal place, and a `courier_reference` of one to forty characters carrying the air-waybill or chain-of-custody number. The booking must be in `CONFIRMED` status with no shipment already recorded, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and the recipient's conditioning must already be under way — `conditioning_status` `STARTED` — or the request is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONING_NOT_STARTED`. This is the hard interlock of the whole system: no product is taken from a volunteer until the recipient is irreversibly committed.

The shipment records its `collection_booking_id`, stamps `collected_at` from the logical clock as the moment collection ended, and derives `must_infuse_by` as that instant advanced by the method's viability allowance — 72 hours, three full days, for a `PBSC` product, and 48 hours, two full days, for a `MARROW` product. It also derives `dose_adequacy`, which is `ADEQUATE` when `collected_cd34_e6_per_kg` is greater than or equal to two million cells per kilogram — the threshold expressed in the same units of one million as the field itself, namely 2.00 — and `INADEQUATE` otherwise. The shipment opens with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `departed_at` null, `delivered_at` null and `transit_event_count` at zero; its five legal states are `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `DISCARDED` and `CANCELLED`, the last three terminal. Recording the product moves the booking `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` — and a `COMPLETED` booking keeps counting towards its centre's `committed_slot_minutes` — moves the candidate `WORKUP_OPEN → COLLECTED`, and moves the case `BOOKED → COLLECTED` on the logistics dimension.

**Courier custody.** Transit events are filed by the collection centre or by `RegistryOperations`, each supplying an `event_type` of `DEPARTED`, `DELIVERED` or `LOST` and an optional `note`. Every accepted event increments the shipment's `transit_event_count` by one. The shipment's status must admit the event: `DEPARTED` is legal only from `SCHEDULED`, and `DELIVERED` and `LOST` are legal only from `IN_TRANSIT`; anything else is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_EVENT_NOT_ALLOWED`. A `DEPARTED` event stamps `departed_at` from the logical clock and moves the shipment `SCHEDULED → IN_TRANSIT`, carrying the case `COLLECTED → IN_TRANSIT`. A `DELIVERED` event filed at or before `must_infuse_by` stamps `delivered_at` and moves the shipment `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`, carrying the case `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`, at which point the recipient may be infused. A `LOST` event, or a `DELIVERED` event filed strictly after `must_infuse_by`, moves the shipment `IN_TRANSIT → DISCARDED` instead and carries the case `IN_TRANSIT → PRODUCT_LOST`; on the late-delivery path `delivered_at` is still stamped, recording when the product actually arrived. A discarded product does not refund anything: the case's claim keeps its collection and courier lines in full. A booking's shipments are listable with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.8. The Conditioning Timeline and Its Dependencies

The recipient's conditioning regimen is driven through a single action-bearing capability shared between the `TransplantCoordinator` and the `MedicalDirector`. Every call carries an `action` of `SCHEDULE`, `START`, `INFUSE` or `ABORT` together with the mandatory `expected_patient_version`; `SCHEDULE` additionally requires `conditioning_start_date`, and `ABORT` additionally requires `reason`. The five legal values of `conditioning_status` are `NOT_SCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED`, `STARTED`, `INFUSED` and `ABORTED`, of which `INFUSED` and `ABORTED` are terminal. Each action is legal only from certain states — `SCHEDULE` from `NOT_SCHEDULED` or `SCHEDULED`, `START` from `SCHEDULED`, `INFUSE` from `STARTED`, and `ABORT` from `SCHEDULED` or `STARTED` — and an action requested from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONDITIONING_ACTION`. Every successful action increments the patient's `version` by one. Only the coordinator who owns the patient may act, whichever of the two roles is calling.

Scheduling from `NOT_SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` requires that the patient already has a search in `WORKUP_ACTIVE` status — conditioning is never scheduled speculatively, and a schedule without such a search is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONING_PRECONDITION_UNMET`. The supplied `conditioning_start_date` must not be earlier than the current logical UTC date; a date in the past is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONING_DATE_IN_PAST`, while today's date is accepted. Scheduling stores the date and thereby opens the legal collection window described in §3.7 for every subsequent booking of this patient.

Re-scheduling is a self-loop: `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED`. The state label does not change, but the derived window and every dependent booking do. Re-scheduling is refused outright once any booking of the patient's workup case has reached `COMPLETED` — the product has already been taken and the timeline can no longer move. Otherwise the new `conditioning_start_date` overwrites the old one, the legal collection window is re-derived from it, and every booking of the patient's `OPEN` workup case in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status whose `collection_date` now falls outside the re-derived window is moved to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CONDITIONING_RESCHEDULED`. Each such cancellation releases that booking's minutes from the centre's `committed_slot_minutes` and moves its `COLLECTION_WINDOW` calendar window to `RELEASED`, restoring the donor's `blocked_day_count`; and for each such cancelled booking the case's logistics dimension reverts to `METHOD_SET`. Bookings whose collection date still falls inside the new window are left exactly as they are.

Starting the regimen moves `SCHEDULED → STARTED` and is the point of no return. It requires that the current logical UTC date is not earlier than the recorded `conditioning_start_date`, and that a booking in `CONFIRMED` status exists for the patient's `OPEN` workup case; a start without such a booking is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONING_PRECONDITION_UNMET`. From `STARTED` onward, an abort is recorded with `conditioning_aborted_reason` and the search is moved to `STALLED` rather than to `BACKUP_REQUIRED`, because a recipient whose marrow has already been ablated cannot simply be re-matched to a backup volunteer.

Infusion moves `STARTED → INFUSED` and closes the case. It requires that a product shipment of the patient's workup case is in `DELIVERED` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_DELIVERED`. That delivered product's `dose_adequacy` must be `ADEQUATE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_DOSE_INADEQUATE`. And the current logical UTC instant must not be after that shipment's `must_infuse_by`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_VIABILITY_EXPIRED`; an infusion recorded at exactly the viability deadline is accepted. A successful infusion moves the workup case `OPEN → COMPLETED` — which is legitimate precisely because its `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` and its `logistics_status` is `DELIVERED` — moves the candidate `COLLECTED → CLOSED`, and moves the search `WORKUP_ACTIVE → FULFILLED`. A fulfilled search is closed for good: further typing requests against any of its candidates are refused.

Aborting is the widest cascade in the system, and it runs identically whether the abort comes from `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` or from `STARTED → ABORTED`. The supplied reason is recorded as the patient's `conditioning_aborted_reason`. Every booking of the patient's `OPEN` workup case in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CONDITIONING_ABORTED`, each releasing its minutes from its centre's `committed_slot_minutes`; bookings already in a terminal status keep whatever `cancel_reason` they already carried and are not rewritten. Every `COLLECTION_WINDOW` calendar window sourced by those bookings becomes `RELEASED`, restoring the donor's `blocked_day_count`. Every confirmatory-typing request of the search's candidates still sitting in `REQUESTED` status becomes `CANCELLED`, after which no response may ever be filed against it. Every product shipment of the case still in `SCHEDULED` status — that is, one that has not departed — becomes `CANCELLED`, after which no further transit event is accepted against it, while the `COMPLETED` booking that produced that shipment is left entirely untouched; a shipment already `DELIVERED` is likewise left untouched. The workup case moves `case_status` `OPEN → ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `CONDITIONING_ABORTED`, and its match candidate is moved to `CLOSED` from either `WORKUP_OPEN` or `COLLECTED`, whichever it held. Every reimbursement claim of that case still in `PROVISIONAL` status becomes `VOID` with its `void_reason` recorded, restoring the patient's `committed_cost_cents` by the voided total. And the search moves `WORKUP_ACTIVE → STALLED`.

### 3.9. Follow-Up, Adverse Events and Cost Reimbursement

Volunteers are followed for a year after donation. A liaison or a `MedicalDirector` files each contact against the donor with an `event_type` of `DAY_7_CONTACT`, `DAY_30_CONTACT`, `ONE_YEAR_CONTACT` or `ADVERSE_EVENT`, a `severity_grade` from 0 to 5 on the standard toxicity scale, and an optional free-text `description` of up to four hundred characters. Grade and event type must agree: a scheduled contact row must carry `severity_grade` exactly 0, while an `ADVERSE_EVENT` row must carry a grade from 1 through 5, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_GRADE_INVALID`. Each filed row stamps `reported_at` from the logical clock and refreshes the donor's `last_followup_at` to the latest `reported_at` on record. An `ADVERSE_EVENT` row additionally raises the donor's `adverse_event_count` by one. Follow-up rows are immutable audit records, so they carry only an `id` and a `created_at` and have neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`, and a donor's follow-ups are listable with an optional `event_type` filter.

A serious adverse event ends the volunteer's donor career. When an `ADVERSE_EVENT` row carries a `severity_grade` of 4 or higher, the row's `triggered_permanent_deferral` is recorded as true — it is false on every other row — and the donor's `availability_status` moves to `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED`, whether the donor was `AVAILABLE` or already `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` at the time. The donor's non-terminal match candidates anywhere in the system are moved to `EXCLUDED` and their typing requests still in `REQUESTED` status are moved to `CANCELLED`. When the donor was `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`, any `ACTIVE` `DEFERRAL` window stays `ACTIVE` — the calendar block is not lifted — while `deferral_until` is cleared to null, because there is no longer a date to be re-screened from.

**The reimbursement claim.** Every workup case carries a claim recording what the donor's home registry may invoice. The claim is created together with the workup case, records the case's `workup_case_id`, the paying `patient_id` resolved through the case's candidate and search, and the `billing_registry_id` of the donor's home registry, which is the invoicing party. Its `currency_code` is copied from that registry's `currency_code`, and its `fx_rate_snapshot` captures that registry's `fx_rate_to_usd` at the moment the current numbers were computed. It opens in `status` `PROVISIONAL` with `variant_applied` `PROVISIONAL`, `finalized_at` null, `void_reason` null and `cancellation_fee_applied` false; its three legal states are `PROVISIONAL`, `FINALIZED` and `VOID`, the latter two terminal. Claims are listable and filterable by `workup_case_id`, by `patient_id`, by `billing_registry_id` and by `status`.

Every claim carries a `line_items` array of seven objects, each with a `line_type` and an `amount_usd_cents`, in one pinned order that never varies: `CT_TYPING`, `WORKUP_PHYSICAL`, `IDM_PANEL`, `COLLECTION`, `COURIER`, `DONOR_LOST_WAGE`, `CANCELLATION`. Zero-valued lines are still present in the array. Each line is rounded half up to a whole cent before the sum is taken; the subtotal is never derived by summing exact fractions and rounding at the end. The lines are built as follows.

The `CT_TYPING` line is 34,500 cents — three hundred and forty-five dollars — for every confirmatory-typing attempt the candidate consumed, so it is 34,500 multiplied by the candidate's `ct_attempt_count`; a candidate typed twice therefore bills two attempts. The `WORKUP_PHYSICAL` line is a flat 78,000 cents and the `IDM_PANEL` line a flat 52,500 cents. The `COLLECTION` line depends on the method the variant is working from: 1,450,000 cents for `PBSC` and 1,980,000 cents for `MARROW`. The `COURIER` line starts from a base of 265,000 cents, and when the billing registry has `is_domestic` false that base is increased by a thirty-five per cent international surcharge — the base multiplied by one plus 0.35, rounded half up to whole cents — while a domestic registry pays the base unchanged. The `DONOR_LOST_WAGE` line compensates the volunteer at 32,000 cents for each day the collection blocks their calendar, so it is 32,000 multiplied by the window-day count the variant is working from. The `CANCELLATION` line is the collection fee for the variant's method multiplied by 0.40, that is forty per cent, rounded half up to whole cents, and it is charged only in the final variant and only when some booking of the case carries `cancelled_after_confirmation` true; in every other situation it is zero. When that line is charged, the claim's `cancellation_fee_applied` is recorded as true.

The `subtotal_usd_cents` is the exact integer sum of those seven already-rounded lines. From the subtotal alone — never from the total, never from any individual line — a `volume_discount_pct` is selected: a subtotal of at least 4,000,000 cents earns 0.09, otherwise a subtotal of at least 2,500,000 cents earns 0.05, and anything below that earns nothing. The `discount_usd_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The `intl_coordination_fee_usd_cents` applies only when the billing registry has `is_domestic` false, in which case it is the subtotal less the discount, multiplied by 0.08, that is eight per cent, rounded half up to whole cents; for a domestic registry it is zero. The `total_usd_cents` is then the subtotal less the discount plus the coordination fee. Finally, `billed_amount_local_cents` converts that total into the registry's own currency: the total divided by `fx_rate_snapshot`, rounded half up to a whole local cent.

The claim is computed twice, once in each variant, and the two variants differ in exactly three inputs. The **provisional** variant runs at claim creation, before any collection method has been decided, so it cannot know the real method: it assumes `PBSC` for the collection line and the cancellation line's basis, assumes a window-day count of 6 for the lost-wage line, always charges a zero cancellation line, and captures `fx_rate_snapshot` from the registry as read at creation. The **final** variant runs at finalisation: it uses the case's actual `collection_method` for the collection line, the case's actual `planned_window_days` for the lost-wage line, charges the cancellation line when a confirmed booking of the case was cancelled, and re-reads `fx_rate_snapshot` from the registry at that moment. Because the rate is re-read, a registry whose `fx_rate_to_usd` changed in between will yield a different `billed_amount_local_cents` for an identical United States dollar total.

Finalisation is a `RegistryOperations` act. The claim must be in `PROVISIONAL` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PROVISIONAL`. And the case must have reached an accountable end: either its `case_status` is `COMPLETED`, or the case holds a booking with `cancelled_after_confirmation` true. Anything else — a case still open, or an aborted case that never confirmed a booking — is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FINALIZABLE`. A successful finalisation moves the claim `PROVISIONAL → FINALIZED`, sets `variant_applied` to `FINAL`, recomputes every line, the discount tier, the coordination fee, the total and the local billed amount against the freshly read rate, and stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock. The patient's `committed_cost_cents` then moves by the difference between the final total and the provisional total it previously carried — upward when the final numbers are larger, downward when they are smaller — because both `PROVISIONAL` and `FINALIZED` claims count towards that commitment.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new, stand-alone resource — a registry, a collection centre, a patient, a donor, a search request, a typing request, a workup case, a collection booking, a product shipment, a calendar window or a follow-up row — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every action-bearing capability, every state transition and every recomputation, return `200 OK`. Releasing a donor calendar window returns `204 No Content` with no body. Every response, whatever its status, is wrapped in the envelope of §2.2.
