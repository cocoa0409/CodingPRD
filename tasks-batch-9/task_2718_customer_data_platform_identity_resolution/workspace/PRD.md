# Customer Data Platform & Identity Resolution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national retailer's customer record does not arrive in one piece. It arrives five times over: as a till transaction, as a loyalty scan, as a web session, as an app event, and as a support ticket — each carrying a different subset of identifiers, each with its own idea of how a phone number should be spelled, and each one legally entitled to a different set of processing permissions. The Customer Data Platform described here is the system of record that reconciles those five streams into one governed customer graph. It ingests raw record batches, scores them for data quality before they are allowed anywhere near the graph, resolves identities both deterministically and probabilistically, groups resolved individuals into households, and then holds the resulting profiles under a consent and suppression regime strict enough that no downstream marketing platform can ever receive an identifier the subject has not authorised.

The platform's hardest requirement is not matching — it is *provable restraint*. Every audience that leaves the building must be traceable to an approved segment definition, a live governance decision, a per-channel opt-in election, and a k-anonymity floor; every erasure request must be provably propagated to every advertising and messaging vendor that ever received the subject's hashed identifier, and provably acknowledged by each of them. Because the same customer's consent may be captured twice on the same day from two systems of differing trustworthiness, and because a legal hold can freeze an erasure that a regulator has already demanded, the rules governing precedence are as important as the rules governing computation. This document specifies both, and the reader should assume that where the two conflict, the precedence rule is the requirement.

The API is mounted under `/cdp`. It is a deterministic, immediately-consistent service: there is no eventual convergence, no background worker, and no wall-clock dependency. Every temporal rule — consent expiry, suppression windows, vendor acknowledgement deadlines, governance validity, activation cooldown, daily hash quota resets — is evaluated against a server-held logical clock that operators drive explicitly, so that the same request sequence always produces the same graph.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it is acting under. If either header is absent, or is present but blank after trimming, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check the platform performs; nothing else — not path resolution, not body parsing — happens before it.

Seven roles exist, and their spellings are exact:

- `DataPlatformAdmin` owns the platform's configuration surface: registering retail feeds, declaring matching rules, registering advertising and messaging destinations, reinstating quarantined feeds, and triggering the retention sweep.
- `DataEngineer` operates the ingestion pipeline: submitting record batches, running data-quality validation, requeuing quarantined batches, and promoting validated batches into the profile graph.
- `IdentitySteward` adjudicates the identity graph: proposing candidate matches between two profiles, confirming or rejecting them, and reconciling household membership by hand.
- `PrivacyOfficer` owns consent, suppression and erasure: capturing channel-level elections, adding and releasing suppression windows, recording erasure requests and fanning them out to vendors.
- `SegmentAnalyst` builds audiences and runs deliveries: defining segments, submitting them for governance review, creating activations, dispatching, pausing and resuming them.
- `DataGovernanceOfficer` approves or rejects segment governance and holds sole authority over sensitive-attribute usage.
- `DestinationPlatform` is the advertising or messaging platform itself, reporting its own match results and acknowledging its own downstream deletion tasks.

If `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven strings, or is one of them but is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called (see the §4 table), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `DataPlatformAdmin`, `DataEngineer`, `IdentitySteward`, `PrivacyOfficer`, `SegmentAnalyst` or `DataGovernanceOfficer`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It does, however, record it wherever a transition demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a segment, as `decided_by_user_id` on an identity link, and as `created_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on a suppression entry — and it compares recorded operator identifiers *to each other* to enforce segregation of duties.

`DestinationPlatform` is different: its identity is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `DestinationPlatform`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Destination`, and ownership checks compare the target resource's destination foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a destination platform reports match results for an activation, `X-User-ID` must equal that activation's `destination_id`; when it acknowledges a downstream deletion task, `X-User-ID` must equal that task's `destination_id`. A platform reporting on somebody else's delivery is rejected with `ERR_DESTINATION_ACTOR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), and this check runs immediately after role authorization, before any resource is looked up by path.

Two operations are additionally split by user identity rather than by role, under a segregation-of-duties principle detailed in §2.10: the operator who records a segment's governance decision must not be the operator who submitted that segment for review, and the `PrivacyOfficer` who releases a legal-hold suppression must not be the one who created it. Both violations answer `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to `10`, its minimum accepted value is `1`, and its maximum accepted value is `100`. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to `0` and its minimum accepted value is `0`, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed query parameter and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific collection states otherwise below, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four collections carry a domain-motivated ordering that overrides the default, and one inverts it outright:

- Retail feeds are listed newest first — `created_at` descending — because operators triage recent registrations.
- Profiles, identity links, suppression entries, activations and downstream deletion tasks are ordered `created_at` ascending and then `id` ascending, so that the profiles minted by a single promotion appear in the record processing order described in §3.3, and the deletion tasks of a single fan-out appear in the destination registration order described in §3.11.
- Match rules and destinations are ordered `created_at` ascending, which is deliberately the same order in which the resolution pass scans rules and the erasure fan-out visits destinations.
- Households are ordered by `sequence_no` ascending within one address, then `created_at` ascending and `id` ascending across addresses, so that the overflow buckets at a single address read in bucket order.
- Consent elections are ordered by `precedence_rank` ascending within a group, so the winning election of each group appears first.

For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every one of the thirteen persisted resource kinds — retail feeds, ingest batches, profiles, households, match rules, identity links, consent records, suppression entries, segments, destinations, activations, deletion requests and vendor deletion tasks — carries the same four system-managed properties, with no exceptions:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, e.g. `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, stamped when the resource is first persisted.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, rewritten on every mutation of the resource, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. A cascade that touches five resources increments five separate `version` counters by 1 each; it never increments one counter by 5.

A resource that is only *read* is never mutated: computed reads such as the per-channel consent projection, the segment performance report and the audience overlap report derive their output at read time and increment nothing.

All timestamps the platform writes — `dq_window_start_at`, `last_reinstated_at`, `validated_at`, `promoted_at`, `last_activity_at`, `decided_at`, `released_at`, `approved_at`, `governance_expires_at`, `dispatched_at`, `terminated_at`, `propagated_at`, `verified_at`, `ack_deadline_at`, `acknowledged_at` — are taken from a server-held **logical clock**, never from the host's wall clock. Before any operator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must not fall back to real time. An admin-visible test-driving capability, available to all seven roles, sets the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `current_utc` and echoes the accepted value back as `{ current_utc }`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and increments no `version`. Every phrase in this document of the form "the logical clock now" resolves against that clock, which is what makes expiry, SLA, cooldown, governance-validity and retention behaviour reproducible.

There is no URL nesting in this API: every resource is addressed at the top level of `/cdp`, and no path carries two resource identifiers. Where a single request nonetheless names several resources — one in the path and one or more in the body or query string — the existence checks are not resolved left-to-right along the URL but strictly in the priority order of §2.8, which is absolute and identical for every endpoint. A request that names both a non-existent segment in the body and a non-existent destination in the body therefore answers the segment's error, because the segment existence rule sits higher on the ladder.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Score Widths

The platform stores three families of numbers, each with its own fixed width, and every rounding operation in the system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero.

**Two decimal places, round half up** applies to per-record data-quality scores, to the batch-level `dq_score`, to `pii_completeness_floor` (which is accepted from the client at no more than two decimals within the inclusive range 0.00 through 1.00), and to `channel_engagement_score`.

**Four decimal places, round half up** applies to the feed-level `dq_pass_rate`, to a match rule's `auto_merge_threshold`, `review_floor`, `min_precision` and computed `precision_estimate`, to an identity link's `confidence`, to a destination's `min_match_rate`, to an activation's `gross_match_rate`, `net_match_rate` and `effective_match_rate`, and to the overlap report's `jaccard_index`, `containment_in_other` and `containment_of_other`. The four threshold fields accepted from the client are constrained to the inclusive range 0.0000 through 1.0000 at no more than four decimals.

**Exact integers** are used for every count and for every monetary amount. Money is held in cents throughout — `lifetime_value_cents`, `combined_lifetime_value_cents`, `revenue_cents` on a reported conversion, `attributed_revenue_cents` and `revenue_per_member_cents` — and no fractional cent is ever stored. Where a monetary computation involves division, the division is applied to the already-summed total and the result is rounded half up exactly once at the very end, as spelled out for `revenue_per_member_cents` in §3.10.

All numeric values in response bodies are emitted as JSON numbers, never as strings.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Versions

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a *parent* resource, and only those three. Each takes its version field as a **required, non-optional** body field; supplying it as null or omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every other endpoint in the API takes no version field at all, and the server must not invent one for them.

1. **Consent capture** — a request to create a consent record must carry `expected_profile_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Profile` named by `profile_id`. If that value does not equal the profile's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROFILE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new election legitimately supersedes the incumbent election for its profile-channel-purpose group, so a capture written from a stale view of the profile would silently displace an election the client never observed.
2. **Activation creation** — a request to create an activation must carry `expected_segment_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Segment` named by `segment_id`. A mismatch with the segment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEGMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every activation created against a segment advances that segment's `version` and its `active_activation_count`, and a governance decision may have re-scoped the audience in between, so an activation built on a stale segment view is refused rather than dispatched against criteria the client never read.
3. **Deletion propagation** — a request to propagate a deletion request must carry `expected_deletion_request_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of that same `DeletionRequest`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DELETION_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A retry propagation legitimately supersedes the prior downstream fan-out by re-dispatching timed-out and failed tasks, so a caller holding a stale version could otherwise re-dispatch a task set that has already changed underneath it.

In all three cases the check is an equality comparison against the current stored `version`, evaluated after existence checks and before any state check.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline: Multi-Hop Propagation of Derived State

The customer graph is dense, and almost every meaningful write ripples. The platform's rule is that a ripple is **complete and immediate within the request that caused it** — there is no deferred reconciliation, and a partially applied cascade is never an acceptable outcome. Where this document describes a cascade in hops, every hop runs, in the order given, inside the originating call, and each resource a hop mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

Cascades in this platform reach three deep in several places, and the second and third hops are the ones implementers most often lose. Confirming an identity link merges two profiles (hop one), which can strip a channel from the survivor's activatable set and therefore pause a delivering activation with its hash quota refunded (hop two), which can drop a segment's active activation count to zero and therefore expire that segment's governance (hop three). Quarantining a feed rejects that feed's still-unvalidated batches. Tombstoning a profile at the end of an erasure voids its outstanding candidate links, tombstones its consent records and can dissolve its household. Each of these is specified in full in §3; §2.7 exists to state that none of them is optional and none of them may be truncated at the first hop.

Two derived quantities are recomputed so often that they are best understood as invariants rather than as fields anyone writes: a profile's `activatable_channels` and its `channel_engagement_score` are re-derived from scratch whenever a consent record is captured or changes status, whenever a suppression entry is created, released or expires, whenever `privacy_status` changes, and at every activation dispatch and resume. A household's `member_count`, `dominant_last_name` and `combined_lifetime_value_cents` are re-derived from scratch on every membership change. Recomputation always reaches a fixed point in a single pass, because no criterion and no derived field in this system references another segment's membership.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

Validation is a strict, absolute priority ladder. All checks that could apply to a request are evaluated in ascending priority number, and the **first** violated rule alone determines the response; later violations are never reported and never merged into a multi-error body. The ladder is global — it does not vary by endpoint — so a request that violates rules 4 and 26 simultaneously always answers rule 4's error. The rungs are as follows.

Rungs 1 through 3 are authentication and actor identity: (1) both auth headers present and non-blank, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`; (2) `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles *and* is listed for this endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; (3) on match reporting and on vendor-task acknowledgement, `X-User-ID` equals the target activation's or task's `destination_id`, else `ERR_DESTINATION_ACTOR_MISMATCH`.

Rung 4 is request shape, applied to every endpoint: every required input field present and non-null, every field of its declared type, every enum value drawn from its declared set, every number inside its declared range and decimal width, every string matching its declared pattern and length bounds, every array inside its declared cardinality bounds, and every query parameter well-formed — `limit` in the inclusive range 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, and `attribution_basis` either `TOTAL` or `INCREMENTAL`. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Rungs 5 through 17 are existence, in this fixed order: (5) the retail feed named by `{sourceSystemId}` or by a `source_system_id` body field exists, else `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`; (6) the batch named by `{batchId}` exists, else `ERR_INGEST_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`; (7) every profile named by `{profileId}`, `profile_id`, `left_profile_id` or `right_profile_id` exists, else `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`; (8) the household named by `{householdId}` or by the `household_id` body field exists, else `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`; (9) the rule named by `{matchRuleId}` or `match_rule_id` exists, else `ERR_MATCH_RULE_NOT_FOUND`; (10) the link named by `{linkId}` exists, else `ERR_IDENTITY_LINK_NOT_FOUND`; (11) the consent record named by `{consentRecordId}` exists, else `ERR_CONSENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; (12) the suppression entry named by `{suppressionId}` exists, else `ERR_SUPPRESSION_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`; (13) the segment named by `{segmentId}`, `segment_id` or the `other_segment_id` query parameter exists, else `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`; (14) the destination named by `{destinationId}` or `destination_id` exists, else `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_FOUND`; (15) the activation named by `{activationId}` exists, else `ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_FOUND`; (16) the erasure request named by `{deletionRequestId}` exists, else `ERR_DELETION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; (17) the vendor task named by `{taskId}` exists, else `ERR_VENDOR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`.

Rungs 18 through 20 are the three optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6, in the order profile, segment, deletion request — `ERR_STALE_PROFILE_VERSION` on consent capture, `ERR_STALE_SEGMENT_VERSION` on activation creation, `ERR_STALE_DELETION_REQUEST_VERSION` on propagation. They sit *above* every state and business check, which means a stale version is reported even when the target's state would also have refused the call.

Rungs 21 through 26 are state and referential integrity: (21) the target's current status permits the requested transition per its lifecycle, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; (22) no profile named as a link endpoint, consent subject, suppression subject or erasure subject has `resolution_status` of `MERGED_AWAY`, else `ERR_PROFILE_MERGED_AWAY`; (23) the referenced feed's `status` is not `QUARANTINED` when submitting a batch, validating one, promoting one, or capturing a consent record that names it, else `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_QUARANTINED`; (24) a rule named on link creation has `status` of `ENABLED`, else `ERR_MATCH_RULE_DISABLED`; (25) the destination's `status` is `ACTIVE` when creating, dispatching or resuming an activation against it, else `ERR_DESTINATION_INACTIVE`; (26) no profile referenced by the write has `privacy_status` of `DELETED`, else `ERR_PROFILE_DELETED`.

Rungs 27 through 33 are the privacy and governance safety band: (27) `ERR_LEGAL_HOLD_ACTIVE`; (28) `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; (29) `ERR_GOVERNANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`; (30) `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_APPROVED`; (31) `ERR_SENSITIVE_ATTRIBUTE_NOT_PERMITTED`; (32) `ERR_EMPTY_AUDIENCE`; (33) `ERR_AUDIENCE_BELOW_K_ANONYMITY`. Their conditions and scopes are given in §3.7, §3.8 and §3.9.

Rungs 34 through 42 are business constraints: (34) `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`; (35) `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_ID`; (36) `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_ORDER`; (37) `ERR_DETERMINISTIC_KEY_MISMATCH`; (38) `ERR_SELF_LINK_FORBIDDEN`; (39) `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTITY_LINK`; (40) `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_FLOOR`; (41) `ERR_MATCH_REPORT_EXCEEDS_DISPATCH`; (42) `ERR_OVERLAP_SAME_SEGMENT`.

Rungs 43 through 47 are temporal: (43) `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; (44) `ERR_FUTURE_CAPTURED_AT`; (45) `ERR_SUPPRESSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; (46) `ERR_ACTIVATION_COOLDOWN_VIOLATION`; (47) `ERR_ACTIVATION_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`.

Rungs 48 and 49 are the fiscal band — (48) `ERR_HASH_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` and (49) `ERR_RETRY_LIMIT_REACHED` — and rungs 50 and 51 close the ladder with the two manual household-assignment guards, (50) `ERR_INVALID_HOUSEHOLD_STATE` and (51) `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of the ladder deserve emphasis. First, the k-anonymity floor at rung 33 is checked *before* the quota check at rung 48, so an audience that is both too small and too expensive reports the privacy failure. Second, the cooldown check at rung 46 is checked before the concurrency cap at rung 47, but the two are mutually exclusive by construction (see §3.9), so their relative order is only observable when a single request would trip neither.

### 2.9. Idempotency and Terminal-State Lockdown

The platform never treats a repeated trigger as a silent no-op. Firing the same transition twice — validating an already-validated batch, dispatching an already-dispatched activation, releasing an already-released suppression, acknowledging an already-acknowledged vendor task — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) on the second attempt, because the entity's status no longer permits the transition. Terminal states are absolute: a `REJECTED` or `PROMOTED` ingest batch, a `RETIRED` segment, a `TERMINATED` activation, a `VERIFIED` deletion request, an `ACKNOWLEDGED` vendor deletion task, a `MERGED_AWAY` profile on the resolution axis and a `DELETED` profile on the privacy axis accept nothing further.

There is exactly one deliberate exception to the rule that an error response leaves state untouched, and it is described in §3.2: the requeue attempt that exhausts a batch's retry budget both fails and advances the batch to `REJECTED`.

### 2.10. Privacy Precedence and Segregation of Duties

A profile carries two independent lifecycle axes on the same row, and the platform must never let one gate the other. `resolution_status` records identity resolution and moves `UNRESOLVED` → `RESOLVED` → `MERGED_AWAY`. `privacy_status` records the subject's privacy standing and starts at `NORMAL`, moving to `SUPPRESSED`, `LEGAL_HOLD`, `DELETION_PENDING` or `DELETED`. A profile may legitimately be `RESOLVED` and `LEGAL_HOLD` at the same instant. Each axis gates different operations, and no transition on one axis is ever a precondition of a transition on the other; in particular, tombstoning a profile at the end of an erasure leaves `resolution_status` exactly as it found it.

When several suppression instruments apply to one subject at once, `privacy_status` is re-derived as the highest-ranking instrument in force, using this precedence: `LEGAL_HOLD` outranks `SUPPRESSED`, and `DELETION_PENDING` outranks `LEGAL_HOLD`, which in turn outranks `NORMAL`. The practical consequence, spelled out again in §3.7, is that a legal hold placed on a subject who is already `DELETION_PENDING` is stored as an in-force entry but does *not* move `privacy_status` — the erasure state is the stronger claim — while still blocking the erasure's downstream fan-out.

Two duties are segregated on `X-User-ID`, and both violations answer `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). First, the operator recording a segment's governance decision must not be the operator recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`; a `SegmentAnalyst` who submits and a `DataGovernanceOfficer` who decides are peers over the same resource, split by step. Second, a `PrivacyOfficer` releasing a `LEGAL_HOLD` suppression entry must not be the operator recorded in that entry's `created_by_user_id`; here the role is split from itself by user id.

### 2.11. The Hash Quota Ledger and Conserved Quantities

Two ledgers in this platform obey compensating invariants, and implementers should treat these invariants as the definition of correctness rather than as a consequence of it.

The **hash quota ledger** governs how many hashed identifiers a destination will accept in a UTC calendar day. Each destination declares `daily_hash_quota`; `remaining_daily_hash_quota` is that figure less the hashes consumed by deliveries that are still in flight. Dispatching or resuming an activation consumes `dispatched_hash_count` from the destination's remaining quota; pausing an activation, or terminating one that was still delivering, refunds exactly the same figure. At every moment, and for as long as `quota_reset_on` is unchanged, `remaining_daily_hash_quota` equals `daily_hash_quota` minus the sum of `dispatched_hash_count` over that destination's activations whose status is `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING`. A retention sweep on a new UTC day resets the remaining quota back to the full `daily_hash_quota` and rewrites `quota_reset_on` to that day's date.

The **customer-value ledger** is conserved across merges. Summed over all profiles whose `resolution_status` is not `MERGED_AWAY`, both `lifetime_value_cents` and `source_record_count` are identical immediately before and immediately after any merge. A merge redistributes those quantities onto the survivor and zeroes the loser's monetary balance; it never creates or destroys value.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Retail Feeds and Their Data-Quality Contract

Everything in the platform begins with a registered **retail feed**. A `DataPlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying a `code` — a stable operator-assigned identifier of three to twenty characters drawn only from upper-case letters, digits and underscores, which must be unique across all feeds, a second feed reusing an existing code being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — together with a `display_name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters after trimming, and a `channel_type` naming which retail stream the feed carries: `TRANSACTIONS`, `LOYALTY`, `WEB`, `APP` or `SUPPORT`.

Three further inputs are optional and shape everything the feed's data will later be worth. `trust_tier` is an integer provenance ranking from 1 through 5 inclusive, defaulting to 3; it decides which feed's identifiers win a merge and which feed's consent election wins a same-instant tie. `dq_scoring_mode` selects which of three record-scoring formulas will be applied to this feed's batches — `UNWEIGHTED_MEAN`, `WEIGHTED_COMPONENTS` (the default) or `PENALIZED_WEIGHTED` — and is described in §3.2. `pii_completeness_floor` is the minimum batch-level quality score a batch from this feed must reach to be accepted, expressed on a zero-to-one scale at no more than two decimals and defaulting to 0.70.

A newly registered feed is `REGISTERED`, not `ACTIVE`. Its `dq_window_start_at` is stamped at the logical clock now, opening a rolling data-quality window; `submitted_record_total` and `valid_record_total` start at 0, `dq_pass_rate` starts at 1.0, and `quarantine_count` starts at 0 with `last_reinstated_at` null. A feed reaches `ACTIVE` only when real data has flowed all the way through, which is to say on the first successful promotion of one of its batches — that promotion moves the feed `REGISTERED` → `ACTIVE` and increments the feed's `version`. A `REGISTERED` feed may nonetheless submit and validate batches; registration is enough to ingest, and the `ACTIVE` marker records that the feed has proved itself.

The rolling window is what makes a feed's quality auditable. `submitted_record_total` is the sum of `record_count` over the feed's in-window batches, and `valid_record_total` is the sum of their `valid_record_count`. An in-window batch is one belonging to this feed whose status is `VALIDATED`, `QUARANTINED`, `PROMOTED` or `REJECTED` — that is, one on which validation has run at least once — and whose `created_at` is at or after `dq_window_start_at`; a batch still sitting in `RECEIVED` without ever having been validated is excluded from both sums. `dq_pass_rate` is then the valid total divided by the submitted total, rounded half up to four decimal places, except that when the submitted total is 0 the rate is exactly 1.0000, because a feed with no evidence is presumed clean. These three figures are recomputed at the end of every validation and again on every reinstatement.

Quality gating fires on cumulative evidence, not on a single bad batch. A feed is driven to `QUARANTINED` when, after a recomputation, its `submitted_record_total` has reached at least five records **and** its `dq_pass_rate` is strictly below its own `pii_completeness_floor`. Both boundaries are deliberate: exactly five in-window records is already enough evidence to act on, and a rate exactly equal to the floor is not a breach. The transition applies from either non-quarantined state — a feed already `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE` → `QUARANTINED`, and a feed that has never promoted anything moves `REGISTERED` → `QUARANTINED` — and in both cases the feed's `quarantine_count` increments by 1, its `version` increments by 1, and a cascade hop rejects every batch of that feed still sitting in `RECEIVED`, setting each of those batches' `rejection_reason` to `SOURCE_QUARANTINED`.

While a feed is `QUARANTINED` it accepts nothing: submitting a new batch against it, validating one of its batches, promoting one of its batches, or capturing a consent election that names it as the capture point all fail with `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_QUARANTINED`. Only a `DataPlatformAdmin` can lift it, by reinstating the feed. Reinstatement requires the feed to be `QUARANTINED`, moves it `QUARANTINED` → `ACTIVE`, stamps `last_reinstated_at` at the logical clock now, and — critically — **resets the quality baseline** by setting `dq_window_start_at` to the logical clock now as well. Because the window now starts in the present, the in-window batch set is empty, so `submitted_record_total` and `valid_record_total` fall back to 0 and `dq_pass_rate` returns to 1.0. Batches created before the reset never re-enter the window and can never trigger a second quarantine on their own. The feed's `version` increments by 1. A feed that has been quarantined and reinstated three times carries a `quarantine_count` of 3 and a `last_reinstated_at` of the most recent lift; the counter is never reset.

### 3.2. Batch Submission, Data-Quality Scoring and the Requeue Loop

A `DataEngineer` submits raw data as an **ingest batch**, naming the contributing feed with `source_system_id`, labelling the batch with a `batch_label` of one to eighty characters after trimming, and supplying `records`: an array of between 1 and 200 raw record payloads. An empty array is refused as a malformed request. Each element of `records` is an object carrying a required `external_id` that must be unique within the batch, a required `event_timestamp` as an ISO 8601 string, and the nullable raw identifier fields `email_raw`, `phone_raw`, `loyalty_number`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `postal_code` and `address_line`, plus a `consent_signals` array that may be empty and whose elements each carry a `channel`, a `purpose` and a `consent_state`. Two records in the same batch sharing an `external_id` are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_ID`. On acceptance the batch is `RECEIVED`, its `record_count` is fixed at the number of elements supplied, `retry_round` is 0, `dq_score` is null, `dq_failure_breakdown` is an empty object, and `valid_record_count` and `invalid_record_count` are 0.

Validation scores the batch. Five identifier components are assessed on every record: `email_norm`, `phone_norm`, `loyalty_number`, `postal_code` and `name_pair`. Each component is in one of three conditions. It **passes** when its raw value is present and satisfies its format check; it **fails** when the raw value is present but malformed; and it is **absent** when the raw value is null or blank after trimming. The format checks are exact: `email_raw` must consist of one or more characters that are neither whitespace nor an at-sign, then an at-sign, then one or more such characters, then a dot, then at least two letters — after which it is lower-cased and trimmed to become `email_norm`; `phone_raw` must reduce to exactly eleven digits beginning with the digit one once plus signs, hyphens, dots, spaces and parentheses are stripped, becoming `phone_norm`; `loyalty_number` must be the two letters `LY` followed by exactly eight digits; `postal_code` must be exactly five digits; and `name_pair` passes only when **both** `first_name` and `last_name` are non-blank after trimming.

The per-record score is then computed by one of three formulas, selected by the contributing feed's `dq_scoring_mode`, and the three genuinely disagree. Under `UNWEIGHTED_MEAN`, the score is the number of passing components divided by five; the weight table is ignored entirely, so a record missing only its email scores exactly the same as a record missing only its postal code, and failing is indistinguishable from being absent. Under `WEIGHTED_COMPONENTS`, the score is the sum of the weights of the passing components, over a weight base that is always the full 1.00: an email that passes contributes 0.30, a phone 0.20, a loyalty number 0.20, a name pair 0.15 and a postal code 0.15 — the five weights summing to exactly 1.00, so email is worth twice postal code — and failing is again indistinguishable from being absent. Under `PENALIZED_WEIGHTED`, the same weighted sum is computed first as a base, then a penalty of 0.10 is subtracted for each component that is present but malformed, and the result is floored at 0.00 so it can never go negative; this is the only mode in which failing and being absent diverge, on the principle that a malformed value actively poisons downstream matching whereas a missing one merely withholds evidence. Whichever mode applies, the per-record score is rounded half up to two decimal places *before* it is compared to any threshold or fed into any average.

A record is **valid** when its score is at least 0.60 and **invalid** when its score is strictly below 0.60; the boundary is inclusive on the valid side, so a score of exactly 0.60 is valid. `valid_record_count` is the number of valid records and `invalid_record_count` is `record_count` less that figure. The batch-level `dq_score` is the sum of the per-record scores over **all** records — invalid ones included — divided by `record_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places; it measures the feed, not the survivors. Alongside it, `dq_failure_breakdown` is written as a map from component name to the count of records in which that component was present but malformed; components that were merely absent never appear in those counts.

Validation then routes the batch. Where the computed `dq_score` is at least the feed's `pii_completeness_floor` — inclusive at equality — the batch moves `RECEIVED` → `VALIDATED` and `validated_at` is stamped at the logical clock now. Where the score is strictly below the floor, the batch moves `RECEIVED` → `QUARANTINED`, with `validated_at` stamped just the same. Both branches write `dq_score`, `valid_record_count`, `invalid_record_count` and `dq_failure_breakdown`, both recompute the feed's `submitted_record_total`, `valid_record_total` and `dq_pass_rate`, and both increment the batch's `version` by 1. The quarantine branch additionally carries the two-hop cascade of §3.1: the recomputed feed rate may breach the floor on cumulative evidence, driving the feed to `QUARANTINED` (hop one), which in turn rejects every sibling batch of that feed still in `RECEIVED` with `rejection_reason` of `SOURCE_QUARANTINED` (hop two).

A quarantined batch is not lost. Requeuing it returns it to the front of the pipeline: while its `retry_round` is strictly below the retry budget of two rounds, requeuing moves it `QUARANTINED` → `RECEIVED`, increments `retry_round` by 1, clears `dq_score`, `dq_failure_breakdown`, `valid_record_count`, `invalid_record_count` and `validated_at`, recomputes the feed's totals and rate now that this batch is no longer in a counted status, and increments the batch's `version` by 1. This is the platform's one genuine cycle: `RECEIVED` → `QUARANTINED` → `RECEIVED`, bounded by the budget.

When the budget is spent, the platform does something it does nowhere else. A requeue attempt on a batch whose `retry_round` has already reached two answers `ERR_RETRY_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409) **and, in that same call, drives the batch `QUARANTINED` → `REJECTED`** with `rejection_reason` set to `RETRY_LIMIT_REACHED` and the batch's `version` incremented by 1. This is the sole error-with-side-effect in the system and the only path by which that error code is reachable. A fourth requeue, now aimed at a terminal batch, is an ordinary `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Promotion: Resolving Records into the Profile Graph

Promotion is the platform's heaviest operation. A `DataEngineer` promotes a `VALIDATED` batch whose feed is not `QUARANTINED`; the batch moves `VALIDATED` → `PROMOTED`, `promoted_at` is stamped at the logical clock now, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. The resolution pass consults only match rules whose `status` is `ENABLED`. Promotion takes no rule argument at all, and it succeeds even when no rule is enabled — in that case every valid record simply mints its own profile.

Only the batch's **valid** records participate; invalid records were already counted in `invalid_record_count` and are dropped without trace in the graph. Candidate profiles for matching are those whose `resolution_status` is not `MERGED_AWAY` and whose `privacy_status` is `NORMAL`. Records are processed in a strict, reproducible order: by `event_timestamp` ascending, and where two records share an instant, by `external_id` ascending. Because `external_id` is unique within a batch, no tie survives both keys.

**Phase one — deterministic attachment.** For each record in that order, the pass walks every enabled deterministic rule in `created_at` ascending order. Each such rule names a `deterministic_key` of `EMAIL_NORM`, `PHONE_NORM` or `LOYALTY_NUMBER`, which selects the record's normalized `email_norm`, `phone_norm` or `loyalty_number` respectively as the lookup value. If that value is non-null and **exactly one** candidate profile carries the same value, the record is attached to that profile, the batch's `matched_deterministic_count` increments by 1, and rule scanning stops for this record. If **more than one** candidate profile carries the value, the key is ambiguous: the rule is skipped and scanning continues to the next rule, leaving the record to the probabilistic phase. If no rule attaches the record, a new profile is minted with `resolution_status` of `UNRESOLVED`, `privacy_status` of `NORMAL` and `source_record_count` of 1, and the batch's `new_profile_count` increments by 1.

Attachment has precise semantics, and they are the reason chronological order matters. The record's non-null identifiers fill only those profile fields that are currently null — the first writer of each field wins, and a later record never overwrites a populated field. The profile's `source_record_count` increases by 1; the contributing feed's `code` is added to `contributing_source_system_codes`, which is held sorted alphabetically ascending with distinct values only; `last_activity_at` becomes the later of its current value and the record's `event_timestamp`; and `loyalty_tier` becomes the greater of its current value and the record's tier signal on the ordinal scale where `NONE` is lowest, then `SILVER`, then `GOLD`, then `PLATINUM` highest. A newly minted profile also records `origin_batch_id` as the promoting batch and `primary_source_system_id` as its contributing feed with the highest `trust_tier`, breaking ties by the earlier feed `created_at`. The identifier fields a profile carries are `email_norm` (lower-cased and trimmed), `phone_norm` (the eleven digits without a leading plus sign), `first_name`, `last_name`, `postal_code` (five digits), `loyalty_number` and `address_hash` — the last being the record's `address_line` lower-cased with runs of whitespace collapsed, which serves as the household grouping key and is null when no address was supplied. The three sensitive attributes a profile may carry — `inferred_health_interest`, `inferred_ethnicity_affinity` and `precise_geolocation_grid`, the last being a one-hundred-metre grid cell — are inferred from web and app signals and are null when absent.

**Phase two — probabilistic candidates.** Once every record of the batch has been placed, the pass revisits each profile created in phase one, in profile `created_at` ascending order, and walks every enabled probabilistic rule in rule `created_at` ascending order. For each rule it builds a blocking set: the candidate profiles, excluding the profile itself and excluding any profile already joined to it by a link in `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` status, that share either a non-null `postal_code` or a non-null `last_name` with it. Each blocked candidate is considered in profile `created_at` ascending order; `evidence` is derived by comparing the four components of the pair exactly or partially, a confidence is computed as described in §3.4 under that rule's `missing_identifier_policy`, and if the confidence is at least the rule's `review_floor` a link is minted in `PROPOSED` status with `match_method` of `PROBABILISTIC` and the batch's `matched_probabilistic_count` increments by 1. Where the confidence additionally reaches the rule's `auto_merge_threshold`, the link is immediately confirmed, which runs the full merge described in §3.4. Where the confidence falls below the review floor, no link is minted and no counter moves.

**Phase three — resolution settle.** Every profile the promotion touched that is not `MERGED_AWAY` and that has no outstanding `PROPOSED` link becomes `RESOLVED`, moving `UNRESOLVED` → `RESOLVED`; a profile still carrying at least one `PROPOSED` link stays `UNRESOLVED`. This transition also computes that profile's `activatable_channels`, `channel_engagement_score` and `contributing_source_system_codes`. The `UNRESOLVED` value is therefore written and, for most records, advanced within the same promotion; it is durably observable only where a candidate link is left outstanding.

**Phase four — household grouping** runs the waterfall of §3.5 for every profile whose `address_hash` is non-null and whose `household_id` is still null.

Interleaved with all four phases, promotion **materialises consent**. Every element of each record's `consent_signals` becomes a consent record whose `captured_at` is the record's `event_timestamp` and whose `source_system_id` is the promoting batch's feed, after which each touched profile's `activatable_channels` is recomputed. Finally, promotion writes `new_profile_count`, `matched_deterministic_count` and `matched_probabilistic_count` onto the batch, and increments the `version` of every entity it creates or mutates.

Two profile fields are populated only later, by activity rather than by ingestion. `lifetime_value_cents` accumulates the `revenue_cents` of every conversion attributed against the profile and starts at 0, and `consent_record_count` is the number of consent records naming this profile whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. A profile also carries `merged_into_profile_id`, which stays null unless its `resolution_status` is `MERGED_AWAY`, in which case it names the survivor.

### 3.4. Match Rules, Candidate Links and Steward Adjudication

A **match rule** is the platform's declared theory of when two records describe the same person, and only a `DataPlatformAdmin` may declare one. A rule carries a `code` unique across all rules — three to twenty-four characters of upper-case letters, digits and underscores, a repeat earning `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — and a `rule_kind` of either `DETERMINISTIC` or `PROBABILISTIC`. The two kinds have complementary shape requirements on `deterministic_key`, which must name one of `EMAIL_NORM`, `PHONE_NORM` or `LOYALTY_NUMBER` when the kind is deterministic and must be null when it is probabilistic; either mismatch — a deterministic rule with no key, or a probabilistic rule that supplies one — is refused with `ERR_DETERMINISTIC_KEY_MISMATCH`.

Three thresholds calibrate a probabilistic rule, each on a zero-to-one scale at no more than four decimals. `review_floor`, defaulting to 0.65, is the minimum confidence at which a candidate link may be created at all. `auto_merge_threshold`, defaulting to 0.92, is the confidence at or above which a link is confirmed on the spot without a human ever seeing it, and it must be **strictly greater** than `review_floor` — equality is refused with `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_ORDER`, because a rule whose two thresholds coincide would have no review band at all. `min_precision`, defaulting to 0.60, is the accuracy floor below which the platform stops trusting the rule. Finally `missing_identifier_policy`, defaulting to `STRICT_ZERO`, chooses how sparse evidence is scored. A new rule is `ENABLED` with `confirmed_count` and `rejected_count` at 0 and `precision_estimate` at 1.0.

An **identity link** is a single candidate pair. An `IdentitySteward` may propose one directly, naming `left_profile_id`, `right_profile_id`, a `match_rule_id` and an `evidence` object. All four evidence keys — `email_similarity`, `phone_similarity`, `name_similarity` and `address_similarity` — are required, and each must be exactly one of 1.0 for an exact component match, 0.5 for a partial match, 0.0 for a mismatch, or null when the component is absent on at least one side of the pair. The two endpoints must differ: naming the same profile twice is `ERR_SELF_LINK_FORBIDDEN`. The pair must be unjoined: where any existing link in `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` status already joins the same unordered pair — in either direction, since the link is symmetric — the proposal is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTITY_LINK`. A rule whose `status` has fallen to `DISABLED` cannot mint anything, which is `ERR_MATCH_RULE_DISABLED`, and neither endpoint may be `MERGED_AWAY` (`ERR_PROFILE_MERGED_AWAY`) or `DELETED` (`ERR_PROFILE_DELETED`).

Confidence is then computed from the four evidence values under a fixed weight table: an email component is worth 0.40, a phone 0.25, a name 0.20 and an address 0.15, the four summing to exactly 1.00. The **raw** score is the sum, over only those components whose evidence value is non-null, of that component's weight multiplied by its similarity value. The two policies then diverge on what that raw score is divided by, and this is one of the platform's sharpest behavioural forks. Under `STRICT_ZERO` the confidence **is** the raw score, measured against the full weight base of 1.00; an absent component therefore contributes nothing and permanently caps what a sparse pair can score, no matter how perfect the evidence it does have. Under `RENORMALIZE` the denominator shrinks to the summed weight of the present components only, so a pair that matches perfectly on everything it has scores 1.0000 even with two of the four components missing; where that summed present weight is 0 — every component absent — the confidence is exactly 0.0000 rather than an undefined division. In both policies the result is rounded half up to four decimal places before any threshold comparison.

The rounded confidence decides the link's fate against the minting rule's own thresholds, and both boundaries are inclusive. Below `review_floor` no link is created and the proposal fails with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_FLOOR`. At or above `review_floor` but below `auto_merge_threshold`, the link is created as `PROPOSED` and waits for a steward. At or above `auto_merge_threshold` the link is created and immediately advanced `PROPOSED` → `CONFIRMED` with `decided_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system` rather than to any header value, `decided_at` stamped at the logical clock now, and the full merge run inside the creating call — the platform's only machine-authored merge. Auto-confirmation additionally requires both profiles to be `RESOLVED` with a `privacy_status` outside `LEGAL_HOLD`, `DELETION_PENDING` and `DELETED`. Every link records its `match_method` copied from the minting rule's `rule_kind`, and holds `survivor_profile_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `void_reason` null until something decides it.

A steward adjudicates a `PROPOSED` link by recording a `decision` of either `CONFIRM` or `REJECT`. This is a **branching fork**: the two branches share no side effect beyond the link's own status, `decided_by_user_id` (set to the acting `X-User-ID`), `decided_at` and its `version` increment. Both require the link to be `PROPOSED`; a decision on an already-decided or voided link is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The confirm branch additionally requires both profiles to be `RESOLVED` and neither to be under `LEGAL_HOLD`, `DELETED` or `DELETION_PENDING`; the reject branch imposes no profile condition at all, since it touches no profile.

**The confirm branch merges.** Survivorship is decided by three discriminators applied in order, the first that separates the pair winning outright: the higher `source_record_count`; then the higher `trust_tier` of the feed named by the profile's `primary_source_system_id`; then the earlier `created_at`. The profile's `id` is never consulted as a tie-breaker. The other profile is the loser. Seven identifier fields — `email_norm`, `phone_norm`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `postal_code`, `address_hash` and `loyalty_number` — follow field-level survivorship: the survivor keeps each of its own non-null values and adopts the loser's value only where its own is null. Four quantities roll up onto the survivor: `source_record_count` and `lifetime_value_cents` each increase by the loser's amount, `contributing_source_system_codes` becomes the sorted union of both lists, `last_activity_at` becomes the later of the two, and `loyalty_tier` becomes the higher of the two on the `NONE`-`SILVER`-`GOLD`-`PLATINUM` ordinal scale. The survivor's `primary_source_system_id` is re-elected as the contributing feed with the highest `trust_tier`, ties broken by the earlier feed `created_at`, and its `channel_engagement_score` is recomputed from its refreshed `activatable_channels`.

The loser is then torn down: its `resolution_status` moves `RESOLVED` → `MERGED_AWAY`, its `merged_into_profile_id` names the survivor, its `household_id` is cleared because household membership follows the surviving identity, its `lifetime_value_cents` is set to 0 because the value moved rather than duplicated, and its `activatable_channels` becomes empty. Every `ACTIVE` consent election of the loser is re-parented to the survivor; where the survivor already holds an `ACTIVE` election in the same channel-and-purpose group, the newly combined group is re-ranked by the precedence order of §3.6 and every election that is not rank one becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Every `ACTIVE` suppression entry of the loser is re-parented to the survivor unchanged. And every **other** `PROPOSED` link touching the loser becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` of `SUPERSEDED` — the loser can no longer be a match candidate for anyone.

Confirmation then propagates three hops further. Hop one recomputes the survivor's `activatable_channels` over the merged consent set. Hop two, caused by hop one, revisits every activation in `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING` whose destination's `channel` has dropped out of the survivor's `activatable_channels`, recomputing that activation's `audience_size` and `suppressed_count`; where an `audience_size` collapses to 0 the activation is paused with `paused_reason` of `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` and its `dispatched_hash_count` is refunded to the destination's `remaining_daily_hash_quota`. Hop three, caused by hop two, decrements the paused activation's segment `active_activation_count`, and where that count reaches 0 while the logical clock is at or after the segment's `governance_expires_at`, the segment moves `APPROVED` → `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED` and its `sensitive_use_approved` is reset to false. Losing the last household member dissolves the household as described in §3.5. Alongside all of this, the rule's `confirmed_count` increases by 1 and its `precision_estimate` is recomputed.

**The reject branch teaches the rule.** No profile is mutated: `survivor_profile_id` stays null and both profiles keep `resolution_status` of `RESOLVED`. Instead the rule's `rejected_count` increases by 1 and `precision_estimate` is recomputed as `confirmed_count` divided by the sum of `confirmed_count` and `rejected_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and held at exactly 1.0 while that denominator is 0. Where the recomputed estimate falls strictly below the rule's own `min_precision`, hop two moves the rule `ENABLED` → `DISABLED`, and hop three voids every **other** link minted by that rule whose status is still `PROPOSED`, each with `void_reason` of `RULE_DISABLED`. Voiding never touches the rule's counters — a voided link is neither a confirmation nor a rejection, and so cannot itself move the precision estimate. There is no re-enable path: a disabled rule is retired for good and replaced by declaring a new rule under a fresh `code`. Links are also voided by erasure, as §3.11 describes; a link already `CONFIRMED` whose `survivor_profile_id` reaches `DELETED` becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` of `PROFILE_DELETED`, while the loser it merged keeps `resolution_status` of `MERGED_AWAY` — voiding the evidence never un-merges an identity.

Links are readable individually and as a list, the latter filterable by `left_profile_id`, `right_profile_id`, `match_rule_id`, `survivor_profile_id` and `status`, which together expose the reverse traversal of all four of the link's foreign keys. Rules are readable individually — exposing the live `precision_estimate` — and as a list ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is exactly the order the resolution passes scan them in, filterable by `rule_kind` and `status`.

### 3.5. Household Formation, Overflow Buckets and Manual Reconciliation

A **household** groups the profiles that share a postal address. Its grouping key is `address_hash`, minted by promotion when the first profile at that address appears, and its `sequence_no` is the overflow index at that address: the first household there is 1, the next 2, and so on. A household holds at most six members.

Assignment is a strict waterfall. For a profile with a non-null `address_hash`, the buckets considered are the `ACTIVE` households at that same address, taken in `sequence_no` ascending order; the first bucket whose `member_count` is below six takes the profile and the search stops there. Where no bucket has room, a new household is minted at that address whose `sequence_no` is one greater than the highest `sequence_no` among **all** households at that address — including `DISSOLVED` ones, so a dissolved bucket's index is retired and never re-issued — and the profile joins it. The consequence is a strictly ordered overflow: the sixth profile to arrive at an address still joins bucket 1, and the seventh opens bucket 2. It also means gaps are refilled ahead of extension: when a member leaves bucket 1 through a merge or an erasure, bucket 1 is once again below capacity, so the next arrival at that address fills the reopened slot rather than joining bucket 2.

Three household fields are derived and are recomputed on **every** membership change. `member_count` is the number of profiles whose `household_id` names this household and whose `resolution_status` is not `MERGED_AWAY` and whose `privacy_status` is not `DELETED`. `dominant_last_name` is the most frequent non-null `last_name` among those members, ties broken alphabetically ascending, and is null when every member's last name is null. `combined_lifetime_value_cents` is the sum of the members' `lifetime_value_cents`. When a recomputation drives `member_count` to 0, the household moves `ACTIVE` → `DISSOLVED`, its `dominant_last_name` is cleared to null and its `combined_lifetime_value_cents` is reset to 0, and its `version` increments by 1. Dissolution is terminal and a dissolved bucket is never reused.

An `IdentitySteward` may override the waterfall to reconcile an address verified by hand, moving a profile into an explicitly named `household_id`. The override is narrowly guarded. The target household's `status` must be `ACTIVE` **and** its `address_hash` must equal the profile's own `address_hash`; either failure — a dissolved bucket, or a bucket at a different address — is `ERR_INVALID_HOUSEHOLD_STATE`. The target's `member_count` must be strictly below six **before** the assignment, a full bucket being refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note the ordering of those two guards: state is judged before capacity, so a dissolved household that is also full reports the state error. On success the profile's `household_id` is set to the target, all three derived fields are recomputed on **both** the vacated household and the receiving one, the vacated household dissolves if the move emptied it, and the profile's `version` plus the `version` of each touched household each increment by 1.

Households are readable individually and as a list; the list's `address_hash` filter is what exposes the overflow buckets at a single address, and a `status` filter separates live buckets from dissolved ones. Within one address the ordering by `sequence_no` ascending is the same order the waterfall walks, so a list read is a direct picture of which bucket the next arrival will fill.

### 3.6. Consent Capture and the Effective-Channel Election

Only a `PrivacyOfficer` may capture a **consent election**. An election names its subject with `profile_id`, its capture point with `source_system_id`, the `channel` it governs — one of `EMAIL`, `SMS`, `PUSH`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `ADVERTISING` — the `purpose` it was collected for, being one of `MARKETING`, `PERSONALIZATION` or `MEASUREMENT`, and the `consent_state` the subject chose, either `OPT_IN` or `OPT_OUT`. It also carries `captured_at`, the instant the subject actually decided, which may not lie in the future — a `captured_at` after the logical clock now is refused with `ERR_FUTURE_CAPTURED_AT` — and an optional `expires_at` at which the election lapses, which must be strictly after `captured_at` when supplied (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`) and which being null means the election never lapses. Capture is guarded by the parent-version check of §2.6 through `expected_profile_version`, refuses a `MERGED_AWAY` subject with `ERR_PROFILE_MERGED_AWAY`, refuses a `DELETED` subject with `ERR_PROFILE_DELETED`, and refuses a capture point whose feed is `QUARANTINED` with `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_QUARANTINED`.

Only elections whose `purpose` is `MARKETING` gate activation. Elections captured for `PERSONALIZATION` or `MEASUREMENT` are stored and ranked within their own group exactly as marketing elections are, but they never affect a profile's `activatable_channels`; they exist so the platform can prove what was collected and for what.

The elections of one subject, channel and purpose form a **group**, and within a group precedence is total. Qualifying elections are ranked by `captured_at` descending — the most recent decision wins — then, where two elections were captured at the same instant, by the `trust_tier` of the capture feed descending, and finally by the election's own `created_at` ascending. `precedence_rank` records that position as a one-based index. Rank one is the winner and is the **only** election in its group whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; every lower-ranked sibling carries rank two, three and so on with `status` of `SUPERSEDED`, each such displacement moving that record `ACTIVE` → `SUPERSEDED` and incrementing its `version`. Capturing a new election therefore re-ranks the whole group, and the same re-ranking runs when promotion materialises the `consent_signals` of a raw record.

Resolving a channel for a subject proceeds in three steps at the logical clock now. **Step one applies three hard gates in order, and the first that fires answers `OPT_OUT` with no election consulted at all.** A `privacy_status` other than `NORMAL` opts the subject out of every channel. Failing that, an in-window `ACTIVE` suppression entry whose `entry_type` is `PRIVACY_DELETE`, `GLOBAL_OPT_OUT` or `LEGAL_HOLD` opts the subject out of every channel. Failing that, an in-window `ACTIVE` entry whose `entry_type` is `DO_NOT_SELL` opts the subject out of the `ADVERTISING` channel only, every other channel falling through to step two. Suppression windows here are half-open — `effective_at` inclusive, `expires_at` exclusive — so an entry whose `expires_at` equals the current instant no longer suppresses anything.

**Step two consults the elections.** The qualifying set for a channel is the subject's elections on that channel with `purpose` of `MARKETING` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` **or** `SUPERSEDED`, whose `captured_at` is at or before now, and whose `expires_at` is either null or strictly after now. Note that superseded elections still qualify — they are ranked, merely outranked — whereas `EXPIRED` and `TOMBSTONED` elections never qualify at all. The rank-one member's `consent_state` is the resolution. **Step three is the default:** where the qualifying set is empty the channel resolves to `OPT_OUT`. Consent here is strictly opt-in; silence never authorises anything.

A channel is activatable for a subject only where its resolution is `OPT_IN`. `activatable_channels` is the sorted alphabetical-ascending list of the activatable channels among the five, and `channel_engagement_score` is the count of those channels divided by five, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a subject reachable on every channel scores 1.00, one reachable on three scores 0.60, and a fully suppressed subject holds an empty channel list and a score of 0.00. `consent_record_count` counts the subject's elections whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. All of this is re-derived whenever an election is captured or changes status, whenever a suppression entry is created, released or expires, whenever the subject's `privacy_status` moves, and at every activation dispatch or resume.

Losing a channel has downstream consequences within the same request. When a channel drops out of a subject's `activatable_channels`, every delivering activation whose destination uses that channel recomputes its audience, and an activation whose `audience_size` reaches 0 is paused with `paused_reason` of `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` and its dispatched hashes refunded, as §3.9 sets out. Capture therefore increments both the election's `version` and the subject profile's `version`.

Any role may inspect a subject's consent position as a computed read that resolves all five channels at once and reports, per channel, both the resolution and which gate or precedence rank produced it. That read returns `profile_id`, an `evaluated_at` instant, the `activatable_channels` list, the `channel_engagement_score` and a `channels` array, and it mutates nothing. Elections are also readable individually — exposing `precedence_rank` within the group — and as a list filterable by `profile_id`, `source_system_id`, `channel`, `purpose` and `status`, the first two being the reverse traversals of the election's two foreign keys, ordered by `precedence_rank` ascending within a group. Profiles themselves are readable individually and as a list filterable by `origin_batch_id`, `primary_source_system_id`, `household_id`, `merged_into_profile_id`, `resolution_status` and `privacy_status` — the first four being the reverse traversals of the profile's four foreign keys.

### 3.7. Suppression Windows, Do-Not-Sell and Legal Holds

A **suppression entry** is a dated instruction to stop using a subject's data. Only a `PrivacyOfficer` may add one, naming the subject with `profile_id`, an `entry_type`, a `reason_code` of one to forty characters after trimming, an `effective_at` that starts the window inclusively, and an optional `expires_at` that ends it exclusively and that must be strictly after `effective_at` when supplied. A null `expires_at` leaves the window open-ended. The four entry types differ only in breadth: `PRIVACY_DELETE`, `GLOBAL_OPT_OUT` and `LEGAL_HOLD` each suppress every channel, while `DO_NOT_SELL` suppresses the `ADVERTISING` channel alone. The subject may be neither `MERGED_AWAY` nor `DELETED`.

Two entries of the same subject and the **same** `entry_type` may not overlap in time, a collision being `ERR_SUPPRESSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the window is half-open, a new entry whose `effective_at` falls exactly on an existing entry's `expires_at` merely touches it and is accepted; and because a null `expires_at` extends forever, an open-ended entry collides with every later window of its type. Entries of *different* types never collide with each other, so a subject may simultaneously carry a `DO_NOT_SELL` and a `LEGAL_HOLD` covering the same days.

A new entry drives the subject's `privacy_status`. The three narrow-purpose types move it `NORMAL` → `SUPPRESSED`; note that `DO_NOT_SELL` moves the axis to `SUPPRESSED` too, even though only the advertising channel is actually masked, because the subject is no longer in an unqualified normal state. A `LEGAL_HOLD` entry moves it `NORMAL` → `LEGAL_HOLD`, and may be raised on a subject that is `NORMAL`, `SUPPRESSED` or `DELETION_PENDING`. It sets `activatable_channels` to empty only where the prior state was `NORMAL` or `SUPPRESSED`; the precedence exception of §2.10 applies to the third case, where a hold raised on a `DELETION_PENDING` subject is stored `ACTIVE` and leaves the privacy axis at `DELETION_PENDING`, whose channel list is already empty. That stored-but-outranked hold still blocks erasure propagation and is the only route by which a deletion request ever becomes blocked. Creation stamps `created_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — or with the literal string `system` for the entries minted by a retention run or by a deletion request — recomputes the subject's `activatable_channels` and `channel_engagement_score`, pauses every delivering activation whose audience lost this subject's channel with `paused_reason` of `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` while refunding its quota, and increments both the entry's `version` and the subject's `version` by 1.

A `PrivacyOfficer` releases an `ACTIVE` entry, which moves it `ACTIVE` → `RELEASED`, stamps `released_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `released_at` at the logical clock now, and increments the entry's `version`. Two conditions narrow the release. Releasing a `LEGAL_HOLD` entry demands segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the entry's `created_by_user_id`, the same officer attempting both roles being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And a `PRIVACY_DELETE` entry whose `deletion_request_id` is non-null is not releasable at all — those are minted by the erasure machinery and answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since a subject's right to erasure cannot be quietly withdrawn by an operator. A `PRIVACY_DELETE` entry raised by hand, with no `deletion_request_id`, is releasable normally.

Release re-derives the subject's privacy position over whatever entries remain. The precedence order of §2.10 is applied afresh: the subject returns to `LEGAL_HOLD` if another active hold remains, to `SUPPRESSED` if another active narrow entry remains, and only to `NORMAL` when no entry of that subject is still `ACTIVE`, at which point `activatable_channels` is recomputed from the surviving elections and the pre-suppression channel set is restored. Releasing a hold carries one further cascade of its own: where the release returns the subject from `LEGAL_HOLD` to `NORMAL`, every deletion request of that subject sitting in `BLOCKED_LEGAL_HOLD` is returned to `RECEIVED` and its `blocked_reason` is cleared, so the erasure that the hold interrupted may proceed.

The same re-derivation runs without a human when a window simply lapses: a retention run moves an `ACTIVE` entry whose `expires_at` is non-null and at or before the logical clock now to `EXPIRED`, then re-derives the subject's `privacy_status` and `activatable_channels` exactly as the release path does. Both `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal; neither an expired nor a released entry can be released again. Entries are readable individually — exposing the window and the release audit fields — and as a list filterable by `profile_id`, `deletion_request_id`, `entry_type` and `status`, the first two being the reverse traversals of the entry's two foreign keys.

### 3.8. Segment Definition and Sensitive-Attribute Governance

A **segment** is a named audience definition authored by a `SegmentAnalyst`. It carries a `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters after trimming and a `criteria` array of between one and six membership predicates. Each predicate is an object with an `attribute`, an `operator` and a `value`. The permitted attributes are `postal_code`, `loyalty_tier`, `lifetime_value_cents`, `channel_engagement_score`, `inferred_health_interest`, `inferred_ethnicity_affinity` and `precise_geolocation_grid`; the permitted operators are `EQ`, `GTE` and `IN`, and an `IN` predicate requires its `value` to be a non-empty array.

Membership is a conjunction: a profile is a member only when **every** predicate holds. Predicates are evaluated left to right and stop at the first failure. `EQ` holds when the profile's attribute is non-null and exactly equal to the predicate value, compared as a case-sensitive string or enum. `GTE` holds when the attribute is non-null and at least the predicate value, numerically — it applies to `lifetime_value_cents` and `channel_engagement_score`, and to `loyalty_tier` on the ordinal scale where `NONE` counts as 0, `SILVER` as 1, `GOLD` as 2 and `PLATINUM` as 3. `IN` holds when the attribute is non-null and is one of the elements of the predicate's array. A null attribute **never** satisfies any operator: absence is not a match, and a segment on an inferred attribute silently excludes every profile for which that attribute was never inferred. The eligible population is narrower still: only profiles whose `resolution_status` is not `MERGED_AWAY` and whose `privacy_status` is exactly `NORMAL` are considered. A merged-away profile is never a member because its attributes now live on the survivor, and a profile in any non-normal privacy state is excluded regardless of what its attributes say.

`member_count` is the size of that member set, and it is a live figure. Any change that moves a profile into or out of the eligible population — a merge setting `resolution_status` to `MERGED_AWAY`, a suppression setting `privacy_status` to `SUPPRESSED`, a conversion raising `lifetime_value_cents`, a consent change moving `channel_engagement_score` — re-derives `member_count` for every non-retired segment whose criteria touch the changed attribute, within the causing request. The recomputation settles in a single pass because criteria never reference another segment's membership. `active_activation_count` separately counts this segment's activations in `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING`.

`uses_sensitive_attributes` is **derived, never supplied**: it is true when any criterion's `attribute` is one of the three sensitive attributes `inferred_health_interest`, `inferred_ethnicity_affinity` or `precise_geolocation_grid`, and a client value for it is not accepted. Sensitivity changes nothing about how membership is computed — a sensitive predicate is evaluated exactly like an ordinary one. What it changes is whether the resulting audience may ever leave the platform: **governance gates activation, not membership.** Two further inputs support that gate. `governance_justification`, which is null or ten to five hundred characters after trimming, is the written case for using sensitive data; and `governance_valid_days`, an integer from 1 to 365 defaulting to 90 days — one calendar quarter — is how long an approval will remain valid. A new segment is `DRAFT` with `sensitive_use_approved` false, `rejection_count` at 0, and its `member_count` computed immediately.

The review loop is a genuine cycle, and a segment may traverse it any number of times. An analyst submits a segment that is `DRAFT` or `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED`, moving it to `PENDING_GOVERNANCE_REVIEW`, stamping `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and recomputing `member_count` over the current population so the reviewer sees a current figure rather than an authoring-time one. Where `uses_sensitive_attributes` is true, submission additionally requires `governance_justification` to be non-null and at least ten characters after trimming; a missing or too-short justification is refused with `ERR_GOVERNANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. A submission from any other state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `DataGovernanceOfficer` then records a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by `sensitive_use_approved`. Both branches require the segment to be `PENDING_GOVERNANCE_REVIEW` and both enforce segregation of duties — the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, an author approving their own audience being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and both stamp `decided_by_user_id` and increment the segment's `version`. Approving moves the segment `PENDING_GOVERNANCE_REVIEW` → `APPROVED`, takes `sensitive_use_approved` from the request body, stamps `approved_at` at the logical clock now, and sets `governance_expires_at` to `approved_at` advanced by `governance_valid_days` days. Where `uses_sensitive_attributes` is true, an approval whose body `sensitive_use_approved` is not true is refused with `ERR_GOVERNANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` — sensitive use must be affirmed explicitly, not inherited from a general approval.

Rejecting reverts the segment `PENDING_GOVERNANCE_REVIEW` → `DRAFT`, increments `rejection_count` by 1, forces `sensitive_use_approved` back to false, and **clears `submitted_by_user_id`** so that the next submission re-arms the segregation check against whoever resubmits. Crucially, a rejection leaves `approved_at` and `governance_expires_at` untouched: a later rejection never rewrites the history of an earlier approval, and those two timestamps continue to describe the approval that once happened.

An approval decays. Once the logical clock reaches `governance_expires_at`, a retention run moves the segment `APPROVED` → `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED` and resets `sensitive_use_approved` to false; the same transition can also be reached mid-request when a cascade drops the segment's `active_activation_count` to 0 at or after that instant. Expiry pauses delivery: every activation of that segment in `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING` moves to `PAUSED` with `paused_reason` of `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED`, and each such activation's `dispatched_hash_count` is refunded to its destination's `remaining_daily_hash_quota`. From `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED` the segment may be resubmitted, closing the second and longer of the two loops.

Retirement is terminal and irreversible. A segment that is `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` or `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED` may be retired; the analyst may retire a `DRAFT`, while a `DataGovernanceOfficer` may retire from any of the three. Retirement sets `status` to `RETIRED`, forces `active_activation_count` to 0, and terminates **every** activation of the segment that is not already terminated — those in `DRAFT`, `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE`, `UNDERPERFORMING` or `PAUSED` — each moving to `TERMINATED` with `paused_reason` of `SEGMENT_RETIRED` and `terminated_at` stamped at the logical clock now. Any activation that was still delivering, meaning it was `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING`, has its `dispatched_hash_count` refunded to its destination. A paused activation's hashes were already refunded when it paused and are not refunded twice. The segment's `version`, each terminated activation's `version` and each touched destination's `version` each increment by 1. Nothing accepts a trigger afterwards. Segments are readable individually and as a list filterable by `status` and `uses_sensitive_attributes`.

### 3.9. Destinations, Audience Resolution and the Activation Lifecycle

A **destination** is an external advertising or messaging platform, registered by a `DataPlatformAdmin` under a `code` unique across all destinations — three to twenty-four characters of upper-case letters, digits and underscores, a repeat being `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Its `platform_kind` is either `ADVERTISING` or `MESSAGING`, and its `channel` is the single one of `EMAIL`, `SMS`, `PUSH`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `ADVERTISING` it delivers on, matched against each profile's `activatable_channels`. Its `hashing_algorithm` fixes the identifier contract: `SHA256_EMAIL_LOWER` requires a profile's `email_norm`, while `SHA256_PHONE_E164` requires its `phone_norm`.

Eight further inputs configure the destination's commercial and safety envelope, seven of them defaulted. `match_rate_basis` chooses which match-rate variant drives the performance state machine, either `GROSS` (the default) or `NET`. `min_match_rate`, defaulting to 0.70 at no more than four decimals, is the rate at or above which a reported activation is considered healthy. `min_audience_size`, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 50, is the k-anonymity floor. `daily_hash_quota`, an integer of at least 1 and the one required commercial input, is how many hashes the platform accepts per UTC calendar day. `cooldown_minutes`, an integer of at least 0 defaulting to 60 — one hour — is the clear time that must separate two **disjoint** delivery windows on this destination. `max_concurrent_activations`, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 2, caps how many windows may cover any single instant. `sensitive_attribute_permitted`, defaulting to false, states whether audiences built on sensitive attributes may be sent here at all. And `deletion_ack_sla_hours`, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 72 — three days — is how long the platform has to acknowledge an erasure task. A destination's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, defaulting to active; an inactive destination refuses activation creation, dispatch and resume with `ERR_DESTINATION_INACTIVE` and is skipped entirely by erasure fan-out. On registration `remaining_daily_hash_quota` is set equal to `daily_hash_quota`, `quota_reset_on` is set to the UTC calendar date of the logical clock now in year-month-day form, and `underperforming_activation_count` starts at 0. Destinations are readable individually — exposing the live `remaining_daily_hash_quota` and `underperforming_activation_count` — and as a list ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is the same order erasure fan-out visits them in, filterable by `platform_kind`, `channel` and `status`.

An **activation** is one delivery of one segment to one destination over one window. A `SegmentAnalyst` creates it by naming `segment_id`, `destination_id`, an inclusive `window_start_at` and an inclusive `window_end_at` that must be strictly later (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`), under the parent-version guard of §2.6 via `expected_segment_version`. Creation is where the destination's two scheduling constraints are enforced, and they divide the space of possible conflicts between them without overlap.

The **cooldown gap** is judged only between windows that do **not** intersect. Activation windows are closed intervals — both endpoints inclusive — so two windows that merely touch at a single instant do intersect and are therefore outside the cooldown's scope entirely. For every existing non-terminated activation on the same destination whose window is disjoint from the new one, the two must be separated by at least `cooldown_minutes` of clear time, measured end-to-start in both directions and inclusive at equality: either the new window starts at or after the other window's end advanced by the cooldown, or the other window starts at or after the new window's end advanced by the cooldown. A violation is `ERR_ACTIVATION_COOLDOWN_VIOLATION`. A `cooldown_minutes` of 0 therefore imposes no separation at all.

The **concurrency cap** governs exactly what the cooldown does not: simultaneous coverage. No instant may be covered by more than `max_concurrent_activations` windows among this destination's activations in `DRAFT`, `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE`, `UNDERPERFORMING` or `PAUSED` — note that draft and paused activations still hold their slot, and only terminated ones release it. The check sweeps the closed intervals, and a window that touches another only at an endpoint still counts as covering that shared instant. Breaching it is `ERR_ACTIVATION_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`. This division is what makes the concurrency cap meaningful: intersecting windows are never a cooldown problem, and disjoint windows are never a concurrency problem. Creation also refuses an inactive destination, and refuses a sensitive audience that either the segment or the destination has not cleared, with `ERR_SENSITIVE_ATTRIBUTE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A new activation is `DRAFT` with every audience and match-rate field at its default, and the parent segment's `version` increments by 1.

**Dispatch** resolves the audience and consumes quota. It requires the activation to be `DRAFT`, the segment to be `APPROVED` with the logical clock now strictly before its `governance_expires_at` — any other governance posture being `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_APPROVED` — the destination to be `ACTIVE`, and, for a sensitive audience, both the segment's `sensitive_use_approved` and the destination's `sensitive_attribute_permitted` to be true. Resolution then proceeds in four counts. `candidate_count` is the size of the segment's member set at this instant. The audience is those candidates whose `activatable_channels` contains the destination's `channel`, and `audience_size` is its size. `suppressed_count` is `candidate_count` less `audience_size` — the members removed by consent or suppression. `dispatched_hash_count` is the number of audience members whose required identifier, selected by the destination's `hashing_algorithm`, is non-null. An audience member without that identifier is silently skipped: it still counts toward `audience_size`, and so still helps satisfy k-anonymity, but it produces no hash and consumes no quota, which is why `dispatched_hash_count` is always at most `audience_size`.

Three gates then fire in this fixed order, each inclusive at equality on the passing side. `audience_size` must be at least 1, an audience emptied by consent or suppression being refused with `ERR_EMPTY_AUDIENCE`. `audience_size` must be at least the destination's `min_audience_size`, a smaller audience being `ERR_AUDIENCE_BELOW_K_ANONYMITY`. And `dispatched_hash_count` must be at most `remaining_daily_hash_quota`, an over-budget dispatch being `ERR_HASH_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. Because the k-anonymity gate precedes the quota gate, an audience that is both too small and too expensive reports the privacy failure rather than the commercial one. On success the activation moves `DRAFT` → `DISPATCHED`, `dispatched_at` is stamped at the logical clock now, the destination's `remaining_daily_hash_quota` decreases by `dispatched_hash_count`, the segment's `active_activation_count` increases by 1, and the activation's, the destination's and the segment's `version` each increment by 1.

**Match reporting** is the destination platform's own duty, and only the `DestinationPlatform` actor whose `X-User-ID` equals the activation's `destination_id` may report — anyone else's identity being `ERR_DESTINATION_ACTOR_MISMATCH`. A report carries `vendor_matched_count`, `unusable_hash_count` and an optional `attributed_conversions` array whose elements each carry a `profile_id`, a `converted_at` instant and a `revenue_cents` amount. Report validity is checked **before** any rate is computed: the sum of `vendor_matched_count` and `unusable_hash_count` may not exceed the `dispatched_hash_count` fixed at dispatch, an inflated report being `ERR_MATCH_REPORT_EXCEEDS_DISPATCH`. Reports are accepted while the activation is `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING`, so a destination may report repeatedly over the window.

Two rates are then computed and **both are always stored**, whichever one the destination has nominated. `gross_match_rate` divides `vendor_matched_count` by `dispatched_hash_count`, or is exactly 0.0000 where nothing was dispatched. `net_match_rate` divides `vendor_matched_count` by the usable count — `dispatched_hash_count` less `unusable_hash_count` — or is exactly 0.0000 where that usable count is zero or negative. Both round half up to four decimal places. The gross basis divides by everything that was sent, so hashes the platform could not even parse count against the marketer and the destination carries no credit for its own data hygiene; the net basis removes the unusable hashes from the denominator, so the rate measures only what the platform could actually evaluate. Identical report data therefore yields a strictly higher net rate and can land the activation in a **different state** than the gross basis would. `effective_match_rate` is the gross rate where the destination's `match_rate_basis` is `GROSS` and the net rate otherwise; all three are null until the first report arrives.

The effective rate routes the activation, inclusively at the boundary: at or above the destination's `min_match_rate` the activation becomes `ACTIVE`, and strictly below it becomes `UNDERPERFORMING`. Both routings are reachable from `DISPATCHED` on a first report, and later reports move the activation back and forth between `ACTIVE` and `UNDERPERFORMING` as the recomputed rate crosses the floor in either direction. Every entry into `UNDERPERFORMING` increments the destination's `underperforming_activation_count` by 1 and every recovery to `ACTIVE` decrements it by 1. A report also appends its conversions to `attributed_conversions` — appended, never replaced, so the array accumulates across reports — recomputes `attributed_conversion_count` as the array's length, and cascades: each referenced profile's `lifetime_value_cents` increases by the reported `revenue_cents` and each such profile's household `combined_lifetime_value_cents` is recomputed. The activation's, each touched profile's, each touched household's and the destination's `version` each increment by 1. Note that raising `lifetime_value_cents` may itself move profiles in or out of segments whose criteria reference that attribute, re-deriving their `member_count`.

**Pausing** stops delivery and returns money. An analyst may pause an activation that is `DISPATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDERPERFORMING` — a dispatched activation that has not yet received a single report is pausable — and the same three arms are driven by the consent and governance cascades described elsewhere. Every arm sets `status` to `PAUSED` and writes `paused_reason`: `OPERATOR` for the explicit call, or `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` or `GOVERNANCE_EXPIRED` for the respective cascades. Every arm refunds `dispatched_hash_count` to the destination's `remaining_daily_hash_quota` and decrements the segment's `active_activation_count` by 1. The arms differ in exactly one respect: pausing from `UNDERPERFORMING` also decrements the destination's `underperforming_activation_count`, whereas pausing from `DISPATCHED` leaves that counter alone because a dispatched activation was never counted there.

**Resuming** re-runs the whole resolution against the world as it now is, which is why a resume can fail where the original dispatch succeeded. It requires the activation to be `PAUSED`, the segment to be `APPROVED` and unexpired, the destination to be `ACTIVE`, and the sensitive-attribute clearances to still hold; it then recomputes `candidate_count`, `audience_size`, `suppressed_count` and `dispatched_hash_count` against the current consent and suppression state and re-applies all three gates in the same order — so an audience that has shrunk below k-anonymity, or a quota that other activations have since consumed, blocks the resume. On success the activation moves `PAUSED` → `ACTIVE`, `paused_reason` is cleared to null, the recomputed `dispatched_hash_count` is deducted from `remaining_daily_hash_quota`, the segment's `active_activation_count` increases by 1, and the activation's, destination's and segment's `version` each increment by 1.

**Termination** arrives only from segment retirement, and it is terminal. Its five arms differ in their accounting, not their intent. From `ACTIVE`, `DISPATCHED` or `UNDERPERFORMING` the activation is terminated with `paused_reason` of `SEGMENT_RETIRED` and `terminated_at` stamped, its `dispatched_hash_count` is refunded, and the segment's `active_activation_count` decrements — the underperforming arm additionally decrementing the destination's `underperforming_activation_count`. From `PAUSED` there is no refund, because a paused activation was refunded when it paused. From `DRAFT` there is neither a refund nor an active-count change, because a draft never consumed quota and was never counted as active. Activations are readable individually — exposing the audience counts, both rate variants, the effective rate and the attributed conversions — and as a list filterable by `segment_id`, `destination_id` and `status`, the first two being the reverse traversals of the activation's two foreign keys.

### 3.10. Attribution and Overlap Measurement

Two computed reads let a `DataPlatformAdmin`, a `SegmentAnalyst` or a `DataGovernanceOfficer` measure a segment without mutating anything. Neither read changes a `version`, and both evaluate membership afresh at read time.

**Performance attribution** answers what an audience earned. Its input set is every activation of the segment whose `status` is not `DRAFT` — a draft activation has never delivered — and, within those, every element of `attributed_conversions` whose `converted_at` is at or after the segment's `approved_at`. The approval instant is the window's anchor: conversions that predate the approval are never credited, and where `approved_at` is null, because the segment has never been approved, the input set is empty and every output is zero. Qualifying conversions are ordered by `converted_at` ascending, then by the owning activation's `created_at` ascending, then by the element's own position within its activation's conversion array.

The `attribution_basis` query parameter chooses between two ways of counting, and they genuinely diverge. Under `TOTAL`, the default, `conversion_count` is the number of qualifying conversions and `attributed_revenue_cents` is the sum of their `revenue_cents` — every repeat purchase by the same person counts again. Under `INCREMENTAL`, only each distinct `profile_id`'s **chronologically first** conversion in the window is credited, so `conversion_count` is the number of distinct first touches and `attributed_revenue_cents` sums only those; a repeat purchase inside the window is treated as retained rather than incremental revenue. Because the first element is picked in the chronological order above, the two bases differ by exactly the repeat purchases, and they agree whenever no profile converted twice. Two outputs are identical under both bases: `unique_converter_count` is the number of distinct `profile_id` values across the qualifying conversions, and `member_count` is the segment's member-set size at read time.

`revenue_per_member_cents` is 0 where `member_count` is 0, and otherwise the `attributed_revenue_cents` figure divided by `member_count`, rounded half up to whole cents. **The order of operations is pinned:** sum first, divide once, round exactly once at the very end. The tempting alternative — dividing each conversion's `revenue_cents` by `member_count`, rounding each share, and then summing those shares — is explicitly not the rule and produces a different integer. With a `member_count` of 3 and three conversions of 100 cents each, the pinned order yields 100 while the per-conversion order would yield 99; implementations must reproduce 100. The read returns `segment_id`, the `attribution_basis` applied, `member_count`, `conversion_count`, `unique_converter_count`, `attributed_revenue_cents` and `revenue_per_member_cents`.

**Overlap measurement** answers how much two audiences share. Both segments' member sets are computed at read time, and the other segment is named by the `other_segment_id` query parameter, which must differ from the segment being read — naming the same segment twice is `ERR_OVERLAP_SAME_SEGMENT`, since a segment's overlap with itself is trivially total. The read reports `segment_member_count` and `other_segment_member_count` as the two set sizes, `overlap_count` as the number of profiles in both, and `union_count` as the two sizes added together less the overlap. Three ratios follow, each rounded half up to four decimal places: `jaccard_index` is the overlap divided by the union, or exactly 0.0000 where the union is empty; `containment_in_other` is the overlap divided by this segment's own size, or exactly 0.0000 where this segment is empty; and `containment_of_other` is the overlap divided by the other segment's size, or exactly 0.0000 where the other segment is empty. The Jaccard index is symmetric, but the two containment ratios are not — reading the pair from the other side swaps them, which is what makes the direction of the read meaningful. The read returns `segment_id`, `other_segment_id` and all seven of those figures.

### 3.11. Erasure, Downstream Verification and Retention Sweeps

An **erasure request** records a subject's right to be forgotten. Only a `PrivacyOfficer` may record one, naming the subject with `profile_id`, the origin of the demand in `request_origin` as one of `DATA_SUBJECT`, `RETENTION` or `REGULATOR`, and an external case reference in `requester_reference` of one to sixty characters after trimming. A subject may have only one erasure in flight: where any request for that subject is already `RECEIVED`, `BLOCKED_LEGAL_HOLD`, `PROPAGATING` or `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`, a second is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The subject's `privacy_status` must be `NORMAL` or `SUPPRESSED`; a `MERGED_AWAY` subject is `ERR_PROFILE_MERGED_AWAY` and an already-`DELETED` one is `ERR_PROFILE_DELETED`, erasure being unrepeatable.

Recording the request moves the subject `NORMAL` → `DELETION_PENDING`, empties its `activatable_channels`, and mints a system-owned suppression entry whose `entry_type` is `PRIVACY_DELETE`, whose `deletion_request_id` names this request, whose `created_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`, whose `effective_at` is the logical clock now and whose `expires_at` is null — an open-ended guard that keeps the subject out of every audience for as long as the erasure is in flight and that no operator may release. Every delivering activation whose audience contained the subject recomputes its counts, and where an audience empties the activation pauses with `paused_reason` of `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` and its hashes are refunded. The recompute also re-derives `gross_match_rate`, `net_match_rate` and `effective_match_rate` on those activations, so an activation whose effective rate has fallen below the destination's `min_match_rate` moves `ACTIVE` → `UNDERPERFORMING` on this path too. The request starts `RECEIVED` with all five task counters at 0, and the subject's `version` increments by 1.

**Propagation** fans the erasure out to the platforms that hold the subject's hashes, and it is guarded by the parent-version check of §2.6 via `expected_deletion_request_version`. It is accepted from `RECEIVED` or `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`, and it has three outcomes.

Where any suppression entry of the subject has `entry_type` of `LEGAL_HOLD` and `status` of `ACTIVE`, the hold wins. The request moves `RECEIVED` → `BLOCKED_LEGAL_HOLD`, `blocked_reason` is set to that entry's `reason_code`, no task is minted, the subject stays `DELETION_PENDING`, the request's `version` increments — and **the call still fails with `ERR_LEGAL_HOLD_ACTIVE`**. The recorded state change is the audit trail of the blocked attempt, which is why this is the second of the platform's two paths where a rejected call nonetheless leaves a mark. A legal hold released afterwards returns the request `BLOCKED_LEGAL_HOLD` → `RECEIVED` and clears `blocked_reason`, and this is the only route into and out of that state.

Where no hold applies, the qualifying destinations are those whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and which have at least one activation whose `status` is not `DRAFT` — a platform that never received a delivery has nothing to erase, and an inactive platform is skipped. Where at least one qualifies, the request moves `RECEIVED` → `PROPAGATING`, one **vendor deletion task** is minted per qualifying destination in destination `created_at` ascending order, `propagated_at` is stamped at the logical clock now, the five counters are recomputed and the request's `version` increments. Each task is created `PENDING` and immediately advanced `PENDING` → `DISPATCHED` within the same call, with `dispatched_at` at the logical clock now, `attempt_count` at 1, and `ack_deadline_at` set to `dispatched_at` advanced by that destination's `deletion_ack_sla_hours` hours.

Where **zero** destinations qualify, there is nothing downstream to verify and the erasure completes inside the propagate call itself: `total_vendor_task_count` stays 0, the request moves `RECEIVED` → `VERIFIED`, `verified_at` is stamped, and the subject is tombstoned immediately. This empty-set boundary is the fastest possible erasure and the only one that needs no external party.

The request's five counters are derived from its children at all times: `total_vendor_task_count` is every task minted across all propagate calls, `pending_vendor_task_count` counts those in `PENDING` or `DISPATCHED`, and `acknowledged_vendor_task_count`, `failed_vendor_task_count` and `timed_out_vendor_task_count` count the three remaining statuses one for one.

**Acknowledgement** is the destination platform's duty, and only the actor whose `X-User-ID` equals the task's `destination_id` may perform it — anyone else being `ERR_DESTINATION_ACTOR_MISMATCH`. A task in `DISPATCHED` or `TIMED_OUT` accepts an `outcome` of either `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `FAILED`, any other value being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, optionally accompanied by a `deletion_evidence_reference` and an `acknowledged_record_count` claiming how many records the platform erased. A successful outcome moves the task to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamps `acknowledged_at`; a failure moves it to `FAILED`, keeping whatever evidence reference was supplied. Either way the parent's counters are recomputed and the task's `version` increments. `ACKNOWLEDGED` is terminal — a second acknowledgement of the same task is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — while `FAILED` and `TIMED_OUT` are recoverable.

The parent's status is **compound**: past `PROPAGATING` it is derived entirely from its children. When `acknowledged_vendor_task_count` equals `total_vendor_task_count`, meaning every platform has confirmed, the request bubbles up to `VERIFIED` — from `PROPAGATING` or, on a late acknowledgement, from `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED` — and `verified_at` is stamped. While any sibling is still `PENDING` or `DISPATCHED` the parent stays `PROPAGATING` no matter how many failures have accumulated, because the outcome is not yet decided.

Reaching `VERIFIED` tombstones the subject, moving it `DELETION_PENDING` → `DELETED`. Seven fields are nulled: `email_norm`, `phone_norm`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `inferred_health_interest`, `inferred_ethnicity_affinity` and `precise_geolocation_grid`. Every consent election of the subject becomes `TOMBSTONED` with `precedence_rank` forced to 0, whatever it held before — active, superseded or expired — and `TOMBSTONED` is terminal for an election. Every identity link of the subject still in `PROPOSED` becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` of `PROFILE_DELETED`, and any `CONFIRMED` link whose `survivor_profile_id` is this subject is voided the same way while the profile it merged keeps `resolution_status` of `MERGED_AWAY`. The subject is detached from its household, whose `member_count` decrements and which dissolves if that count reaches 0. The subject's `resolution_status` is left entirely untouched — the two axes are independent, and a deleted profile remembers whether it was ever resolved.

**Retention runs** are how time itself acts on the platform. Only a `DataPlatformAdmin` may trigger one, it takes no input, and it performs four steps against the logical clock now **in this fixed order**. Step one expires every consent election whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` is non-null and at or before now, moving each `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED`, then re-ranks the affected groups — which promotes the next-ranked superseded sibling to `precedence_rank` one and `ACTIVE` — and recomputes each owner's `activatable_channels` and `channel_engagement_score`. Step two expires every suppression entry whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` is non-null and at or before now, moving each `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED`, and lifts each subject's `privacy_status` back toward `NORMAL` by the precedence rule of §2.10 wherever no active entry remains. Step three times out every vendor task that is `DISPATCHED` and whose `ack_deadline_at` is at or before now, moving each `DISPATCHED` → `TIMED_OUT`; where a parent request in `PROPAGATING` is left with no `PENDING` or `DISPATCHED` child and at least one child that is `TIMED_OUT` or `FAILED`, it moves `PROPAGATING` → `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`. A partially verified request keeps its subject at `DELETION_PENDING` — partial downstream erasure never completes the tombstone, because the platform will not claim a subject was forgotten while a vendor has not confirmed it. Step four expires every segment approval whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `governance_expires_at` is non-null and at or before now, with the pause-and-refund cascade of §3.8.

A retention run also resets the hash ledger: whenever the run's own UTC calendar date differs from a destination's `quota_reset_on`, that destination's `remaining_daily_hash_quota` is restored to its full `daily_hash_quota`, `quota_reset_on` is rewritten to the run's date and the destination's `version` increments. The run reports what it did as `run_at`, `expired_consent_record_count`, `expired_suppression_entry_count`, `timed_out_vendor_task_count`, `partially_verified_deletion_request_count`, `governance_expired_segment_count`, `paused_activation_count` and `quota_reset_destination_count`, and it increments the `version` of every entity it mutates by 1. Because every one of the four steps is driven by comparisons against the logical clock, and because the clock is only ever advanced by the explicit test-driving capability of §2.4 that echoes back `current_utc`, the entire expiry surface of the platform is reproducible.

A **retry** closes the final loop. Propagating a `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED` request re-dispatches every child that is `TIMED_OUT` or `FAILED` — each moving back to `DISPATCHED`, its `attempt_count` incrementing by 1 and its `dispatched_at` and `ack_deadline_at` refreshed against the current clock and the destination's SLA — and the request returns `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED` → `PROPAGATING` with `propagated_at` restamped. It does **not** mint duplicate tasks for destinations that already acknowledged, so `total_vendor_task_count` does not grow on a retry and a platform is never asked twice for evidence it has already given. Erasure requests are readable individually — exposing the compound status and all five counters — and as a list filterable by `profile_id` and `status`; tasks are readable individually — exposing the SLA deadline, the attempt count and the acknowledgement evidence — and as a list filterable by `deletion_request_id`, `destination_id` and `status`, the first two being the reverse traversals of the task's two foreign keys and their `created_at` ascending order matching the destination order in which they were minted.
