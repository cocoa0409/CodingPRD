# Commercial Laundry Equipment Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A commercial laundry equipment manufacturer does not sell a single product; it sells three quite different businesses that happen to share a factory. On-premises laundry machines go into hospitals, hotels and correctional facilities. Vended machines go into laundromats, where they must accept coins, cards or a phone. Industrial tunnel systems go into central plants that process tens of thousands of pounds of linen a day. Each of those product lines carries its own capacity envelope, its own utility-rebate story, its own assembly sequence and its own warranty exposure profile. Today that reality is spread across a nameplate spreadsheet, a shop-floor whiteboard, a compliance binder of efficiency test reports, a distributor training log kept in email, and a warranty claim queue that nobody can reconcile against parts inventory. The consequence is predictable: units get released to the floor against models whose efficiency certification has lapsed, rebate paperwork is filed against retired utility programs, distributors quote financing terms the channel will not honour, and warranty credits are issued for parts that were never in stock.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. The system tracks a machine model from catalogue registration through efficiency and water-consumption certification, utility rebate qualification, and release to the assembly floor; it tracks each serialized unit through drum fabrication, bearing installation and control integration, with station contention, technician certification and bearing preload all enforced at the moment a station operation is logged; it tracks the distributor channel from installation training through territory assignment, authorization, probation and termination; it prices equipment package deals with two-stage laundromat financing; and it settles warranty claims against a live service-parts inventory, rolling the result up into per-model exposure analysis by component class and duty cycle.

Three cross-cutting commitments shape almost every rule that follows. **Segregation of duties** is real and enforced: the compliance officer who prepared a certification dossier may not review it, the two review dimensions of a dossier must be decided by two different people, and the inspector who signs off a unit must not have worked a station on it. **Consistency is immediate**: when a technician's certification is revoked, every unit that technician touched is put on quality hold and every open warranty claim on those units is blocked before the response is returned — there is no background job and no eventual convergence. **Money is exact**: every amount is an integer number of cents, every ratio is carried at full decimal precision, and each formula names the single point at which rounding is permitted.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every resource path in this specification is mounted under the top-level prefix `/clm`, with the single exception of the test-driving logical-clock endpoint described in §2.5. The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 — this check runs before anything else, including body parsing and path resolution. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine role names below, or if it is a valid role name that the addressed endpoint does not list as authorized, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The nine roles are `ProductEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlantManager`, `TrainingAdministrator`, `ChannelManager`, `WarrantyAnalyst`, `Technician`, `QualityInspector` and `Distributor`.

`ProductEngineer` owns the product catalogue: machine models, service parts, and the manual release and suspension of a model. `ComplianceOfficer` prepares and reviews efficiency and water-consumption certification dossiers and maintains the utility rebate program catalogue. `PlantManager` runs the factory floor: assembly stations, build orders, floor releases and service-part stock movements. `TrainingAdministrator` owns technician records, certification grants, renewals and revocations, and distributor installation-training completions. `ChannelManager` owns the distributor network and the channel side of quoting: territory assignment, probation and termination, and quote decisions including credit verification. `WarrantyAnalyst` adjudicates and voids warranty claims and reads per-model warranty exposure. `Technician` is a factory or distributor technician acting on the shop floor. `QualityInspector` is a technician holding the QA sign-off scope, acting in the final-inspection capacity. `Distributor` is an equipment distributor acting on its own quotes and its own warranty claims.

Six of the nine roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProductEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlantManager`, `TrainingAdministrator`, `ChannelManager` or `WarrantyAnalyst`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `prepared_by_user_id` on a certification dossier, as `energy_reviewer_user_id` or `water_reviewer_user_id` on the dimension that operator decided, as `decided_by_user_id` on a package quote, or as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a warranty claim — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`, and it never requires that such an operator identifier resolve to a stored row.

Three roles carry an **entity-backed** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Technician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician` row; ownership and identity checks on any resource that references a technician compare that resource's technician reference to `X-User-ID`, which is why a station log's `technician_id` is taken from the acting header rather than trusted from the body. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of a `Technician` row — the same row space as the `Technician` role — and it is exactly this sharing that makes the segregation-of-duties check on final inspection meaningful: the inspector must not appear as the technician on any station log of the unit being signed off, and the inspector's identifier is retained as `qa_inspector_user_id` on the unit. When `X-User-Role` is `Distributor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a distributor compare that resource's distributor reference to `X-User-ID`.

For the two technician-backed roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a stored `Technician` row is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404 on the station-log and final-inspection operations. For the `Distributor` role, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a stored `Distributor` row is rejected with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404.

Ownership is enforced separately from authorization. A `Distributor` that addresses a package quote or a warranty claim whose distributor reference is not its own `X-User-ID`, or that submits a creation body naming a different distributor, is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. This applies to quote creation, reading a single quote, adding a quote line, deleting a quote line, submitting a quote, filing a warranty claim, and reading a single warranty claim. A `ChannelManager` and a `WarrantyAnalyst` are not scoped this way; they see the whole channel.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Paginated responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads do not carry these four fields.

Unless a collection states otherwise, rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections have a domain-natural order that overrides that default, and the ordering is part of the contract: machine models and package quotes are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending; rebate programs are ordered by `effective_from` ascending and then by `program_code` ascending; service parts by `part_number` ascending; assembly stations by `station_code` ascending; technicians by `employee_code` ascending; distributors by `distributor_code` ascending; station logs by `started_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Build orders, quote lines, warranty claims, and the per-model dossier collection are all ordered by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no domain-encoded structure. A client never supplies an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a creation body is an unknown field and is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, stamped once when the row is first written and never modified thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `MachineModel`, `RebateProgram`, `CertificationDossier`, `ServicePart`, `AssemblyStation`, `Technician`, `BuildOrder`, `Distributor`, `PackageQuote` and `WarrantyClaim` all carry the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Two resources are deliberately narrower. A `StationLog` and a `QuoteLine` are immutable journal rows: each carries only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, and no request may attempt to bump either. A quote line is never edited in place — it is added, or it is deleted and re-added.

A version bump on one row is not a version bump on another. When an operation on a child row changes a parent row's derived fields, it is the parent's `version` that advances, and this specification names the affected row explicitly wherever a cascade occurs. A cascade that changes several rows advances the `version` of each row it actually touches, and leaves untouched rows at their existing versions.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Its Two Sweeps

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. The server starts with the logical clock at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time for any comparison, stamp or expiry decision. A test-driving capability at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, authorized for the six operator roles `ProductEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlantManager`, `TrainingAdministrator`, `ChannelManager` and `WarrantyAnalyst`, sets the clock to the instant supplied as `to_iso8601`.

Advancing the clock runs two sweeps, in this order and never the reverse. The first sweep handles technician certification lapse: every `Technician` whose `cert_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `cert_expires_on` is at or before the new instant moves to `EXPIRED` with `cert_scopes` emptied to an empty array, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each swept technician's `version` advances by 1. The second sweep handles rebate program retirement: every `RebateProgram` whose `program_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` date falls strictly before the date component of the new instant moves to `RETIRED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each swept program's `version` advances by 1. The response reports the resulting `current_utc`, the `expired_technician_count` swept in the first pass, and the `retired_program_count` swept in the second.

Expiry is emphatically not revocation. A technician who lapses into `EXPIRED` loses their scopes and can no longer log station work, but no integrity cascade fires: no build order changes state, no `integrity_hold` is raised, and no warranty claim is blocked. Only an explicit revocation carries those consequences, and §2.11 explains why.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no floating-point currency fields and no minor-unit strings. Percentage inputs are decimal numbers expressed in percent units with exactly two decimal places, so a stored value of `7.25` means seven and one quarter percent and must be divided by one hundred before it participates in arithmetic. Basis-point figures are integers, so a stored value of `1450` means fourteen and one half percent.

Every ratio, percentage and intermediate product is evaluated in exact decimal arithmetic carrying at least twenty-eight significant digits, with **no intermediate rounding whatsoever**. Each calculation in §3 names exactly one point at which rounding is permitted, and rounding at that point is always **round half up**: a fractional remainder of exactly one half is resolved away from zero. Rounding earlier than the named point — for example rounding a discounted unit price before multiplying by a quantity, or rounding a monthly interest rate to six decimals before computing a payment — produces answers that differ by cents and is a defect, not a tolerable approximation.

Two derived quantities round to two decimal places rather than to whole cents: a model's first-pass-yield percentage and every percentage-typed input field. Two derived quantities use truncation toward negative infinity rather than rounding: a station log's billable labour in whole minutes, and a warranty part's duty-adjusted design life in whole cycles. Each of those is called out at the point of use.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system carry an explicit expected-version field, because in both cases the write is legitimately repeatable and therefore no state precondition can protect a client from acting on figures that have already moved.

Adding a line to a package quote requires a non-optional `expected_quote_version` field carrying the `version` of the parent `PackageQuote` as the client last observed it. Adding two identical lines to a quote is a perfectly valid two-line quote, so the operation cannot be guarded by a state check; the version check is what tells a client that the money fields it based its decision on — `line_subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents` and `total_cents` — have already changed. If `expected_quote_version` does not equal the quote's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. Deleting a quote line carries the same `expected_quote_version` field under the same rule and the same rejection.

Adjudicating a warranty claim requires a non-optional `expected_part_version` field carrying the `version` of the claimed `ServicePart` as the analyst last observed it. Adjudicating a second, different claim against the same part is legitimate on its own terms, so no state precondition covers it; the version check forces the analyst to re-read `on_hand_qty` and `stock_state` before committing another unit of that part. If `expected_part_version` does not equal the part's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and neither the claim nor the part is modified.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to any other write endpoint that include one are treated as carrying an unknown field, which is ignored.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

A single malformed or illegitimate request frequently violates several rules at once. The rule set is therefore **strictly and globally ordered**: when a request violates more than one rule, the response carries the error of the highest-ranked violated rule and no other, and the request is rejected before any state change or derived recomputation occurs. The order below is the whole contract; text order within later sections carries no precedence of its own.

The precedence chain runs authentication, then authorization, then request schema, then path existence left-to-right, then body foreign-key existence, then acting-identity existence, then ownership, then state preconditions, then optimistic-concurrency checks, then segregation of duties, then certification and safety, then referential fitment, then temporal and contention rules, then uniqueness and structural business constraints, and finally fiscal constraints. Spelled out rule by rule:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the nine declared roles and that role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. The request body parses, every required input field is present, every enum-typed value is a declared member of its enumeration, every integer sits inside its declared range, and every decimal carries no more than its declared scale. Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. Applies to every endpoint.
4. A machine-model identifier in the path resolves to a stored model — otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one model, reading a model's warranty analysis, applying a model lifecycle action, opening a certification dossier for a model, and listing a model's dossiers.
5. A rebate-program identifier in the path resolves to a stored program — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one rebate program.
6. A dossier identifier in the path resolves to a stored dossier — otherwise `ERR_DOSSIER_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one dossier, recording a review decision, and submitting a resurvey.
7. A service-part identifier in the path resolves to a stored part — otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one part and adjusting a part's stock.
8. An assembly-station identifier in the path resolves to a stored station — otherwise `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one station.
9. A technician identifier in the path resolves to a stored technician — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one technician and applying a certification action.
10. A build-order identifier in the path resolves to a stored build order — otherwise `ERR_BUILD_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one build order, releasing a unit to the floor, final inspection sign-off, and station-log creation.
11. A station-log identifier in the path resolves to a stored log — otherwise `ERR_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one station log.
12. A distributor identifier in the path resolves to a stored distributor — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one distributor, recording a training completion, and applying a channel status action.
13. A quote identifier in the path resolves to a stored package quote — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one quote, adding a line, deleting a line, submitting the quote, and recording a channel decision.
14. The `line_id` segment resolves to a quote line whose `quote_id` equals the quote already resolved by the preceding rule — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This is checked only after the parent quote itself resolved, and it also guards reading one quote line directly.
15. A warranty-claim identifier in the path resolves to a stored claim — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards reading one claim, adjudicating it, and voiding it.
16. A body `model_id` resolves to a stored machine model — otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards build-order creation and quote-line creation.
17. A body `part_id` resolves to a stored service part, and a body `supersedes_part_id` resolves to a stored service part when it is present — otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards service-part creation and warranty-claim filing.
18. A body `build_order_id` resolves to a stored build order — otherwise `ERR_BUILD_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards warranty-claim filing.
19. A body `station_id` resolves to a stored assembly station — otherwise `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards station-log creation.
20. A body `technician_id` or `reported_by_technician_id` resolves to a stored technician — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards training-completion recording and warranty-claim filing.
21. A body `distributor_id` resolves to a stored distributor, and a technician's own `distributor_id` resolves when that technician's `employer` is `DISTRIBUTOR` — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. This guards technician creation, quote creation and warranty-claim filing.
22. The acting `X-User-ID` of an entity-backed role resolves to a row of its backing entity — a `Technician` row for the `Technician` and `QualityInspector` roles, a `Distributor` row for the `Distributor` role. A technician-backed failure is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404, and it guards station-log creation and final-inspection sign-off.
23. For the `Distributor` role, the addressed resource's distributor reference — the quote's `distributor_id`, the claim's `distributor_id`, or the body `distributor_id` on a creation — equals `X-User-ID`; otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`, HTTP 403. This guards quote creation, reading one quote, adding a quote line, deleting a quote line, submitting a quote, filing a warranty claim, and reading one warranty claim.
24. A model's `certification_state` permits the requested lifecycle action: a release action requires `CERTIFIED`, a suspend action requires `RELEASED`. Otherwise `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on the model lifecycle action.
25. A dossier's `dossier_state` permits the operation: a review requires `IN_REVIEW` with the addressed dimension still `PENDING`, and a resurvey submission requires `RESURVEY_REQUIRED`. Otherwise `ERR_DOSSIER_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on review recording and resurvey submission.
26. A build order's `build_state` permits the operation: release to the floor requires `PLANNED` or `REWORK`, final inspection requires `QA_HOLD`, a station log requires the state whose next station is the submitted `station_type`, and filing a warranty claim requires `ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on release to floor, final inspection, station-log creation and warranty-claim filing.
27. The build order's model has `certification_state` of `CERTIFIED` or `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`, HTTP 409, on release to floor.
28. A technician's `cert_state` permits the requested certification action: a grant requires `UNCERTIFIED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`; a renewal requires `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`; a revocation requires `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on the certification action.
29. A distributor's `distributor_state` permits the operation: training completions require `PENDING_TRAINING`; a probation action requires `AUTHORIZED`; a reinstatement requires `PROBATION`; a termination requires `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION`; and a `TERMINATED` distributor accepts nothing at all. Otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on training-completion recording, channel status actions, quote creation and warranty-claim filing.
30. A quote's `quote_state` permits the operation: line creation and line deletion require `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, submission requires `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and a channel decision requires `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on line creation, line deletion, submission and decision.
31. A claim's `claim_state` permits the operation: adjudication requires `SUBMITTED` and a void requires `APPROVED`. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409, on adjudication and void.
32. `expected_quote_version` equals the quote's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION`, HTTP 412, on quote-line creation and quote-line deletion.
33. `expected_part_version` equals the claimed part's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION`, HTTP 412, on claim adjudication.
34. The acting `X-User-ID` is neither the dossier's `prepared_by_user_id` nor the reviewer identifier already recorded on the other dimension — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403, on review recording.
35. The acting inspector's `X-User-ID` does not appear as `technician_id` on any station log of the unit being signed off — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403, on final inspection.
36. The technician the operation depends on has `cert_state` of `ACTIVE`, has `cert_expires_on` strictly after the current logical-clock instant, and holds the scope the operation demands: the referenced station's `station_type` for a station log, `QA_SIGNOFF` for a final inspection, and the claimed part's `requires_cert_scope` for the claim's `reported_by_technician_id` whenever that scope is not `NONE`. Otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, HTTP 422, on station-log creation, final inspection and warranty-claim filing.
37. `bearing_preload_micron` sits inside the inclusive acceptance window selected by the model's `drum_alloy_grade`, and the field is supplied for a bearing-installation log and left null for every other station type — otherwise `ERR_PRELOAD_OUT_OF_SPEC`, HTTP 422, on station-log creation.
38. The claimed service part fits the built model: the model's `product_line` appears in the part's `fits_product_lines`, and the model's `capacity_rating_lb` sits between `fits_min_capacity_lb` and `fits_max_capacity_lb` inclusive. Otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_FITTED`, HTTP 422, on claim adjudication.
39. The submitted `station_type` equals the `station_type` of the referenced assembly station — otherwise `ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422, on station-log creation.
40. The built model's `product_line` appears in the station's `supported_product_lines` — otherwise `ERR_STATION_LINE_UNSUPPORTED`, HTTP 422, on station-log creation.
41. The referenced technician has `employer` equal to `DISTRIBUTOR` and a `distributor_id` equal to the distributor the operation addresses — the addressed distributor when recording a training completion, the claiming distributor when filing a claim. Otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DISTRIBUTOR_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422, on training-completion recording and warranty-claim filing.
42. Payment-module compatibility holds. On a quote line, a `payment_module` of `NONE` is always accepted, and any of `COIN`, `CARD` or `MOBILE` must appear in the quoted model's `payment_module_options`. On model creation, `payment_module_options` must be non-empty when `product_line` is `VENDED` and must be empty for every other product line. Otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_MODULE_NOT_SUPPORTED`, HTTP 422, on quote-line creation and model creation.
43. The referenced assembly station has `station_state` of `OPEN`; a station in `MAINTENANCE` accepts no station logs. Otherwise `ERR_STATION_UNAVAILABLE`, HTTP 409, on station-log creation.
44. Interval sanity holds: `started_at` is strictly before `completed_at` on a station log, and `effective_from` is not after `effective_to` on a rebate program. Otherwise `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`, HTTP 400, on station-log creation and rebate-program creation.
45. The number of existing station-log occupation windows at the same station that overlap the new occupation window — closed intervals, changeover buffer included — is strictly fewer than the station's `parallel_capacity`. Otherwise `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409, on station-log creation.
46. No existing station log of the same technician has a work interval overlapping the new one, where a work interval is half-open from `started_at` up to but not including `completed_at`. Otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, HTTP 409, on station-log creation.
47. For a given `utility_code` and `program_class`, the new validity window is separated from every existing non-retired window by at least one full clear day. Otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409, on rebate-program creation.
48. The submitted natural key is unique across its own resource type: `model_code` for a machine model, `program_code` for a rebate program, `part_number` for a service part, `station_code` for an assembly station, `employee_code` for a technician, `distributor_code` for a distributor, `serial_number` for a build order. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`, HTTP 409, on creation of a machine model, rebate program, service part, assembly station, technician, distributor or build order.
49. `capacity_rating_lb` sits inside the band its `product_line` allows, inclusive at both ends — otherwise `ERR_CAPACITY_RATING_OUT_OF_BAND`, HTTP 422, on model creation.
50. The model has no dossier in `IN_REVIEW`, `CERTIFIED` or `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_DOSSIER_ALREADY_OPEN`, HTTP 409, on dossier creation.
51. Territory exclusivity is honoured in both directions — otherwise `ERR_TERRITORY_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409, on distributor creation.
52. The composite training key formed from the technician identifier and the training module is not already present in the distributor's `completed_training_keys` — otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_DUPLICATE`, HTTP 409, on training-completion recording.
53. The quote has at least one line, that is, `line_count` is 1 or greater — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_LINES`, HTTP 422, on quote submission.
54. The acting or referenced distributor's standing permits the fiscal operation: quote submission requires `distributor_state` of `AUTHORIZED`, and a certification grant for a distributor-employed technician requires `distributor_state` of `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION`. Otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, HTTP 409, on quote submission and on the technician certification action.
55. The claim is coverable: `in_service_months` does not exceed the part's `warranty_months`, and `failure_cycle_count` does not exceed the computed `effective_rated_cycles`. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_COVERABLE`, HTTP 422, on claim adjudication.
56. The claimed part has `on_hand_qty` of at least 1 at approval time — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK`, HTTP 409, on claim adjudication.
57. A stock adjustment leaves `on_hand_qty` at zero or above once `delta_qty` is applied — otherwise `ERR_STOCK_ADJUSTMENT_NEGATIVE`, HTTP 422, on a part stock adjustment.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because every state precondition outranks every business rule, a request that is both in the wrong state and fiscally impossible reports the state error; a claim adjudication against an already-denied claim reports `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` and never `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK`. And because the optimistic-concurrency checks sit above segregation of duties, certification and fitment, a stale `expected_part_version` on an adjudication reports `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION` even when the part would also have failed the fitment test.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed through a nested path — a dossier collection under a model, a station-log collection under a build order, a line collection under a quote. Path segments are always resolved **strictly left to right**, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Addressing a line under a quote that does not exist reports `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` and never `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, even when the supplied `line_id` is itself unknown. Once the parent has resolved, a child identifier must belong to that parent: a `line_id` naming a real quote line whose `quote_id` points at a different quote is reported as `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, not as a successful cross-quote deletion.

Body-borne foreign keys are resolved only after every path segment has resolved. A station-log creation naming a nonexistent build order in the path and a nonexistent station in the body reports the build-order failure.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two independent segregation controls run in this system, and both report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

On the compliance side, a certification dossier carries the identifier of the officer who prepared it. That officer may not decide either of the dossier's two review dimensions, and once one dimension has been decided the officer who decided it may not decide the other. In effect a dossier requires three distinct operator identifiers before it can reach a certified verdict: one preparer and two reviewers.

On the shop-floor side, final inspection is a genuine second pair of eyes. The inspector's `X-User-ID` — which is a `Technician` row identifier, as §2.1 explains — must not appear as `technician_id` on any station log belonging to the unit under inspection, whether that log recorded a pass or a failure, and whether it was written in the current rework round or an earlier one. Station logs are never deleted, so a technician who worked a unit before a rework cycle remains permanently barred from signing that unit off.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline: Integrity Holds, Yield Breaches and Fulfilment Blocks

The most consequential behaviour in this system is that several single operations mutate many rows. Three named cascades exist, all executed synchronously inside the triggering request, and all completed before the response is returned. Where a cascade iterates over a set of rows, the iteration order is `created_at` ascending on the entity being iterated, so that the outcome is deterministic.

The **certification-revocation cascade** begins when a technician's certification is revoked and reaches build orders, warranty claims and distributor counters. The **certification-regrant cascade** is its partial inverse and fires when a revoked technician is granted certification again. The **yield-breach cascade** begins when a model's first-pass yield falls below its declared floor and reaches the model's certified dossier, the model itself, and every unit of that model still in production. Each is specified in full in §3, at the workflow that triggers it.

Two invariants hold across all three. First, **terminal states are never re-opened**: a unit that has reached `ACCEPTED` or `SCRAPPED` never returns to an in-production state, so a cascade that would otherwise move a unit to `QUALITY_HOLD` instead only records the integrity flag on an accepted unit, and leaves a scrapped unit entirely untouched. Second, **a block is a consequence of a change, not of a condition**: a warranty claim moves to `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` because a part's stock position or a unit's integrity standing *changed*, never because a newly filed claim happens to find an unfavourable condition already in place.

### 2.12. Natural Keys and Code Formats

Seven resources carry a human-facing natural key in addition to their UUID `id`: a machine model's `model_code`, a rebate program's `program_code`, a service part's `part_number`, an assembly station's `station_code`, a technician's `employee_code`, a distributor's `distributor_code`, and a build order's `serial_number`. Each of these is between 1 and 32 characters long and may contain only uppercase letters A through Z, digits 0 through 9, and the hyphen character. Each must be unique within its own resource type; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`.

A distributor's `territory_code` follows the same character rules but is capped at 16 characters, and it is deliberately not unique — several distributors may share a territory, subject to the exclusivity rule in §3.8. Free-text names are looser: a technician's `full_name` runs from 1 to 120 characters, a distributor's `legal_name` from 1 to 160 characters, a rebate program's `utility_code` from 1 to 32 characters, a quote's `customer_name` from 1 to 160 characters and its `laundromat_site_code` from 1 to 32 characters, and a claim's `diagnostics_code` from 1 to 32 characters.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Product Catalogue: Machine Models and Service Parts

A `ProductEngineer` opens the catalogue by registering a machine model. The registration payload names the catalogue code as `model_code`, the `product_line` the machine belongs to — one of `ON_PREMISES`, `VENDED` or `INDUSTRIAL_TUNNEL` — and the rated dry-load capacity in pounds as `capacity_rating_lb`. Nameplate efficiency figures are supplied as `rated_water_gal_per_cwt`, the water consumption in gallons per hundred pounds of dry load, and `rated_kwh_per_cwt`, the energy consumption in kilowatt-hours per the same hundred pounds; both must be greater than zero and both must carry exactly two decimal places. The drum shell alloy is declared as `drum_alloy_grade`, taking one of `AISI_304`, `AISI_316` or `DUPLEX_2205`, and this choice matters far downstream: it selects the bearing preload acceptance window applied when the unit reaches bearing installation. The book price per unit is `list_price_cents`, a positive integer count of cents.

Capacity is not a free number. Each product line has a band, and both ends of each band are inclusive: an `ON_PREMISES` machine must be rated between 20 and 200 pounds, a `VENDED` machine between 20 and 80 pounds, and an `INDUSTRIAL_TUNNEL` system between 150 and 600 pounds. A registration whose `capacity_rating_lb` falls outside the band its own `product_line` allows is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_RATING_OUT_OF_BAND`. The bands overlap deliberately — a 60-pound machine is a legitimate on-premises unit and a legitimate vended unit — so the check is always relative to the declared line, never absolute.

Payment hardware follows the same line-relative logic. The `payment_module_options` array lists the payment modules orderable on the model, drawn without duplicates from `COIN`, `CARD` and `MOBILE`, and it defaults to an empty array. A `VENDED` model must offer at least one module, because a laundromat machine that cannot take payment is not a product. Every other product line must offer none at all: an on-premises or tunnel machine is metered by the facility, not by the customer, so its `payment_module_options` must be an empty array. Either violation — an empty options list on a vended model, or a non-empty list on any other line — is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_MODULE_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

Two optional quality governors may be supplied at registration and both have defaults. `max_rework_cycles` is the ceiling on how many times a unit of this model may be pushed back for rework before the factory gives up and scraps it; it accepts an integer from 0 through 3 and defaults to 2 when omitted. `min_first_pass_yield_pct` is the first-pass-yield floor below which the model's certified efficiency dossier is forced back to resurvey; it accepts a value from `0.00` through `100.00` with exactly two decimal places and defaults to `80.00` when omitted. A model registered without either field therefore tolerates two rework rounds per unit and expects four finished units in five to clear inspection on the first attempt.

A newly registered model is not yet sellable or buildable. Its `certification_state` begins at `DRAFT` and its `suspended_reason` at `NONE`. Its production counters — `accepted_unit_count`, `scrapped_unit_count` and `quality_hold_unit_count` — all begin at 0, its `first_pass_yield_pct` begins at `0.00`, and its rebate snapshot begins empty, with `rebate_program_codes` an empty array and `rebate_amount_cents` at 0. A duplicate `model_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`. The catalogue may be browsed newest-first and narrowed by the `product_line` and `certification_state` filters.

Service parts are the other half of the catalogue and are also owned by the `ProductEngineer`. A part is created with its `part_number`, its `component_class` — one of `DRUM`, `BEARING`, `CONTROL_BOARD`, `PAYMENT_MODULE`, `MOTOR` or `DIAGNOSTIC_TOOL`, and the axis on which warranty exposure is later rolled up — and a fitment envelope. That envelope has two parts: `fits_product_lines`, a non-empty array without duplicates drawn from the three product lines the part is released for, and a capacity range given as `fits_min_capacity_lb` and `fits_max_capacity_lb`, both inclusive bounds and both zero or greater. A part whose `fits_max_capacity_lb` is below its `fits_min_capacity_lb` is a malformed envelope and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The warranty terms of a part are declared at creation and never renegotiated per claim. `rated_duty_cycles` is the design life in wash cycles and must be greater than zero; it is the denominator of the pro-rata settlement. `warranty_months` is the term measured in whole months from the in-service date and must be no shorter than twelve months and no longer than sixty months — one to five years. `unit_price_cents` is the distributor price per unit in cents, must be positive, and doubles as the absolute ceiling on any covered warranty amount: no settlement ever pays more than one unit of the part. `requires_cert_scope` names the certification scope that a field technician must hold in order to report a claim naming this part, taking one of `NONE`, `DIAGNOSTICS_TOOL` or `CONTROL_INTEGRATION` and defaulting to `NONE`.

Parts supersede one another over a product's life, and the catalogue records that chain directly. The optional `supersedes_part_id` names the part this one replaces; it is null for an original part, it may never name the row's own identifier, and a value that does not resolve to a stored part is rejected with `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`. Because the reference is self-referential, the supersession chain can be walked in either direction: the parts collection accepts a `supersedes_part_id` filter that returns every part declaring a given predecessor, alongside `component_class` and `stock_state` filters.

Inventory begins empty. A freshly created part has `on_hand_qty` at 0 and `reserved_qty` at 0, and its derived `stock_state` therefore begins at `BACKORDERED`. The `reorder_point_qty` field, defaulting to 2 and never negative, sets the inclusive threshold at or below which a non-zero on-hand position is reported as `LOW` rather than `IN_STOCK`. Neither `on_hand_qty` nor `reserved_qty` is ever set directly by a client; §3.9 explains the only three movements that change them. A duplicate `part_number` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`.

### 3.2. Utility Rebate Programs and Their Non-Colliding Windows

Utilities pay commercial customers to install efficient equipment, and a manufacturer that can hand a laundromat operator a pre-qualified rebate packet wins deals. A `ComplianceOfficer` maintains that catalogue of programs. Each program is registered with its `program_code`, the sponsoring utility as `utility_code`, and a `program_class` declaring which consumption dimension the utility actually pays on: `WATER`, `ENERGY` or `COMBINED`. The `eligible_product_lines` array is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of the three product lines the program accepts.

A program's qualification thresholds are all inclusive, and this is a deliberate design decision rather than an accident of implementation. `max_water_gal_per_cwt` is the ceiling that a dossier's measured water consumption is compared against, `max_kwh_per_cwt` is the ceiling for measured energy consumption, and both must be greater than zero with exactly two decimal places. `min_capacity_rating_lb` is the floor that a model's `capacity_rating_lb` is compared against and must be zero or greater. A machine measuring exactly the published water ceiling still qualifies; a machine rated exactly at the published capacity floor still qualifies.

The payable value is built from three integer fields. `rebate_base_cents` is the base value in cents before any bonus and must be positive. `capacity_bonus_cents_per_10lb` adds cents for each whole ten pounds of capacity above the program's own floor, defaults to 0, and is never negative. `rebate_cap_cents` is the inclusive ceiling on the computed value and must be at least as large as `rebate_base_cents`, so a program can never be configured to cap below its own base.

Validity is a date window. `effective_from` is the first instant of the window and `effective_to` is its last instant, inclusive; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps, and only the UTC **date** component of each participates in window arithmetic. A program whose `effective_to` date falls before its `effective_from` date is rejected with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. A new program begins with `program_state` at `ACTIVE`.

Utilities do not run two overlapping incentives on the same consumption dimension, and neither does this catalogue. For a given pairing of `utility_code` and `program_class`, a new window must be separated from the window of every existing program that is not yet retired by **at least one full clear day**. Concretely, a conflict is declared whenever the new window's start date falls on or before one day after an existing window's end date, and at the same time that existing window's start date falls on or before one day after the new window's end date. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The practical effect is that back-to-back windows are illegal: a program ending on the last day of June and another beginning on the first day of July collide, because there is no clear day between them, and the second must begin on the second of July or later. Programs of the same utility on a different `program_class`, and programs already in `RETIRED`, are ignored by the check entirely.

Retirement is driven by the logical clock rather than by any operator action. When the clock advances past a program's `effective_to` date — strictly past, so a clock landing on the final day of the window leaves the program `ACTIVE` — the program's `program_state` becomes `RETIRED` and its `version` advances by 1. A retired program qualifies nothing afterwards, because qualification requires an `ACTIVE` program. Crucially, retirement is **not retroactive**: `rebate_program_codes` and `rebate_amount_cents` already stored on a machine model are audit snapshots of the certification instant and are never rewritten by a later retirement. A model certified in August under a program that retires in December keeps its August rebate figures forever, or until it is recertified. The program catalogue may be narrowed by the `utility_code` and `program_state` filters and is returned ordered by `effective_from` then `program_code`.

### 3.3. Efficiency Certification, Model Release and Recertification

No unit of a machine model may be built until the model has passed efficiency and water-consumption certification, and certification is a two-reviewer affair by design. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a **certification dossier** against a model, supplying the results of the efficiency test run: `measured_water_gal_per_cwt` and `measured_kwh_per_cwt`, both greater than zero with exactly two decimal places, and `test_cycle_count`, the number of wash cycles the run covered. A test run of fewer than fifty cycles is not statistically usable and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; fifty cycles is acceptable. The dossier's `model_id` is taken from the path the dossier was created under and is never read from the body, so a dossier can never be misfiled against a different model.

The opening officer's `X-User-ID` is retained as `prepared_by_user_id`, and that stamp is what disqualifies them from reviewing their own work. A model may have only one dossier in play at a time: opening a dossier while the model already has one in `IN_REVIEW`, `CERTIFIED` or `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` is rejected with `ERR_DOSSIER_ALREADY_OPEN`. A dossier that ended in `REJECTED` does not block a fresh attempt.

A new dossier begins with `dossier_state` at `IN_REVIEW`, with both review dimensions at `PENDING` — `energy_review_state` and `water_review_state` — with both reviewer stamps null, namely `energy_reviewer_user_id` and `water_reviewer_user_id`, with `resurvey_reason` at `NONE`, `resurvey_count` at 0, `certified_at` null, `qualified_program_codes` an empty array and `rebate_amount_cents` at 0.

**The two review dimensions are independent state axes carried on a single row**, and understanding that is the key to the whole workflow. A review decision addresses exactly one dimension, named by the `dimension` field as either `ENERGY` or `WATER`, and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `note`. Approving one dimension while the other is still `PENDING` sets that dimension's review state to `APPROVED` and stamps the acting `X-User-ID` into the matching reviewer field, but leaves `dossier_state` exactly where it was, at `IN_REVIEW` — a single approval never certifies anything. Only the second approval, the one that makes both dimensions `APPROVED`, advances the dossier from `IN_REVIEW` to `CERTIFIED`.

Every review decision, on either dimension and in either direction, must satisfy the same four conditions, and §2.8 fixes their relative rank. The dossier must be in `IN_REVIEW`, and the dimension being addressed must still be `PENDING`; either failure is `ERR_DOSSIER_STATE_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, and it must differ from the reviewer identifier already recorded on the other dimension whenever that identifier is set; either failure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A rejection is where the two dimensions stop being symmetric in effect but not in specification: a `REJECT` on either dimension sets that dimension's review state to `REJECTED`, stamps the deciding `X-User-ID` into the matching reviewer field, leaves the other dimension at whatever state it had already reached, empties `qualified_program_codes` and resets `rebate_amount_cents` to 0, and moves `dossier_state` from `IN_REVIEW` to `REJECTED`. `REJECTED` is terminal for a dossier; there is no appeal path, and the parent model is untouched and remains in `certification_state` `DRAFT`. Each of the three review outcomes — the intermediate `IN_REVIEW` to `IN_REVIEW` approval, the certifying `IN_REVIEW` to `CERTIFIED` approval, and the `IN_REVIEW` to `REJECTED` rejection — advances the dossier's `version` by 1.

The certifying approval does three things beyond the state change. It stamps `certified_at` with the current logical-clock instant. It runs the rebate qualification, writing `qualified_program_codes` and `rebate_amount_cents`. And it cascades into the parent model, which is the first hop of the certification cascade.

**Rebate qualification** works as follows. A program qualifies the model when all six of these conditions hold together: the program's `program_state` is `ACTIVE`; the model's `product_line` appears in the program's `eligible_product_lines`; the dossier's `measured_water_gal_per_cwt` is at most the program's `max_water_gal_per_cwt`; the dossier's `measured_kwh_per_cwt` is at most the program's `max_kwh_per_cwt`; the model's `capacity_rating_lb` is at least the program's `min_capacity_rating_lb`; and the date component of `certified_at` falls within the program's window, at or after the `effective_from` date and at or before the `effective_to` date. Every threshold and both window ends are inclusive.

For each qualifying program, the payable value is computed in three steps. First, the number of whole bonus steps is the model's `capacity_rating_lb` minus the program's `min_capacity_rating_lb`, divided by ten and truncated downward to a whole number, so a model exactly nine pounds above the floor earns no bonus step at all. Second, the uncapped value is the program's `rebate_base_cents` plus its `capacity_bonus_cents_per_10lb` multiplied by that number of bonus steps. Third, the payable value is the lesser of the uncapped value and the program's `rebate_cap_cents` — the cap is applied as a minimum against the ceiling, not as a rounding step, and no rounding occurs anywhere in this calculation because every operand is already an integer count of cents.

Selecting the winner uses a three-key ranking, and here lies the subtlety worth stating twice: **the ranking is performed on the uncapped value while the amount stored is the capped one.** Qualifying programs are ranked by uncapped value descending; ties are broken by `effective_from` ascending, and any remaining tie by `program_code` ascending. `rebate_amount_cents` on the dossier is the *payable* value of the top-ranked program, so a program can win the ranking on a large uncapped figure and still pay less than a runner-up whose cap was more generous. Separately, `qualified_program_codes` lists the `program_code` of **every** qualifying program, not merely the winner, sorted by code ascending. When no program qualifies, `qualified_program_codes` is an empty array and `rebate_amount_cents` is 0.

The **certification cascade** then reaches the model. A model in `certification_state` `DRAFT` whose dossier just reached `CERTIFIED` moves from `DRAFT` to `CERTIFIED`, copying the dossier's already-sorted `qualified_program_codes` into the model's `rebate_program_codes`, copying the dossier's `rebate_amount_cents` into the model's `rebate_amount_cents`, resetting `suspended_reason` to `NONE`, advancing the model's `version` by 1 and refreshing its `updated_at`. A model in `SUSPENDED` whose dossier re-enters `CERTIFIED` after a resurvey moves from `SUSPENDED` to `CERTIFIED` with the same effects: `suspended_reason` back to `NONE`, and both rebate fields recomputed from the dossier. In both cases the dossier's `model_id` must equal the model being cascaded into.

Certification makes a model buildable but not yet released. A `ProductEngineer` applies a lifecycle action carrying an `action` of either `RELEASE` or `SUSPEND` and an optional `reason`. A release moves the model from `CERTIFIED` to `RELEASED` and advances its `version` by 1; attempting it from any other state is `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`. A manual suspension moves the model from `RELEASED` to `SUSPENDED`, sets `suspended_reason` to `MANUAL`, empties `rebate_program_codes` and resets `rebate_amount_cents` to 0, and advances the model's `version` by 1; attempting it from any state other than `RELEASED` is likewise `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`. Note the asymmetry the state machine imposes: a manual suspension is only available on a released model, whereas the automatic suspension described in §3.6 can also strike a model that was certified but never released.

Recovery from suspension runs through the dossier, not through the lifecycle action. A dossier in `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` accepts a resurvey submission from a `ComplianceOfficer`, carrying fresh `measured_water_gal_per_cwt`, `measured_kwh_per_cwt` and `test_cycle_count` values under the same constraints as the original submission. The submission moves the dossier from `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` back to `IN_REVIEW`, replaces the three measured values with the submitted ones, resets both `energy_review_state` and `water_review_state` to `PENDING` and both reviewer identifiers to null, resets `resurvey_reason` to `NONE`, and advances the dossier's `version` by 1. It also **replaces `prepared_by_user_id` with the submitting officer's `X-User-ID`**, which re-arms segregation of duties for the new round: whoever resubmits the measurements is barred from reviewing them, even if a different officer prepared the original dossier. A resurvey submission against a dossier in any other state is `ERR_DOSSIER_STATE_INVALID`. Because a resurveyed model returns only as far as `CERTIFIED`, a model suspended for a yield breach must be explicitly released again before it reaches `RELEASED`. Dossiers may be read individually, or listed for one model with an optional `dossier_state` filter.

### 3.4. Technician Certification, Lapse and the Revocation Cascade

Every hand that touches a machine is a certified hand, and certification is the single most load-bearing gate in the factory. A `TrainingAdministrator` registers a technician with an `employee_code` badge, a `full_name` for display, and an `employer` of either `FACTORY` or `DISTRIBUTOR`. Factory technicians staff the assembly stations; distributor technicians run field diagnostics and report warranty claims. The `distributor_id` field is required when `employer` is `DISTRIBUTOR` and must be null when `employer` is `FACTORY`; a distributor-employed technician whose `distributor_id` does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, and a duplicate `employee_code` with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`. A new technician begins with `cert_state` at `UNCERTIFIED`, `cert_scopes` an empty array, `cert_expires_on` null, `last_cert_action_at` null and `revocation_count` at 0.

Certification is administered through a single action endpoint carrying an `action` of `GRANT`, `RENEW` or `REVOKE`. A grant and a renewal both require the `scopes` array and the `expires_on` instant; a revocation requires a `reason`. Omitting a conditionally required field is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `scopes` array is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of five scopes: `DRUM_FAB`, `BEARING_INSTALL`, `CONTROL_INTEGRATION`, `QA_SIGNOFF` and `DIAGNOSTICS_TOOL`. An empty or undeclared scope set is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is an `expires_on` that is not strictly after the current logical-clock instant — the system refuses to issue a certification that is already stale.

A grant is legal from `UNCERTIFIED`, from `EXPIRED` and from `REVOKED`; a renewal is legal from `ACTIVE` and from `EXPIRED`; a revocation is legal only from `ACTIVE`. Any other combination is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_STATE_INVALID`. A grant on a distributor-employed technician additionally requires that the employing distributor's `distributor_state` be either `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION`; a distributor still in `PENDING_TRAINING`, or one already `TERMINATED`, cannot have its people certified, and the attempt is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. All four successful paths — the grant from `UNCERTIFIED` to `ACTIVE`, the renewal that keeps the technician `ACTIVE` while replacing their credentials, the re-grant or renewal from `EXPIRED` back to `ACTIVE`, and the re-grant from `REVOKED` back to `ACTIVE` — replace `cert_scopes` with the submitted `scopes`, replace `cert_expires_on` with the submitted `expires_on`, stamp `last_cert_action_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and advance the technician's `version` by 1.

Lapse is quiet. When the logical clock reaches or passes a technician's `cert_expires_on` while their `cert_state` is `ACTIVE`, the sweep described in §2.5 moves them from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` and empties `cert_scopes` to an empty array, advancing their `version` by 1. Nothing else happens. No build order changes state, no `integrity_hold` is raised anywhere, and no warranty claim is blocked — an expired certification means the technician cannot start new work, not that their past work is suspect.

Revocation is loud, because revocation *does* mean the past work is suspect. A revocation moves the technician from `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED` and runs a four-hop cascade inside the same request. The first hop touches the technician: `cert_scopes` is emptied, `revocation_count` increases by 1, `last_cert_action_at` is stamped with the current instant, and the technician's `version` advances by 1. The second hop finds every build order carrying at least one station log written by this technician and, walking them in `created_at` ascending order, puts each one under an integrity hold: a unit in `ON_FLOOR`, `DRUM_DONE`, `BEARINGS_DONE` or `QA_HOLD` moves to `QUALITY_HOLD` with `integrity_hold` set to true; a unit already in `ACCEPTED` keeps its terminal state and takes only `integrity_hold` set to true, because a shipped unit is never re-opened; a unit in `SCRAPPED` is left entirely untouched. Each affected unit's `version` advances by 1, and the owning model's `quality_hold_unit_count` is recomputed from the units now sitting in `QUALITY_HOLD`. The third hop finds every warranty claim in `SUBMITTED` filed against any of those units and, walking them in `created_at` ascending order, moves each to `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` with `block_reason` set to `BUILD_INTEGRITY_HOLD` and its `version` advanced by 1. The fourth hop recomputes `open_claim_count` on every distributor that owned one of those claims.

Re-granting certification to a revoked technician partially unwinds all of that, in three hops. First, every build order carrying `integrity_hold` set to true whose station-log technicians are now **all** back in `cert_state` `ACTIVE` has `integrity_hold` cleared to false — a unit touched by two revoked technicians stays held until both are restored. Among those released units, any that is sitting in `QUALITY_HOLD` moves from `QUALITY_HOLD` to `REWORK` with `rework_count` increased by 1; if that increase pushes `rework_count` above the model's `max_rework_cycles`, the unit is scrapped immediately by the rule in §3.6. The owning model's `quality_hold_unit_count` is recomputed and each touched unit's `version` advances by 1. Second, every claim sitting in `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `BUILD_INTEGRITY_HOLD` against one of those units returns to `SUBMITTED` with `block_reason` reset to `NONE` — but only when the claimed part's `stock_state` is not `BACKORDERED`, since a part with nothing on the shelf is still an obstacle. Third, `open_claim_count` is recomputed on the affected distributors.

Technician records may be read individually or listed by `employee_code`, narrowed with the `distributor_id`, `cert_state` and `employer` filters — the `distributor_id` filter is how the channel finds every technician a given distributor employs.

### 3.5. The Assembly Floor: Stations, Occupation Windows and Build Progression

A `PlantManager` tools the floor before anything is built. Each assembly station is created with a `station_code`, a `station_type` naming the operation it performs — `DRUM_FAB`, `BEARING_INSTALL` or `CONTROL_INTEGRATION` — and two capacity parameters that together define how the station behaves under load. `parallel_capacity`, an integer from 1 through 4, is how many overlapping occupations the station tolerates at one instant. `changeover_buffer_minutes`, an integer from 0 through 120 — nothing longer than two hours — is the machine-changeover time appended to every operation's finish instant to form the station's true occupation window. The `supported_product_lines` array is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of the three product lines the station is tooled for. The optional `station_state` accepts `OPEN` or `MAINTENANCE` and defaults to `OPEN`; an `OPEN` station accepts work, a station in `MAINTENANCE` accepts none. Stations are listed by `station_code` and may be narrowed by the `station_type` and `station_state` filters. A duplicate `station_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`.

A serialized unit enters the system as a build order, opened by the `PlantManager` against a `model_id` with a unique `serial_number` and a `planned_start_at` instant. That planned instant is informational scheduling metadata only — it never participates in any overlap or contention arithmetic, which is driven exclusively by the actual work intervals recorded on station logs. A new unit begins with `build_state` at `PLANNED`, `rework_count` at 0, `integrity_hold` false, `labor_minutes_total` at 0, `station_pass_count` at 0, `scrap_reason` at `NONE`, `qa_inspector_user_id` null and `accepted_at` null. A duplicate `serial_number` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`. Build orders are listed oldest-first and may be narrowed by the `model_id`, `build_state` and `integrity_hold` filters.

Releasing a unit to the floor moves it from `PLANNED` to `ON_FLOOR`, or from `REWORK` back to `ON_FLOOR`, and advances its `version` by 1. Two conditions gate it. The unit must be in `PLANNED` or `REWORK`; any other state, including a unit sitting under an integrity hold in `QUALITY_HOLD`, is `ERR_BUILD_STATE_INVALID` — a held unit is released by clearing the hold, never by re-releasing it to the floor. And the unit's model must have `certification_state` of `CERTIFIED` or `RELEASED`; a model still in `DRAFT`, or one that has fallen to `SUSPENDED`, blocks the release with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`. When a unit returns from `REWORK`, the station chain restarts from the beginning at drum fabrication, and the station logs already written for that unit are retained: they are never deleted, they continue to contribute to `labor_minutes_total`, and they continue to bar their authors from signing the unit off.

Station work is recorded by the acting `Technician` as a station log against a unit. The submission names the `station_id` of the station occupied, the `station_type` of the operation claimed, the work interval as `started_at` and `completed_at`, and an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL`. The log's `build_order_id` is taken from the path, and its `technician_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID` rather than from the body, so a technician can never file work under someone else's badge. Two conditional fields complete the payload. `defect_code` — one of `NONE`, `DRUM_WELD`, `BEARING_PRELOAD`, `CONTROL_FIRMWARE` or `PAYMENT_MODULE`, defaulting to `NONE` — must be `NONE` when the `outcome` is `PASS` and must be something other than `NONE` when the `outcome` is `FAIL`; either violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `bearing_preload_micron` is required when the `station_type` is `BEARING_INSTALL` and must be null for every other station type, and it carries exactly two decimal places.

The chain is strictly sequential, and the state the unit is in dictates which operation may be logged next. A unit in `ON_FLOOR` accepts a `DRUM_FAB` log, and a passing one advances `ON_FLOOR` to `DRUM_DONE`. A unit in `DRUM_DONE` accepts a `BEARING_INSTALL` log, and a passing one advances `DRUM_DONE` to `BEARINGS_DONE`. A unit in `BEARINGS_DONE` accepts a `CONTROL_INTEGRATION` log, and a passing one advances `BEARINGS_DONE` to `QA_HOLD`, where the unit waits for final inspection. Submitting a log whose `station_type` is not the operation the current `build_state` expects is `ERR_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`, and so is submitting any log at all against a unit in `PLANNED`, `REWORK`, `QUALITY_HOLD`, `QA_HOLD`, `ACCEPTED` or `SCRAPPED`.

A failing log short-circuits the chain from wherever it occurs. A `FAIL` logged while the unit is in `ON_FLOOR`, `DRUM_DONE` or `BEARINGS_DONE` writes the log, moves the unit to `REWORK`, increases `rework_count` by 1, and advances the unit's `version` by 1 — and if that increase pushes `rework_count` above the model's `max_rework_cycles`, the unit is scrapped immediately by the rule set out in §3.6. Every station log, passing or failing, is created as a new resource, and every one of them advances the parent unit's `version` by 1 and triggers recomputation of `labor_minutes_total` and `station_pass_count`, where `station_pass_count` counts the logs of that unit whose `outcome` is `PASS`.

Bearing installation carries an acceptance window keyed on the model's drum alloy, because preload tolerance is a property of the shell material. For a drum of `AISI_304`, the measured `bearing_preload_micron` must fall between `18.00` and `32.00` inclusive. For `AISI_316`, between `20.00` and `34.00` inclusive. For `DUPLEX_2205`, the stiffest of the three, between `24.00` and `40.00` inclusive. A measurement outside the window selected by the model's `drum_alloy_grade`, or a bearing-installation log that omits the measurement entirely, or a non-bearing log that supplies one, is rejected with `ERR_PRELOAD_OUT_OF_SPEC`.

Three referential checks guard the station itself. The submitted `station_type` must equal the `station_type` recorded on the referenced station, or the log is rejected with `ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH` — a technician cannot log a bearing operation at a drum-fabrication cell. The `product_line` of the unit's model must appear in the station's `supported_product_lines`, or the log is rejected with `ERR_STATION_LINE_UNSUPPORTED`; a station tooled only for vended machines cannot build a tunnel system. And the station's `station_state` must be `OPEN`; a station in `MAINTENANCE` rejects the log with `ERR_STATION_UNAVAILABLE`.

Certification is checked on the acting technician, not merely on the badge. The technician must have `cert_state` of `ACTIVE`, must have a `cert_expires_on` strictly after the current logical-clock instant, and must hold the scope matching the referenced station's `station_type` in their `cert_scopes` — a drum-fabrication log demands the `DRUM_FAB` scope, a bearing log the `BEARING_INSTALL` scope, a control log the `CONTROL_INTEGRATION` scope. Any of those failures is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

**Occupation windows** are the heart of floor contention, and the asymmetry between the two windows a single log produces is deliberate. The work interval must be non-degenerate: `started_at` strictly before `completed_at`, or the log is rejected with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. From that interval the system derives three stored values. `occupation_from` equals `started_at`. `occupation_to` equals `completed_at` advanced by the station's `changeover_buffer_minutes`. And `labor_minutes` is the elapsed time from `started_at` to `completed_at` expressed in whole minutes with any partial minute truncated downward — the changeover buffer is **not** billable labour and never appears in `labor_minutes`. The unit's `labor_minutes_total` is the sum of `labor_minutes` across every station log belonging to that unit, including logs written in earlier rework rounds.

Station contention is evaluated over occupation windows treated as **closed** intervals at both ends. Two windows overlap when the first one's `occupation_from` is at or before the second one's `occupation_to` and the second one's `occupation_from` is at or before the first one's `occupation_to`. The system counts how many existing station logs at the same station have a window overlapping the new one; when that count reaches or exceeds the station's `parallel_capacity`, the log is rejected with `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`. Because both ends are closed, a new operation beginning at exactly the instant an existing occupation window ends still conflicts — with a thirty-minute changeover buffer on a one-hour operation ending at nine o'clock, the window runs to half past nine and a new operation at half past nine is refused, while one starting a minute later is accepted.

Technician contention is evaluated over the **work** intervals rather than the occupation windows, and those intervals are **half-open**: they include `started_at` and exclude `completed_at`. Two work intervals overlap when the first one's `started_at` falls strictly before the second one's `completed_at` and the second one's `started_at` falls strictly before the first one's `completed_at`. If any existing log of the same technician overlaps the new one under that rule, the log is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. The consequence of the half-open rule is that a technician may legitimately begin a new operation at the exact instant a previous one finished — a person walks between cells, whereas a machine still needs its changeover. The changeover buffer extends the station window only; it never extends the technician's window.

Station logs may be read individually or listed by `started_at` and then `created_at`, and the collection accepts `build_order_id`, `station_id`, `technician_id` and `outcome` filters — the three identifier filters are how a supervisor reconstructs a unit's history, a station's utilization, or one technician's day.

### 3.6. Final Inspection, Rework Ceilings and the Yield-Breach Cascade

A unit waiting in `QA_HOLD` is inspected by a `QualityInspector`, who submits a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with an optional `note`; a rejection additionally requires a `defect_code` other than `NONE`, and omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three conditions gate the decision, in the precedence §2.8 fixes. The unit must be in `QA_HOLD`, or the request is `ERR_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. The acting inspector must hold the `QA_SIGNOFF` scope with `cert_state` of `ACTIVE` and a `cert_expires_on` strictly after the current logical-clock instant, or the request is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And the inspector's own `X-User-ID` must not appear as `technician_id` on any station log of this unit, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — nobody inspects their own welds.

Approval is the happy path and it is where the factory's quality metric moves. The unit advances from `QA_HOLD` to `ACCEPTED`, `accepted_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, `qa_inspector_user_id` records the deciding inspector's identifier, and the unit's `version` advances by 1. On the model, `accepted_unit_count` increases by 1 and `first_pass_yield_pct` is recomputed. If the recomputed yield has fallen below the model's `min_first_pass_yield_pct`, the yield-breach cascade fires from its fourth hop — an accepted unit can trigger a suspension, because accepting a unit that needed rework drags the model's first-pass record down.

Rejection sends the unit back for rework. The unit's `rework_count` increases by 1, `qa_inspector_user_id` records the deciding inspector, the unit's `version` advances by 1, and the unit moves from `QA_HOLD` to `REWORK` — from which the `PlantManager` may release it to the floor again, restarting the station chain at drum fabrication. This is the closing edge of the production loop that runs `QA_HOLD` to `REWORK` to `ON_FLOOR` and back around through `DRUM_DONE` and `BEARINGS_DONE` to `QA_HOLD`, and the loop is bounded rather than infinite.

The bound is the model's `max_rework_cycles`. Whenever `rework_count` is increased — by a failing station log, by a rejecting inspection, or by the release of an integrity hold described in §3.4 — the new value is compared against the model's `max_rework_cycles`, and if it is **strictly greater**, the unit immediately moves from `REWORK` to `SCRAPPED` within the same request. A model configured with the default ceiling of 2 therefore tolerates a second rework round and scraps on the third. A scrapped unit records `scrap_reason` as `REWORK_LIMIT`, increases the model's `scrapped_unit_count` by 1, triggers recomputation of the model's `first_pass_yield_pct`, and advances its own `version` by 1. `SCRAPPED` and `ACCEPTED` are the two terminal states of a unit; neither is ever left.

**First-pass yield** is the model's headline quality figure and the trigger metric for the whole cascade. It is computed over the model's finished units only. The denominator, the terminal unit count, is the number of that model's build orders whose `build_state` is either `ACCEPTED` or `SCRAPPED`. The numerator, the first-pass unit count, is the number of that model's build orders whose `build_state` is `ACCEPTED` **and** whose `rework_count` is exactly 0. When the terminal unit count is zero, `first_pass_yield_pct` is `0.00`; otherwise it is the first-pass unit count multiplied by one hundred, divided by the terminal unit count, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Units still on the floor, in rework, or sitting on quality hold are excluded from both numerator and denominator, and an accepted unit that needed one or more rework rounds counts in the denominator but not in the numerator.

A **breach** requires two things at once: at least one terminal unit must exist, and `first_pass_yield_pct` must be **strictly less than** the model's `min_first_pass_yield_pct`. The strictness matters at the boundary — four accepted first-pass units and one scrapped unit give a yield of exactly `80.00`, which does not breach a floor of `80.00`. The terminal-unit condition matters at the start of a model's life: a brand-new model shows `0.00` yield, which is well below any floor, but it does not breach, because it has finished nothing yet. Conversely, a model whose very first finished unit is a scrapped one shows a yield of `0.00` against one terminal unit, and that *does* breach.

The **yield-breach cascade** runs synchronously from the request that caused the breach, and its six hops are numbered from the operation that started them. Hops one and two live in the rejection path already described: the `rework_count` increase, and the immediate scrap when the ceiling is exceeded. Hop three is the model bookkeeping — `scrapped_unit_count` or `accepted_unit_count` increased and `first_pass_yield_pct` recomputed. Hop four reaches the compliance record: the model's dossier sitting in `CERTIFIED` moves from `CERTIFIED` to `RESURVEY_REQUIRED`, with `resurvey_reason` set to `YIELD_BREACH`, `resurvey_count` increased by 1, `qualified_program_codes` emptied, `rebate_amount_cents` reset to 0, and the dossier's `version` advanced by 1. Both `energy_review_state` and `water_review_state` are deliberately **left at `APPROVED`** rather than reset, because the original review verdicts remain part of the audit trail; only the resurvey submission described in §3.3 resets them to `PENDING`.

Hop five reaches the model itself. A model in `RELEASED` moves from `RELEASED` to `SUSPENDED`, and a model that was certified but never released moves from `CERTIFIED` to `SUSPENDED`; both paths set `suspended_reason` to `YIELD_RESURVEY`, empty `rebate_program_codes`, reset the model's `rebate_amount_cents` to 0, and advance the model's `version` by 1. The two paths are identical in every effect, including hop six, and differ only in the state they start from. Hop six reaches production in flight: every build order of that model whose `build_state` is `ON_FLOOR`, `DRUM_DONE`, `BEARINGS_DONE` or `QA_HOLD` moves to `QUALITY_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each advancing its own `version` by 1, and the model's `quality_hold_unit_count` is recomputed once hop six has finished. Units already in `ACCEPTED` or `SCRAPPED` are not touched, because terminal states are never re-opened.

One distinction between the two cascades that reach `QUALITY_HOLD` must be kept sharp. The yield-breach cascade parks units on quality hold **without** raising `integrity_hold` — the units are suspect because the model's certification is suspect, not because the labour on them is suspect — and it therefore does **not** block any warranty claim. Only the certification-revocation cascade of §3.4 sets `integrity_hold` to true, and only that flag drives claims into `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `BUILD_INTEGRITY_HOLD`.

### 3.7. The Distributor Channel: Installation Training, Territory and Standing

Commercial laundry equipment is sold and installed through a distributor network, and a distributor that installs a tunnel washer incorrectly costs the manufacturer far more than the margin on the sale. Channel admission is therefore gated on training. A `ChannelManager` creates a distributor with a `distributor_code`, a `legal_name`, an assigned `territory_code` and a boolean `territory_exclusive` flag that defaults to false. A new distributor begins with `distributor_state` at `PENDING_TRAINING`, `installation_training_state` at `NOT_STARTED`, `probation_reason` at `NONE`, `completed_training_keys` an empty array, `trained_technician_count` at 0, `warranty_credit_cents` at 0 and `open_claim_count` at 0. A duplicate `distributor_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`.

Territory exclusivity is enforced in both directions and considers every distributor that has not been terminated — a distributor in `PENDING_TRAINING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION` all count, and only `TERMINATED` distributors are ignored. Creating a distributor with `territory_exclusive` set to true is rejected when any non-terminated distributor already holds that `territory_code` at all, whether exclusively or not. And creating any distributor at all — exclusive or not — in a `territory_code` that a non-terminated distributor already holds exclusively is likewise rejected. Either direction reports `ERR_TERRITORY_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`. Two non-exclusive distributors may happily share a territory.

Training is recorded one module at a time by a `TrainingAdministrator`, naming the `technician_id` who completed it, the `training_module` completed — one of `INSTALLATION`, `PAYMENT_SYSTEMS` or `DIAGNOSTICS` — and the `completed_on` date of completion. Each record is stored as a composite key formed from the technician identifier and the module name joined by a vertical bar, appended to the distributor's append-only `completed_training_keys` array; that composite key is precisely what makes a duplicate submission detectable, and re-submitting the same technician for the same module at the same distributor is rejected with `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_DUPLICATE`. Two further conditions apply: the distributor must still be in `PENDING_TRAINING`, or the request is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_STATE_INVALID`, and the named technician must have `employer` of `DISTRIBUTOR` with a `distributor_id` equal to the distributor being addressed, or the request is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DISTRIBUTOR_MISMATCH` — a factory technician's training, or another distributor's technician, cannot be credited here.

Each accepted record appends its key, recomputes `trained_technician_count` as the number of **distinct** technician identifiers appearing anywhere in `completed_training_keys`, moves `installation_training_state` from `NOT_STARTED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and advances the distributor's `version` by 1. As long as the authorization threshold is not yet met, the distributor remains in `PENDING_TRAINING` — a self-loop that can repeat as many times as there are technicians and modules.

The **authorization threshold** is met when, after the new key has been appended, two conditions hold together: at least two distinct technicians appear in `completed_training_keys`, and both the `INSTALLATION` module and the `PAYMENT_SYSTEMS` module appear somewhere among those keys. The `DIAGNOSTICS` module is trainable and recordable but is not part of the threshold. The record that satisfies both conditions moves the distributor from `PENDING_TRAINING` to `AUTHORIZED`, sets `installation_training_state` to `COMPLETE`, and advances the `version` by 1. Note that the two conditions are independent: one technician who completed both installation and payment-systems training does not authorize the distributor, and two technicians who both completed only installation training do not either.

Channel standing is managed afterwards through a status action carrying an `action` of `PROBATION`, `REINSTATE` or `TERMINATE`, with a `reason` required for probation and for termination. A probation action moves the distributor from `AUTHORIZED` to `PROBATION` and records the submitted reason in `probation_reason` as either `WARRANTY_ABUSE` or `MANUAL`. A reinstatement moves the distributor from `PROBATION` back to `AUTHORIZED` and resets `probation_reason` to `NONE`. A termination moves the distributor from either `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION` to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal in every sense: a terminated distributor accepts no operation whatsoever thereafter. Each of the three advances the distributor's `version` by 1, and an action attempted from a state the transition does not permit is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_STATE_INVALID`.

Probation is a middle ground with precise consequences, and they are worth collecting in one place because they are enforced in three different subsections. A distributor on `PROBATION` may still draft quotes and add lines to them, and may still file warranty claims, but may **not** submit a quote for a channel decision — submission demands `AUTHORIZED` and anything else is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. A distributor on `PROBATION` may still have its technicians certified, since a certification grant accepts `AUTHORIZED` or `PROBATION`. And a distributor on `PROBATION` carries a 150-basis-point risk add-on — one and one half percent — on every financing computation performed for its quotes, as §3.8 specifies.

Two derived figures on the distributor row are maintained by the warranty workflow rather than by the channel. `warranty_credit_cents` is a running credit ledger that begins at 0, is increased on each claim approval by the approved amount, and is decreased by exactly the same amount when that approval is voided; it never goes negative. `open_claim_count` is the number of that distributor's warranty claims currently sitting in `SUBMITTED`, and it is recomputed on every event that could change it. Distributors are listed by `distributor_code` and may be narrowed with the `territory_code` and `distributor_state` filters.

### 3.8. Package Quoting and Laundromat Financing

A laundromat is bought as a package, not as a list of machines, and it is almost always financed. A `Distributor` drafts an equipment package quote naming its own `distributor_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, or the request is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` — together with the `customer_name`, the `laundromat_site_code` the package is destined for, and the `stated_credit_score` the distributor has collected from the buyer, an integer from 300 through 850. The commercial shape of the deal is declared up front: `financing_program` accepts `NONE`, `TERM_36` or `TERM_60` and defaults to `NONE`, where `NONE` means a cash deal and suppresses every financing computation; `down_payment_cents` is a cash down payment in cents, zero or greater, defaulting to 0; `package_discount_pct` is a package-level discount from `0.00` through `15.00` with exactly two decimal places, defaulting to `0.00`; and `freight_cents` is freight in cents, zero or greater, defaulting to 0. A quote may not be drafted by a `TERMINATED` distributor, which is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_STATE_INVALID`.

A new quote begins in `quote_state` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `line_count` at 0, `line_subtotal_cents` at 0, `discount_cents` at 0 and `total_cents` equal to `freight_cents` — the money rollup over an empty line set. All six financing outputs begin null: `verified_credit_score`, `provisional_apr_bp`, `provisional_monthly_payment_cents`, `final_apr_bp` and `monthly_payment_cents` are null, and `origination_fee_cents` and `financed_principal_cents` sit at 0 until a submission computes them.

Lines are added one machine model at a time while the quote is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; any other state is `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`. A line names the `model_id` being quoted, the `quantity` of units as an integer from 1 through 50, the `payment_module` ordered per unit — `NONE`, `COIN`, `CARD` or `MOBILE`, defaulting to `NONE` — and a `line_discount_pct` from `0.00` through `20.00` with exactly two decimal places, defaulting to `0.00`. It also carries the `expected_quote_version` field described in §2.7. A `payment_module` of `NONE` is always acceptable; any of the three real modules must appear in the quoted model's `payment_module_options`, or the line is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_MODULE_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

At the moment a line is created, the quoted model's `list_price_cents` is **snapshotted** into the line's `unit_price_cents`. That snapshot is permanent: a later repricing of the model never re-prices an existing line, so a quote issued in March holds its March prices even if the catalogue moves in April. The per-unit payment-module surcharge is recorded as `payment_surcharge_cents` and is selected purely by the line's `payment_module`: a `NONE` module adds nothing, a `COIN` module adds thirty-five dollars per unit, a `CARD` module adds one hundred twenty-five dollars per unit, and a `MOBILE` module adds ninety dollars per unit — all expressed as integer cents on the stored field.

The extended line amount, `line_total_cents`, is computed in one pass and **rounded exactly once, at the very end**. The unit price and the per-unit surcharge are added together; that per-unit figure is multiplied by the `quantity`, because the surcharge is charged per machine and therefore sits inside the quantity multiplication; the product is then multiplied by one minus the `line_discount_pct` divided by one hundred; and only that final extended amount is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Rounding the discounted per-unit price first and then multiplying by the quantity is a defect: whenever the per-unit result carries a one-half-cent fraction, the two orders disagree, and the per-unit-first order is the wrong one.

Every line creation and every line deletion re-derives the quote's money fields from scratch. `line_count` is the number of lines on the quote. `line_subtotal_cents` is the **sum of the stored, already-rounded** `line_total_cents` values of the surviving lines; it never recomputes a line from its unit price, so line-level rounding is never re-litigated at the package level. `discount_cents` is the `line_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `package_discount_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up once to a whole number of cents. `total_cents` is `line_subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents` plus `freight_cents` — the package discount applies to the subtotal only and **never** to freight, which is passed through at cost. An empty quote has `line_count` 0, `line_subtotal_cents` 0, `discount_cents` 0, and `total_cents` equal to `freight_cents`. After every line create and every line delete, two identities must hold: `line_subtotal_cents` equals the sum of the surviving `line_total_cents` values, and `total_cents` equals `line_subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents` plus `freight_cents`. The same four figures are re-read, not recomputed from unit prices, when the quote is submitted and again when the channel decides it, so the financing computations of §3.8 always act on the money identity as the last line edit left it.

Deleting a line is the only way to change one; lines are not edited in place. A deletion addresses the line by its `line_id` under its parent quote, carries the same `expected_quote_version` field under the same stale-version rule, removes the line row, re-runs the money rollup, advances the parent quote's `version` by 1, and returns the **updated parent quote** rather than the deleted line. A line creation, by contrast, returns the newly created line and likewise advances the parent quote's `version` by 1. Quote lines may be read individually or listed with `quote_id` and `model_id` filters; quotes are listed newest-first with `distributor_id` and `quote_state` filters, and a `Distributor` sees only its own.

Submitting a quote for a channel decision moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, or from `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `SUBMITTED`, and advances the quote's `version` by 1. Four conditions gate it, in the order §2.8 fixes: the quote must be in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or the request is `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`; the acting distributor's `distributor_state` must be `AUTHORIZED`, so a distributor on `PROBATION` is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; the quote must have at least one line, or the request is `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_LINES`; and when `financing_program` is anything other than `NONE`, `down_payment_cents` must be strictly less than `total_cents`, since a buyer cannot finance a negative or zero balance, and a violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A resubmission after a revision request recomputes the provisional figures from the quote's current lines and the distributor's current standing, so a distributor placed on probation between two submissions sees a worse rate on the second.

**Financing is computed twice, from two different credit scores, and the two computations write different fields.** The provisional computation runs at submission on the distributor-stated score. The final computation runs at approval on the channel-verified score. Both share the same principal, the same fee formula and the same payment formula; they differ only in which score feeds the APR adjustment and in which fields they write.

The shared part comes first. The pre-fee principal is `total_cents` minus `down_payment_cents`. The origination rate is tiered on that **pre-fee** principal and on nothing else: two and one half percent when the pre-fee principal is at most fifty thousand dollars, one and three quarters percent when it is above fifty thousand dollars and at most one hundred fifty thousand dollars, and one percent when it is above one hundred fifty thousand dollars. `origination_fee_cents` is the pre-fee principal multiplied by that rate divided by one hundred, rounded half up once to a whole number of cents. `financed_principal_cents` is the pre-fee principal plus `origination_fee_cents`. The tier is keyed on the pre-fee principal, never on the financed principal — the fee does not re-tier itself after being added, and a pre-fee principal just under a tier boundary that crosses it once the fee is added still pays the lower tier's rate.

The APR is assembled from three integer components in basis points and then clamped. The base rate is 1450 basis points, fourteen and one half percent, for a `TERM_36` program, and 1690 basis points, sixteen and nine tenths percent, for a `TERM_60` program. The score adjustment subtracts 250 basis points when the governing score is 760 or above, subtracts 100 basis points when it is at least 700 but below 760, applies no adjustment when it is at least 640 but below 700, and adds 300 basis points when it is below 640. A distributor whose `distributor_state` is `PROBATION` adds a further 150 basis points; any other standing adds nothing. The three components are summed first, and only the sum is clamped into the range from 900 basis points to 2400 basis points inclusive — the clamp is applied once to the total, never to each adjustment separately.

The level monthly payment follows from the APR and the term. The term is 36 months for `TERM_36` and 60 months for `TERM_60`. The monthly rate is the APR in basis points divided by ten thousand and then divided by twelve, and it is **carried at full decimal precision and never pre-rounded** — rounding the rate to four or six decimals before the payment formula changes the resulting payment. The payment is the `financed_principal_cents` multiplied by the monthly rate, divided by the quantity obtained by subtracting, from one, the value of one plus the monthly rate raised to the power of the negated term in months; the result of that division is rounded half up once to a whole number of cents.

The provisional variant runs at submission, takes `stated_credit_score` as its governing score, and writes four fields: `provisional_apr_bp`, `provisional_monthly_payment_cents`, `origination_fee_cents` and `financed_principal_cents`. It is the **only** variant that computes the fee and the principal. The final variant runs at approval, takes `verified_credit_score` as its governing score, writes `final_apr_bp` and `monthly_payment_cents`, leaves the provisional pair untouched, and reuses the principal frozen at submission time without recomputing it — an approval therefore never changes how much is being financed, only what it costs. When `financing_program` is `NONE`, no fee is charged and all six financing fields stay at their initial null-or-zero values on both passes.

A `ChannelManager` decides a `SUBMITTED` quote, submitting a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DECLINE` or `REQUEST_REVISION` with an optional `note`; a decision on a quote in any other state is `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID`. Every decision records the deciding operator in `decided_by_user_id` and advances the quote's `version` by 1. An approval requires `verified_credit_score`, an integer from 300 through 850, and omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the score is stored, the final financing variant runs, and the quote moves from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`. A decline moves the quote from `SUBMITTED` to `DECLINED` and leaves `final_apr_bp` and `monthly_payment_cents` null — a declined deal has no final terms. A revision request moves the quote from `SUBMITTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, increases `revision_round` by 1, resets `provisional_apr_bp` and `provisional_monthly_payment_cents` back to null so that the next submission recomputes them from whatever the lines then say, and makes the lines editable again. `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are terminal; `REVISION_REQUESTED` closes the loop that runs `SUBMITTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and back to `SUBMITTED`, and that loop may repeat without limit.

### 3.9. Warranty Claims, Part Stock Movement and Exposure Analysis

A warranty claim is filed by a `Distributor` against a specific serialized unit for a specific failed component. The filing names the `build_order_id` of the unit, the `part_id` of the component claimed as failed, its own `distributor_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` or the request is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` — and the `reported_by_technician_id` of the field technician who ran the diagnostics. It records the failure conditions as `failure_cycle_count`, the number of wash cycles the unit had run at failure and greater than zero; `in_service_months`, the whole months in service at failure and zero or greater; `duty_cycle_class`, the observed duty regime as `LIGHT`, `STANDARD` or `HEAVY`; and `diagnostics_code`, the fault code the diagnostics tool captured in the field.

Only a finished machine can fail in the field, so the claimed unit must be in `build_state` `ACCEPTED`; a claim against a unit still in production, in rework, on hold or scrapped is `ERR_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. The reporting technician must have `employer` of `DISTRIBUTOR` and a `distributor_id` equal to the claiming distributor, or the filing is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DISTRIBUTOR_MISMATCH`. Where the claimed part declares a `requires_cert_scope` other than `NONE`, that reporting technician must additionally be in `cert_state` `ACTIVE`, be within `cert_expires_on`, and hold that scope in `cert_scopes`, or the filing is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` — a control-board claim needs someone qualified to have diagnosed a control board. A `TERMINATED` distributor may not file at all, which is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_STATE_INVALID`.

A new claim begins in `claim_state` `SUBMITTED` with `block_reason` at `NONE`, `coverage_variant` at `NONE`, `covered_amount_cents` at 0, `denial_reason` at `NONE`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` null and `voided_at` null, and the filing recomputes the claiming distributor's `open_claim_count`. One point about creation is easy to get wrong and is therefore stated flatly: **a claim is created in `SUBMITTED` even when the claimed part is currently `BACKORDERED` and even when the claimed unit already carries `integrity_hold`.** `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` is an event-driven consequence of a stock position or an integrity standing *changing*, never a condition evaluated at creation time.

What *is* computed at creation is `effective_rated_cycles`, the duty- and product-line-adjusted design life of the part on this particular machine. It starts from the part's `rated_duty_cycles` and applies two multipliers. The duty multiplier reflects how hard the machine is worked: a `LIGHT` duty cycle multiplies by `1.25`, a `STANDARD` duty cycle by `1.00`, and a `HEAVY` duty cycle by `0.70`. The line factor reflects how the product line loads its components: an `ON_PREMISES` machine multiplies by `1.00`, a `VENDED` machine by `0.85`, and an `INDUSTRIAL_TUNNEL` system by `1.30`, taken from the `product_line` of the claimed unit's model. The two multipliers are multiplied together with the rated design life in a single exact product, and only that product is **truncated downward** to a whole number of cycles — the multipliers are never truncated one at a time, and the result is floored rather than rounded.

A `WarrantyAnalyst` adjudicates a `SUBMITTED` claim with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, the `expected_part_version` field described in §2.7, and — for a denial — a `denial_reason` of `OUT_OF_WARRANTY`, `DUTY_CYCLE_ABUSE` or `PART_NOT_FITTED`; a denial without a reason, or with `NONE` as the reason, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both outcomes record `adjudicated_by_user_id`, advance the claim's `version` by 1, recompute the claiming distributor's `open_claim_count`, and — this is deliberate and applies to **both** APPROVE and DENY — advance the claimed `ServicePart`'s `version` by 1, because every adjudication is journalled against the part even when no unit moves. A denial stores the reason, leaves `covered_amount_cents` at 0 and `coverage_variant` at `NONE`, leaves the part's stock quantities entirely untouched, and moves the claim from `SUBMITTED` to `DENIED`, which is terminal.

An approval must clear four business gates beyond the state and version checks, in the order §2.8 fixes. The part must fit the built machine — the model's `product_line` present in the part's `fits_product_lines`, and the model's `capacity_rating_lb` between `fits_min_capacity_lb` and `fits_max_capacity_lb` inclusive — or the approval is `ERR_PART_NOT_FITTED`. The claim must be coverable: `in_service_months` must not exceed the part's `warranty_months`, and `failure_cycle_count` must not exceed the computed `effective_rated_cycles`; a failure outside either bound is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_COVERABLE`. And the part must have at least one unit on the shelf, `on_hand_qty` of 1 or more, or the approval is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK`.

**Settlement takes one of two forms, and the selector is not the same test as the coverability test.** The full-replacement form applies when both of two conditions hold: `in_service_months` is at most 12, and twice the `failure_cycle_count` is at most `effective_rated_cycles` — in other words, the part failed inside its first year and before it had consumed half of its adjusted design life. In that case `coverage_variant` is `FULL_REPLACEMENT` and `covered_amount_cents` is the part's full `unit_price_cents`, with no depletion applied at all. In every other case the pro-rata form applies: `coverage_variant` is `PRO_RATA` and the settlement is depleted by whichever entitlement has been consumed faster. Two ratios are formed — the cycle-remaining ratio, being `effective_rated_cycles` minus `failure_cycle_count` divided by `effective_rated_cycles`; and the month-remaining ratio, being `warranty_months` minus `in_service_months` divided by `warranty_months` — and `covered_amount_cents` is the part's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the **lesser** of those two ratios, rounded half up once to a whole number of cents. Both ratios are carried at full decimal precision until that single rounding step.

An approval then moves inventory and money together. The claim advances from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`. The part's `on_hand_qty` decreases by 1 while its `reserved_qty` increases by 1, so the sum of the two is conserved — a reservation is not a consumption. The part's `stock_state` is re-projected from the new on-hand position: it becomes `BACKORDERED` when `on_hand_qty` has reached exactly 0, `LOW` when `on_hand_qty` is above 0 but at or below `reorder_point_qty` — the boundary is inclusive, so an on-hand position exactly equal to the reorder point reads `LOW` and not `IN_STOCK` — and `IN_STOCK` otherwise. If that re-projection has just driven the part into `BACKORDERED`, every **other** claim on that part still sitting in `SUBMITTED` moves to `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` with `block_reason` set to `PART_BACKORDERED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each advancing its own `version` by 1 and each triggering a recomputation of its own distributor's `open_claim_count`. Finally the claiming distributor's `warranty_credit_cents` increases by the `covered_amount_cents` just settled.

Voiding an approval reverses the money and the inventory but preserves the audit trail. A void is available only on a claim in `APPROVED`; anything else is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. It carries a `reason`, moves the claim from `APPROVED` to `VOIDED`, and stamps `voided_at` with the current logical-clock instant. `covered_amount_cents` and `coverage_variant` are **retained** on the voided claim for audit rather than being zeroed, even though the corresponding money is reversed: the distributor's `warranty_credit_cents` decreases by exactly the amount that claim had credited, which is why the ledger can never drift and never goes negative. The part's `on_hand_qty` increases by 1 and its `reserved_qty` decreases by 1, again conserving their sum, and the part's `version` advances by 1. The part's `stock_state` is re-projected, and if the part has just left `BACKORDERED`, every claim on it sitting in `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `PART_BACKORDERED` whose claimed unit has `integrity_hold` false returns to `SUBMITTED` with `block_reason` reset to `NONE`. `open_claim_count` is recomputed on every affected distributor, and the voided claim's `version` advances by 1.

Stock also moves outside the claim workflow. A `PlantManager` adjusts a part's inventory by submitting a `delta_qty`, positive for a receipt and negative for shrinkage, together with a `reason`. The `on_hand_qty` changes by that delta; an adjustment that would drive it below zero is rejected with `ERR_STOCK_ADJUSTMENT_NEGATIVE` and nothing is written. The same `stock_state` re-projection runs afterwards, with the same two cascades attached: entering `BACKORDERED` blocks every `SUBMITTED` claim on the part with `block_reason` `PART_BACKORDERED`, and leaving `BACKORDERED` returns the blocked ones to `SUBMITTED`. The part's `version` advances by 1.

Unblocking is conjunctive, and this is the rule that reconciles the two independent blocking causes. A claim leaves `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` and returns to `SUBMITTED` only when **both** obstacles are gone: the claimed part's `stock_state` is something other than `BACKORDERED`, **and** the claimed unit's `integrity_hold` is false. A claim blocked by a backorder on a unit that is also under an integrity hold stays blocked when the shelf is restocked, and stays blocked when the technician is re-certified, until both conditions clear. Every entry into and exit from `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` sets or clears `block_reason` accordingly, recomputes the owning distributor's `open_claim_count`, and advances the claim's `version` by 1.

Finally, the manufacturer needs to see where warranty money is actually going, and that is the purpose of the per-model warranty exposure read, available to a `WarrantyAnalyst` and to a `ProductEngineer` and optionally narrowed by a `duty_cycle_class` filter. It aggregates every warranty claim whose claimed unit belongs to the addressed model and reports: the `model_id` addressed; `claim_count`, the total number of matching claims; `approved_claim_count`, `denied_claim_count`, `blocked_claim_count` and `voided_claim_count`, the counts sitting in `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `FULFILLMENT_BLOCKED` and `VOIDED` respectively; `covered_amount_total_cents`, the summed settled amount **excluding** voided claims, since voided money was reversed even though the claim retains its figure; `full_replacement_count` and `pro_rata_count`, the number of settlements produced by each coverage form; `component_class_breakdown`, the exposure grouped by the `component_class` of the claimed part; and `duty_cycle_breakdown`, the same exposure grouped by `duty_cycle_class`. Claims themselves may be read individually or listed oldest-first with `build_order_id`, `part_id`, `distributor_id`, `reported_by_technician_id` and `claim_state` filters.
