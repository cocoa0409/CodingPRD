# Appliance Parts Distribution & Independent Servicer Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An appliance parts distributor sits between a handful of original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and several thousand independent repair businesses. Neither side is patient. A technician standing in a customer's kitchen needs to know, in one call, whether the part stamped on the failed component is still sold, what replaced it if it is not, which of the distributor's branches can hand it over today, what it will cost under the servicer's contract, and whether the servicer's account still has credit headroom to buy it. The same distributor must simultaneously protect itself: parts age out of production, price books change monthly, electrical components cannot be resold once they leave the counter, core deposits on rebuildable assemblies represent real money sitting in customers' vans, and a servicer who stops paying must be frozen before the next truck loads.

This document specifies the API that runs that business. It governs a catalog whose parts point at one another through supersession chains, a multi-branch inventory pool from which each order line is reserved individually, servicer accounts carrying credit terms and core-deposit liability, technician certifications that gate what a servicer is even allowed to buy, warranty claims filed with the OEM on the servicer's behalf, obsolete-part substitution, return authorizations with tiered restocking fees, and a price-file publication pipeline that can be rolled back after the fact. Two independent lifecycles run on every order at once — one physical, one financial — and the interesting behaviour of the system lives in how those two dimensions read each other without ever driving each other directly.

The system is built for determinism above convenience. Every time-dependent rule reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall time; every money amount is an integer count of cents; every allocation decision follows a fixed, fully specified ranking with a deterministic final tie-break; and every rule that could plausibly be evaluated in more than one order has its position in the evaluation sequence pinned down. Implementers should treat ambiguity in this document as a defect to be reported, not a degree of freedom.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and audit columns, arithmetic discipline, stale-version protection, and the order in which a request is allowed to fail.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity it acts in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Servicer`, `BranchCounterRep`, `CreditManager`, `WarrantyAdmin`, `PricingAdmin`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `Servicer` is an independent appliance-repair business buying parts on account. It opens orders, adds lines, cancels its own orders and lines, raises return authorizations, withdraws substitute requests, and reads. A `BranchCounterRep` is counter and warehouse staff at one distribution branch; it owns the physical side — opening inventory positions, posting count adjustments, allocating orders, handing them over, and receiving returns. A `CreditManager` owns the money side: account creation and maintenance, periodic account review, order credit decisions, payments, return-credit approvals, and warranty credits; it can never allocate or ship. A `WarrantyAdmin` owns the OEM-facing work: technician certifications, the warranty-claim lifecycle up to adjudication, and substitute-part decisions for obsolete lines; it can never issue the resulting credit. A `PricingAdmin` owns the catalog and the price book: parts, supersession links, model cross-references, pricing tiers, and price-file publication and rollback; it never touches accounts, orders, or money. A `PlatformAdmin` registers branches and controls the logical clock, and is additionally permitted on every business write as a break-glass operator — but it is still bound by the segregation-of-duties rule on restocking-fee waivers described in §2.8.

Two of the six roles are backed by persisted entities, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Servicer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServicerAccount`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `ServicerAccount` compare the resource's `servicer_account_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Servicer` acting on a `PartOrder`, `OrderLine`, `SubstituteRequest`, `ReturnAuthorization`, `WarrantyClaim`, or `ServicerAccount` that belongs to a different account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). For an `OrderLine`, a `SubstituteRequest`, a `ReturnAuthorization`, or a `WarrantyClaim`, the account compared against is the one on the owning order or the row's own `servicer_account_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `BranchCounterRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Branch`. Ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to whichever branch foreign key the operation acts through: `pickup_branch_id` when a `WILL_CALL` order is allocated or handed over, an `allocation_branch_id` of one of the order's allocated lines when an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order is handed over, `return_branch_id` when a return is physically received, and `branch_id` when an inventory position is opened or adjusted. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `CreditManager`, `WarrantyAdmin`, `PricingAdmin`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `credit_decided_by_user_id` on a `PartOrder` when a credit decision is taken, as `decided_by_user_id` on a `SubstituteRequest` or a `ReturnAuthorization`, and as `created_by_user_id` on a `ReturnAuthorization` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. The one place a recorded operator identifier is compared to a live `X-User-ID` is the restocking-fee waiver check of §2.8, which compares two operator identifiers with each other.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before collecting the page and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. There are exactly two exceptions in this API: the branch list is ordered by `created_at` descending (newest branch first), and the pricing-tier list is ordered by `tier_code` ascending.

List responses report `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Filter parameters are ANDed together, and filtering happens before pagination.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first persisted.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only change computed money fields. There is one exception: a `ModelPartXref` is an immutable, append-only configuration row. It carries `id` and `created_at` only; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no endpoint that modifies one.

All temporal reasoning in the system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying a `current_utc` value; every subsequent evaluation of "now" — elapsed-day counts, certification coverage intervals, price-file effectivity, cutoff-hour comparisons, and the `last_counted_at`, `published_at`, `submitted_at`, `fulfilled_at`, and `received_at` stamps — uses that instant. Setting the clock does not itself recompute any stored value; it only changes what later requests observe.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in stored state or in any response body. Field names ending in `_cents` are integers; field names ending in `_pct` are decimal fractions where 1.0 means one hundred percent.

Wherever a monetary amount is produced by multiplying by a percentage, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Two orderings matter enough to state explicitly, because getting them backwards produces off-by-one-cent errors:

- Line pricing rounds the **unit** price to a whole cent first, and only then multiplies the rounded unit price by the line quantity. It never multiplies first and rounds once at the end.
- A restocking fee is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the corresponding credit is then taken as the exact integer remainder, so that fee plus credit always equals the gross return amount exactly.

Aggregates such as an order subtotal are plain integer sums of amounts that are already rounded; they are never re-rounded. Where a rule counts whole days from an elapsed-second difference, the count is floored. Where a rule counts weight blocks, the count is rounded up to the next whole block. The one percentage stored with sub-integer precision is a substitute request's price variance, which is rounded half up to four decimal places and carries a sign.

Discount percentages supplied on input must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive; each of the four contract discount percentages on a pricing tier is further capped at 0.45.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Protection

Two writes in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`. Both are writes that would otherwise succeed a second time under the product semantics, which is exactly why no state precondition can catch a client working from an out-of-date view.

Adding a line to an order requires the request to carry `expected_part_order_version`, holding the `version` of the target `PartOrder` as the client last observed it. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no line is created. The protection matters because every accepted line recomputes the order's `total_cents` and therefore the account's projected credit headroom; a client working from a stale order view would be making a purchasing decision against numbers that no longer exist. Note that a second line for the same part and quantity is a perfectly legitimate second line — repeatability is the norm here, so the version check is the only defence.

Recording a supersession pointer on a part requires the request to carry `expected_part_version`, holding the `version` of the target `Part` as the client last observed it. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the part's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no pointer is written. A supersession legitimately overwrites any pointer already recorded, so a sequential retry would succeed; the version check is what stops a writer from silently clobbering a chain link — or a lifecycle change applied by a price-file publication — that it never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

Validation is strictly ordered. Rules are evaluated in ascending priority, the **first** violated rule decides the response, and no later rule is evaluated. This is the single most consequential contract in the document, because many requests violate several rules at once and only one error identifier may be returned. The full ladder, in evaluation order:

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list. Applies to every endpoint.
3. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — the request body conforms to the endpoint's input schema: every required field is present, no unknown field is supplied, enum values are in range, integer money and unit values are not negative, any `quantity` is at least 1, discount percentages fall between 0 and 1 inclusive, and datetimes are ISO 8601 UTC. Applies to every endpoint.
4. A 404 existence error — every path parameter resolves to a persisted row, validated left to right. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. A 404 existence error — every foreign key in the request body resolves to a persisted row. The body foreign keys in question are `part_id`, `pricing_tier_id`, `pickup_branch_id`, `return_branch_id`, `order_line_id`, `substitute_part_id`, `branch_id`, `servicer_account_id`, `requested_part_id`, and `superseded_by_part_id`. Applies to every endpoint with foreign-key input.
6. `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403) — when the acting role is `Servicer`, `X-User-ID` equals the `servicer_account_id` of the targeted order, line, substitute request, return authorization, warranty claim, or account. Guards nine operations: `POST /part-orders`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/cancel`, `POST /order-lines/{id}/cancel`, `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/withdraw`, `POST /return-authorizations`, `GET /part-orders/{id}`, `GET /servicer-accounts/{id}`, and `GET /servicer-accounts/{id}/credit-summary`.
7. `ERR_BRANCH_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403) — when the acting role is `BranchCounterRep`, `X-User-ID` equals the branch the operation acts for, as described in §2.1. Guards five operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`, `POST /return-authorizations/{id}/receive`, `POST /inventory-records`, and `POST /inventory-records/{id}/adjustments`.
8. `ERR_SERVICER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) — the owning account's `account_status` is `ACTIVE`. Accounts in `CREDIT_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` may not open orders, add lines, be allocated, be shipped, or file warranty claim submissions. Guards five operations: `POST /part-orders`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`, and `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/submit`.
9. `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_OPEN` (409) — every branch the operation acts through has `branch_status` equal to `OPEN`. A branch in `COUNT_FREEZE` blocks reservations and receipts; a `CLOSED` branch blocks everything. Guards four operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`, `POST /return-authorizations/{id}/receive`, and `POST /inventory-records/{id}/adjustments`.
10. `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (409) — the order's `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT` and its `settlement_status` is `PENDING_CREDIT`. Guards two operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines` and `POST /part-orders/{id}/credit-decision`.
11. `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (409) — allocation requires `fulfillment_status` equal to `DRAFT` and `settlement_status` in `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `PAID`; hand-over requires `fulfillment_status` equal to `ALLOCATED`; order cancellation requires `fulfillment_status` to be one of `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, or `HELD`. Guards three operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`, `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`, and `POST /part-orders/{id}/cancel`.
12. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409) — a credit decision requires `settlement_status` equal to `PENDING_CREDIT`; a payment requires `settlement_status` to be either `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `INVOICED`. Guards two operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/credit-decision` and `POST /part-orders/{id}/payments`.
13. `ERR_LINE_NOT_SHIPPED` (409) — the targeted line's `line_status` is `SHIPPED` or `RETURNED`. Guards two operations: `POST /return-authorizations` and `POST /warranty-claims`.
14. `ERR_LINE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the targeted line's `line_status` is one of `RESOLVED`, `ALLOCATED`, or `BACKORDERED`, and its parent order is neither `FULFILLED` nor `CANCELLED`. Guards `POST /order-lines/{id}/cancel`.
15. `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (409) — a claim's `claim_status` is `DRAFT` for submission, `SUBMITTED` for an OEM response, `OEM_INFO_REQUESTED` for resubmission, and either `OEM_APPROVED` or `OEM_DENIED` for closing. Guards four operations: `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/submit`, `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/oem-response`, `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/resubmit`, and `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/credit`.
16. `ERR_RA_STATE_INVALID` (409) — a return authorization's `ra_status` is `REQUESTED` for a decision and `APPROVED` for a receipt. Guards two operations: `POST /return-authorizations/{id}/decision` and `POST /return-authorizations/{id}/receive`.
17. `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_ALREADY_DECIDED` (409) — the substitute request's `request_status` is `PENDING`. Guards two operations: `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/decision` and `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/withdraw`.
18. `ERR_PRICE_FILE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — a price file's `price_file_status` is `DRAFT` for adding lines and for publication, and `PUBLISHED` for rollback; additionally, rollback requires that no substitute request created by that file has left `PENDING`. Guards three operations: `POST /price-files/{id}/lines`, `POST /price-files/{id}/publish`, and `POST /price-files/{id}/rollback`.
19. `ERR_CERT_ALREADY_REVOKED` (409) — the certification's `cert_status` is not already `REVOKED`. Guards `POST /technician-certifications/{id}/revoke`.
20. `ERR_PRICE_FILE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE` (409) — the file's `effective_at` is at or before the logical clock. Guards `POST /price-files/{id}/publish`.
21. `ERR_PRICE_FILE_HAS_NO_LINES` (422) — the file's `line_count` is at least 1. Guards `POST /price-files/{id}/publish`.
22. `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (412) — `expected_part_order_version` equals the order's current `version`. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`.
23. `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION` (412) — `expected_part_version` equals the part's current `version`. Guards `POST /parts/{id}/supersession`.
24. `ERR_OEM_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (422) — the resolved part's `oem_code` appears in the account's `oem_authorizations`. Guards three operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`, `POST /warranty-claims`, and `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/decision`.
25. `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CERT_REQUIRED` (422) — the account holds an effective certification for the part's `oem_code` at a `cert_level` that meets or exceeds the part's `cert_level_required`. Guards two operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines` and `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/submit`.
26. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — the operator waiving a restocking fee is not the operator recorded in `credit_decided_by_user_id` on the order behind the return. Guards `POST /return-authorizations/{id}/decision`.
27. `ERR_PART_NO_LONGER_AVAILABLE` (422) — the supersession walk does not terminate on a part whose `lifecycle_status` is `NLA`. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`.
28. `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CHAIN_BROKEN` (422) — no part in the walk is `SUPERSEDED` while its `superseded_by_part_id` is null. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`.
29. `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE_DETECTED` (422) — the walk does not revisit a part it has already seen. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`.
30. `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CHAIN_TOO_LONG` (422) — the walk terminates within the permitted hop budget. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines`.
31. `ERR_SUPERSESSION_TARGET_INVALID` (422) — a supersession target is a different part from the source, does not already point back at the source, and shares the source's `oem_code`. Guards `POST /parts/{id}/supersession`.
32. `ERR_PART_NON_RETURNABLE` (422) — the resolved part's `is_returnable` is true. Guards `POST /return-authorizations`.
33. `ERR_RETURN_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED` (422) — the requested quantity plus quantities already committed to other authorizations of the same line does not exceed the line's `quantity`. Guards `POST /return-authorizations`.
34. `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422) — the elapsed-day count since fulfillment is inside the applicable return window. Guards `POST /return-authorizations`.
35. `ERR_WARRANTY_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (422) — the elapsed-day count since fulfillment is inside the warranty-claim window. Guards `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/submit`.
36. `ERR_PRICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) — the publishing file's effectivity window does not collide with a published file on any shared part. Guards `POST /price-files/{id}/publish`.
37. `ERR_CERT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) — the new certification's coverage interval does not touch or overlap an active certification for the same technician and OEM on the same account. Guards `POST /technician-certifications`.
38. `ERR_CERT_INTERVAL_INVALID` (422) — `training_completed_at` is strictly before `expires_at`. Guards `POST /technician-certifications`.
39. `ERR_PRICE_INTERVAL_INVALID` (422) — `effective_until` is either absent or strictly after `effective_at`. Guards `POST /price-files`.
40. `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_PART_INVALID` (422) — an approving decision supplies a `substitute_part_id` whose part has `lifecycle_status` equal to `ACTIVE`, an `oem_code` equal to the obsolete part's `oem_code`, and an `id` different from the obsolete part's; a rejecting decision supplies no substitute at all. Guards `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/decision`.
41. `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_PRICE_VARIANCE` (422) — the absolute value of the signed list-price variance between substitute and obsolete part does not exceed the permitted cap. Guards `POST /substitute-requests/{id}/decision`.
42. `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) — the claim's `resubmission_count` is still below the resubmission ceiling. Guards `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/resubmit`.
43. `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES` (422) — the order has at least one line whose `line_status` is not `CANCELLED`. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`.
44. `ERR_FULFILLMENT_MODE_MISMATCH` (422) — a `WILL_CALL` order supplies `pickup_branch_id` and omits `ship_to_zip`; an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order supplies `ship_to_zip` and omits `pickup_branch_id`. Guards `POST /part-orders`.
45. `ERR_WILL_CALL_NOT_ENABLED` (422) — the designated pickup branch has `will_call_enabled` true. Guards two operations: `POST /part-orders` and `POST /part-orders/{id}/allocate`.
46. `ERR_INVENTORY_NEGATIVE` (422) — the adjustment leaves `on_hand_units` at or above `reserved_units` and at or above zero. Guards `POST /inventory-records/{id}/adjustments`.
47. `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409) — uniqueness holds: `part_number` is unique across parts, `tier_code` across pricing tiers, `file_version` across price files, the pair of `branch_id` and `part_id` across inventory records, the triple of `model_number`, `part_id`, and `position_code` across cross-reference rows, and the pair of `price_file_id` and `part_id` across price-file lines. Guards six operations: `POST /parts`, `POST /pricing-tiers`, `POST /price-files`, `POST /inventory-records`, `POST /model-part-xrefs`, and `POST /price-files/{id}/lines`.
48. `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402) — the account's credit exposure, recomputed with the pending change applied, does not exceed `credit_limit_cents`. Guards two operations: `POST /part-orders/{id}/lines` and `POST /part-orders/{id}/credit-decision`.
49. `ERR_PREPAY_REQUIRED` (402) — an account whose `credit_terms` is `PREPAY` has reached `settlement_status` equal to `PAID` before hand-over. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`.
50. `ERR_CORE_LIABILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402) — the account's `core_liability_cents` plus the deposits this shipment would add does not exceed `core_liability_limit_cents`. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/fulfill`.
51. `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) — `amount_cents` equals the exact amount due. Guards `POST /part-orders/{id}/payments`.

Three consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get wrong. Account standing (rule 8) and branch posture (rule 9) are checked before any target-state rule, so a frozen account attempting to allocate an already-shipped order reports `ERR_SERVICER_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The two stale-version checks (rules 22 and 23) sit after every existence and state check but before every safety, graph, and fiscal check, so a line add that is both stale and over the credit limit reports `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`. And the electrical-part prohibition (rule 32) precedes the return-window check (rule 34), so an out-of-window electrical return still reports `ERR_PART_NON_RETURNABLE`.

The 404 errors named abstractly in rules 4 and 5 resolve to one identifier per entity: `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_PART_XREF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICING_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICER_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_FILE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_RETURN_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`. Where a path contains more than one identifier, the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides which of these is returned.

### 2.8. Separation of the Physical, the Financial and the OEM-Facing

The role model is deliberately partitioned so that no single operator can both commit inventory and forgive money. Allocation and hand-over belong to the branch; credit decisions, payments, and credit approvals belong to the credit desk; certifications, claim adjudication, and substitute approval belong to the warranty desk; the catalog and price book belong to pricing.

One cross-check makes this partition binding rather than advisory. When a credit decision is taken on an order, the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stored on the order as `credit_decided_by_user_id`. Later, if a return authorization against a line of that same order is approved with `waive_restocking_fee` set to true, the approving operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from that stored value. If they match, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The check applies to a `PlatformAdmin` acting as break-glass operator exactly as it applies to a `CreditManager`. Approving a return without waiving the fee is unaffected by this rule, and rejecting a return is unaffected too.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Many writes in this system touch more than one row. When they do, every affected row is mutated in the same request, and every one of those mutations refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`. The system never defers a cascade to a later request, never leaves a derived aggregate stale, and never partially applies a cascade: if the triggering write is rejected, nothing downstream of it is written either.

Cascades in this system run as deep as five hops. Receiving a return, for example, posts a credit, which lowers an account's exposure, which can lift a credit hold, which releases that account's held orders, which re-reserves branch inventory — all inside the single receiving request. Publishing a price file can mark parts obsolete, which pushes open order lines into substitution, which puts their orders on hold, which changes their accounts' exposure. Each of those hops is specified in the §3 workflow it belongs to.

Two conservation invariants govern the compensating side of these cascades. First, **inventory unit conservation**: whenever a reservation is released — by a line cancellation, an order cancellation, a credit freeze, an obsolescence cascade, or an account suspension — the affected inventory record's `reserved_units` falls by exactly the line quantity that had been reserved, and its `available_units` returns to exactly its pre-allocation value. Second, **catalog price conservation**: rolling back a published price file restores every part it touched to its pre-publication list price, core deposit, and lifecycle status, so the sum of `list_price_cents` across the whole catalog returns to its pre-publication value.

Cascades are conditional where the specification says they are conditional. An account entering `CREDIT_HOLD` freezes only its orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED`; its `FULFILLED`, `CANCELLED`, and `DRAFT` orders are untouched. A return receipt restocks inventory only for some return reasons. Implementers should not generalise a cascade beyond the rows the relevant subsection names.

### 2.10. Reads, Filters and Reverse Reachability

Every collection in this API sits at the top level of the URL space; there is no shared prefix. Each entity therefore exposes a list read and a single-resource read by `id`, and each meaningful foreign key exposes a filter on the corresponding list so that relationships can be traversed in both directions. The parts list can be filtered by `superseded_by_part_id` to find every part that a given part replaces, which is the reverse of a supersession hop. The cross-reference list can be filtered by `model_number` to find the parts that fit an appliance, or by `part_id` to find the models a part fits. The order-line list can be filtered by `part_order_id`, `requested_part_id`, `resolved_part_id`, `allocation_branch_id`, or `line_status`. The substitute-request list can be filtered by `created_by_price_file_id` to see everything one publication created. The inventory list can be filtered by `branch_id` or `part_id` to see multi-branch availability for a single part.

Only two paths are nested, and both express composition rather than addressing: adding a line to an order, and adding a line to a price file. Where a path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved left to right and the first unresolvable one determines the 404 returned, as stated in §2.7.

One read is purely computed and persists nothing. The credit-summary read for an account returns `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_used_cents`, `available_credit_cents` (the limit minus the used exposure), `core_liability_cents`, `core_liability_limit_cents`, `delinquency_score_rolling`, `delinquency_score_lifetime`, `rolling_12m_volume_cents`, `open_order_count` (the number of that account's orders whose exposure currently counts toward `credit_used_cents`), and `effective_cert_count`. It recomputes exposure and **both** delinquency variants on the fly so that the divergence between the rolling-window and lifetime views is directly observable, and it writes neither back to the account row.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Catalog: Parts, Supersession Chains and Model Cross-References

The catalog is the foundation everything else reads. A `PricingAdmin` creates a **Part** by supplying its `part_number` (unique across the catalog), the `oem_code` of the brand it belongs to, a human `description`, a commodity `category`, a `list_price_cents` greater than zero, and a `weight_grams` greater than zero. Three further inputs are optional: `lifecycle_status`, which defaults to `ACTIVE`; `core_required`, which defaults to false and marks a rebuildable assembly whose used core must come back; and `core_deposit_cents`, which defaults to 0 and is the per-unit deposit charged when a core is required. A part's `superseded_by_part_id` always starts null.

The `category` is one of `ELECTRICAL`, `MECHANICAL`, `COSMETIC`, or `SEALED_SYSTEM`, and it does three jobs at once: it selects which of the servicer's contract discounts applies, it determines what technician competence the part demands, and it determines whether the part can ever come back. Two derived attributes carry those last two consequences. `cert_level_required` is `SEALED_SYSTEM` when the category is `SEALED_SYSTEM`, `ADVANCED` when the category is `ELECTRICAL`, and `NONE` for every other category. `is_returnable` is true when the category is anything other than `ELECTRICAL` **and** the `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `NLA`; electrical parts are non-returnable once they leave the counter, and that prohibition is absolute. Both derived attributes are re-derived whenever the category or lifecycle changes, and both appear on every part read.

A part's `lifecycle_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `OBSOLETE`, or `NLA` (no longer available). Recording a supersession — a `PricingAdmin` action, guarded by `expected_part_version` as described in §2.6 — sets the part's `lifecycle_status` to `SUPERSEDED` and writes the supplied `superseded_by_part_id`, linking it into a chain. The target must be a different part from the source, must not already point back at the source (an immediate two-part loop is refused up front), and must share the source's `oem_code`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_TARGET_INVALID`. Each supersession lengthens the chain that order-line resolution will later have to walk.

That walk is the heart of the catalog. Resolution starts at the requested part with a hop count of zero and a visited set containing that part alone, then inspects the current part's `lifecycle_status` and behaves as follows. If it is `ACTIVE`, the walk stops and the current part is the resolved part. If it is `OBSOLETE`, the walk also stops at the current part, but the line is flagged as needing a substitute rather than being straightforwardly resolvable. If it is `NLA`, the request is rejected with `ERR_PART_NO_LONGER_AVAILABLE`. If it is `SUPERSEDED`, the walk needs to take a hop: when `superseded_by_part_id` is null there is nowhere to go and the request is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CHAIN_BROKEN`; otherwise the walk looks at the part that pointer names. If that part is already in the visited set, the request is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE_DETECTED`. Only after the cycle check does the walk increment the hop count by one, and if the hop count is then greater than five the request is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CHAIN_TOO_LONG`. Five hops is the budget; a chain that reaches a terminal lifecycle on its fifth hop is fine, a sixth hop is not. Otherwise the newly reached part joins the visited set, becomes the current part, and the inspection repeats. Whatever part the walk stops on becomes the line's `resolved_part_id`, and the number of pointers followed to get there becomes its `supersession_hops` — zero when the requested part was already `ACTIVE` or already `OBSOLETE`.

Alongside the parts themselves, a `PricingAdmin` records **model-to-part cross-references** so that a technician holding an appliance's nameplate can find the right part. Each cross-reference row names the `model_number` of the appliance, the `part_id` it fits, the `position_code` identifying its place in the exploded view, and an `xref_source` of either `OEM_PUBLICATION` or `MANUAL_ENTRY`. The combination of `model_number`, `part_id`, and `position_code` must be unique; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. These rows are append-only: once written they are never modified, and as noted in §2.4 they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

### 3.2. Servicer Accounts, Contract Tiers and Technician Competence

Contract pricing is expressed as **pricing tiers**, each created by a `PricingAdmin` with a unique `tier_code`, a display `name`, four discount percentages — `discount_pct_electrical`, `discount_pct_mechanical`, `discount_pct_cosmetic`, and `discount_pct_sealed_system`, each between 0 and 0.45 inclusive — and a `min_annual_volume_cents` of at least zero that records the rolling twelve-month invoiced volume an account must reach to qualify for the tier. The `tier_code` doubles as the deterministic tie-break key when tiers are compared during a review.

A `CreditManager` opens a **servicer account** by supplying the `business_name`, the array of `oem_authorizations` listing the OEM brand codes the servicer is factory-authorized for, and the `pricing_tier_id` of the contract tier that will price its lines. Three inputs are optional: `credit_terms`, which defaults to `PREPAY` and may otherwise be `NET_15`, `NET_30`, or `NET_45`; `credit_limit_cents`, which defaults to 0; and `core_liability_limit_cents`, which also defaults to 0 and caps how much outstanding core deposit the account may carry. A new account starts with `account_status` equal to `ACTIVE`, with `credit_used_cents`, `core_liability_cents`, and `rolling_12m_volume_cents` all at zero, and with `delinquency_score` initialised from the rolling-window scoring variant described below. The same fields — plus `oem_authorizations`, `pricing_tier_id`, and `account_status` — may all be amended later by a `CreditManager`, each amendment being a mutation of the account row.

An account's `account_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `CREDIT_HOLD`, or `SUSPENDED`, and it cycles between the first two. Moving an account from `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD` freezes it: every one of that account's orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED` moves to `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `CREDIT_HOLD`, each of those orders' lines whose `line_status` is `ALLOCATED` falls back to `BACKORDERED`, the corresponding `reserved_units` on each affected inventory record falls by that line's quantity, and each such line's `allocation_branch_id` is cleared to null. Orders of the same account that are already `FULFILLED` or `CANCELLED`, and orders still in `DRAFT`, are left exactly as they were. Moving an account from `CREDIT_HOLD` back to `ACTIVE` reverses the freeze: every order of that account whose `fulfillment_status` is `HELD` **and** whose `hold_reason` is `CREDIT_HOLD` is put through a fresh re-allocation sweep, becoming `ALLOCATED` if at least one of its lines manages to reserve stock, and otherwise remaining `HELD` with `hold_reason` rewritten to `BACKORDER`.

`SUSPENDED` is terminal and can be reached from either `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD`. Suspension is the harshest cascade in the system: every order of the account whose `settlement_status` is `INVOICED` moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, every order whose `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, or `HELD` moves to `CANCELLED` with all of its reservations released, and the account's `credit_used_cents` is then recomputed. A suspended account has no further transitions.

Technician competence is recorded as **certifications** hanging off the account. A `WarrantyAdmin` records one by supplying the `servicer_account_id` of the employing account, the `technician_name` the certification belongs to, the `oem_code` whose training it records, a `cert_level` of `BASIC`, `ADVANCED`, or `SEALED_SYSTEM`, and the coverage interval bounded by `training_completed_at` and `expires_at`. The interval is **closed** — both endpoints count as covered — and `training_completed_at` must fall strictly before `expires_at` or the request is rejected with `ERR_CERT_INTERVAL_INVALID`. A new certification starts with `cert_status` equal to `ACTIVE`.

Because a technician cannot hold two live certifications for the same OEM at the same instant, a new certification is checked against the account's existing certifications that share the same `technician_name` and `oem_code` and whose `cert_status` is `ACTIVE`. Two closed intervals collide when the new certification's `training_completed_at` falls at or before the existing one's `expires_at` **and** the existing one's `training_completed_at` falls at or before the new one's `expires_at`; a collision is rejected with `ERR_CERT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Merely touching endpoints do collide here: a certification expiring exactly at one instant and a renewal trained exactly at that same instant overlap on that instant and are refused. Peers whose `cert_status` is `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` are ignored by this check, so a revoked certification can be replaced immediately.

Whether a certification actually grants anything is captured by `is_effective`, which is true only when `cert_status` is `ACTIVE` **and** `training_completed_at` falls at or before the logical clock **and** `expires_at` falls at or after the logical clock. Both interval endpoints are inclusive, so a certification whose `expires_at` equals the current logical instant exactly is still effective. Certification levels are ranked — `NONE` below `BASIC`, `BASIC` below `ADVANCED`, `ADVANCED` below `SEALED_SYSTEM` — and a part's requirement is satisfied when either the part's `cert_level_required` is `NONE`, or the ordering account holds at least one effective certification whose `oem_code` matches the part's `oem_code` and whose `cert_level` ranks at or above the part's `cert_level_required`. A `SEALED_SYSTEM` certification therefore satisfies an `ADVANCED` requirement, while an `ADVANCED` certification does not satisfy a `SEALED_SYSTEM` requirement. Certifications held by other accounts never count.

A `WarrantyAdmin` may revoke a certification at any time, supplying a `revocation_reason`. Revocation moves `cert_status` from `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED`, or from `EXPIRED` to `REVOKED`, and immediately makes `is_effective` false — which in turn gates new order lines for that OEM and level and blocks warranty-claim submissions that depended on it. `REVOKED` is a terminal lockdown with no further transitions, and attempting to revoke an already-revoked certification is rejected with `ERR_CERT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. Certifications also expire without anyone asking: the periodic account review described in §3.5 moves every `ACTIVE` certification whose `expires_at` lies in the past to `EXPIRED`, again making `is_effective` false.

### 3.3. The Branch Network and Its Inventory Positions

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each distribution **branch** with a `code` (unique across branches, three to six characters, printed on pick tickets), a `name`, and a `region_code` naming the distribution region. Three optional inputs shape how the branch behaves in allocation: `will_call_enabled`, which defaults to true and says whether servicers may pick up at that branch's counter; `cutoff_hour_utc`, an hour from 0 through 23 that defaults to 20, after which an overnight shipment out of that branch incurs a late surcharge; and `overnight_surcharge_cents`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, which is both a per-shipment freight adder and the branch's cost ranking key. A branch is created with `branch_status` equal to `OPEN`; a `PlatformAdmin` may later amend `name`, `will_call_enabled`, `cutoff_hour_utc`, `overnight_surcharge_cents`, or `branch_status`. The three postures are `OPEN`, `COUNT_FREEZE`, and `CLOSED`, and only an `OPEN` branch is ever an allocation candidate — putting a branch into `COUNT_FREEZE` or `CLOSED` silently removes it from future candidate sets without disturbing reservations it already holds.

Stock is tracked as an **inventory position** per branch and part. A `BranchCounterRep` opens one by supplying the `branch_id` and the `part_id`, optionally with an initial `on_hand_units` (defaulting to 0) and a `reorder_point` (defaulting to 0 and used only for replenishment reporting). Only one position may exist for a given branch-and-part pair; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. A fresh position starts with `reserved_units` at zero. The metric allocation actually consults is `available_units`, which is always `on_hand_units` minus `reserved_units`.

Physical counts are corrected by posting a signed adjustment, supplying `delta_units` and an `adjustment_reason`. The delta is added to `on_hand_units`, `available_units` is recomputed, and `last_counted_at` is stamped with the logical clock. An adjustment that would drive `on_hand_units` below zero, or below the units already reserved, is refused with `ERR_INVENTORY_NEGATIVE` — reservations are promises, and a count correction may not break them. Adjustments are also refused when the branch is not `OPEN`.

The two writes that move stock without an adjustment are hand-over and return receipt. Hand-over decrements both `on_hand_units` and `reserved_units` by the shipped quantity, because the units both leave the shelf and stop being reserved. A restockable return receipt increments `on_hand_units` at the receiving branch, creating the branch-and-part position with `on_hand_units` equal to the returned quantity if none existed.

### 3.4. Order Intake: Line Resolution and Quoted Pricing

A `Servicer` (or a `BranchCounterRep` taking the order at the counter) opens a **parts order** by naming the `servicer_account_id` and choosing a `fulfillment_mode` of either `WILL_CALL` or `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP`. The mode dictates which of two location fields must be present: a `WILL_CALL` order must carry a `pickup_branch_id` and must not carry a `ship_to_zip`, while an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order must carry a `ship_to_zip` and must not carry a `pickup_branch_id`. Getting that pairing wrong is rejected with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_MODE_MISMATCH`, and naming a pickup branch whose `will_call_enabled` is false is rejected with `ERR_WILL_CALL_NOT_ENABLED`. The servicer's own `purchase_order_ref` may be supplied and is carried for reference only.

Every order carries **two independent lifecycles on the same row**, and understanding that they are independent is essential. The physical dimension is `fulfillment_status`, which starts at `DRAFT` and ranges over `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, `HELD`, `FULFILLED`, and `CANCELLED`, with `FULFILLED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. The money dimension is `settlement_status`, which starts at `PENDING_CREDIT` and ranges over `PENDING_CREDIT`, `CREDIT_APPROVED`, `CREDIT_DECLINED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID`, and `WRITTEN_OFF`, with `PAID`, `CREDIT_DECLINED`, and `WRITTEN_OFF` terminal. Neither dimension's transitions are triggered by the other; they only read each other as preconditions. A prepaid order can reach `PAID` while its `fulfillment_status` is still `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, or `HELD`. A `hold_reason` of `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING`, `CREDIT_HOLD`, or `BACKORDER` accompanies `HELD` and is null whenever `fulfillment_status` is anything else. A new order starts with `pricing_basis` equal to `ORDER_DATE` and with `subtotal_cents`, `core_deposit_total_cents`, `freight_cents`, and `total_cents` all at zero; `invoiced_total_cents`, `fulfilled_at`, `tracking_ref`, and `credit_decided_by_user_id` all start null.

Lines are added to a `DRAFT` order one at a time. Each add supplies the `requested_part_id` the servicer asked for, a `quantity` of at least one, and the `expected_part_order_version` demanded by §2.6. The line first walks the supersession chain of §3.1 to establish its `resolved_part_id` and `supersession_hops`. It then faces three gates in sequence: the resolved part's `oem_code` must appear in the account's `oem_authorizations`, or the add is rejected with `ERR_OEM_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`; the account must hold an effective certification meeting the resolved part's `cert_level_required`, or the add is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CERT_REQUIRED`; and the account's credit exposure recomputed **with this line included** must not exceed its `credit_limit_cents`, or the add is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. That last check needs care: the order's own total is already inside the exposure aggregate, so the new line's contribution must be folded into that aggregate rather than added on top of it.

A line's `line_status` is one of `REQUESTED`, `RESOLVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED`, `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING`, `SHIPPED`, `RETURNED`, or `CANCELLED`, with `CANCELLED` and `RETURNED` terminal. `REQUESTED` is the initial value but exists only inside the creating request: the resolution above always moves the line onward before the response is written, so no persisted line is ever observed in `REQUESTED`. The normal outcome is `REQUESTED → RESOLVED`. The exception is a chain that terminates on an `OBSOLETE` part: that line goes `REQUESTED → SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` instead, its `resolved_part_id` set to the terminal obsolete part and priced off that part, and a substitute request is opened for it as described in §3.7. Either way the parent order's `part_order_id` links the line back to its order, the order's totals are recomputed, and the order's `version` increments by one.

Quoted pricing runs at line creation on what this document calls the **`ORDER_DATE` basis**, and it works as follows. Take the line's resolved part and the account's current pricing tier. Select the base discount percentage from the tier by the part's `category`: `discount_pct_electrical` for `ELECTRICAL`, `discount_pct_mechanical` for `MECHANICAL`, `discount_pct_cosmetic` for `COSMETIC`, and `discount_pct_sealed_system` for `SEALED_SYSTEM`. Add a volume bonus of 0.02 when the line's `quantity` is ten units or more, and nothing at all when it is fewer than ten. Take the smaller of that sum and 0.45 as the effective discount — no combination of contract discount and volume bonus may ever exceed forty-five percent. Multiply the part's `list_price_cents` by one minus the effective discount and round the result half up to a whole cent; that is the line's `unit_price_cents`. Multiply that rounded unit price by the `quantity`; that is the line's `extended_price_cents`. As stressed in §2.5, the rounding happens on the unit price before the multiplication.

The quoted price is then **locked**. A later price-file publication changes the catalog but never rewrites an existing line's `unit_price_cents` or `extended_price_cents`, and a later tier reassignment never reprices an existing line either. Only hand-over reprices, and it does so into different fields.

Order totals are recomputed after every line add, line cancellation, allocation, substitute decision, and hand-over. The `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `extended_price_cents` over the order's lines whose `line_status` is `RESOLVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED`, `SHIPPED`, or `RETURNED` — which is to say it excludes `CANCELLED` lines and excludes `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` lines, and that exclusion is precisely why an obsolescence cascade lowers an order's total. The `core_deposit_total_cents` is the sum, over lines whose `line_status` is `SHIPPED` or `RETURNED` and whose resolved part has `core_required` true, of that line's `core_deposit_cents` multiplied by its `quantity`; it is zero before hand-over, because core deposits are invoiced rather than quoted. Freight is computed **after** the subtotal, because the free-freight rule reads the subtotal. Finally `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `core_deposit_total_cents` plus `freight_cents`. None of these sums re-rounds an already-rounded line amount.

A `Servicer` may cancel a single line, supplying a `cancellation_reason`. Cancellation is permitted while the line's `line_status` is `RESOLVED`, `ALLOCATED`, or `BACKORDERED` and while the parent order's `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, or `HELD`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_LINE_STATE_INVALID`. The line moves to `CANCELLED`. If it had been `ALLOCATED`, its reservation is compensated exactly: the holding inventory record's `reserved_units` falls by the line's `quantity`, its `available_units` returns to the value it had before the allocation, and the line's `allocation_branch_id` is cleared to null. Any `PENDING` substitute request on that line becomes `WITHDRAWN`. The order's `subtotal_cents`, `freight_cents`, and `total_cents` are then recomputed, followed by the account's `credit_used_cents`, and both the line's and the order's `version` increment.

Cancelling the whole order is available to the `Servicer` that owns it and to a `CreditManager` without ownership restriction, again with a `cancellation_reason`. It is permitted from `DRAFT`, from `ALLOCATED`, and from `HELD`, and in all three cases only while `settlement_status` is not `INVOICED`. The order moves to `CANCELLED`, and the side effects are identical whichever of the three states it started in: every line whose `line_status` is `RESOLVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `BACKORDERED`, or `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` moves to `CANCELLED`, with reservations released for those that had been `ALLOCATED`; every `PENDING` substitute request on those lines becomes `WITHDRAWN`; a `settlement_status` of `CREDIT_APPROVED` is rewritten to `CREDIT_DECLINED`; `subtotal_cents` and `total_cents` are set to zero; and the account's `credit_used_cents` is recomputed.

### 3.5. Credit Decisioning, Payment and Account Standing

Before an order can be allocated, a `CreditManager` must decide it. The decision request carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` and an optional `decision_note`, and it is only available while the order's `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT` and its `settlement_status` is `PENDING_CREDIT`. Approval requires the owning account to be `ACTIVE` and requires the account's credit exposure — which already includes this order's `total_cents` — to be no greater than its `credit_limit_cents`; an exposure exactly equal to the limit passes, one cent more fails with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. An order that has no lines at all approves cleanly at a `total_cents` of zero; the empty-order objection is raised later, at allocation.

Both outcomes stamp the acting operator's `X-User-ID` into `credit_decided_by_user_id` and both refresh the account's `delinquency_score` from the rolling-window variant of the scoring rule below. Approval moves `settlement_status` to `CREDIT_APPROVED` and recomputes the account's `credit_used_cents`. Declining moves `settlement_status` to `CREDIT_DECLINED`, and then does something more: if the freshly computed `delinquency_score` is strictly below 40, the account moves from `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD`, dragging the whole freeze cascade of §3.2 onto that account's other allocated orders.

**Delinquency scoring** starts every account at a base of 80 points and adjusts from there. Fifteen points are deducted for each overdue invoice, twenty for each denied warranty claim, and eight for each credited no-longer-needed return. A bonus of five points is added for each currently effective technician certification, but that bonus is capped at fifteen points no matter how many certifications the account holds. The result is then clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive. The three penalty counts are defined as follows. An overdue invoice is one of the account's orders whose `settlement_status` is `INVOICED` and where the whole days elapsed since its `fulfilled_at` — the elapsed seconds divided by 86400 and floored — exceed the day count implied by the account's `credit_terms`, namely 15 days for `NET_15`, 30 for `NET_30`, 45 for `NET_45`, and 0 for `PREPAY`. A denied warranty claim is one whose `claim_status` is `OEM_DENIED` or `VOID`. A no-longer-needed return is a return authorization whose `ra_status` is `CREDITED` and whose `reason_code` is `NO_LONGER_NEEDED`. The certification bonus counts the account's certifications whose `is_effective` is true at the moment of evaluation.

Two variants of that score coexist, and they differ **only** in which penalty rows they count. The **rolling** variant counts a penalty row only when its anchor timestamp falls within 180 days of the logical clock: the anchor is `fulfilled_at` for an overdue invoice, `submitted_at` for a denied claim, and `received_at` for a no-longer-needed return. Rows older than that window are ignored, so an account that has cleaned up its behaviour scores higher on this variant. The **lifetime** variant applies no window filter at all and counts every qualifying row regardless of age, which means that on identical data it yields a score less than or equal to the rolling variant whenever any penalty row predates the window. The certification bonus is computed identically in both. The rolling variant is what a credit decision uses and what is persisted into `delinquency_score`; the lifetime variant is what the periodic review uses; the credit-summary read of §2.10 reports both side by side as `delinquency_score_rolling` and `delinquency_score_lifetime`.

**Credit exposure** is the account-level aggregate that `credit_used_cents` reflects. Compute the open exposure by walking the account's orders and contributing, per order: `invoiced_total_cents` when `settlement_status` is `INVOICED`; `total_cents` when `settlement_status` is `PENDING_CREDIT` or `CREDIT_APPROVED` **and** `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `ALLOCATED`, or `HELD`; and nothing at all otherwise, which covers `PAID`, `CREDIT_DECLINED`, `WRITTEN_OFF`, and cancelled orders. Then compute the credits as the sum of `credit_amount_cents` over the account's return authorizations whose `ra_status` is `CREDITED`, plus the sum of `credit_amount_cents` over the account's warranty claims whose `claim_status` is `CREDITED`. Subtract the credits from the open exposure — from the aggregate, never from individual orders — and floor the result at zero, so an over-credited account reads exactly 0 rather than a negative number. That floored figure is `credit_used_cents`, and it is recomputed on every line add and cancellation, every allocation, every credit decision, every hand-over, every payment, every return credit, every warranty credit, every substitute decision, and every account status change.

**Payments** are posted by a `CreditManager` supplying `amount_cents` and an optional `payment_reference`, and partial payments are never accepted. For an order whose `settlement_status` is `CREDIT_APPROVED` the amount must equal `total_cents` exactly; for one whose `settlement_status` is `INVOICED` it must equal `invoiced_total_cents` exactly; any other figure is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The payment path from `CREDIT_APPROVED` to `PAID` is how a `PREPAY` account settles before shipment, and the path from `INVOICED` to `PAID` is how a NET-terms account settles after it. Either way `settlement_status` becomes `PAID`, the order leaves the exposure set, and `credit_used_cents` is recomputed.

A payment can also lift a freeze. After the recomputation, if the account's `account_status` is `CREDIT_HOLD` and its `credit_used_cents` has fallen to or below ninety percent of its `credit_limit_cents` — precisely, to or below the floor of `credit_limit_cents` multiplied by 0.90 — the account moves `CREDIT_HOLD → ACTIVE`, which in turn releases that account's orders held for credit reasons back toward `ALLOCATED` with a re-allocation sweep, exactly as described in §3.2.

The **periodic account review** is a single `CreditManager` operation that does four things to one account in one request, and it takes no input at all. First, every `ACTIVE` certification of the account whose `expires_at` lies before the logical clock moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Second, `rolling_12m_volume_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `invoiced_total_cents` over the account's orders whose `settlement_status` is `INVOICED` or `PAID` and whose whole days elapsed since `fulfilled_at` are at most 365; cancelled, written-off, and unfulfilled orders never contribute. Third, the account is reassigned a tier: the eligible tiers are those whose `min_annual_volume_cents` is less than or equal to that volume, and the account's `pricing_tier_id` becomes the eligible tier with the **largest** `min_annual_volume_cents`, breaking ties by taking the smaller `tier_code` in ascending order; if no tier is eligible at all, the account falls to the tier with the **smallest** `min_annual_volume_cents`, breaking ties the same way. An account sitting exactly on a tier's floor qualifies for that tier. Existing lines are not repriced by a reassignment — only future line creations and future hand-over invoicing see the new tier. Fourth, `delinquency_score` is refreshed from the **lifetime** variant, and if that score is strictly below 40 the account moves from `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD` with the full freeze cascade.

### 3.6. Allocation, Freight and Hand-Over

Allocation is a `BranchCounterRep` operation on a credit-cleared order, and it takes no request body. The order must be in `DRAFT` with a `settlement_status` of either `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `PAID`, its account must be `ACTIVE`, and it must have at least one line whose `line_status` is not `CANCELLED` — an order with nothing left in it is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES` rather than allocating an empty shipment. The acting branch matters: for a `WILL_CALL` order the caller's `X-User-ID` must be the order's `pickup_branch_id`, that branch must be `OPEN`, and its `will_call_enabled` must be true; for an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order the caller's `X-User-ID` must resolve to a branch whose `branch_status` is `OPEN`.

The sweep then processes the order's lines whose `line_status` is `RESOLVED`, one at a time, in ascending `created_at` order, and **applies each accepted reservation before ranking the next line**. That sequencing has a visible consequence: an earlier line can consume the depth at a branch that a later line would have preferred, pushing the later line to a different branch or to `BACKORDERED` entirely.

Selecting a branch for one line works like this. The candidate set is every inventory position whose `part_id` equals the line's `resolved_part_id`, whose `available_units` is greater than or equal to the line's full `quantity`, and whose branch has `branch_status` equal to `OPEN`. Partial fills are never made — a position holding fewer units than the line needs is simply not a candidate. For a `WILL_CALL` order the candidate set is further narrowed to the single position at the order's `pickup_branch_id`, and that branch must additionally have `will_call_enabled` true. If the candidate set is empty the line becomes `BACKORDERED`, its `allocation_branch_id` stays null, and no inventory is touched. Otherwise the candidates are ranked by three keys in strict order: `available_units` descending, so the deepest stock is drawn first; then the branch's `overnight_surcharge_cents` ascending, so the cheapest branch to ship from wins a tie on depth; then the branch's `created_at` ascending as the deterministic final tie-break. The first candidate after that ranking wins.

Each line that finds a branch moves `RESOLVED → ALLOCATED`, records the winning branch as its `allocation_branch_id`, raises that inventory record's `reserved_units` by the line's `quantity`, has its `available_units` recomputed, and increments that inventory record's `version`. Each line that finds nothing moves `RESOLVED → BACKORDERED`. The order then recomputes `freight_cents` and `total_cents`, and the account's `credit_used_cents` is recomputed.

The order's own outcome depends on the sweep. If at least one line ended `ALLOCATED`, the order moves `DRAFT → ALLOCATED`. If **every** non-cancelled line ended `BACKORDERED`, the order does not become `ALLOCATED` at all — it moves `DRAFT → HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `BACKORDER`, no `reserved_units` anywhere change, and `freight_cents` is set to zero before `total_cents` is recomputed. Every other precondition for the two outcomes is identical; only the "at least one line reserved" test distinguishes them.

**Freight** is assessed at allocation, again at hand-over, after a line cancellation, and during any re-allocation sweep. A `WILL_CALL` order never carries freight at all: its `freight_cents` is zero, full stop, because the customer collects. For an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order, four buckets are computed and then one waiver is applied. The shipment weight is the sum, over the order's lines whose `line_status` is `ALLOCATED` or `SHIPPED`, of the resolved part's `weight_grams` multiplied by the line's `quantity`; lines sitting in `BACKORDERED` or `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` contribute neither weight nor branch surcharge. The base bucket is a flat 1,495 cents. The weight bucket takes the shipment weight in grams, divides by 5,000 grams (five kilograms) to a **rounded-up** whole number of blocks, and charges 250 cents per block. The surcharge bucket takes the distinct `allocation_branch_id` values of those same lines and charges the **largest** `overnight_surcharge_cents` among their branches — one truck per order, so the worst branch sets the price. The late bucket charges 500 cents when the UTC hour of the logical clock is greater than or equal to the **smallest** `cutoff_hour_utc` among those same branches, and nothing otherwise.

The waiver is where implementations go wrong. If the order's `subtotal_cents` is at least 50,000 cents (five hundred dollars), the base, weight, and surcharge buckets are all waived and `freight_cents` is the late bucket alone — the late-cutoff charge **survives** free freight. Otherwise `freight_cents` is the sum of all four buckets. The waiver is applied after the four buckets are computed, and it waives exactly three of them: returning zero outright, or waiving all four, are both wrong.

**Hand-over** is the `BranchCounterRep` operation that ships or physically releases an allocated order, optionally recording a carrier `tracking_ref`. The order must be `ALLOCATED` and its account must be `ACTIVE`. Settlement must be far enough along, and how far depends on terms: an account whose `credit_terms` is `PREPAY` must already have `settlement_status` equal to `PAID`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PREPAY_REQUIRED`, while an account on `NET_15`, `NET_30`, or `NET_45` needs only `CREDIT_APPROVED`. The acting branch must match: for a `WILL_CALL` order the caller's `X-User-ID` must be the `pickup_branch_id`, and for an `OVERNIGHT_DROP_SHIP` order it must equal the `allocation_branch_id` of at least one of the order's `ALLOCATED` lines. Finally, core liability is checked: the account's current `core_liability_cents` plus the sum, over the order's `ALLOCATED` lines whose resolved part has `core_required` true, of that part's `core_deposit_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`, must not exceed the account's `core_liability_limit_cents`; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_CORE_LIABILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Hand-over then does a great deal at once. The order's `pricing_basis` flips from `ORDER_DATE` to `SHIP_DATE`. Every `ALLOCATED` line moves `ALLOCATED → SHIPPED` and is repriced on the **`SHIP_DATE` basis**: the same discount arithmetic as §3.4 runs again, but reading the resolved part's **current** `list_price_cents` and the account's **current** pricing tier, and writing the result into `invoiced_unit_price_cents` rather than `unit_price_cents`. Identical line data therefore yields a different amount whenever a price file published between order date and ship date, and the quoted fields stay untouched as a record of what was promised. Each shipping line also snapshots `core_deposit_cents` from its resolved part when that part has `core_required` true, and 0 when it does not. Each shipping line decrements its holding inventory record's `on_hand_units` and `reserved_units` by the line's `quantity`. The order recomputes `core_deposit_total_cents` and `freight_cents`, then locks `invoiced_total_cents` as the ship-date subtotal plus `core_deposit_total_cents` plus `freight_cents`, and records `price_variance_cents` as `invoiced_total_cents` minus `total_cents` — zero for as long as `invoiced_total_cents` is null. The order's `fulfillment_status` becomes `FULFILLED`, `fulfilled_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the supplied `tracking_ref` is stored. The account's `core_liability_cents` rises by each shipped line's `core_deposit_cents` multiplied by its `quantity` for core-required parts, and `credit_used_cents` is recomputed.

Lines left in `BACKORDERED` or `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` are **not** shipped, are not modified, and remain queryable on the fulfilled order. And on the money dimension, an order for an account on NET terms moves `CREDIT_APPROVED → INVOICED` in the same request, at which point its contribution to exposure switches from `total_cents` to `invoiced_total_cents`; a `PREPAY` order, already `PAID`, stays `PAID`.

`fulfilled_at` is the anchor for everything that follows: return windows, warranty windows, overdue-invoice counting, and rolling volume all measure from it.

**Core liability** is maintained as its own account-level aggregate. It is the sum, over the account's lines whose `line_status` is `SHIPPED` or `RETURNED` and whose resolved part has `core_required` true, of the line's `core_deposit_cents` multiplied by its `quantity`, minus the sum of `core_deposit_refund_cents` over the account's return authorizations whose `ra_status` is `CREDITED` and whose `reason_code` is `CORE_RETURN`. Because the refund is computed from the line's snapshotted deposit rather than the part's current one, a part shipped with a deposit and later core-returned in full brings the account's `core_liability_cents` back to exactly its pre-shipment value, and a price file that changes the deposit in between cannot break that.

### 3.7. Obsolescence: Substitute Sourcing, Price-File Publication and Rollback

A **substitute request** is opened whenever an order line ends up pointing at an obsolete part, and there are exactly two ways that happens. The first is line resolution: a supersession walk that terminates on a part whose `lifecycle_status` is `OBSOLETE` leaves the line in `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING`, priced off that terminal part, and opens a request whose `obsolete_part_id` is that part and whose `source` is `CHAIN_TERMINAL_OBSOLETE`. The second is a price file turning a part obsolete under an order that is already open, in which case `source` is `PRICE_FILE_OBSOLESCENCE` and `created_by_price_file_id` names the publishing file. A request starts with `request_status` equal to `PENDING`, with `proposed_substitute_part_id` and `price_variance_pct` both null, and with `decided_by_user_id` null; the `order_line_id` ties it to its line. `PENDING` is the only non-terminal state; `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

A `WarrantyAdmin` decides a pending request by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally a `decision_note`, and — on an approval only — the `substitute_part_id` being offered. An approval demands that the substitute be a different part from the obsolete one, that its `lifecycle_status` be `ACTIVE`, and that its `oem_code` equal the obsolete part's `oem_code`; a rejection must supply no substitute at all. Violating any of that is rejected with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_PART_INVALID`. An approval also demands that the substitute be priced comparably. The **price variance** is the substitute part's current `list_price_cents` minus the obsolete part's current `list_price_cents`, divided by the obsolete part's current `list_price_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places. It is signed, so a cheaper substitute produces a negative figure, and the cap is applied to its absolute value: a variance whose magnitude is strictly greater than 0.25 is rejected with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_PRICE_VARIANCE`, which means a substitute more than twenty-five percent more expensive **and** a substitute more than twenty-five percent cheaper are both refused, while a magnitude of exactly 0.2500 is accepted. Note that this arithmetic reads the two parts' current catalog list prices, not the order's quoted price.

The two branches diverge sharply in their effects, and both stamp `decided_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. On approval, `request_status` moves `PENDING → APPROVED`, `proposed_substitute_part_id` is set to the offered part, `price_variance_pct` is written, and the line moves `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING → RESOLVED` with its `resolved_part_id` rewritten to the substitute and its `unit_price_cents` and `extended_price_cents` recomputed off the substitute; the line's `supersession_hops` is left unchanged, because a substitution is not a supersession hop. On rejection, `request_status` moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, `proposed_substitute_part_id` stays null, `price_variance_pct` stays null, and the line moves `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING → CANCELLED` with its `extended_price_cents` set to zero. Either way the order's `subtotal_cents` and `total_cents` are recomputed and the account's `credit_used_cents` follows.

A `Servicer` may instead withdraw its own pending request, supplying a `withdrawal_reason`; ownership is checked against the owning order's `servicer_account_id`. Withdrawal moves `request_status` to `WITHDRAWN` and moves the line `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING → BACKORDERED`, leaving it priced off the obsolete part while the servicer sources it elsewhere. Requests are also withdrawn on the servicer's behalf in two situations: cancelling an order withdraws every pending request on its lines, and rolling back a price file withdraws every request that publication created — in the rollback case the line returns `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING → RESOLVED` instead of going to `BACKORDERED`, because its part has become `ACTIVE` again and its `unit_price_cents` was never disturbed.

In all of the above, once **no** `PENDING` request remains anywhere on an order that is sitting in `HELD`, and provided the owning account is `ACTIVE`, the order is released: it moves `HELD → ALLOCATED` with `hold_reason` cleared to null, and a re-allocation sweep runs over its lines whose `line_status` is `RESOLVED` or `BACKORDERED`, taking each to `ALLOCATED` with a fresh reservation where the branch selection succeeds and leaving it `BACKORDERED` where it does not. Freight and totals are recomputed, and the account's exposure follows. If not a single line manages to reserve, the order stays `HELD`.

**Price files** are how the catalog is repriced in bulk. A `PricingAdmin` opens one with a `file_version` (unique across price files and greater than zero) and an `effective_at` instant, optionally with an `effective_until` instant. The window is **half-open**: it covers `effective_at` and everything up to but not including `effective_until`. An `effective_until` that is present but not strictly after `effective_at` is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_INTERVAL_INVALID`; a null `effective_until` means open-ended. A new file starts with `price_file_status` equal to `DRAFT`, a `line_count` of zero, and `published_at` null. Its lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, then `PUBLISHED`, and then either `SUPERSEDED` or `ROLLED_BACK`, both terminal.

Each **price-file line** targets one part with the `price_file_id` of its owning draft file, the `part_id` being repriced, a `new_list_price_cents` greater than zero, and two optional instructions: `new_core_deposit_cents`, which when null leaves the part's existing deposit untouched, and `new_lifecycle_status`, which when null leaves the part's lifecycle untouched and otherwise names one of `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `OBSOLETE`, or `NLA`. Only one line per part per file is allowed. Adding a line increments the file's `line_count` and its `version`. Three snapshot columns — `previous_list_price_cents`, `previous_core_deposit_cents`, and `previous_lifecycle_status` — stay null until publication.

Publication is gated on three things beyond the file being `DRAFT`: it must have at least one line, its `effective_at` must be at or before the logical clock (a file whose window has not opened yet is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_FILE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`), and its window must not collide with a published file on any shared part. **Window reconciliation** works per shared part, and only against files whose `price_file_status` is `PUBLISHED` — draft, superseded, and rolled-back files are ignored entirely, and files touching disjoint parts never collide however their windows sit. Treating a null `effective_until` as unbounded on the right, an existing published file and the publishing file conflict when the existing file's `effective_at` falls strictly before the publishing file's window end **and** the publishing file's `effective_at` falls strictly before the existing file's window end. There is one licensed exception to that conflict: if the existing file's `effective_until` is null and its `effective_at` falls strictly before the publishing file's `effective_at`, the existing file is simply **auto-closed** — its `effective_until` is written to the publishing file's `effective_at` and it moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`, leaving the two windows adjacent with zero gap. Any other conflict is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the earlier window excludes its own end instant, a file ending exactly where another begins does not collide at all.

Publication itself is a four-hop cascade. In the first hop, every line snapshots the part's current values into `previous_list_price_cents`, `previous_core_deposit_cents`, and `previous_lifecycle_status`, then writes `new_list_price_cents` into the part's `list_price_cents`, writes `new_core_deposit_cents` into its `core_deposit_cents` where supplied, and writes `new_lifecycle_status` into its `lifecycle_status` where supplied, incrementing the part's `version`. In the second hop, for every part whose `lifecycle_status` has just become `OBSOLETE`, every order line whose `resolved_part_id` is that part and whose `line_status` is `RESOLVED` or `ALLOCATED` moves to `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING`; a line that had been `ALLOCATED` releases its reservation, lowering the holding inventory record's `reserved_units` by the line's `quantity` and clearing its `allocation_branch_id` to null; and a `PENDING` substitute request is opened for each such line with `source` equal to `PRICE_FILE_OBSOLESCENCE` and `created_by_price_file_id` naming this file. In the third hop, each of those lines' orders moves `ALLOCATED → HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING`, and their `subtotal_cents` and `total_cents` are recomputed with the now-`SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` lines excluded. In the fourth hop, each of those orders' accounts has `credit_used_cents` recomputed. The file's own `price_file_status` becomes `PUBLISHED` and `published_at` is stamped with the logical clock. What publication emphatically does **not** do is reprice existing order lines: every `unit_price_cents` already quoted stays exactly as it was, and only hand-over reprices, on the `SHIP_DATE` basis.

Rollback undoes all of it. A `PricingAdmin` rolls back a `PUBLISHED` file by supplying a `rollback_reason`, and it is permitted only while every substitute request that file created still has `request_status` equal to `PENDING` — if any of them has already been decided or withdrawn the rollback is refused with `ERR_PRICE_FILE_STATE_INVALID`. Rollback replays each line's snapshot back onto its part, restoring `list_price_cents`, `core_deposit_cents`, and `lifecycle_status` and incrementing the part's `version`; moves every substitute request this file created from `PENDING` to `WITHDRAWN` and its line from `SUBSTITUTE_PENDING` back to `RESOLVED`; releases every order this publication had put on hold from `HELD` back to `ALLOCATED` with a re-allocation sweep and recomputed totals; and returns every file this publication had auto-closed to `PUBLISHED` with its `effective_until` set back to null. The file's own `price_file_status` becomes `ROLLED_BACK`. As stated in §2.9, the catalog's total list price is conserved across a publish-then-rollback pair.

### 3.8. Warranty Claims Filed with the OEM

When a part fails under OEM warranty, the distributor files the claim on the servicer's behalf. A `WarrantyAdmin` opens a claim by naming the `servicer_account_id`, the `order_line_id` of the shipped line whose part failed, the OEM's `defect_code`, and the `appliance_model_number` of the appliance the part was installed in. The targeted line must have a `line_status` of `SHIPPED` or `RETURNED`, and the account must hold the relevant OEM authorization. The claim's `oem_code` is copied from the line's resolved part at creation and is never supplied by the client. A new claim has `claim_status` equal to `DRAFT`, `resubmission_count` at zero, and `submitted_at`, `oem_reference`, `claim_amount_cents`, and `credit_amount_cents` all unset.

Submission moves the claim `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and optionally records the OEM-side `oem_reference`. Three conditions gate it beyond the claim being `DRAFT`: the account must be `ACTIVE`, the whole days elapsed since the order's `fulfilled_at` must be at most 90, and the account must hold an effective certification for the claim's `oem_code`. Exceeding the ninety-day window is rejected with `ERR_WARRANTY_PERIOD_EXPIRED`; lacking the certification is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CERT_REQUIRED`, which makes a revocation by the warranty desk immediately felt on the claim side as well as the ordering side. Submission stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock and **locks** the claim amount: `claim_amount_cents` is the line's `invoiced_unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity` minus its `returned_units`, so that the OEM is never billed for units the distributor has already taken back for credit. That figure reads the invoiced unit price, never the quoted one, and it is frozen at first submission — units returned afterwards do not change it, and a resubmission does not recompute it.

The OEM's answer is recorded as an `outcome` of `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`, with an optional `oem_reference` and an optional `oem_note`, and it is only accepted while the claim is `SUBMITTED`. `APPROVED` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → OEM_APPROVED` and updates `oem_reference` when one is supplied. `DENIED` moves it `SUBMITTED → OEM_DENIED`, updates `oem_reference` when supplied, and quietly changes the account's risk profile: from that moment the claim counts as a denied claim in both delinquency variants. `INFO_REQUESTED` moves it `SUBMITTED → OEM_INFO_REQUESTED` and updates `oem_reference` when supplied, leaving `claim_amount_cents` untouched.

Resubmission closes the cycle. A `WarrantyAdmin` resubmits a claim sitting in `OEM_INFO_REQUESTED` by supplying `additional_info`; the claim moves `OEM_INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, its `resubmission_count` increments by one, and `submitted_at` is restamped with the logical clock. The cycle is bounded: resubmission is permitted only while `resubmission_count` is strictly below 2, so a claim may make the round trip at most twice, and the third attempt is rejected with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Closing an adjudicated claim is a `CreditManager` operation with no request body, and it is the only path into either terminal state. An `OEM_APPROVED` claim moves `OEM_APPROVED → CREDITED` with `credit_amount_cents` set to the locked `claim_amount_cents`, and the account's `credit_used_cents` is recomputed — a credited claim reduces exposure. An `OEM_DENIED` claim moves `OEM_DENIED → VOID` with `credit_amount_cents` set to 0 and no change to exposure at all; the denied-claim penalty in the delinquency score persists past voiding. Both `CREDITED` and `VOID` are terminal, and any further action on such a claim is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Returns, Restocking Fees and Core Deposit Settlement

A **return authorization** is raised by a `Servicer` (or by a `BranchCounterRep` at the counter) against a shipped line, naming the `servicer_account_id`, the `order_line_id`, the `quantity` to send back, a `reason_code`, and the `return_branch_id` of the branch that will physically receive the goods. The four reason codes are `WRONG_PART`, `DEFECTIVE_ON_ARRIVAL`, `NO_LONGER_NEEDED`, and `CORE_RETURN`, and the reason drives fee waiver, restock eligibility, and which window applies. A new authorization has `ra_status` equal to `REQUESTED`, `fee_waived` false, `created_by_user_id` stamped with the raising operator's `X-User-ID`, `decided_by_user_id` and `received_at` null, and `restocking_fee_pct` null.

Three admissions tests apply at creation, in this order. First, returnability: the resolved part's `is_returnable` must be true, so an `ELECTRICAL` part or a part whose `lifecycle_status` is `NLA` is refused with `ERR_PART_NON_RETURNABLE`, and no `reason_code` — not even `DEFECTIVE_ON_ARRIVAL` — lifts that prohibition. Second, quantity: the requested `quantity` plus the quantities already committed to that line's other authorizations in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `RECEIVED`, or `CREDITED` must not exceed the line's `quantity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RETURN_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED`; core returns are counted in their own class, independent of goods returns, because they release deposits rather than units. Third, the window.

The window test is computed from a figure the authorization then keeps forever. `days_since_fulfilled_at_request` is the whole days between the order's `fulfilled_at` and the logical clock at the moment the authorization is raised — the difference in epoch seconds divided by 86400 and floored. The applicable window is 60 days when the `reason_code` is `CORE_RETURN` and 30 days for every other reason. A day count strictly greater than the window is refused with `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`; a count exactly equal to the window is still inside it and is accepted. Because the day count is snapshotted at creation, later movements of the logical clock cannot change which fee tier this authorization eventually lands in. An unfulfilled order has a null `fulfilled_at` and never gets this far — its line is not `SHIPPED`, so the request has already been refused with `ERR_LINE_NOT_SHIPPED`.

At creation the authorization also fixes its `gross_return_cents`: the line's `invoiced_unit_price_cents` multiplied by the authorization's `quantity` for a goods return, and exactly 0 for a `CORE_RETURN`, because a core return refunds the deposit and never the part price.

A `CreditManager` then decides the authorization with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, an optional `decision_note`, and an optional `waive_restocking_fee` flag. Where the flag is true, the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.8 applies and the deciding operator must differ from the order's `credit_decided_by_user_id`. The two branches diverge as sharply as they do for substitutes, and both stamp `decided_by_user_id`. Rejection moves the authorization `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, sets `restocking_fee_pct` to null, sets `restocking_fee_cents`, `credit_amount_cents`, and `core_deposit_refund_cents` all to 0, and produces no inventory, exposure, or core-liability effect whatsoever; `REJECTED` is terminal. Approval moves it `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, records `fee_waived` from the supplied flag, and computes all four money figures.

The **restocking fee** is computed as follows. Start from the reason: when `reason_code` is `DEFECTIVE_ON_ARRIVAL` or `CORE_RETURN`, the rate is 0. Otherwise consult the pinned `days_since_fulfilled_at_request`: a count of 15 days or fewer gives a rate of 0.10, and a count above 15 gives 0.20. Then, if the authorization's `gross_return_cents` is at least 100,000 cents (one thousand dollars) **and** the rate so far is greater than zero, add 0.05 to the rate — a large return costs the servicer an extra five points, but a rate already at zero stays at zero. Finally, if `fee_waived` is true, the rate collapses to 0 regardless of everything above. That rate is `restocking_fee_pct`. Multiply it by `gross_return_cents` and round half up to a whole cent to get `restocking_fee_cents`, then take `credit_amount_cents` as `gross_return_cents` minus `restocking_fee_cents` — an exact integer remainder, so fee plus credit always reconstitutes the gross exactly. Every percentage in this rule is keyed on `gross_return_cents`, the amount **before** the fee is deducted; keying the large-return adder or the tier lookup on the post-fee credit would select a different branch and produce a different fee. Separately, `core_deposit_refund_cents` is the line's snapshotted `core_deposit_cents` multiplied by the authorization's `quantity` when the `reason_code` is `CORE_RETURN`, and 0 otherwise — so a core return has a zero gross, a zero fee, a zero credit, and a non-zero deposit refund.

Physical receipt is a `BranchCounterRep` operation with no request body, permitted only while `ra_status` is `APPROVED`, only by the branch named in `return_branch_id`, and only while that branch is `OPEN`. It stamps `received_at` with the logical clock and restocks conditionally: `WRONG_PART` and `NO_LONGER_NEEDED` raise `on_hand_units` at the receiving branch's position for the resolved part by the authorization's `quantity`, creating that branch-and-part position with `on_hand_units` equal to the quantity if none exists; `DEFECTIVE_ON_ARRIVAL` does not restock, because the unit is scrapped; and `CORE_RETURN` does not restock either, because the core ships onward to the OEM.

Crediting happens in the very same request. The authorization moves `RECEIVED → CREDITED` immediately, and five hops follow. For a non-core reason, the line's `returned_units` rises by the authorization's `quantity`, and when `returned_units` reaches the line's full `quantity` the line moves `SHIPPED → RETURNED`; a partial return leaves the line in `SHIPPED` with `returned_units` below `quantity`. Second, the account's `credit_used_cents` falls by `credit_amount_cents` and its `core_liability_cents` falls by `core_deposit_refund_cents`. Third, if the account's `account_status` is `CREDIT_HOLD` and its exposure has now fallen to or below the floor of `credit_limit_cents` multiplied by 0.90, the account moves `CREDIT_HOLD → ACTIVE`. Fourth, that release moves every order of the account sitting in `HELD` with `hold_reason` equal to `CREDIT_HOLD` back toward `ALLOCATED`. Fifth, the re-allocation sweep on those orders re-reserves branch inventory line by line, leaving lines that still find no stock in `BACKORDERED` and leaving orders where no line could reserve in `HELD` with `hold_reason` rewritten to `BACKORDER`. `CREDITED` is terminal.
