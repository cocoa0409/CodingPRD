# SMA Model Delivery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Separately Managed Accounts sit at an awkward junction of the wealth-management industry. An asset manager authors a model portfolio; a sponsor broker-dealer distributes it onto its platform; and thousands of individual client accounts must then be dragged toward that model one at a time, each carrying its own restrictions, its own tax lots, its own cash balance and its own tolerance for tracking error. Today that translation step is performed by spreadsheets, overnight batch files and phone calls, and the industry pays for it in implementation lag, in wash-sale violations that surface only at year end, and in performance dispersion between accounts that were supposedly managed identically.

This document specifies the SMA Model Delivery platform: a single service that carries a model from the manager's desk through compliance review, publication, sponsor distribution, per-account implementation, street execution and allocation, tax-lot accounting, tax-loss harvesting, fee billing with a sponsor revenue split, and finally cross-account dispersion reporting. Every one of those stages is transactional and auditable. The platform's core promise is that no share, no cent and no realized loss is ever created or destroyed by an operational mistake: allocations can be unwound, invoices can be voided, and both compensations restore the ledgers they touched to their exact prior values.

The service is mounted under `/sma`, and the intended audience of this document is the engineering team that will build it. Where this document states a rounding order, an evaluation order, or an inclusive-versus-exclusive boundary, that statement is a hard requirement rather than a suggestion — the entire settlement chain depends on two independent implementations arriving at the same integer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is missing, is present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five roles enumerated below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Authentication is decided before anything else about the request is examined — before the URL is resolved, before the body is parsed.

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `PlatformAdmin` — the operations desk of the SMA platform. It owns the instrument master, street execution and allocation, and the logical clock.
- `PortfolioManager` — asset-manager staff who author, submit and publish model versions, distribute them onto sponsor platforms, and drive tax-loss harvesting.
- `ComplianceOfficer` — the independent reviewer. This role approves or rejects model versions, owns account restrictions, freezes and unfreezes accounts, and gates harvest proposals.
- `SponsorOperator` — sponsor-platform operations staff, who open and fund accounts, activate distributions, run implementations, rebalances and drift evaluations, cancel orders and assemble street blocks.
- `BillingOfficer` — owns the entire fee-invoice lifecycle, from computation through issue, payment, void, delinquency and write-off.

Once the caller is authenticated, the role is checked against the set of roles authorized for the requested operation, listed in the endpoint table of §4. A caller whose role is not listed there is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Read operations are deliberately open: every one of the five roles may issue every read.

**Identity semantics differ by role, and this matters.** When `X-User-Role` is `SponsorOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Sponsor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Sponsor` compare the resource's sponsor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, on a mutating endpoint a `SponsorOperator` may act only within its own sponsor: the account's `sponsor_id` for every account-scoped write, the distribution's `sponsor_id` when activating a distribution, the `sponsor_id` supplied in the body when opening an account, and the sponsor of every member order's account when assembling a block. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `PortfolioManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `BillingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a `ModelVersion`, or as `approved_by_user_id` on a `HarvestProposal`, where the relevant transition writes it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

One further identity rule cuts across the model-approval workflow: **segregation of duties**. The `X-User-ID` that submitted a model version for review may never be the `X-User-ID` that approves or rejects that same version, even when the second request legitimately carries the `ComplianceOfficer` role. Such an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A supplied value outside those bounds is a request-validation failure.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two collections carry a documented tie-breaker because they are written in bursts within a single operation and can share a timestamp: trade-order listings and tax-lot listings break `created_at` ties by `sequence_number` ascending. No list ordering ever consults `id`. For every list response, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Audit Trail

Every persisted resource in this system — instruments, sponsors, models, model versions, distributions, accounts, restrictions, trade orders, blocks, tax lots, harvest proposals and fee invoices alike — carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a downstream consequence of some other operation. There are no exceptions in this domain: no entity carries only a partial housekeeping set.

That last clause deserves emphasis because this platform mutates rows indirectly with unusual frequency. When publishing a model version notifies an account, the account's `version` increases and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though no one addressed a request to that account. When a block allocation opens a tax lot and debits cash, the account's `version` moves. When a restriction is lifted, both the restriction row and its owning account advance. Any client holding a cached copy of a row must therefore assume its `version` can move underneath it, which is precisely the property the concurrency rules of §2.6 exploit.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this platform is logical, not wall-clock. A single server-wide instant governs every deadline computation, every wash-sale window, every effective window comparison, every billing period boundary and every implementation-lag measurement. Before any caller sets it, that instant reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may advance or reset the clock through the platform's test-driving capability, supplying the new instant as `to_iso8601`; the response echoes the resulting instant as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes nothing — stored drift figures, stored fee figures and stored valuations all remain exactly as they were until the next business operation reads them.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder

Many operations in this platform can fail for several reasons at once. A rebalance can be requested by the wrong sponsor, on a suspended account, whose fees have been written off, that is not drifted, and that would generate no tradable delta — all simultaneously. To make the API deterministic, every request is evaluated against a single fixed ladder of twenty-eight checks in ascending order, and the **first** check that fails determines the response. No later check may mask an earlier one, and no earlier check may be skipped because a later one looks more specific.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Sponsor scope.** When the acting role is `SponsorOperator` on a mutating endpoint, `X-User-ID` must equal the `Sponsor` `id` that owns the target resource. Otherwise `ERR_SPONSOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This check guards exactly ten operations: account creation, cash deposits, transfer-in booking, implementation, drift evaluation, rebalance, performance-observation recording, distribution activation, block creation, and trade-order cancellation.
4. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the target model version's `submitted_by_user_id`; a submitter may never clear or reject their own submission. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This check guards exactly two operations: model-version approval and model-version rejection.
5. **Path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the request path resolves to an existing row, validated strictly left to right. The failure code names the missing type: `ERR_SECURITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESTRICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRADE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HARVEST_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
6. **Body reference existence.** Every reference identifier carried in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are listed in that endpoint's input. The failure code is one of `ERR_SECURITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRADE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. This check guards nine operations: model-version creation, sleeve replacement, distribution creation, account creation, transfer-in booking, restriction creation, block creation, harvest-proposal creation and fee-invoice creation.
7. **Request shape.** Every required input field is present; every value matches its declared type, its enum membership and its numeric range; `max_position_bps` is present when `restriction_type` is `MAX_POSITION` and absent for the other two restriction types; `period_end` is strictly later than `period_start`; and `effective_until`, when it is not null, is strictly later than `effective_from`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that carries a request body.
8. **Stale parent version.** The supplied expected-version field equals the parent row's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). This check guards exactly two operations: distribution creation and harvest-proposal creation. §2.7 specifies the mechanism.
9. **Account not suspended.** The target account — or, for block creation, *every* member order's account — has `implementation_status` other than `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` (409). This check guards exactly ten operations: cash deposits, transfer-in booking, implementation, drift evaluation, rebalance, block creation, block allocation, harvest-proposal creation, harvest-proposal approval and harvest-proposal execution.
10. **Account not written off.** The target account has `billing_status` other than `WRITTEN_OFF`. Otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_WRITTEN_OFF` (409). This check guards exactly eight operations: cash deposits, transfer-in booking, implementation, drift evaluation, rebalance, reinstatement, harvest-proposal creation and fee-invoice creation.
11. **From-state.** The subject row occupies the from-state that the requested transition declares. Opening an account against a model that has never published a version fails with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_PUBLISHED`; requesting a rebalance on an account whose `implementation_status` is not `DRIFTED` fails with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_DRIFTED`; every other from-state mismatch, including requesting an implementation on an account that is not in `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. All three are HTTP 409. Applies to every transition endpoint.
12. **Referenced sibling state.** The referenced sibling row is in the state the operation requires. Distribution creation and distribution activation both require the referenced model version to be `PUBLISHED`, failing otherwise with `ERR_MODEL_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`; an implementation requires an `ACTIVE` distribution covering the current clock instant that links the account's sponsor to the account's pending version, failing otherwise with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Both are HTTP 409. This check guards those three operations.
13. **Restricted securities.** No `BUY` order in a block being assembled targets a security that is restricted for its account. Otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_RESTRICTED` (422). Guards block creation only.
14. **Wash-sale coordination.** Both clauses of the wash-window geometry described in §2.10 hold. Otherwise `ERR_WASH_SALE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (422). This check guards two operations: harvest-proposal creation and block creation.
15. **Distribution window overlap.** The new distribution's effective window does not collide with any existing `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` distribution on the same sponsor-and-model key. Otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Guards distribution creation only.
16. **Billing period overlap.** The new invoice's billing period does not collide with any non-`VOIDED` invoice of the same account. Otherwise `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Guards fee-invoice creation only.
17. **Payment term elapsed.** The current logical clock instant is at or after the invoice's `period_end` plus 30 days. Otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Guards the delinquency declaration only.
18. **Unique keys.** The declared unique key is free — `ticker` for an instrument, `platform_code` for a sponsor, `strategy_code` for a model, `account_number` for an account. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409). This check guards four creation operations: instrument, sponsor, model and account.
19. **Balanced weights.** The model version carries at least one sleeve and its `total_target_weight_bps` equals exactly 10000. Otherwise `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_BALANCED` (422). Guards model-version submission only.
20. **Block composition.** The `trade_order_ids` list is non-empty, every listed order is in status `PROPOSED`, and all listed orders share one `security_id` and one `side`. Otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_COMPOSITION_INVALID` (422). Guards block creation only.
21. **Fill magnitude.** The reported `filled_shares` is at least 1 and at most the block's `requested_shares`. Otherwise `ERR_FILL_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` (422). Guards block execution only.
22. **Unwind safety.** Every tax lot whose `opening_trade_order_id` is a member order of the block is still in status `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_UNWIND_BLOCKED` (409). Guards block unwind only.
23. **Non-empty cohort.** The dispersion cohort contains at least one account. Otherwise `ERR_DISPERSION_SET_EMPTY` (422). Guards the dispersion read only.
24. **Sponsor minimum.** The account's `market_value_cents` is greater than or equal to its sponsor's `min_account_value_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_BELOW_SPONSOR_MINIMUM` (422). Guards implementation only.
25. **Sufficient shares.** The account holds at least the requested share count across its `OPEN` tax lots of that security, so the disposal walk can complete. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES` (422). This check guards two operations: harvest-proposal creation and harvest-proposal execution.
26. **Harvest budget.** The account's `tax_overlay_enabled` flag is true, and its `realized_loss_ytd_cents` plus the loss figure produced by the completed disposal walk is less than or equal to its `harvest_budget_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_HARVEST_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). This check guards two operations: harvest-proposal creation and harvest-proposal execution.
27. **Sufficient cash.** Every `BUY` allocation is covered by its account's `cash_cents`, and an invoice payment is covered by the paying account's `cash_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH` (422). This check guards two operations: block allocation and invoice payment.
28. **Non-empty generation.** The order generator produced at least one trade order. Otherwise `ERR_NO_TRADES_GENERATED` (422). This check guards two operations: rebalance and implementation.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out, because they are easy to get backwards. A suspended account whose fees were also written off reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_ACCOUNT_WRITTEN_OFF`, because rung 9 precedes rung 10. And a harvest proposal that both requests more shares than the account holds and would blow the harvest budget reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`, because rung 25 precedes rung 26 — the budget cannot be evaluated until the disposal walk has completed anyway.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Rows

Two creation operations in this platform are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* row rather than against themselves. In both cases the guarded write is one that no state precondition can reject — it is legitimate on its face — and the only thing that makes a particular attempt wrong is that the client computed it from an out-of-date view of the parent.

Creating a sponsor distribution requires the non-optional input field `expected_model_version_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the model version being distributed. If that number does not equal the model version's current `version` at the moment the request is processed, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Distributing the same version to an additional sponsor is a perfectly legitimate operation, so no state check can catch a client working from a stale picture; what the version comparison catches is that the client's view of the model version — including its `distribution_count` and its publication state — has been overtaken.

Creating a harvest proposal requires the non-optional input field `expected_account_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target account. If that number does not equal the account's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a newer proposal for the same account and security legitimately supersedes the prior one that is still in `PROPOSED`, a re-proposal computed against a current view would itself succeed; the version comparison is exactly what distinguishes a deliberate re-proposal from one computed against a lot ledger that has since moved.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to any other operation neither require nor accept an expected-version input, and are never rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

### 2.8. Integer Money, Basis Points, and Rounding Discipline

No floating-point money exists anywhere in this system. Every monetary quantity is a whole number of cents and every proportion is a whole number of basis points, where 10000 basis points is the whole. Share counts are likewise whole numbers.

Because integers cannot absorb a division remainder silently, each calculation names both its rounding *method* and the *point in the computation* at which rounding happens, and both are binding. Three distinct disciplines are used, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Round half up to a whole unit.** Used for the per-tier fee amounts and the two non-residual revenue shares of an invoice, for the carved cost basis of a split tax lot, for each position's actual weight in basis points during a drift evaluation, and for the mean and mean-absolute-deviation figures of a dispersion report. A value exactly halfway between two whole units rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.
- **Floor.** Used where an over-allocation would break a conservation invariant: the pro-rata division of effective sleeve weights, the two nested conversions from investable cents to target share counts, and the pro-rata division of a block fill across its member orders. In each case the remainder left behind by flooring is then handed out one unit at a time in a specified walk order, so nothing is lost.
- **Exact residual.** Used for the last member of a set whose siblings have already been rounded: the manager's share of an invoice is whatever is left after the sponsor's and the platform's rounded shares are subtracted from the gross, and the residual basis of a partially consumed tax lot is whatever stays with the parent after the carved slice's rounded basis is removed.

Where a calculation specifies an evaluation order, that order is part of the contract. Rounding a sum instead of summing already-rounded parts, or converting to shares before applying a weight, produces different integers and is not an acceptable implementation.

### 2.9. The Two State Dimensions of an Account, and Cascade Discipline

An account carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and this is the single most important structural fact about the domain. `implementation_status` tracks the account's relationship to its model and takes the values `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, `SYNCED`, `DRIFTED` and `SUSPENDED`, starting at `SYNCED`. `billing_status` tracks the account's relationship to its fees and takes the values `CURRENT`, `INVOICED`, `DELINQUENT` and `WRITTEN_OFF`, starting at `CURRENT`. Neither dimension is a substate of the other. A `SUSPENDED` account is still invoiced, paid and marked delinquent exactly as any other; a `DELINQUENT` account still evaluates drift, still implements and still trades.

Exactly one edge couples the two dimensions, and it runs in only one direction: writing off a delinquent invoice drives `billing_status` to `WRITTEN_OFF`, which in turn forces `implementation_status` to `SUSPENDED` regardless of the value it held before. `WRITTEN_OFF` is terminal for the billing dimension; the implementation dimension has no terminal state, since a suspension can always be reversed.

Three named cascades run in this platform, and each is described where its workflow lives in §3. What they share is a discipline worth stating once, globally: a cascade hop fires only because the hop before it fired, each hop is conditional on the state of the rows it reaches, rows that fail the condition are skipped rather than forced, and every row a cascade actually touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. Cascade A is the publication fan-out, in which publishing a model version supersedes a sibling version, lapses distributions, notifies accounts and stales their open orders. Cascade B is the allocation chain, in which a block allocation books shares, opens or closes tax lots, moves cash and stamps wash windows. Cascade C is the write-off chain described above. Cascades B and C both have compensating counterparts — the block unwind and the invoice void — which restore every counter they touch to its exact prior integer value.

### 2.10. Interval Semantics: Effective Windows, Billing Periods, and Wash Windows

Three different resources in this platform are guarded by interval arithmetic, and they deliberately use different interval conventions. Implementers should not generalize from one to another.

**Sponsor distribution effective windows** are half-open intervals on the instant timeline running from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive, where a null `effective_until` means the window never ends. The occupied resource is the sponsor-and-model pair, and only distributions in status `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` occupy it; a `LAPSED` distribution has vacated the resource and can never collide. Two windows collide when the first window's start is strictly earlier than the second's end and the second window's start is strictly earlier than the first's end, treating a null end as unbounded. Because both comparisons are strict, **adjacency is legal**: a new window whose start instant equals an existing window's end instant does not collide. There is no buffer of any kind, and comparison is at full ISO 8601 instant precision.

**Fee-invoice billing periods** use the same half-open convention, running from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive, on the account key. Occupants are the account's invoices in status `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; a `VOIDED` invoice has vacated the period and never collides. The collision test and the legality of adjacency are identical to the distribution case.

**Wash windows** are different in every respect: they are **closed** intervals measured in whole UTC dates, formed as a symmetric buffer of 30 days on each side of a point event, and the occupied resource is the account-and-security pair. Both endpoints are inside the window, and the time-of-day component of every instant involved is discarded before comparison. Two clauses use this geometry. The **lookback clause** applies when a harvest proposal is created: the proposal is rejected when a tax lot exists for that account and security whose `origin` is `TRADE`, whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and whose `open_date` falls on a date within the closed interval running from 30 days before the current clock date to 30 days after it — a purchase exactly 30 days before the proposal collides, one 31 days before does not. Lots whose `origin` is `TRANSFER_IN` never satisfy this clause, because an in-kind delivery is not a purchase. The **occupation clause** applies when a block is assembled for a `BUY`: the block is rejected when a tax lot exists for that account and security whose `status` is `CLOSED` and whose `realized_gain_cents` is negative, and whose stamped window from `wash_window_start` to `wash_window_end` contains the current clock date, again inclusive at both ends. Lots in status `REVERSED` satisfy neither clause, since an unwound disposal never happened.

### 2.11. Resource Addressing and Filtered Reads

This domain has no nested URL hierarchies. Relationships between resources are expressed as foreign-key fields on the rows themselves — an account names its sponsor and its model, a trade order names its account, its security, its driving model version and its block, a tax lot may name its parent lot and the orders that opened and closed it — and every resource is addressed at its own top-level collection by its own identifier. Where an operation acts on a subordinate concept, it appears as an action segment appended to the owning resource's address rather than as a nested collection.

Identifiers appearing in a request path are resolved strictly from left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 code, as rung 5 of §2.6 specifies. Collection reads narrow their result with query parameters rather than with path segments: model versions filter by `model_id` and `status`; distributions filter by `sponsor_id`, `model_version_id`, `model_id` and `status`; accounts filter by `sponsor_id`, `model_id`, `current_model_version_id`, `pending_model_version_id`, `implementation_status` and `billing_status`; restrictions filter by `account_id`, `security_id` and `status`; trade orders filter by `account_id`, `security_id`, `model_version_id`, `block_id`, `status`, `source` and `side`; blocks filter by `security_id`, `status` and `side`; tax lots filter by `account_id`, `security_id`, `parent_tax_lot_id`, `opening_trade_order_id`, `closing_trade_order_id`, `status` and `origin`; harvest proposals filter by `account_id`, `security_id`, `replacement_security_id` and `status`; and fee invoices filter by `account_id` and `status`. Every one of these filters is optional, and all of them combine conjunctively with `limit` and `offset`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Instrument Master, Sponsor Platforms, and Strategy Registration

Nothing in this platform can be valued until the instrument master is populated. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each tradable instrument with an uppercase exchange `ticker` of one to eight characters drawn from the letters A through Z, which must be unique across every registered instrument, a human-readable `name` for the issuer or instrument, an `asset_class` chosen from `US_EQUITY`, `GLOBAL_EQUITY`, `FIXED_INCOME` and `CASH_EQUIV`, and a `last_price_cents` mark of at least 1. An optional `restricted_globally` flag, false unless supplied, is the platform's blanket veto: while it is true, no purchase of that instrument may enter any account whatsoever. A ticker already in use is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Marks move, and re-marking an instrument is its own operation, also reserved to a `PlatformAdmin`, which replaces `last_price_cents` and advances the instrument's `version`. Re-marking is deliberately non-retroactive. Every valuation, drift evaluation, order generation and harvest computation performed *after* the re-mark uses the new number, but no account's `drift_bps` is recomputed as a side effect, and figures already committed to storage — the `cost_basis_cents` on existing tax lots and every amount on an existing fee invoice — are never rewritten.

Sponsor platforms are registered next, again by a `PlatformAdmin`. A sponsor carries a `name`, a `platform_code` of two to twelve characters that must be unique across all sponsors, and a `revenue_share_bps` between 0 and 9000 inclusive, which is the sponsor's cut of the gross fee on every invoice raised against its accounts. Two optional contractual parameters shape how the sponsor's accounts behave downstream: `implementation_lag_days`, an integer from 0 to 10 that defaults to 2, is the number of days an account is contractually given to implement a newly published model version once it has been notified; and `min_account_value_cents`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 0, is the floor an account's market value must reach before an implementation may run. A denormalized `account_count` tracks how many accounts the sponsor holds and rises by one each time one is opened. The `id` minted for a sponsor is operationally significant: it is exactly the value a `SponsorOperator` must present as `X-User-ID`.

Strategies belong to the asset manager, and a `PortfolioManager` registers each one with a `manager_name` identifying the owning firm, a `name` for display, a `strategy_code` of two to twelve characters unique across all strategies, an `asset_class` from the same four-member enumeration used for instruments, and a `base_fee_bps` between 1 and 500 inclusive, which is the tier-one annual management fee that the invoice calculation of §3.9 discounts for higher tiers. An optional `drift_threshold_bps`, between 1 and 10000 inclusive and defaulting to 300, sets the tracking-error tolerance for every account following that strategy. A strategy also carries two derived pointers that begin empty: `version_count`, starting at 0 and counting the versions authored against it, and `published_model_version_id`, starting null and holding the identifier of whichever of its versions is currently published. A duplicate `strategy_code`, like a duplicate `platform_code` or `ticker`, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

### 3.2. Authoring a Model Version: Drafting, Review, and the Revision Loop

A strategy is an empty shell until a version is authored against it. A `PortfolioManager` opens a new version by naming the owning `model_id`; the platform assigns a `version_number` equal to the strategy's `version_count` plus one, starts the version in status `DRAFT`, and advances both the strategy's `version_count` and the strategy's own `version`. A freshly opened version has an empty `sleeves` array, a `sleeve_count` of 0, a `total_target_weight_bps` of 0, a null `submitted_by_user_id`, a null `approved_by_user_id`, a null `rejection_note`, a null `published_at`, a null `superseded_at`, and a `distribution_count` of 0. The full status enumeration for a version is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `PUBLISHED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and a distribution's is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` and `LAPSED`.

The substance of a version is its sleeve list, and it is edited wholesale rather than incrementally: each edit supplies a complete `sleeves` array that replaces whatever was there before. Each sleeve entry names a `security_id` that must resolve to a registered instrument, a `target_weight_bps` between 1 and 10000, and an optional `max_position_bps`, also between 1 and 10000, which defaults to 10000 when it is not supplied and caps how much of an account may sit in that sleeve. No two entries in one array may name the same `security_id`. A sleeve edit is only reachable while the version is in `DRAFT`; after each edit the platform recomputes `sleeve_count` as the length of the array and `total_target_weight_bps` as the sum of the sleeve targets, and advances the version's `version` by one.

Submission moves the version `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL` and is performed by a `PortfolioManager`. It is refused with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_BALANCED` unless the version carries at least one sleeve **and** its `total_target_weight_bps` equals exactly 10000. No tolerance band applies to this comparison: 9999 and 10001 are both rejected. A successful submission records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, clears any `rejection_note` left over from a prior review round back to null, and advances the version's `version`.

Review is where segregation of duties bites. A `ComplianceOfficer` other than the submitter either approves or rejects. Approval moves the version `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, records the reviewing `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, and pins the sleeve list permanently: no sleeve edit is reachable from `APPROVED` or from any state beyond it, so the array that was reviewed is the array that will eventually be delivered. Rejection is the mirror branch and closes the revision cycle `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL → DRAFT`: it moves the version back to `DRAFT`, records the reviewer's supplied text as `rejection_note`, and clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null so that the revised draft may be resubmitted by anyone at all, including the original author. Sleeves become editable again the moment the version returns to `DRAFT`. Both branches advance the version's `version` by one, and both are refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` is the one recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`.

Across the whole lifecycle one invariant holds at the strategy level: at most one version of a given strategy may sit in status `PUBLISHED` at any instant. `SUPERSEDED` is the terminal state of the version lifecycle; a superseded version is never revived.

### 3.3. Publication and the Model-Change Notification Fan-Out

Publication is the single most consequential operation in the platform. A `PortfolioManager` publishes an approved version, moving it `APPROVED → PUBLISHED`, and that one request propagates through five conditional hops — the cascade referred to in §2.9 as Cascade A. Each hop fires only because the hop before it fired.

The **first hop** stamps `published_at` with the current logical clock instant, repoints the owning strategy's `published_model_version_id` at the newly published version, and advances both the version's `version` and the strategy's `version`.

The **second hop** exists only because a strategy may hold at most one published version at a time. If a sibling version of the same strategy was sitting in `PUBLISHED`, it transitions `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_at` stamped with the same clock instant. When the strategy had no previously published version, this hop and everything downstream of it simply does not occur.

The **third hop** exists only because a distribution may not carry a superseded version. Every sponsor distribution whose `model_version_id` is the just-superseded version and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → LAPSED` with `lapsed_at` stamped at the clock instant. Distributions still in `PENDING` are left alone; `LAPSED` is terminal for a distribution.

The **fourth hop** exists only because those accounts' model feed has just lapsed. Consider every account whose `sponsor_id` appears among the sponsors of the distributions that just lapsed, whose `current_model_version_id` is the superseded version, and whose `implementation_status` is either `SYNCED` or `DRIFTED`. Each such account transitions to `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`; its `pending_model_version_id` is set to the newly published version; its `notified_at` is stamped at the clock instant; its `implementation_deadline` is set to `notified_at` advanced by the sponsor's `implementation_lag_days` in days — for a sponsor on the default two-day contract, forty-eight hours after notification; and its `implemented_late` flag is reset to false. This cascade is conditional, not blanket: an account already in `SUSPENDED` or already in `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION` is skipped entirely and none of those fields are written. An account arriving from `DRIFTED` keeps whatever `drift_bps` its last evaluation produced — the number is retained, it simply no longer gates a rebalance, because the account is now pending implementation rather than drifted.

The **fifth hop** exists only because those accounts' targets have just gone stale. Every trade order belonging to one of the accounts touched by the fourth hop, whose `model_version_id` is the superseded version and whose `status` is either `PROPOSED` or `BLOCKED`, transitions to `STALE`. A staled order that was sitting in a block also has its `block_id` cleared, and the block it leaves recomputes its `requested_shares` and its `order_count` from its remaining members. `STALE` is terminal for a trade order.

Delivering a version onto a sponsor platform is a separate act from publishing it. A `PortfolioManager` creates a distribution by naming the `sponsor_id` receiving the model, the `model_version_id` being delivered, an `effective_from` instant, an optional `effective_until` instant which may be omitted or null for an open-ended window, and the `expected_model_version_version` guard described in §2.7. The referenced version must be in status `PUBLISHED`, failing which the creation is refused with `ERR_MODEL_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the new window must not collide with any `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` window on the same sponsor-and-strategy key under the half-open geometry of §2.10, failing which it is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. On success the distribution is born in status `PENDING` with a null `activated_at` and a null `lapsed_at`, copies `model_id` from the referenced version so that the overlap key is available without a join, and raises the version's `distribution_count` by one along with the version's `version`.

Activation belongs to the receiving sponsor and to nobody else: a `SponsorOperator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the distribution's `sponsor_id` moves it `PENDING → ACTIVE`, which stamps `activated_at` at the clock instant and advances the distribution's `version`. Activation is guarded across the entity boundary — the referenced version must *still* be `PUBLISHED` at the moment of activation, so a distribution left pending across an intervening publication can no longer be activated and is refused with `ERR_MODEL_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

### 3.4. Opening, Funding, and Customizing an SMA Account

A `SponsorOperator` opens an account by naming its own `sponsor_id`, the `model_id` of the strategy the account will track, and an `account_number` of three to twenty characters that must be unique across every account on the platform — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. Four optional customization inputs may accompany the request: `cash_reserve_bps`, an integer from 0 to 2000 defaulting to 100, which is the fraction of market value the generator of §3.5 holds back from investment; `drift_basis`, either `SETTLED` or `TRADE_DATE` and defaulting to `SETTLED`, which selects the account's default drift convention; `tax_overlay_enabled`, a boolean defaulting to false, which is the master switch for tax-loss harvesting; and `harvest_budget_cents`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 0, which is the annual ceiling on realized losses.

An account may only be opened against a strategy that has already published a version. If the strategy's `published_model_version_id` is null, the request is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Otherwise the new account's `current_model_version_id` is set to exactly that published version, its `pending_model_version_id` is null, its `implementation_status` starts at `SYNCED`, its `billing_status` starts at `CURRENT`, its `cash_cents` starts at 0, its `realized_loss_ytd_cents` and `fees_billed_ytd_cents` both start at 0, its `next_order_sequence` starts at 1, and its `drift_bps`, `drift_basis_used`, `drift_evaluated_at`, `notified_at`, `implementation_deadline`, `last_implementation_lag_days`, `latest_return_bps`, `latest_return_period_end` and `latest_ending_value_cents` all start null, with `implemented_late` starting false. Opening an account also raises the owning sponsor's `account_count` and the sponsor row's `version`.

Funding arrives in two forms. Cash is credited by naming an `amount_cents` to deposit, which raises `cash_cents` and, with it, the account's market value. Existing positions are booked in kind by naming a `security_id`, a whole `shares` count, the `cost_basis_cents` attached to those shares, and optionally an `origin` of either `TRANSFER_IN` or `TRADE`, defaulting to `TRANSFER_IN`. Booking a position creates a tax lot in status `OPEN` whose `open_date` is the current logical clock instant and whose `sequence_number` is drawn from the account's `next_order_sequence` counter. The `origin` distinction is not cosmetic: `TRANSFER_IN` records an in-kind delivery, while `TRADE` records an externally executed purchase and therefore taints the wash-sale lookback described in §2.10 for the following thirty days in each direction.

An account's `market_value_cents` is never stored independently of its constituents. It is at all times the account's `cash_cents` plus, summed over every tax lot of that account still in status `OPEN`, that lot's `shares_open` multiplied by the current `last_price_cents` of the lot's security. Because the mark moves, so does the market value, which is why the sponsor-minimum check of the implementation workflow is evaluated at implementation time rather than remembered from account opening.

Account-level customization is the province of the `ComplianceOfficer`, who attaches restrictions by naming the `account_id`, the `security_id` being constrained, and a `restriction_type` of `DO_NOT_BUY`, `DO_NOT_HOLD` or `MAX_POSITION`. A `MAX_POSITION` restriction must carry a `max_position_bps` between 1 and 10000 inclusive; the other two types must not carry one at all, and either mistake is a request-shape failure. A restriction is born `ACTIVE` with a null `lifted_at`, and creating one advances the owning account's `version` as well. Lifting a restriction moves it `ACTIVE → LIFTED`, stamps `lifted_at` at the clock instant, and advances both the restriction's `version` and the account's `version`; from the next generation onward the lifted restriction stops participating in the effective-weight computation entirely, although any orders it already shaped are left exactly as they were.

Freezing is likewise a compliance act. Suspension is reachable from `SYNCED`, from `DRIFTED` and from `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, and drives `implementation_status` to `SUSPENDED` while leaving the billing dimension completely untouched — a suspended account keeps being invoiced. When an account is suspended out of `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, its `pending_model_version_id`, `notified_at` and `implementation_deadline` are retained unchanged, so the outstanding obligation survives the freeze. Reinstatement reverses it and is deliberately state-aware: an account whose `pending_model_version_id` is null returns `SUSPENDED → SYNCED`, while an account still carrying a pending version returns `SUSPENDED → PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, resuming the obligation where it left off. Reinstatement is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_WRITTEN_OFF` when the account's `billing_status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`, because the suspension imposed by a write-off is not meant to be reversible.

### 3.5. Effective Weights and the Order Generator

Two operations generate trade orders — adopting a newly notified model version, and rebalancing a drifted account — and both run the same two-stage engine. The first stage translates the driving version's sleeves into the account's own **effective weights**, folding in that account's customizations. The second stage turns those weights into whole-share orders.

**Stage one: effective weights.** The inputs are the sleeve array of the driving model version — the account's pending version when adopting a new model, its current version when rebalancing — together with the account's restrictions that are still in status `ACTIVE`. Restrictions in status `LIFTED` are ignored entirely. Each sleeve's base weight is its `target_weight_bps`. Each sleeve also has a cap, which is the smaller of the sleeve's own `max_position_bps` and the `max_position_bps` of an `ACTIVE` `MAX_POSITION` restriction on that account and security; where no such restriction exists, the restriction side of that comparison is treated as 10000, which is to say it never binds. A sleeve whose security carries an `ACTIVE` restriction of type `DO_NOT_BUY` or `DO_NOT_HOLD` is excluded outright: its effective weight is 0 and it is pinned before the loop begins.

The remainder of the allocation is settled by a loop whose evaluation order is fixed, because each quantity in it depends on the others. Each account's effective weight depends on the residual left by the pinned sleeves, the residual depends on which sleeves are pinned, and which sleeves become pinned depends on the weights just computed. The loop runs at most three passes, and each pass proceeds as follows. The free sleeves are those not yet pinned, ordered by their security's `ticker` ascending; if none are free, the loop stops. The residual is 10000 minus the sum of the effective weights of every pinned sleeve. The pool is the sum of the base weights of the free sleeves. Each free sleeve's effective weight is set to the residual multiplied by that sleeve's base weight, divided by the pool, rounded **down** to a whole basis point. The remainder — the residual minus the sum of those rounded-down weights — is then handed out one basis point at a time, walking the free sleeves in `ticker` ascending order and wrapping back to the start if the remainder exceeds the number of free sleeves. Finally the pass looks for free sleeves whose newly computed weight now exceeds their cap; if there are none, the loop stops. Otherwise each breached sleeve's weight is set to exactly its cap, that sleeve is pinned, and the next pass runs. The cash target in basis points is 10000 minus the sum of the effective weights over all sleeve securities, and by construction the effective weights plus the cash target sum to exactly 10000.

**Stage two: order generation.** Start from the account's `market_value_cents`. The cash reserve held back from investment is the larger of one hundred dollars — that is, 10000 cents — and the account's `cash_reserve_bps` applied to market value, computed as market value multiplied by `cash_reserve_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded down to whole cents. The investable amount is market value minus that reserve. The generation set is the union of two collections: the securities named in the driving version's sleeves, and the securities in which the account currently holds a non-zero settled position, meaning at least one tax lot in status `OPEN` with a positive `shares_open`. That union is walked in `ticker` ascending order.

For each security in the walk, take its effective weight from stage one, using 0 for a security that has a position but no sleeve. The target share count is computed with two rounding steps applied strictly in this order: first the investable amount multiplied by the effective weight, divided by 10000, rounded **down** to whole cents; then that cent figure divided by the security's `last_price_cents`, again rounded **down**, to whole shares. Converting to shares before applying the weight is not an admissible implementation and yields different numbers. The account's current holding is the sum of `shares_open` across its `OPEN` tax lots of that security, and the delta is the target share count minus that current holding. If the absolute value of the delta multiplied by `last_price_cents` is less than fifty dollars — that is, less than 5000 cents — the security is skipped as economically immaterial. Otherwise an order is created in status `PROPOSED`, with `side` set to `BUY` when the delta is positive and `SELL` when it is negative, `shares` set to the absolute value of the delta, `target_shares` recording the post-trade target the generator solved for, `effective_weight_bps` recording the weight that produced it, `model_version_id` naming the driving version, `account_id` and `security_id` naming the account and instrument, a null `block_id`, an `allocated_shares` of 0, a null `fill_price_cents`, and a `sequence_number` taken from the account's `next_order_sequence`, which then advances by one per order created. If the walk produces no orders at all, the whole operation is refused with `ERR_NO_TRADES_GENERATED` and nothing is written.

One property of the generator's input set is deliberate and frequently surprising: only **settled** positions count. Orders already sitting in `PROPOSED`, `BLOCKED` or `ALLOCATED` status do not reduce the computed delta, so two generations run back to back against an unchanged lot ledger produce two full sets of orders rather than a second empty one. Operators are expected to allocate or cancel before regenerating.

**Adopting a model version.** A `SponsorOperator` runs an implementation on an account sitting in `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION`, moving it `PENDING_IMPLEMENTATION → SYNCED`. Beyond the account's own state, the operation requires a distribution in status `ACTIVE` whose sponsor is the account's sponsor and whose model version is the account's `pending_model_version_id`, and whose effective window covers the current clock instant — the clock at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_until`, or at or after `effective_from` when `effective_until` is null. Failing that, the request is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. It further requires the account's `market_value_cents` to be at least its sponsor's `min_account_value_cents`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_BELOW_SPONSOR_MINIMUM`. On success, `current_model_version_id` is set to the value of `pending_model_version_id`, which is then cleared to null; `last_implementation_lag_days` records the number of whole UTC days between `notified_at` and the current clock instant; `implemented_late` is set to true when the clock instant is strictly after `implementation_deadline` and to false otherwise; and the generator emits orders whose `source` is `IMPLEMENTATION` and whose `model_version_id` is the newly current version. The response reports `account_id`, the resulting `current_model_version_id`, a `generated_order_count`, the list of `trade_order_ids` in the generator's own `ticker` ascending order, `last_implementation_lag_days` and `implemented_late`.

**Rebalancing.** A `SponsorOperator` rebalances an account that is currently `DRIFTED`, and the account stays `DRIFTED` afterwards — rebalancing generates orders, it does not by itself re-measure tracking error, so the return to `SYNCED` comes only from the next drift evaluation. The request is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_DRIFTED` when the account is in any other implementation state, and it requires a non-null `current_model_version_id`, which is the version whose sleeves drive the generation. Orders emitted here carry a `source` of `REBALANCE`. The response reports `account_id`, the driving `model_version_id`, a `generated_order_count` and the ordered `trade_order_ids`.

### 3.6. Drift Monitoring, Performance Recording, and Cross-Account Dispersion

Tracking error is measured on demand rather than continuously. Either a `SponsorOperator` or a `PortfolioManager` may run a drift evaluation on an account, and the evaluation both records a measurement and moves the implementation dimension between `SYNCED` and `DRIFTED`.

The evaluation runs under one of two bases, and the two produce genuinely different numbers from identical stored data. The caller may supply a `basis` of `SETTLED` or `TRADE_DATE`; when the field is omitted, the account's own `drift_basis` decides. Under the `SETTLED` basis, each security's position is the sum of `shares_open` across the account's tax lots of that security in status `OPEN`, and the cash figure is the account's `cash_cents`; open orders are invisible. Under the `TRADE_DATE` basis, each security's position is that same settled share count adjusted by the signed pending delta of every trade order of the account whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `BLOCKED` — adding the order's `shares` for a `BUY` and subtracting them for a `SELL` — and the cash figure is the account's `cash_cents` reduced by the sum, over those same orders, of each `BUY` order's `shares` multiplied by its security's `last_price_cents`, and increased by the corresponding sum over the `SELL` orders.

Whichever basis is selected, the arithmetic that follows is identical. The valuation base is the cash figure plus, summed across securities, each position multiplied by the security's `last_price_cents`. The scan then covers the union of the sleeve securities of the account's `current_model_version_id` and every security whose position under the selected basis is non-zero. For each of those securities, the actual weight in basis points is the position multiplied by `last_price_cents`, multiplied by 10000, divided by the valuation base, rounded half up to a whole basis point; the deviation is the absolute difference between that rounded actual weight and the security's effective weight from stage one of §3.5. Rounding must happen per position **before** the deviation is taken; taking deviations on unrounded weights is not admissible. The account's `drift_bps` is the largest deviation found across the scanned securities. Cash is deliberately excluded from the deviation scan even though it participates in the valuation base.

A drift result is judged against the strategy's `drift_threshold_bps` with an **inclusive** comparison: an account is breached when `drift_bps` is greater than or equal to the threshold. An account in `SYNCED` whose result is breached moves `SYNCED → DRIFTED`; an account in `DRIFTED` whose result is strictly below the threshold moves `DRIFTED → SYNCED`, closing the monitoring cycle `SYNCED → DRIFTED → SYNCED`. In every case the evaluation stores `drift_bps`, records which convention was actually applied as `drift_basis_used`, stamps `drift_evaluated_at` with the clock instant, and advances the account's `version`. A drift evaluation additionally requires that the account's `billing_status` is not `WRITTEN_OFF`.

Performance is reported into the platform rather than derived by it. A `SponsorOperator` or a `PlatformAdmin` records an observation by supplying the `period_end` instant, the `gross_return_bps` achieved over the period, and the `ending_value_cents` at which the period closed. The account stores these as `latest_return_bps`, `latest_return_period_end` and `latest_ending_value_cents`, overwriting whatever the previous observation left, and advances its `version`. Only the most recent observation is retained, and it is the sole input the dispersion report consumes from each account.

Dispersion is reported per model version, over the cohort of accounts that are actually running that exact version. An account joins the cohort when its `current_model_version_id` is the version in question, its `implementation_status` is anything other than `SUSPENDED`, and it has reported at least one performance observation — an account whose `latest_return_bps` is still null is excluded from the count, from the mean and from the minimum-and-maximum scan alike. If the cohort is empty the report is refused with `ERR_DISPERSION_SET_EMPTY`. Otherwise `account_count` reports the cohort size, `min_return_bps` and `max_return_bps` report the smallest and largest `latest_return_bps` in the cohort, and `dispersion_bps` is the difference between the largest and the smallest. Those three figures are the same under both weighting conventions.

The mean and the average spread around it are not. A `basis` query field selects between them, defaulting to `EQUAL_WEIGHTED` when omitted. Under `EQUAL_WEIGHTED`, `mean_return_bps` is the sum of the cohort's returns divided by the cohort size, rounded half up to a whole basis point, and `mean_abs_deviation_bps` is the sum, over the cohort, of the absolute difference between each account's return and that rounded mean, divided by the cohort size and rounded half up. Every account counts once regardless of size. Under `ASSET_WEIGHTED`, each account's weight is its `latest_ending_value_cents`: `mean_return_bps` is the sum over the cohort of each account's return multiplied by its ending value, divided by the sum of the ending values, rounded half up; and `mean_abs_deviation_bps` is the sum over the cohort of each account's ending value multiplied by the absolute difference between its return and that rounded mean, divided by the sum of the ending values, rounded half up. Intermediate sums stay exact and rounding is applied exactly once to the mean and once to the mean absolute deviation. The report echoes the `model_version_id` it covered and the `basis` it applied alongside the six figures.

### 3.7. Street Blocks, Allocation, and the Tax-Lot Ledger

Individual account orders are never sent to the street on their own. A `SponsorOperator` aggregates them into a block by supplying a list of `trade_order_ids`. The list must be non-empty, every listed order must be in status `PROPOSED`, and all of them must share one `security_id` and one `side`; any deviation is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_COMPOSITION_INVALID`. Because a block spans accounts, the sponsor-scope rule bites collectively: every member order's account must belong to the sponsor presenting the request. Two safety gates apply to a `BUY` block. No member may target a security that is restricted for its own account — either the security's `restricted_globally` flag is true, or an `ACTIVE` restriction of type `DO_NOT_BUY` or `DO_NOT_HOLD` exists on that account and security — and a violation is refused with `ERR_SECURITY_RESTRICTED`. And no member may buy back into a wash window that the account's own realized losses have already occupied, under the occupation clause of §2.10, on pain of `ERR_WASH_SALE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A successful assembly moves every listed order `PROPOSED → BLOCKED` and stamps each with the new block's `block_id`. The block itself is born in status `OPEN`, copying `security_id` and `side` from its members, with `requested_shares` equal to the sum of `shares` over its member orders in status `BLOCKED` or `ALLOCATED`, `order_count` equal to the number of those members, `filled_shares` and `allocated_shares` at 0, `unallocated_shares` at 0, and null values for `avg_fill_price_cents`, `executed_at` and `allocated_at`. A block's status enumeration is `OPEN`, `FILLED` and `ALLOCATED`; a trade order's is `PROPOSED`, `BLOCKED`, `ALLOCATED`, `CANCELLED` and `STALE`; and a tax lot's is `OPEN`, `CLOSED` and `REVERSED`.

Execution is a `PlatformAdmin` act and records what the street actually did. The block must be `OPEN` and must hold at least one member order. The admin reports `filled_shares` — which must be at least 1 and at most the block's `requested_shares`, on pain of `ERR_FILL_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` — and the `avg_fill_price_cents` at which the fill was struck, and the block moves `OPEN → FILLED` with `executed_at` stamped at the clock instant. At that moment `unallocated_shares` becomes the whole `filled_shares` while `allocated_shares` remains 0, which is the first appearance of an invariant that holds at every point of the block's round trip: `filled_shares` always equals `allocated_shares` plus `unallocated_shares`.

**Allocation** moves the block `FILLED → ALLOCATED` and each member order `BLOCKED → ALLOCATED`, and it is the second of the three named cascades. It begins with a pro-rata waterfall over the members still in status `BLOCKED`; members that were cancelled or staled are excluded from the requested total and receive nothing. Let the requested total be the sum of `shares` over those participating members. Each member's provisional allocation is the block's `filled_shares` multiplied by that member's `shares`, divided by the requested total, rounded **down** to whole shares. The shortfall between `filled_shares` and the sum of those rounded-down allocations is then handed out one whole share at a time, walking the participating members sorted by their `shares` descending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, wrapping back to the start if the shortfall exceeds the number of members. The allocations therefore sum to exactly `filled_shares`.

The **first hop** of the cascade writes each member's `allocated_shares` from that waterfall and copies the block's `avg_fill_price_cents` into the order's `fill_price_cents`. A member whose pro-rata allocation resolves to 0 shares does not become `ALLOCATED` at all — it transitions to `CANCELLED` instead. Before any of this is committed, each `BUY` member's account must hold enough `cash_cents` to cover its own allocation cost, failing which the operation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH`; and no member's account may be in `SUSPENDED`.

The **second hop** exists only because shares actually changed hands. A `BUY` allocation opens a new tax lot for the member's account and security, in status `OPEN`, with `origin` set to `TRADE`, `open_date` at the clock instant, `shares_open` equal to the allocated share count, `cost_basis_cents` equal to that share count multiplied by the fill price, `opening_trade_order_id` naming the order, a `sequence_number` drawn from the account's `next_order_sequence`, and `shares_closed` at 0 with `realized_gain_cents`, `close_date`, `closing_trade_order_id`, `parent_tax_lot_id`, `wash_window_start` and `wash_window_end` all null; the account's `cash_cents` is debited by the lot's cost basis. A `SELL` allocation instead disposes shares through the **highest-in, first-out walk**, and credits the account's `cash_cents` with the proceeds.

The disposal walk is worth stating precisely, because both this path and the harvest path of §3.8 use it verbatim. The candidate lots are the account's lots of that exact security in status `OPEN` with a positive `shares_open`, sorted by cost per share — each lot's `cost_basis_cents` divided by its `shares_open` — descending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. Lots in status `CLOSED` or `REVERSED` are never candidates. Walking that order, each lot gives up the lesser of its own `shares_open` and the shares still to be disposed. The basis attributed to those shares is the lot's `cost_basis_cents` multiplied by the shares taken, divided by the lot's `shares_open`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the realized amount for that slice is the shares taken multiplied by the disposal price minus that basis. When the shares taken equal the lot's entire `shares_open`, the lot is closed **in place**: no new row appears, and the lot itself moves `OPEN → CLOSED` with `shares_closed` set to the shares taken, `shares_open` set to 0, `realized_gain_cents` set to the realized amount, `close_date` stamped at the clock instant, and — when the disposal was driven by an allocation — `closing_trade_order_id` naming the selling order. When the shares taken are strictly fewer than the lot's `shares_open`, the lot is **split** instead: a brand-new row is created in status `CLOSED` whose `parent_tax_lot_id` points at the surviving lot, carrying the shares taken as its `shares_closed`, 0 as its `shares_open`, the carved basis as its `cost_basis_cents` and the realized amount as its `realized_gain_cents`, while the parent lot stays `OPEN` with its `shares_open` and its `cost_basis_cents` each reduced by the carved portion — so parent plus slice reconstitute the original basis exactly. The walk continues until the requested shares are exhausted; if the candidates run out first, the operation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES` and nothing is written.

The **third hop** exists only because a disposal produced a negative realized amount. Every closed slice whose `realized_gain_cents` is negative has its `wash_window_start` stamped at 30 days before its `close_date` and its `wash_window_end` at 30 days after it, and the account's `realized_loss_ytd_cents` rises by the absolute value of that slice's loss. Realized gains never reduce that running total. Once every member has been processed the block stamps `allocated_at`, recomputes `allocated_shares` as the sum over its members and `unallocated_shares` as `filled_shares` minus that sum, and advances its `version`.

**Unwinding** is the compensating reversal, available to a `PlatformAdmin`, and it closes the cycle `BLOCKED → ALLOCATED → BLOCKED` for the orders and `FILLED → ALLOCATED → FILLED` for the block. It is permitted only while every tax lot that this block's allocation opened is still in status `OPEN`; if any of them has since been disposed, the reversal would have nothing coherent to undo and the request is refused with `ERR_UNWIND_BLOCKED`. When it proceeds, every member order returns to `BLOCKED` with `allocated_shares` reset to 0 and `fill_price_cents` reset to null. Every lot the allocation opened transitions `OPEN → REVERSED` with `shares_open` set to 0, and the account's `cash_cents` is credited back by that lot's `cost_basis_cents`. Every lot the allocation closed is restored according to how it was closed: a lot closed in place returns `CLOSED → OPEN` with `shares_open` set back to its `shares_closed`, `shares_closed` reset to 0, and `realized_gain_cents`, `close_date`, `wash_window_start` and `wash_window_end` all cleared to null; a split slice instead transitions `CLOSED → REVERSED`, and its parent lot regains exactly the slice's `shares_closed` and the slice's `cost_basis_cents`. The account's `realized_loss_ytd_cents` is reduced by exactly the amount the allocation added — no more and no less. The block clears `allocated_at`, returns `allocated_shares` to 0 and `unallocated_shares` to the full `filled_shares`, and advances its `version`. `REVERSED` is terminal for a tax lot.

Individual orders can also be withdrawn. A `SponsorOperator` cancels an order in status `PROPOSED` outright. An order already in `BLOCKED` may be cancelled only while its owning block is still `OPEN`; once the block has been executed the order is committed to the street and the cancellation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A cancellation out of `BLOCKED` clears the order's `block_id` and makes the block recompute its `requested_shares` and `order_count`. `CANCELLED`, like `STALE`, is terminal.

### 3.8. Tax-Loss Harvesting and Wash-Sale Coordination

Harvesting is a three-party workflow: the manager proposes, compliance decides, and the manager executes. A `PortfolioManager` opens a proposal by naming the `account_id`, the `security_id` to be sold, the whole number of `shares` to dispose — at least 1 — and, optionally, a `replacement_security_id` naming the instrument the manager intends to buy in its place, which may be omitted or null. The `expected_account_version` guard of §2.7 is mandatory here.

Two gates specific to harvesting apply at proposal time. The account's `tax_overlay_enabled` flag must be true — an account without the tax overlay may not create proposals at all, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_HARVEST_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, the same code used when the budget itself is exhausted. And the wash-sale lookback clause of §2.10 must be clear: a `TRADE`-origin lot of that account and security opened anywhere inside the closed thirty-day-either-side window around the current clock date blocks the proposal with `ERR_WASH_SALE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. That same code also rejects the degenerate case where `replacement_security_id` names the very security being harvested, since selling and repurchasing the identical instrument is the wash sale the rule exists to prevent.

The proposal captures the security's `last_price_cents` at that moment as its `quoted_price_cents`, then runs the highest-cost-first disposal walk of §3.7 **read-only** at that quoted price. No lot is mutated, no window is stamped and no counter moves; only the arithmetic is kept. That arithmetic yields two deliberately different loss figures, and confusing them is the most common way to get this wrong. `target_loss_cents` is the **signed net** realized amount summed across every slice the walk touched, so a winning lot nets against a losing one and the figure can even come out positive. `proposed_loss_cents` is the absolute value of the sum of **only the negative** per-slice realized amounts, with gains not netted against it at all. The two coincide when every slice lost money and diverge the moment the walk touches both a losing and a winning lot. If the account's `OPEN` lots of that security cannot cover the requested `shares`, the proposal is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`; and the account's `realized_loss_ytd_cents` plus `proposed_loss_cents` must not exceed its `harvest_budget_cents`, on pain of `ERR_HARVEST_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

A successful proposal is born in status `PROPOSED` with a null `realized_loss_cents`, a null `approved_by_user_id`, a null `rejection_note` and a null `executed_at`, and it advances the owning account's `version` — which is exactly why the next proposal against that account needs a freshly read `expected_account_version`. Creating a proposal also **supersedes** any earlier proposal for the same account and security that is still sitting in `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → SUPERSEDED`. That is the third branch out of `PROPOSED`, alongside approval and rejection, and it is driven by the system rather than by an operator.

The two operator branches out of `PROPOSED` are deliberately asymmetric in what they write. Approval by a `ComplianceOfficer` moves the proposal `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, records the reviewing `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, and writes nothing else; it requires the account not to be `SUSPENDED`. Rejection by a `ComplianceOfficer` moves it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, records the reviewer's supplied text as `rejection_note`, and writes nothing else — `realized_loss_cents` stays null, and not a single tax lot is touched. Both branches advance the proposal's `version` by one, and `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the only states reachable from `PROPOSED`. The full status enumeration for a proposal is therefore `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, `EXECUTED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED`, of which `EXECUTED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

Execution by a `PortfolioManager` moves an approved proposal `APPROVED → EXECUTED`, and it is emphatically not a replay of the quote. The disposal walk is re-run from scratch against the **current** lot ledger at the security's **execution-time** `last_price_cents`, closing lots in place and splitting them into parented slices exactly as the `SELL` allocation path does. Two things can therefore have moved since the proposal was quoted — the mark and the lots themselves — and the platform re-checks both: the account must still hold at least `shares` in `OPEN` lots of that security, or the execution is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`; and the account's `realized_loss_ytd_cents` plus the freshly re-evaluated loss must still fit within `harvest_budget_cents`, or it is refused with `ERR_HARVEST_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The account must also not be `SUSPENDED`.

On success, `realized_loss_cents` records the re-evaluated per-slice loss total, which may legitimately differ from the `proposed_loss_cents` computed at quote time; the account's `cash_cents` is credited by the disposed `shares` multiplied by the execution-time `last_price_cents`; the account's `realized_loss_ytd_cents` rises by `realized_loss_cents`; every newly closed slice whose `realized_gain_cents` is negative gets its `wash_window_start` and `wash_window_end` stamped 30 days either side of its `close_date`; and `executed_at` is stamped at the clock instant. Lots closed by a harvest carry a null `closing_trade_order_id`, since no trade order drove them. Both the proposal's `version` and the account's `version` advance by one.

### 3.9. Fee Billing, the Sponsor Revenue Split, and the Delinquency Path

Billing is owned end to end by the `BillingOfficer`. An invoice is computed by naming the `account_id`, the `period_start` instant at which the billing period begins inclusively, the `period_end` instant at which it ends exclusively, and the `average_daily_value_cents` — a non-negative figure supplied by the caller, deliberately not derived from the lot ledger, representing the average daily billable market value across the period. The period must not collide with any of the account's non-`VOIDED` invoices under the half-open geometry of §2.10, on pain of `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, and `period_end` must be strictly later than `period_start`.

The computation proceeds in a pinned order. First, `billing_days` is the number of whole UTC days from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive. Next the average daily value is cut into three tier slices: the first slice is the average daily value capped at two hundred fifty thousand dollars, that is 25000000 cents; the second slice is the amount by which the average daily value exceeds that first ceiling, itself capped at one million dollars, that is 100000000 cents, and floored at zero; and the third slice is whatever exceeds the second ceiling, again floored at zero. Each slice carries its own annual rate in basis points, taken from the strategy the account tracks: the first tier bills at the strategy's `base_fee_bps`, the second at `base_fee_bps` reduced by 10 basis points, and the third at `base_fee_bps` reduced by 20 basis points.

Each tier's fee is then prorated over the period and rounded **independently**: `tier1_fee_cents`, `tier2_fee_cents` and `tier3_fee_cents` are each that tier's slice multiplied by its rate in basis points, multiplied by `billing_days`, divided by 10000 and again by 365, then rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_fee_cents` is the sum of those three already-rounded numbers. Summing the three exact tier fees and rounding once at the end is not an admissible implementation and produces a different number of cents.

The three-way revenue split follows the same discipline. `sponsor_share_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by the sponsor's `revenue_share_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. `platform_share_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by the platform's own fixed 500-basis-point cut, divided by 10000, rounded half up. `manager_share_cents` is then simply what is left: `gross_fee_cents` minus the sponsor's share minus the platform's share. Rounding the manager's share directly is not admissible, because the residual is what guarantees the invariant that the three shares always add back to exactly `gross_fee_cents`. Both the strategy's `base_fee_bps` and the sponsor's `revenue_share_bps` are read once, at invoice creation, and never re-read afterwards.

An invoice's status enumeration is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `PAID`, `VOIDED` and `WRITTEN_OFF`, of which the last three are terminal. A newly computed invoice is born in status `DRAFT` with null values for `issued_at`, `paid_at`, `voided_at` and `void_reason`. Across that lifecycle the account's `fees_billed_ytd_cents` is, at every instant, the sum of `gross_fee_cents` over exactly those of its invoices currently in status `ISSUED`, `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. Issuing it moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at`, adds its `gross_fee_cents` to the account's `fees_billed_ytd_cents`, and drives the account's billing dimension `CURRENT → INVOICED`; it requires the account's `billing_status` to be `CURRENT`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

From `ISSUED` three roads lead onward. **Payment** moves the invoice `ISSUED → PAID`, stamps `paid_at`, debits the account's `cash_cents` by `gross_fee_cents` — refusing with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH` when the cash will not cover it — and returns the billing dimension to `CURRENT` from either `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT`. Payment deliberately leaves `fees_billed_ytd_cents` untouched: the fee was billed when it was issued, and paying it does not un-bill it. **Voiding** is the compensating branch and works from either `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`. It stamps `voided_at` and records the supplied `void_reason`. Voiding a `DRAFT` invoice changes no counter, because nothing was ever added. Voiding an `ISSUED` invoice subtracts `gross_fee_cents` back out of `fees_billed_ytd_cents`, restoring the exact pre-issue value, and returns the billing dimension to `CURRENT`. **Delinquency** moves the account's billing dimension `INVOICED → DELINQUENT` and requires that the payment term has run out: the current clock instant must be at or after the invoice's `period_end` plus thirty days, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED`. The invoice itself stays `ISSUED` through delinquency.

**Write-off** is the last resort and the third named cascade. It is available only on an `ISSUED` invoice whose account is already `DELINQUENT`, and a write-off attempted against an account in any other billing state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Its first hop moves the invoice `ISSUED → WRITTEN_OFF`. The second hop, which fires only because the first did, moves the account's billing dimension `DELINQUENT → WRITTEN_OFF`, a terminal state. The third hop fires only because the second did, on the principle that an account whose fees have been abandoned may not keep trading: the account's `implementation_status` is forced to `SUSPENDED` regardless of whatever value it held before, and this is the single point at which the billing dimension reaches into the implementation dimension. The fourth hop fires only because the third did: every trade order of that account in status `PROPOSED` or `BLOCKED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, with any `block_id` cleared and the affected blocks recomputing their `requested_shares` and `order_count`. Throughout all of it the account's `fees_billed_ytd_cents` is **not** reduced — a written-off fee stays billed, which is precisely what distinguishes a write-off from a void.
