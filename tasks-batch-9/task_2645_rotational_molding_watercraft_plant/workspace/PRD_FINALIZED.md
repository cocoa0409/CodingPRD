# Rotational Molding Watercraft Plant API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Rotomolded kayaks and canoes are built by charging powdered polyethylene into a steel tool, swinging that tool through a gas oven on a rotating arm until the powder fuses into a hollow shell, then cooling it in a bay before demoulding, trimming, outfitting and inspecting the hull. This document specifies the HTTP API for a plant that runs that process end to end: it holds the mould inventory and the oven and cooling resource pools, admits resin lots only when their grade, colour, additive package and melt index match the hull model being built, caps the regrind share of every charge, records the oven and cooling parameters that determine wall-thickness distribution and warpage, gates each hull through fixture-based assembly and a three-axis quality inspection, admits build-ahead inventory against seasonal forecast quotas and warehouse footprint, nests bulky low-density hulls onto trailers for freight, and adjudicates hull-deformation warranty claims against a per-model money reserve.

The service is a flat collection of sixteen peer resources joined by explicit foreign keys. There is no owning aggregate in the URL topology: moulds, oven stations, cooling bays, technicians, customers, warehouses, demand buckets, resin lots, resin charges, production orders, moulding cycles, finished units, shipments, shipment lines and warranty claims are all mounted at the API root, and the relationships between them are carried in request bodies rather than in path nesting. Three of those resources are pure resource pools whose occupancy is booked over time windows — an oven station arm, a cooling bay slot and a mould — and each of the three uses deliberately different interval arithmetic, so the temporal rules in this document must be read literally rather than by analogy.

Two design commitments shape every rule below. First, consistency is immediate: every cascade described here completes inside the request that triggers it, and every derived field — a warehouse's committed cube, an order's completed quantity, a demand bucket's build-ahead commitment, a resin lot's three-bucket mass ledger, a hull model's warranty reserve — is recomputed and persisted before the response body is written. Second, the plant enforces segregation of duties: the technician who ran a moulding cycle may never inspect the hulls it produced, and the technician who outfitted a hull may never inspect that hull. Those two commitments, together with an optimistic-concurrency guard on five write paths, are what make an otherwise mechanical manufacturing workflow demanding to implement correctly.

## 2. Global Specifications

### 2.1. Roles and Authorization

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which names the role being asserted. When either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). When `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names below the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). When the role is recognised but the endpoint being called does not list it among its authorised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Roles differ in how `X-User-ID` is interpreted. Three roles — `PlantAdmin`, `LogisticsPlanner` and `WarrantyAgent` — have an opaque identity: any non-empty `X-User-ID` string is accepted and no lookup is performed. Four roles — `ResinTechnician`, `MoldingOperator`, `AssemblyTechnician` and `QualityInspector` — are backed by the `Technician` entity, so `X-User-ID` must resolve to a `Technician` row; if it does not, the request fails with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 401). One role, `Customer`, is backed by the `Customer` entity, and the same failure applies when `X-User-ID` does not resolve to a `Customer` row.

Because all four shop-floor roles share a single backing entity, the role asserted in the header must agree with the backing technician's `role_code`: `ResinTechnician` requires `role_code` `RESIN_TECH`, `MoldingOperator` requires `MOLDING_OPERATOR`, `AssemblyTechnician` requires `ASSEMBLY_TECH`, and `QualityInspector` requires `QUALITY_INSPECTOR`. Asserting one shop-floor role with the identifier of a technician carrying a different `role_code` fails with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The same error is returned when an endpoint requires the acting principal to be the technician named in the body — for example when `created_by_technician_id` on a resin charge or `operator_technician_id` on a new moulding cycle does not equal the acting `X-User-ID`.

The `Customer` role is scoped: every read and every write it performs must target rows whose `customer_id` equals its own identifier, and a claim, hull or shipment belonging to another customer is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A suspended shop-floor technician (`status` `SUSPENDED`) may not act and may not be named as the subject of an operation; either case yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 403). A technician who lacks the certification an operation requires is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles and their remits are:

- **PlantAdmin** — plant operations administrator, identity opaque. Owns all reference data (hull models, moulds, oven stations, cooling bays, technicians, sites), equipment up-and-down control, mould servicing, the logical clock, and the two administrative overrides: waiving a storage finding on a warranty claim and voiding an already-approved claim. Shares order and site creation with `LogisticsPlanner`, resin-lot intake with `ResinTechnician`, cycle abort with `MoldingOperator`, and may read every collection except the customer-scoped reads.
- **ResinTechnician** — blends and reserves resin charges, identity backed by `Technician` with `role_code` `RESIN_TECH`. Must hold the `RESIN_BLENDING` certification. Exclusively creates and releases resin charges; shares resin-lot intake with `PlantAdmin`; reads resin lots, charges, cycles, orders, hull models and the clock.
- **MoldingOperator** — schedules and runs oven and cooling cycles, identity backed by `Technician` with `role_code` `MOLDING_OPERATOR`. Exclusively creates cycles and drives them through oven start, cooling entry and demould, and becomes each cycle's `operator_technician_id`, which permanently bars that technician from inspecting the hulls the cycle produces. Shares cycle abort with `PlantAdmin`.
- **AssemblyTechnician** — trims hulls and installs hardware and outfitting on fixtures, identity backed by `Technician` with `role_code` `ASSEMBLY_TECH`. Must hold `FIXTURE_ASSEMBLY` to outfit. Becomes the hull's `outfitted_by_technician_id`, which bars that technician from inspecting the hull.
- **QualityInspector** — inspects hulls for wall thickness, impact resistance and cosmetic defects, scraps rejects, and quarantines and releases resin lots. Identity backed by `Technician` with `role_code` `QUALITY_INSPECTOR`. Must hold both `WALL_GAUGE` and `IMPACT_RIG`, and must be neither the producing cycle's operator nor the hull's outfitter.
- **LogisticsPlanner** — owns finished-goods storage, seasonal build-ahead admission, trailer creation, loading, nesting, sealing, dispatch, delivery and the freight summary. Identity opaque. Shares customer, site, demand-bucket and production-order creation, release and cancellation with `PlantAdmin`, and shares the storage-guidance acknowledgement with `PlantAdmin` and `Customer`.
- **WarrantyAgent** — handles hull-deformation, hardware and cosmetic warranty claims: intake, review, the independent storage-guidance review, approval against the model's reserve, denial and return-to-queue. Identity opaque. Cannot waive its own storage finding and cannot void an approval; both of those are reserved to `PlantAdmin`.
- **Customer** — boat dealer or end owner, identity backed by the `Customer` entity. Acknowledges storage guidance for itself, submits claims restricted to its own `customer_id`, and may withdraw its own claim, but never prices or decides one. All of its reads are scoped to its own rows.

Segregation of duties is enforced at inspection and outfitting time and is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Concretely: the inspecting technician may not be the producing cycle's `operator_technician_id`, may not be the hull's `outfitted_by_technician_id`, and an outfitting technician may not be a prior inspector of the same hull.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` bounds the page size; it defaults to 10, must be at least 1 and at most 100, and a value outside that range is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). `offset` is the zero-based index of the first row returned; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. Both are integers; a non-integer value is a schema failure and also yields `ERR_VALIDATION`.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the row identifier as a stable tie-breaker.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are exact-match and are combined with logical AND. Each filter is optional; supplying an unrecognised value for an enum-typed filter is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422), and supplying an identifier that matches no row is not an error — it simply yields an empty page with `total_count` zero.

### 2.4. Common Fields, Types and Precision

Every persisted resource carries four common fields. `id` is a server-generated opaque string identifier, unique across its collection and never derived from business data. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock described in section 2.5; `created_at` is fixed at insertion and `updated_at` advances on every persisted mutation of the row. Every other instant the service emits is rendered in the same normalised form — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.sssZ`, with exactly three fractional digits and a literal `Z` — whether it is a client-supplied instant echoed back, a projected window boundary, a recorded actual time, or a stamp read from the logical clock; an instant a client wrote as `2026-08-05T09:00:00Z` therefore reads back as `2026-08-05T09:00:00.000Z`, and two renderings that denote the same instant are equal regardless of how many fractional digits the client supplied. Date-only fields are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every persisted mutation, including mutations that occur as cascade side effects of a request aimed at a different resource. Where section 3 states how far a particular row's `version` moves inside one request — that a multi-link cascade advances one row by exactly 1 in total however many of its links write that row, or that recomputing a derived roll-up on its own advances nothing — that specific statement governs. `version` is the value the optimistic-concurrency guards in section 2.6 compare against.

Money is always an integer count of cents; no monetary field is ever fractional. Masses in kilograms carry three decimal places, cube volumes in cubic metres carry four, percentages and wall-thickness readings carry two, oven temperature carries one, and nesting factors carry four. Where a field declares a decimal-place count, a supplied value with more precision than that is a schema failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`; where a field declares numeric bounds, a value outside those bounds is reported as `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY` (HTTP 422). A field whose declared type does not match the supplied JSON type, a required field that is absent, and a timestamp or date string that does not parse are all `ERR_VALIDATION`. A value outside an enum's declared set is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.

Unless a calculation says otherwise, rounding is half-up at the stated number of decimal places, and every intermediate result that a later step reads is rounded before that step runs. Several calculations in this document depend on that ordering — notably the wall-thickness distribution, which takes its standard deviation about the already-rounded mean, and the per-charge and per-line money roll-ups, which round each component to whole cents before summation rather than rounding the sum.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read the host wall clock. All timestamps it stamps, all date comparisons it performs, and all expiry and window evaluations are driven by a single mutable logical clock. At startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and that same instant is reported as the clock's default.

`PlantAdmin` may move the clock by submitting a `current_utc` instant. The instant must parse as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp or the request fails with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Setting the clock is not a passive assignment: after the new instant is stored, the service sweeps resin lots whose `expires_on` calendar date now lies strictly before the clock's calendar date and expires them, with all of the cascade consequences described in section 3.2. The clock's read endpoint reports `current_utc`, `default_utc`, and three counters describing what the most recent set operation swept — `expired_resin_lot_count`, `released_resin_charge_count`, `aborted_molding_cycle_count` and `blocked_production_order_count`.

Dates supplied as `YYYY-MM-DD` — a resin lot's `expires_on`, a demand bucket's `window_start_on` and `window_end_on` — are compared against the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC, never against its time of day. Instants supplied as full timestamps are compared instant-to-instant.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency

Five write paths mutate a resource that is not the resource named in the path, and each of those paths requires the caller to declare which revision of that other resource it believes it read. The declaration is a required integer field in the request body, and if the declared number does not equal the current `version` of the named row the write is refused with HTTP 412 and nothing is persisted — no child row is created, no ledger is moved, no cascade runs.

- Creating a moulding cycle requires `expected_production_order_version`, compared against the `version` of the `ProductionOrder` named by `production_order_id`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_ORDER_VERSION`.
- Creating a resin charge requires `expected_resin_lot_version`, compared against the `version` of the `ResinLot` named by `resin_lot_id`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_RESIN_LOT_VERSION`.
- Storing a finished unit requires `expected_warehouse_version`, compared against the `version` of the destination `Warehouse`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_WAREHOUSE_VERSION`.
- Creating a shipment line requires `expected_shipment_version`, compared against the `version` of the `Shipment` named by `shipment_id`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_SHIPMENT_VERSION`.
- Approving a warranty claim requires `expected_product_model_version`, compared against the `version` of the `ProductModel` that owns the warranty reserve being drawn. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_MODEL_VERSION`.

These guards are evaluated late — after authorization, after existence, after schema, after every state and capacity rule the endpoint declares — so a request that is both stale and otherwise invalid reports the other failure, not the stale-version failure. The practical consequence for callers is that any earlier accepted write against the parent row, including one that happened only as a cascade side effect, invalidates a previously read version number and the caller must re-read the parent before retrying.

### 2.7. Validation Order

Each endpoint evaluates its applicable checks in a fixed ascending priority order and returns on the first failure it finds; no endpoint reports two errors, and no later check runs after an earlier one has failed. The bands are:

1. **Authentication and authorization** — header presence, role validity, endpoint permission, identity resolution, role/identity agreement, customer scope.
2. **Existence** — path identifiers first, then body foreign keys, each reported with its entity-specific not-found code where one exists and with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` otherwise.
3. **Schema, range, enum and uniqueness** — required fields, JSON types, numeric bounds, enum membership, measurement-count minimums, date-window sanity, and business-code uniqueness.
4. **Resource and lifecycle state** — the status of the target row and of every row the operation touches, including equipment up/down state, mould and lot disposition, order schedulability, cycle and claim transition legality, and the two independent finished-unit axes.
5. **Product safety and process compliance** — regrind ceiling, lot qualification, melt-index band, charge-mass tolerance, cooling-time floor, cooling-mode agreement, size-class capability, and storage-guidance acknowledgement.
6. **Referential agreement and temporal constraints** — cross-entity field agreement, oven and mould window conflicts, past-dated oven starts, warehouse/trailer origin agreement, build-ahead window membership and warranty term.
7. **Capacity, quota and money** — mould eligibility and occupancy, cooling-bay slots, resin sufficiency, zone and site cube, build-ahead quota, trailer cube and payload, order overbuild, and warranty reserve sufficiency.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the five stale-version guards of section 2.6, last of all.

Five specific precedences are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to invert, and a single request can genuinely violate both members of each pair. When a cycle targets an order that is not schedulable *and* asks for an oven arm window that is already taken, the order-state failure wins. When a candidate charge would both breach the blended regrind ceiling *and* draw more mass than the lot has available, the regrind failure wins. When a hull is stored into a site whose posture is frozen *and* its demand bucket is already at its build-ahead cap, the frozen-site failure wins. When a claim is approved for a suspended customer *and* the payout would exceed the model's remaining reserve, the suspended-customer failure wins. When a claim is approved with an incomplete storage review *and* the hull is out of warranty term, the storage-review failure wins.

### 2.8. Resource Model and Field Reference

Sixteen resources make up the domain. Each carries the four common fields of section 2.4 in addition to the fields listed here. Fields marked as client-supplied are accepted on creation (and, where an endpoint allows it, on update); fields marked as derived are computed by the service and are never accepted from a client — supplying one is ignored rather than honoured, and the service's own value always wins.

**ProductModel** is the configuration hub for a hull design. Client-supplied: `model_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique with case-sensitive exact matching, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `hull_type` (required, `KAYAK` or `CANOE`); `size_class` (required, one of `COMPACT`, `MID`, `TANDEM`, `OVERSIZE`); `resin_grade` (required, one of `HDPE_LINEAR`, `HDPE_XL`, `MDPE`); `master_color_code` (required, 2–16 characters); `max_color_delta_e` (greater than zero, two decimals, default 1.50); `required_additive_package_code` (required, 2–16 characters); `max_regrind_pct` (0.00 to 100.00 inclusive, two decimals, default 30.00, compared with strictly-greater so a charge set exactly at the ceiling passes); `min_melt_index_g10min` (greater than zero, two decimals, default 2.50); `max_melt_index_g10min` (strictly greater than `min_melt_index_g10min`, two decimals, default 6.00); `nominal_charge_kg` (required, greater than zero, three decimals — the design shot mass for one cavity); `charge_tolerance_pct` (greater than 0.00 and at most 25.00, two decimals, default 5.00, compared with strictly-greater so a deviation exactly at tolerance passes); `min_cooling_minutes` (required integer, at least 1, compared at demould with strictly-less so an exactly equal cooling time passes); `target_wall_thickness_mm` (required, greater than zero, two decimals); `wall_thickness_min_mm` (required, greater than zero and strictly less than `target_wall_thickness_mm`, two decimals); `wall_cv_max_pct` (greater than zero, two decimals, default 12.00, compared with strictly-greater); `impact_test_min_joules` (required, greater than zero, two decimals, compared with strictly-less so an exactly equal result passes); `gross_cube_m3` (required, greater than zero, four decimals — the bounding-box cube of one hull); `nest_factor` (required, 0.1000 to 1.0000 inclusive, four decimals — the fraction of gross cube each additional nested hull of the same model adds); `unit_weight_kg` (required, greater than zero, three decimals); `msrp_cents` (required integer, at least 1); `warranty_basis` (required, `PRO_RATA_AGE` or `FLAT_SCHEDULE`); `initial_warranty_reserve_cents` (required integer, at least 0); and `seasonal_family` (required, 2–32 characters). Derived: `warranty_reserve_cents` and `warranty_paid_cents`, both integers defaulting to 0, held under the invariant that their sum always equals `initial_warranty_reserve_cents`.

**Mold** is a physical tool. Client-supplied: `mold_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `product_model_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` — a tool produces exactly one hull model); `size_class` (required, one of the four classes, and must equal the referenced model's `size_class` or `ERR_MOLD_SIZE_CLASS_MISMATCH`); `cavity_count` (required integer, 1 to 4 inclusive — the number of hulls one completed cycle yields); `nominal_charge_kg` (required, greater than zero, three decimals, and must equal the model's `nominal_charge_kg` multiplied by `cavity_count` and rounded to three decimals, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`); `service_interval_cycles` (required integer, at least 1); and the optional `replaces_mold_id` (default null; when present it must resolve or `ERR_MOLD_NOT_FOUND`, must have `status` `RETIRED` or `ERR_MOLD_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and must reference a tool of the same `product_model_id` — and must not be the tool itself — or `ERR_INVALID_MOLD_REPLACEMENT`). Derived: `cycles_since_service` (integer, at least 0, default 0) and `status` (default `AVAILABLE`, one of `AVAILABLE`, `IN_CYCLE`, `MAINTENANCE`, `RETIRED`).

**OvenStation** is the first resource pool. Client-supplied: `station_code` (required, 2–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `arm_count` (required integer, 1 to 6 inclusive — the number of independently schedulable rotating arms); `max_size_class` (required, one of the four classes, which are ordered `COMPACT` below `MID` below `TANDEM` below `OVERSIZE`); `changeover_buffer_min` (required integer, at least 0 — whole minutes of purge and changeover required on both sides of every oven occupancy on a given arm); and `energy_cents_per_min` (required integer, at least 0). Derived: `status` (default `OPERATIONAL`, either `OPERATIONAL` or `DOWN`).

**CoolingBay** is the second resource pool, and its occupancy semantics differ from the oven's on purpose. Client-supplied: `bay_code` (required, 2–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `concurrent_slot_capacity` (required integer, at least 1 — how many cooling windows may overlap in this bay at any instant); and `airflow_mode` (required, one of `AMBIENT`, `FORCED_AIR`, `WATER_MIST`). Derived: `status` (default `OPERATIONAL`, either `OPERATIONAL` or `DOWN`).

**Technician** is the identity anchor for the four shop-floor roles. Client-supplied: `employee_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `full_name` (required, 1–120 characters); `role_code` (required, one of `RESIN_TECH`, `MOLDING_OPERATOR`, `ASSEMBLY_TECH`, `QUALITY_INSPECTOR` — the single shop-floor role this person may assert); and `certifications` (array, default empty, elements drawn without repetition from `RESIN_BLENDING`, `FIXTURE_ASSEMBLY`, `WALL_GAUGE`, `IMPACT_RIG`; an unknown element or a repeated element is `ERR_VALIDATION`). Derived: `status` (default `ACTIVE`, either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`).

**Customer** is the warranty claimant. Client-supplied: `customer_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `email` (required, must contain an `@` character); and `region_code` (required, 2–8 characters, used to select the storage-guidance packet). Derived: `storage_guidance_acknowledged_at` (default null, stamped from the logical clock when the account acknowledges the hull storage guidance) and `status` (default `ACTIVE`, either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`).

**Warehouse** is the third resource pool, measured in cube rather than in time. Client-supplied: `site_code` (required, 2–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `footprint_capacity_cube_m3` (required, greater than zero, four decimals, and must equal the sum of the zone capacities rounded to four decimals or `ERR_VALIDATION`); `zones` (required array of 1 to 12 elements, each an object holding `zone_code` — 2–16 characters and unique within the site, a repeat being `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — and `capacity_cube_m3`, greater than zero with four decimals); `restricted_threshold_pct` (greater than 0.00 and strictly less than `frozen_threshold_pct`, two decimals, default 85.00, compared with at-or-above); and `frozen_threshold_pct` (strictly greater than `restricted_threshold_pct` and at most 100.00, two decimals, default 95.00, compared with at-or-above). Derived: `committed_cube_m3` (four decimals, default 0.0000, held equal to the rounded sum of `stored_footprint_cube_m3` over the site's units whose `logistics_status` is `STORED` or `LOADED`); `utilization_pct` (two decimals, default 0.00); and `posture` (default `NORMAL`, one of `NORMAL`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`).

**DemandForecastBucket** is a seasonal build-ahead quota window. Client-supplied: `season_code` (required, 3–32 characters, unique in combination with `product_model_id`, a repeat of that pair being `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `product_model_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`); `forecast_qty` (required integer, at least 1); `build_ahead_cap_pct` (greater than 0.00 and at most 100.00, two decimals, default 60.00); `window_start_on` (required, `YYYY-MM-DD`); and `window_end_on` (required, `YYYY-MM-DD`, not earlier than `window_start_on` — an inverted window is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`). Derived: `build_ahead_cap_qty` (integer, default 0) and `build_ahead_committed_qty` (integer, default 0, never exceeding `build_ahead_cap_qty`).

**ResinLot** is polyethylene inventory under a three-bucket mass ledger. Client-supplied: `lot_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `resin_grade` (required, one of `HDPE_LINEAR`, `HDPE_XL`, `MDPE`); `melt_index_g10min` (required, greater than zero, two decimals); `color_code` (required, 2–16 characters); `color_delta_e` (required, at least 0, two decimals — the measured CIE dE against the lot's colour master); `additive_package_code` (required, 2–16 characters); `regrind_pct_by_mass` (0.00 to 100.00 inclusive, two decimals, default 0.00); `cost_cents_per_kg` (required integer, at least 0); `received_kg` (required, greater than zero, three decimals — the conserved total of the ledger); and `expires_on` (required, `YYYY-MM-DD`). Derived: `available_kg`, `reserved_kg` and `consumed_kg` (each three decimals, default 0.000, held under the invariant that their rounded sum always equals `received_kg`), and `status` (default `AVAILABLE`, one of `AVAILABLE`, `DEPLETED`, `QUARANTINED`, `EXPIRED`). On intake `available_kg` is set to `received_kg` while the other two buckets stay at zero.

**ResinCharge** couples a cycle to a lot. Client-supplied: `molding_cycle_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_MOLDING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`); `resin_lot_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_RESIN_LOT_NOT_FOUND`); `created_by_technician_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, and must equal the acting identifier or `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`); `charge_kg` (required, strictly greater than 0.000 or `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, three decimals); and `regrind_kg` (default 0.000, at least 0.000 and not greater than `charge_kg` or `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, three decimals). Derived: `virgin_kg` (`charge_kg` minus `regrind_kg` rounded to three decimals, default 0.000); `charge_regrind_pct` (default 0.00, two decimals); `status` (default `RESERVED`, one of `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED`); and `released_reason` (default null, and when set one of `CYCLE_ABORTED`, `LOT_QUARANTINED`, `LOT_EXPIRED`, `ORDER_CANCELLED`, `STATION_DOWN`, `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`).

**ProductionOrder** is a compound aggregate whose status is a function of its children and of the resources it depends on. Client-supplied: `order_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `product_model_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`); the optional `demand_bucket_id` (default null; when present it must resolve or `ERR_DEMAND_BUCKET_NOT_FOUND`, and its own `product_model_id` must equal the order's or `ERR_VALIDATION`); `target_warehouse_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_WAREHOUSE_NOT_FOUND`); and `ordered_qty` (required integer, at least 1 or `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`). Derived: `completed_qty` and `scrapped_qty` (integers, default 0); `status` (default `DRAFT`, one of `DRAFT`, `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, `BLOCKED_RESIN`, `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `COMPLETE`, `CANCELLED`); `blocked_reason` (default null, and when set either `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT` or `WAREHOUSE_FROZEN`); and the four money fields `material_cost_cents`, `oven_energy_cost_cents`, `overhead_cents`, `total_cost_cents` together with `unit_cost_cents`, all integers defaulting to 0.

**MoldingCycle** is the temporal core; one row books three resource pools at once. Client-supplied: `production_order_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_PRODUCTION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`); `oven_station_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_OVEN_STATION_NOT_FOUND`); `arm_index` (required integer, at least 1 and no greater than the station's `arm_count` or `ERR_VALIDATION`); `cooling_bay_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_COOLING_BAY_NOT_FOUND`); `operator_technician_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, and must equal the acting identifier at creation or `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`); `oven_start_at` (required ISO 8601 UTC instant); `scheduled_oven_minutes` (required integer, 1 to 600 inclusive); `scheduled_cooling_minutes` (required integer, 1 to 600 inclusive); `oven_temp_c` (required, at least 200.0 and at most 360.0, one decimal, out-of-band values rejected as `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`); `arm_rpm_major` (required, greater than zero, two decimals); `arm_rpm_minor` (required, strictly greater than zero or `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, two decimals); and `cooling_mode` (required, one of `AMBIENT`, `FORCED_AIR`, `WATER_MIST`, and must equal the booked bay's `airflow_mode` or `ERR_COOLING_MODE_MISMATCH`). Derived: `mold_id`, always chosen by the server and never accepted from a client; `oven_end_at` (`oven_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_oven_minutes`); `cooling_window_start_at` (equal to `oven_end_at`); `cooling_window_end_at` (`cooling_window_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_cooling_minutes`); the actual-time stamps `actual_oven_start_at`, `actual_oven_end_at` and `actual_cooling_end_at` (all default null); the elapsed integers `actual_oven_minutes` and `actual_cooling_minutes` (default 0); the charge roll-ups `charged_mass_kg` (three decimals, default 0.000), `weighted_melt_index_g10min` (two decimals, default 0.00) and `cycle_regrind_pct` (two decimals, default 0.00); `provisional_warpage_index` (default null, three decimals, clamped between 0.000 and 5.000); `status` (default `SCHEDULED`, one of `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING`, `COOLING`, `DEMOLDED`, `ABORTED`); and `abort_reason` (default null, and when set one of `OPERATOR_ABORT`, `LOT_QUARANTINED`, `LOT_EXPIRED`, `ORDER_CANCELLED`, `STATION_DOWN`, `BAY_DOWN`, `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`).

**FinishedUnit** is never created directly; it is minted by the demould cascade, and it carries two independent state axes on one row. Set by the cascade and thereafter immutable: `molding_cycle_id`, `product_model_id` (copied from the producing tool) and `cavity_index` (a 1-based cavity position no greater than the tool's `cavity_count`). Derived thereafter: `hull_serial` (globally unique, deterministic, never derived from the row identifier); `warehouse_id` (default null, non-null only while the hull is stored, loaded or in transit, and then always equal to the producing order's `target_warehouse_id`); `outfitted_by_technician_id` and `inspected_by_technician_id` (both default null, and never equal to one another); `trim_waste_kg` (three decimals, default 0.000); `hardware_kit_serial` (default null, 3–32 characters when set); `wall_thickness_points` (array, default empty, each element greater than zero with two decimals); `mean_wall_thickness_mm` and `wall_sd_mm` (three decimals, default null); `wall_cv_pct` and `min_wall_thickness_mm` (two decimals, default null); `impact_test_joules` (at least 0, two decimals, default null); `cosmetic_defect_grade` (default null, one of `A`, `B`, `C`, `REJECT`); `final_warpage_index` (three decimals, default null, clamped between 0.000 and 5.000); `inspection_verdict` (default `PENDING`, one of `PENDING`, `PASS`, `REWORK`, `SCRAP`); `rework_count` (integer, default 0, never above 2); `build_status` (default `DEMOLDED`, one of `DEMOLDED`, `TRIMMED`, `OUTFITTED`, `SELLABLE`, `REWORK`, `SCRAPPED`); `logistics_status` (default `UNALLOCATED`, one of `UNALLOCATED`, `STORED`, `LOADED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`); `storage_zone_code` (default null, always one of the site's declared zone codes when set); `stored_footprint_cube_m3` (four decimals, default 0.0000, zero whenever the hull is not stored); `is_zone_anchor` (boolean, default false, with at most one anchor per site-zone-model group among stored hulls); `stored_at` (default null, stamped on storage and cleared on release); `demand_bucket_id` (default null, copied from the producing order at storage time); and `delivered_at` (default null — the warranty clock origin).

**Shipment** is a trailer. Client-supplied: `shipment_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `origin_warehouse_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_WAREHOUSE_NOT_FOUND`); `destination_zone` (required, one of `Z1`, `Z2`, `Z3`); `carrier_code` (required, 2–16 characters); `trailer_cube_capacity_m3` (required, greater than zero, four decimals); and `trailer_max_payload_kg` (required, greater than zero, three decimals). Derived: `loaded_cube_m3` and `nested_cube_m3` (four decimals, default 0.0000); `loaded_weight_kg` (three decimals, default 0.000); `cube_utilization_pct` and `density_kg_per_m3` (two decimals, default 0.00, the latter being zero when no line is loaded); `quoted_freight_cents` and `billed_freight_cents` (integers, default null); `status` (default `PLANNED`, one of `PLANNED`, `LOADING`, `SEALED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED`); and the stamps `sealed_at`, `departed_at` and `delivered_at`, all default null.

**ShipmentLine** is the junction that realises the many-to-many between trailers and hulls, and it is the unit of freight rounding. Client-supplied: `shipment_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`) and `finished_unit_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_FINISHED_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, with at most one loaded line per hull — a second one is `ERR_UNIT_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE`). Derived: `nest_position` (integer, default 1, at least 1); `line_gross_cube_m3` and `line_nested_cube_m3` (four decimals, default 0.0000); `line_freight_cents` (integer, default 0); and `status` (default `LOADED`, either `LOADED` or `REMOVED`).

**WarrantyClaim** carries two independent axes and draws on shared model money. Client-supplied: `claim_code` (required, 3–32 characters, globally unique, duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`); `finished_unit_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_FINISHED_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`); `customer_id` (required, must resolve or `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, and when the acting role is `Customer` it must equal the acting identifier or `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); `claim_type` (required, one of `HULL_DEFORMATION`, `HARDWARE`, `COSMETIC`); `reported_deformation_mm` (default 0.00, at least 0, two decimals, and for a `HULL_DEFORMATION` claim at least the 3.00 mm deformation threshold or `ERR_DEFORMATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`); and `storage_description` (required, 10–500 characters). Derived: `claim_status` (default `SUBMITTED`, one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `VOIDED`); `storage_review_status` (default `NOT_STARTED`, one of `NOT_STARTED`, `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED`, `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED`, `WAIVED`); `months_in_service` (integer, default 0); `payout_basis` (default null, copied from the model's `warranty_basis` at approval, either `PRO_RATA_AGE` or `FLAT_SCHEDULE`); the money fields `pre_fee_gross_cents`, `storage_deduction_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_payout_cents` (integers, default null, each at least 0 once set); and `denial_reason` (default null, and when set one of `STORAGE_GUIDANCE_VIOLATED`, `NOT_A_MANUFACTURING_DEFECT`, `TERM_EXPIRED`, `APPROVAL_VOIDED`).

### 2.9. Resource Occupancy and Interval Arithmetic

A moulding cycle books three resources over time, and the three use deliberately different boundary conventions. Implementers must not unify them.

When a cycle is scheduled, three projected instants are derived from the client's inputs: `oven_end_at` is `oven_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_oven_minutes`; `cooling_window_start_at` is exactly `oven_end_at`, so the oven and cooling phases are butt-jointed with no gap; and `cooling_window_end_at` is `cooling_window_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_cooling_minutes`. Those projections are what the three overlap checks read while the cycle is scheduled, and they are frozen once the cycle's status leaves `SCHEDULED`. Timestamps are compared at second resolution.

The **oven arm** is an exclusive resource, and the resource is the arm rather than the station: cycles sharing an `oven_station_id` but holding different `arm_index` values never conflict, so a station whose `arm_count` is 4 can hold four fully coincident windows. Occupancy is the half-open span from `oven_start_at` up to but not including `oven_end_at`. The station's `changeover_buffer_min` is applied symmetrically to both ends of each *already-stored* cycle's span — never to the candidate's — so the buffer is counted exactly once per comparison. Only cycles in `SCHEDULED` or `OVEN_RUNNING` occupy an arm; `COOLING`, `DEMOLDED` and `ABORTED` cycles do not. A single overlap is enough to refuse the booking with `ERR_OVEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the span is half-open, a new cycle whose `oven_start_at` equals an existing cycle's `oven_end_at` plus the buffer, to the second, is accepted, while one starting a second earlier is refused.

The **cooling bay** differs on all three axes: capacity is aggregate rather than exclusive, the interval is closed rather than half-open, and no buffer applies. The occupants counted are cycles in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` sharing the `cooling_bay_id` — a cycle still in the oven already holds its future bay slot — and the interval tested is the closed span from `cooling_window_start_at` through `cooling_window_end_at` inclusive. Because the interval is closed, a new window whose start equals an existing window's end to the second *does* intersect and consumes a slot, which is the exact opposite of the arm rule. When the count of intersecting cycles has already reached the bay's `concurrent_slot_capacity`, the booking is refused with `ERR_COOLING_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); a bay whose capacity is 2 therefore accepts a second overlapping cycle and refuses the third. The constraint is collective, so every individual window can be legal and the booking still fail.

The **mould** is exclusive over the union of both phases: the half-open span from `oven_start_at` up to but not including `cooling_window_end_at`, evaluated against cycles in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING`. A new cycle whose `oven_start_at` equals an existing cycle's `cooling_window_end_at` to the second is accepted.

Actual elapsed minutes are always **floored**, never rounded. `actual_oven_minutes` is the whole minutes between `actual_oven_start_at` and `actual_oven_end_at`, and `actual_cooling_minutes` is the whole minutes between `actual_oven_end_at` and `actual_cooling_end_at`. Flooring has a consequence the plant relies on: 29 minutes and 59 seconds of cooling is 29 minutes, so a demould one second early breaches the model's `min_cooling_minutes` floor and is refused with `ERR_COOLING_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). A cooling time exactly equal to `min_cooling_minutes` passes.

### 2.10. Derived Values, Ledgers and Cascade Discipline

No derived value in this service is an independently maintained counter that can drift. Each is defined as an aggregate over a named row set with a named status filter, and each is recomputed from that set inside every request that could change it. In particular: a warehouse's `committed_cube_m3` is always the rounded sum of `stored_footprint_cube_m3` over its units in `logistics_status` `STORED` or `LOADED`; a demand bucket's `build_ahead_committed_qty` is always the count of its charged units that are `SELLABLE` and physically present (`STORED` or `LOADED`); a cycle's `charged_mass_kg` is always the rounded sum of `charge_kg` over its charges in `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`; and an order's `completed_qty` and `scrapped_qty` are always counts over its units' two axes. Nothing is cached across requests.

Two ledgers are conserved and must be checked before commit. A resin lot holds a three-bucket ledger in which the rounded sum of `available_kg`, `reserved_kg` and `consumed_kg` always equals the immutable `received_kg`. Reserving mass moves it from available to reserved; consuming it at oven start moves it from reserved to consumed; releasing a still-reserved charge moves it back from reserved to available. Consumption is irreversible — `consumed_kg` is never decremented — which is why an abort after the oven has started returns no resin. A hull model holds a two-bucket ledger in which `warranty_reserve_cents` plus `warranty_paid_cents` always equals the immutable `initial_warranty_reserve_cents`; approving a claim moves the net payout from reserve to paid, and voiding an approval moves the identical integer back.

Cascades are transactional and complete inside the triggering request. When any link of a cascade cannot be applied, or when any validation the triggering request declares fails, nothing at all is persisted: no child row, no ledger movement, no status change, no version increment. Conversely, when a cascade succeeds, every row it touched has its `updated_at` restamped and its `version` incremented, which is precisely why a caller holding a version number read before a cascade must re-read before attempting an optimistic-concurrency write. Section 3 names each cascade and the exact order of its links.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The plant's work divides into nine themes: standing up reference data and equipment, receiving and qualifying resin, releasing build orders and keeping their blocked states honest, booking oven and cooling windows, running a cycle from oven start to demould, finishing and judging hulls, nesting hulls into warehouse zones under a seasonal quota, loading and freighting trailers, and adjudicating warranty claims against a conserved reserve. Eight named cascades — A through H — connect the themes; each is listed with its links in the order they must run. Every rule stated here is enforced on the write paths described; nothing in this section is advisory.

### 3.1. Standing Up the Plant: Models, Tooling, Equipment and People

`PlantAdmin` alone registers hull models, mould tools, oven stations and cooling bays, and alone registers technicians. Registering a hull model establishes its process envelope and, at the same instant, opens its warranty ledger: `warranty_reserve_cents` is set to the supplied `initial_warranty_reserve_cents` and `warranty_paid_cents` to 0, and `version` starts at 1. A model's structural identity — `model_code`, `hull_type`, `size_class`, `warranty_basis` and `initial_warranty_reserve_cents` — is immutable after creation. The amendable fields are the process and quality limits: `max_color_delta_e`, `max_regrind_pct`, `min_melt_index_g10min`, `max_melt_index_g10min`, `nominal_charge_kg`, `charge_tolerance_pct`, `min_cooling_minutes`, `target_wall_thickness_mm`, `wall_thickness_min_mm`, `wall_cv_max_pct`, `impact_test_min_joules`, `gross_cube_m3`, `nest_factor`, `unit_weight_kg` and `msrp_cents`. Amending them does not re-judge any measurement already stored on a hull; the new limits bind subsequent inspections only, and the model's `version` advances by 1. Models are listed with optional `hull_type`, `size_class`, `resin_grade`, `warranty_basis` and `seasonal_family` filters, and a single-model read exposes the live `warranty_reserve_cents` and `warranty_paid_cents`.

A mould tool belongs to exactly one hull model. When it is registered, `cycles_since_service` starts at 0 and `status` at `AVAILABLE`. The optional `replaces_mold_id` records the worn-out tool this one succeeds; when supplied it must resolve (`ERR_MOLD_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404), must name a *different* tool of the *same* `product_model_id`, and that tool's `status` must already be `RETIRED` — any of those three failing yields `ERR_INVALID_MOLD_REPLACEMENT` (HTTP 422). A tool whose `size_class` disagrees with its model's is rejected with `ERR_MOLD_SIZE_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Tools are listed with optional `product_model_id`, `replaces_mold_id`, `status` and `size_class` filters, so the replacement chain is traversable in both directions.

The mould lifecycle has four states and eight edges, all reserved to `PlantAdmin` except the two that the shop floor drives implicitly. From `AVAILABLE`, a service hold moves the tool to `MAINTENANCE`; the request may carry a `hold_note`. The hold is not a passive flag — it is the head of a compensating cascade: every cycle on that tool still in `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`; each of those cycles' `RESERVED` charges becomes `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`, moving `charge_kg` from the lot's `reserved_kg` back to its `available_kg`; any lot that was `DEPLETED` and now holds mass returns to `AVAILABLE`; and every order in `BLOCKED_RESIN` that those revived lots qualify for is unblocked. The tool's `version` advances by 1. A hold attempted on a tool that is not `AVAILABLE` is `ERR_MOLD_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).

Completing service requires `status` `MAINTENANCE`, may carry a `service_note`, resets `cycles_since_service` to zero — a full reset, never a decrement by the interval, so a serviced tool with a hundred lifetime demoulds reads 0 — and returns `status` to `AVAILABLE`, restoring the tool to the selection pool. Retiring a tool requires a `retire_reason` and requires `status` to be `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE`; `RETIRED` is terminal and makes the tool a legal `replaces_mold_id` target. A retirement is refused with `ERR_MOLD_HAS_ACTIVE_CYCLES` (HTTP 409) when any cycle on that tool sits in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING`. The remaining edges are driven by production: starting an oven run moves the tool `AVAILABLE` to `IN_CYCLE`; a completed demould returns it `IN_CYCLE` to `AVAILABLE` (or sends it straight to `MAINTENANCE` when its counter has reached its interval); and an abort returns it `IN_CYCLE` to `AVAILABLE` without wearing it.

Oven stations and cooling bays each have exactly two states, `OPERATIONAL` and `DOWN`, and both begin `OPERATIONAL`. Taking a station down requires a `down_reason` and is the head of **CASCADE-E**, whose five links run in this order: (1) the station's `status` moves `OPERATIONAL` to `DOWN`; (2) every cycle on the station in `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `STATION_DOWN`; (3) their `RESERVED` charges become `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `STATION_DOWN`; (4) lots that regain mass move `DEPLETED` to `AVAILABLE`; and (5) orders in `BLOCKED_RESIN` that those lots now qualify for move to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, except that an order whose target site posture is `FROZEN` moves to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` instead. Cycles already `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` are deliberately untouched — the heat is already in the part. Taking a station down that is already `DOWN` is `ERR_OVEN_STATION_DOWN` (HTTP 409); restoring a station that is already up is `ERR_OVEN_STATION_ALREADY_OPERATIONAL` (HTTP 409). Restoring takes no body, moves `DOWN` to `OPERATIONAL`, and does not resurrect a single aborted cycle.

Taking a cooling bay down works the same way and also requires a `down_reason`: `SCHEDULED` cycles booked into that bay become `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `BAY_DOWN`, their `RESERVED` charges are released, revived lots unblock resin-blocked orders, and cycles already `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` are left alone. A bay already down reports `ERR_COOLING_BAY_DOWN`, and restoring an operational bay reports `ERR_COOLING_BAY_ALREADY_OPERATIONAL`. Stations are listed with optional `status` and `max_size_class` filters, bays with optional `status` and `airflow_mode` filters.

Technicians move between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`. A technician is created `ACTIVE` with a `role_code` and a `certifications` array; the created identifier is the value that person presents as `X-User-ID`. Suspension requires a `suspend_reason` and requires `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 403). Suspension mutates no cycle and no hull: it only causes that technician's *next* action to be refused, because every shop-floor write first checks that the acting technician is `ACTIVE`. Reinstatement takes no body, requires `status` `SUSPENDED`, and otherwise reports `ERR_TECHNICIAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Technicians are listed with optional `role_code` and `status` filters.

Customers are registered by `PlantAdmin` or `LogisticsPlanner` from a `customer_code`, an `email` and a `region_code`, starting `ACTIVE` with `storage_guidance_acknowledged_at` null. Acknowledging the hull storage guidance — permitted to `PlantAdmin`, `LogisticsPlanner` or the `Customer` acting on its own record — stores an explicit `acknowledged_at` instant into `storage_guidance_acknowledged_at`. That timestamp matters later: a warranty storage review can only verify when the acknowledgement is dated at or before the claimed hull's `delivered_at`. Suspending a customer requires a `suspend_reason` and `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409); the suspension voids that customer's open warranty claims, moving every claim in `claim_status` `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` to `VOIDED` with `denial_reason` `APPROVAL_VOIDED`. No money moves on those voids, because no payout had been booked. Reinstatement requires `status` `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), and it does *not* resurrect a voided claim. Customers are listed with optional `region_code` and `status` filters.

Warehouses are registered by `PlantAdmin` or `LogisticsPlanner` with a `zones` array whose per-zone `capacity_cube_m3` values must sum, at four decimals, to the declared `footprint_capacity_cube_m3`. `committed_cube_m3` starts at 0.0000, `utilization_pct` at 0.00 and `posture` at `NORMAL`; `restricted_threshold_pct` and `frozen_threshold_pct` default to 85.00 and 95.00 when omitted. Sites are listed with an optional `posture` filter. A computed footprint read reports `warehouse_id`, `footprint_capacity_cube_m3`, `committed_cube_m3`, `remaining_cube_m3`, `utilization_pct`, `posture`, a `zones` array of `{ zone_code, capacity_cube_m3, committed_cube_m3, remaining_cube_m3, anchor_product_model_ids, stored_unit_count }`, and `blocked_production_order_count` — the number of orders sitting in `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` that target this site — so the nesting arithmetic of section 3.7 is observable without recomputation.

Demand buckets are declared by `PlantAdmin` or `LogisticsPlanner`. A bucket names one `product_model_id`, a `season_code` unique in combination with that model, a `forecast_qty`, an inclusive date window `window_start_on` through `window_end_on`, and an optional `build_ahead_cap_pct` defaulting to 60.00. On creation `build_ahead_cap_qty` is computed as the floor of `forecast_qty` multiplied by `build_ahead_cap_pct` divided by 100 and `build_ahead_committed_qty` starts at 0. Flooring is load-bearing: a `forecast_qty` of 25 at 60.00 caps at 15, and a `forecast_qty` of 26 also caps at 15. An inverted window is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422); a model that does not resolve is `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Buckets are listed with optional `product_model_id` and `season_code` filters.

### 3.2. Resin Intake, Qualification, Reservation and the Mass Ledger

Resin lots are received by `PlantAdmin` or `ResinTechnician`. Intake records `lot_code`, `resin_grade`, `melt_index_g10min`, `color_code`, `color_delta_e`, `additive_package_code`, `regrind_pct_by_mass`, `cost_cents_per_kg`, `received_kg` and `expires_on`. On acceptance `available_kg` is set to `received_kg`, `reserved_kg` and `consumed_kg` are set to 0.000, `status` is `AVAILABLE`, and `version` is 1. Intake is itself an unblocking event: every order sitting in `BLOCKED_RESIN` that this new lot qualifies for is moved to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, or to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` when that order's target site posture is `FROZEN`. Lots are listed with optional `resin_grade`, `color_code`, `additive_package_code`, `status` and `expires_on_or_before` filters, and a single-lot read exposes the live three-bucket ledger.

**Lot qualification** is the predicate that decides everything downstream. A lot qualifies for a hull model when *all* of the following hold: the lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, so `QUARANTINED`, `EXPIRED` and `DEPLETED` lots never qualify; the lot's `resin_grade` exactly equals the model's `resin_grade`; the lot's `additive_package_code` exactly equals the model's `required_additive_package_code`; either the lot's `color_code` exactly equals the model's `master_color_code` *or* the lot's `color_delta_e` is at most the model's `max_color_delta_e`, a lot sitting exactly at the maximum delta-E qualifying; the lot's `melt_index_g10min` lies inside the model's band with *both* bounds inclusive, from `min_melt_index_g10min` to `max_melt_index_g10min`; the lot's `regrind_pct_by_mass` is at most the model's `max_regrind_pct`, a lot exactly at the ceiling qualifying; the lot's `expires_on` is on or after the logical clock's calendar date, so a lot expiring today still qualifies; and the lot's `available_kg` is at least the model's minimum shot mass, computed as `nominal_charge_kg` multiplied by one minus `charge_tolerance_pct` divided by 100, with that threshold rounded to three decimals *before* the comparison. The qualified lot count for a model is the number of lots satisfying that predicate. It is never cached: it is recomputed inside any request that changes a lot's `status`, a lot's `available_kg`, the logical clock date, or the model's grade, colour, additive, melt-index or regrind fields.

The same count is read in two different ways. Used as a **release gate**, a count of zero sends an order to `BLOCKED_RESIN` with `blocked_reason` `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT` instead of `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, and the releasing request still succeeds with HTTP 200. Used as an **unblock sweep** from any cascade link that releases a charge, clears a quarantine or completes a service, a count of one or more moves `BLOCKED_RESIN` orders to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` — unless the order's target site posture is `FROZEN`, in which case the very same count sends it to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` instead. Implementers must keep those two destinations distinct; the count alone does not determine the destination state.

`ResinTechnician` alone reserves a charge, and only a technician holding the `RESIN_BLENDING` certification may do so — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 403). A charge names `molding_cycle_id`, `resin_lot_id`, `charge_kg`, `regrind_kg`, and the concurrency declaration `expected_resin_lot_version`. Both foreign keys must resolve. The cycle must be in `SCHEDULED`; the lot's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409); the lot must qualify for the cycle's hull model, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_LOT_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 422), and a melt index outside the model's band is reported specifically as `ERR_MELT_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). On acceptance `status` is `RESERVED`, `created_by_technician_id` is the acting identifier, `virgin_kg` is `charge_kg` minus `regrind_kg` rounded to three decimals, and `charge_regrind_pct` is `regrind_kg` divided by `charge_kg` times 100 rounded to two decimals. The lot's `available_kg` falls and its `reserved_kg` rises by `charge_kg`, preserving the invariant that the rounded sum of `available_kg`, `reserved_kg` and `consumed_kg` equals `received_kg`; a lot whose `available_kg` reaches exactly 0.000 moves `AVAILABLE` to `DEPLETED`. The cycle's `charged_mass_kg`, `weighted_melt_index_g10min` and `cycle_regrind_pct` are recomputed, and both the lot's and the cycle's `version` advance by 1. Charges are listed with optional `molding_cycle_id`, `resin_lot_id`, `created_by_technician_id`, `status` and `released_reason` filters.

Two ceilings guard the reservation, and their relative order matters. The **blended regrind ceiling** is evaluated first, on the post-add blend: over the cycle's charges in `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`, `total_regrind_kg` is the sum of `regrind_kg` rounded to three decimals and `total_charge_kg` is the sum of `charge_kg` rounded to three decimals; the cycle's regrind percentage is then `total_regrind_kg` divided by `total_charge_kg` times 100, rounded to two decimals — the two masses are rounded *before* the division, and when `total_charge_kg` is 0.000 the percentage is 0.00. A percentage strictly greater than the model's `max_regrind_pct` is refused with `ERR_REGRIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); a blend landing at exactly the ceiling — 30.00 against a default ceiling of 30.00 — is accepted, and 30.01 is not. Only after that ceiling passes is **mass availability** tested: the lot's `available_kg` must be at least `charge_kg`, equality being legal and draining the lot to exactly 0.000, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). A candidate charge that would both breach the ceiling and overdraw the lot reports the regrind failure, never the insufficiency. A refused charge writes nothing: no row, no mass movement, no version increment.

Releasing a charge is the compensating move and belongs to `ResinTechnician`. The charge's `status` must be `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_RELEASABLE` (HTTP 409) — a `CONSUMED` charge can never be released, because consumption is irreversible. The optional `release_reason` supplies `released_reason`; when it is omitted, `released_reason` is set to `CYCLE_ABORTED`. On release `charge_kg` moves from the lot's `reserved_kg` back to its `available_kg`, a `DEPLETED` lot that regains mass returns to `AVAILABLE` and unblocks the resin-blocked orders it qualifies for, and the cycle's charge roll-ups are recomputed over the surviving participating charges. The charge's, the lot's and the cycle's `version` each advance by 1.

The **charge roll-ups** on a cycle read only charges in `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`; a `RELEASED` charge drops out the instant it is released, which is why every release path recomputes them. `charged_mass_kg` is the sum of `charge_kg` over the participating charges rounded to three decimals. `weighted_melt_index_g10min` is the mass-weighted mean melt index: the sum, over participating charges, of `charge_kg` multiplied by that charge's lot's `melt_index_g10min`, divided by `charged_mass_kg`, and rounded to two decimals *once* at the end — the per-charge products are never rounded individually, because doing so drifts. When `charged_mass_kg` is 0.000 the weighted index is null. While the cycle is `SCHEDULED` the participating charges are all `RESERVED` and the roll-up can still shrink; once the cycle has reached `OVEN_RUNNING`, `COOLING` or `DEMOLDED` they are all `CONSUMED` and the roll-up is frozen.

`QualityInspector` alone quarantines a lot, supplying a `quarantine_reason`, and quarantine is the head of **CASCADE-F**: (1) the lot moves `AVAILABLE` or `DEPLETED` to `QUARANTINED`; (2) every `RESERVED` charge drawn on that lot becomes `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `LOT_QUARANTINED`, moving `charge_kg` from `reserved_kg` back to `available_kg`; (3) every cycle in `SCHEDULED` that held one of those charges becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `LOT_QUARANTINED`; and (4) every affected order left with no remaining qualified lot moves to `BLOCKED_RESIN` with `blocked_reason` `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT`. The lot's `version` advances by 1. Releasing a quarantine, also reserved to `QualityInspector`, carries a `release_note` and requires `status` `QUARANTINED`; the lot returns to `AVAILABLE` when `available_kg` is strictly greater than 0.000 and to `DEPLETED` otherwise. A return to `AVAILABLE` unblocks the `BLOCKED_RESIN` orders the lot qualifies for, again diverting to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` where the target site is frozen. Cycles aborted by the quarantine are never resurrected.

Expiry is driven by the logical clock rather than by any lot-level operation, and it is **CASCADE-G**. When `PlantAdmin` sets `current_utc`, the service (1) stores the new instant, then (2) moves every lot whose `expires_on` is *strictly before* the new clock's calendar date to `EXPIRED` — a lot whose `expires_on` equals the new date is *not* expired; (3) every `RESERVED` charge on those lots becomes `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `LOT_EXPIRED`, returning `charge_kg` to `available_kg`; (4) every `SCHEDULED` cycle holding one of those charges becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `LOT_EXPIRED`; and (5) every order left with no qualified lot moves to `BLOCKED_RESIN` with `blocked_reason` `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT`. The response reports `current_utc`, the constant `default_utc` of `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the four sweep counters `expired_resin_lot_count`, `released_resin_charge_count`, `aborted_molding_cycle_count` and `blocked_production_order_count`. The clock is never rolled backward past a recorded actual timestamp. A lot in `EXPIRED` is terminal for qualification purposes: nothing returns it to `AVAILABLE`.

### 3.3. Build Orders: Release, Blocking, Completion and Cost

A build order is created by `PlantAdmin` or `LogisticsPlanner` from an `order_code`, a `product_model_id`, a `target_warehouse_id`, an `ordered_qty` and an optional `demand_bucket_id`. It starts in `DRAFT` with `completed_qty` and `scrapped_qty` at 0, all four cost fields at 0, `unit_cost_cents` null and `version` 1. A supplied `demand_bucket_id` must belong to the same hull model as the order. Orders are listed with optional `product_model_id`, `demand_bucket_id`, `target_warehouse_id`, `status` and `blocked_reason` filters.

**Release** is permitted to `PlantAdmin` and `LogisticsPlanner`, takes no body, and requires `status` `DRAFT`. Five preconditions are evaluated: the order is `DRAFT`; `ordered_qty` is at least 1; the target site's `posture` is not `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_WAREHOUSE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409); at least one mould exists for the order's hull model in `status` `AVAILABLE`, `IN_CYCLE` or `MAINTENANCE`, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOLD` (HTTP 409); and at least one resin lot qualifies for the model, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). When all five hold the order moves `DRAFT` to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, `blocked_reason` is cleared to null, the cost roll-up is recomputed (all zeros at this point, since no charge is consumed and no cycle demoulded), and `version` advances by 1. Two soft outcomes also exist, and both still return HTTP 200: when the target site's `posture` is `FROZEN` the order moves `DRAFT` to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` with `blocked_reason` `WAREHOUSE_FROZEN`; when the qualified-lot count is zero the order moves `DRAFT` to `BLOCKED_RESIN` with `blocked_reason` `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT`. The warehouse test is evaluated *before* the mould test and *before* the resin test, so an order that is both frozen-out and resin-starved lands in `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE`, and an order that is both frozen-out and tool-less also lands in `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` with HTTP 200 rather than reporting `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOLD`: once the target site's posture is found to be `FROZEN` the mould gate is not consulted at all.

Once released, the order's status tracks its children and its dependencies through twelve system-driven edges. The first accepted cycle moves `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` to `IN_PRODUCTION`; a cycle created while the order is already `IN_PRODUCTION` leaves the status alone but still advances `version` by 1, which is the same increment the concurrency guard of section 2.6 consumes. When the completion roll-up finds `completed_qty` at or above `ordered_qty` while the order is `IN_PRODUCTION`, the order moves to `COMPLETE` and its cost roll-up is recomputed; surplus cycles already scheduled are *not* aborted and remain schedulable. When a demotion drops `completed_qty` below `ordered_qty` while the order is `COMPLETE`, the order returns to `IN_PRODUCTION` and the cost roll-up is recomputed — this edge makes the machine cyclic, and `COMPLETE` to `IN_PRODUCTION` to `COMPLETE` may repeat with each demotion and re-pass.

Four blocking edges and two unblocking edges complete the machine. From either `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, an order whose qualified-lot count has fallen to zero while `completed_qty` is still below `ordered_qty` moves to `BLOCKED_RESIN` with `blocked_reason` `NO_QUALIFIED_RESIN_LOT`; that is what the quarantine and expiry cascades trigger. From either of the same two states, an order whose target site has just become `FROZEN` moves to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` with `blocked_reason` `WAREHOUSE_FROZEN`; that is what the storage cascade triggers. An order in `BLOCKED_RESIN` for which a qualified lot has returned to `AVAILABLE` moves to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` with `blocked_reason` cleared **only if** the target site's `posture` is not `FROZEN`; when the site is frozen at that same instant the order moves `BLOCKED_RESIN` to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` with `blocked_reason` `WAREHOUSE_FROZEN` instead, so the two blocking reasons are strictly ordered rather than ambiguous. An order in `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` whose site has left `FROZEN` — that is, whose `posture` is now `NORMAL` or `RESTRICTED` — moves to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` with `blocked_reason` cleared, but only when its qualified-lot count is at least 1. Every one of these edges advances the order's `version` by 1.

**Cancellation** is permitted to `PlantAdmin` and `LogisticsPlanner`, requires a `cancel_reason`, and is legal from `DRAFT`, `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, `BLOCKED_RESIN`, `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; from `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED` it is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). All five source states share one cascade: every cycle of this order in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `ORDER_CANCELLED`; each aborted cycle's `RESERVED` charges become `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `ORDER_CANCELLED`, returning `charge_kg` from `reserved_kg` to `available_kg`; each lot whose `available_kg` rose above 0.000 from `DEPLETED` returns to `AVAILABLE`, in turn unblocking other resin-blocked orders; each mould left `IN_CYCLE` by an aborted cycle returns to `AVAILABLE`; and any hull already produced keeps both of its state axes and is *not* scrapped. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

**Order cost** is recomputed at oven start (when charges become consumed), at demould, on an abort, and on every inspection that changes `completed_qty`. The recomputation is not itself a version-advancing event on the order: on the three cycle edges that trigger it — oven start, demould and abort — the order's `version` advances only when that same request also moves the order's `status`, so a demould that leaves the order `IN_PRODUCTION`, an abort that leaves the order's status untouched, and an oven start on an order already `IN_PRODUCTION` each persist the new cost figures without advancing the order's `version`. Inspection and scrap are different: as section 3.6 states, they advance the order's `version` whether or not the roll-up figures move. It is built in five ordered steps. First, material: over the order's charges in `status` `CONSUMED` — reached through the order's cycles — each charge contributes `charge_kg` multiplied by its lot's `cost_cents_per_kg`, **rounded half-up to whole cents per charge and only then summed** into `material_cost_cents`; summing unrounded products and rounding once yields a different total and is wrong. Second, oven energy: over the order's cycles in `status` `DEMOLDED` only, each contributes `actual_oven_minutes` multiplied by its station's `energy_cents_per_min`, again rounded to whole cents per cycle before summation into `oven_energy_cost_cents`; cycles in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING`, `COOLING` or `ABORTED` contribute nothing. Third, the pre-overhead subtotal is `material_cost_cents` plus `oven_energy_cost_cents`, and the overhead rate is the rate of the *first* tier whose ceiling is at or above that subtotal — 18.00 percent up to 500000 cents, 14.50 percent up to 2000000 cents, and 11.00 percent above that. The tier is chosen once from the pre-overhead subtotal and is **never re-selected** after overhead is added, even when `total_cost_cents` lands in a different band. Fourth, `overhead_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by the chosen rate divided by 100, rounded half-up, and `total_cost_cents` is the subtotal plus overhead. Fifth, `unit_cost_cents` is the **floor** of `total_cost_cents` divided by `completed_qty` when `completed_qty` is at least 1, and null otherwise; because it is floored, the summed unit costs may fall below `total_cost_cents` and the remainder is deliberately not redistributed. While the order is `IN_PRODUCTION` the unit cost moves with every passing verdict; once `COMPLETE` it stabilises, and a demotion back to `IN_PRODUCTION` recomputes it *upward*, because the same total is divided by a smaller `completed_qty`.

A computed cost read, available to `PlantAdmin` and `LogisticsPlanner`, exposes that arithmetic directly: `production_order_id`, `consumed_charge_count`, `material_cost_cents`, `demolded_cycle_count`, `oven_energy_cost_cents`, `pre_overhead_subtotal_cents`, `overhead_rate_pct`, `overhead_cents`, `total_cost_cents`, `completed_qty` and `unit_cost_cents`.

The **completion roll-up** itself is recomputed on every inspection and every scrap. Over all hulls reached through the order's cycles, `completed_qty` counts hulls whose `build_status` is `SELLABLE` **or** whose `logistics_status` is one of `STORED`, `LOADED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED` — a hull that has shipped still counts as completed, so the two axes are read together with a logical OR. `scrapped_qty` counts hulls whose `build_status` is `SCRAPPED`. Hulls in `DEMOLDED`, `TRIMMED`, `OUTFITTED` or `REWORK` count toward neither figure. Completion uses an at-or-above comparison, so over-production keeps the order `COMPLETE`.

Finally, an **overbuild ceiling** protects the order from unbounded scheduling: the number of scheduled plus completed hulls for an order may not exceed `ordered_qty` multiplied by 1.20 and floored to an integer. A cycle that would breach it is refused with `ERR_ORDER_OVERBUILD` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Booking a Cycle: Mould Selection and Three Resource Pools

Only `MoldingOperator` schedules a cycle, and the operator becomes the cycle's `operator_technician_id`. The request supplies `production_order_id`, `oven_station_id`, `arm_index`, `cooling_bay_id`, `oven_start_at`, `scheduled_oven_minutes`, `scheduled_cooling_minutes`, `oven_temp_c`, `arm_rpm_major`, `arm_rpm_minor`, `cooling_mode`, the concurrency declaration `expected_production_order_version`, and an optional `exclude_mold_ids` array. There is deliberately **no** `mold_id` field: the server picks the tool.

Checks run in the order of section 2.7. The three body foreign keys must resolve, reported as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (or the entity-specific `ERR_PRODUCTION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVEN_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COOLING_BAY_NOT_FOUND` where declared). `arm_index` must be at least 1 and no greater than the station's `arm_count`. The acting technician must be `ACTIVE`. The station's `status` must be `OPERATIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_OVEN_STATION_DOWN`; the bay's `status` must be `OPERATIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_COOLING_BAY_DOWN`. The order's `status` must be `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; a `DRAFT`, `BLOCKED_RESIN`, `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE`, `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED` order cannot take a new cycle and is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` (HTTP 409). The requested `cooling_mode` must equal the bay's `airflow_mode`, otherwise `ERR_COOLING_MODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The order's hull model's `size_class` — which every tool registered for that model necessarily shares, since a tool's `size_class` must equal its model's — must fit the station, meaning its rank in the ordering `COMPACT` (1), `MID` (2), `TANDEM` (3), `OVERSIZE` (4) is at most the rank of the station's `max_size_class`, otherwise `ERR_SIZE_CLASS_NOT_SUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). This rung reads the model rather than a chosen tool, so it needs no selection to have happened and sits above the mould pool: once the earlier rungs have passed, a station that cannot take the model reports `ERR_SIZE_CLASS_NOT_SUPPORTED` and never `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOLD`, even though the size-class clause inside the candidate pool would have emptied that pool as well. `oven_start_at` must be at or after the logical clock — equality passes — otherwise `ERR_OVEN_START_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422); cycles may not be back-dated. Then the oven arm window, then the bay slot count, then the mould pool, then the overbuild ceiling, and last of all `expected_production_order_version`.

**Mould selection** is a two-step policy with a distinguishable empty-pool fallback. The candidate pool is every tool whose `product_model_id` equals the order's, whose `status` is exactly `AVAILABLE` — `MAINTENANCE`, `IN_CYCLE` and `RETIRED` tools are excluded before ranking — whose `size_class` rank is at most the station's `max_size_class` rank, whose `cycles_since_service` is **strictly less** than its `service_interval_cycles` (a tool sitting exactly at its interval is *not* eligible), and which is unoccupied over the new cycle's mould span. Occupancy is evaluated only against cycles in `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING`; `DEMOLDED` and `ABORTED` cycles never block a tool. The span compared is the half-open interval from `oven_start_at` up to but not including `cooling_window_end_at`, so a new cycle whose `oven_start_at` equals an existing cycle's `cooling_window_end_at` to the second is accepted.

When the request carries `exclude_mold_ids`, those tools are removed **after** the eligibility filter and **before** ranking, so the second-ranked tool is returned instead of the first; exclusion never alters the ranking expression itself. Ranking is total and deterministic: ascending `cycles_since_service`, then ascending `mold_code`. Because `mold_code` is globally unique the order is total, and the tool's row identifier is never consulted. The first ranked candidate is written to `mold_id` and returned in the response so the choice is observable. When the pool is empty the two failure modes are kept distinct: if at least one tool for this hull model is in `status` `AVAILABLE` but every such tool was removed by the occupancy clause, the request fails with `ERR_MOLD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409); otherwise — no available tool at all, or an `exclude_mold_ids` list that emptied the pool — it fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOLD` (HTTP 409). An exclusion list that empties the pool always yields `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOLD`, never the window conflict.

The **oven arm** and **cooling bay** checks use the interval conventions of section 2.9 and must not be unified. The arm check gathers cycles sharing both `oven_station_id` and `arm_index` in `status` `SCHEDULED` or `OVEN_RUNNING`, expands each stored cycle's span by `changeover_buffer_min` on *both* sides, and tests the candidate's half-open span from `oven_start_at` to `oven_end_at` against it; a single overlap refuses the booking with `ERR_OVEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the buffer applies to the stored cycle rather than the candidate, it is counted once per comparison, and a candidate whose `oven_start_at` equals an existing `oven_end_at` plus the buffer is accepted while one second earlier is refused. Cycles on a different `arm_index` of the same station never conflict. The bay check gathers cycles sharing `cooling_bay_id` in `status` `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` — a cycle still in the oven already holds its future bay slot — and counts those whose closed span from `cooling_window_start_at` through `cooling_window_end_at` intersects the candidate's closed span; when that count has already reached the bay's `concurrent_slot_capacity` the booking is refused with `ERR_COOLING_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Closed intervals mean a candidate window starting exactly at an existing window's end *does* consume a slot, the opposite of the arm rule.

When every check passes, the three projected instants are written: `oven_end_at` is `oven_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_oven_minutes`; `cooling_window_start_at` equals `oven_end_at` exactly, butt-jointed with no gap; and `cooling_window_end_at` is `cooling_window_start_at` advanced by `scheduled_cooling_minutes`. `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `charged_mass_kg` is 0.000, `operator_technician_id` is the acting identifier, and the order moves `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` to `IN_PRODUCTION` on the first accepted cycle. The order's `version` advances by 1 in every case. A refused booking writes nothing at all — no cycle row, no order version bump. Cycles are listed with optional `production_order_id`, `mold_id`, `oven_station_id`, `cooling_bay_id`, `operator_technician_id`, `status`, `abort_reason`, `oven_start_from` and `oven_start_to` filters, and a single-cycle read exposes the server-selected `mold_id`, all projected and actual instants, the charge roll-ups and `provisional_warpage_index`.

### 3.5. Running the Cycle: Oven, Cooling, Demould and Abort

The cycle machine has five states and seven edges, and every manual edge is driven by the cycle's own `operator_technician_id` — a different `MoldingOperator` presenting a different `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, and a suspended operator with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`. A cycle addressed in the wrong state is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409): `SCHEDULED` is required to start the oven, `OVEN_RUNNING` to enter cooling, `COOLING` to demould, and `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING` to abort.

**Starting the oven** supplies `actual_oven_start_at` and carries nine further preconditions beyond the state and identity checks: the selected tool's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_MOLD_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the station's `status` must still be `OPERATIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_OVEN_STATION_DOWN`; at least one charge on the cycle must be in `status` `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_INSUFFICIENT`; every lot referenced by those charges must still be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_RESIN_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the charge mass must be inside tolerance; the blended regrind share must still be at or below the model's `max_regrind_pct`, otherwise `ERR_REGRIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; and the cycle's `weighted_melt_index_g10min` must lie inside the model's inclusive band from `min_melt_index_g10min` to `max_melt_index_g10min`, otherwise `ERR_MELT_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The **charge mass tolerance** test compares the cycle's `charged_mass_kg` against the tool's `nominal_charge_kg` — which is the model's `nominal_charge_kg` multiplied by the tool's `cavity_count` — as an absolute percentage deviation: the absolute difference divided by the tool's `nominal_charge_kg`, times 100, rounded to two decimals, must be at most the model's `charge_tolerance_pct`. Equivalently, `charged_mass_kg` must lie between the nominal mass scaled by one minus and one plus `charge_tolerance_pct` divided by 100, both bounds inclusive on values rounded to three decimals. A deviation outside tolerance is `ERR_CHARGE_MASS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 422).

On success the cycle moves `SCHEDULED` to `OVEN_RUNNING` and `actual_oven_start_at` is recorded from the logical clock. Every `RESERVED` charge on the cycle becomes `CONSUMED`, moving `charge_kg` from its lot's `reserved_kg` to its lot's `consumed_kg` while `available_kg` is untouched; any such lot whose `available_kg` already stands at exactly 0.000 while `AVAILABLE` becomes `DEPLETED`. The tool moves `AVAILABLE` to `IN_CYCLE`. The order moves `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` to `IN_PRODUCTION` if it is not already there. The cycle's and the tool's `version` each advance by 1. Because consumption is irreversible, this is the point past which no abort returns resin.

**Entering cooling** supplies `actual_oven_end_at`, requires the bay's `status` to still be `OPERATIONAL` (`ERR_COOLING_BAY_DOWN`), and requires the count of cycles currently in `status` `COOLING` in that bay to be strictly below the bay's `concurrent_slot_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_COOLING_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The cycle moves `OVEN_RUNNING` to `COOLING`, `actual_oven_end_at` is recorded from the logical clock, and `actual_oven_minutes` is recomputed as the floored whole minutes between `actual_oven_start_at` and `actual_oven_end_at`. The arm is thereby freed for scheduling, because arm occupancy counts only `SCHEDULED` and `OVEN_RUNNING` cycles.

**Demoulding** supplies `actual_cooling_end_at` and requires that the floored whole minutes elapsed since `actual_oven_end_at` reach the model's `min_cooling_minutes`, equality passing, otherwise `ERR_COOLING_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The cycle moves `COOLING` to `DEMOLDED`, `actual_cooling_end_at` is recorded and `actual_cooling_minutes` recomputed by flooring, and `provisional_warpage_index` is computed. The tool moves `IN_CYCLE` to `AVAILABLE` and its `cycles_since_service` is incremented by 1 — only a completed demould wears a tool; an abort never does. One hull is then minted per cavity. **CASCADE-B** runs in five links: (1) when the incremented `cycles_since_service` has reached the tool's `service_interval_cycles`, using an at-or-above comparison, the tool moves `AVAILABLE` to `MAINTENANCE`; (2) because that tool is in `MAINTENANCE`, every *other* cycle on it in `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`; (3) those cycles' `RESERVED` charges become `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`, moving `charge_kg` from `reserved_kg` back to `available_kg`; (4) lots whose `available_kg` rose above 0.000 move `DEPLETED` to `AVAILABLE`; and (5) orders in `BLOCKED_RESIN` that those lots qualify for move to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, or to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` when their target site is frozen. The cycle's and the tool's `version` each advance by 1.

**Hull serial assignment** is deterministic and uses no random value. `build_seq` is 1 plus the number of hulls already created for the producing order, counted across all of its cycles. For each `cavity_index` from 1 to the tool's `cavity_count`, in ascending order, one hull is created with `hull_serial` formed as the order's `order_code`, a hyphen, `cavity_index` zero-padded to two digits, a hyphen, and `build_seq` plus `cavity_index` minus 1 zero-padded to four digits. Each new hull starts with `build_status` `DEMOLDED`, `logistics_status` `UNALLOCATED`, `inspection_verdict` `PENDING` and `rework_count` 0, and inherits the cycle-level charged mass and provisional warpage while receiving its own measurements later. A single-cavity tool yields one hull and advances the sequence by 1; a multi-cavity tool yields `cavity_count` hulls in one request, and because they are created in ascending cavity order their `created_at` ordering matches their cavity order. Sibling hulls from one cycle can end in different verdicts. Because there is no creation endpoint for hulls, the filtered hull list is their only discovery channel.

**Aborting** is permitted to the cycle's own `MoldingOperator` and to `PlantAdmin`, requires an `abort_reason`, and is legal from `SCHEDULED`, `OVEN_RUNNING` or `COOLING`. No hull is ever created by an abort. Compensation is deliberately asymmetric. Aborting from `SCHEDULED` is the head of **CASCADE-A**: (1) the cycle becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `OPERATOR_ABORT`; (2) every charge of the cycle in `RESERVED` becomes `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `CYCLE_ABORTED`, moving `charge_kg` from `reserved_kg` back to `available_kg`; (3) lots whose `available_kg` rose above 0.000 move `DEPLETED` to `AVAILABLE`; and (4) orders in `BLOCKED_RESIN` that those lots qualify for move to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, or to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` when the target site is `FROZEN`. Aborting from `OVEN_RUNNING` or from `COOLING` returns the tool `IN_CYCLE` to `AVAILABLE` *without* incrementing `cycles_since_service`, but charges already `CONSUMED` are **not** restored: `consumed_kg` stays on the lot as a scrapped shot, `available_kg` does not rise, and consequently no order is unblocked. In all three cases the oven arm window and the cooling bay slot are freed and the cycle's `version` advances by 1.

A seventh edge is system-driven: a cycle in `SCHEDULED` is moved to `ABORTED` by five upstream events — a tool reaching service (`MOLD_SERVICE_DUE`), an oven station going down (`STATION_DOWN`), a lot being quarantined (`LOT_QUARANTINED`), a lot expiring (`LOT_EXPIRED`) and its order being cancelled (`ORDER_CANCELLED`) — with `abort_reason` set to the value matching the originating cascade and every `RESERVED` charge released with the matching `released_reason`. `DEMOLDED` and `ABORTED` are both terminal.

### 3.6. Finishing, Measuring and Judging Hulls

A hull carries two independent state axes on one row: `build_status`, which the shop floor drives, and `logistics_status`, which the warehouse and trailer workflows drive. The build axis has six states and ten edges. Hulls are listed with optional `molding_cycle_id`, `product_model_id`, `warehouse_id`, `outfitted_by_technician_id`, `inspected_by_technician_id`, `demand_bucket_id`, `build_status`, `logistics_status`, `inspection_verdict`, `storage_zone_code` and `is_zone_anchor` filters.

`AssemblyTechnician` **trims** a hull, supplying `trim_waste_kg`. Trimming is legal from `build_status` `DEMOLDED` or `REWORK` and moves the hull to `TRIMMED`; any other build state is `ERR_UNIT_INVALID_BUILD_STATE` (HTTP 409). On the `REWORK` entry the previously recorded `hardware_kit_serial` is cleared to null, so outfitting must be redone from scratch. `AssemblyTechnician` then **outfits** the hull, supplying `hardware_kit_serial`; this requires `build_status` `TRIMMED`, requires the `FIXTURE_ASSEMBLY` certification (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`), moves the hull to `OUTFITTED`, and stamps `outfitted_by_technician_id` with the acting identifier — which permanently bars that technician from ever inspecting this hull. Both operations additionally require `logistics_status` `UNALLOCATED`; a hull that has been stored, loaded, shipped or delivered can no longer be trimmed, outfitted, re-inspected or scrapped, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_REINSPECTABLE` (HTTP 409).

`QualityInspector` **inspects** a hull, supplying `wall_thickness_points`, `impact_test_joules` and `cosmetic_defect_grade`. Inspection requires `build_status` `OUTFITTED` (a first inspection) or `SELLABLE` (a re-inspection), requires `logistics_status` `UNALLOCATED`, requires both the `WALL_GAUGE` and `IMPACT_RIG` certifications, and requires at least six numeric measurements each strictly greater than 0.000 in `wall_thickness_points`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MEASUREMENTS` (HTTP 422). **Segregation of duties** is enforced here and it is not advisory: the inspecting technician may be neither the producing cycle's `operator_technician_id` nor the hull's `outfitted_by_technician_id`, and conversely the outfitting technician may not be a hull's `inspected_by_technician_id` from a prior inspection. A violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same constraint applies to scrapping.

The **wall-thickness distribution** is computed in four steps whose rounding order is mandatory. With `n` the number of supplied points: `mean_wall_thickness_mm` is the sum of the points divided by `n`, rounded to three decimals and stored. `wall_sd_mm` is then the population standard deviation taken **about that already-rounded mean** — the sum over the points of the squared difference from `mean_wall_thickness_mm`, divided by `n` (the population divisor, never `n` minus 1), square-rooted, and rounded to three decimals. Using the unrounded arithmetic mean here produces a different standard deviation and is wrong. `wall_cv_pct` is then `wall_sd_mm` divided by `mean_wall_thickness_mm` times 100, rounded to two decimals, computed from the two already-rounded values, and is 0.00 when the mean is 0.000. `min_wall_thickness_mm` is the smallest supplied point rounded to three decimals. Only the array supplied on this call participates, in the order given; a re-inspection replaces the previous array wholesale rather than merging it.

The **warpage index** is a weighted score over four input families, multiplied by two lookup factors and clamped. Term 1, thermal shoot: the absolute difference between the cycle's `oven_temp_c` and the reference 288, divided by the span 40, rounded to three decimals. Term 2, cooling deficit: the model's `min_cooling_minutes` minus the applicable cooling minutes, floored at zero, divided by `min_cooling_minutes`, rounded to three decimals. Term 3, rotation departure: the rotation ratio `arm_rpm_major` divided by `arm_rpm_minor` rounded to three decimals, then the absolute difference between that ratio and the ideal 4.000 divided by 4.000, rounded to three decimals. Term 4, distribution penalty: `wall_cv_pct` minus the model's `wall_cv_max_pct`, floored at zero, divided by 10, rounded to three decimals. The base is then the weighted sum over the **already-rounded** terms — 0.40 times the thermal term plus 0.35 times the cooling term plus 0.25 times the rotation term plus 0.50 times the distribution term — rounded to three decimals. Two multiplicative lookups follow: a size factor from the model's `size_class` (`COMPACT` 0.850, `MID` 1.000, `TANDEM` 1.150, `OVERSIZE` 1.400) and a mode factor from the cycle's `cooling_mode` (`AMBIENT` 1.000, `FORCED_AIR` 0.900, `WATER_MIST` 1.250). The product of base, size factor and mode factor is clamped to the range 0.000 through 5.000 **before** rounding and the clamped value is then rounded once to three decimals; rounding before clamping would let a value such as 5.0004 escape the ceiling.

That single formula has two variants which are both retained side by side on different rows. The **provisional** variant runs at demould, writes `provisional_warpage_index` on the cycle, uses `scheduled_cooling_minutes` for term 2, and forces the distribution term to 0.000 because no hull has been measured yet. The **final** variant runs at inspection, writes `final_warpage_index` on the hull, uses the cycle's `actual_cooling_minutes` for term 2, and takes a live distribution term from the inspected `wall_cv_pct`. Both substitutions are made in the same pass, so the two figures legitimately diverge on the identical cycle row — a cycle that cooled for less than its scheduled minutes, or a hull whose `wall_cv_pct` exceeds the model ceiling, scores higher on the final index. Only the final index drives the verdict. Aborted cycles are never scored.

The **verdict ladder** is then evaluated top to bottom, and the first matching clause wins; the remaining clauses are not evaluated. Note that the comparison operators are deliberately mixed. (1) `min_wall_thickness_mm` strictly less than the model's `wall_thickness_min_mm` gives `SCRAP`; a hull at exactly the minimum passes this clause. (2) `impact_test_joules` strictly less than the model's `impact_test_min_joules` gives `SCRAP`; exactly equal passes. (3) `cosmetic_defect_grade` equal to `REJECT` gives `SCRAP`. (4) `final_warpage_index` strictly greater than 2.500 gives `SCRAP`; exactly 2.500 does not scrap. (5) `wall_cv_pct` strictly greater than the model's `wall_cv_max_pct` gives `REWORK`; exactly equal passes. (6) `final_warpage_index` strictly greater than 1.200 gives `REWORK`. (7) `cosmetic_defect_grade` equal to `C` gives `REWORK`. (8) Otherwise the verdict is `PASS`. Grade `B` never reworks on its own; it matters only through the absence of clause 7. After the ladder, one override applies: a `REWORK` verdict on a hull whose `rework_count` already equals 2 is converted to `SCRAP`, because the rework budget is exhausted. Comparisons use the stored field precision and nothing is re-rounded inside the ladder. No sibling hull and no cycle-level average participates — each hull is judged alone.

The verdict writes `inspection_verdict` and moves `build_status`: a `PASS` moves `OUTFITTED` to `SELLABLE`; a `REWORK` moves `OUTFITTED` to `REWORK` and increments `rework_count`; a `SCRAP` moves `OUTFITTED` to `SCRAPPED`. `inspected_by_technician_id` is stamped with the acting identifier. The order's `completed_qty` and `scrapped_qty` are recomputed, the order may move `IN_PRODUCTION` to `COMPLETE`, and the order's cost roll-up and `unit_cost_cents` are recomputed. Both the hull's and the order's `version` advance by 1.

A **re-inspection** of a hull already in `SELLABLE` runs the identical ladder in the opposite direction of travel, and this is **CASCADE-D**: (1) an adverse verdict moves the hull `SELLABLE` to `REWORK` (incrementing `rework_count`) or `SELLABLE` to `SCRAPPED`; (2) because the hull no longer counts, the order's `completed_qty` is recomputed downward and its cost roll-up and `unit_cost_cents` are recomputed — upward, since the same total is divided by a smaller quantity; and (3) an order that was `COMPLETE` and now has `completed_qty` below `ordered_qty` returns to `IN_PRODUCTION`, which re-opens cycle scheduling for that order. A hull whose `logistics_status` has left `UNALLOCATED` cannot be re-inspected at all and is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_REINSPECTABLE`.

`QualityInspector` may also **scrap** a hull outright with a `scrap_reason`, without a full inspection. Scrapping is legal from `build_status` `OUTFITTED`, `SELLABLE` or `REWORK`, requires `logistics_status` `UNALLOCATED`, and is subject to the same segregation-of-duties constraint. It sets `build_status` to `SCRAPPED` — terminal — and `inspection_verdict` to `SCRAP`, recomputes the order's `scrapped_qty` and `completed_qty`, returns a `COMPLETE` order to `IN_PRODUCTION` when `completed_qty` falls below `ordered_qty`, and advances both the hull's and the order's `version` by 1. The tenth build edge is the rework loop itself: a hull in `REWORK` re-enters `TRIMMED` through trimming, is outfitted again, and is inspected again, and that loop may run at most twice before the budget override forces `SCRAP`.

### 3.7. Nesting Hulls into Warehouse Zones, Build-Ahead Quota and Site Posture

The logistics axis has five states and six edges, all driven by `LogisticsPlanner`. `UNALLOCATED` is where a hull is minted; `STORED`, `LOADED` and `IN_TRANSIT` are transient; `DELIVERED` is terminal and starts the warranty clock.

**Storing** a hull requires `build_status` `SELLABLE` (`ERR_UNIT_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`), `logistics_status` `UNALLOCATED` (`ERR_UNIT_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE`), and the concurrency declaration `expected_warehouse_version`. The destination is not chosen by the caller: it is the producing order's `target_warehouse_id`, and the zone within it is chosen by the server. Four gates apply in priority order. First, the site's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_WAREHOUSE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Second, when the producing order names a `demand_bucket_id`, the logical clock's calendar date must lie inside that bucket's window from `window_start_on` through `window_end_on`, **both bounds inclusive**, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_AHEAD_WINDOW_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) — and when the date is out of window the quota check is never reached at all. Third, an eligible zone must exist, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ZONE` (HTTP 409). Fourth, the bucket's quota and then the site's total cube must both hold. When the site is frozen *and* the bucket is already at its cap, the frozen-site failure is the one reported.

**Zone selection** treats a nest group as the triple of site, `storage_zone_code` and `product_model_id`. Bulky hulls nest inside one another, so only the first hull of a group pays its full cube. A hull is counted as occupying a zone when its `logistics_status` is `STORED` **or** `LOADED` — a loaded hull is still physically in its zone until the trailer departs. For each zone of the target site: an anchor exists when some occupying hull in that zone shares the candidate's `product_model_id` and carries `is_zone_anchor` true; the candidate footprint is the model's `gross_cube_m3` multiplied by its `nest_factor` and rounded to four decimals when an anchor exists, and simply `gross_cube_m3` rounded to four decimals when none does; the zone's committed cube is the rounded sum of `stored_footprint_cube_m3` over its occupying hulls; and the remaining cube after admission is the zone's `capacity_cube_m3` minus that committed sum minus the candidate footprint, rounded to four decimals. A zone is eligible when that remainder is at or above 0.0000, so a zone that fills to exactly capacity is eligible. Eligible zones are ranked with anchor affinity first (zones already anchoring this hull model win), then tightest fit by ascending remaining cube, then ascending `zone_code`, which is unique within the site and therefore makes the order total; neither the hull's identifier nor the zone's array position is ever consulted. The chosen `storage_zone_code` is returned in the response.

The two selection variants have materially different cost. Opening a **new nest group** — no eligible zone anchors this model — charges the full `gross_cube_m3` and sets `is_zone_anchor` true. **Joining an existing nest group** charges `gross_cube_m3` multiplied by `nest_factor` and leaves `is_zone_anchor` false, and the affinity term forces that zone even when a wholly empty zone would give a looser fit.

Storing writes `logistics_status` `STORED`, copies `warehouse_id` and `demand_bucket_id` from the producing order, stamps `stored_at` from the logical clock, and writes `storage_zone_code`, `stored_footprint_cube_m3` and `is_zone_anchor`. **CASCADE-H** then runs: (2) the site's `committed_cube_m3` is recomputed as the rounded sum of `stored_footprint_cube_m3` over its hulls in `STORED` or `LOADED`; (3) `utilization_pct` is recomputed as `committed_cube_m3` divided by `footprint_capacity_cube_m3` times 100, rounded to two decimals, and `posture` is re-derived from it; and (4) a site that has reached `frozen_threshold_pct` sends its orders in `READY_TO_SCHEDULE` and `IN_PRODUCTION` that target it to `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` with `blocked_reason` `WAREHOUSE_FROZEN`. The bucket's `build_ahead_committed_qty` is recomputed. Both the site's and the hull's `version` advance by 1.

**Posture** is derived, never stored independently: `posture` is `FROZEN` when `utilization_pct` is at or above `frozen_threshold_pct`, `RESTRICTED` when it is at or above `restricted_threshold_pct` but below the frozen threshold, and `NORMAL` otherwise. Both comparisons are at-or-above and both read the **rounded** `utilization_pct`, so at exactly 85.00 a default site is `RESTRICTED`, at exactly 95.00 it is `FROZEN`, 84.99 is `NORMAL`, 94.99 is `RESTRICTED`, and a raw utilisation of 94.995 rounds to 95.00 and freezes the site. A tightening recompute that crosses the frozen threshold blocks orders; one that crosses only the restricted threshold blocks no order but does cap new trailer sizing. A loosening recompute that leaves `FROZEN` unblocks orders, and — because anchor promotion can add cube back — a footprint decrease does not always lower `utilization_pct`. Separately, the site's total committed cube after a store must not exceed `footprint_capacity_cube_m3`, at-or-equal being accepted, otherwise `ERR_WAREHOUSE_CUBE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). That site-total check is distinct from the per-zone fit failure `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ZONE`.

The **build-ahead quota** limits how much seasonal inventory may sit in stock. A bucket's `build_ahead_cap_qty` is the floor of `forecast_qty` multiplied by `build_ahead_cap_pct` divided by 100. Its `build_ahead_committed_qty` is the count of hulls whose `demand_bucket_id` is this bucket, whose `build_status` is `SELLABLE` and whose `logistics_status` is `STORED` or `LOADED`. The admission test at store time is applied to the post-increment count: when `build_ahead_committed_qty` plus 1 exceeds `build_ahead_cap_qty` the store is refused with `ERR_BUILD_AHEAD_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Storing the hull that lands exactly *on* the cap is accepted; the next one is refused. A hull that departs on a trailer, is reworked, or is scrapped drops out of the count and frees capacity in the bucket.

**Releasing storage** requires `logistics_status` `STORED` and a `release_reason`, and is the head of **CASCADE-C**: (1) the hull moves `STORED` to `UNALLOCATED`, its `warehouse_id`, `storage_zone_code`, `stored_at` and `demand_bucket_id` are cleared, its `stored_footprint_cube_m3` is set to 0.0000 and `is_zone_anchor` to false; (2) when the released hull *was* its nest group's anchor and followers remain, the earliest surviving occupant of the same site-zone-model group — ordered by ascending `stored_at` with ascending `hull_serial` as the total tie-break — is promoted: its `is_zone_anchor` becomes true and its `stored_footprint_cube_m3` is re-charged to the full `gross_cube_m3` rounded to four decimals. Because `hull_serial` is a deterministic business string rather than a random identifier, the promotion order is reproducible. Promotion **raises** the group's total footprint by `gross_cube_m3` multiplied by one minus `nest_factor`, so releasing one hull can leave the site more utilised than a naive subtraction predicts. (3) The site's `committed_cube_m3`, `utilization_pct` and `posture` are recomputed. (4) A site that has left `FROZEN` returns its orders in `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` to `READY_TO_SCHEDULE`, subject to those orders having at least one qualified lot. The bucket's `build_ahead_committed_qty` is recomputed downward, and both the site's and the hull's `version` advance by 1.

The remaining four logistics edges belong to the trailer workflow of section 3.8. Loading moves a hull `STORED` to `LOADED` and — importantly — **does not move its warehouse footprint**: a loaded hull still occupies its zone, still counts in `committed_cube_m3`, and still counts toward the build-ahead quota. Removing a line or cancelling a trailer moves the hull `LOADED` back to `STORED`, again with no footprint movement, because it never left the zone. Departure moves the hull `LOADED` to `IN_TRANSIT`, and *that* is the event which frees footprint: `storage_zone_code`, `stored_at` and `demand_bucket_id` are cleared and `stored_footprint_cube_m3` is zeroed, and the vacate half of the footprint rule — including anchor promotion — runs once per vacated nest group. Delivery moves the hull `IN_TRANSIT` to `DELIVERED`, stamps its own `delivered_at` and clears `warehouse_id`.

### 3.8. Trailer Loading, Cube Rollups, Freight Quoting and Delivery

A `Shipment` is a trailer booked by a `LogisticsPlanner` against one `customer_id`, one `origin_warehouse_id` and one `destination_zone` from `Z1`, `Z2` or `Z3`, with a declared `trailer_cube_capacity_m3` and `trailer_max_payload_kg`. Creation requires the customer to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409) and the origin site to exist. One posture rule applies at booking time: when the origin site's `posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` and the requested `trailer_cube_capacity_m3` exceeds the site's remaining cube — `footprint_capacity_cube_m3` minus `committed_cube_m3` — the booking is refused with `ERR_WAREHOUSE_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409). A tightened site therefore cannot be used to book oversized trailers, even though tightening alone blocks no existing trailer. A new shipment starts in `PLANNED` with `nest_position` numbering empty, all rollups at zero — `loaded_cube_m3` and `nested_cube_m3` at 0.0000, `loaded_weight_kg` at 0.000, `cube_utilization_pct` at 0.00, `density_kg_per_m3` at 0.000 — and `quoted_freight_cents`, `billed_freight_cents`, `sealed_at`, `departed_at` and `delivered_at` all null.

**Adding a line** attaches one hull to one trailer. It requires `expected_shipment_version`; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_SHIPMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The shipment must be in `PLANNED` or `LOADING` (`ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`), the hull's `build_status` must be `SELLABLE` (`ERR_UNIT_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`) and its `logistics_status` must be `STORED` (`ERR_UNIT_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE`), and the hull's `warehouse_id` must equal the shipment's `origin_warehouse_id`, otherwise `ERR_WAREHOUSE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — a trailer cannot pick from two sites. The accepted line records `shipment_id`, `finished_unit_id`, `nest_position`, `line_gross_cube_m3`, `line_nested_cube_m3` and `line_freight_cents`, and starts in `LOADED`; the hull moves `STORED` to `LOADED` with no footprint movement. The first accepted line on a `PLANNED` shipment moves it to `LOADING`.

**Cube rollups** are recomputed from scratch on every line addition and every line removal, never patched incrementally. The surviving `LOADED` lines of the shipment are ordered by ascending `created_at` with the hull's `hull_serial` ascending as the total tie-break. Walking that order, a line's `nest_position` is one plus the number of earlier lines whose hull shares the same `product_model_id`, so numbering is **per product model**, not per trailer: the first kayak and the first canoe are both position 1. A line's `line_gross_cube_m3` is always the model's `gross_cube_m3` rounded to four decimals. Its `line_nested_cube_m3` is the full gross cube when its `nest_position` is 1, and `gross_cube_m3` multiplied by `nest_factor`, rounded to four decimals, for every position after the first. The shipment's `loaded_cube_m3` is the sum of the per-line gross cubes and `nested_cube_m3` the sum of the per-line nested cubes, each rounded to four decimals; `loaded_weight_kg` is the sum of the hulls' `unit_weight_kg` rounded to three decimals; `cube_utilization_pct` is `nested_cube_m3` divided by `trailer_cube_capacity_m3` times 100, rounded to two decimals; and `density_kg_per_m3` is `loaded_weight_kg` divided by `nested_cube_m3` rounded to three decimals, or exactly 0.000 when `nested_cube_m3` is 0.0000 — division by zero is never attempted.

Two admission tests gate a line addition, and both are applied to the **rounded post-addition** figures, not to raw sums. The candidate's nested contribution — full gross cube if it would take position 1 in its model group, nested cube otherwise — is added to `nested_cube_m3`, rounded to four decimals, and must be at or below `trailer_cube_capacity_m3`, otherwise `ERR_TRAILER_CUBE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The candidate's `unit_weight_kg` is added to `loaded_weight_kg`, rounded to three decimals, and must be at or below `trailer_max_payload_kg`, otherwise `ERR_TRAILER_PAYLOAD_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Both are at-or-below, so a hull that fills the trailer to exactly its cube capacity or exactly its payload ceiling is accepted. The candidate is tested **before** the row is written: a rejected line leaves no `ShipmentLine` behind, does not touch the hull, and does not advance the shipment's `version`, so a caller may retry with the same `expected_shipment_version`.

**Removing a line** is triggered either directly by a planner, who supplies a `remove_reason`, or indirectly by cancelling the trailer. The line must be `LOADED`, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_LINE_ALREADY_REMOVED` (HTTP 409), and the shipment must be in `PLANNED`, `LOADING` or `SEALED`, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE` — nothing may be pulled off a trailer that has already departed. The line moves `LOADED` to `REMOVED`, the hull returns `LOADED` to `STORED` in the zone it never left, and the rollups plus the whole `nest_position` sequence are recomputed over the surviving `LOADED` lines. Renumbering is the subtle part: removing a position-1 line promotes the next hull of that model into position 1, which re-charges it from its nested cube to its full gross cube, so a removal can **raise** `nested_cube_m3` and `cube_utilization_pct` rather than lower them. Removing the last `LOADED` line returns the shipment from `LOADING` to `PLANNED` and resets every rollup to its initial zero. Removing a line from a `SEALED` shipment reverts it to `LOADING` and clears `quoted_freight_cents` back to null, because the quote no longer describes the load.

**Sealing** commits the load and prices it. The shipment must be `LOADING` (`ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`), it must carry at least one `LOADED` line (`ERR_SHIPMENT_EMPTY`, HTTP 409), its `cube_utilization_pct` must be at or above the minimum seal utilisation of 60.00 percent (`ERR_TRAILER_UNDERUTILIZED`, HTTP 409, with exactly 60.00 passing), and every referenced hull must still be `SELLABLE` and `LOADED` (`ERR_UNIT_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`). Sealing sets `quoted_freight_cents` and stamps `sealed_at` from the logical clock.

**Freight** is computed per line and then aggregated, and the two variants differ only in which cube each line is billed on. For each line, a dimensional weight is derived as the billable cube multiplied by the dimensional factor of 120.000 kilograms per cubic metre, rounded to three decimals. The chargeable weight is the greater of the hull's actual `unit_weight_kg` and that dimensional weight, so bulky-but-light hulls are billed on volume. The line's `line_freight_cents` is the chargeable weight multiplied by the destination zone rate, rounded half-up to a whole cent, where the zone rates in cents per kilogram are 42 for `Z1`, 61 for `Z2` and 88 for `Z3`. The pre-discount total is the sum of the **already-rounded** per-line amounts: rounding happens per line and only then are the lines summed, never the reverse. A volume discount is then chosen by walking a fixed ordered tier list and taking the **first** row whose minimum utilisation is at or below the shipment's `cube_utilization_pct`: 90.00 percent utilisation earns 12.00 percent, 75.00 earns 7.50, 60.00 earns 3.00, and anything below 60.00 earns 0.00. That tier is selected once, from the utilisation at seal time, and is never re-selected later in the computation. The discount amount is the pre-discount total multiplied by the tier percentage divided by 100, rounded half-up. Finally a fuel surcharge of 9.50 percent is applied, rounded half-up, to the **discounted** base. The order is pinned: discount first, then fuel on the reduced amount, never fuel on the gross followed by a discount. The total is the discounted base plus the fuel surcharge.

The `QUOTED` variant, produced at seal, bills every line on `line_nested_cube_m3`, so nesting savings flow through to the quote. The `BILLED` variant, produced at delivery, bills every line on `line_gross_cube_m3`, so the delivered invoice is computed on gross cube and normally exceeds the nested quote for any trailer holding more than one hull per model. Both variants use the identical zone rates, dimensional factor, tier ladder, surcharge rate and rounding discipline; only the per-line cube basis changes.

**Unsealing** carries an `unseal_reason` and is permitted only while `departed_at` is null; it returns the shipment from `SEALED` to `LOADING` and clears both `quoted_freight_cents` and `sealed_at` to null. **Departing** requires `SEALED` and a non-null `quoted_freight_cents`, otherwise `ERR_FREIGHT_QUOTE_MISSING` (HTTP 409); it stamps `departed_at`, moves the shipment to `IN_TRANSIT`, and moves every referenced hull `LOADED` to `IN_TRANSIT`, firing the four links of CASCADE-C once per hull so that footprints are released, anchors are promoted, the origin site's cube, `utilization_pct` and `posture` are recomputed, and a site that leaves `FROZEN` unblocks its `BLOCKED_WAREHOUSE` orders. **Delivering** requires `IN_TRANSIT`; it sets `billed_freight_cents` from the `BILLED` variant, stamps the shipment's `delivered_at`, and moves every hull `IN_TRANSIT` to `DELIVERED` with its own `delivered_at` copied from the same clock reading — that per-hull timestamp is the origin of the warranty clock and is what a claim measures against.

**Cancelling** is allowed from `PLANNED`, `LOADING` and `SEALED` and is terminal. From `PLANNED` there is nothing to unwind. From `LOADING` and `SEALED`, every `LOADED` line moves to `REMOVED` and every referenced hull moves `LOADED` back to `STORED`, staying in the zone it occupied throughout, so no footprint moves and the origin site's cube is unchanged. Cancelling from `SEALED` additionally clears `quoted_freight_cents` and `sealed_at`. A shipment in `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED` cannot be cancelled (`ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`).

Two computed reads expose the trailer maths without mutating anything. The freight breakdown read returns the current per-line freight decomposition, the pre-discount total, the selected discount tier and amount, the fuel surcharge and the resulting total, computed on demand for the shipment's present lines under the `QUOTED` basis for an unsealed or sealed trailer and reflecting the persisted `billed_freight_cents` once delivered. The site footprint read returns the origin or any site's `committed_cube_m3`, `utilization_pct`, derived `posture`, remaining cube, and the per-zone breakdown of `zone_code`, `capacity_cube_m3`, committed cube, remaining cube and anchor occupancy. Both are read-only and never advance a `version`.

### 3.9. Warranty Claims: Two Independent Axes and the Reserve Ledger

A `WarrantyClaim` carries **two state machines on the same row**, and they advance independently. The adjudication axis `claim_status` runs `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `VOIDED`. The storage-guidance axis `storage_review_status` runs `NOT_STARTED`, `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED`, `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED` and `WAIVED`. Deciding the storage axis never moves the adjudication axis — the claim stays `UNDER_REVIEW` — and approval reads the storage axis as a precondition rather than driving it.

**Opening a claim** is available to a `WarrantyAgent` or to a `Customer`. The body carries a globally unique `claim_code` of 3 to 32 characters (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409), the `finished_unit_id` of the hull, the claimant's `customer_id`, a `claim_type` of `HULL_DEFORMATION`, `HARDWARE` or `COSMETIC`, an optional `reported_deformation_mm` at two decimals defaulting to 0.00, and an optional `storage_description` of 10 to 500 characters. When the caller's role is `Customer`, the submitted `customer_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A deformation claim must be substantiated: for `claim_type` `HULL_DEFORMATION` the `reported_deformation_mm` must be at or above the deformation threshold of 3.00 millimetres, otherwise `ERR_DEFORMATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422); exactly 3.00 is accepted and the threshold is not applied to the other two claim types. A new claim starts with `claim_status` `SUBMITTED`, `storage_review_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `months_in_service` null and every money field — `payout_basis`, `pre_fee_gross_cents`, `storage_deduction_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `net_payout_cents` — and `denial_reason` null, with `version` 1. Claims may be listed and filtered by `finished_unit_id`, `customer_id`, `claim_type`, `claim_status`, `storage_review_status` and `denial_reason`; a `Customer` reading claims sees only its own.

**Starting review** moves `claim_status` `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`. The claim must be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and the claimant must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). It computes and freezes `months_in_service` as the number of **complete calendar months** from the hull's `delivered_at` to the logical clock, floored: a hull delivered twelve months and twenty-nine days ago reads 12, not 13.

**Deciding the storage axis** is a `WarrantyAgent` action carrying an `outcome` of `VERIFIED` or `VIOLATED` plus a `storage_description`. The claim must be `UNDER_REVIEW` (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`) and the storage axis must still be `NOT_STARTED`, otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_REVIEW_ALREADY_DONE` (HTTP 409) — the axis is decided once per pass through review. An outcome of `VERIFIED` additionally requires that the customer's `storage_guidance_acknowledged_at` is not null **and** falls at or before the hull's `delivered_at`, otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_GUIDANCE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (HTTP 422); an acknowledgement timestamp exactly equal to `delivered_at` passes, one dated after delivery does not, because the guidance must have been read before the boat arrived. `VERIFIED` sets the axis to `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED` and guarantees a zero storage deduction; `VIOLATED` sets it to `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED`. Either way the submitted `storage_description` is stored and only the claim's `version` advances.

**Waiving a storage finding** is reserved to `PlantAdmin` and carries a `waiver_note`. The claim must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and that rung is evaluated first; only then is the storage axis read, and it must say `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED`, otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_FINDING_NOT_WAIVABLE` (HTTP 409). A claim that has been returned to the queue therefore reports `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, even though its reset axis is unwaivable too. The axis becomes `WAIVED`. A waiver unblocks approval but does **not** restore full value: the 35.00 percent storage deduction still applies to a claim approved from `WAIVED`.

**Approving** is the only path that spends money, and it is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the hull model. The body carries `expected_product_model_version`; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written, because the reserve balance the agent priced against has already moved. In order: the claim must be `UNDER_REVIEW` (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`); the storage axis must be `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`, otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409) — a claim still `NOT_STARTED` or standing at an unwaived `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED` is refused before pricing begins; the version check follows; the claimant must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_ACTIVE`); the hull's `logistics_status` must be `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DELIVERED` (HTTP 409), since a hull still in the plant has no warranty clock; the frozen `months_in_service` must be at or below the warranty term of 60 months, otherwise `ERR_WARRANTY_TERM_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422), with exactly 60 still inside the term; and finally the computed payout must fit the reserve.

**Pricing** proceeds in a fixed order and every intermediate is rounded before it is used again. First the coverage share, which depends on the model's `warranty_basis`. Under the `PRO_RATA_AGE` variant the coverage percentage is 60 minus `months_in_service`, divided by 60, times 100, rounded to two decimals — a straight-line decay in which each additional month costs roughly 1.67 percent of MSRP, so a claim at 0 months covers 100.00 percent and one at exactly 60 months covers 0.00 percent. Under the `FLAT_SCHEDULE` variant the coverage percentage is the value of the **first** band whose maximum months is at or above `months_in_service`, from an ordered ladder of 12 months at 80.00 percent, 36 months at 50.00 percent and 60 months at 25.00 percent — a step function that is flat inside a band and drops at 13 and at 37 months. For the same hull at the same age the two variants yield different payouts, and the divergence can also push the claim into a different administrative-fee band.

Second, the gross entitlement: `pre_fee_gross_cents` is the model's `msrp_cents` multiplied by the coverage percentage divided by 100, multiplied by the claim-type factor — 1.00 for `HULL_DEFORMATION`, 0.60 for `HARDWARE`, 0.30 for `COSMETIC` — and rounded half-up to whole cents. Third, the administrative fee: `admin_fee_cents` is the gross multiplied by the rate of the **first** tier whose maximum gross is at or above `pre_fee_gross_cents`, from an ordered ladder of 50000 cents at 12.00 percent and 200000 cents at 8.00 percent, falling through to a top rate of 5.00 percent, rounded half-up. That tier is chosen **once, from the gross, before any deduction**, and is never re-selected against the reduced amount: a claim whose post-deduction value would have fallen into a cheaper band still pays the expensive band's fee. Fourth, the deduction: `storage_deduction_cents` is the gross multiplied by 35.00 percent, rounded half-up, when `storage_review_status` is `WAIVED`, and exactly 0 when it is `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED`. Fifth, `net_payout_cents` is the gross minus the deduction minus the fee, floored at 0 so it can never go negative. Sixth, the reserve gate: when `net_payout_cents` exceeds the model's `warranty_reserve_cents` the approval is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_RESERVE_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409); at-or-below passes, so a payout that exactly drains the reserve to zero is approved.

On success the claim records `payout_basis` copied from the model's `warranty_basis`, together with `pre_fee_gross_cents`, `storage_deduction_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_payout_cents`, and moves `claim_status` `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`. The **reserve ledger** then moves: the model's `warranty_reserve_cents` decreases by `net_payout_cents` and its `warranty_paid_cents` increases by the identical integer. No rounding happens in the ledger itself, because the amount was already rounded during pricing. The invariant `warranty_reserve_cents` plus `warranty_paid_cents` equals `initial_warranty_reserve_cents` holds after every ledger move, without exception. Both the model's and the claim's `version` advance by 1.

**Denying** a claim under review requires the storage axis to be anything other than `NOT_STARTED`, otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` — a claim cannot be refused before its storage story has been assessed. The claim moves `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DENIED`. Its `denial_reason` is **forced** to `STORAGE_GUIDANCE_VIOLATED` whenever the storage axis reads `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED`, regardless of what the request body asked for; otherwise it is taken from the body, where `NOT_A_MANUFACTURING_DEFECT` and `TERM_EXPIRED` are the meaningful choices. All four money fields are set to 0 — denial produces an explicit zero record, not nulls — and no reserve moves.

**Returning a claim to the queue**, with a `return_note` recording why, is the documented backward edge and it resets both axes at once. `claim_status` moves `UNDER_REVIEW` back to `SUBMITTED`, and whichever of `GUIDANCE_VERIFIED`, `GUIDANCE_VIOLATED` or `WAIVED` the storage axis held returns to `NOT_STARTED`, revoking any administrative waiver. Both `months_in_service` and `storage_description` are cleared to null, so a second pass through review re-ages the claim against the clock as it then stands and must decide the storage axis afresh.

**Voiding** an approved claim is a `PlantAdmin` action carrying a `void_reason`. It moves `claim_status` `APPROVED` to `VOIDED`, sets `denial_reason` to `APPROVAL_VOIDED`, and reverses the ledger exactly: the model's `warranty_reserve_cents` increases and its `warranty_paid_cents` decreases by the claim's recorded `net_payout_cents`, restoring the conserved sum. This is the **only** path in the system that raises a reserve. Unlike denial, voiding **retains** the claim's money fields as the audit record; they are not zeroed. Both versions advance by 1.

**Withdrawing** is the claimant's own escape hatch. A `Customer` may withdraw a claim from either `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, giving a `withdraw_reason`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the claim's `customer_id`, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. No money moves. Separately, suspending a customer voids that customer's open work: every claim of theirs in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` moves to `VOIDED` with `denial_reason` `APPROVAL_VOIDED`, again with no reserve movement, because nothing had yet been paid. Approved, denied and already-withdrawn claims are untouched by a suspension, and reinstating the customer does not resurrect a voided claim.
