# GPO Rebate Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A restaurant group purchasing organization sits between thousands of independent operators and the manufacturers whose products they buy. Its economic promise is simple to state and painfully hard to administer: pool the purchasing volume of many small restaurants, negotiate manufacturer deals none of them could win alone, and pass the resulting rebate dollars back to the members who generated them. Everything between the promise and the payment is paperwork — distributor authorization letters that permit purchase data to be released at all, item-level rate cards that price a rebate per case or per dollar, distributor feeds arriving in each distributor's own item vocabulary, manufacturer claims that rarely agree with the GPO's own accrual to the cent, and members who dispute their share when it lands lower than they expected.

This document specifies the service that administers that middle. It maintains the reference data of the program — the tier configurations that decide how rebate value is split, the distributors contracted to bill at a declared cost-plus markup, the members enrolled against a spend commitment, and the manufacturer deals with their contracted items and rate cards. It ingests distributor purchase feeds, normalizes each invoice line onto a canonical contracted item, accrues the manufacturer-funded rebate that line earned, and splits that rebate into a GPO administration fee, the member's share and the GPO's retention. It then reconciles the GPO's accrued position against what each manufacturer says it owes, arbitrates member disputes with a strict separation between the analyst who accrued a figure and the arbiter who rules on it, audits every distributor invoice for markup billed above the contracted ceiling, and recovers the overage.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. The first is that money is never approximate: every rebate figure is a whole number of cents, every share is a basis-point fraction applied in a pinned order, and a split's residual is always assigned rather than rounded away, so that a member's share plus the GPO's retention plus the administration fee reconstructs the gross exactly. The second is that operations which the business considers reversible must be reversible in the ledger too: unwinding a normalized feed, or upholding a member's dispute, must retract the exact cents that were posted, component by component, and never a recomputed approximation of them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads, writes and the administrative clock alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six declared role names, compared with exact case sensitivity. When the role is recognized but the endpoint does not list it among its authorized roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authentication is therefore checked before authorization, and both are checked before anything about the request's target or body is examined.

The six roles are:

- **`GpoAdmin`** — the GPO contract administrator. Owns all reference data (program tiers, distributors, members, manufacturer deals, contracted items and rate cards), settles overbilling recoveries, closes rebate periods, and drives the logical clock. A `GpoAdmin` cannot ingest purchase data and cannot decide disputes.
- **`RebateAnalyst`** — the GPO rebate operations analyst. Normalizes purchase feeds, maps ambiguous lines by hand, accrues and validates rebates, reconciles manufacturer claims, audits cost-plus markup and opens recovery requests. A `RebateAnalyst` cannot approve rate cards, cannot settle recoveries and cannot decide disputes.
- **`DisputeArbiter`** — the independent dispute arbiter. The only role permitted to request evidence on a dispute or to decide one.
- **`MemberOperator`** — a restaurant operator acting on its own behalf.
- **`DistributorFeed`** — a distributor's data-release service account.
- **`ManufacturerRep`** — a manufacturer representative, who submits and restates rebate claims against its deals and reads deal-side reference data, and who never touches purchase feeds, accruals or disputes.

Three of these roles carry an opaque identity and three do not, and the distinction governs how `X-User-ID` is interpreted. When `X-User-Role` is `GpoAdmin`, `RebateAnalyst`, `DisputeArbiter` or `ManufacturerRep`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — as `accrued_by_user_id` on a purchase batch when an analyst accrues it, as `validated_by_user_id` on an accrual when an analyst validates it, or as `decided_by_user_id` on a dispute when an arbiter rules on it — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `MemberOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's member foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Five operations are scoped this way: submitting an authorization letter (compared against the `member_id` in the body), revoking an authorization letter (compared against the letter's `member_id`), opening a dispute and submitting evidence on one (compared against the cited accrual's `member_id`), and requesting a member savings report (compared against the member id in the path). A mismatch on any of the five is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Because the comparison sits at step 5 of §2.7, ahead of body-shape validation, the two body-driven cases behave differently. Letter submission compares whatever the raw body carries under `member_id`, so a body that omits that key, or supplies it as a JSON value other than a string, has nothing that can match the caller and is refused as a scope violation rather than as a malformed body. Dispute creation must first look the cited accrual up, so when the body's `accrual_id` is absent, is not a string, or names no stored accrual, there is no owner to compare against, no scope decision is taken at all, and evaluation falls through to the later steps that report the malformed body or the unresolvable foreign key. When a `MemberOperator` opens a dispute, its `X-User-ID` is additionally recorded as `opened_by_user_id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `DistributorFeed`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; ownership checks compare the resource's distributor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Three operations are scoped this way: countersigning an authorization letter (compared against the letter's `distributor_id`), opening a purchase batch (compared against the `distributor_id` in the body), and ingesting a purchase line (compared against the owning batch's `distributor_id`). A mismatch on any of the three is rejected with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The same asymmetry applies at step 6: opening a batch compares the raw body's `distributor_id` directly, so a value that is absent or not a string is refused as a scope violation, while ingesting a purchase line must resolve the body's `purchase_batch_id` before it has an owner to compare, and takes no scope decision at all when that value is absent, is not a string, or names no stored batch.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A resource object carries a fixed property set per resource type: the server never exposes its internal bookkeeping, and a property that this document declares for that type but which has no value yet is present in the object with a JSON `null` rather than omitted from it.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); to count as an integer the supplied text must be a run of decimal digits, optionally preceded by a minus sign and optionally surrounded by whitespace, so an empty, fractional or non-numeric value is rejected too. Beyond `limit` and `offset`, a list endpoint honours only the filter parameters its §4 entry names for it, and a query parameter it does not declare is ignored rather than rejected. A declared filter is matched by exact equality against the raw parameter text, so a filter value that no record carries — including one outside a status field's declared value set — narrows the result to an empty `data` array with `total_count` of 0 rather than drawing an error.

List results are ordered by `created_at` descending — newest record first — across every collection in the system, so that a distributor's most recent feed or a member's most recent dispute appears at the head of the first page. Because `created_at` is the logical-clock instant of §2.11 rather than wall-clock time, records created without an intervening clock move share it to the last digit; wherever this document orders or walks records by `created_at` — a list read here, or a §3 rule that processes records in ascending `created_at` — the server's own monotonic creation sequence breaks a shared `created_at`, so of two records bearing the identical instant the later-created one sorts first under a descending order and last under an ascending one. That tie-break is on creation sequence, never on identifier. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the values in effect, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching record remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties, Versioning and Immutable Records

Every stored record is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning: a member id and a rate card id are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must not parse them. Alongside its `id`, each record carries a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Records that can be modified after creation carry two further properties. `updated_at` uses the same ISO 8601 UTC format and is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant whenever any field of the record changes. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, whether the mutation was requested directly or produced as a downstream consequence of some other operation. Where a rule in this document says a counter is "recomputed" or a status is "set", the owning record's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed as part of the same operation. A single request advances a given record's `version` by exactly one step, however many of that record's fields the request rewrites and however many cascade hops reach it: the rule §3.2 states for a superseding rate-card proposal — "once per call, not once per card touched" — is the general discipline, so terminating a member whose `enrollment_status`, `payout_hold` and `active_letter_count` all move across two hops still leaves that member one version higher than it started, and a clock advance that expires three of a distributor's letters at once advances that distributor by one version, not three.

`ComplianceReview` is the one exception. A review is a point-in-time verdict rather than a living record: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no endpoint that modifies it, and its computed verdict fields are frozen at the moment it is written.

### 2.5. Flat Resource Topology and Identifier Resolution

The API is deliberately flat. Each resource type lives in its own top-level collection, and a child record names its parent through a foreign key in the request body rather than through a nested URL. Multi-segment paths exist only for actions performed on an existing record and for one computed read. Path variables therefore always name a single record: `tier_id`, `distributor_id`, `member_id`, `letter_id`, `deal_id`, `item_id`, `rate_card_id`, `batch_id`, `line_id`, `accrual_id`, `claim_id`, `dispute_id` and `review_id`.

Identifier resolution happens in two stages and in a fixed order. Path variables are resolved first, left to right, and an unresolvable one yields HTTP 404 with the not-found code belonging to that resource type — `ERR_PROGRAM_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LETTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTED_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys carried in the request body are resolved later, after the body's shape has been checked, and are resolved in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields; the same not-found codes apply.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities in this system are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere: not in a rebate, not in an administration fee, not in an overbilling figure, and not in a recovery allocation. All proportions — tier splits, administration fees, markup ceilings, compliance minimums and variance measurements — are expressed in basis points, where 10000 basis points is the whole. Applying a basis-point proportion to an amount means multiplying the amount by the basis-point figure and dividing by 10000.

Two rounding modes are used, and which one applies is never left to the implementer's discretion. The default is round-half-up: the exact quotient is rounded to the nearest whole cent, and a quotient ending in exactly one half rounds away from zero. Round-half-up governs the administration fee, the member share, the allowed cost-plus unit ceiling, the prorated spend commitment and the claim-implied per-accrual amount. The exception is any figure the business treats as a threshold measurement, where the value is truncated toward zero — floored — so that a fraction of a basis point can never lift a measurement over a bar it did not actually clear. Flooring governs the compliance purchase ratio, both claim variance measurements, the savings capture rate and each member's proportional slice of a recovery. Where a rule composes several arithmetic steps, rounding is applied only at the points the rule names, never to intermediate products.

### 2.7. Order of Request Evaluation

Requests are validated as an ordered ladder, and the **first** check that fails determines the response. No later check runs, so a request that would fail three ways reports only the earliest failure. This ordering is part of the contract: it decides, for example, that a caller lacking permission on an endpoint learns only that it is forbidden, never that the record it aimed at does not exist.

The ladder is:

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared role names, matched case-sensitively — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. `X-User-Role` appears in the target endpoint's authorized role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. Every identifier in the path resolves to a stored record, checked left to right — otherwise the matching 404 code.
5. A `MemberOperator` caller's `X-User-ID` matches the member that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
6. A `DistributorFeed` caller's `X-User-ID` matches the distributor that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. The request body parses, every required field is present with its declared JSON type, every enum-valued field carries a declared member of its value set, and no unknown field is supplied — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This step also enforces those per-field scalar constraints that §3 states **without** naming a dedicated code, and reports each of them as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: minimum and maximum string lengths, the uppercase-letter/digit/hyphen character set on code and normalized-key fields, the `YYYY-MM-DD` shape of a calendar date together with its calendar validity and the ISO 8601 shape of an instant, the 0-through-10000 bound on a program tier's and a distributor's basis-point fields, and the "at least 1" floor on figures such as `dispute_window_days`, `reversal_posture_limit`, `required_purchase_window_days`, `pack_size`, `baseline_unit_cost_cents` and `contract_unit_cost_cents`. Constraints that **do** carry a dedicated code — `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, `ERR_TIER_SPLIT_NOT_BALANCED`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_SHAPE_INVALID` (which owns the floors on the rate fields themselves and every `tier_breaks` structural rule), `ERR_INVALID_CASE_QUANTITY` and `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SHARE` — are not tested here but at their own positions further down this ladder. On an endpoint that declares body fields, a body that is absent, empty or `{}` satisfies this step whenever none of those fields is required, while a body that parses as valid JSON but is not an object fails it. An endpoint that declares no body field at all — every transition action whose §3 narrative names no payload, among them member activation and termination, letter revocation, deal activation, suspension and resumption, rate-card approval, batch normalization, renormalization, accrual, markup audit and recovery request, claim reconciliation, and an arbiter's evidence request — never examines the request body, and therefore accepts any body whatever, including one carrying unknown fields and one that is not a JSON object. A required field present with an explicit JSON `null` fails exactly as if it were absent; where this document gives an optional field a stated default, that field present with an explicit `null` is treated as omitted and takes the default.
8. `limit` and `offset` are within their accepted ranges on a list read — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
9. Every foreign key in the request body resolves to a stored record, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs — otherwise the matching 404 code.
10. The supplied expected-version field matches the parent record's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION`.
11. The target record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and for a create whose container must still be open the container is in its accepting state — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
12. The referenced member has `enrollment_status` of `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ENROLLED`.
13. The referenced distributor has `status` of `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`.
14. The referenced manufacturer deal has `status` of either `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`.
15. The purchasing member has an authorization letter in force with the feed's distributor — otherwise `ERR_LOA_NOT_ACTIVE`.
16. The cited manufacturer claim has `status` of `RECONCILED` — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RECONCILED`.
17. The contracted item offered for a manual mapping is one of the line's eligible candidates — otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_A_CANDIDATE`.
18. The deciding `X-User-ID` does not already appear on the workflow being decided — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
19. No overlapping authorization letter exists for the same member and distributor — otherwise `ERR_LOA_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
20. No overlapping rate card exists for the same contracted item — otherwise `ERR_RATE_CARD_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
21. A supersede request finds exactly one qualifying incumbent card — otherwise `ERR_NO_INCUMBENT_RATE_CARD`.
22. Every range for which §3 declares an ordering rule is correctly ordered — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. Six ranges are guarded here: a manufacturer deal's and a rate card's `effective_from` through `effective_to`, an authorization letter's half-open effectivity window, a purchase batch's and a manufacturer claim's `period_start` through `period_end`, and a compliance review's `window_start` through `window_end`. The period that a rebate-period close or a member savings report names is deliberately not among them and is never tested for ordering.
23. A purchase line's `invoice_date` falls inside its batch's period — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`.
24. The dispute window on the cited accrual is still open — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
25. A review window does not extend beyond today — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE`.
26. No reconciled claim already covers the same deal and period — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_ALREADY_RECONCILED`.
27. A tier's two shares add up to the whole — otherwise `ERR_TIER_SPLIT_NOT_BALANCED`.
28. A rate card's rate fields match its `rate_type` — otherwise `ERR_RATE_CARD_SHAPE_INVALID`.
29. A purchase line's quantity and cost figures are each at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_QUANTITY`.
30. `dba_code` is unused — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DBA_CODE`.
31. `deal_code` is unused, and `tier_code` is unused — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEAL_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE` respectively.
32. `manufacturer_sku` is unused within the same deal — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_SKU`.
33. `source_reference` is unused within the same distributor — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_REFERENCE`.
34. A deal being activated has an approved-rate-carrying active item — otherwise `ERR_DEAL_HAS_NO_APPROVED_RATES`.
35. A member being activated holds at least one active letter — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_HAS_NO_ACTIVE_LOA`.
36. A batch being accrued has at least one accruable line — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACCRUABLE_LINES`.
37. No batch overlapping the period is still awaiting normalization or accrual — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_UNACCRUED_BATCHES`.
38. No other dispute on the same accrual is still live — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_DISPUTE`.
39. A recovery settlement does not collect more than was overbilled — otherwise `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OVERBILLING`.
40. A disputed amount lies within the accrual's member share — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SHARE`.
41. The accrual's member is not under a payout hold — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_PAYOUT_HELD`.

Each of these checks has a defined scope, and §3 states, workflow by workflow, exactly which operations each one guards and what the check tests. Checks whose scope does not include the endpoint in hand are simply skipped; the surviving checks keep their relative order.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two creates in this system reshape a parent record that other writers may be holding a stale view of, and both therefore demand that the client prove what it last saw.

Proposing a rate card requires `expected_contracted_item_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ContractedItem` the card prices. If that value differs from the item's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is concrete: a rate card create that asks to supersede truncates the incumbent approved card for the same item and advances the item's `version`, so a writer holding an older view of the item would otherwise price it on top of a card layout that no longer exists.

Submitting a manufacturer claim requires `expected_deal_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ManufacturerDeal` the claim is filed against. If that value differs from the deal's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the hazard is real: a claim covering a deal period that already has a live claim is a restatement that supersedes the incumbent and advances the deal's `version`, so a writer holding a stale deal view would otherwise restate against a claim generation it never observed.

Both fields exist to detect a version that has moved on since the client read it; neither has anything to do with requests arriving close together in time. No other endpoint in this system accepts an expected-version field, and supplying one where it is not declared is an unknown field.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Rebate arbitration is only credible if the people who produced a number are not the people who rule on a challenge to it. The system therefore records, on the artifacts themselves, who performed each consequential step: `accrued_by_user_id` on a purchase batch names the analyst whose accrual run created the batch's accruals, `validated_by_user_id` on an accrual names the analyst who validated it, `opened_by_user_id` on a dispute names the member operator who raised it, and `decided_by_user_id` on a dispute names the arbiter who closed it.

When an arbiter requests evidence on a dispute or decides one, the caller's `X-User-ID` is compared against the identifiers already recorded on that workflow. Requesting evidence is barred when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the dispute's `opened_by_user_id`. Deciding is barred when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the dispute's `opened_by_user_id`, or the cited accrual's `validated_by_user_id`, or the `accrued_by_user_id` of the purchase batch the accrual's line arrived on. Any of these collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The bar is on the identifier, not on the role name: two different `DisputeArbiter` operators are free to act on each other's disputes, and a single operator who wore an analyst's hat earlier cannot then wear the arbiter's.

### 2.10. Cascade Integrity and Derived Counters

A large part of this specification is bookkeeping that fans out from a single request. Terminating a member revokes its letters; losing a deal delists its items, expires their rate cards and un-matches the purchase lines that pointed at them; upholding a dispute retracts money from three member counters and may flip that member's posture, which in turn puts a hold on batches the member has accruals in. Cascades are described in §3 as numbered hops, and the numbering is meaningful: each hop is caused by the hop before it, and every hop's effects are part of the same request. There is no deferred or eventually-consistent processing anywhere in this system — when a request returns, every consequence of it is already visible to the next read.

Derived counters are never adjusted by guesswork. Where a rule says a count is recomputed — `active_letter_count` on a member or a distributor, `contracted_item_count` on a deal, `approved_rate_card_count` on a contracted item, `matched_cases` and `unmatched_cases` on a batch — the counter is rewritten from the current facts, as the count or sum the field's definition names, not incremented blindly. Where a rule says money is decremented by an accrual's components, the exact stored component values are subtracted, so that a reversal is the arithmetic inverse of the posting it undoes rather than a fresh computation that might land a cent away.

### 2.11. Calendar Dates, Instants and the Logical Clock

The system distinguishes two kinds of time. Contract effectivity for deals and rate cards, invoice dating, purchase periods, claim periods and review windows are **calendar dates** in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, with no time component. Authorization-letter effectivity and the recorded moments of countersignature and reconciliation are **instants** in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits. That three-digit form is how the server stores and returns every instant, but a client-supplied instant — an authorization letter's `effective_from` or `effective_to`, or the clock's `current_utc` — is accepted in any ISO 8601 form that fixes its own offset: seconds are mandatory, zero through six fractional digits are permitted, and the text must end in either `Z` or a numeric `±HH:MM` offset, a bare local time with no offset being rejected under step 7 of §2.7. Such a value is converted to UTC and rewritten into the canonical three-digit form before it is stored or echoed, and every later comparison is made on the resulting instant rather than on the text the client sent, so `2026-09-01T12:00:00+02:00` and `2026-09-01T10:00:00.000Z` are the same moment.

Interval semantics differ by artifact and are not interchangeable. An authorization letter's window is half-open: `effective_from` is included and `effective_to` is excluded, so a letter whose `effective_to` is exactly the current instant is no longer in force. A rate card's window is closed: both `effective_from` and `effective_to` are included, so a card whose `effective_to` equals an invoice date still prices that invoice. Deal effectivity, batch periods, claim periods, review windows and savings-report periods are likewise closed on both ends.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the machine's real time. A `GpoAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `letters_expired` and `rate_cards_expired`, the counts of records that aged out as a consequence. Advancing the clock expires every authorization letter whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` is at or before the new instant, decrementing the member's and the distributor's `active_letter_count` for each, and expires every rate card whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` and whose `effective_to` calendar date lies strictly before the new instant's calendar date, decrementing the owning item's `approved_rate_card_count` for each card that had been approved. The clock is moved before those expiries are worked out, so every record the move ages out — the letter or card itself and the member, distributor or item whose counter it decremented — carries the new `current_utc` as its `updated_at`. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and reverses nothing that has already expired.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Configuration, Member Enrollment and Data-Release Authorization

Everything in the program hangs off a **program tier**, the configuration record that decides how much of a rebate a member keeps and how strictly it is held to its commitments. A `GpoAdmin` creates a tier by supplying a `tier_code` — one of `CORE`, `PLUS` or `ELITE`, and no two tiers may share one, a collision being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE` — together with the two halves of the value split, `member_share_bps` and `gpo_retention_bps`. Those two must add up to exactly 10000 basis points; anything else is rejected with `ERR_TIER_SPLIT_NOT_BALANCED`. The tier also fixes two administration-fee rates, `admin_fee_bps_low` and `admin_fee_bps_high`, of which exactly one applies to a given accrual depending on the member's frozen year-to-date position; a `compliance_min_ratio_bps` that a member's purchasing must reach for a compliance review to pass; a `markup_cap_bps` that ceilings what any distributor may bill this tier's members above base cost; a `dispute_window_days` count of whole days during which a member may still contest an accrual; and a `reversal_posture_limit`, the number of dispute-driven reversals at which the member's posture hardens. Every one of the basis-point fields accepts values from 0 through 10000 inclusive, `dispute_window_days` must be at least 1, and `reversal_posture_limit` must be at least 1 and defaults to 2 when the field is omitted.

Distributors are registered next. A distributor record carries a `name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `dba_code` of 3 to 20 characters drawn only from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen that must be unique across all distributors — a reused code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DBA_CODE` — and a `contracted_markup_bps` between 0 and 10000 inclusive, the cost-plus markup this distributor has agreed to bill above its own reported base cost. A newly registered distributor's `status` is `ACTIVE`, with `active_letter_count` and `overbilled_recovered_cents` both at 0. The only path to `SUSPENDED` is a written-off recovery settlement, described in §3.7; a `SUSPENDED` distributor may neither open new purchase feeds nor countersign letters.

Enrolling a restaurant operator produces a **member** record from a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `tier_id` naming the program tier whose split, fee bands, markup cap, dispute window and posture limit will govern it, and a `committed_annual_spend_cents` figure of at least 1 cent representing the annual contracted-spend commitment the operator signed. A new member's `enrollment_status` is `PENDING`, its `reconciliation_posture` is `CLEAR`, its `payout_hold` is false, and its counters — `active_letter_count`, `accrued_member_share_cents`, `attributed_gpo_retention_cents`, `attributed_admin_fee_cents`, `ytd_net_snapshot_cents`, `dispute_reversal_count` and `recovery_credit_cents` — all start at 0.

Before a member can transact, at least one distributor must be authorized to release its purchase data. An **authorization letter** is submitted either by the `MemberOperator` itself or by a `GpoAdmin` on its behalf, and carries `member_id`, `distributor_id`, the window bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to`, a `signatory_name` of 1 to 120 characters naming the member officer who signed, and a `scope`. Scope is either `PURCHASE_DATA_ONLY` or `PURCHASE_DATA_AND_PRICING`, and the distinction matters later: only a `PURCHASE_DATA_AND_PRICING` letter makes that member's lines auditable for cost-plus markup. The window is half-open and its `effective_to` must be strictly after its `effective_from`; an `effective_to` equal to or before the start is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. A new letter's `status` is `SUBMITTED` and its `countersigned_at` is null, and no counter anywhere moves until the distributor responds. Because two live authorizations for the same pairing would make data release ambiguous, a submission is rejected with `ERR_LOA_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when some existing letter for the same member and distributor whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACTIVE` overlaps the requested window under half-open semantics — that is, when the new window's start falls strictly before the existing window's end and the existing window's start falls strictly before the new window's end. Windows that merely touch, where one ends exactly where the next begins, do not overlap and are both accepted.

The distributor's response is a countersignature carrying a `decision`, and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves the letter `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE`, stamps `countersigned_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and increments both the member's and the distributor's `active_letter_count` by 1. A `decision` of `DECLINE` moves the letter `SUBMITTED → DECLINED`, leaves `countersigned_at` null, and changes no counter on either side. Either branch requires the letter to still be `SUBMITTED`, requires the acting `DistributorFeed` account's `X-User-ID` to equal the letter's `distributor_id`, and — for the accepting branch as for the declining one — requires the distributor's own `status` to be `ACTIVE`, a suspended distributor being turned away with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`.

Three exits lead out of `ACTIVE`, and all three are terminal. A `MemberOperator` or a `GpoAdmin` may withdraw an authorization, moving it `ACTIVE → REVOKED` and decrementing both `active_letter_count` figures by 1. Terminating the member revokes every one of its `ACTIVE` letters as a cascade, described below. And when the logical clock reaches or passes a letter's `effective_to`, the letter moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with the same pair of decrements. `DECLINED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are all final: none of them can be reopened, and a member wanting authorization again must submit a fresh letter.

Member activation is the gate the letter machinery guards. A `GpoAdmin` activating a `PENDING` member moves it `PENDING → ACTIVE`, and activating a `SUSPENDED` one reinstates it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and additionally clears `payout_hold` to false. Both directions require the member to hold at least one `ACTIVE` authorization letter — an `active_letter_count` of at least 1 — and a member with none is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_HAS_NO_ACTIVE_LOA`. Both increment the member's `version` by 1. Reinstatement is a narrow mercy: it clears the payout hold but never clears `reconciliation_posture`, because a `RESTRICTED` posture is one-way and survives every subsequent reinstatement. Attempting to activate a member that is already `ACTIVE`, or one that has been `TERMINATED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Termination is available from `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` alike — any state other than `TERMINATED`, and a second attempt on an already-terminated member is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It sets `enrollment_status` to `TERMINATED`, sets `payout_hold` to true, and runs a two-hop cascade. In hop 1, every authorization letter of that member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED`. In hop 2, caused by hop 1, the member's `active_letter_count` and the `active_letter_count` of each distributor that lost a letter are recomputed as the count of that side's letters whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `TERMINATED` is the end of the member's lifecycle. One state change is not reachable by any operator request at all: a member goes `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` only as a consequence of a compliance review returning a breach verdict, which also sets `payout_hold` to true and increments the member's `version` by 1.

### 3.2. Manufacturer Deals, Contracted Items and Rate Card Governance

A **manufacturer deal** is the contract the GPO negotiated on its members' behalf. Creating one takes a `manufacturer_name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `deal_code` of 3 to 30 characters unique across all deals — a duplicate drawing `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEAL_CODE` — the inclusive calendar bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to` with the end not before the start, and a `required_purchase_window_days` of at least 1, which fixes how long a zero-purchase stretch has to be before the deal is considered abandoned. A new deal's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `contracted_item_count` is 0, its `last_purchase_date` is null and its `version` is 1.

Under a deal sit its **contracted items**, each one a product the deal prices. An item is created with `deal_id`, a `manufacturer_sku` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique among that deal's items — a repeat within the same deal is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_SKU` — a `normalized_item_key` of 1 to 60 characters drawn only from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen, a `pack_size` of at least 1 selling unit per case, and the two savings reference costs. `baseline_unit_cost_cents` is the pre-GPO reference cost per case and `contract_unit_cost_cents` is the negotiated cost per case, both at least 1 cent; together they define what a member could have saved by buying on contract. The normalized key is the linchpin of the whole ingestion pipeline: it is the canonical cross-distributor identity that every distributor's own item number is later resolved against. A new item's `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `approved_rate_card_count` is 0 and its `version` is 1, and the owning deal's `contracted_item_count` is recomputed. Items may only be added while the deal's `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `LOST` deal is closed to changes and rejects the attempt with `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`.

A **rate card** prices the rebate on one contracted item over one closed date range, and it is the only place a rebate mechanic is declared. A card names its `contracted_item_id`, carries the mandatory `expected_contracted_item_version` described in §2.8, declares a `rate_type`, supplies exactly the rate fields that `rate_type` calls for, and fixes its own inclusive `effective_from` and `effective_to` calendar dates with the end not before the start. Like item creation, card creation is only permitted while the deal behind the item has `status` of `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `LOST` deal rejects the proposal with `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`. There are three mechanics. `PER_CASE` requires `flat_rate_cents_per_case`, a rebate in cents for each case bought, at least 1 when present. `PERCENT_OF_SPEND` requires `percent_bps`, the rebate as basis points of the line's invoiced extended cost, from 1 through 10000 when present. `TIERED_VOLUME` requires `tier_breaks`, an ordered array of volume tiers where each entry pairs a `min_cases` threshold with a `rate_cents_per_case` and must itself be a JSON object carrying exactly those two keys and no others, both of them integers; the array must hold at least 2 entries, the first entry's `min_cases` must be 0, `min_cases` must strictly increase down the array, and every `rate_cents_per_case` must be at least 1. Each mechanic forbids the other two mechanics' fields outright. A card whose supplied rate fields do not match its declared `rate_type`, or whose `tier_breaks` violates any of those structural rules, is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_SHAPE_INVALID`.

Rate cards for the same item must not fight over the same dates. A proposal is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when some existing card for the same `contracted_item_id` whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` overlaps the requested range under closed-interval semantics — that is, when the new range's start is on or before the existing range's end and the existing range's start is on or before the new range's end. Note the contrast with authorization letters: because both ends of a rate card's range are inclusive, two ranges that touch on a single calendar date **do** overlap and the later one is refused.

The escape hatch is `supersede`, an optional boolean that defaults to false when the field is omitted. A proposal carrying `supersede` set to true skips the overlap check entirely and instead truncates the incumbent, and it requires the item to have exactly one card whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `effective_to` falls on or after the new card's `effective_from`; when no such incumbent exists the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_INCUMBENT_RATE_CARD`. When it does, the incumbent's `effective_to` is rewritten to the new card's `effective_from` minus one calendar day, the incumbent moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, the new card's `supersedes_rate_card_id` is set to the incumbent's `id`, and the item's `approved_rate_card_count` is decremented by 1. A proposal that omits `supersede` or sets it false leaves `supersedes_rate_card_id` null and takes the overlap check.

However a proposal arrives, the created card's `status` is `PROPOSED` and its `pinned_item_version` is null, and the owning contracted item's `version` is incremented by exactly 1 for the call as a whole — once per call, not once per card touched, so a superseding proposal that mutates two card records still advances the item's version by a single step.

Approval is what makes a card usable. A `GpoAdmin` approving a `PROPOSED` card moves it `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, stamps `pinned_item_version` with the contracted item's `version` as observed at that instant, increments the item's `approved_rate_card_count` by 1 and increments the item's `version` by 1. Approval requires the card's contracted item to have `status` of `ACTIVE` — a `DELISTED` item is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, tested alongside the card's own status and therefore ahead of the deal gate — and requires that item's deal to have `status` of either `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, a deal that is `SUSPENDED` or `LOST` drawing `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`. A card that is not `PROPOSED` cannot be approved and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Cards also age out on their own: when the logical clock advances past a card's `effective_to` calendar date, an `APPROVED` card moves `APPROVED → EXPIRED` and decrements the item's `approved_rate_card_count` by 1, while a `PROPOSED` card moves `PROPOSED → EXPIRED` and leaves `approved_rate_card_count` untouched, since it was never counted in the first place. `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal.

With items priced, the deal itself can go live. A `GpoAdmin` activating a `DRAFT` deal moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, recomputes `contracted_item_count` as the number of that deal's items whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and increments the deal's `version` by 1. Activation demands real pricing: at least one of the deal's items must have `status` of `ACTIVE` and an `approved_rate_card_count` of at least 1, and a deal that cannot show one is rejected with `ERR_DEAL_HAS_NO_APPROVED_RATES`. An `ACTIVE` deal can be suspended, moving `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and incrementing `version` by 1, and a `SUSPENDED` deal can be resumed, moving `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and again incrementing `version` by 1; the pair may cycle as many times as the commercial relationship requires. Suspension does not touch the deal's items — they stay `ACTIVE` — but it does make them ineligible for normalization, so purchase lines normalized while the deal sits `SUSPENDED` are set aside rather than matched, as §3.3 explains. `LOST` is the deal's terminal state and no operator can request it; only a compliance review can drive a deal there, which §3.8 covers in full.

### 3.3. Purchase Feed Ingestion and Item Normalization

Purchase data arrives in **batches**, each batch standing for one file a distributor released for one closed calendar period. Either the `DistributorFeed` account itself or a `RebateAnalyst` opens a batch by supplying `distributor_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` with the end not before the start, and a `source_reference` of 3 to 40 characters that is the distributor's own file reference; that reference must be unique among that distributor's batches, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_REFERENCE`. A suspended distributor cannot open a feed at all and is turned away with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`. A new batch begins with `normalization_status` of `RECEIVED`, `audit_status` of `NOT_AUDITED`, a `line_count` of 0, `total_cases_ingested`, `matched_cases`, `unmatched_cases`, `overbilled_cents` and `recovered_cents` all at 0, `accrued_by_user_id` null and `payout_hold` false.

These two status fields are **independent dimensions of the same batch record**. `normalization_status` tracks how far the feed has progressed through item resolution and accrual; `audit_status` tracks the separate question of whether the distributor's pricing on that feed has been audited and any overage recovered. Advancing one dimension never reads or writes the other, with two explicit exceptions stated where they arise: the markup audit requires the feed to have been normalized before it will run, and once the audit dimension has passed into recovery the normalization dimension can no longer be unwound.

Individual invoice lines are ingested one at a time by the `DistributorFeed` account, each naming its `purchase_batch_id` and the `member_id` that bought the goods, and carrying the distributor's own `distributor_sku` of 1 to 40 characters, a `vendor_item_description` of 1 to 200 characters lifted from the invoice, the `normalized_item_key_reported` of 1 to 60 uppercase letters, digits and hyphens that the distributor asserts for the line, a `cases_shipped` count, an `invoice_unit_cost_cents` figure for what the member was actually billed per case, a `distributor_base_cost_cents` figure for the distributor's declared pre-markup cost per case, and the `invoice_date`. Lines may only be added while the batch's `normalization_status` is `RECEIVED`; a batch that has already been normalized or accrued rejects further lines with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The buying member must have `enrollment_status` of `ACTIVE`, and a `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` member is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ENROLLED`. Data release must also actually be authorized at this moment: the member must hold at least one authorization letter whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `distributor_id` is the batch's distributor, and whose half-open window contains the current logical-clock instant, and a line lacking one is rejected with `ERR_LOA_NOT_ACTIVE`. The `invoice_date` must fall inside the batch's inclusive `period_start` through `period_end` range, a stray date drawing `ERR_INVOICE_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`. Finally, `cases_shipped`, `invoice_unit_cost_cents` and `distributor_base_cost_cents` must each be at least 1, and anything below that is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_QUANTITY`.

A stored line's invoiced extended cost is computed immediately and exactly: `invoice_extended_cents` is `cases_shipped` multiplied by `invoice_unit_cost_cents`, and because both operands are integers no rounding arises. The line begins with `match_status` of `UNMATCHED`, `contracted_item_id` null, `accrual_block_reason` of `NONE` and `overbilled_cents` of 0. Its arrival increments the batch's `line_count` by 1 and adds the line's `cases_shipped` to both `total_cases_ingested` and `unmatched_cases`.

**Normalization** is the act of resolving those distributor-vocabulary lines onto contracted items. A `RebateAnalyst` runs it on a batch whose `normalization_status` is `RECEIVED` and which holds at least one line, moving the batch `RECEIVED → NORMALIZED`; a batch in the wrong state, or an empty batch with a `line_count` of 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The run visits every line whose `match_status` is `UNMATCHED`, in ascending order of the line's `created_at`, and classifies it in two steps. First it gathers the **key candidates**: all contracted items whose `normalized_item_key` equals the line's `normalized_item_key_reported`. Then it narrows those to the **eligible candidates**: the key candidates whose own `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose deal's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose deal's inclusive `effective_from` through `effective_to` range contains the line's `invoice_date`. Both gates matter independently — an `ACTIVE` item sitting under a `SUSPENDED` deal is a key candidate but never an eligible one.

The outcome follows from the two candidate sets, and there are four of them. When exactly one eligible candidate exists, the line's `contracted_item_id` is set to that item, `match_status` becomes `MATCHED` and `accrual_block_reason` stays `NONE`. When two or more eligible candidates exist, `match_status` becomes `AMBIGUOUS`, `contracted_item_id` stays null and `accrual_block_reason` stays `NONE`; the server never picks among equally eligible candidates on its own. When there are no key candidates at all — an item key nobody has contracted — the line stays `UNMATCHED` with `accrual_block_reason` of `NONE`, so a later run can pick it up once the item exists. And when key candidates exist but none is eligible, `match_status` becomes `EXCLUDED` and `accrual_block_reason` records why: `ITEM_DELISTED` when every key candidate has `status` of `DELISTED`, and `DEAL_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise. Whichever of the four outcomes it lands on, a line the run visited is written and therefore advances its own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at` — including the line the run leaves sitting at `UNMATCHED` with `NONE` because no contracted item carries its key, whose stored fields are identical afterwards.

Once every line has been classified, the batch's `matched_cases` is rewritten as the sum of `cases_shipped` over its lines whose `match_status` is `MATCHED`, and `unmatched_cases` becomes `total_cases_ingested` minus `matched_cases` — a single figure covering the `UNMATCHED`, `AMBIGUOUS` and `EXCLUDED` lines together. `total_cases_ingested` is never recomputed by normalization; it records what the distributor sent and stays fixed for the life of the batch. Every manufacturer deal that gained a matched line also has its `last_purchase_date` refreshed to the greatest `invoice_date` among the lines matched to that deal's items. That greatest date is taken across every `MATCHED` purchase line in the system pointing at one of those items, not merely the lines of the batch just normalized, and the deal is written — advancing its `version` — only when the resulting date differs from the one the deal already carried, so a re-run that lands on the same date leaves the deal record untouched.

Ambiguity is resolved by hand. A `RebateAnalyst` may map an `AMBIGUOUS` line by supplying the `contracted_item_id` it should resolve to, moving the line `AMBIGUOUS → MATCHED` and recomputing the batch's `matched_cases` and `unmatched_cases`. A manual mapping writes nothing further: the line's `accrual_block_reason` was already `NONE` and stays there, and no deal's `last_purchase_date` is refreshed by it — that refresh belongs to a normalization run alone, so a deal whose only matched line arrived by hand keeps whatever `last_purchase_date` it already had. Mapping requires the line's batch to be sitting at `normalization_status` of `NORMALIZED`, and requires the supplied item to be a member of that line's eligible candidate set — same normalized key, item `ACTIVE`, deal `ACTIVE` and deal dates covering the invoice date. An item outside that set is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_A_CANDIDATE`, which is how an analyst is prevented from quietly attaching a line to whatever item produces the largest rebate.

**Renormalization** is the compensating action that puts a feed back to the beginning. A `RebateAnalyst` may drive a batch `NORMALIZED → RECEIVED`, and also `ACCRUED → RECEIVED`, which makes the sequence `RECEIVED → NORMALIZED → ACCRUED → RECEIVED` a cycle that may repeat as often as the data demands. Either direction resets every line to `match_status` of `UNMATCHED` with `contracted_item_id` null and `accrual_block_reason` of `NONE`, which means each line travels `MATCHED → UNMATCHED`, `AMBIGUOUS → UNMATCHED` or `EXCLUDED → UNMATCHED` according to where it stood. It sets the batch's `matched_cases` to 0 and its `unmatched_cases` to `total_cases_ingested`, leaving `total_cases_ingested` itself alone, and it clears `accrued_by_user_id` back to null. It also resets the audit dimension: `audit_status` returns to `NOT_AUDITED` from either `AUDIT_CLEAN` or `AUDIT_OVERBILLED`, the batch's `overbilled_cents` returns to 0, and every line's `overbilled_cents` returns to 0. Every line of the batch is rewritten by that reset whatever state it stood in, so each one advances its own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at` — a line that was already `UNMATCHED` with `NONE` and 0 is written along with the rest even though nothing about it actually changes. Renormalization is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the batch's `audit_status` has already reached `RECOVERY_REQUESTED`, `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` — money has by then been demanded from or settled with the distributor on the strength of these figures — and it is likewise refused when the batch's `normalization_status` is `ACCRUED` and any accrual of that batch has `status` of `VALIDATED`, `DISPUTED` or `PAID`, since those accruals have entered downstream workflows that cannot be silently unwound.

Unwinding from `ACCRUED` carries the ledger consequences too. Every accrual of the batch whose `status` is `ACCRUED` or `FLAGGED` moves to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` set to `RENORMALIZED`, and the owning member's `accrued_member_share_cents`, `attributed_gpo_retention_cents` and `attributed_admin_fee_cents` are each decremented by that accrual's own stored `member_share_cents`, `gpo_retention_cents` and `admin_fee_cents` respectively. This reversal is bookkeeping, not blame: the member's `dispute_reversal_count` is **not** incremented and its `reconciliation_posture` is **not** touched, which is exactly what separates an operational re-run from an upheld dispute.

### 3.4. Accrual: Rate Selection, Gross Rebate and the Split Waterfall

Accrual is where matched purchase volume becomes money. A `RebateAnalyst` accrues a batch whose `normalization_status` is `NORMALIZED`, moving it `NORMALIZED → ACCRUED` and recording the caller's `X-User-ID` in the batch's `accrued_by_user_id` — the first half of the segregation-of-duties record described in §2.9. A batch in any other normalization state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a normalized batch in which not one `MATCHED` line resolves an approved rate card for its own `invoice_date` is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCRUABLE_LINES`, because an accrual run that would produce nothing should not advance the batch's state.

Each matched line is priced by exactly one rate card, chosen as follows. The candidates are the rate cards whose `contracted_item_id` is the line's resolved item, whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and whose closed `effective_from` through `effective_to` range contains the line's `invoice_date` — both ends inclusive, so a card ending on the invoice date still prices it. Cards in `PROPOSED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED` status are never selectable no matter how well their dates fit. When no candidate survives, the line is given `accrual_block_reason` of `NO_RATE_CARD` and produces no accrual, while the rest of the run proceeds. When candidates do survive, they are ordered by `effective_from` descending — the latest-starting card wins, as the most recently negotiated pricing — and ties on `effective_from` are broken by `created_at` ascending, the earlier-created card winning. The tie-break is on creation time, never on identifier.

The **gross rebate** the manufacturer funds on that line then follows from the selected card's mechanic. Under `PER_CASE`, the gross is the line's `cases_shipped` multiplied by the card's `flat_rate_cents_per_case`. Under `PERCENT_OF_SPEND`, the gross is the line's `invoice_extended_cents` multiplied by the card's `percent_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Under `TIERED_VOLUME`, the gross is read off the card's `tier_breaks` ladder against a cumulative case count, and rounding half up is applied once to the final gross figure and never to an intermediate per-tier product.

The cumulative count is the one thing the system computes two different ways, and at accrue time it uses the **`MARGINAL_BATCH`** variant. Under that variant the cumulative count is scoped to the accruing batch alone: the batch's lines are walked in ascending `invoice_date`, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties on the same date, and each line's cases are laid into the tier ladder on top of the cases already laid by earlier lines **of that same batch for that same contracted item**. The ladder therefore restarts at zero for every batch. The scoping key is the batch and the contracted item alone, with no member dimension: two lines naming the same item resolve to the same ladder even when different members bought them, which is the deliberate contrast with the per-member scoping of the close-time variant in §3.9. Every line whose `match_status` is `MATCHED` lays its `cases_shipped` into that ladder as the walk reaches it, including a line that resolves no approved rate card of its own and so produces no accrual — such a line still raises the cumulative position seen by the lines behind it — while a line in any other `match_status` contributes nothing to the ladder at all. Because the count is marginal, a line that straddles a `min_cases` threshold is split: the cases lying below the threshold are paid at the lower tier's `rate_cents_per_case` and the cases beyond it at the higher tier's, each slice at its own rate, and the slices are summed. Case by case, that means a case is priced by the last tier in the ladder whose `min_cases` is less than or equal to the cumulative count of cases already laid into the ladder strictly before that case: the case that carries the cumulative total up to a tier's `min_cases` is itself still priced by the preceding tier, and the case immediately after it is the first one priced by the new tier. A tier whose `min_cases` is 50 therefore first prices the fifty-first case laid into that ladder. Every accrual records which variant produced its current figure in its `basis` field, and at accrue time that field reads `MARGINAL_BATCH`.

Splitting the gross is a strict waterfall executed in a pinned order, and the order is part of the contract because the same inputs yield different cents if the steps are rearranged. **Step one** selects the administration-fee rate from a band: when the member's `ytd_net_snapshot_cents` is strictly below 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars of year-to-date net rebate — the tier's `admin_fee_bps_low` applies, and when it is at or above that figure the tier's `admin_fee_bps_high` applies. The chosen figure is frozen onto the accrual as `admin_fee_bps_applied`. **Step two** takes the fee off the top: `admin_fee_cents` is the accrual's `gross_rebate_cents` multiplied by `admin_fee_bps_applied` and divided by 10000, rounded half up. **Step three** computes what remains to distribute: `net_distributable_cents` is `gross_rebate_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`. **Step four** gives the member its cut: `member_share_cents` is `net_distributable_cents` multiplied by the tier's `member_share_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up. **Step five** assigns the residual: `gpo_retention_cents` is `net_distributable_cents` minus `member_share_cents`, so the GPO's retention absorbs whatever the rounding left behind and the three parts reconstruct the gross to the cent. Deriving the member's share straight from the gross, or rounding the retention independently from the tier's `gpo_retention_bps`, produces different numbers and is wrong.

There is a subtlety in step one worth stating plainly. The band reads `ytd_net_snapshot_cents`, a **frozen** snapshot, and never the member's live running totals. That snapshot is rewritten by exactly one operation, the period close described in §3.9. The band therefore cannot move while a period is open, and that is precisely what breaks what would otherwise be a circular dependency, where the fee depends on the net position and the net position depends on the fee.

Each accruable line yields one accrual record — at most one accrual per line, ever. It carries `purchase_line_id` pointing at the line it monetizes, a `member_id` copied from that line, the `rate_card_id` of the selected card, `accrued_cases` equal to the line's `cases_shipped`, the computed `gross_rebate_cents`, the frozen `admin_fee_bps_applied`, and the four split figures `admin_fee_cents`, `net_distributable_cents`, `member_share_cents` and `gpo_retention_cents`. Its `final_adjustment_cents` starts at 0, its `status` is `ACCRUED`, its `reversal_reason` is `NONE` and its `validated_by_user_id` is null. Posting it moves the member's ledger: `accrued_member_share_cents`, `attributed_gpo_retention_cents` and `attributed_admin_fee_cents` are each incremented by the accrual's corresponding component. A member's three counters therefore always equal the summed components of its accruals whose `status` is one of `ACCRUED`, `VALIDATED`, `FLAGGED`, `DISPUTED` or `PAID` — reversed accruals having been subtracted out again.

### 3.5. Manufacturer Claims, Reconciliation and Accrual Validation

The manufacturer's own view of what it owes arrives as a **claim**. A `ManufacturerRep`, or a `GpoAdmin` acting on the manufacturer's behalf, files one against a deal by supplying `deal_id`, the mandatory `expected_deal_version` from §2.8, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` of the claimed period with the end not before the start, a `claimed_units` count of cases the manufacturer says it funded rebate on, and a `claimed_amount_cents` figure for the rebate it says it owes. Both figures may be 0 but neither may be negative. The deal must be open to changes — `status` of `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — and a `SUSPENDED` or `LOST` deal is refused with `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`. A period that has already been settled cannot be reopened: when a claim whose `status` is `RECONCILED` already exists for the same `deal_id`, `period_start` and `period_end`, the new filing is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_ALREADY_RECONCILED`.

A filing that survives those checks stores a claim with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `accrued_amount_cents` of 0, `variance_bps` of 0 and `reconciled_at` null, and it advances the owning deal's `version` by exactly 1 for the call as a whole. Restatement is built in: any incumbent claim for the same `deal_id`, `period_start` and `period_end` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` as part of the same request, so a manufacturer that discovers an error simply files again. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, as is `RECONCILED`.

**Reconciliation** is the analyst's comparison of that claim against the GPO's own books. A `RebateAnalyst` reconciles a claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`; a claim in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The comparison is only meaningful once the underlying purchase data has been processed, so reconciliation is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_UNACCRUED_BATCHES` while any purchase batch whose own period overlaps the claim period still has `normalization_status` of `RECEIVED` or `NORMALIZED`.

The deal-period comparison works like this. `accrued_amount_cents` is the sum of `gross_rebate_cents` over every accrual whose rate card belongs to a contracted item of the claim's deal and whose purchase line's `invoice_date` falls inside the claim's inclusive period, counting only accruals whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, `VALIDATED`, `FLAGGED` or `PAID`. Accruals in `REVERSED` status are excluded, since the GPO no longer asserts them; accruals in `PAID` status are included, since they were genuinely earned. `variance_bps` then measures the disagreement as basis points of the GPO's own accrued figure: it is 0 when both the accrued figure and `claimed_amount_cents` are 0; it is 10000 when the accrued figure is 0 while `claimed_amount_cents` is greater than 0, the maximum possible disagreement standing in for an undefined ratio; and otherwise it is the absolute difference between `claimed_amount_cents` and `accrued_amount_cents`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `accrued_amount_cents` and floored to a whole basis point.

Both computed figures are written to the claim, and the verdict forks on a tolerance of 200 basis points — two percent. When `variance_bps` is at or below 200, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → RECONCILED` and `reconciled_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant. When `variance_bps` is above 200, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → VARIANCE_FLAGGED` and `reconciled_at` stays null. Because the variance is floored, a variance of exactly 200 basis points is inside tolerance while 201 is outside. Only a `RECONCILED` claim can back an accrual validation, so a flagged claim must be restated by a fresh filing rather than argued about in place.

**Validation** carries the same comparison down to the individual accrual. A `RebateAnalyst` validates an accrual whose `status` is `ACCRUED` by citing a `claim_id`; an accrual in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, a cited claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` or `SUPERSEDED` rather than `RECONCILED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RECONCILED`, and an accrual whose member has `payout_hold` set to true is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_PAYOUT_HELD`.

The accrual-level comparison first converts the claim into a per-accrual expectation. The claim-implied amount is 0 when the claim's `claimed_units` is 0; otherwise it is `claimed_amount_cents` multiplied by the accrual's `accrued_cases`, divided by `claimed_units`, and rounded half up to whole cents. The accrual-level variance is 10000 basis points when the accrual's `gross_rebate_cents` is 0; otherwise it is the absolute difference between the claim-implied amount and `gross_rebate_cents`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `gross_rebate_cents` and floored. The same 200-basis-point tolerance applies, and the accrual forks on it: at or below tolerance it moves `ACCRUED → VALIDATED`, and above tolerance it moves `ACCRUED → FLAGGED`. Both branches record the caller's `X-User-ID` as the accrual's `validated_by_user_id`, and neither branch changes a single money field — validation is an assertion about agreement, not a correction. The consequence of the fork is felt later: only a `VALIDATED` accrual can ever be paid, while a `FLAGGED` one remains earned but unpayable until it is disputed or the feed is re-run.

### 3.6. Disputes, Evidence Rounds and the Reversal Cascade

When a member believes its share is wrong, it opens a **dispute**. Only the `MemberOperator` that owns the accrual may do so, and only while its own `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`, an inactive member being refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ENROLLED`. The filing names the `accrual_id` it contests, may optionally cite a `claim_id` as the manufacturer evidence it relies on — the field is simply absent, and stored as null, when the member has no claim to point at — states a `reason_code` of `RATE_MISMATCH`, `UNITS_MISMATCH`, `ITEM_MISMATCH` or `TIER_MISMATCH`, and quantifies the grievance as `disputed_amount_cents`. That amount must be at least 1 cent and must not exceed the cited accrual's `member_share_cents`; anything outside that range is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SHARE`, since a member cannot claim to have been short-paid more than it was owed in the first place.

Two further gates guard the filing. The accrual must be contestable — any `status` other than `REVERSED` or `PAID`, and those two alone are refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, so an accrual already sitting at `DISPUTED` passes this particular gate and is left to the gates that follow it — and no other dispute on the same accrual may still be live, a second concurrent filing being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_DISPUTE` when an existing dispute on that accrual has `status` of `OPEN` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`. Timeliness is the other gate: the logical clock's current calendar date must be at most the tier's `dispute_window_days` whole days after the accrual's own `created_at` calendar date, with the boundary day itself still open. A member arriving one day later than that is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A successful filing stores the dispute with `status` of `OPEN`, an `evidence_round_count` of 0, `opened_by_user_id` set to the caller's `X-User-ID`, and both `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` null. Critically, it also captures `accrual_status_before` — the accrual's `status` at that exact instant, one of `ACCRUED`, `VALIDATED` or `FLAGGED` — and then moves the accrual to `DISPUTED`. That captured value is what makes a rejected dispute perfectly reversible, because the accrual will be restored to it rather than to a guessed default.

Evidence gathering is a loop. A `DisputeArbiter` may send an `OPEN` dispute back for support, moving it `OPEN → EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` while the cited accrual stays `DISPUTED` throughout. The member then answers with an `evidence_note` of 1 to 500 characters describing what it is supplying, which moves the dispute `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED → OPEN` and increments `evidence_round_count` by 1; the note itself is not among the dispute's stored properties, so only the round count records that the answer arrived. Submitting evidence requires the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the accrual's `member_id`. The loop may repeat as many times as the arbiter needs, and only the `OPEN` state accepts a decision — an attempt to decide a dispute sitting at `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Requesting evidence is barred when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the dispute's `opened_by_user_id`, which draws `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Deciding takes a `decision` of either `UPHOLD` or `REJECT` together with a `decision_note` of 1 to 500 characters recording the arbiter's rationale, and both branches record `decided_by_user_id` and store the note. Both branches also enforce the full segregation-of-duties bar from §2.9: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the dispute's `opened_by_user_id`, from the cited accrual's `validated_by_user_id`, and from the `accrued_by_user_id` of the batch the accrual's line arrived on, any collision drawing `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Beyond that shared frame the two branches diverge completely.

A `REJECT` decision is a pure state restore. The dispute moves `OPEN → REJECTED` and the accrual returns from `DISPUTED` to exactly the status recorded in `accrual_status_before` — `DISPUTED → ACCRUED`, `DISPUTED → VALIDATED` or `DISPUTED → FLAGGED` as the case may be — with the accrual's `validated_by_user_id` preserved where it had one. Not one money field and not one counter anywhere in the system changes. Because the accrual becomes contestable again, the sequence `ACCRUED → DISPUTED → ACCRUED` may repeat for as long as the tier's dispute window remains open.

An `UPHOLD` decision runs a four-hop cascade, and every hop is caused by the one before it. **Hop 1**: the dispute moves `OPEN → UPHELD` with `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` written. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1: the accrual moves `DISPUTED → REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` set to `DISPUTE_UPHELD`. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2: the member's `accrued_member_share_cents` is decremented by that accrual's stored `member_share_cents`, its `attributed_gpo_retention_cents` by the accrual's `gpo_retention_cents` and its `attributed_admin_fee_cents` by the accrual's `admin_fee_cents`, each by the exact stored component rather than a recomputed one, and the member's `dispute_reversal_count` is incremented by 1. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3 when the incremented `dispute_reversal_count` reaches the tier's `reversal_posture_limit`: the member's `reconciliation_posture` moves `CLEAR → RESTRICTED`, its `payout_hold` is set to true, and every purchase batch that holds at least one purchase line of that member and whose `normalization_status` is `ACCRUED` has its own `payout_hold` set to true with its `version` incremented by 1. Hop 4 fires only while the posture is still `CLEAR`; `RESTRICTED` is a one-way destination that no reinstatement clears. Both `UPHELD` and `REJECTED` are terminal dispute states: a decided dispute is never reopened, and a member wanting to contest the same accrual again — possible only after a rejection, since an upheld dispute leaves nothing to contest — must open a fresh dispute while the window allows.

Consider a member on a tier whose `reversal_posture_limit` is 2. Its first upheld dispute retracts one accrual's money and lifts `dispute_reversal_count` to 1, and nothing else happens. Its second upheld dispute retracts a second accrual and lifts the count to 2, at which point hop 4 fires: the member is restricted, its payouts are held, and its accrued batches are flagged. From then on every attempt to validate one of its accruals is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_PAYOUT_HELD`, and no accrual of that member will be paid at period close, until a `GpoAdmin` reinstates it — which clears the hold but leaves the posture `RESTRICTED` forever. Note the deliberate asymmetry against the renormalization reversal of §3.3: an operational re-run reverses the same three money counters but never touches `dispute_reversal_count` or the posture, so only a member's genuine disputes can restrict it.

### 3.7. Cost-Plus Markup Audit and Overbilling Recovery

Rebates are only half of the value the GPO promises; the other half is making sure the distributor billed what it agreed to bill. Distributors invoice on a cost-plus basis, declaring a base cost per case and adding a contracted markup, and the audit dimension of a purchase batch exists to test that arithmetic invoice by invoice.

A `RebateAnalyst` runs the markup audit on a batch whose `audit_status` is `NOT_AUDITED` and whose `normalization_status` is either `NORMALIZED` or `ACCRUED`; a batch still sitting at `RECEIVED`, or one already audited, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The run leaves `normalization_status` completely untouched — the two dimensions genuinely are independent, and auditing a feed neither advances nor rewinds its accrual progress.

The audit visits every line of the batch in ascending `invoice_date`, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, and it visits them **regardless of `match_status`**, because overbilling is a pricing question rather than a rebate question: an unmatched or excluded line was still billed to a member. Auditability, however, depends on the paperwork. A line is auditable only when its member holds an authorization letter whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `scope` is `PURCHASE_DATA_AND_PRICING`, and whose `distributor_id` is the batch's distributor. A line whose member has no such letter — including a member whose only letter is `PURCHASE_DATA_ONLY` — has its `overbilled_cents` set to 0 and is skipped, because the GPO was never authorized to see that member's pricing from this distributor.

For each auditable line the calculation runs in a fixed order. The allowed markup in basis points is the lower of two ceilings: the distributor's own `contracted_markup_bps` and the `markup_cap_bps` of the tier the line's member belongs to — whichever protects the member more. The allowed unit cost is then the line's `distributor_base_cost_cents` plus that base cost multiplied by the allowed markup and divided by 10000, with the markup portion rounded half up to whole cents. The excess per case is the line's `invoice_unit_cost_cents` minus the allowed unit cost, floored at zero so that a distributor billing under its ceiling generates no negative offset. The line's `overbilled_cents` is that per-case excess multiplied by the line's `cases_shipped`. Only after every line is done is the batch's `overbilled_cents` set to the sum of its lines' figures. Every line the run visits is written, so it advances its own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at` whether or not its `overbilled_cents` actually moved — that covers the non-auditable line whose figure was already 0 and the auditable line whose computed excess comes out at 0 as much as the line that turns out to have been overbilled. The rounding point matters: the half-up rounding is applied to each line's allowed markup before the multiplication by cases, and rounding a batch-level total instead would produce a different answer whenever two lines' base costs round in opposite directions.

The audit's verdict forks on the computed total. When the batch's `overbilled_cents` comes out at 0, the batch moves `NOT_AUDITED → AUDIT_CLEAN` and the audit is over. When it comes out above 0, the batch moves `NOT_AUDITED → AUDIT_OVERBILLED` and enters the recovery track. Both branches write the same per-line and batch-level figures; only the destination state and what may follow differ.

Recovery proceeds in two steps by two different roles. A `RebateAnalyst` opens the demand, moving the batch `AUDIT_OVERBILLED → RECOVERY_REQUESTED` and incrementing the batch's `version` by 1; the batch's distributor must have `status` of `ACTIVE` for a demand to be opened, an already-suspended distributor drawing `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`. A `GpoAdmin` then settles it by supplying an `outcome` of either `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, with `recovered_cents` required when the outcome is `RECOVERED` and required to be **omitted** when the outcome is `WRITTEN_OFF` — supplying it in the write-off case is an unknown-field violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the key counts as supplied even when its value is an explicit `null`. Where the field is required it must be an integer of at least 0 — a recovery of exactly 0 cents is a legitimate settlement — and a negative figure draws the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both halves of that coupling belong to step 7 of the ladder, so a body whose `recovered_cents` contradicts its `outcome` is reported ahead of the state gate, even on a batch whose `audit_status` never reached `RECOVERY_REQUESTED`. Settlement requires the batch's `audit_status` to be `RECOVERY_REQUESTED`, anything else drawing `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The two outcomes have entirely disjoint consequences. An outcome of `RECOVERED` stores the supplied `recovered_cents`, which must not exceed the batch's `overbilled_cents` — a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OVERBILLING` — moves the batch `RECOVERY_REQUESTED → RECOVERED`, credits the overbilled members as described next, and adds `recovered_cents` to the distributor's lifetime `overbilled_recovered_cents`. An outcome of `WRITTEN_OFF` forces `recovered_cents` to 0, moves the batch `RECOVERY_REQUESTED → WRITTEN_OFF`, credits no member at all, and cascades into the distributor relationship: the batch's distributor moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with its `version` incremented by 1, putting the contract under review and blocking that distributor from opening further feeds or countersigning further letters. No operation in this system returns a `SUSPENDED` distributor to `ACTIVE`. Both `RECOVERED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are final.

Allocating a recovery is a proportional waterfall with an exact residual rule. First, each member's overbilling on this batch is totalled as the sum of `overbilled_cents` over that member's lines within the batch, and only members whose total is strictly positive participate — a member whose lines audited clean receives nothing even if it has other lines in the same feed. Each participating member's provisional slice is the batch's `recovered_cents` multiplied by that member's overbilling total, divided by the batch's `overbilled_cents`, and floored to whole cents. Because flooring loses cents, the remainder — the recovered total minus the sum of the provisional slices — is then distributed one cent at a time: members are ordered by their overbilling total descending, ties broken by the member's own `created_at` ascending, and the first several members in that order each receive one additional cent, as many members as there are remainder cents. The tie-break is on creation time, never on identifier. Each member's final slice is added to its `recovery_credit_cents`, and the slices sum to `recovered_cents` exactly. Whenever the settlement recovers at least one cent, every participating member is written by it, so its `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed even when its own slice floors to 0 cents and no remainder cent reaches it; a member that did not participate is not written at all, and a settlement whose `recovered_cents` is 0 allocates nothing and writes no member whatever.

### 3.8. Contract Compliance Reviews and Deal Loss

A **compliance review** answers two questions at once about a closed calendar window: did this member buy enough to honour its commitment, and did anybody at all buy under this deal. Either a `GpoAdmin` or a `RebateAnalyst` runs one by supplying `member_id`, `deal_id`, and the inclusive `window_start` and `window_end`. The window's end may not precede its start, which draws `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, and it may not extend beyond the logical clock's current calendar date, which draws `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE` — a review of a period that has not finished happening would understate purchasing by construction.

The review computes and freezes five figures. `window_days` is the number of days from `window_start` to `window_end` with **both endpoints counted**, so a window whose start and end are the same date is 1 day long. `committed_spend_prorated_cents` scales the member's annual promise down to the window: it is `committed_annual_spend_cents` multiplied by `window_days`, divided by 365, and rounded half up. `contracted_spend_cents` is the sum of `invoice_extended_cents` over that member's purchase lines whose `match_status` is `MATCHED`, whose resolved contracted item belongs to the reviewed deal, and whose `invoice_date` falls inside the inclusive window; lines in `AMBIGUOUS`, `EXCLUDED` or `UNMATCHED` status contribute nothing even when their reported key names a contracted item of the deal. `deal_wide_contracted_spend_cents` is that identical sum computed without the member filter — every member's matched spend against this deal inside the window. `purchase_ratio_bps` measures performance against the prorated promise: it is 0 when `committed_spend_prorated_cents` is 0, and otherwise `contracted_spend_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `committed_spend_prorated_cents`, and **floored**, so a true ratio of 7999.9 basis points is recorded as 7999 and does not reach an 8000-basis-point minimum.

The verdict written to `result` is decided by four branches evaluated in a fixed order, and the deal-loss branch is evaluated **first** so that an abandoned deal is never masked by a member's own shortfall. `DEAL_LOST` is the verdict when `deal_wide_contracted_spend_cents` is 0, `window_days` is at least the deal's `required_purchase_window_days`, and the deal's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Failing that, `PASS` is the verdict when `purchase_ratio_bps` is at or above the tier's `compliance_min_ratio_bps` — a ratio exactly equal to the minimum passes. Failing that, `WARN` is the verdict when `purchase_ratio_bps` is at or above the tier's `compliance_min_ratio_bps` less 500 basis points, a warning band five percentage points wide immediately below the bar. Otherwise the verdict is `BREACH`.

A `PASS` or a `WARN` changes nothing beyond writing the review. A `BREACH` costs the member its standing: it moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, its `payout_hold` is set to true and its `version` is incremented by 1, which stops both further data ingestion under its name and any payout of what it has already earned until a `GpoAdmin` reinstates it. A review may be run against a member in any `enrollment_status`, and that breach consequence is conditional on the member actually standing at `ACTIVE` when the verdict lands: a `BREACH` on a member already sitting at `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is recorded on the review and changes nothing whatever about the member — not its `enrollment_status`, not its `payout_hold` and not its `version`.

A `DEAL_LOST` verdict is the most consequential single request in the system, and it runs a four-hop cascade in which each hop is caused by the hop before it. **Hop 1**: the deal moves `ACTIVE → LOST` and the review's `triggered_deal_loss` is set to true — that flag is true exactly when this review's `result` is `DEAL_LOST` and this review is the one that moved the deal out of `ACTIVE`. **Hop 2**, caused by the deal leaving `ACTIVE`: every contracted item of that deal whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → DELISTED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the deal's `contracted_item_count` becomes 0. **Hop 3**, caused by those items becoming delisted: every rate card belonging to one of them whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` moves to `EXPIRED`, and each affected item's `approved_rate_card_count` becomes 0. **Hop 3b**, also caused by hop 2: every purchase line whose resolved item is one of the newly delisted ones and whose batch has `normalization_status` of `NORMALIZED` moves `MATCHED → EXCLUDED`, with `accrual_block_reason` set to `ITEM_DELISTED` and `contracted_item_id` cleared to null. **Hop 4**, caused by those lines leaving `MATCHED`: each affected purchase batch recomputes its `matched_cases` and `unmatched_cases`, while `total_cases_ingested` is left exactly as it was. Nothing beyond those hops moves: a deal loss reverses no accrual and adjusts none of the members' money counters, it leaves the deal's own `last_purchase_date` standing at whatever date it had reached, and it leaves every purchase batch's `normalization_status` and `audit_status` untouched, so an already-`ACCRUED` batch keeps both its state and its `MATCHED` lines even though the items behind them are now delisted. `LOST` is terminal — a lost deal can never be resumed, reactivated or amended, and every later attempt to add items or cards to it is refused with `ERR_DEAL_NOT_OPEN_FOR_CHANGES`. `DELISTED` is likewise terminal for a contracted item: no operation returns a delisted item to `ACTIVE`, and because normalization only ever considers `ACTIVE` items, a delisted item can never match another purchase line.

The review record itself is a permanent artifact of that decision. It has no `version` and no `updated_at`, there is no operation that edits it, and the five computed figures plus `result` and `triggered_deal_loss` remain readable exactly as they were written, alongside the `member_id`, `deal_id`, `window_start` and `window_end` the request supplied.

### 3.9. Period Close, Blended Rebasing and Member Savings Reporting

Closing a rebate period is the moment the GPO stops treating a deal's accruals as provisional. A `GpoAdmin` closes a period on one deal by supplying the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` of the period being closed. The close is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_UNACCRUED_BATCHES` while any purchase batch whose own period overlaps the closing period still has `normalization_status` of `RECEIVED` or `NORMALIZED`, because rebasing against a partially processed period would produce figures that the next accrual run would immediately invalidate. Those two dates are the close's only body fields, and that unaccrued-batch test is its only rejection beyond the ladder's shared steps. A close applies neither the period-ordering check of step 22 — a `period_end` lying before its `period_start` is accepted, selects no accrual whatever, and still advances the deal's `version` by 1 — nor any gate on the deal's own `status`, so a `DRAFT`, a `SUSPENDED` or even a `LOST` deal may be closed exactly as an `ACTIVE` one may.

The close touches every accrual whose rate card belongs to a contracted item of the deal, whose purchase line's `invoice_date` falls inside the closing period, and whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, `VALIDATED` or `FLAGGED`. Each such accrual is first **rebased** from the accrue-time variant to the close-time variant. Under the **`BLENDED_PERIOD`** variant, the cumulative case count that drives a `TIERED_VOLUME` ladder is no longer the single batch's running total but the total `MATCHED` cases for the same member and the same contracted item across **every** batch whose lines fall inside the closing period. One single rate is then chosen from the ladder using that period-wide total — the last tier whose `min_cases` is at or below that total, so a total that merely reaches a tier's `min_cases` is already enough to select it — and applied to **all** of the line's cases, with no straddle split at all. The per-case position rule of the marginal variant plays no part here. The practical effect is the point of the whole exercise: a member whose volume only reaches an upper tier once its batches are combined is paid that upper rate across the entire period, not merely on the marginal cases that crossed the threshold.

Rebasing rewrites three things on the accrual. `gross_rebate_cents` becomes the blended figure, `final_adjustment_cents` becomes the new gross minus the old gross — a signed delta that is positive when combining batches lifted the member into a better tier and negative in the rarer case where it did not — and `basis` becomes `BLENDED_PERIOD`. All three are rewritten on every touched accrual, not only on the ones a `TIERED_VOLUME` ladder prices: an accrual priced by a `PER_CASE` or a `PERCENT_OF_SPEND` card recomputes an identical gross, so its `final_adjustment_cents` lands on 0 while its `basis` still moves to `BLENDED_PERIOD`. The split is then recomputed from the rebased gross by the same five-step waterfall of §3.4, with one crucial difference: the band selection step is **not** re-run. The accrual's already-frozen `admin_fee_bps_applied` is reused verbatim, and only `admin_fee_cents`, `net_distributable_cents`, `member_share_cents` and `gpo_retention_cents` are rewritten from it. Each of the member's three money counters is then moved by the **signed delta** between the new component and the old one, so the ledger tracks the rebasing without being rebuilt from scratch.

With the money settled, payment follows. Every touched accrual whose `status` is `VALIDATED` and whose member has `payout_hold` false moves `VALIDATED → PAID`. An accrual left in `ACCRUED` or `FLAGGED` status is not paid — it was never validated against a reconciled claim — and neither is a `VALIDATED` accrual belonging to a member under a payout hold, whether that hold came from a compliance breach, a termination or a restricted reconciliation posture. `PAID` is terminal, as is `REVERSED`; between them they are the only two ways an accrual's life ends.

The last act of the close is to refreeze the administration-fee band. Each affected member's `ytd_net_snapshot_cents` is rewritten to that member's post-close attributed net total, namely its `accrued_member_share_cents` plus its `attributed_gpo_retention_cents` as those counters stand once every rebasing delta has been applied. An affected member is one owning at least one of the touched accruals, and every one of them is written by the close — its `version` advances once and its `updated_at` is refreshed — even when all three of its signed deltas work out to 0 and the refrozen snapshot lands on exactly the figure it already held; a member with no touched accrual in this close is not written at all. That single figure decides, for every accrual created after this close, which of the tier's two administration-fee rates applies — which is why the snapshot must not drift while a period is open. The deal's own `status` is unchanged by a close, but its `version` is incremented by 1.

Members judge the program by one number, and the **savings report** is where they find it. A `MemberOperator` may request its own report, and a `GpoAdmin` or `RebateAnalyst` may request any member's. The report's three inputs arrive as query parameters rather than in a body: `period_start` and `period_end`, the inclusive bounds of the calendar period to be reported, are both required, while `basis` is optional, is either `AS_PURCHASED` or `FULL_BASKET`, and defaults to `AS_PURCHASED` when omitted. A period bound that is absent or not in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, or a `basis` outside those two values, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested after the path's `member_id` has resolved and the member-scope comparison has passed. The two bounds are tested for that `YYYY-MM-DD` shape alone — neither for calendar validity nor for ordering — so a period whose end precedes its start is accepted and simply selects no line. The response is one computed object rather than a collection, so it carries no pagination block and `limit` and `offset` play no part in it. Only the named member's own purchase lines are considered, and only those whose `invoice_date` falls inside the requested inclusive period.

**Realized savings** has two components. The first is hard price savings: over the member's `MATCHED` period lines, the contracted item's `baseline_unit_cost_cents` minus the line's `invoice_unit_cost_cents`, multiplied by the line's `cases_shipped`, summed across lines. The second is rebate the member actually earned: the sum of `member_share_cents` over that member's accruals in the period whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, `VALIDATED`, `FLAGGED`, `DISPUTED` or `PAID`. An accrual counts as being in the period when its `purchase_line_id` names one of the member's period lines, so it is the line's `invoice_date` and never the accrual's own `created_at` that decides membership, and an accrual survives in the sum even if its line has since stopped being `MATCHED`. Accruals in `REVERSED` status never count toward realized savings. The two components together form `realized_cents`, and the rebate component alone is reported separately as `rebate_member_share_cents`.

**Opportunity** measures what was available rather than what was captured: over a basis line set, the contracted item's `baseline_unit_cost_cents` minus its `contract_unit_cost_cents`, multiplied by the line's `cases_shipped`, summed to give `opportunity_cents`. Which lines form that basis set is exactly what the two bases disagree about. Under `AS_PURCHASED`, the basis set is the `MATCHED` lines only, so opportunity is measured only where the member actually bought on contract and an unmatched or excluded line contributes to neither side of the comparison. Under `FULL_BASKET`, the basis set additionally includes the member's `AMBIGUOUS` and `EXCLUDED` period lines, priced against the contracted item named by the line's own `normalized_item_key_reported` — and where several items share that key, against the one with the lowest `contract_unit_cost_cents`. That lookup considers every contracted item carrying the key whatever its own `status` and whatever the `status` or effectivity of the deal above it — a `DELISTED` item under a `LOST` deal is still a legitimate reference price here — it breaks a tie on the lowest `contract_unit_cost_cents` by the earlier `created_at`, and where no contracted item carries the key at all the line simply contributes nothing to either total. Those extra lines add to `opportunity_cents` but never to `realized_cents`, so on identical data `FULL_BASKET` reports a lower capture rate and a higher missed opportunity than `AS_PURCHASED`. That is the intended reading: `FULL_BASKET` prices the leakage of buying off contract.

From those two totals come two more. `missed_opportunity_cents` is `opportunity_cents` minus `realized_cents`, floored at zero so that a member who beat the contracted target is reported as having missed nothing. `capture_rate_bps` is 0 when `opportunity_cents` is zero or negative, and otherwise `realized_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `opportunity_cents`, and floored to a whole basis point.

The report's remaining figures make its arithmetic auditable. `matched_cases` is the sum of `cases_shipped` over the member's period lines whose `match_status` is `MATCHED`; `unmatched_cases` is that sum over the period lines whose `match_status` is `UNMATCHED`, `AMBIGUOUS` or `EXCLUDED`; and `invoice_spend_cents` is the sum of `invoice_extended_cents` over **all** the member's period lines regardless of match status. `recovery_credit_cents` reports the member's stored lifetime overbilling credit as it stands, deliberately **not** period-scoped, so a member can see recovery money alongside rebate money without the report having to attribute a settlement to a period. The response also echoes the `member_id`, `period_start`, `period_end` and `basis` it was computed under. The report reads state and writes nothing.
