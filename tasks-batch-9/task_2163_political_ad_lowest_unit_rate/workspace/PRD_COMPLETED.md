# Political Ad Lowest Unit Rate API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An FCC-licensed broadcast station sells the same thirty seconds of airtime to a supermarket chain, to a ballot-measure committee, and to a legally qualified candidate for federal office — and the law treats those three sales completely differently. The candidate is entitled to the *lowest unit charge*: within a statutory window before the election, the station may charge that candidate no more than it charged its most favoured commercial advertiser for the same class and period of time. The ballot-measure committee gets no such entitlement. Both, however, must carry a sponsorship identification announcement, both generate entries in the station's public political file that must be posted within a hard deadline, and both expose the licensee to complaint proceedings when the paperwork slips. Stations currently run this on spreadsheets, and the failure modes are expensive: a rate determined too high produces a refund obligation discovered months later; a political file entry posted a day late becomes a licence-renewal problem.

This document specifies the API for a political advertising compliance and rate platform operated on behalf of licensed stations. The platform carries the entire chain: it classifies each buyer as a legally qualified candidate or an issue advertiser through a bounded review loop; it prices orders from a published rate card with daypart weighting and volume discounting; it computes the lowest unit charge twice — once provisionally at acceptance to cap a candidate's spots, and once finally after the window closes, retroactively repricing every candidate spot that was charged above the final figure and writing the rebate the station owes; it tracks equal-opportunity requests from opposing candidates against a twenty-four-hour response deadline; it drives the public political file with per-entry due times and a lateness measure; and it holds complaints, cure periods and an audit checkpoint that can freeze a station's ability to take new business.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every temporal rule reads a server-controlled logical clock**, never wall-clock time, so that deadline behaviour is reproducible and testable. Second, **money is never approximated**: all charges, commissions and rebates are integer cents, and the order in which rounding is applied is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participants, Headers and Request Attribution

Every request to the API — without exception, including read-only ones — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the five roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-formed but insufficiently privileged request — a role that is not listed for the endpoint being called in the endpoint table of Section 4 — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `StationComplianceOfficer`, `TrafficScheduler`, `AdvertiserAgent` and `PublicComplainant`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the regulatory operations administrator. This role provisions stations, declares elections, publishes and withdraws rate classes, drives the logical clock, and runs the compliance audit checkpoint. A `StationComplianceOfficer` is the station's political-file and compliance officer: this role classifies advertisers, decides order acceptance, computes lowest-unit-charge determinations, posts entries to the public political file, and answers both equal-opportunity requests and FCC complaints. A `TrafficScheduler` is the station's traffic and continuity operator and is the *only* role permitted to record what actually ran on air; the separation from `StationComplianceOfficer` is deliberate, so that the party deciding acceptance never also certifies the airing. An `AdvertiserAgent` acts on the buy side for exactly one advertiser: placing orders and spots, settling and refunding, cancelling, and filing equal-opportunity requests. A `PublicComplainant` is a member of the public or an opposing interest who inspects the public political file and files FCC complaints.

Identity binding differs by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `AdvertiserAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Advertiser`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Advertiser` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, on order endpoints the comparison is against the order's `advertiser_id`; on equal-opportunity request endpoints it is against the request's `requesting_advertiser_id`; and on creation calls it is against the `advertiser_id` or `requesting_advertiser_id` supplied in the request body. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The comparison only runs where there is something to compare against, and that decides how it interleaves with the existence checks of Section 2.7. Where the guarded resource is named by a path parameter, the comparison waits until that resource resolves, so an order identifier naming no order skips the ownership comparison entirely and falls through to `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. Where the advertiser is named in the request body, the comparison runs against the raw supplied value ahead of every existence check, so a body `advertiser_id` or `requesting_advertiser_id` that differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even when it names no advertiser at all; and a body field that is absent, null or not a non-empty string is skipped by the comparison altogether and left to the payload-shape check. This ownership check guards exactly six operations, and only when the acting role is `AdvertiserAgent`: opening an order, adding a spot to an order, submitting an order, recording a payment on an order, cancelling an order, and filing an equal-opportunity request. Cancellation is also available to a `StationComplianceOfficer`, who is not subject to the check. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `StationComplianceOfficer`, `TrafficScheduler` or `PublicComplainant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `classified_by_user_id` on an advertiser, as `accepted_by_user_id` on an order, or as `responded_by_user_id` on a complaint, where a transition writes it — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

One further attribution rule exists purely for segregation of duties. When a complaint names a specific order, the officer answering that complaint must not be the same person who accepted the order: if `X-User-ID` equals the `accepted_by_user_id` of the `CommercialOrder` referenced by the complaint's `order_id`, the response is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The check is skipped when the complaint carries no `order_id`, and equally when the order it names has never been accepted and so carries a null `accepted_by_user_id`; it also cannot run at all against a path that names no complaint, which falls through to `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` instead.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer within the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of zero or greater, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Collections are always ordered by `created_at` ascending before the page window is applied, regardless of any filters supplied. Because every `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, exact ties are commonplace rather than exceptional; rows sharing a `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which the server created them, so insertion order is the tie-break everywhere a `created_at` ordering is called for in this document. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Broadcast Clock

Every resource the platform persists carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock on each such mutation. The one exception is the rebate ledger. A `RebateLedgerEntry` is an immutable accounting record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely.

Clients never supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` on a create; any such value in a request body is ignored.

All temporal reasoning in this system — deadline comparisons, election window membership, air-date eligibility, cure clocks, lateness measurement — reads a single server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint mounted at `/_clock/set_current_utc`, which responds with the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock has no side effects of its own: it sweeps nothing, expires nothing, and posts nothing. Time-driven state changes happen only when a request explicitly asks for them, which is why the audit checkpoint described in Section 3.9 exists as a separate operation.

### 2.5. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a stored field or in a response. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, it is resolved by **rounding half up** — a value of exactly one half of a cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent.

The unit of political airtime is the **thirty-second unit**. A rate class publishes a `posted_rate_cents` figure that is always the rate for one thirty-second unit; a sixty-second spot is therefore priced at twice the thirty-second product before any other adjustment, and any per-spot charge can be normalised back to a comparable thirty-second figure. That normalisation is stored on each spot as `unit_equivalent_rate_cents`, computed by taking the spot's `charged_rate_cents`, multiplying by thirty, dividing by the spot's `duration_seconds`, and rounding half up. Because lowest-unit-charge comparison operates on these normalised integers, a sixty-second spot and a thirty-second spot are always comparable.

Where an amount is expressed as a count of units rather than money — the equal-opportunity entitlement, for instance — the value is a whole number and any fractional part is discarded downward toward zero rather than rounded.

### 2.6. Stale-View Protection on Determinations and Settlements

Two operations in this system can silently destroy work a client has not yet observed, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control against a named parent resource.

The first is `POST /political-ads/elections/{id}/luc-determinations`, the creation of a lowest-unit-charge determination against an election. Such a request must carry `expected_election_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Election` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the election's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ELECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection is necessary because a determination legitimately supersedes the prior active determination for the same rate class and variant, and because a final-variant determination retroactively reprices spots and writes rebates; a client working from a stale view of the election would supersede a determination it has never seen.

The second is `POST /political-ads/orders/{id}/payments`, the recording of a settlement movement against an order. Such a request must carry `expected_order_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `CommercialOrder` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Payments are additive, so a second settlement call would otherwise succeed on its own terms; the stale-version check is the only thing preventing a client from settling against a charge figure that a reprice, a preemption, or a cancellation has already moved.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Advertiser review, order submission, order acceptance, spot disposition, file-entry posting, equal-opportunity responses, complaint responses and the audit checkpoint all take no expected-version input and are never rejected for staleness.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Scoping

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names nothing produces the not-found error for its own resource type before any deeper check runs. The existence errors are `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` for a station, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND` for an election, `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_FOUND` for an advertiser, `ERR_RATE_CLASS_NOT_FOUND` for a rate class, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for a commercial order, `ERR_SPOT_NOT_FOUND` for a scheduled spot, `ERR_FILE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` for a political file entry, `ERR_EO_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for an equal-opportunity request, `ERR_LUC_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND` for a lowest-unit-charge determination, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` for an FCC complaint, and `ERR_REBATE_NOT_FOUND` for a rebate ledger entry. All eleven carry HTTP 404. The same errors are raised when a *request body* names a non-existent resource of that type, not only when a path segment does.

Existence checking is deliberately ordered by resource type rather than by position in the payload, so that a create naming several bad references reports the earliest type in that fixed order. The order is station, then election, then advertiser, then rate class, then order, then spot, then file entry, then equal-opportunity request, then determination, then complaint, then rebate. That fixed type order governs path identifiers and body identifiers alike, in one combined pass rather than two: a request whose body names a non-existent order and whose path names a non-existent equal-opportunity request answers `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, because order precedes equal-opportunity request in the list even though the order was the one named in the body.

Beyond existence, the platform enforces **single-station scoping** on order creation: the referenced rate class, the referenced advertiser and the referenced election must every one of them belong to the same station named in the request body's `station_id`. Any divergence is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CLASS_STATION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record, and the platform treats the secondary effects as part of the contract rather than as best-effort bookkeeping. Three cascade families recur throughout Section 3 and are stated once here.

**Counter-then-posture.** The station carries four derived counters: `pending_file_entry_count`, the number of its political file entries currently awaiting posting; `overdue_file_entry_count`, the number that have passed their deadline unposted; `late_posting_count`, the cumulative number that were eventually posted late; and `open_complaint_count`, the number of its complaints currently unanswered or reopened. All four start at zero when the station is registered. Whenever `overdue_file_entry_count` or `open_complaint_count` moves, the station's `compliance_posture` is immediately recomputed from the new values, and the recomputation is part of the same request that moved the counter.

**Reprice-then-settle.** Whenever the population of chargeable spots on an order changes — a spot is added, a spot is preempted, a spot is cancelled, or a final determination caps a spot's price — the order's `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents` are all recomputed, and only then is the money already collected compared against the new amount due. If the comparison shows the advertiser has overpaid by more than the rebates already on file, the shortfall is written to the rebate ledger and the order's settlement state advances.

**Event-then-file.** Three kinds of event are disclosable and must reach the station's public political file: the acceptance of an order, any adjustment to a rate already charged, and any response to an equal-opportunity request. Each such event creates a `PoliticalFileEntry` in `PENDING` state and increments the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one, in the same request that produced the event.

Every one of these cascades bumps the `version` and refreshes the `updated_at` of each entity it actually changes, not merely the entity that was the direct target of the request. Three qualifications make that precise. A spot swept up in a reprice is bumped only when the reprice genuinely moves its `charged_rate_cents` or its `unit_equivalent_rate_cents`, and a spot created by the very request that triggers the reprice is never bumped by that reprice, so a newly added spot is returned carrying `version` 1 even though the reprice has priced it; equally, a spot the request has already bumped in an earlier hop of a cascade is not bumped a second time by the reprice that follows. The station is bumped at most once per request, however many file entries, postings or counter movements that one request produces. And each distinct lifecycle transition on an order counts on its own, so an airing that both starts and completes an order raises that order's `version` by two.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed rather than arbitrary. Checks are evaluated in the following ladder, and the first failing check terminates the request.

1. **Authentication and authorisation.** Header presence and role validity (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`) come first, then endpoint role membership (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then buy-side ownership (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), then segregation of duties on complaint responses (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
2. **Pagination.** On collection reads, `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
3. **Existence.** The eleven not-found checks of Section 2.7, in the resource-type order given there.
4. **Payload shape.** `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, covering every structural defect described below.
5. **Stale-view protection.** `ERR_STALE_ELECTION_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`.
6. **Lifecycle state.** The state-position checks — `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_ORDER_TERMINAL`, `ERR_SPOT_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_FILE_ENTRY_ALREADY_POSTED`, `ERR_EO_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_REFUND_NOT_DUE` — in that order.
7. **Compliance and safety gates.** `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_FROZEN`, then `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_CLASSIFIED`, then `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_QUALIFIED_CANDIDATE`, then `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, then `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NONCONFORMING`, then `ERR_DISCLOSURE_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`, then `ERR_RATE_CLASS_NOT_PREEMPTIBLE`.
8. **Referential consistency.** `ERR_RATE_CLASS_STATION_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_RATE_CLASS_WITHDRAWN`, then `ERR_EO_NOT_OPPOSING_CANDIDATE`, then `ERR_EO_TRIGGER_ORDER_NOT_AIRED`.
9. **Temporal admissibility.** `ERR_SPOT_AIR_DATE_IN_PAST`, then `ERR_SPOT_AIR_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, then `ERR_EO_REQUEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`, then `ERR_EO_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`.
10. **Order composition and placement geometry.** `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_SPOTS`, then the three placement checks in the fixed order `ERR_SPOT_SLOT_COLLISION`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_SEPARATION_VIOLATION`, `ERR_STATION_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
11. **Fiscal rules.** `ERR_PAYMENT_TERMS_NOT_PERMITTED`, then `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID`.

The payload-shape check at rung 4 applies to every write endpoint and covers: a required body field missing; a field carrying the wrong type; an enumerated field carrying a value that is not a declared member of its enumeration; a `duration_seconds` that is neither 30 nor 60; a `start_offset_seconds` outside the inclusive range of 0 through 86399; a `posted_rate_cents` below 1; a `preemption_priority` that is not 0 when `preemptible` is false or that is not within the inclusive range of 1 through 5 when `preemptible` is true; an `amount_cents` below 1; an empty `preemption_reason` when the disposition is a preemption; an empty `denial_reason` when the decision is a denial; an absent `granted_order_id` when the decision is a grant; an empty `rejection_reason` when the decision is a rejection; a calendar date that does not parse as `YYYY-MM-DD`; or a timestamp that does not parse as ISO 8601 UTC. All of these carry `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Whether a required string may be empty is fixed field by field rather than uniformly: the identifiers a body supplies, together with `call_sign`, `rejection_reason`, `preemption_reason`, `denial_reason` and `granted_order_id`, must be non-empty once trimmed, while the descriptive and free-text fields — `licensee_name`, `market_code`, the election's `office_sought`, `class_code`, `legal_name`, `program_code`, `complainant_name`, `narrative`, `response_text`, `public_file_url` and `cancellation_reason` — are still required keys but accept an empty string without complaint.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Station and the Compliance Posture Ladder

A `PlatformAdmin` brings a licensee onto the platform by registering the station: its FCC `call_sign`, which must be a non-empty string and unique across every station on the platform — a `call_sign` already carried by another station is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and that uniqueness comparison is made only after every other field on the request has passed its own shape check; the `licensee_name` under which the licence is held; and the `market_code` of the designated market area the station serves. Three further settings are configurable at registration and each has a default, so a minimal registration supplies only the first three. The `political_file_posting_deadline_hours` setting fixes how many hours after a disclosable event the corresponding public-file entry must be posted; it defaults to 24 hours — one full day — and must be an integer of at least 1. The `daily_political_seconds_cap` setting fixes how many total seconds of political spot inventory the station is willing to carry on any single broadcast day; it defaults to 300 seconds, which is five minutes of airtime, and must be an integer of at least 30. Finally `eo_parity_basis` selects which parity algorithm the station applies when it grants an equal-opportunity request; it defaults to `SPOT_COUNT` and its only other permitted value is `DOLLAR_VALUE`.

Registration initialises all four compliance counters — `pending_file_entry_count`, `overdue_file_entry_count`, `late_posting_count` and `open_complaint_count` — to zero, sets `compliance_posture` to `OPEN`, and leaves `last_sweep_at` null until the station's first audit checkpoint is run.

The station's `compliance_posture` is a derived gate on new business, and it is derived in a way that is easy to get subtly wrong. Take the station's current count of overdue political file entries and its current count of open complaints, and consider **the larger of those two numbers** — never their sum. If that larger number is 2 or more, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if it is 1 or more, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Both thresholds are inclusive at their boundary: a station with exactly two overdue file entries and no complaints is `FROZEN`, and a station with exactly one open complaint and no overdue entries is `RESTRICTED`. A station with one overdue entry and one open complaint is `RESTRICTED`, not `FROZEN`, precisely because the two counters are compared rather than added.

What the posture gates is described where it bites, in Section 3.5 and Section 3.9, but the shape of the rule is worth stating up front: a `RESTRICTED` station keeps serving legally qualified candidates and stops taking new issue advertising, while a `FROZEN` station stops taking new business altogether and has its unpaid accepted orders suspended at the next audit checkpoint.

### 3.2. Elections, Statutory Windows, and Advertiser Classification

An election is declared by a `PlatformAdmin` against a station, naming the `office_sought`, the `election_type`, and the `election_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The `election_type` is one of `PRIMARY`, `GENERAL`, `RUNOFF` or `SPECIAL`, and it alone fixes the length of the pre-election window during which the lowest unit charge applies. A `GENERAL` election carries a window of 60 days; `PRIMARY`, `RUNOFF` and `SPECIAL` elections each carry a window of 45 days. That length is stored as `luc_window_days` when the election is declared. From it the platform derives `window_start_date`, the calendar date arrived at by counting `luc_window_days` days backward from the `election_date`, which is the first date *inside* the window; and `window_end_date`, which is simply equal to the `election_date` and is the last date inside the window. Both endpoints are inclusive, and this matters in practice: a spot whose air date falls exactly on `window_start_date` is inside the window, while a spot one day earlier is outside it.

An election's `status` is not stored as a fact but re-derived every time the election is read, by comparing the date portion of the logical clock against the window. While that date is earlier than `window_start_date`, the status reads `SCHEDULED`. While it falls on or after `window_start_date` and on or before the `election_date`, the status reads `WINDOW_OPEN`. Once it is later than the `election_date`, the status reads `CLOSED`.

Buyers enter the system through an intake record opened by a `StationComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin`. Intake records the buyer's `station_id`, the `legal_name` of the buying entity — which will later be matched verbatim inside the sponsorship identification — and `requested_class`, the classification the buyer asserts for itself, either `CANDIDATE` or `ISSUE`. Four further fields are optional at intake: `election_id`, binding the buyer to a declared election and left null for buyers with no election linkage; `candidate_name` and `office_sought`, both null for issue advertisers; and `qualification_evidence_ref`, a reference to the filed evidence of legal qualification, left null when nothing has yet been filed. Intake always sets the advertiser's `status` to `SUBMITTED`, its `review_round` to 0, and its `entitled_to_luc` flag to false, whatever the buyer asserted about itself.

Classification is a review loop, and it is the officer's judgement rather than the buyer's assertion that decides the outcome. Opening a review moves an advertiser from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`; the same operation is also the return leg of the loop, moving an advertiser from `INFO_REQUESTED` back to `UNDER_REVIEW` after the buyer has filed more evidence. An advertiser in any other position rejects the operation with `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_IN_REVIEW` (HTTP 409). The same error guards the decision operation from the other side: a classification decision may only be recorded on an advertiser sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW`.

A decision carries a `decision` value and, optionally, a `reason`. Four decision values exist. `REQUEST_INFO` is the loop leg: while `review_round` is strictly below the ceiling of three rounds, it increments `review_round` by one and moves the advertiser `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`. But the fourth such request is refused rather than looped: when `review_round` has already reached 3, the same `REQUEST_INFO` decision instead moves the advertiser `UNDER_REVIEW → CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED`, sets `classification_reason` to the literal string `MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`, records `classified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves `entitled_to_luc` false. `CLASSIFY_CANDIDATE` moves the advertiser `UNDER_REVIEW → CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE` and is the only decision that sets `entitled_to_luc` to true; it also records `classified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and sets `classification_reason` to the supplied `reason`, or to the literal string `LEGALLY_QUALIFIED` when no reason was supplied. `CLASSIFY_ISSUE` moves the advertiser `UNDER_REVIEW → CLASSIFIED_ISSUE`, sets `entitled_to_luc` to false, and records `classified_by_user_id`. `REJECT` moves the advertiser `UNDER_REVIEW → CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED`, sets `entitled_to_luc` to false, and records `classified_by_user_id`. On `CLASSIFY_ISSUE` and `REJECT` a supplied `reason` is written to `classification_reason` and an omitted one leaves that field exactly as it stood — null on an advertiser that has never carried one — with no default substituted, unlike `CLASSIFY_CANDIDATE`, which does substitute one. The looping `REQUEST_INFO` leg is the single decision that writes neither `classification_reason` nor `classified_by_user_id`, and any `reason` sent with it is discarded; only when that same decision falls through to the rejection branch above are both fields written.

Three additional preconditions guard `CLASSIFY_CANDIDATE` specifically, and all three must hold: the advertiser's `requested_class` must be `CANDIDATE`, its `election_id` must not be null, and its `qualification_evidence_ref` must be a non-empty string. A decision failing any one of the three is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); because these three read stored advertiser state rather than the request body, they are evaluated after the `UNDER_REVIEW` position check rather than alongside the other payload-shape checks at rung 4. `CLASSIFY_ISSUE` and `REJECT` carry no such preconditions beyond the advertiser being `UNDER_REVIEW`. The three classification outcomes `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`, `CLASSIFIED_ISSUE` and `CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED` are all terminal: no operation moves an advertiser out of them, and `entitled_to_luc` reads true if and only if the advertiser is sitting in `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`.

### 3.3. The Rate Card: Dayparts, Preemptibility and Withdrawal

Inventory is published by a `PlatformAdmin` as rate classes against a station. Each class carries a station-facing `class_code`, the `daypart` it sells, a `preemptible` flag, and a `posted_rate_cents` figure which must be an integer of at least 1 and which is always the posted thirty-second unit rate *before* daypart weighting and volume discounting.

The `daypart` is one of five values, and each carries a fixed multiplier applied to the posted rate. `EARLY_NEWS` is the reference daypart and multiplies by 1.00. `PRIME` is the premium and multiplies by 2.25. `MORNING` multiplies by 0.60, `DAYTIME` by 0.45, and `LATE_NIGHT` by 0.35. The product of `posted_rate_cents` and the daypart multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents, is what this document calls the class's **card unit rate** — the undiscounted thirty-second rate for that class. It shows up twice in later sections: as the fallback lowest unit charge when no qualifying spot exists, and as the reference denominator in dollar-value equal-opportunity parity.

Preemptibility is the classic broadcast trade: a preemptible class sells cheaper because the station reserves the right to bump the spot for a better-paying buyer, while a fixed class is guaranteed to run. The `preemption_priority` field orders bumping within the preemptible classes. It must be exactly 0 when `preemptible` is false, and an integer within the inclusive range of 1 through 5 when `preemptible` is true; it defaults to 0. A class is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, and the only other value it can take is `WITHDRAWN`, reached when a `PlatformAdmin` withdraws the class from sale; withdrawing a class that is already `WITHDRAWN` is not an error and simply restamps it, answering HTTP 200 with a further `version` increment. Withdrawal is one-way and it does not disturb existing charges: spots already priced from the class keep their `charged_rate_cents`. What withdrawal blocks is new use — adding a spot to a draft order priced from a withdrawn class, or accepting an order that names one, both fail with `ERR_RATE_CLASS_WITHDRAWN` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Assembling an Order: Spots, Placement Geometry and Volume Pricing

An `AdvertiserAgent` opens a draft order against a station by naming the `station_id` it is buying from, the `advertiser_id` it is acting for, and the `rate_class_id` every spot on the order will be sold from — a single order buys from exactly one rate class. Optionally the order names an `election_id`, attributing it to a declared election, and sets `agency_placed` to true when the buy runs through an agency; `agency_placed` defaults to false. The order is created in lifecycle state `DRAFT` and settlement state `UNBILLED`, with `spot_count`, `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents`, `net_due_cents`, `rebate_total_cents`, `paid_cents` and `refunded_cents` all at zero, and with `accepted_at`, `accepted_by_user_id`, `payment_due_at`, `first_air_at` and `rejection_reason` all null. Single-station scoping applies here, as described in Section 2.7.

Spots are added to the order one at a time, and only while the order is still `DRAFT`; an attempt on an order that has left `DRAFT` fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). Each spot names its `air_date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, its `start_offset_seconds` — the number of seconds after midnight of the local broadcast day at which the spot begins, an integer within the inclusive range of 0 through 86399 — its `duration_seconds`, which must be either 30 or 60, and a `program_code` naming the programme adjacency it runs in. The spot's `order_id` and its `rate_class_id` are not supplied by the client: the owning order supplies the first and copies its own rate class into the second. A new spot begins in `SCHEDULED` state with `luc_capped` false, `luc_cap_cents` null, and both `aired_at` and `preemption_reason` null. An `air_date` earlier than the date portion of the logical clock is refused outright with `ERR_SPOT_AIR_DATE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422); today's date is acceptable.

Three placement checks then run, always in this order, so that a placement failing more than one of them reports the earliest.

**First, inventory collision.** Treat every spot as the half-open interval that begins at its `start_offset_seconds` and ends `duration_seconds` later — the start instant belongs to the spot, the end instant does not. Gather every existing spot that is priced from the same rate class as the new spot, that falls on the same `air_date`, that is itself `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`, and whose owning order is in any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`. The new spot collides with such a spot when the new spot's start is strictly earlier than that spot's end *and* that spot's start is strictly earlier than the new spot's end. Any collision is rejected with `ERR_SPOT_SLOT_COLLISION` (HTTP 409). Because the intervals are half-open, exact adjacency is legal: a spot beginning at the precise second another one ends does not collide, and neither does one ending at the precise second another one begins.

**Second, candidate separation.** Opposing legally qualified candidates are kept apart on air. Gather every existing spot on the same `air_date`, in any rate class, that is `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`, whose owning order is attributed to the same election as the new spot's order — both `election_id` values must be non-null and equal — whose owning order belongs to a *different* advertiser, whose owning order is in any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`, and where the advertiser behind that order *and* the advertiser behind the new spot's order are **both** sitting in `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`. For each such spot, measure the gap: take the later of the two start offsets and the earlier of the two end offsets, subtract the second from the first, and treat any negative result as a gap of zero. If any gap is strictly less than the separation buffer of 900 seconds — fifteen minutes — the placement is rejected with `ERR_CANDIDATE_SEPARATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Overlapping and merely adjacent intervals both yield a gap of zero and are therefore both violations; a gap of exactly 900 seconds is permitted.

**Third, the station's daily cap.** Sum the `duration_seconds` of every `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED` spot that falls on the same `air_date` and whose owning order belongs to the same station and is in any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`. This check ignores placement entirely and looks only at total seconds across the whole broadcast day. If that sum plus the new spot's own `duration_seconds` would exceed the station's `daily_political_seconds_cap`, the placement is rejected with `ERR_STATION_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Landing exactly on the cap is permitted; exceeding it by one second is not.

Once a spot survives all three checks, **the whole order is repriced from scratch**. Pricing is never frozen at the moment a spot was added, and the reason is the volume discount: adding a spot can move the order into a cheaper tier, and every spot on the order — including the ones already there — must feel that move. Repricing proceeds in three steps.

*Step one* reselects the volume tier. Recount `spot_count` as the number of spots on the order whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`; spots that are `PREEMPTED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded from the count entirely. The discount is then read from the tier the recounted `spot_count` falls into: a `spot_count` of 1 through 4 earns no discount at all; 5 through 9 earns 5 percent; 10 through 19 earns 12.5 percent; and 20 or more earns 20 percent.

*Step two* reprices every surviving spot — that is, every spot whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`. For each such spot, take the rate class's `posted_rate_cents`, multiply by the daypart multiplier for that class's `daypart`, multiply by the spot's `duration_seconds` divided by thirty, and multiply by one minus the reselected discount fraction. **Round that single product half up to whole cents once, and store it as the spot's `charged_rate_cents`.** If the spot is already carrying a lowest-unit-charge cap — that is, if `luc_capped` is true — then reduce `charged_rate_cents` to the smaller of the value just computed and the spot's binding cap scaled to its length, where that scaled cap is `luc_cap_cents` multiplied by `duration_seconds`, divided by thirty, and rounded half up. Finally set the spot's `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` to `charged_rate_cents` multiplied by thirty, divided by `duration_seconds`, rounded half up. Every spot on the order that is `PREEMPTED` or `CANCELLED` has both its `charged_rate_cents` and its `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` forced to zero.

*Step three* sums. The order's `gross_charge_cents` is the sum of the per-spot `charged_rate_cents` values **after** each has been individually rounded. The order of operations here is part of the contract: round once per spot, then add the rounded figures. Adding the unrounded per-spot products and rounding the total once is a different — and wrong — answer whenever a per-spot product carries a fractional cent.

Agency commission follows immediately, and its evaluation order resolves what would otherwise be a circular definition. The commission tier table is written against the amount payable on the order, but the amount payable already contains the commission; the platform breaks the loop by fiat rather than by iterating to a fixed point. When `agency_placed` is false, `agency_commission_cents` is zero. When it is true, the commission percentage is selected **from `gross_charge_cents` alone** — never from `net_due_cents`, and never reselected after the commission is added. An order whose gross is below 50000 cents earns no commission; a gross from 50000 through 249999 cents earns 2 percent; a gross of 250000 cents or more earns 3.5 percent. The commission is that percentage of the gross, rounded half up to whole cents. The order's `net_due_cents` is then simply `gross_charge_cents` plus `agency_commission_cents`.

With those figures settled, the platform computes how much of the money already collected is now owed back: take `paid_cents`, subtract `refunded_cents`, subtract `net_due_cents`, and treat a negative result as zero. Rebate ledger rows never feed into `net_due_cents`; they only record the refund this subtraction has already implied.

### 3.5. Sponsorship Identification, Submission and the Acceptance Decision

Every political order carries a sponsorship identification announcement that runs with each of its spots. Two fields on the order describe it: `sponsorship_disclosure_text`, the announcement itself, and `disclosure_duration_seconds`, the on-screen or audible length of that announcement in seconds, which must be an integer of zero or greater. The two are required differently when the order is opened. `sponsorship_disclosure_text` is a required key but a nullable value: an explicit null is accepted and grades as `MISSING`, an empty or whitespace-only string is likewise accepted and grades as `MISSING`, and only omitting the key altogether — or supplying something that is neither a string nor null — is a payload-shape rejection. `disclosure_duration_seconds` is strictly required and not nullable: omitting it or supplying null is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) exactly as a negative or non-integer value is. The order also declares its `payment_terms`, either `PREPAY` or `NET_30`, under the station's order acceptance policy.

The platform grades the announcement into a `disclosure_status` of `MISSING`, `NONCONFORMING` or `CONFORMING`, and it does so twice: once when the order is first opened, and again when the order is submitted. Grading walks four branches in strict top-to-bottom order and the first branch that matches wins. If `sponsorship_disclosure_text` is null, or consists only of whitespace once trimmed, the status is `MISSING`. Otherwise, if the text does not begin with the exact prefix `PAID FOR BY ` — those four words followed by a single space, matched case-sensitively — the status is `NONCONFORMING`. Otherwise, if the owning advertiser's `legal_name` does not appear as a substring anywhere within the text, again matched case-sensitively, the status is `NONCONFORMING`. Otherwise, if `disclosure_duration_seconds` is less than the four-second statutory minimum, the status is `NONCONFORMING`. Only when all four branches are passed does the status read `CONFORMING`. An order is created with `disclosure_status` defaulting to `MISSING` before this grading runs.

Submission moves the order `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is performed by the `AdvertiserAgent` acting for the order's advertiser. The order must still be in `DRAFT`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409) — the same error that guards spot addition. Two further conditions guard it. The order must carry at least one spot — a `spot_count` of zero is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_SPOTS` (HTTP 422). And the freshly recomputed `disclosure_status` must read `CONFORMING`; a status of `MISSING` or `NONCONFORMING` is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NONCONFORMING` (HTTP 422).

Acceptance is the `StationComplianceOfficer`'s decision and is the busiest moment in the system. The decision carries a `decision` value of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, and may carry a `rejection_reason`. The order must be sitting in `SUBMITTED`; anything else fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409).

An `ACCEPT` decision must clear six further gates. The station's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the acceptance fails with `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The ordering advertiser must have reached a terminal classification of either `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE` or `CLASSIFIED_ISSUE`, or the acceptance fails with `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_CLASSIFIED` (HTTP 409) — an advertiser still `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, or one that ended in `CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED`, cannot buy. When the ordering advertiser is `CLASSIFIED_ISSUE`, the station's posture must additionally not be `RESTRICTED`, or the acceptance fails with `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409); this is the asymmetry that lets a lapsed station keep honouring candidate buys while it stops taking new issue advertising. When the ordering advertiser is `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`, the `sponsorship_disclosure_text` must additionally contain the literal string `AUTHORIZED BY` somewhere within it, or the acceptance fails with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422). That candidate-only authorisation requirement is deliberately *not* part of the four-branch grading above, because at submission time the advertiser may still have been under review and its classification unknown. The rate class named by the order must still be `ACTIVE`, or the acceptance fails with `ERR_RATE_CLASS_WITHDRAWN` (HTTP 409). And when the ordering advertiser is `CLASSIFIED_ISSUE`, the order's `payment_terms` must be `PREPAY`; an issue advertiser presented on `NET_30` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_TERMS_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422).

A cleared `ACCEPT` moves the order `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and fires a chain of effects in a fixed order. It stamps `accepted_at` with the logical clock and `accepted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. It advances the settlement dimension `UNBILLED → INVOICED`. When the advertiser's `entitled_to_luc` flag is true and the order names a non-null `election_id`, it computes and records a lowest-unit-charge determination of variant `PROVISIONAL` for the pair of that election and that rate class — superseding whatever prior active `PROVISIONAL` determination existed for the same pair — and then applies that figure as a cap. The cap reaches every spot on the order that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `air_date` falls on or between the election's `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` inclusive: each such spot's `charged_rate_cents` is reduced to the smaller of what it already was and the determination's `luc_cents` multiplied by the spot's `duration_seconds`, divided by thirty, rounded half up. Where that cap strictly lowers the charge, the spot records `luc_cap_cents` as the determination's `luc_cents` and sets `luc_capped` to true. The order's `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents` are then recomputed exactly as described in Section 3.4. The order's `payment_due_at` is computed: on `PREPAY` terms it is the `accepted_at` instant itself, so the money is due immediately; on `NET_30` terms it is 30 days after `accepted_at`. Finally the acceptance files a public political file entry of `event_type` `ORDER_ACCEPTED` against the order, in `PENDING` posting state, and increments the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one.

A `REJECT` decision is the branch sibling of `ACCEPT` and its effects are entirely disjoint from them: no determination is computed, no cap is applied, no political file entry is filed, and nothing is invoiced. A rejection requires a non-empty `rejection_reason` in the request body; the order moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stores that string in its `rejection_reason` field, sets every spot on the order that is still `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED` with its `charged_rate_cents` zeroed, and recomputes `spot_count`, `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents` — all of which fall to zero, since no chargeable spot survives.

### 3.6. Running the Schedule: Airing, Preemption, Settlement and Cancellation

An accepted order carries two state dimensions that advance independently of one another. The lifecycle dimension is exposed as the order's `status` and runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED`, with `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. The settlement dimension is exposed as `payment_status` and runs `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID`, `REFUND_DUE` and `REFUNDED`, with `REFUNDED` terminal. The two never gate each other structurally: an order that is `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` may sit at any of `INVOICED`, `PAID` or `REFUND_DUE`.

Settlement movements are recorded by the `AdvertiserAgent` acting for the order's advertiser, each carrying a `direction` of either `CHARGE` or `REFUND`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and the `expected_order_version` described in Section 2.6. A `CHARGE` requires the order's lifecycle state to be one of `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` — anything earlier or terminal fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409) — but carries no check of its own on the settlement dimension, and there is accordingly no error for charging an order that is not sitting in `INVOICED`. The amount must settle the invoice exactly: it must equal `net_due_cents` minus `paid_cents` plus `refunded_cents`, which on an order already paid in full comes to zero and is therefore unreachable by an `amount_cents` of at least 1 — the amount test, not a state test, is what forecloses a second charge. A `REFUND` requires the settlement state to be `REFUND_DUE`, or it fails with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409), and its amount must equal `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus `net_due_cents`. Either amount being off by even a single cent is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A cleared `CHARGE` adds `amount_cents` to `paid_cents` and advances the settlement dimension `INVOICED → PAID`; a cleared `REFUND` adds `amount_cents` to `refunded_cents` and advances it `REFUND_DUE → REFUNDED`, closing the money invariant under which what the advertiser has paid net of refunds equals what the order is due.

The `TrafficScheduler` is the only role that records what actually ran, and does so by giving a spot a `disposition` of either `AIRED` or `PREEMPTED`. In both cases the spot must still be `SCHEDULED`; a spot that has already left that state fails with `ERR_SPOT_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409).

Both dispositions also require the owning order to have been accepted and not to have gone terminal; an order outside that range is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409). Marking a spot `AIRED` carries three further preconditions. The owning order must be in `ACCEPTED` or `RUNNING`. When the order's `payment_terms` are `PREPAY`, its settlement state must already read `PAID`, or the airing is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID` (HTTP 409) — a prepay order genuinely may not run before the money arrives. And the spot's `air_date` must be on or before the date portion of the logical clock; a spot marked aired before its date has arrived is refused with `ERR_SPOT_AIR_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The transition `SCHEDULED → AIRED` stamps the spot's `aired_at` with the logical clock. If this is the order's first airing — recognisable because `first_air_at` is still null — the order advances `ACCEPTED → RUNNING` and stamps `first_air_at` with the logical clock. If, after this airing, no spot on the order remains `SCHEDULED`, the order advances `RUNNING → COMPLETED`. The airing also refreshes `fulfilled_units` on every granted equal-opportunity request whose grant is attached to this order.

Preemption is where the pricing model shows its teeth. A spot may only be preempted when its rate class carries `preemptible` true; a spot sold from a fixed class is refused with `ERR_RATE_CLASS_NOT_PREEMPTIBLE` (HTTP 409). The owning order must be in `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `RUNNING`, and the request must carry a non-empty `preemption_reason`. The cascade then runs four hops deep. The first hop moves the spot `SCHEDULED → PREEMPTED`, zeroes both its `charged_rate_cents` and its `unit_equivalent_rate_cents`, and stores the supplied string in `preemption_reason`. The second hop, caused by the first, recomputes the order's `spot_count` — which no longer counts the preempted spot — and therefore reselects the volume tier and reprices every remaining `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED` spot at that reselected tier before recomputing `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents`. The counter-intuitive consequence is worth stating plainly: preempting a spot can drop the order out of a discount tier and *raise* the charge on every surviving spot. The third hop, caused by the second, compares money: take `paid_cents`, subtract `refunded_cents`, subtract the new `net_due_cents`, and if that figure exceeds the `rebate_total_cents` already on the ledger, write one rebate ledger entry against the order with `reason` `SPOT_PREEMPTED` and `amount_cents` equal to the excess — the difference between that figure and the existing `rebate_total_cents`, never the whole figure — then refresh `rebate_total_cents` to the new figure and advance the settlement dimension `PAID → REFUND_DUE`. An order already sitting in `REFUND_DUE` stays there and simply accrues the additional entry. The fourth hop, caused by the third, files a public political file entry of `event_type` `RATE_ADJUSTMENT` against the order in `PENDING` posting state and increments the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one. If, after the preemption, no spot on the order remains `SCHEDULED`, the order advances `RUNNING → COMPLETED`.

Cancellation is available to the `AdvertiserAgent` acting for the order and to a `StationComplianceOfficer`, and requires a `cancellation_reason` in the request body. It applies to any order in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `RUNNING`; an order already `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). Cancelling records the supplied string in the order's `rejection_reason` field, sets every spot still `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED` with its `charged_rate_cents` zeroed, and recomputes `spot_count`, `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents`. Where the order had already begun running, its `AIRED` spots keep their charges and `net_due_cents` therefore remains above zero; where nothing had aired, no chargeable spot survives and `net_due_cents` falls to zero. The money comparison then runs exactly as in the preemption cascade: take `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus the new `net_due_cents`, and if it exceeds `rebate_total_cents`, write one rebate ledger entry with `reason` `ORDER_CANCELLED` for the excess only, refresh `rebate_total_cents`, and move the settlement dimension to `REFUND_DUE` — from `PAID` if it was there, and staying in `REFUND_DUE` if it was already there. Cancellation also releases any equal-opportunity grant that was attached to this order, as described in Section 3.8.

The rebate ledger is the audit trail for every cent the station owes back. Each entry names the `order_id` it was written against, the `luc_determination_id` of the determination that produced it — null when the `reason` is `SPOT_PREEMPTED` or `ORDER_CANCELLED` — the `amount_cents` owed at the instant it was written, the `reason` that produced it, and `occurred_at`, the logical-clock instant it was written. Entries are never modified or deleted once written, which is why they carry no version of their own.

### 3.7. Determining the Lowest Unit Charge, and the Final Reprice

A determination answers one question for one rate class inside one election's window: what is the lowest thirty-second unit rate this station actually charged? A `StationComplianceOfficer` requests one against an election by naming the `rate_class_id` to be priced, the `variant` — either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — and the `expected_election_version` required by Section 2.6. The recorded determination copies the election's `station_id` and `election_id`, snapshots the election's `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` as they stood at computation time, stamps `computed_at` with the logical clock, and is created with `status` `ACTIVE`. Creating a determination for an election, rate class and variant that already has an active determination moves that earlier one `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`; superseding is the only way a determination leaves `ACTIVE`, and it never edits the superseded figures beyond that status move, which does bump the superseded record's own `version`. Recording a determination through this endpoint also bumps the target election's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, so a client computing a second determination against the same election must re-read it for a fresh `expected_election_version`; the `PROVISIONAL` determination that an acceptance decision computes as a side effect leaves the election untouched.

The computation gathers a candidate set of spots. A spot qualifies when it is priced from the named rate class; when the order owning it belongs to the same station as the election and is attributed to that same election; when its `air_date` falls on or after the window's `window_start_date` and on or before its `window_end_date`, both endpoints inclusive; and when the spot's own status and its owning order's status both fall within the sets the chosen variant admits. When the computation runs as a side effect of an acceptance decision, the order being accepted is excluded from its own candidate set.

The two variants differ only in which statuses they admit, and that difference is the whole point of computing twice. The `PROVISIONAL` variant admits spots that are `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`, on orders that are `ACCEPTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`. It is what runs at acceptance time to cap a legally qualified candidate's spots, and it can also be requested on demand. Because it counts merely-scheduled spots on merely-accepted orders, a cheap commercial order that has been accepted but has never actually run will drag the provisional figure down. The `FINAL` variant is computed after the window has closed and admits only spots that actually reached `AIRED`, on orders that reached `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`. The same data therefore yields a different figure, and the final figure is the one that carries retroactive force.

If the candidate set comes out empty, the determination falls back to the rate card. Its `luc_cents` becomes the card unit rate for the class — `posted_rate_cents` multiplied by that class's daypart multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents — its `basis` reads `POSTED_RATE_FALLBACK`, its `basis_spot_id` is null, and its `qualifying_spot_count` is zero. If the candidate set is non-empty, `luc_cents` is the smallest `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` among the candidates, `basis` reads `LOWEST_QUALIFYING_SPOT`, and `qualifying_spot_count` is the size of the candidate set. The `basis_spot_id` names the candidate attaining that minimum; when several candidates tie at the minimum, the winner is the one with the earliest `created_at`, and if that still ties, the earliest `air_date`, and if that still ties, the smallest `start_offset_seconds`, and if all three still tie, the spot the server created first. Because `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` is already a half-up-rounded whole-cent figure normalised to thirty seconds, the minimum is taken over integers and no further rounding arises.

A `PROVISIONAL` determination stops there. Its `repriced_spot_count` and `rebate_total_cents` are always zero, because a provisional figure caps only the spots on the order that triggered it and never reaches back across the election.

A `FINAL` determination, by contrast, retroactively reprices. It gathers its targets: every spot priced from the determination's rate class, on an order attributed to the determination's election — whatever lifecycle state that order is in, because the target set carries no order-status filter at all, so a spot on a still-`DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` order is repriced alongside one on a `COMPLETED` order — whose owning advertiser is sitting in `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`, whose own status is `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED`, whose `air_date` falls within the determination's snapshotted window inclusive of both endpoints, and whose `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` is strictly greater than the determination's `luc_cents`. Spots belonging to issue advertisers are never targets: the lowest unit charge is a candidate entitlement and nothing else. Targets are processed in ascending `created_at` order, then ascending `air_date`, then ascending `start_offset_seconds`, and finally in server creation order where all three tie; each target drives a four-hop cascade.

The first hop reprices the spot: its `charged_rate_cents` becomes the determination's `luc_cents` multiplied by the spot's `duration_seconds`, divided by thirty, rounded half up; its `unit_equivalent_rate_cents` becomes the determination's `luc_cents` exactly; its `luc_cap_cents` records that same figure; and `luc_capped` becomes true. The second hop, caused by the first, recomputes the owning order's `gross_charge_cents`, `agency_commission_cents` and `net_due_cents` by the rules of Section 3.4. The third hop, caused by the second, compares `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus the new `net_due_cents` against the order's existing `rebate_total_cents`, and where the former exceeds the latter, writes one rebate ledger entry naming this order, naming this determination as its `luc_determination_id`, carrying `reason` `LUC_FINAL_REPRICE` and an `amount_cents` equal to the excess alone; it then refreshes the order's `rebate_total_cents` to the new figure and moves the settlement dimension `PAID → REFUND_DUE`, with an order already in `REFUND_DUE` staying there and accruing the entry. The fourth hop, caused by the third, files a public political file entry of `event_type` `RATE_ADJUSTMENT` against the order in `PENDING` posting state and increments the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one.

When the cascade is finished, the determination records `repriced_spot_count` as the number of targets it processed and `rebate_total_cents` as the sum of the `amount_cents` of every rebate ledger entry it wrote.

### 3.8. Equal Opportunity: Requests, Deadlines and Parity

When one legally qualified candidate's advertising has actually run on a station, an opposing legally qualified candidate acquires a right to comparable time. The opposing candidate's `AdvertiserAgent` files an equal-opportunity request naming the `station_id` it is served on, the `requesting_advertiser_id` making the claim, and the `trigger_order_id` — the opponent's prior use that opened the right. The request copies its `election_id` from the trigger order rather than taking one from the client, stamps `received_at` with the logical clock, and enters `RECEIVED` with `owed_units` and `fulfilled_units` at zero and `granted_order_id`, `parity_basis_applied`, `denial_reason` and `responded_at` all null.

Four admissibility rules guard the filing. The requesting advertiser must itself be sitting in `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`; an issue advertiser or an unclassified buyer is refused with `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_QUALIFIED_CANDIDATE` (HTTP 409). The trigger order must belong to a *different* advertiser than the requester, that advertiser must itself be in `CLASSIFIED_CANDIDATE`, and the trigger order's `election_id` must be non-null and equal to the requesting advertiser's own `election_id`; any of these failing is refused with `ERR_EO_NOT_OPPOSING_CANDIDATE` (HTTP 422). The trigger order must have reached `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`, so that a first use has genuinely aired; a trigger order still sitting in `ACCEPTED` is refused with `ERR_EO_TRIGGER_ORDER_NOT_AIRED` (HTTP 409). And the filing must be timely: the logical clock must be on or before the instant 7 days — one week — after the trigger order's `first_air_at`. A filing at exactly that instant is admissible; one instant later is refused with `ERR_EO_REQUEST_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).

Once admitted, the request starts a response clock. Its `response_due_at` is the `received_at` instant plus 24 hours — one full day, and no longer. A `StationComplianceOfficer` responds by supplying a `decision` of either `GRANT` or `DENY`, and the response is admissible only while the logical clock is on or before `response_due_at`; responding at exactly the deadline is in time, and responding after it is refused with `ERR_EO_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 409). The request must also still be sitting in `RECEIVED`; a request already granted, denied or expired is refused with `ERR_EO_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409).

A `GRANT` requires a `granted_order_id` naming the requester's own order that the grant attaches to, and that order must satisfy four conditions at once: it must belong to the `requesting_advertiser_id`, it must be sitting in `ACCEPTED`, its `election_id` must equal the request's `election_id`, and its `rate_class_id` must equal the trigger order's `rate_class_id` — parity is measured within a single class of time, not across the rate card. A `granted_order_id` that names a real order but fails any one of the four is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and because those conditions read stored order state they are evaluated after the request's open-state and response-deadline checks rather than at rung 4. The transition `RECEIVED → GRANTED` computes `owed_units`, records `parity_basis_applied` as the station's `eo_parity_basis` in force at that moment, stores `granted_order_id`, stamps `responded_at` with the logical clock, and sets `fulfilled_units` to zero. It then files a public political file entry of `event_type` `EQUAL_OPPORTUNITY_RESPONSE` against the *granted* order, in `PENDING` posting state, and increments the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one.

How many units are owed depends on which parity basis the station has configured, and the two bases give systematically different answers on identical facts. In both cases the measurement runs over the trigger order's spots that are `SCHEDULED` or `AIRED` only; the opponent's `PREEMPTED` and `CANCELLED` spots create no entitlement at all. Under `SPOT_COUNT`, `owed_units` is the sum, across those spots, of each spot's `duration_seconds` divided by thirty — so a sixty-second opponent spot owes 2 units and a thirty-second spot owes 1, regardless of what either cost. Under `DOLLAR_VALUE`, the platform first sums the `charged_rate_cents` of those same spots, then divides that total by the card unit rate of the trigger order's rate class — that class's `posted_rate_cents` multiplied by its daypart multiplier, rounded half up — and discards any fractional part of the quotient downward, never rounding up. Because that denominator is the *undiscounted* card rate while the numerator reflects whatever volume discount the opponent earned, a discounted opponent order awards strictly fewer units under `DOLLAR_VALUE` than the very same spots would award under `SPOT_COUNT`.

Fulfilment is then tracked against the granted order as it runs: `fulfilled_units` is the sum, over the granted order's spots that have reached `AIRED`, of each spot's `duration_seconds` divided by thirty, refreshed on every airing of a spot on that order.

A `DENY` requires a non-empty `denial_reason`. The transition `RECEIVED → DENIED` stores that reason and stamps `responded_at`, and it too files a public political file entry of `event_type` `EQUAL_OPPORTUNITY_RESPONSE` in `PENDING` posting state, incrementing the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one — but against the *trigger* order rather than a granted one, since no grant exists. `DENIED` is terminal.

Two things can happen to a request without an officer touching it. If the response deadline passes with the request still `RECEIVED`, the next audit checkpoint on that station moves it `RECEIVED → EXPIRED`, which is terminal. And if the order a grant was attached to is later cancelled, the grant is unwound rather than left dangling: the request reverts `GRANTED → RECEIVED`, clearing `granted_order_id`, `parity_basis_applied` and `responded_at`, and resetting both `owed_units` and `fulfilled_units` to zero. That reversion is the compensating action that restores the election's outstanding equal-opportunity obligation, and it leaves the request live again for a fresh grant.

### 3.9. The Public Political File, FCC Complaints and the Audit Checkpoint

Every disclosable event produces an entry in the station's public political file, and the entry is created by the platform rather than by any client. It records the `station_id` whose file must carry it, the `order_id` the event concerns, the `event_type` — one of `ORDER_ACCEPTED`, `RATE_ADJUSTMENT` or `EQUAL_OPPORTUNITY_RESPONSE` — and `event_occurred_at`, the logical-clock instant of the event. From that instant and the station's configured `political_file_posting_deadline_hours` the platform derives `due_at`, the moment by which the entry must be posted. The deadline is frozen into the entry at creation: changing the station's configuration later does not move the due time of entries already written. The entry also captures `disclosure_snapshot`, a copy of the order's `sponsorship_disclosure_text` as it read at `event_occurred_at`, so that the file preserves what was actually announced rather than what the order says today. A new entry begins in `PENDING` with `posted_at` and `public_file_url` null and `late_by_seconds` at zero.

A `StationComplianceOfficer` posts an entry by supplying the `public_file_url` at which it now appears. Posting stamps `posted_at` with the logical clock, stores that URL, and settles timeliness. If the entry was sitting in `OVERDUE`, the outcome is `LATE_POSTED` regardless of anything else. Otherwise, if the logical clock is on or before `due_at`, the outcome is `POSTED`; posting at exactly the due instant is on time. Otherwise the outcome is `LATE_POSTED`. `late_by_seconds` records the whole seconds elapsed from `due_at` to `posted_at` whenever the logical clock has passed `due_at`, and stays at zero whenever it has not — a measurement keyed to the clock rather than to the outcome, so an entry already swept to `OVERDUE` and then posted after the clock has been moved back before its deadline still reports `LATE_POSTED` but with `late_by_seconds` at zero. Posting from `PENDING` decrements the station's `pending_file_entry_count` by one, and where the outcome was `LATE_POSTED` it also increments `late_posting_count` by one. Posting from `OVERDUE` decrements `overdue_file_entry_count` by one, increments `late_posting_count` by one, and triggers a recomputation of the station's `compliance_posture` from the reduced overdue count. `POSTED` and `LATE_POSTED` are both terminal: a second posting attempt on an entry in either state is refused with `ERR_FILE_ENTRY_ALREADY_POSTED` (HTTP 409).

Anyone may complain. A `PublicComplainant` files an FCC complaint naming the `station_id` complained about, the `complainant_name` of the complaining party, an `allegation_code` drawn from `LUC_OVERCHARGE`, `FILE_TIMELINESS`, `EQUAL_OPPORTUNITY_DENIED` or `DISCLOSURE_DEFECT`, and a free-text `narrative`. Optionally the complaint names an `order_id`, tying the allegation to a specific order; a station-level allegation leaves it null. Filing stamps `filed_at` with the logical clock, derives `response_due_at` as 240 hours — ten days — after that instant, opens the complaint in `OPEN` with `remedy_code`, `cure_due_at`, `responded_at`, `response_text` and `responded_by_user_id` all null and `late_response` false, then increments the station's `open_complaint_count` by one and recomputes its `compliance_posture`.

A `StationComplianceOfficer` other than the officer who accepted the named order — the segregation rule of Section 2.1 — answers by supplying a `response_text` and a `remedy_code` of `CURED`, `PENDING_CURE` or `NO_VIOLATION`. The complaint must be sitting in `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, or the response is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Responding late is permitted but recorded: the response is accepted whenever the complaint is open, and `late_response` is set to true when the logical clock is strictly later than `response_due_at`. The response stores `response_text` and `remedy_code`, stamps `responded_at` with the logical clock and `responded_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and — when and only when the remedy is `PENDING_CURE` — sets `cure_due_at` to 720 hours, that is thirty days, after `responded_at`; for `CURED` and `NO_VIOLATION` the `cure_due_at` field is left null. The transition, whether it runs `OPEN → RESPONDED` or `REOPENED → RESPONDED`, decrements the station's `open_complaint_count` by one and then recomputes `compliance_posture` from the reduced count; the two source states produce identical effects.

The audit checkpoint is the only operation that lets time itself change state, and a `PlatformAdmin` runs it against one station. It executes six steps in a fixed order, and the later steps depend on what the earlier ones did.

Step one sweeps the public file. Every entry on the station still `PENDING` whose `due_at` the logical clock has passed moves `PENDING → OVERDUE`, processed in ascending `created_at` order; each such move decrements `pending_file_entry_count` by one and increments `overdue_file_entry_count` by one, and each in turn causes the station's `compliance_posture` to be recomputed from the new overdue count.

Step two expires stale equal-opportunity requests. Every request on the station still `RECEIVED` whose `response_due_at` the logical clock has passed moves `RECEIVED → EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order.

Step three settles complaints. A complaint sitting in `RESPONDED` whose `remedy_code` is `PENDING_CURE` and whose `cure_due_at` the logical clock has reached — on it or past it — moves `RESPONDED → REOPENED`, which increments `open_complaint_count` by one and recomputes `compliance_posture`. A complaint sitting in `RESPONDED` whose `remedy_code` is `CURED` or `NO_VIOLATION` moves `RESPONDED → CLOSED`, which is terminal. A reopened complaint can be answered again, and the loop between `RESPONDED` and `REOPENED` is bounded only by the officer eventually supplying a remedy other than `PENDING_CURE`.

Step four recomputes the station's `overdue_file_entry_count`, `open_complaint_count` and `compliance_posture` from the state the first three steps have left behind.

Step five applies a frozen posture to live business. If step four left the posture at `FROZEN`, every order on the station sitting in `ACCEPTED` whose settlement state is anything other than `PAID` moves `ACCEPTED → SUSPENDED`. This suspension is the third hop of the checkpoint's cascade: it is caused by the posture flip in step four, not directly by the request itself.

Step six is the return leg. If step four left the posture at `OPEN`, every order on the station sitting in `SUSPENDED` moves `SUSPENDED → ACCEPTED`, restoring it to live status once the station has cleared its overdue file entries and its open complaints. An order therefore cycles `ACCEPTED → SUSPENDED → ACCEPTED` across successive checkpoints as the station's posture deteriorates and recovers.

Finally, the checkpoint stamps the station's `last_sweep_at` with the logical clock and returns the station in its post-sweep condition.
