# Manufactured Home Chattel Lending API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Manufactured housing finance sits uncomfortably between two bodies of law. A home that rolls off a factory line on a steel chassis is, on the day it is delivered, personal property secured by a certificate of title — a chattel. Bolt the same home to a permanent foundation on land the borrower owns, record a deed and an affixation affidavit, and the state will begin treating it as real property secured by a deed of trust. Everything a lender cares about changes with that reclassification: the instrument that perfects the lien, the maximum term it can grant, the rate it must charge, the amount of hazard coverage it must demand, the order in which a monthly payment is applied, and whether a default is worked as a repossession or as a foreclosure. Today those consequences are tracked by hand across title clerks, insurance trackers, community operators and recovery vendors, and the seams between them are where losses accumulate.

This document specifies the API for a manufactured-home chattel lending platform that makes collateral classification the organizing fact of the system rather than a footnote on a paper file. The platform carries the whole arc of a loan: registering the unit and its placement (a rented lot in a land-lease community, or private land the borrower owns), valuing it against a published appraisal guide with condition, location and installation adjustments, pricing rate and term off leverage and credit, perfecting the lien through the state title agency or the county recorder, enforcing the insurance requirement matrix and force-placing coverage when the borrower lets it lapse, servicing cash through a classification-dependent payment waterfall, and — when the loan fails — running the correct enforcement path, refurbishing the recovered unit and remarketing it, then settling the deficiency or the surplus.

Two structural commitments shape the whole design. First, every collection is addressed flatly beneath a single mount point, and every relationship between resources is expressed as an identifier the caller supplies in a request body or a list filter rather than as a nested URL. Second, the platform is driven entirely by a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that deadlines, accrual windows, coverage intervals and depreciation years are all reproducible. The API is mounted under `/chattel`; every path in this document sits beneath that prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is absent, or is present but empty or made up entirely of whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called — see the authorization column of the endpoint table in §4 — the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `CreditAnalyst`, `ClosingAgent`, `Servicer`, `RecoveryManager`, `CommunityOperator` and `Borrower`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is operations staff. It owns reference data (guide editions, borrower registration, community registration), the logical clock, and the inbound channel through which a recording office reports the outcome of a filing. It also proposes and terminates community operator agreements, and shares unit registration with a `ClosingAgent`.
- A `CreditAnalyst` owns underwriting: it values collateral, opens loan applications, prices them, and approves or declines credit. Valuation creation and certification are shared with a `RecoveryManager`.
- A `ClosingAgent` owns closing and perfection: recording land documents, driving the classification conversion cycle, submitting and resubmitting lien filings, and funding an approved loan. Policy creation is shared with a `Servicer`.
- A `Servicer` owns cash and coverage: posting payments, reversing them, running periodic servicing assessment, and force-placing insurance.
- A `RecoveryManager` owns default enforcement: declaring default, working the repossession or foreclosure arm, recording refurbishment cost, listing the unit and finalizing the sale.
- A `CommunityOperator` owns the lot-rent relationship for one community: it executes that community's own operator agreement and reports lot-rent standing on loans secured by units in its park.
- A `Borrower` is a read-only actor over its own portfolio.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters because it determines what `X-User-ID` is compared against. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `CreditAnalyst`, `ClosingAgent`, `Servicer` or `RecoveryManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for instance as `certified_by_user_id` when a valuation is certified, or as `approved_by_user_id` when a loan is approved — and it may compare two such recorded identifiers to each other, but it never compares an opaque operator identifier to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Community`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Community` compare the resource's community foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a `CommunityOperator` may act on its own community, on units whose `community_id` equals its `X-User-ID`, and on loans whose collateral unit sits in that community. When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Borrower`; ownership checks compare the resource's `borrower_id` — or, for a payment or a recovery case, the `borrower_id` of the loan it hangs from — to `X-User-ID`. A `ManufacturedUnit` carries no `borrower_id`, so a `Borrower` owns a unit when either the unit's `land_owner_borrower_id` equals its `X-User-ID` or at least one loan anywhere in the system is secured by that unit and carries that `borrower_id`; the servicing or underwriting standing of the qualifying loan is irrelevant to the test, and a unit satisfying neither limb is not the borrower's.

Ownership violations are rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The check applies to the operator-agreement endpoint, to lot-rent reporting, and to every read endpoint: a `CommunityOperator` acting on a community other than the one its `X-User-ID` names, or a `Borrower` reading a loan, unit, policy, payment or recovery case that does not belong to it, is refused. Roles whose identity is opaque are never subject to this ownership check.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three envelope-level identifiers sit outside the catalogue of §5 because the transport layer rather than a business rule produces them, and none of them is subject to the authorization ladder because no endpoint has been reached: a request to a path no endpoint claims is answered `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) whatever headers it carries, a request whose method is not defined on an otherwise existing path is answered `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unhandled server fault is answered `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). All three use the error envelope above. A trailing slash is not significant, so `/chattel/units/` and `/chattel/units` reach the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of matching items to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Either parameter supplied as anything other than a plain base-ten integer literal — a decimal figure, a sign-prefixed figure, or a non-numeric string — is rejected the same way, while either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its default. Surrounding whitespace on an otherwise valid integer is ignored.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its items ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows would otherwise tie, ascending creation order remains the only sanctioned ordering — no ordering, selection or tie-break in this system may depend on a resource's randomly assigned `id`.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every item matching the filters rather than the items on the current page, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items actually on this page is less than `total_count`. Where a list endpoint is additionally narrowed to the caller's own resources by the ownership rule of §2.1, that narrowing is applied after the query filters and before the page is cut, so `total_count` counts only the rows the caller is entitled to see. The `meta` object of a single-object success response carries none of the four pagination fields.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this platform reads wall-clock time. A single server-wide logical UTC clock supplies the instant for every accrual window, every coverage interval, every statutory deadline and every depreciation year. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must not fall back to real time in that state.

A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant. The clock may only move forward or stay where it is; a request that would move it backwards is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock mutates no entity — it only changes what subsequent reads of the clock return, which is echoed back as the sole field `current_utc` of the response `data` object; no entity is returned. A request that sets the clock to exactly where it already stands is accepted.

All date arithmetic in this system counts whole calendar days over half-open intervals: an interval that runs from one instant up to but not including another. When a rule says a deadline has been reached, it means the clock is at or after that instant.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Every other instant-valued field on every resource is rendered in that same form, and a field that holds no value is rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted — the single exception being the `reversal_reason` of §3.7, which is written only onto the rows the reversal endpoint creates and is therefore absent, rather than null, on every other payment row. Fields the platform keeps only for internal bookkeeping are never part of a resource's serialized representation.

Most resources also carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation of the resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly one on every mutation. `Borrower`, `Community`, `ManufacturedUnit`, `Valuation`, `Loan`, `LienFiling`, `InsurancePolicy` and `RecoveryCase` all carry the full set of four properties.

Two resources are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, because nothing in the system ever mutates them in place. A `BookValueGuide` is a published edition and is immutable once created. A `PaymentTransaction` is an accounting row; a posting is never edited, and a mistaken posting is corrected by writing a compensating reversal row rather than by changing the original. The one field a reversal does change on the original row, `reversed`, is a bookkeeping flag on an otherwise immutable ledger entry and does not produce an `updated_at` or a `version` on that entity.

A derived-field refresh is a mutation like any other. When funding increases a borrower's `total_outstanding_principal_cents`, when a default hold sets a unit's `recovery_hold`, or when a conversion cascade rewrites a loan's `collateral_classification`, the affected resource's `updated_at` moves and its `version` increments.

### 2.6. Path and Foreign-Key Resolution Order

Identifiers reach the server in two ways: as a path parameter naming the resource being acted on, and as a foreign key inside a request body. Both are resolved before any business rule runs, and path parameters are resolved first, left to right, so that the first identifier in the path that does not resolve determines the error returned.

Each identifier has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_FOUND` for `borrower_id` or `land_owner_borrower_id`, `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND` for `community_id`, `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND` for `guide_id`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` for `unit_id`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND` for `valuation_id`, `origination_valuation_id` or `recovery_valuation_id`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND` for `loan_id`, `refinanced_from_loan_id` or `resale_loan_id`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND` for `filing_id`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` for `policy_id`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `payment_id`, and `ERR_RECOVERY_CASE_NOT_FOUND` for `case_id`.

Body foreign keys are resolved after path parameters, on the seven endpoints that accept them: unit registration, valuation creation, loan application, lien-filing submission, insurance-policy creation, refurbishment start, and sale finalization. The foreign keys resolved there are `borrower_id`, `unit_id`, `guide_id`, `community_id`, `land_owner_borrower_id`, `origination_valuation_id`, `refinanced_from_loan_id`, `loan_id`, `recovery_valuation_id` and `resale_loan_id`. Where one body carries more than one of them, they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares them and the first that does not resolve wins: on unit registration `community_id` is resolved before `land_owner_borrower_id`, on valuation creation `unit_id` is resolved before `guide_id`, and on loan application the declared order is `borrower_id`, then `unit_id`, then `origination_valuation_id`, then `refinanced_from_loan_id`. A body foreign key that is present but is not a non-empty string is a shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, raised at this point in the ladder rather than deferred; an optional one supplied as JSON `null` counts as absent and is not resolved at all, while a mandatory one supplied as `null` or omitted is that same shape failure.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

Requests are validated in a fixed order and the first failure wins; no later check may mask an earlier one. Implementers should treat the following as a single ladder, climbed from the top.

First come the three authorization checks: header presence and role validity (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then role authorization for the endpoint (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`), then resource ownership for the two identity-backed roles (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`). Ownership can genuinely precede existence only where the check needs nothing but the identifier itself: a `CommunityOperator` acting on a `community_id` that is not its own `X-User-ID`, and a `Borrower` reading a `borrower_id` that is not its own, are refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even when that identifier resolves to nothing at all. Where ownership can only be judged by reading the target row — a unit, a loan, a policy, a payment or a recovery case — the path identifier is resolved first, so an identifier that names nothing yields that identifier's own not-found code rather than an ownership refusal.

Next come the two existence layers of §2.6 — path identifiers, then body foreign keys — followed by request-shape validation: every required body field present, every enum value a declared member of its enumeration, every amount named with a `_cents` or `_bps` suffix an integer inside its declared range, every date field a parseable ISO 8601 UTC instant, and pagination within bounds. Any of those failures is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Clock regression is checked at this level of the ladder as well.

Four conventions settle what "present" and "parseable" mean at that rung. A request body that is absent, empty, or literal JSON `null` is read as an empty object, so the endpoint's required fields simply report as missing; a body that parses as a JSON value other than an object is itself malformed. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as absent — a required field reports as missing and an optional field falls to its declared default. A field declared as a string must be a non-empty, non-blank string; a field declared as an integer rejects a boolean even though JSON booleans are integers in some languages. And an instant is parsed leniently before it is judged: a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric UTC offset, and a bare calendar date with no time part are all accepted, an offset other than zero is converted to the equivalent UTC instant rather than refused, and a value carrying no zone at all is read as already being UTC; only a value no ISO 8601 parse accepts is malformed.

Below the shape layer come the domain-specific checks, in this order: guide effectiveness for a valuation; duplicate HUD label, placement-field consistency and lot-occupancy conflict for a unit registration; the two stale-version checks of §2.9, with the unit check on valuation creation preceding the loan check on policy creation; then the state-machine gates — unit classification state, recovery hold, valuation certifiability, operator-agreement state, filing state, recovery-path availability, recovery-case state, loan underwriting state, loan servicing state, and payment-reversal eligibility, in that sequence.

Only after all of those does the system evaluate the substantive credit, perfection, insurance and cash rules: segregation of duties, then valuation variant suitability, then filing-type match, filing document completeness and the filing attempt limit, then insurance date ordering and coverage-period overlap, then land-document completeness for a conversion, then the three funding insurance gates (existence of active coverage, wind endorsement sufficiency, dwelling coverage sufficiency), then force-placement eligibility, then the operator-agreement requirement for funding, then lender access rights for possession, then perfection establishment, then the LTV cap and the term floor, then borrower suspension, then the exposure cap, then default eligibility, then the cure-period, redemption-window-open and redemption-window-closed timing checks, and finally the two cash-integrity checks on a payment: overpayment refusal and payoff-amount exactness.

One ordering subtlety deserves calling out because it is easy to get backwards. When a borrower attempts redemption on a case whose enforcement path is repossession rather than foreclosure, the path-availability check fires **before** the case-status check. A repossession-path case can never reach the redemption window at all, so the caller must be told the action does not exist on this arm — `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_PATH_MISMATCH` — rather than being told its status is wrong.

### 2.8. Integer Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents, and every rate, spread, multiplier and adjustment ratio is an integer number of basis points, where ten thousand basis points is one whole. No monetary or rate field is ever transmitted as a decimal fraction or as a string.

Wherever a formula in §3 requires a division, the whole product is evaluated in exact arithmetic and then the single resulting quotient is rounded half up to the nearest whole cent. For a quotient that comes out negative — the condition and location adjustments of §3.3 are the only quantities in the system that do — half up means half away from zero, so an exact −0.5 cent becomes −1 cent, not 0. Where a calculation shows more than one rounding point, each rounding point is pinned separately: the intermediate result is rounded, and the rounded value — not the exact one — feeds the next step. Rounding points may never be merged, skipped or reordered. The clearest example is force-placed pricing, where the annual premium is rounded first and the pro-rated premium is then derived from the already-rounded annual figure.

A related discipline applies to interest. Accrual is computed once per span, over one multiplication and one rounding. Accruing thirty-one days and then sixty-one days does not produce the same result as accruing ninety-two days in a single call, and the difference is visible in the interest allocated on a payment row. The platform therefore accrues exactly once per servicing operation, from the loan's stored accrual watermark up to the logical clock.

The payment waterfall is the one place where no rounding occurs at all: each bucket absorbs the smaller of the cash still unallocated and the bucket's own outstanding amount, and those are already whole cents.

### 2.9. Stale-Read Protection on Valuation and Policy Creation

Exactly two write operations in this system carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, and both guard against a client acting on a parent it read too long ago. Each of the two requires a mandatory field — it is not optional and it has no default — carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent resource.

Creating a `Valuation` requires `expected_unit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ManufacturedUnit` being valued. If it does not equal the unit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard exists is that a new valuation legitimately supersedes the prior certified valuation of the same variant on the same unit; a writer holding a stale view of the unit would otherwise retire a valuation that was computed from unit attributes the writer never saw — a re-graded `condition_grade`, a newly recorded affixation affidavit, a completed classification conversion, or a recovery hold. The guard forces the writer to re-read the unit and re-derive the valuation from current attributes.

Creating an `InsurancePolicy` requires `expected_loan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Loan` the policy covers. If it does not equal the loan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Both version fields are required to be integers but are bounded by no range: a figure no live resource could carry — zero, or a negative integer — is not a shape failure but simply fails to match, so it draws the guard's own 412 rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Here the hazard is that a new policy supersedes the loan's prior active borrower-provided policy and can cancel an active force-placed policy; a writer working from a stale loan view would score its coverage against a requirement matrix that no longer applies, because the loan's `collateral_classification` may have moved to `REAL_PROPERTY` — raising required coverage from 8000 to 10000 basis points of certified value — or a force-placed premium may already have been advanced.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Every other write in the system is unguarded by design, and supplying a version field where none is specified does not create a concurrency check.

### 2.10. Cross-Entity Cascade Integrity

A single call in this system routinely mutates several resources, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Four cascade families run through the whole specification and are worth naming up front.

**Collateral to loan.** A unit's classification is snapshotted onto every loan written against it, and when the unit's classification changes the snapshot on every non-terminal loan is re-synced. A loan is non-terminal for this purpose when its `servicing_state` is neither `PAID_OFF` nor `CHARGED_OFF` and its `underwriting_state` is not `DECLINED`; every other loan on the unit is re-synced, including one still sitting in `DRAFT` or `UNDERWRITING` that has never been funded. That re-sync is not cosmetic: it selects the perfection instrument, the payment waterfall variant, the required coverage ratio, the maximum term and the default enforcement path.

**Loan to filing.** The loan's lien dimension and the filing's own lifecycle move together. A recorded instrument being released, or a released instrument being restored, is always accompanied by the matching movement on the loan.

**Loan to policy.** Whenever a loan's classification changes, or a policy is created and in the same call supersedes or cancels another, the loan's `insurance_compliance` is recomputed from whichever policies remain `ACTIVE` — `COMPLIANT` when at least one of them is itself graded `COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT` otherwise. A lapse is the exception and drives `insurance_compliance` straight to `NON_COMPLIANT` as §3.6 describes, and a force-placement sets it straight to `COMPLIANT`, neither of them polling the rest of the policy set. Re-scoring an existing policy's own `required_coverage_cents` and `compliance_state` is narrower still: only the classification cascade of §3.8 moves the requirement matrix out from under policies already on file, so that cascade is the one event that re-grades them, and it touches a policy only where a stored field actually moves. Every other write grades the row it creates and leaves every other policy's grading exactly as it stood.

**Unit to borrower.** A recovery hold on any unit that secures a borrower's loan suspends that borrower's credit standing, and lifting the last such hold restores it.

Each of these cascades mutates the affected resources in place, refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version` on each, and each is immediate — there is no deferred reconciliation, no eventual consistency window, and no background sweep. A read taken after a cascading call always observes the fully settled state.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in Underwriting

One control in this platform exists purely to keep two judgments in separate hands. The operator identity that certifies the origination valuation for a loan may never be the operator identity that credit-approves the loan secured by it, even though both actions belong to the `CreditAnalyst` role. The certifier's `X-User-ID` is durably recorded as `certified_by_user_id` on the valuation at certification, and the approver's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `approved_by_user_id` on the loan at approval. If the two would be equal, approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Beyond this one control the operational roles are peers: none of them can substitute for another, and none of them supervises another.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data and the Community Operator Relationship

Before any lending can happen, three kinds of reference record must exist: the appraisal guide the platform values against, the obligors it lends to, and the communities its collateral sits in. All three are registered by a `PlatformAdmin`.

An appraisal guide is published as an immutable edition. It is described by a `guide_edition` label, a `region_code` naming the guide region whose base rate applies, and an `effective_date` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant from which the edition may be cited. Its numeric content is a `base_value_per_sqft_cents` figure greater than zero, two width multipliers expressed in basis points (`single_wide_multiplier_bps` and `multi_wide_multiplier_bps`, both greater than zero), and an `annual_depreciation_bps` figure between 0 and 1000 inclusive that expresses straight-line depreciation per year of unit age. A guide edition is never edited after publication; a corrected guide is a new edition.

An obligor is registered with a `full_name`, a `credit_score` between 300 and 850 inclusive, a `monthly_income_cents` figure of zero or more representing verified monthly income, and a two-letter `state_code` for the obligor's residence. A `state_code` is stored exactly as supplied and is validated only as a non-empty string — neither here nor on a community does the platform enforce a length, an alphabet or a list of known states. Three fields are managed by the platform rather than supplied. A borrower's `status` begins at `ACTIVE` and is the credit-standing gate for new originations; the only other value it takes is `SUSPENDED`. A borrower's `total_outstanding_principal_cents` begins at 0 and is at all times the sum of `principal_balance_cents` across that borrower's loans whose servicing standing is `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT`, `DEFAULT` or `RECOVERY` — loans that have reached `PAID_OFF` or `CHARGED_OFF` are excluded from the total entirely. A borrower's `units_under_recovery_hold_count` also begins at 0 and is the number of *distinct* units that both secure a loan of this borrower and currently carry a recovery hold; because one borrower may hold two loans on the same unit, the count is over distinct units, not over loans. The relationship between the two derived fields and credit standing is mechanical: `status` is `SUSPENDED` whenever `units_under_recovery_hold_count` is greater than zero and `ACTIVE` otherwise. This exposure recomputation runs at credit approval and on every funding, every payment posting and reversal — payoffs and charge-offs among them — every recovery-hold change, and every lot-rent report; registering a unit or writing its placement does not trigger it.

A land-lease community is registered with a `name`, a two-letter `state_code`, a `lot_count` greater than zero, a `monthly_lot_rent_cents` figure of zero or more giving the posted monthly lot rent, and an optional `sales_commission_bps` between 0 and 500 inclusive — the referral commission the operator charges the lender when a remarketed unit is sold in-community. When `sales_commission_bps` is omitted it is 0. A newly registered community starts with `operator_agreement_status` at `NONE`, `lender_access_rights` false, and `agreement_executed_at` null.

The operator agreement is the contract that lets the lender onto the operator's land, and it moves through four states. A `PlatformAdmin` proposes an agreement with the `action` value `PROPOSE`, which is available from `NONE` and also from `TERMINATED` — a lapsed relationship can be re-proposed — and moves the community to `PENDING`. From `PENDING`, the `action` value `EXECUTE` moves the community to `EXECUTED`, and this is the one write in the system reserved to the `CommunityOperator` role: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the community's own `id`. Execution sets `lender_access_rights` to true and stamps `agreement_executed_at` with the logical clock, and it also raises the location adjustment applied to future valuations of units in that community, as described in §3.3. From `EXECUTED`, the `action` value `TERMINATE` — again a `PlatformAdmin` action — moves the community to `TERMINATED`, sets `lender_access_rights` back to false, and from that moment blocks the funding of any further loan on a unit in that community. Termination does not clear `agreement_executed_at`, which stays where execution stamped it as a historical record; a later re-execution simply overwrites it with the clock of that execution. Every operator-agreement call increments the community's `version`. An `action` that does not match the community's current standing — proposing while `PENDING` or `EXECUTED`, executing while not `PENDING`, terminating while not `EXECUTED` — is rejected with `ERR_OPERATOR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Both roles reach this one endpoint, so the split of arms between them is enforced inside it: an `action` belonging to the other role — a `PlatformAdmin` sending `EXECUTE`, or a `CommunityOperator` sending `PROPOSE` or `TERMINATE` — is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403), and that refusal is judged after the community has been resolved and `action` confirmed to be a declared member, but before the standing gate just described.

### 3.2. Registering Collateral, Claiming a Lot, and Recording Land Documents

A manufactured unit is registered by either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ClosingAgent`. Its physical description consists of a `hud_label_number` (the HUD certification label, which must be globally unique across all units), a `manufacturer`, a `model_year` that drives both depreciation and remaining useful life and is required to be an integer but is bounded by no range, a `width_class` of either `SINGLE_WIDE` or `MULTI_WIDE`, a `square_feet` figure greater than zero giving heated floor area, a `condition_grade` of `EXCELLENT`, `GOOD`, `FAIR` or `POOR`, an `installation_type` of either `PIER_AND_ANCHOR` or `PERMANENT_FOUNDATION`, and a `wind_zone` of 1, 2 or 3 identifying the HUD wind zone of the site. Registering a second unit under a `hud_label_number` another unit already carries is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HUD_LABEL` (HTTP 409).

Placement is where the two worlds of manufactured housing diverge, and the `placement_type` field — `COMMUNITY` or `PRIVATE_LAND` — selects which set of placement fields must be supplied. A community placement requires a non-null `community_id`, a `lot_number` labelling the lot the unit occupies, and a `placement_start_date` opening the occupancy interval, and it requires `land_owner_borrower_id` to be null. A private-land placement requires a non-null `land_owner_borrower_id` naming the fee owner of the parcel, a `land_parcel_apn` (the assessor parcel number), and a `land_county` (the recording county for the land-home instruments), and it requires `community_id` to be null. The test the platform actually applies is exactly that pair of clauses and nothing wider: a `COMMUNITY` placement fails unless `community_id`, `lot_number` and `placement_start_date` are all supplied and `land_owner_borrower_id` is absent, and a `PRIVATE_LAND` placement fails unless `land_owner_borrower_id`, `land_parcel_apn` and `land_county` are all supplied and `community_id` is absent; either failure is rejected with `ERR_PLACEMENT_FIELDS_INCONSISTENT` (HTTP 422). Only the two foreign keys are cross-checked for absence, so the descriptive fields of the other world are tolerated rather than refused: a `land_parcel_apn` or `land_county` sent alongside a community placement, and a `lot_number` or `placement_start_date` sent alongside a private-land placement, are accepted and stored exactly as supplied. Two further placement fields are optional in both cases: `placement_end_date` closes the occupancy interval, and a null value there means occupancy is open-ended; `land_value_cents` records the assessed land value and defaults to 0. That land value is deliberately walled off from collateral valuation — it is separate collateral reached through the deed of trust, never part of the unit's book value.

Lot occupancy is exclusive, and it stays exclusive for a week after a unit moves out. When a new placement claims a `community_id` and `lot_number` pair, the platform examines every existing unit already claiming that same pair. Each incumbent occupies a half-open interval that begins at its `placement_start_date` and ends seven days after its `placement_end_date`; those seven days are the turnover buffer during which the lot is being cleaned and re-set. If the new `placement_start_date` falls inside any incumbent's buffered interval — at or after the incumbent's `placement_start_date` and strictly before seven days after its `placement_end_date` — the registration is refused with `ERR_LOT_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Two boundary cases complete the rule. An incumbent whose `placement_end_date` is null has never moved out, so its interval has no end and no buffer at all: any new `placement_start_date` at or after that incumbent's own start conflicts with it. An incumbent that never opened an interval — `placement_start_date` null — is ignored by the test entirely. A new start strictly earlier than an incumbent's start never conflicts with that incumbent, and a start at or after a closed incumbent's buffered end is clear of it.

Every newly registered unit starts with `classification` at `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, `title_status` at `ACTIVE_TITLE`, `recovery_hold` false, and both land-document flags — `deed_recorded` and `affixation_affidavit_recorded` — false.

Those two flags are the gate on classification conversion, and a `ClosingAgent` sets them one at a time by recording a land document against the unit's parcel. The `document_type` value `DEED` sets `deed_recorded` to true; the value `AFFIXATION_AFFIDAVIT` sets `affixation_affidavit_recorded` to true. Recording a document increments the unit's `version`, and it does so even when the flag it names was already true — a repeat recording is accepted, leaves the flag at true, and still refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`. Land documents are only meaningful on a private-land placement, but recording one is not itself gated on placement: the flag is set on whichever unit is named, whatever its `placement_type`, and the private-land requirement bites only when a conversion is initiated. A unit that is under a recovery hold, or that secures any loan whose servicing standing is `RECOVERY` or `CHARGED_OFF`, is off limits to land-document recording: the request is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The same guard applies to classification conversion.

Units can be listed and filtered by `community_id`, by `land_owner_borrower_id`, by `classification` and by `placement_type`, which is how a `CommunityOperator` finds the collateral in its own park and how a recovery desk finds every unit of a given classification. Filter values are judged the same way on every list endpoint in this specification: a value for an enumerated filter such as `classification` or `placement_type` that is not a declared member of its enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), whereas an identifier filter such as `community_id` or `land_owner_borrower_id` is matched as an opaque string and is never resolved, so an identifier that names nothing simply matches no rows rather than raising a not-found. A filter supplied as an empty string is treated as if it had been omitted.

### 3.3. Valuing the Unit: Two Variants of the Same Guide Walk

A `CreditAnalyst` or a `RecoveryManager` values a unit by naming the unit as `unit_id`, the guide edition as `guide_id`, the algorithm to run as `variant`, and the unit's last observed version as `expected_unit_version`. The `variant` is either `ORIGINATION` — the value that underwriting leans on — or `RECOVERY_LIQUIDATION`, the value a recovered unit is expected to fetch in a forced sale. Both variants share the first four steps of the walk and diverge only at the end. The cited guide must already be in force: if its `effective_date` is after the logical clock, the request is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 422). Exactly one unit and one guide feed the computation, plus the unit's community when the placement is a community lot; earlier valuations on the same unit contribute nothing.

The walk begins with age. The platform stamps `valuation_year` with the calendar year of the logical clock at the moment of creation, which fixes the age used for depreciation for the life of this valuation. Age in years is `valuation_year` minus the unit's `model_year`, floored at zero so that a unit newer than the current year never ages negatively. Depreciation in basis points is that age in years multiplied by the guide's `annual_depreciation_bps`, but capped: the depreciation may never exceed 7000 basis points, which is ten thousand basis points less a 3000-basis-point residual floor, so at least thirty percent of guide value always survives no matter how old the unit is.

Next comes the guide base. The width multiplier is the guide's `multi_wide_multiplier_bps` when the unit's `width_class` is `MULTI_WIDE` and its `single_wide_multiplier_bps` otherwise. The platform then computes `book_base_cents` as the unit's `square_feet` multiplied by the guide's `base_value_per_sqft_cents`, multiplied by the width multiplier in basis points divided by ten thousand, multiplied by the surviving fraction of value — ten thousand basis points less the depreciation in basis points, divided by ten thousand. That entire product is evaluated exactly and rounded half up to a whole cent once, at the end; `book_base_cents` is the first of the pinned rounding points described in §2.8.

The third step reaches across from the unit into its community to decide `location_key`, and it has exactly three outcomes. When the unit's `placement_type` is `PRIVATE_LAND`, the key is `PRIVATE_LAND`. Otherwise the unit sits on a community lot, and the key is `COMMUNITY_WITH_AGREEMENT` when that community's `operator_agreement_status` is `EXECUTED` and `COMMUNITY_WITHOUT_AGREEMENT` in every other case — including `PENDING` and `TERMINATED`, neither of which grants the lender anything.

The fourth step applies three adjustments, and a detail here is easy to get wrong: both proportional adjustments are taken on `book_base_cents` itself, never on a running subtotal that already includes the other adjustment. The `condition_adjustment_cents` figure is `book_base_cents` multiplied by the condition rate for the unit's `condition_grade` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — where the rate is a credit of 800 basis points for `EXCELLENT`, exactly zero for `GOOD`, a charge of 1200 basis points for `FAIR`, and a charge of 2500 basis points for `POOR`. The `location_adjustment_cents` figure is `book_base_cents` multiplied by the location rate for `location_key` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — a credit of 300 basis points for `COMMUNITY_WITH_AGREEMENT`, a charge of 700 basis points for `COMMUNITY_WITHOUT_AGREEMENT`, and a credit of 900 basis points for `PRIVATE_LAND`. Both of these are signed quantities: a charge is stored as a negative number of cents. The `installation_adjustment_cents` figure is not proportional at all — on the `ORIGINATION` variant it is a flat 850,000 cents for a unit on a `PERMANENT_FOUNDATION` and a flat 250,000 cents for a unit on `PIER_AND_ANCHOR`, and on the `RECOVERY_LIQUIDATION` variant it is dropped entirely and recorded as zero. The adjusted subtotal is `book_base_cents` plus all three adjustments.

Now the two variants part company, and each carries the full complement of fields so that a reader of either can see the whole picture.

On the `ORIGINATION` variant, both liquidation deductions are recorded as zero — `forced_sale_discount_cents` is 0 and `detach_and_move_cost_cents` is 0 — and `certified_value_cents` is the adjusted subtotal exactly as computed. This is the value that drives leverage at underwriting and the insurance requirement at closing.

On the `RECOVERY_LIQUIDATION` variant, two deductions apply. The `forced_sale_discount_cents` figure is the adjusted subtotal multiplied by 2200 basis points divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — the haircut a forced sale takes. The `detach_and_move_cost_cents` figure is a flat 675,000 cents when, and only when, `location_key` is `COMMUNITY_WITHOUT_AGREEMENT`; on private land, or in a community whose operator agreement is executed, it is zero, because in those cases the unit can be sold where it stands rather than detached and hauled away. Then `certified_value_cents` is the adjusted subtotal less the forced-sale discount less the detach-and-move cost, floored at zero so that a badly depreciated unit reports a value of 0 rather than a negative one.

Creating a valuation has three consequences beyond the new row. It moves every earlier `CERTIFIED` valuation of the same unit and the same variant to `SUPERSEDED`, each row so retired having its own `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented — which is precisely why the stale-version guard of §2.9 exists on this write, and why `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` is returned when `expected_unit_version` does not match. A `DRAFT` valuation of the same unit and variant is left alone, and a certified valuation of the *other* variant is never disturbed. It increments the unit's own `version`, so a client that creates two valuations back to back must re-read the unit between them. And it leaves the new valuation in `status` `DRAFT`, with `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` both null.

A draft valuation is a working figure; only a certified one may be cited by a loan or a recovery case. Certification is performed by a `CreditAnalyst` or a `RecoveryManager` and freezes the row: `status` moves to `CERTIFIED`, `certified_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, and `certified_at` is stamped with the logical clock. The identity recorded here is the counterparty of the segregation-of-duties control in §2.11, so the analyst who certifies is deliberately spending their ability to approve the resulting loan. Certification is only available on a valuation whose `status` is `DRAFT`; attempting it on one that is already `CERTIFIED` or has been `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_CERTIFIABLE` (HTTP 409).

Valuations can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `guide_id`, `variant` and `status`, and read individually with their full adjustment breakdown, which is how an auditor reconstructs how a number was reached.

### 3.4. Origination: Application, Pricing, Credit Decision and Funding

A loan in this system carries three independent state dimensions on one row, and they advance separately. `underwriting_state` tracks the origination decision and runs `DRAFT`, `UNDERWRITING`, `APPROVED`, `FUNDED` and `DECLINED`, with `FUNDED` and `DECLINED` both terminal on that dimension. `servicing_state` tracks cash and collections and runs `NOT_SERVICING`, `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT`, `DEFAULT`, `RECOVERY`, `PAID_OFF` and `CHARGED_OFF`, with `PAID_OFF` and `CHARGED_OFF` terminal. `perfection_state` tracks the lien and runs `UNPERFECTED`, `FILING_SUBMITTED`, `PERFECTED`, `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` and `RELEASED`, with `RELEASED` terminal. Reading a loan returns all three dimensions together with every obligation bucket.

A `CreditAnalyst` opens an application by naming the obligor as `borrower_id`, the collateral as `unit_id`, a certified origination valuation as `origination_valuation_id`, the `principal_cents` to be advanced (which must be greater than zero), and the `requested_term_months` the applicant asked for (also greater than zero) before any cap is applied. When the new loan retires an earlier loan on the same unit, the earlier loan is named as `refinanced_from_loan_id`; the field is otherwise null, and it is the mechanism by which a refinance chain is traceable. That parent is resolved for existence and then stored as given — the platform never checks that the loan it names sits on this same unit, belongs to this same borrower, or stands in any particular state. The cited valuation must be `CERTIFIED`, must be of variant `ORIGINATION`, and must have been computed on the same unit the loan is secured by; a valuation that fails any of those three tests is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_VARIANT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A borrower whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` cannot have a new application opened at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BORROWER_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409).

Creation takes two snapshots off the unit that then live on the loan. The loan's `collateral_classification` is copied from the unit's current classification and is either `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` or `REAL_PROPERTY`; a unit sitting in `CONVERSION_PENDING` is mid-flight and has not yet become real property, so the snapshot taken from it settles to `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`. The mapping is exactly that: `REAL_PROPERTY` for a unit already classified `REAL_PROPERTY`, and `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` for anything else. The loan's `placement_type` is copied from the unit's placement and is `COMMUNITY` or `PRIVATE_LAND`. Both snapshots then drive pricing, term, the perfection instrument, the payment waterfall and the default path — and the first of them is re-synced later if the unit converts, as §3.9 describes. A new loan starts with `underwriting_state` `DRAFT`, `servicing_state` `NOT_SERVICING` and `perfection_state` `UNPERFECTED`.

Submitting the application for underwriting is the pricing act: it advances `underwriting_state` from `DRAFT` to `UNDERWRITING` and, in doing so, computes and stores `ltv_bps`, `apr_bps` and `term_months`, all three of which are null until this succeeds. It takes no request body. The inputs are the loan itself, its borrower, its unit and the certified origination valuation it cites — and the valuation's suitability is re-verified here rather than trusted from application time, so a cited valuation that is no longer `CERTIFIED`, that is not of variant `ORIGINATION`, or that was computed on a different unit than the loan's collateral, fails the submission with `ERR_VALUATION_VARIANT_MISMATCH`.

Leverage comes first. The `ltv_bps` figure is `principal_cents` multiplied by ten thousand and divided by the valuation's `certified_value_cents`, rounded half up to a whole basis point — the only rounded quantity in the whole pricing walk. If the result exceeds 9500 basis points the submission is refused with `ERR_LTV_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); a computed value of exactly 9500 passes. A `certified_value_cents` of zero or less admits no division at all and is treated as leverage no cap could ever accommodate: the submission draws that same `ERR_LTV_CAP_EXCEEDED` without any quotient being formed, and `ltv_bps` stays null along with the other two priced fields.

Rate is then assembled as an additive stack of integer basis-point spreads, and only after the entire stack has been summed is a two-sided clamp applied — never term by term. The stack starts at a base of 875 basis points. To it is added a placement spread of 225 basis points when the loan's `placement_type` is `COMMUNITY` and 50 basis points when it is `PRIVATE_LAND`. To that is added a classification spread of 175 basis points when `collateral_classification` is `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` and zero when it is `REAL_PROPERTY`. To that is added a credit-tier spread read off the borrower's `credit_score`: a credit of 200 basis points at a score of 780 or above, a credit of 125 basis points from 720 through 779, exactly zero from 660 through 719, a charge of 200 basis points from 600 through 659, and a charge of 425 basis points below 600. To that is added a leverage-tier spread read off the `ltv_bps` just computed: zero at 8000 basis points or below, 75 basis points from 8001 through 9000, and 150 basis points from 9001 through 9500 — anything above 9500 having already been refused. Finally, a wind-exposure surcharge of 60 basis points is added when, and only when, the unit's `wind_zone` is 3 **and** its `installation_type` is `PIER_AND_ANCHOR`; a zone-3 unit on a permanent foundation, or a piered unit in zone 1 or 2, takes no surcharge. The summed figure is then clamped: `apr_bps` is the stack raised to a floor of 799 basis points if it fell below that, and lowered to a ceiling of 1899 basis points if it rose above it.

Term is the smallest survivor of four independent caps, every one of which applies. The first is the borrower's own `requested_term_months`. The second is a placement cap of 240 months for a `COMMUNITY` placement and 300 months for `PRIVATE_LAND`. The third is a classification cap of 240 months for `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` and 360 months for `REAL_PROPERTY`. The fourth is the collateral's remaining useful life in months, computed as forty years less the age implied by the valuation — that is, forty less the difference between the valuation's `valuation_year` and the unit's `model_year` — multiplied by twelve, and floored at zero so an over-age unit contributes a cap of zero rather than a negative one. The granted `term_months` is the minimum of those four. If that minimum falls below the five-year floor of 60 months, the submission is refused with `ERR_TERM_FLOOR_UNREACHABLE` (HTTP 422). A successful submission increments the loan's `version`.

Credit approval, also a `CreditAnalyst` action, advances the loan from `UNDERWRITING` to `APPROVED` and enforces three gates in order. The segregation-of-duties control of §2.11 fires first: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `certified_by_user_id` of the loan's origination valuation, or the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The borrower's credit standing is checked next — but the two derived exposure fields of §3.1 are recomputed from that borrower's current loans and holds before the gate reads them, so the standing judged is freshly derived rather than whatever was last stored, and that recomputation may on its own move the borrower's `total_outstanding_principal_cents`, `units_under_recovery_hold_count`, `status`, `updated_at` and `version` even on a request that is about to be refused; a borrower who comes out `SUSPENDED` yields `ERR_BORROWER_SUSPENDED`. Then comes the concentration limit: the borrower's `total_outstanding_principal_cents` plus this loan's `principal_cents` must not exceed 15,000,000 cents, which is a hundred and fifty thousand dollars of aggregate exposure per obligor; a total that lands exactly at the cap passes, and a total above it is refused with `ERR_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A successful approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the loan's `version`.

The alternative outcome is a decline, which moves the loan from `UNDERWRITING` to `DECLINED` with no preconditions at all and accepts an optional `decline_reason`. That reason is stored on the loan verbatim as whatever string the caller sent, the empty or blank string included, and stays null when the field is omitted or sent as JSON `null`. A declined loan is finished: `DECLINED` is terminal on the underwriting dimension and the loan's `servicing_state` stays at `NOT_SERVICING` forever. Declining also increments the loan's `version`. Any origination action attempted from the wrong underwriting standing — submitting from anything but `DRAFT`, approving or declining from anything but `UNDERWRITING`, funding from anything but `APPROVED` — is refused with `ERR_LOAN_UNDERWRITING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Funding is the `ClosingAgent`'s act and it is the most heavily gated write in the system, because it is the moment money leaves. It requires a `first_due_date` and accepts an optional `escrow_target_cents` of zero or more. Four conditions must hold, and because each carries its own refusal the sequence they are judged in is itself part of the contract — the sequence §2.7 gives, which is deliberately not the sequence a reader would guess. First, every filing on the loan standing `RECORDED` must carry the `filing_type` that matches the loan's `collateral_classification` — a title lien for personal property, a land-home deed of trust for real property — and any recorded filing that does not is refused with `ERR_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH`; a loan with no recorded filing at all satisfies this condition vacuously. Second, the insurance requirement of §3.6 must be satisfied on all three of its counts. Third, when the loan's `placement_type` is `COMMUNITY`, the unit's community must be in `EXECUTED` standing on its operator agreement; otherwise funding is refused with `ERR_OPERATOR_AGREEMENT_MISSING` (HTTP 409), which is exactly what a terminated agreement causes. Only last is perfection itself weighed: the loan's `perfection_state` must be `PERFECTED` with exactly one filing in `RECORDED` standing, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERFECTION_NOT_ESTABLISHED` (HTTP 409). Two consequences follow from that ordering. A loan that is both unperfected and uninsured answers with the insurance refusal rather than the perfection one, and a loan carrying two correctly typed recorded instruments passes the first condition and is refused here at the last.

A successful funding advances `underwriting_state` from `APPROVED` to `FUNDED` and, as a cascade onto the servicing dimension, advances `servicing_state` from `NOT_SERVICING` to `CURRENT`. It stamps `funded_at` and `last_accrual_date` with the logical clock and sets `next_due_date` to the supplied `first_due_date`. It sets `principal_balance_cents` to `principal_cents`, opening the loan's unpaid principal. It records `escrow_target_cents` from the request, defaulting to 0 when the field is omitted — and a target of zero means the escrow bucket of the payment waterfall is permanently empty, which materially changes how cash is applied. It increases the borrower's `total_outstanding_principal_cents` by `principal_cents`. And it increments the loan's `version`.

Loans can be listed and filtered by `borrower_id`, by `unit_id`, by `origination_valuation_id`, by `refinanced_from_loan_id` and by `servicing_state`.

### 3.5. Perfecting the Lien: Two Instruments, One Table, and a Rejection Cycle

Perfection is what turns a promise to pay into a secured claim, and which instrument does the job depends entirely on how the collateral is classified. A `ClosingAgent` tenders an instrument by naming the loan as `loan_id`, choosing a `filing_type`, and listing the document codes tendered with the attempt as `submitted_documents`. A `filing_type` of `TITLE_LIEN` is filed with the state title agency and perfects against personal property; a `filing_type` of `LAND_HOME_DEED_OF_TRUST` is recorded with the county and perfects against real property. The chosen instrument must match the loan's `collateral_classification` — title lien for `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, deed of trust for `REAL_PROPERTY` — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Each instrument has its own document schedule. A title lien requires both `TITLE_APPLICATION` and `LIEN_ENTRY_FORM`. A land-home deed of trust requires all three of `DEED_OF_TRUST`, `AFFIXATION_AFFIDAVIT` and `TITLE_SURRENDER_RECEIPT`. The platform records the shortfall in `missing_documents`, which holds exactly those required codes for the chosen `filing_type` that are absent from `submitted_documents`. A tender that omits any required code is refused with `ERR_FILING_DOCUMENTS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422), and the same document check applies again on every re-tender.

A successful tender copies `unit_id` onto the filing from the loan's collateral — the caller never supplies it — stores `submitted_documents` exactly as sent, computes `missing_documents` (necessarily the empty list, an incomplete tender having already been refused), opens the filing at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `attempt_number` 1, and advances the loan's lien dimension from `UNPERFECTED` to `FILING_SUBMITTED` while incrementing the loan's `version`. The `submitted_documents` field must be an array of strings and is otherwise unpoliced: codes outside the chosen instrument's schedule are tolerated rather than refused, and duplicates and the caller's ordering are preserved as sent. Nothing else about the loan or the unit gates a tender — no servicing standing, no recovery hold, and no ceiling on how many filings one loan may carry — and the lien dimension is written to `FILING_SUBMITTED` from whatever value it held, which is exactly how the `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` loan of §3.8 re-perfects. The new filing's `agency_reference`, `rejection_code`, `recorded_at` and `released_at` all start null.

The recording office answers through a `PlatformAdmin`, who reports an `outcome` of either `RECORDED` or `REJECTED` on a filing whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`. Reporting an outcome on a filing that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `RECORDED` outcome — which requires that `missing_documents` be empty — moves the filing from `SUBMITTED` to `RECORDED`, stores the supplied `agency_reference`, stamps `recorded_at` with the logical clock, and advances the loan's lien dimension from `FILING_SUBMITTED` to `PERFECTED`. When the recorded instrument is a `TITLE_LIEN`, it additionally moves the unit's `title_status` from `ACTIVE_TITLE` to `TITLE_ENCUMBERED`, because the state has now noted the lender on the certificate of title; that move is conditional on the unit actually standing at `ACTIVE_TITLE`, so a unit already `TITLE_ENCUMBERED` or one that has surrendered its title is left exactly where it is and neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moves. A `REJECTED` outcome moves the filing from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_code` — one of `MISSING_DOCUMENT`, `NAME_MISMATCH` or `FEE_SHORTFALL` — and returns the loan's lien dimension from `FILING_SUBMITTED` to `UNPERFECTED`. Neither `agency_reference` nor `rejection_code` is mandatory: each is optional, each is recorded only on its own arm — a `rejection_code` sent with a `RECORDED` outcome and an `agency_reference` sent with a `REJECTED` one are each validated for shape and then discarded — and one that is omitted simply leaves its field null. Each arm writes the loan's lien dimension outright from whatever it held, which is visible only on a loan carrying more than one filing, where an outcome on the second instrument overwrites the standing the first established. Either outcome increments the loan's `version`.

Rejection is not the end, and this is where the filing lifecycle becomes cyclic. A `ClosingAgent` re-tenders a `REJECTED` filing with a corrected `submitted_documents` list, which moves the filing back from `REJECTED` to `SUBMITTED`, increases `attempt_number` by exactly one, clears `rejection_code` back to null, recomputes `missing_documents` from the new list, and returns the loan's lien dimension from `UNPERFECTED` to `FILING_SUBMITTED`, incrementing the loan's `version`. The corrected list replaces the stored `submitted_documents` wholesale rather than merging into it, and the filing keeps the `filing_type` it was opened with — a re-tender never re-checks that type against the loan's `collateral_classification`, so a rejected title lien on a loan that has since converted to real property is re-tendered as a title lien without complaint. Patience is finite: three attempts is the ceiling, so a re-tender is only permitted while `attempt_number` is still below 3 at the moment of the call, and an attempt beyond that is refused with `ERR_FILING_ATTEMPT_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Re-tendering a filing that is not in `REJECTED` standing is refused with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`. Both the agency response and the re-tender answer with the filing row, not with the loan whose lien dimension they moved.

A recorded instrument is eventually released, and release always arrives as a cascade rather than as a direct request — a completed classification conversion, a payoff, or a recovery close-out. Releasing moves the filing from `RECORDED` to `RELEASED` and stamps `released_at`. When the release is driven by the loan being satisfied, the loan's lien dimension also moves to `RELEASED` — written outright from whatever it held rather than only from `PERFECTED` — and every filing on that loan standing `RECORDED` is released together with it. That arm is gated on nothing of its own: it runs as part of the payoff of §3.7 or the settlement of §3.9, both of which have already zeroed the loan's obligation buckets by the time it fires, so the buckets are zero as a consequence rather than as a precondition. One release is reversible: a released title lien whose release was caused by a conversion completion — and not by a payoff — is restored from `RELEASED` back to `RECORDED` when that conversion is rescinded, with `released_at` cleared to null and the loan's lien dimension restored to `PERFECTED`. That compensation arm exists so that the number of recorded instruments is conserved across a full conversion round trip.

Filings can be listed and filtered by `loan_id`, by `unit_id`, by `filing_type` and by `status`, and read individually with their attempt count and agency outcome.

### 3.6. Hazard Coverage: The Requirement Matrix, Force-Placement and Refunds

A loan starts life with `insurance_compliance` at `UNKNOWN` and can only be funded once coverage has been proved. Either a `Servicer` or a `ClosingAgent` records a borrower-provided policy against the loan, naming the loan as `loan_id`, the issuing `carrier_name`, the carrier's `policy_number`, the stated `dwelling_coverage_cents` (zero or more), the named-storm `wind_deductible_cents` (zero or more), a `wind_endorsement` boolean saying whether a wind or named-storm endorsement is attached, and the coverage interval as `effective_date` and `expiry_date`. That interval is half-open: `effective_date` is the first instant of coverage and is included; `expiry_date` is the first instant after coverage and is excluded. The `expiry_date` must be strictly after the `effective_date`, and a non-positive interval is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) — a rule that governs force-placed coverage just as it governs borrower-provided coverage. Policy creation also requires `expected_loan_version` under §2.9. The platform computes `coverage_days` as the whole calendar days from `effective_date` up to `expiry_date`, which counts whole days between the two calendar dates: an interval that opens and closes inside a single calendar day is a positive interval by the instant comparison above and yet has a `coverage_days` of 0. No standing on the loan gates this write — a policy may be recorded against a loan in any underwriting or servicing state, which is exactly what lets coverage be proved on a loan still sitting in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED`.

Two policies from the borrower may not cover the same stretch of time. A new policy's half-open interval must not intersect the half-open interval of any policy on the same loan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `source` is `BORROWER_PROVIDED`, and an intersection is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Touching endpoints do not intersect: if one interval's `expiry_date` equals the other's `effective_date`, the two are back-to-back and both are allowed. A lender-placed policy is deliberately out of scope for this overlap test, because a borrower buying real coverage that overlaps a force-placed policy is precisely the intended way to displace it.

How much coverage is enough is settled by a requirement matrix that reads the collateral, not the policy. The insurable value is the `certified_value_cents` of the valuation the loan cites as its `origination_valuation_id`, read as that row now stands whatever its `status` — a valuation since retired to `SUPERSEDED` still supplies the figure, and the matrix never re-verifies the variant or the certification it was accepted under at application time. Where the loan cites a valuation that resolves to nothing at all, the insurable value is 0, which collapses the requirement onto exposure alone. The exposure is the loan's `principal_balance_cents` once its `underwriting_state` is `FUNDED`, and its `principal_cents` at every earlier standing — so the requirement is measured against the amount to be advanced while the loan is still being closed, and against the amortising balance thereafter. The `required_coverage_cents` figure is then the greater of that exposure and the insurable value multiplied by the required-coverage ratio for the loan's `collateral_classification` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — where the ratio is 8000 basis points, four fifths of insurable value, for `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` and 10000 basis points, the whole insurable value, for `REAL_PROPERTY`. Every policy stores the figure that governed it as its own `required_coverage_cents` at creation, and that stored snapshot is rewritten only by the re-score of §3.8.

Wind is graded separately and it is graded first. Wind coverage is required when the unit's `wind_zone` is 3, and also when the `wind_zone` is 2 and the `installation_type` is `PIER_AND_ANCHOR` — an anchored unit in a moderate zone being treated as exposed as a foundation-set unit in the worst zone. A policy's `compliance_state` is therefore `DEFICIENT_WIND` when wind coverage is required and the policy carries no `wind_endorsement`; otherwise it is `DEFICIENT_COVERAGE` when the policy's `dwelling_coverage_cents` is below `required_coverage_cents`; otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. The ordering matters: a policy that fails both tests reports `DEFICIENT_WIND`, never `DEFICIENT_COVERAGE`.

A borrower-provided policy is recorded with `source` `BORROWER_PROVIDED`, `status` `ACTIVE`, `premium_cents` 0 and `unearned_premium_refund_cents` 0 — the borrower pays their own carrier, so the lender advances nothing. Creating it moves every earlier `ACTIVE` borrower-provided policy on the same loan to `SUPERSEDED`, refreshing each retired row's `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` while leaving its grading untouched, and then recomputes the loan's `insurance_compliance` from whichever policies remain `ACTIVE`: `COMPLIANT` when at least one of them is itself graded `COMPLIANT` — a force-placed policy that survives the write counts for this — and `NON_COMPLIANT` otherwise. A loan with no policy row at all is left at whatever `insurance_compliance` it already held. The new row is graded once, at creation, against the matrix as it stands at that moment, and no other policy's grading is recomputed by this write; consistently with §3.8, a re-score that would leave both `required_coverage_cents` and `compliance_state` where they already stand does not touch the policy at all. The loan's `version` increments whether or not the recomputation actually moved `insurance_compliance`.

Funding checks coverage three times, in a fixed order. First, at least one policy on the loan must be `ACTIVE` with a coverage interval that contains the logical clock — those policies, and only those, are the candidates the next two counts read — or funding is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_NON_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 409). Second, if the unit requires wind coverage some candidate must carry a `wind_endorsement`, or funding is refused with `ERR_WIND_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Third, some candidate's `dwelling_coverage_cents` must be at least `required_coverage_cents`, or funding is refused with `ERR_DWELLING_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Where several candidates cover the clock the two sufficiency counts are judged independently of one another, so the endorsement may come from one candidate and the sufficient dwelling limit from a different one. Neither count consults the loan's stored `insurance_compliance` or any policy's stored `required_coverage_cents`: the requirement is recomputed from the matrix at this moment, with exposure still the loan's `principal_cents` because funding has not yet advanced the underwriting dimension. A policy's own `compliance_state` is likewise not read here — the three counts stand on their own.

Coverage lapses, and when it does the lender buys its own. A `Servicer` force-places coverage on a loan whose `insurance_compliance` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, naming a `carrier_name`, a `policy_number` and the `effective_date` and `expiry_date` of the placed coverage. There is nothing to force-place against a loan that is already compliant, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_ALREADY_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 409). The created policy carries `source` `FORCE_PLACED`, `status` `ACTIVE`, `wind_endorsement` true, `wind_deductible_cents` 0, `unearned_premium_refund_cents` 0, and `dwelling_coverage_cents` set exactly to `required_coverage_cents` — the lender buys precisely what the matrix demands and no more, which is also why the row is graded `COMPLIANT` on the very matrix that priced it, whatever the unit's wind exposure.

Pricing the placed coverage takes two pinned roundings, in order. The annual premium is `required_coverage_cents` multiplied by the sum of a base rate of 185 basis points and a wind surcharge read off the unit's `wind_zone` — zero basis points in zone 1, 150 in zone 2 and 400 in zone 3 — divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. Then `premium_cents` is that already-rounded annual figure multiplied by `coverage_days` and divided by 365, rounded half up again. The resulting `premium_cents` is added to the loan's `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents`, which is the unreimbursed advance the borrower now owes on top of principal, interest and fees; a placement whose interval spans no whole calendar day therefore prices at 0 and advances nothing. Force-placement sets the loan's `insurance_compliance` to `COMPLIANT` outright rather than deriving it from the surviving policy set, supersedes nothing and re-scores nothing, and increments the loan's `version`. Like policy creation, this write answers with the newly created policy row rather than with the loan whose bucket and compliance it moved.

When the borrower finally buys real coverage, the lender gives back what it has not earned. If a newly created borrower-provided policy scores as `COMPLIANT` while a force-placed policy is still `ACTIVE`, every such force-placed policy moves from `ACTIVE` to `CANCELLED` — whether or not its own interval still covers the clock — and each one's `unearned_premium_refund_cents` is computed pro-rata by remaining coverage: the force-placed policy's own `premium_cents` multiplied by the whole calendar days from the logical clock up to that policy's `expiry_date`, divided by its `coverage_days`, rounded half up; a policy whose `coverage_days` is not positive refunds 0 outright rather than dividing. The remaining-days count is not floored, so on a policy whose `expiry_date` the clock has already passed it is negative and the pro-rata figure is negative with it, stored and applied exactly as computed. Each refund then reduces the loan's `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents`, that bucket alone being floored at zero so it never goes negative. A policy that is superseded or lapses rather than being cancelled keeps `unearned_premium_refund_cents` at 0.

The four policy states are therefore `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED`, the last three all terminal. Lapse is driven by the clock rather than by a request: a periodic servicing assessment that observes the logical clock at or after an `ACTIVE` policy's `expiry_date` moves that policy to `LAPSED` and sets the loan's `insurance_compliance` to `NON_COMPLIANT`, which in turn is what makes force-placement available. Policies can be listed and filtered by `loan_id`, by `status` and by `source`, and read individually with their compliance grading and premium.

### 3.7. Servicing: Accrual, the Classification-Dependent Waterfall, Reversal and Delinquency

Once a loan is funded, five obligation buckets sit on it and every servicing act moves one or more of them: `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents` holds interest that has accrued but has not yet been satisfied, `fees_outstanding_cents` holds unpaid late fees, `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents` holds the unreimbursed lender-advanced premium, `escrow_balance_cents` holds cash held for taxes and insurance against the `escrow_target_cents` the servicer is collecting toward, and `principal_balance_cents` holds unpaid principal. Principal is set once at funding and thereafter moves only through payment allocation, the reversal of a payment, the zeroing a payoff performs, and the settlement zeroing of §3.9. Servicing writes are only available while the loan's `servicing_state` is `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT` — posting a payment, periodic assessment, lot-rent reporting and force-placement all require one of those two, and default declaration requires `DELINQUENT` specifically. Any of them attempted from the wrong servicing standing is refused with `ERR_LOAN_SERVICING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Reversing a payment is deliberately outside that gate and carries no servicing-standing check at all, so a posting may still be reversed on a loan that has since reached `PAID_OFF` or `CHARGED_OFF`.

Interest accrues on demand rather than on a schedule, and it accrues exactly once per servicing call. The `accrual_days` figure is the whole calendar days from the loan's `last_accrual_date` up to the logical clock, over a half-open interval — and, as with every day count in this system, both endpoints are first truncated to their UTC calendar date and the two dates alone are subtracted, so the time of day on either side never contributes a partial day and a watermark and a clock falling inside the same calendar date count zero days. A span that would come out negative counts 0 days rather than a negative number, an endpoint that is null counts 0 days, and a loan whose `apr_bps` is null accrues nothing. The accrued amount is the loan's `principal_balance_cents` multiplied by its `apr_bps` divided by ten thousand, multiplied by `accrual_days` and divided by 365 — an actual-over-365 day count — with the whole product evaluated exactly and rounded half up to a whole cent one time. That amount is added to `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents`, and `last_accrual_date` is advanced to the logical clock. A call that finds zero days elapsed accrues zero cents and is not an error. Accrual runs only on a loan whose `underwriting_state` is `FUNDED` and whose servicing standing has not reached `PAID_OFF` or `CHARGED_OFF`.

A `Servicer` posts cash by supplying an `amount_cents` and a `payment_kind` of `SCHEDULED`, `PARTIAL`, `PAYOFF` or `REVERSAL`. The platform stamps `posted_at` with the logical clock, records on the row the `accrual_days` it counted and the `accrued_interest_at_posting_cents` it added to the loan, and then applies the cash through a waterfall. The `amount_cents` figure is required to be an integer but is bounded by no range at the shape rung, so zero and negative figures are accepted there and run through the same waterfall. Nothing is committed until every cash-integrity check below has passed: a posting refused for overpayment or for a payoff mismatch writes no payment row and leaves the loan exactly as it stood, with `last_accrual_date` still on its old watermark and not a cent of the interest it had just computed added.

Which waterfall is the single most consequential effect of collateral classification on the servicing side, and the row records the choice in `allocation_variant`. When the loan's `collateral_classification` is `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` the order is `CHATTEL_CONTRACT_ORDER`: accrued interest, then outstanding fees, then the force-placed premium, then the escrow shortfall, then principal. When the classification is `REAL_PROPERTY` the order is `REAL_PROPERTY_ESCROW_FIRST`: the escrow shortfall, then the force-placed premium, then accrued interest, then outstanding fees, then principal. The practical consequence is worth stating plainly, because the same payment against the same balance sheet produces two different outcomes: under the chattel order a partial payment on a delinquent loan clears interest and fees before it protects the tax-and-insurance escrow, while under the real-property order the escrow shortfall is funded first and accrued interest is left untouched.

Filling is strictly sequential and involves no rounding whatsoever. The escrow bucket for this purpose is the shortfall — the `escrow_target_cents` less the current `escrow_balance_cents`, floored at zero, so a fully funded escrow absorbs nothing — and the interest bucket is the loan's stored `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents` plus the interest this very call just accrued, never the pre-accrual figure. Each bucket in turn absorbs the smaller of the cash still unallocated and its own outstanding amount, and the cash remaining is reduced by what was absorbed. The five amounts absorbed are recorded on the row as `to_interest_cents`, `to_fees_cents`, `to_force_placed_cents`, `to_escrow_cents` and `to_principal_cents`, and the loan's corresponding buckets are decremented by exactly those amounts while `escrow_balance_cents` is credited by the escrow allocation.

Cash that the buckets cannot absorb is refused rather than held. If a strictly positive amount remains unallocated after the whole waterfall has run and the `payment_kind` is `SCHEDULED` or `PARTIAL`, the posting is rejected with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). Because the test is on a strictly positive residue it never fires on a non-positive `amount_cents`: with every bucket standing at zero or above, the smaller of the cash still unallocated and the first bucket's own amount is the negative figure itself, so the first bucket of the order absorbs it whole — *increasing* that bucket rather than reducing it — and nothing is left for the buckets behind it. A `payment_kind` of `REVERSAL` may also be posted directly through this endpoint, and it too is outside the overpayment refusal, so cash the buckets cannot absorb is simply discarded and recorded nowhere; such a row carries a null `reverses_payment_id` and flags no other row as reversed.

A payoff is the exception, and it must be exact to the cent. For a `payment_kind` of `PAYOFF`, the `amount_cents` must equal the sum of `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents` measured after accrual has run, plus `fees_outstanding_cents`, plus `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents`, plus `principal_balance_cents`, less `escrow_balance_cents` — the escrow being the borrower's own money and therefore a credit against what is owed. Any other figure, high or low, is refused with `ERR_PAYOFF_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). That expected figure is not floored: where `escrow_balance_cents` exceeds the four obligation buckets taken together the exact payoff is a negative number of cents, and only a posting carrying that same negative `amount_cents` is accepted. A payoff that matches zeroes every obligation bucket, refunds whatever sat in `escrow_balance_cents` by zeroing that bucket too, resets `delinquent_days` to 0, moves the loan's servicing standing from `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT` to `PAID_OFF`, writes the lien dimension to `RELEASED` outright from whatever it held, and moves every filing on the loan standing `RECORDED` — not merely one — to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, each such release being caused by settlement and therefore never restorable under §3.8. The payoff row records what it satisfied: `to_interest_cents`, `to_fees_cents`, `to_force_placed_cents` and `to_principal_cents` are the full bucket amounts it cleared, while `to_escrow_cents` is the negative of the escrow balance refunded. `next_due_date` is left exactly where it stood.

A delinquent loan that is fully cured comes back on its own. When allocation leaves both `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents` and `fees_outstanding_cents` at zero — whatever the `payment_kind` was, and whatever the other three buckets still hold — the loan returns from `DELINQUENT` to `CURRENT`, `next_due_date` advances by one month, and `delinquent_days` resets to 0. Advancing by one month keeps the day of month where the next month admits it and clamps it down to that month's last day where it does not, carrying the time of day across unchanged, so a due date of 31 January advances to the last day of that February and one of 31 March to 30 April; a `next_due_date` that is null is left null. A payment posted on a loan already standing `CURRENT` moves neither `next_due_date` nor `delinquent_days`. Every payment posting increments the loan's `version`, and every posting and every reversal also re-derives the borrower's two exposure fields of §3.1 from that borrower's current loans and holds, which may move the borrower's own `status`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Mistakes are corrected by compensation, never by editing history. A `Servicer` reverses a posted payment with an optional `reversal_reason`, and the platform writes a new row whose `payment_kind` is `REVERSAL`, whose `reverses_payment_id` points at the original, whose `amount_cents` is the arithmetic negation of the original's, and whose five allocation fields are the exact negatives of the original row's five. The rest of the compensating row is settled the same way every time: `posted_at` is the logical clock, `allocation_variant` is copied from the original row rather than re-derived from the loan, `accrual_days` and `accrued_interest_at_posting_cents` are both 0, and the new row's own `reversed` flag is false. The `reversal_reason` is stored on it verbatim as whatever string the caller sent, the empty or blank string included, and is null when the field is omitted or sent as JSON `null`; that field exists only on the rows this endpoint writes — a row posted through the payment endpoint carries no `reversal_reason` key at all rather than a null one, even where its own `payment_kind` is `REVERSAL`. Applying those negatives restores every loan obligation bucket to its pre-payment amount, and it does so *without* re-accruing interest — the reversal undoes an allocation, it does not rewind the clock. Nothing else the original posting did is rewound either: the loan's `servicing_state`, `next_due_date`, `delinquent_days` and `last_accrual_date` all stay exactly where that posting left them, and a payoff reversed this way restores the five buckets while leaving the loan `PAID_OFF` with its lien dimension and its filings still `RELEASED`. The original row's `reversed` flag is set to true, and the loan's `version` increments. A row that has already been reversed, or a row that is itself a `REVERSAL`, cannot be reversed again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409). Reversed rows are excluded from any later allocation arithmetic. Payment rows can be listed and filtered by `loan_id`, by `reverses_payment_id` and by `payment_kind`, always in ascending creation order, and read individually with their five allocation buckets.

Time passing is itself an event, and a `Servicer` makes the platform notice it by running a periodic servicing assessment, which takes no request body. The assessment runs four steps in this order. It accrues interest up to the logical clock. It refreshes `delinquent_days` as the whole calendar days from `next_due_date` up to the logical clock floored at zero — again over endpoints truncated to their calendar date, so a due date falling inside the same calendar day as the clock counts zero days. It lapses **every** `ACTIVE` policy on the loan whose `expiry_date` the clock has reached or passed, not merely the first of them. And finally, when the freshly refreshed `delinquent_days` has reached 30 or more and the loan still stands `CURRENT`, it moves the loan to `DELINQUENT` and adds a flat late fee of 3,500 cents to `fees_outstanding_cents`. The lapse step is blunt on purpose: each policy it lapses is touched, and if it lapsed even one the loan's `insurance_compliance` is written to `NON_COMPLIANT` outright rather than recomputed from the policies that remain, so a loan still carrying an `ACTIVE` `COMPLIANT` force-placed policy alongside the expired one nonetheless comes out `NON_COMPLIANT` — which is what makes a further force-placement available. That late fee is charged once per crossing of the threshold, not once per assessment: repeated assessments on an already-delinquent loan accrue interest but do not stack further flat fees. The assessment increments the loan's `version` on every call, whether or not any of the four steps moved anything, it recomputes no borrower exposure, and it answers with the loan row.

Lot rent is the one signal that comes from outside the lender, and it can pull default eligibility forward. A `CommunityOperator` reports the borrower's standing on the lot by supplying `months_delinquent` — required to be an integer of zero or more, with no upper bound — and a `lot_rent_state` of `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT` or `EVICTION_FILED`. The report writes `lot_rent_months_delinquent` from the reported months and `lot_rent_state` from the reported standing, so a report that returns the standing to `CURRENT` with zero months delinquent clears the counter back to 0. A loan starts life with `lot_rent_months_delinquent` at 0 and `lot_rent_state` at `CURRENT`. Reporting increments the loan's `version`, re-derives the borrower's exposure fields of §3.1, and answers with the loan row. The operator may only report on loans whose collateral sits in its own community.

### 3.8. Converting Classification, and Compensating the Conversion

The most consequential thing that can happen to a manufactured unit mid-loan is a change of legal character, and the platform models it as a three-step cycle driven by a `ClosingAgent` supplying an `action`. Because the cycle rewrites the collateral character of every loan on the unit, it is available only while the unit is clean: a unit whose `recovery_hold` is set, or that secures any loan whose servicing standing is `RECOVERY` or `CHARGED_OFF`, is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). An `action` that does not match the unit's current classification — initiating from anything but `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, completing from anything but `CONVERSION_PENDING`, rescinding from anything but `REAL_PROPERTY` — is refused with `ERR_UNIT_CLASSIFICATION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Every conversion call increments the unit's `version`.

The `INITIATE` action moves the unit's `classification` from `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` to `CONVERSION_PENDING` and does nothing else — it is a declaration of intent that changes no other resource. It is nonetheless gated on real paperwork: the unit's `placement_type` must be `PRIVATE_LAND`, and both `deed_recorded` and `affixation_affidavit_recorded` must already be true. A unit on a community lot, or one missing either land document, is refused with `ERR_LAND_DOCUMENTS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). A home on a rented lot can never become real property, because the borrower does not own the ground.

The `COMPLETE` action moves the unit from `CONVERSION_PENDING` to `REAL_PROPERTY` and fans out into the widest cascade in the system. It requires that no filing on this unit still be in `SUBMITTED` standing, because an instrument in flight at the recording office cannot be reasoned about; one that is still submitted refuses the completion with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and that check runs after both the classification gate and the recovery-hold gate above have passed. Completion then does four things. The unit's `title_status` moves to `TITLE_SURRENDERED`, since the certificate of title is handed back to the state. Every filing on the unit that is a `RECORDED` `TITLE_LIEN` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, because a title lien has nothing left to attach to. Every non-terminal loan on the unit has its `collateral_classification` rewritten to `REAL_PROPERTY`, and any such loan whose lien dimension stood at `PERFECTED` is moved to `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` — the loan is now unsecured in substance until a deed of trust is recorded. And every `ACTIVE` policy on those loans is re-scored against the real-property requirement of 10000 basis points of insurable value, which is a materially higher bar than the 8000 basis points that applied a moment earlier, so a policy that was `COMPLIANT` can land on `DEFICIENT_COVERAGE` and flip the loan's `insurance_compliance` to `NON_COMPLIANT`. Each of those loans has its own `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented unconditionally, whether or not any of its own fields actually moved; a policy, by contrast, is touched only when the re-score genuinely changes a stored value, so a policy whose `required_coverage_cents` and `compliance_state` both come out where they already stood keeps its existing `updated_at` and `version`. A loan carrying no policy at all has nothing to recompute from and keeps whatever `insurance_compliance` it already held — `UNKNOWN` on a loan that never proved coverage — while a loan whose policies exist but none of which is `ACTIVE` recomputes to `NON_COMPLIANT`.

Recovering from that state is a matter of filing the right instrument. A loan sitting at `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` is re-perfected by tendering a fresh `LAND_HOME_DEED_OF_TRUST` filing — a second filing on the same loan, coexisting with the released title lien — which moves the lien dimension from `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` to `FILING_SUBMITTED` and then, on a recorded outcome, to `PERFECTED`. The instrument tendered must match the loan's *new* `collateral_classification`, so a title lien tendered at this point is refused with `ERR_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

The `RESCIND` action is the compensation arm, and it exists because conversions are sometimes unwound — a county rejects the affixation, or a borrower reverses course. It moves the unit from `REAL_PROPERTY` back to `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, and it is permitted only while no loan on the unit has a servicing standing of `RECOVERY` or `CHARGED_OFF`. Rescission reverses each effect of completion with equal care: `title_status` returns to `ACTIVE_TITLE`; every title lien that completion released is restored from `RELEASED` to `RECORDED` with `released_at` cleared to null; every affected loan has `collateral_classification` rewritten back to `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` and its lien dimension moved from `RE_PERFECTION_REQUIRED` back to `PERFECTED`; and every `ACTIVE` policy is re-scored against the personal-property requirement of 8000 basis points, with the loan's `insurance_compliance` recomputed from the result. The invariant this arm protects is that the number of recorded instruments on the unit is conserved across a full `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` → `CONVERSION_PENDING` → `REAL_PROPERTY` → `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` round trip. One asymmetry is deliberate: only a release *caused by a conversion completion* is restored this way. A filing released because the loan was paid off, or because a recovery case closed, stays `RELEASED` forever. The platform therefore remembers why each release happened, but that cause is internal bookkeeping and is not a field on the serialized filing. Restoration is also narrow in two further ways: only a `TITLE_LIEN` is ever restored, and only from `RELEASED` back to `RECORDED` — a deed of trust, or a title lien sitting in any other standing, is untouched by the rescission.

### 3.9. Default, Enforcement, Refurbishment and Remarketing

Default eligibility is where classification reaches into collections. A `RecoveryManager` declares default on a loan whose servicing standing is `DELINQUENT`, and the declaration takes no request body. It neither accrues interest nor refreshes the counter before judging it: the gate reads `delinquent_days` exactly as the last periodic assessment left it, so a clock that has moved on since that assessment does not on its own make a loan eligible. The gate is `delinquent_days` against a classification-specific threshold: 60 days for a loan classified `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, and 90 days for one classified `REAL_PROPERTY` — real property enjoys the longer runway that mortgage practice affords it. That threshold is then pulled forward in one circumstance: when the loan's `placement_type` is `COMMUNITY` and the operator has reported `lot_rent_months_delinquent` of 2 or more, the threshold is reduced by 30 days, but never below the 30-day delinquency threshold itself. A borrower two months behind on lot rent is about to be evicted, and the lender's collateral is about to be stranded on someone else's land. If `delinquent_days` has not reached the threshold that survives that adjustment, the declaration is refused with `ERR_DEFAULT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409).

A successful declaration is a five-way cascade. The loan's servicing standing moves from `DELINQUENT` to `DEFAULT`. A recovery case is created — the client never supplies the case's `loan_id`, which is written by the cascade itself — opening at `status` `OPENED` with `path` derived from the loan's `collateral_classification`: `REPOSSESSION` for `PERSONAL_PROPERTY` and `FORECLOSURE` for `REAL_PROPERTY`. The case's `recovery_expense_cents` is seeded with the path's expense constant, 185,000 cents for a repossession and 425,000 cents for a foreclosure — the foreclosure arm costing well over twice as much because it runs through a court. Every other field on the new case starts empty: `recovery_valuation_id`, `resale_loan_id`, `notice_reference`, `cure_deadline_date`, `redemption_window_ends_at`, `possession_date`, `asking_price_cents`, `resale_price_cents`, `gross_proceeds_cents`, `commission_bps`, `commission_cents`, `net_proceeds_cents`, `payoff_amount_cents`, `deficiency_balance_cents` and `surplus_cents` are all null, `refurbishment_line_items` is an empty array, and `refurbishment_cost_cents` is 0. The platform never checks whether the loan already carries a recovery case, so a loan that redeemed its way back to `CURRENT` and then fell delinquent again opens a second, wholly independent case on its next declaration. The collateral unit has `recovery_hold` set to true and `title_status` set to `TITLE_ENCUMBERED`. And the borrower's `units_under_recovery_hold_count` rises by one, which by the rule in §3.1 sets that borrower's `status` to `SUSPENDED` and shuts off any new origination against them. The loan's `version` increments.

The case's own lifecycle then forks by `path`, rejoins, and finally closes on one of four terminal outcomes. Its `status` runs across `OPENED`, `REPO_NOTICE_ISSUED`, `REDEMPTION_WINDOW`, `POSSESSION_OBTAINED`, `REFURBISHING`, `REMARKETING`, `CLOSED_REDEEMED`, `CLOSED_DEFICIENCY`, `CLOSED_SURPLUS` and `CLOSED_SETTLED`, the last four terminal. Every recovery action requires the case to be in the standing that action expects — notice issuance requires `OPENED`, redemption requires `REDEMPTION_WINDOW`, possession requires `REPO_NOTICE_ISSUED` or `REDEMPTION_WINDOW`, refurbishment start requires `POSSESSION_OBTAINED`, listing and cost recording both require `REFURBISHING`, and sale finalization requires `REMARKETING` — and any mismatch is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_CASE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Issuing the enforcement notice is one call that behaves as two, and the arm is chosen by `path`. The `RecoveryManager` supplies a `notice_reference`, which is the right-to-cure notice reference on the repossession arm and the foreclosure docket reference on the foreclosure arm. On a `REPOSSESSION` case, the status moves from `OPENED` to `REPO_NOTICE_ISSUED` and `cure_deadline_date` is set to the logical clock plus 10 days, the chattel right-to-cure period; `redemption_window_ends_at` remains null on this arm. On a `FORECLOSURE` case, the status moves from `OPENED` to `REDEMPTION_WINDOW` and `redemption_window_ends_at` is set to the logical clock plus 45 days, the statutory redemption period — a month and a half rather than a week and a half; `cure_deadline_date` remains null on this arm. Both stamps are plain instant arithmetic on the clock rather than calendar-date arithmetic: the deadline is exactly ten, or forty-five, times twenty-four hours after the clock instant and carries the clock's own time of day, and every later comparison against it compares whole instants rather than counting calendar days. Both arms move the loan's servicing standing from `DEFAULT` to `RECOVERY` — but only where it actually stands at `DEFAULT`; a loan found in any other standing is left untouched and its `version` does not move, while the case advances either way. Like every recovery action in this section, the call answers with the recovery-case row rather than with the loan it moved.

Redemption is available on the foreclosure arm only. Because a repossession-path case carries a cure period rather than a redemption window and can never reach `REDEMPTION_WINDOW` at all, an attempt to redeem such a case is refused with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_PATH_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) — and, as §2.7 notes, that answer comes before any complaint about the case's status. On a foreclosure case in `REDEMPTION_WINDOW`, redemption is only open while the logical clock is strictly before `redemption_window_ends_at`; at or after that instant the window has shut and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). A timely redemption moves the case from `REDEMPTION_WINDOW` to `CLOSED_REDEEMED`, returns the loan's servicing standing from `RECOVERY` to `CURRENT`, clears the unit's `recovery_hold` to false, and reduces the borrower's `units_under_recovery_hold_count` by one — restoring that borrower's `status` to `ACTIVE` once no other unit of theirs remains held. The standing move is conditional on the loan actually being at `RECOVERY`, a loan in any other standing keeping whatever it holds, while the loan is touched and its exposure fields re-derived either way; the unit, by contrast, is touched only where a hold was genuinely cleared. Redemption deliberately undoes nothing else: the unit's `title_status` stays at `TITLE_ENCUMBERED` where the declaration put it, the loan's five obligation buckets, `delinquent_days`, `next_due_date` and `last_accrual_date` are all left exactly as they stood, no interest is accrued, and the recorded filing keeps its `RECORDED` standing with the lien dimension still `PERFECTED`.

Taking possession is the join point where the two arms converge, and it weighs three conditions in the ladder order of §2.7 — lender access first, then perfection, and only last the timing rule appropriate to the arm it is called on — which is deliberately not the order they read most naturally in. When the unit sits on a community lot, that community's `lender_access_rights` must be true, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_LENDER_ACCESS_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 409): without an executed operator agreement granting on-site access, the lender cannot lawfully drive onto the operator's land. That test reads the unit's own current `community_id` rather than the loan's snapshotted `placement_type`, and a community whose agreement has since been terminated fails it. Both arms then require that the lien actually be recorded — at least one filing on **the loan** standing `RECORDED`, together with the loan's lien dimension at `PERFECTED` — failing which the attempt is refused with `ERR_PERFECTION_NOT_ESTABLISHED` (HTTP 409); a lender with no perfected security interest has no right to seize anything. Only then does timing bite: on the repossession arm the logical clock must have reached or passed `cure_deadline_date`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409); on the foreclosure arm the logical clock must have reached or passed `redemption_window_ends_at`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_WINDOW_OPEN` (HTTP 409). A successful possession moves the case to `POSSESSION_OBTAINED` and stamps `possession_date` with the logical clock, and it touches nothing but the case — no loan field, no unit field and no borrower field moves.

Refurbishment begins by attaching a liquidation figure. The `RecoveryManager` names a `recovery_valuation_id`, which must reference a valuation that is `CERTIFIED`, is of variant `RECOVERY_LIQUIDATION`, and was computed on this same unit; anything else is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_VARIANT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Attaching it moves the case from `POSSESSION_OBTAINED` to `REFURBISHING`.

Refurbishment cost is then recorded one line at a time, and the accounting treatment chosen on each line changes the arithmetic of the eventual settlement. Each call supplies a `category` describing the work, a `cost_cents` amount of zero or more, and a `cost_class` of either `CAPITALIZED` or `EXPENSED`. The platform appends an object carrying `category`, `cost_cents`, `cost_class` and `recorded_at` to the case's append-only `refurbishment_line_items` list. A `CAPITALIZED` line adds its `cost_cents` to the case's `refurbishment_cost_cents`, which will later be deducted from sale proceeds. An `EXPENSED` line instead adds its `cost_cents` immediately to the case's `recovery_expense_cents`, which raises the amount the collateral has to cover rather than reducing what it fetched. The two treatments therefore push the settlement in the same direction but through different doors, and mixing them up produces a wrong deficiency. A case with no capitalized line at all reports `refurbishment_cost_cents` as 0.

Listing the refurbished unit for sale requires an `asking_price_cents` greater than zero and moves the case from `REFURBISHING` to `REMARKETING`, recording the list price.

Finalizing the sale is the settlement act. The `RecoveryManager` supplies the contract price as `resale_price_cents` — an integer of zero or more, bounded above by nothing — and, when the buyer finances the purchase with this same lender, the new loan written on the recovered unit as `resale_loan_id`. That optional identifier is resolved for existence ahead of the rest of the body, so a request naming a loan that does not exist answers `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND` even when `resale_price_cents` is itself missing or malformed, and it is then stored exactly as given: the platform never checks that the loan it names sits on this unit, belongs to any particular borrower, or stands in any particular state. The case's `gross_proceeds_cents` equals `resale_price_cents` exactly.

Commission is tiered off gross proceeds — never off net proceeds, never off the asking price, and never off the liquidation valuation. The tier is 700 basis points when `gross_proceeds_cents` is 3,000,000 cents or less, 550 basis points when it is above that but 6,000,000 cents or less, and 400 basis points above 6,000,000 cents. To that tier is added the community's `sales_commission_bps` when, and only when, the unit's `placement_type` is `COMMUNITY` and that community's `operator_agreement_status` is `EXECUTED` — the operator earns its referral fee only where it has a live agreement. The sum is stored as `commission_bps`, and `commission_cents` is `gross_proceeds_cents` multiplied by `commission_bps` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up in a single operation from the combined rate rather than tier and surcharge separately.

Net proceeds follow: `net_proceeds_cents` is `gross_proceeds_cents` less `commission_cents` less `refurbishment_cost_cents`, that last figure being the sum of the `cost_cents` of every `CAPITALIZED` line item and nothing else — expensed lines are deliberately excluded here because they were already charged to `recovery_expense_cents` when they were recorded. Settlement re-derives that sum from the stored line items and writes it back onto the case's `refurbishment_cost_cents`, which for a case whose capitalized lines all arrived through the cost endpoint is the figure already standing there.

What the collateral had to cover is `payoff_amount_cents`, computed as the loan's `principal_balance_cents` plus `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents` plus `fees_outstanding_cents` plus `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents` plus the case's `recovery_expense_cents`, less the loan's `escrow_balance_cents`. Those four loan buckets are read exactly as they stand — finalizing a sale accrues no interest and moves no accrual watermark, so interest sits wherever the last servicing call left it — and the `recovery_expense_cents` term already carries the path's seed plus every `EXPENSED` refurbishment line. The figure itself is not floored: an `escrow_balance_cents` larger than everything else makes `payoff_amount_cents` negative, and a negative payoff simply enlarges the surplus by that much. Then `deficiency_balance_cents` is `payoff_amount_cents` less `net_proceeds_cents`, floored at zero, and `surplus_cents` is `net_proceeds_cents` less `payoff_amount_cents`, also floored at zero. At most one of the two is non-zero, and on an exact settlement both are zero.

Which of the three closing arms runs is decided by those two figures, and all three carry the same clean-up. When `deficiency_balance_cents` is greater than zero, the case closes at `CLOSED_DEFICIENCY` and the loan's servicing standing moves from `RECOVERY` to `CHARGED_OFF` — the shortfall is charged off against the borrower. When both `deficiency_balance_cents` and `surplus_cents` are zero, the case closes at `CLOSED_SETTLED` and the loan moves from `RECOVERY` to `PAID_OFF`. When `surplus_cents` is greater than zero, the case closes at `CLOSED_SURPLUS`, the loan moves from `RECOVERY` to `PAID_OFF`, and the surplus owed back to the borrower is recorded as the case's `surplus_cents` and nothing more — no cash moves, no payment row is written, and no field on the loan or the borrower carries it. In every one of the three arms, all four obligation buckets on the loan — `principal_balance_cents`, `interest_accrued_unpaid_cents`, `fees_outstanding_cents` and `force_placed_premium_outstanding_cents` — are zeroed while `escrow_balance_cents` alone is left exactly where it stood, being the borrower's own money and already credited inside `payoff_amount_cents`; the unit's `recovery_hold` is cleared to false and its `title_status` returns to `ACTIVE_TITLE`, with the unit touched whether or not the hold was still set; every filing on the loan standing `RECORDED` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped and the loan's lien dimension is written to `RELEASED` outright from whatever it held, that release being caused by settlement and therefore never restorable under §3.8; the borrower's `units_under_recovery_hold_count` falls by one with `status` returning to `ACTIVE` at zero; and the case's and the loan's `version` each increment.

Recovery cases can be listed and filtered by `loan_id`, by `recovery_valuation_id`, by `resale_loan_id`, by `path` and by `status`, and read individually with their refurbishment lines and settlement figures.
