# Writers Room Staffing & Script Development API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Scripted television lives or dies on two calendars that never quite agree with each other. One is the staffing calendar: a season opens a hiring window, a showrunner builds a room of writers at guild-recognised levels, each writer arrives with their own availability and exclusivity commitments, and the studio pays a weekly burn that has to stay inside a budget agreed months before anyone was hired. The other is the script calendar: outlines, first drafts, second drafts and production polishes are all due some fixed number of days before cameras roll, and if the count of finished, legally cleared scripts falls short when photography begins, the production stops. This service is the system of record where those two calendars meet.

The platform models the whole chain under a single Minimum Basic Agreement rate card: writer level determination from career episodic credits, minimum weekly compensation and mini-room floors, script fees and episodic guarantees, room configuration across in-person, remote and mini-room formats with duration commitments, script assignment with an outline-and-draft milestone schedule pinned to the episode's production start, legal clearance of names, trademarks and depictions, rewrite and production-polish chains with showrunner or studio approval, and finally credit determination — submissions, provisional shares, and a three-arbiter guild panel whose ballots either concur or deadlock. Every fee, floor, deadline and threshold in the system resolves against one rate card row, so a season's economics are reproducible from its governing agreement rather than from operator habit.

Two design commitments shape everything below. First, **segregation of duties is enforced, not advised**: a showrunner may not approve a script they wrote themselves, and an arbitration panel may not contain a writer who is participating in the credit dispute it is deciding. Second, **derived state is always recomputed, never patched**: seat compensation, room weekly burn, season staffing commitment, script coverage posture, clearance risk and credit shares are all functions of their inputs, re-evaluated whenever an input moves, so a release, a reconfiguration or a reschedule leaves the system arithmetically consistent rather than merely plausible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every endpoint in the service. Where a later business-workflow narrative appears to contradict a rule here, the rule here governs the mechanics of the envelope, the identifiers and the precedence, and the narrative governs the domain outcome.

### 2.1. Identity, Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which the caller acts. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is present but is not one of the five roles the system recognises — `StudioExecutive`, `Showrunner`, `Writer`, `GuildAdministrator`, `LegalCounsel` — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). A recognised role that is not listed for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403); §4 is the authoritative table of which roles may call what.

The five roles divide the work as follows. A `StudioExecutive` is the studio's production executive: they alone create seasons, reschedule a season's production start, order episodes, reconfigure a room's format and duration commitment, and activate a room; they share with others the release of a confirmed seat, the approval or voiding of a script assignment, wrapping a room, locking an episode for production, and read access across seasons, rooms, seats, episodes, assignments, milestones, clearance records and the fee ledger. A `Showrunner` is the season's creative lead: they alone open a room for staffing, offer seats, withdraw a seat offer, create script assignments and issue notes on a delivered script; they share room configuration, seat release, assignment approval and voiding, room wrap, closing the credit submission window, locking an episode, and broad read access. A `Writer` is a guild writer: they alone accept or decline a seat offer, deliver a milestone, submit a credit claim, and cast an arbitration ballot when serving on a panel; they may also declare an availability window and read the resources they participate in. A `GuildAdministrator` maintains the guild side of the record: they alone register a guild agreement, register a writer, award episodic credits, convene a credit arbitration panel and apply an arbitration determination or policy override; they may also declare an availability window on a writer's behalf, close a credit submission window, and read agreements, writers, seasons, rooms, seats, episodes, assignments, milestones, credit records and the fee ledger. A `LegalCounsel` owns script clearance end to end: opening a clearance review, adding clearance items, deciding items and granting waivers are theirs exclusively, alongside read access to agreements, seasons, rooms, episodes, assignments, milestones and clearance records. All five roles may set the logical clock.

Two of the five roles are entity-backed and three are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `Writer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Writer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Writer` compare the resource's writer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Showrunner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Writer` row, and authority is additionally scoped to the season whose `showrunner_writer_id` equals that id. For both of these roles, an `X-User-ID` that is not the id of an existing `Writer` is rejected with `ERR_ACTING_WRITER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `StudioExecutive`, `GuildAdministrator` or `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check applies to those three roles.

Three ownership rules follow from that identity model, and each has its own rejection code. A `Writer` acting on a seat must be the seat's writer, a `Writer` delivering a milestone must be the writer of the milestone's assignment, and a `Writer` submitting a credit claim must supply their own id as the claim's writer; any of these mismatches is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `Showrunner` acting on an episode of a season must be that season's `showrunner_writer_id`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SEASON_SHOWRUNNER` (HTTP 403). A `Showrunner` may not approve a script assignment they authored — that approval must come from a `StudioExecutive` — and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). A writer casting an arbitration ballot who is not named on that arbitration's panel is rejected with `ERR_ARBITER_NOT_ON_PANEL` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Both parameters are echoed back in the `meta` object of the response alongside `total_count` — the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true when records remain beyond the current page. The default ordering for list endpoints is `created_at` ascending, so the oldest record appears first. Two lists override that default because their domain reading order differs: the guild agreement list returns rate cards newest-created first, and the milestone list is ordered by `sequence_index` ascending, which walks a script's chain in `OUTLINE`, `FIRST_DRAFT`, `SECOND_DRAFT`, `PRODUCTION_POLISH` order. All filter parameters on a list endpoint combine conjunctively: a record must match every supplied filter to appear.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply ids for the resources they create. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `created_at`, set once when the row is inserted, `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. When a workflow mutates several rows at once — a seat offer that moves the room's counters, a clearance decision that moves the review's risk score — each mutated row's own `version` increments and its own `updated_at` is refreshed independently.

There is one deliberate exception. Fee ledger entries are append-only accounting records: once written they are never mutated and never deleted, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`. A fee ledger entry has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and no endpoint exists to change one. Every other entity in the system — guild agreements, seasons, writers, availability windows, writers rooms, room seats, episodes, script assignments, milestones, clearance reviews, clearance items, credit submissions and credit arbitrations — carries the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

### 2.5. The Logical Production Clock

Deadlines, staffing windows and lateness are all evaluated against a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so a season's timeline can be driven deterministically. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time. Any of the five roles may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and every subsequent read of the clock returns that instant.

Moving the clock is not itself a domain event: it mutates no entity. A milestone that has slipped past its deadline does not flip to `LATE` at the moment the clock passes the deadline — it flips on the next write that touches the milestone's assignment or the milestone's season, which is where the re-evaluation is performed. Whenever a narrative below says "the logical clock", it means this value and not the request's arrival time.

### 2.6. The Guild Agreement Rate Card

A guild agreement is the immutable rate card that governs a season's money, levels and deadlines. It is identified by its `mba_year`, an integer between 2020 and 2035 inclusive that is unique across all guild agreements. Six of its fields are keyed objects whose key sets are fixed and must be supplied exactly: `min_weekly_cents_by_level` and `guarantee_offset_pct_by_level` are keyed by the five writer levels `STAFF_WRITER`, `STORY_EDITOR`, `EXEC_STORY_EDITOR`, `CO_PRODUCER` and `PRODUCER`; `script_fee_cents_by_length` is keyed by the two episode length formats `HALF_HOUR` and `ONE_HOUR`; `type_share_pct_by_assignment_type` is keyed by the five assignment types `STORY`, `TELEPLAY`, `STORY_AND_TELEPLAY`, `REWRITE` and `PRODUCTION_POLISH`; `facility_fee_tiers` and `format_surcharge_pct` are keyed by `TIER_1`, `TIER_2`, `TIER_3` and by `IN_PERSON`, `REMOTE`, `MINI_ROOM` respectively. Every value in `min_weekly_cents_by_level` and `script_fee_cents_by_length` is an integer of at least 1; every value in `type_share_pct_by_assignment_type` is a number greater than 0 and at most 1; every value in `guarantee_offset_pct_by_level` and `format_surcharge_pct` is a number from 0 through 1 inclusive; every value in `facility_fee_tiers` is an integer of at least 0. A missing key, an extra key or an out-of-range value fails the body-schema check.

Two more objects carry ordering constraints of their own. `level_credit_thresholds` is keyed by the four levels above staff writer — `STORY_EDITOR`, `EXEC_STORY_EDITOR`, `CO_PRODUCER`, `PRODUCER` — with integer values of at least 1 that must be strictly increasing in that key order. `facility_tier_subtotal_bounds` has exactly the keys `TIER_1_MAX` and `TIER_2_MAX`, both integers of at least 1, with `TIER_1_MAX` strictly less than `TIER_2_MAX`.

The remaining fields are scalars, several with defaults that apply when the client omits them. `diversity_subsidy_pct` is greater than 0 and at most 1 and defaults to 0.5; `diversity_subsidy_cap_weekly_cents` is a required integer of at least 1; `mini_room_floor_multiplier` is at least 1 and defaults to 1.2; `short_commitment_weeks_threshold` is an integer of at least 1 and defaults to 20; `short_commitment_uplift_pct` runs from 0 through 1 inclusive and defaults to 0.1; `max_free_revision_rounds` is an integer of at least 0 and defaults to 2; `credit_share_threshold_pct` is an integer from 1 through 100 and defaults to 33; `arbitration_concurrence_pct` is greater than 0 and at most 1 and defaults to 0.667; `arbitration_concurrence_band_pct` is an integer from 1 through 100 and defaults to 10; `turnaround_buffer_days` is an integer of at least 0 and defaults to 2; `clearance_escalation_score` is an integer of at least 0 and defaults to 30; and `clearance_item_weights` is required, keyed by exactly `NAME`, `TRADEMARK` and `DEPICTION`, with integer values of at least 1.

The four milestone lead times are required and mutually ordered. `outline_lead_days`, `first_draft_lead_days`, `second_draft_lead_days` and `polish_lead_days` are each integers of at least 1, and they must strictly decrease in that order: `first_draft_lead_days` is less than `outline_lead_days`, `second_draft_lead_days` is less than `first_draft_lead_days`, and `polish_lead_days` is less than `second_draft_lead_days`. Read as calendar distance from the start of photography, the outline is due earliest and the production polish is due last.

### 2.7. Money, Counting and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **whole cents**; no field carries fractional currency. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent, meaning a remainder of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. Percentages and share fractions are supplied as numbers — a share of 0.5 means one half — while credit shares are integer percentage points that sum to exactly 100.

Rounding is applied at the points the calculations name, and only there. Where a value is the product of several factors, the factors are multiplied first and the single product is rounded once, never rounded factor by factor. Where a rate is derived and then multiplied by a count, the derived rate is rounded first and the rounded rate is then multiplied, because rounding after the multiplication produces a different number of cents. The one non-monetary rounding in the system is the arbitration concurrence fraction, which is rounded half up to three decimal places. Counts — seats, guarantee slots, ready scripts, revision rounds, late milestones, risk scores and credit percentage points — are always exact integers and are never rounded.

### 2.8. Interval and Calendar Semantics

Three different interval conventions coexist in this domain and the distinction is load-bearing. An **availability window** and a **room seat** both occupy a half-open interval: the interval runs from its `start_date` inclusive to its `end_date` exclusive, so two seats where one ends exactly when the other begins do not overlap. A **writers room** operating interval is likewise half-open. An **episode shoot window**, by contrast, is a closed interval that begins on the episode's `production_start_date` and ends on the day that is `shoot_days` minus one day later, so an eight-day shoot starting on the first of a month occupies the first through the eighth inclusive. Any supplied interval whose `end_date` is not strictly after its `start_date` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422); the same code covers a season whose `staffing_window_end` precedes its `staffing_window_start`, or whose `production_start_date` is not strictly after `staffing_window_end`.

Milestone deadlines are calendar-derived rather than clock-derived. A due date is computed by taking the UTC calendar date of the owning episode's `production_start_date`, subtracting the whole number of lead days for that milestone type, and stamping the resulting UTC date at `23:59:59.999Z`. Lateness is measured in whole calendar days between the UTC calendar date of the delivery and the UTC calendar date of the deadline, never in hours, and it is never negative: an early delivery is zero days late.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Rooms, Episodes and Seasons

Four write operations supersede state that a client must have observed before acting, and each of them requires the client to prove which revision it observed. In every case the version field is a **required, non-optional** input, and a value that does not equal the parent row's current `version` at the moment of the write is rejected before any side effect is applied.

- `POST /rooms/{room_id}/seats` requires `expected_room_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `WritersRoom`. A seat offer consumes one of the room's finite open seats and moves the room's derived counters, so an offer issued from a stale view of the room would over-subscribe capacity or price the seat against a superseded configuration. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /rooms/{room_id}/reconfigure` requires `expected_room_version` for the same room. A reconfiguration legitimately supersedes the prior format and duration commitment, so a reconfigure carrying a version the room has already advanced past is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than being applied on top of a configuration the client never saw.
- `POST /episodes/{episode_id}/script-assignments` requires `expected_episode_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Episode`. A new assignment legitimately supersedes the episode's script posture — a rewrite reopens a ready script, a production polish stacks on an approved draft — so a create from a stale episode view is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EPISODE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /seasons/{season_id}/reschedule` requires `expected_season_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Season`. A reschedule supersedes the season's entire milestone calendar, so a reschedule carrying a version that has already advanced is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` (HTTP 412) instead of shifting dates derived from a production start the client never observed.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Accepting a seat, declining it, delivering a milestone, approving or voiding an assignment, deciding a clearance item, casting a ballot and every other write in the system are guarded by state preconditions alone and must not be sent an expected-version input.

### 2.10. Cascading Recomputation and Conserved Quantities

Derived values in this system are never patched in place by the operation that disturbs them; they are recomputed from their inputs, and because several derived values feed each other, a single call can propagate along a chain. Three such chains recur often enough to be named.

**The reschedule chain** starts at a season's production start date. Moving it shifts every one of that season's episodes that is not already locked by the same signed delta, which re-derives the four due-date fields of every non-void script assignment on those episodes, which re-derives every milestone due date and re-evaluates each pending or late milestone against the logical clock, which finally recomputes each assignment's late-milestone count. The propagation reaches a fixed point in a single pass, because no milestone feeds back into an episode date.

**The seat-release chain** starts when a confirmed seat stops being confirmed. Because the seat is no longer confirmed, every non-terminal script assignment held through that seat moves to reassignment; because those assignments left their working states, each affected episode's script readiness is re-derived; and because those readiness values changed, the season's ready-script count, coverage gap and coverage posture are recomputed. Each link is caused by the previous link's mutation rather than directly by the release call.

**The reconfiguration chain** starts when a room's format or duration commitment changes. Because the format and duration changed, every confirmed seat's compensation figures are re-derived; because those seat figures changed, the room's seat subtotal, facility tier, facility surcharge and weekly burn are recomputed; and because the room's weekly burn changed, the season's staffing commitment is recomputed.

Three quantities are conserved across compensating operations, and the system's counters must reflect that. **Room seats**: a room's `seat_capacity` always equals its `filled_seat_count` plus its `open_seat_count`, where the filled count is the number of that room's seats in `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED` status and the confirmed count is the number in `CONFIRMED` status; an offer followed by its withdrawal returns the open count to its pre-offer value exactly. **Episodic guarantee slots**: for every seat, `episodic_guarantee_count` always equals `guarantee_slots_consumed` plus `guarantee_slots_remaining`, and the consumed figure always equals the number of that seat's non-void assignments carrying an applied guarantee slot; voiding an assignment that held a slot returns the remaining figure to its pre-assignment value. **Committed script money**: a season's `script_fee_committed_cents` always equals the signed sum of the amounts of every fee ledger entry belonging to that season, and the entries of any voided assignment always sum to exactly zero, so a create followed by its compensating void leaves the season's committed figure byte-identical to its pre-create value.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve determines the error. The hierarchies are: guild agreement, then season, then episode, then script assignment, then milestone; writers room, then room seat; clearance review, then clearance item; and the standalone resources credit arbitration, credit submission, fee ledger entry, writer and availability window. A path variable that names no existing row yields HTTP 404 with the code for that resource type: `agreement_id` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `season_id` yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `writer_id` yields `ERR_WRITER_NOT_FOUND`, `window_id` yields `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `room_id` yields `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `seat_id` yields `ERR_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `episode_id` yields `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `assignment_id` yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `milestone_id` yields `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `review_id` yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `item_id` yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `submission_id` yields `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `arbitration_id` yields `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_FOUND` and `entry_id` yields `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Resources referenced from a request body rather than the path are checked with the same codes: `guild_agreement_id`, `agreement_id`, `showrunner_writer_id`, `writer_id`, every id in `arbiter_writer_ids`, `predecessor_assignment_id` and `reviewed_draft_milestone_id` must all resolve to existing rows.

When more than one rule could reject a request, the checks run in a fixed precedence and the first failure is the one reported. The order is:

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five recognised roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The acting role is permitted on the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. For endpoints open to the entity-backed roles `Writer` and `Showrunner`, `X-User-ID` is an existing writer's id — otherwise `ERR_ACTING_WRITER_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Every path-segment resource exists, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code.
6. Every body-referenced resource exists — otherwise the matching not-found code.
7. The request body satisfies its declared schema: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, integers inside their declared ranges, and rate-card objects carrying exactly the declared keys — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
8. On list endpoints, pagination parameters are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
9. Supplied intervals are well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.
10. On a ballot, the vote share map is structurally valid — otherwise `ERR_VOTE_SHARES_INVALID`.
11. Resource ownership for an acting `Writer` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
12. Season-showrunner authority for an acting `Showrunner` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_SEASON_SHOWRUNNER`.
13. The optimistic-concurrency version field matches the parent row's current `version` — otherwise the matching stale-version code.
14. The target resource is in a state that admits the operation — otherwise the matching conflict code.
15. Segregation-of-duties rules: self-approval and arbiter participation.
16. Domain safety rules: guild compensation floor, level requirement, diversity funding eligibility, diversity staffing requirement, clearance gates and the conditional-note requirement.
17. Temporal rules: staffing window containment, staffing window closure and reschedule date validity.
18. Structural business constraints: writer interval exclusivity, availability containment, production-unit turnaround, capacity and uniqueness, and the revision cap.
19. Referential business rules: staffing linkage, predecessor linkage, review linkage, panel membership and submission-set emptiness.
20. Fiscal ceilings: the season staffing budget and the script fee budget.
21. Coverage: the ready-script target on a production lock.

Ownership and season-showrunner checks therefore precede optimistic concurrency, and optimistic concurrency precedes every state and domain check — a caller holding a stale room version learns that before learning that the room is full. Within step 14, note that a locked episode is a hard stop: any write against an episode whose script readiness is `LOCKED` is rejected with `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_LOCKED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Guild Rate Card, the Writer Registry and Availability

Everything begins with the guild administrator. Registering a Minimum Basic Agreement creates a configuration row and nothing else: no season, room or writer is touched by it, and the row is never mutated afterwards, so a studio that renegotiates its terms registers a new rate card for the new `mba_year` rather than editing the old one. Because the row is immutable and every downstream figure resolves through it, a season's arithmetic is reproducible from the pair (season, governing agreement) alone.

Registering a writer captures who they are and what they have done. The guild administrator supplies the writer's `legal_name` as it appears on guild paperwork (1 to 200 characters), their `guild_member_id` (1 to 40 characters), and the `agreement_id` of the rate card whose credit thresholds should be applied to them. Membership numbers are unique across the whole registry, and a second registration under a number already on file is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GUILD_MEMBER_ID`. Three optional inputs round out the record: `career_episodic_credits`, the count of produced episodic credits the writer brings with them, which must not be negative and defaults to 0; `diversity_program_participant`, which defaults to false and is the prerequisite for ever holding a diversity-program-funded seat; and `is_showrunner_eligible`, which also defaults to false and gates whether the writer may be named as a season's showrunner. A newly registered writer starts with no live seats and no on-screen credits, so `active_seat_count` and `on_screen_credit_count` both begin at 0.

**Level determination** runs at registration and again on every later credit award. The rule is a descending threshold walk against the supplied agreement's `level_credit_thresholds`, and every comparison is inclusive — credits exactly equal to a threshold do reach that level. If the writer's career episodic credits are at least the `PRODUCER` threshold, `determined_level` becomes `PRODUCER`; otherwise, if they are at least the `CO_PRODUCER` threshold, the level is `CO_PRODUCER`; otherwise, if they are at least the `EXEC_STORY_EDITOR` threshold, the level is `EXEC_STORY_EDITOR`; otherwise, if they are at least the `STORY_EDITOR` threshold, the level is `STORY_EDITOR`; and if none of those hold, the level is `STAFF_WRITER`. Whichever agreement produced the answer is recorded in `level_determination_agreement_id`, which is null only for a writer who has never been determined. Nothing else participates in this computation — no seat, no room and no season is read, and a seat's `level_at_hire` is emphatically not an input to it.

Awarding episodic credits is the only way career credits move after registration. The award carries `episodic_credits_awarded` and the `agreement_id` to re-determine against; the awarded count is added to the writer's `career_episodic_credits`, the threshold walk above is then re-run against the *post-award* total, and `determined_level` may consequently rise. Because the walk is a pure function of the post-award total, a single large award can carry a writer up more than one level at once. The writer's `version` increments and the updated writer record is returned with HTTP 200.

Availability is declared by the writer themselves or by a guild administrator acting on their behalf. A window names the writer it belongs to through the path, and carries a `start_date` and an `end_date` — the interval is half-open, so it covers the start instant but not the end instant — plus a `commitment_type` of either `EXCLUSIVE`, meaning the window may back at most one occupying seat interval at a time, or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`, meaning overlapping seats may draw on it. Every window is created `OPEN` with no consuming seat, so its `consumed_by_seat_id` is null; it becomes `CONSUMED` only when a seat acceptance claims it, and returns to `OPEN` when that seat is released. Windows are the writer's own inventory of time: without an `OPEN` window that fully contains a proposed seat interval, the writer cannot be offered or confirmed into that seat at all.

### 3.2. Opening a Season, Ordering Episodes and Rescheduling Photography

A studio executive opens a season by naming its governing rate card through `guild_agreement_id`, its creative lead through `showrunner_writer_id`, and its `title` (1 to 200 characters). The named writer must exist and must be showrunner-eligible; naming a writer whose eligibility flag is false fails the body-schema check with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The season declares how much television it intends to make through `episode_order`, an integer from 1 to 30, and the default `length_format` for those episodes, either `HALF_HOUR` or `ONE_HOUR`. Three dates frame the work: `staffing_window_start` and `staffing_window_end` bound the period in which seat offers may start, both inclusive, with the end at or after the start; and `production_start_date` is the instant principal photography begins, which must be strictly after the staffing window closes. A season also declares `ready_script_target`, the number of ready scripts (1 to 30) the season must be able to count before any episode may be locked for production, and two ceilings: `staffing_budget_weekly_cents`, the maximum combined weekly burn of its rooms, and `script_fee_budget_cents`, the maximum committed script money. Both ceilings are non-negative integers.

One further season input selects which of two guarantee-settlement policies the season runs under. `guarantee_application_mode` is either `FORWARD_COMMIT` or `CHRONOLOGICAL_ON_ACCEPTANCE`, and it defaults to `CHRONOLOGICAL_ON_ACCEPTANCE`. The two modes are described in §3.5; the choice is made once, at season creation, and every assignment on the season settles under it. A freshly created season has committed nothing and finished nothing, so `staffing_commitment_weekly_cents`, `script_fee_committed_cents` and `ready_script_count` all start at 0, and the coverage figures are computed immediately from that empty state.

Episodes are ordered onto the season one at a time by the studio executive. Each carries an `episode_number` — a 1-based integer that may not exceed the season's `episode_order` and is unique within the season, a second use being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EPISODE_NUMBER` — its own `length_format`, which selects the base script fee for its scripts and need not match the season default, the `production_unit` that will photograph it (`UNIT_A` or `UNIT_B`), its own `production_start_date`, which must be at or after the season's production start, and `shoot_days`, an integer from 1 to 30 that defaults to 8.

**Production-unit turnaround** is the constraint that keeps a single camera unit from being double-booked. Each episode occupies a closed shoot window running from its production start through the day `shoot_days` minus one day later. For any two episodes of the same season sharing the same production unit, those windows are each expanded by the agreement's `turnaround_buffer_days` on both sides, and the two expanded windows must not intersect. Concretely, a new episode is rejected when its start falls at or before the existing episode's end plus the buffer *and* the existing episode's start falls at or before the new episode's end plus the buffer; that rejection carries `ERR_PRODUCTION_UNIT_CONFLICT`. With the default buffer of two days, two eight-day shoots on the same unit must be separated by more than two clear days on the calendar, while the same two shoots on `UNIT_A` and `UNIT_B` may be scheduled on top of each other without complaint. Within step 18 of the §2.11 precedence the turnaround test is evaluated ahead of the episode-number uniqueness test, so an episode that simultaneously collides with a same-unit shoot window and reuses an `episode_number` already taken on the season reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_UNIT_CONFLICT` rather than `ERR_DUPLICATE_EPISODE_NUMBER`.

A new episode starts at the bottom of all three of its state dimensions: `script_readiness` is `NOT_STARTED`, `credit_status` is `PENDING`, and `clearance_posture` is `PENDING`. It has no approved teleplay, so `approved_teleplay_assignment_id` is null; it has no credit maps, so `provisional_credit_shares` and `final_credit_shares` are empty objects and `determined_credit_string` is null; and it is not locked, so `locked_at` is null. Adding the episode also re-runs the season's coverage computation, because the season now has one more episode that might or might not be ready.

Slipping the whole season's photography is a single studio-executive operation, and it is the head of the reschedule chain described in §2.10. The caller supplies `new_production_start_date` together with `expected_season_version`. The proposed date must be strictly after the season's `staffing_window_end` and strictly after the logical clock — a production cannot be rescheduled into the past or back inside the hiring window — and a date failing either test is rejected with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_DATE_INVALID`. When the date is accepted, the delta between the old and new season production start is computed once and applied uniformly: every episode of the season whose `script_readiness` is not `LOCKED` has its own `production_start_date` shifted by that same signed delta, so relative episode spacing and unit assignments survive the move untouched. Locked episodes are frozen and keep their dates.

The consequences ripple outward from those shifted episode dates. Every non-void script assignment on a shifted episode has all four of its due-date fields re-derived from the new episode date, and every milestone of those assignments has its `due_date` re-derived to match. Each re-derived milestone is then re-evaluated against the logical clock: a `PENDING` milestone whose new deadline has already passed flips to `LATE`, and — this is the direction that only a reschedule can produce — a `LATE` milestone whose re-derived deadline is now at or after the logical clock flips back to `PENDING`. Every affected assignment's `late_milestone_count` is recomputed from the resulting milestone statuses, the season's coverage figures are recomputed, and the season's `version` increments by one. Void assignments and their milestones are deliberately left alone: they keep their stale dates forever and are never re-derived.

### 3.3. Configuring the Room and Offering Seats

A season may run several rooms — a main room and a mini room, an in-person core and a remote satellite — and each is configured independently by the showrunner or the studio executive. A room carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters ("Main Room", "Mini Room B"), a `format` of `IN_PERSON`, `REMOTE` or `MINI_ROOM`, a `seat_capacity` from 1 to 12, a `duration_weeks_committed` from 1 to 52 expressing how long the studio commits the room for, a `start_date` that must fall inside the span from the season's staffing window opening through the season's production start, and an `end_date` strictly after it; the room's operating interval is half-open. Optionally the room declares `diversity_seats_required`, a count from 0 through the room's capacity that defaults to 0 and sets how many confirmed diversity-funded seats the room must hold before it may be activated. A mini room is a small room by definition: configuring or reconfiguring a `MINI_ROOM` with a capacity above 4 is rejected with `ERR_MINI_ROOM_SEAT_LIMIT`.

A new room is `OPEN` and empty. Its `open_seat_count` equals its `seat_capacity`, its `filled_seat_count`, `confirmed_seat_count` and `confirmed_diversity_seat_count` are all 0, and the room's weekly-cost figures are computed immediately over that empty seat set, which lands `facility_tier` at `TIER_1`. An `OPEN` room, however, is not yet a staffing commitment against the season's budget — see the room-posture rules in §3.4.

Opening the room for offers is the showrunner's move, and it takes the room from `OPEN` to `STAFFING`. It is refused with `ERR_ROOM_NOT_ACCEPTING_SEATS` unless the room is currently `OPEN`, and refused with `ERR_STAFFING_WINDOW_CLOSED` if the logical clock has already passed the season's `staffing_window_end` — hiring cannot begin after the hiring window has shut, though the clock standing exactly on the closing instant is still in time. The transition recomputes the room's weekly-cost figures (with no confirmed seats the subtotal is still 0, so `TIER_1` applies and the room's whole burn is the format surcharge on the tier-1 facility fee), recomputes the season's staffing commitment now that this room counts towards it, and increments the room's `version`.

Offering a seat is the showrunner's exclusive act and the single most heavily guarded write in the system. The offer names the `writer_id` being hired, the `level_at_hire` the seat is written at, the `offered_weekly_compensation_cents` the studio proposes (an integer of at least 1), the seat's `exclusivity` (`EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`), and the seat's own half-open `start_date` and `end_date`. Two optional inputs shape the money: `episodic_guarantee_count`, a count from 0 to 30 that defaults to 0 and says how many episode scripts are guaranteed to the writer under this seat, and `diversity_program_funded`, which defaults to false. The offer must additionally carry `expected_room_version` as described in §2.9.

The offer's guards are evaluated in the global precedence order of §2.11, which matters because several of them can fail at once. First the room must be in `STAFFING`, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_NOT_ACCEPTING_SEATS`. Then the room version must match, otherwise `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION`. Then the money floor: the offered weekly compensation must be at least the agreement's minimum weekly cents for the seat's `level_at_hire`, otherwise `ERR_BELOW_GUILD_MINIMUM`. Then the level requirement: reading the levels in the fixed order `STAFF_WRITER`, `STORY_EDITOR`, `EXEC_STORY_EDITOR`, `CO_PRODUCER`, `PRODUCER`, the seat's `level_at_hire` must be at or below the writer's `determined_level`, except that a diversity-program-funded seat may be written at exactly one position above the determined level — two positions above is never allowed. A violation is rejected with `ERR_LEVEL_ABOVE_DETERMINED`. Then funding eligibility: a seat may only be marked `diversity_program_funded` when the writer's `diversity_program_participant` flag is true, otherwise `ERR_DIVERSITY_FUNDING_INELIGIBLE`. Then staffing-season timing: the seat's `start_date` must fall within the season's staffing window, at or after `staffing_window_start` and at or before `staffing_window_end`, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_STAFFING_WINDOW`.

Two writer-side collision checks follow. **Interval exclusivity** compares the new seat's half-open interval against every other seat of the same writer whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED`. The pair collides only when at least one of the two seats is `EXCLUSIVE`; two `NON_EXCLUSIVE` seats may overlap freely. Because the intervals are half-open, a new seat starting exactly when an existing seat ends does not overlap it. A genuine collision is rejected with `ERR_WRITER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **Availability containment** then requires that the writer has at least one availability window in `OPEN` status whose interval fully contains the seat's: the window must start at or before the seat's start and end at or after the seat's end. Partial coverage is not coverage, and the absence of any such window is rejected with `ERR_WRITER_UNAVAILABLE`. Finally the room must have room — `open_seat_count` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — and the season must be able to afford the hire, checked last as described below.

**Seat weekly compensation** is derived, not accepted as given. Five inputs interact. The starting point is the agreement's minimum weekly cents for the seat's `level_at_hire`. That level minimum is multiplied by a format multiplier, which is the agreement's `mini_room_floor_multiplier` when the room's format is `MINI_ROOM` and exactly 1 for both `IN_PERSON` and `REMOTE` — a mini room pays a premium because it asks fewer writers to carry a whole season. It is then multiplied by one plus a short-commitment uplift, where the uplift is the agreement's `short_commitment_uplift_pct` when the room's `duration_weeks_committed` is strictly below the agreement's `short_commitment_weeks_threshold`, and zero when the commitment is at or above that threshold; with the default threshold of 20 weeks, a room committed for exactly 20 weeks attracts no uplift while a 19-week room does. Rounding happens exactly once, on the single product of level minimum, format multiplier and the uplift factor, and the rounded result is the seat's `guild_floor_weekly_cents`. The seat's `weekly_compensation_cents` is then the greater of the studio's `offered_weekly_compensation_cents` and that floor, so a generous offer stands and a thin one is lifted to the floor.

The diversity program then pays part of the bill. When the seat is `diversity_program_funded` and the writer is a `diversity_program_participant`, the seat's `diversity_subsidy_weekly_cents` is the lesser of two figures: the seat's weekly compensation multiplied by the agreement's `diversity_subsidy_pct` and rounded to whole cents, and the agreement's `diversity_subsidy_cap_weekly_cents`. In every other case the subsidy is 0. The seat's `studio_charge_weekly_cents` is its weekly compensation minus that subsidy, and it is the figure the studio actually bears — though note that the room's burn and the season's staffing commitment are built from full weekly compensation, not from the studio charge.

**Prorated commitment** converts a weekly rate into the seat's total studio commitment across its interval, and the order of operations is pinned because it changes the answer in cents. Count the whole UTC days in the seat's half-open interval; divide that day count by 7 and take the whole-number quotient as the number of full weeks, with the remainder as the residual days. Round the weekly compensation divided by 7 to whole cents **first** to obtain a daily rate, then multiply that rounded daily rate by the residual days, and add the product of the full weeks and the full weekly compensation. Deriving the residual by multiplying the weekly rate by the residual days and rounding at the end is a different computation and must not be used: on a seat paying 450,000 cents a week with three residual days, the pinned order yields three times 64,286, or 192,858 cents, while the rejected order yields 192,857 — a one-cent divergence on every seat whose weekly rate is not a multiple of seven cents. The result is stored as `prorated_commitment_cents`.

**Room weekly burn** is then re-derived for the room in four ordered steps. Step one sums `weekly_compensation_cents` across only those seats of the room whose status is `CONFIRMED`, producing `seat_subtotal_weekly_cents`; seats merely `OFFERED` contribute nothing. Step two selects `facility_tier` from that subtotal, and from the subtotal alone: `TIER_1` when the subtotal is at or below the agreement's `TIER_1_MAX` bound, otherwise `TIER_2` when it is at or below `TIER_2_MAX`, otherwise `TIER_3`. Step three multiplies the facility fee for the selected tier by the agreement's format surcharge fraction for the room's `format` and rounds the product to whole cents, giving `facility_surcharge_weekly_cents`. Step four adds the subtotal and the surcharge to give `weekly_burn_cents`. The order matters: the tier is keyed to the pre-surcharge subtotal, never to the burn total, because keying it to the total would make the definition circular.

**Season staffing commitment** is the sum of `weekly_burn_cents` over the season's rooms whose status is `STAFFING` or `ACTIVE`; rooms that are still `OPEN` and rooms that have `WRAPPED` retain their computed burn but are excluded from the sum. That total is the season's `staffing_commitment_weekly_cents`. The **staffing budget** test on a seat offer adds the new seat's own weekly compensation to the season's current commitment and requires the result to be at or below `staffing_budget_weekly_cents`; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_SEASON_STAFFING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

A surviving offer creates the seat in `OFFERED` status with all four compensation figures and the prorated commitment computed, `guarantee_slots_consumed` at 0 and `guarantee_slots_remaining` equal to the seat's `episodic_guarantee_count`, `confirmed_at` and `consumed_window_id` both null. The room's `filled_seat_count` increases by one and its `open_seat_count` is recomputed as capacity minus filled seats; the writer's `active_seat_count` increases by one; the room's weekly figures and the season's staffing commitment are recomputed; and the room's `version` increments.

### 3.4. Seat Responses, Release, and the Room's Operating Posture

An offer is a proposal, and the writer answers it. Accepting is available only to the writer who holds the seat: the seat must still be `OFFERED` (otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_OFFERED`), the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the seat's writer (otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), the room must still be `STAFFING` (otherwise `ERR_ROOM_NOT_ACCEPTING_SEATS`), and the writer must still have an `OPEN` availability window containing the seat's whole interval (otherwise `ERR_WRITER_UNAVAILABLE`). Acceptance moves the seat `OFFERED → CONFIRMED` and stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock.

Acceptance also consumes exactly one window, chosen deterministically. The candidate set is every availability window of the seat's writer whose status is `OPEN` and whose interval contains the seat's half-open interval; candidates are ranked by the window's `start_date` ascending, and ties are broken by the window's `created_at` ascending; the first candidate wins. An empty candidate set is the `ERR_WRITER_UNAVAILABLE` case above. The winning window moves to `CONSUMED` and records the seat in its `consumed_by_seat_id`, while the seat records the window in its `consumed_window_id` — a two-way link that makes the later release reversible. The room's `confirmed_seat_count` increases by one, and when the seat is `diversity_program_funded` its `confirmed_diversity_seat_count` also increases by one. Because a confirmed seat now contributes to the subtotal, the room's `seat_subtotal_weekly_cents`, `facility_tier`, `facility_surcharge_weekly_cents` and `weekly_burn_cents` are recomputed, and the season's `staffing_commitment_weekly_cents` follows. The seat's own `version` increments, as do the room's and the consumed window's.

Declining is the writer's other option and needs only that the seat is still `OFFERED` and that the caller owns it; an optional `reason` may accompany it. The seat becomes `DECLINED`, the room's `filled_seat_count` drops by one and `open_seat_count` is recomputed as capacity minus filled seats — which restores the room's seat-conservation identity exactly — the writer's `active_seat_count` drops by one, and the room's weekly figures and the season's staffing commitment are recomputed even though a merely offered seat never contributed to the subtotal. Withdrawing is the mirror image from the studio side: the showrunner pulls an outstanding offer, optionally citing a `reason`, and the seat becomes `WITHDRAWN` with exactly the same counter arithmetic. `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal; a writer cannot later accept a seat they declined, and the same `ERR_SEAT_NOT_OFFERED` guards both operations against a seat that has already moved on.

Releasing a confirmed writer is a heavier act, permitted to a studio executive or a showrunner, optionally with a `reason`, and it is the head of the seat-release chain. The seat must be `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_CONFIRMED`. On release the seat becomes `RELEASED`; the window it consumed returns to `OPEN` with its `consumed_by_seat_id` cleared and the seat's `consumed_window_id` cleared, freeing that stretch of the writer's calendar for another room; the room's `filled_seat_count` and `confirmed_seat_count` each drop by one, `confirmed_diversity_seat_count` drops too when the seat was diversity-funded, and `open_seat_count` is recomputed; the writer's `active_seat_count` drops by one; and the room's weekly figures and then the season's staffing commitment are recomputed.

Then the chain fires. Because the seat is no longer `CONFIRMED`, every script assignment held through that seat whose status is `DRAFTING`, `DELIVERED` or `IN_NOTES` moves to `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING`; the assignment keeps the seat reference it was created with, so the seat's guarantee counters are unaffected by the release, and the assignment's milestones keep their current statuses and due dates untouched. Because those assignments left their working states, each affected episode's `script_readiness` is re-derived: an episode sitting at `READY` returns to `IN_PROGRESS` when it has no `approved_teleplay_assignment_id` and at least one non-void assignment survives, and an episode at `IN_PROGRESS` falls back to `NOT_STARTED` when every one of its assignments is void. Because those readiness values changed, the season's `ready_script_count`, `coverage_gap` and `coverage_posture` are recomputed. The room's `version` increments.

Reconfiguring a room is the studio executive's lever over format and duration mid-staffing, and it is a self-loop: the room is in `STAFFING` before the call and in `STAFFING` after it. The call supplies a new `format`, a new `duration_weeks_committed`, and `expected_room_version`. Beyond the room-status and version guards, a reconfiguration to `MINI_ROOM` is refused with `ERR_MINI_ROOM_SEAT_LIMIT` when the room's `seat_capacity` exceeds 4, and it likewise requires that the room's `filled_seat_count` be no greater than 4 — a room that has already filled five seats cannot become a mini room. The reconfiguration chain then runs in order: every seat of the room whose status is `CONFIRMED` has `guild_floor_weekly_cents`, `weekly_compensation_cents`, `diversity_subsidy_weekly_cents` and `studio_charge_weekly_cents` re-derived under the new format and duration, and its `prorated_commitment_cents` re-derived from the new weekly figure; the room's `seat_subtotal_weekly_cents`, `facility_tier`, `facility_surcharge_weekly_cents` and `weekly_burn_cents` are then recomputed from those new seat figures; and the season's `staffing_commitment_weekly_cents` is recomputed from the new room burn. The room's `version` increments, and so does the `version` of every seat that was re-derived. Note what a reconfiguration can do: switching a 24-week `IN_PERSON` room to a 12-week `MINI_ROOM` raises every confirmed seat's floor by the mini-room multiplier and adds the short-commitment uplift on top, which can push the subtotal across a facility-tier boundary and change the surcharge in the same call.

Activation closes staffing and opens creative work; only a studio executive may perform it, and it takes the room `STAFFING → ACTIVE`. Four gates apply in order. The room must be in `STAFFING`. No seat of the room may still be `OFFERED` — every offer must be answered or withdrawn first, otherwise `ERR_SEATS_STILL_OFFERED`. The room must hold at least one confirmed seat, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_HAS_NO_CONFIRMED_SEATS`. The room's `confirmed_diversity_seat_count` must be at least its `diversity_seats_required`, otherwise `ERR_DIVERSITY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`. And the season's `staffing_commitment_weekly_cents` must be at or below its `staffing_budget_weekly_cents`, otherwise `ERR_SEASON_STAFFING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` — the budget is tested again here because seat acceptances since the offers were made may have moved the commitment. Activation recomputes the room's weekly burn and the season's staffing commitment and increments the room's `version`. Availability windows are untouched by activation: a window moves to `CONSUMED` only when a seat is accepted, never when a room is activated.

Wrapping ends the room's life and is available to a showrunner or a studio executive. The room must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The room becomes `WRAPPED`, which is terminal, and every seat of the room still in `CONFIRMED` status is released as part of the same call — each such seat runs the full release chain described above, including the return of its consumed availability window, the counter arithmetic, the move of its live assignments to `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING`, and the episode-readiness and season-coverage recomputation that follows. Seats already sitting in `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `RELEASED` are left exactly as they are. The room's weekly figures are recomputed and the season's staffing commitment is recomputed, and because a `WRAPPED` room is excluded from the commitment sum, wrapping is what actually returns the season's weekly burn to the budget. The room's `version` increments.

### 3.5. Assigning Scripts: Fees, Guarantee Slots and the Fee Ledger

Only the season's showrunner may hand out scripts, and they do it one episode at a time. The assignment names the `writer_id` who will write, the `assignment_type` describing the service — `STORY`, `TELEPLAY`, `STORY_AND_TELEPLAY`, `REWRITE` or `PRODUCTION_POLISH` — an optional `predecessor_assignment_id`, and `expected_episode_version`. A showrunner who is not the `showrunner_writer_id` of the episode's season is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SEASON_SHOWRUNNER`; a mismatched episode version with `ERR_STALE_EPISODE_VERSION`; and an episode whose `script_readiness` is already `LOCKED` with `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_LOCKED`.

Three domain guards follow, in this order. **Type exclusivity**: an episode may hold at most one live assignment of any given type, so a create is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_TYPE_CONFLICT` when another assignment of the same `assignment_type` on the same episode is in `DRAFTING`, `DELIVERED`, `IN_NOTES` or `APPROVED`. An assignment in `VOID` or `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING` does not block a replacement of the same type, which is precisely how a released writer's work is picked up by someone else. **Staffing linkage**: the writer must be seated. The system resolves the assignment's `room_seat_id` by filtering to seats of the named writer whose status is `CONFIRMED` in a writers room belonging to this episode's season whose own status is `ACTIVE`; candidates are ranked by the seat's `confirmed_at` ascending with the seat's `created_at` breaking ties, and the first candidate is stored on the assignment. There is no freelance path in this system: an empty candidate set is rejected with `ERR_WRITER_NOT_STAFFED`. **Predecessor linkage**: a `REWRITE` or a `PRODUCTION_POLISH` must name a `predecessor_assignment_id` that belongs to the same episode and whose status is `APPROVED`, and an absent, foreign-episode or unapproved predecessor is rejected with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_APPROVED`; conversely a `STORY`, `TELEPLAY` or `STORY_AND_TELEPLAY` must omit the field entirely, and supplying it is rejected with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_ALLOWED`. Because rewrites chain off approved drafts, the predecessor reference makes an assignment lineage that can be walked back to the original teleplay.

**The script fee** is derived from two lookups and one rounding. The base is the agreement's script fee in cents for the *episode's* `length_format`, not the season's; the share is the agreement's type share for the assignment's `assignment_type`. The base multiplied by that share, rounded half up to whole cents, is the assignment's `gross_fee_cents`. A story-and-teleplay on a one-hour episode therefore earns a larger gross than a production polish on the same episode purely through the share table.

**Episodic guarantees** then decide how much of that gross the writer is actually paid on top of their weekly salary. A seat carries a finite number of guaranteed episode scripts in `episodic_guarantee_count`, and settlement is a waterfall: slots are allocated to the first candidates in an ordered list of the seat's assignments until the seat's capacity is exhausted, one slot per assignment regardless of type. An assignment inside the allocated prefix has `guarantee_slot_applied` set true, and its `guarantee_offset_cents` becomes its gross fee multiplied by the agreement's guarantee offset fraction for the level recorded on the seat's `level_at_hire`, rounded half up to whole cents; an assignment outside the prefix has the flag false and an offset of 0. Either way, `payable_fee_cents` is the gross fee minus the offset. After every settlement the seat's `guarantee_slots_consumed` is recounted as the number of its non-void assignments carrying an applied slot, and `guarantee_slots_remaining` is the seat's capacity minus that count.

The **order of that candidate list is the season's policy choice**, and the two modes give genuinely different money. Under `FORWARD_COMMIT`, the candidates are the seat's assignments whose status is not `VOID`, ordered by `created_at` ascending; a slot is therefore committed at creation time, the earliest-created assignment wins the guarantee even if a later-created assignment is approved first, and approval performs no re-settlement at all. Under `CHRONOLOGICAL_ON_ACCEPTANCE`, the candidates are only those assignments whose status is `APPROVED`, ordered by `approved_at` ascending with `created_at` breaking ties; a brand-new assignment therefore carries no slot at creation and its `payable_fee_cents` equals its `gross_fee_cents` in full, and the slot is granted later, at approval time, in approval order. Consider a seat with a guarantee capacity of 1 held at `STORY_EDITOR` level with a guarantee offset fraction of 0.5, an assignment A1 on a one-hour episode with a gross of 3,500,000 cents created first, and an assignment A2 on a half-hour episode with a gross of 1,750,000 cents created second but approved first. Under forward commitment, A1 carries the slot: A1 is payable at 1,750,000, A2 at 1,750,000, and the season's committed script money is 3,500,000. Under chronological settlement on acceptance, A2 wins the slot by approving first: A2 is payable at 875,000, A1 at its full 3,500,000, and the season's committed script money is 4,375,000.

Every movement of script money is recorded in an **append-only fee ledger**, whose entries are never mutated or deleted. Each entry records the `season_id` whose committed total it moves, the `assignment_id` that produced it, the `writer_id` the money is owed to or reversed from, an `entry_type`, a signed `amount_cents`, and a human-readable `memo` such as "void reversal of TELEPLAY fee". The four entry types partition the money's history: `SCRIPT_FEE_ACCRUAL` is written on assignment creation for the positive gross fee; `GUARANTEE_OFFSET` is written when a guarantee slot absorbs part of that fee, carrying the negative offset; `FEE_REVERSAL` is written on a void, negating every prior entry of the assignment; and `GUARANTEE_REALLOCATION` is written when a chronological settlement moves a slot between assignments, carrying the signed delta. Creating an assignment therefore appends a `SCRIPT_FEE_ACCRUAL` entry for the full gross and, when a slot applied at creation, a `GUARANTEE_OFFSET` entry for the negative offset. The season's `script_fee_committed_cents` is then recomputed as the signed sum of every ledger entry of that season — it is never incremented in place.

The **script fee budget** is checked before any of this is persisted: the season's current `script_fee_committed_cents` plus the new assignment's `payable_fee_cents` must be at or below `script_fee_budget_cents`, and exceeding the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_SCRIPT_FEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Note the interaction with the settlement mode — under chronological settlement a fresh assignment is payable at its full gross, so the same assignment can breach the budget in a season that would have absorbed it under forward commitment.

Creation also lays out the writer's calendar. The assignment is created in `DRAFTING`, its `revision_round` at 0, its `approved_at` and `approved_by_role` null, and its `late_milestone_count` at 0. All four due-date fields — `outline_due_date`, `first_draft_due_date`, `second_draft_due_date` and `polish_due_date` — are always populated on the assignment itself regardless of its type, each computed by taking the UTC calendar date of the episode's `production_start_date`, subtracting the agreement's lead days for that milestone kind, and stamping the resulting date at `23:59:59.999Z`. The milestone rows themselves, by contrast, are synthesized to match the type, each carrying its kind in `milestone_type`: a `STORY` assignment gets a single `OUTLINE` milestone; a `TELEPLAY` and a `STORY_AND_TELEPLAY` each get `OUTLINE`, `FIRST_DRAFT` and `SECOND_DRAFT`; a `REWRITE` gets a single `FIRST_DRAFT`; and a `PRODUCTION_POLISH` gets a single `PRODUCTION_POLISH`. Each milestone is numbered by `sequence_index` starting at 1 in that chain order, copies the matching due-date field into its own `due_date`, and starts `PENDING` — or `LATE` immediately, if the logical clock has already passed that deadline at the moment the assignment is created.

Finally the episode moves. An episode at `NOT_STARTED` advances to `IN_PROGRESS` on its first non-void assignment. An episode already at `READY` that receives a `REWRITE` returns to `IN_PROGRESS`, because a rewrite reopens the script — and it retains its `approved_teleplay_assignment_id`, so the clearance mirror keeps pointing at the draft that was actually approved. Whenever readiness leaves `READY`, the season's `ready_script_count`, `coverage_gap` and `coverage_posture` are recomputed. The episode's `version` increments, and the created assignment is returned with HTTP 201.

### 3.6. Delivery, Notes, Approval and the Void Path

Writers deliver against milestones, not against assignments. A delivery is available only to the writer named on the milestone's owning assignment, and a caller who is not that writer is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The milestone must be in a deliverable status — `PENDING`, `LATE` or `NOTES_ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_DELIVERABLE`. The owning assignment must be in `DRAFTING` or `IN_NOTES`, otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_DRAFTING`, which is what makes an approved or voided assignment permanently undeliverable. And the episode must not be locked, otherwise `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_LOCKED`.

A delivery does several things in a fixed order. If the milestone is still `PENDING` and the logical clock already stands past its `due_date`, it flips to `LATE` first, so lateness is recorded even when the writer never saw a warning. Then `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the milestone becomes `DELIVERED`. Then `days_late` is frozen at the number of whole days by which the UTC calendar date of `delivered_at` exceeds the UTC calendar date of `due_date`, floored at 0 — an on-time or early delivery is 0. The owning assignment's `late_milestone_count` is then recomputed as the number of its milestones that are either sitting in `LATE`, or sitting in `DELIVERED`, `NOTES_ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED` with a `days_late` above 0; lateness once incurred is not erased by a subsequent on-time redelivery of the same milestone. The milestone's `version` increments.

The delivery may then move the assignment, and this is where the revision cycle closes. When the delivered milestone was sitting in `NOTES_ISSUED`, the assignment first moves `IN_NOTES → DRAFTING`, because the writer has resumed work; the same call then checks whether every milestone of the assignment now holds a status in `DELIVERED`, `NOTES_ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`, and if so moves the assignment `DRAFTING → DELIVERED`. A single delivery can therefore execute both transitions in that order, which is exactly how the loop `DRAFTING → DELIVERED → IN_NOTES → DRAFTING → DELIVERED` is traversed. Redelivering a milestone that is in `NOTES_ISSUED` overwrites its `delivered_at` and recomputes `days_late` against its unchanged `due_date`.

Issuing notes belongs to the season's showrunner alone and requires a `notes` payload. The assignment must be in `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_DELIVERED`, and the guild's free-revision allowance must not be exhausted: the assignment's `revision_round` plus one must be at or below the agreement's `max_free_revision_rounds`, and an attempt beyond it is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. With the default allowance of two, notes may be issued twice on one assignment and a third attempt fails. Notes move the assignment `DELIVERED → IN_NOTES` and increment `revision_round` by one, and every milestone of the assignment currently in `DELIVERED` becomes `NOTES_ISSUED`. If the episode happened to be sitting at `READY` when notes were issued, its `script_readiness` returns to `IN_PROGRESS` and the season's coverage figures are recomputed. The assignment's `version` increments.

Approval is where segregation of duties bites. Either a showrunner or a studio executive may approve, and the assignment must be in `DELIVERED` (otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_DELIVERED`) with every one of its milestones in `DELIVERED` or `NOTES_ISSUED`. When the acting role is `Showrunner`, two further conditions hold: the actor must be the `showrunner_writer_id` of the episode's season, otherwise `ERR_NOT_SEASON_SHOWRUNNER`; and the actor must not be the assignment's own writer, otherwise `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` — a showrunner who has written the script must hand the approval to a studio executive, who is under no such restriction. Approval sets the assignment to `APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock, and records `approved_by_role` as `SHOWRUNNER` or `STUDIO_EXECUTIVE` so the audit trail shows which side of the duty split signed off. Every milestone of the assignment becomes `ACCEPTED`.

Approval then propagates in four directions. When the approved `assignment_type` is `TELEPLAY` or `STORY_AND_TELEPLAY`, the episode's `approved_teleplay_assignment_id` is set to this assignment and its `script_readiness` moves `IN_PROGRESS → READY`; approving a `STORY`, `REWRITE` or `PRODUCTION_POLISH` does neither. Independently of readiness, the episode's `credit_status` moves `PENDING → SUBMISSIONS_OPEN` on the episode's *first* approved assignment of any type — a story approval is enough to open the credit window. When the season runs `CHRONOLOGICAL_ON_ACCEPTANCE`, guarantee settlement is re-run for the seat behind this assignment over its approved assignments in approval order; every assignment whose `guarantee_slot_applied` flips has its `guarantee_offset_cents` and `payable_fee_cents` rewritten and appends a `GUARANTEE_REALLOCATION` ledger entry carrying the signed delta of its payable fee, after which the season's `script_fee_committed_cents` is recomputed from the ledger and the seat's `guarantee_slots_consumed` and `guarantee_slots_remaining` are recounted. Under `FORWARD_COMMIT` no re-settlement occurs and no reallocation entry is written. Finally the season's coverage figures are recomputed, and both the assignment's and the episode's `version` increment.

Voiding is the escape hatch, open to a showrunner or a studio executive and requiring a `reason`. The assignment must be non-terminal — `DRAFTING`, `DELIVERED`, `IN_NOTES` or `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING` — and an attempt against an assignment already `APPROVED` or `VOID` is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; a void against a locked episode is rejected with `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_LOCKED`. The assignment becomes `VOID` and its money is unwound exactly: a single `FEE_REVERSAL` entry is appended whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the sum of every prior ledger entry of that assignment, which drives the assignment's own ledger contribution to exactly 0, and the assignment's `guarantee_slot_applied` is cleared to false with `guarantee_offset_cents` and `payable_fee_cents` both set to 0. The season's `script_fee_committed_cents` is recomputed from the ledger — so a create followed by its void returns the season's committed figure to the value it held before the create — and the seat's guarantee counters are recounted, returning the freed slot to `guarantee_slots_remaining`. If the season runs `CHRONOLOGICAL_ON_ACCEPTANCE` and the void freed a slot, settlement is re-run for that seat and any assignment whose slot flips appends its own `GUARANTEE_REALLOCATION` entry.

The void's structural consequences are narrower. The assignment's milestones are frozen at whatever status they held — the milestone lifecycle has no cancelled member — and no further delivery can be made against them, because delivery requires the owning assignment to be in `DRAFTING` or `IN_NOTES`. The episode's `script_readiness` falls `IN_PROGRESS → NOT_STARTED` only when every assignment of that episode is void after this one is applied; otherwise readiness is unchanged. The season's coverage figures are recomputed and the assignment's `version` increments.

### 3.7. Script Clearance for Names, Trademarks and Depictions

Clearance is production legal counsel's exclusive domain: no other role may open a review, raise an item, decide an item or grant a waiver, and an attempt by any other role fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

A review is opened against one script assignment and must name the draft it examines. The `reviewed_draft_milestone_id` must belong to that same assignment and must have a status in `DELIVERED`, `NOTES_ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`; a milestone of another assignment, or one still `PENDING` or `LATE`, is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_IN_ASSIGNMENT`. A new review starts with `posture` at `PENDING`, `risk_score` 0, `item_count` 0, `decided_item_count` 0, `legal_waiver_granted` false and `waiver_memo` null, and it sets the episode's `clearance_posture` to `PENDING`, which is also where a fresh episode already sits. Every later posture change is mirrored onto the episode's `clearance_posture` only when the review's `assignment_id` is the episode's `approved_teleplay_assignment_id` — clearance on a story or a superseded draft moves the review but leaves the episode's mirror alone, which is why a rewrite retains `approved_teleplay_assignment_id` even as readiness reopens.

An item records one exposure: an `item_type` of `NAME`, `TRADEMARK` or `DEPICTION`, a `descriptor` of 1 to 200 characters naming the thing being cleared, and a `scene_reference` of 1 to 80 characters locating it in the script. A new item's `decision` is `SUBMITTED`, its `conditional_note` is null, and its `risk_weight` is 0 — an undecided item carries no risk weight even though its type already implies one. Raising the item increments the review's `item_count`, re-derives the review's `risk_score`, `decided_item_count` and `posture`, mirrors the posture where applicable and increments the review's `version`. Adding an item to a review already in `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED` is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_WAIVED`: a waived review is closed to new exposure.

Deciding an item takes a `decision` of `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED` and an optional `conditional_note`. The item must still be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_ALREADY_DECIDED` — decisions are one-way and are never revised. The parent review must not be `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WAIVED`. A `CONDITIONAL` decision must carry a non-empty `conditional_note`, otherwise `ERR_CONDITIONAL_NOTE_REQUIRED`; the note is stored on the item and remains null for the other two decisions.

The decision sets the item's `risk_weight` to the agreement's `clearance_item_weights` entry for the item's `item_type` multiplied by a decision multiplier of 0 for `CLEARED`, 1 for `CONDITIONAL` and 4 for `BLOCKED`. A cleared item therefore contributes nothing regardless of its type, while a blocked item contributes four times its type weight. The review's `risk_score` is then the sum of `risk_weight` over its items whose `decision` is `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `BLOCKED`, capped at 100 — the cap applies to the sum, not to any individual weight — and `decided_item_count` is the count of those same items. Items still `SUBMITTED` contribute nothing to either figure.

The review's `posture` is then re-derived from scratch, in this order, as a pure function of its items:

1. `BLOCKED` when any item's `decision` is `BLOCKED`, whatever the score.
2. `ESCALATED` when the `risk_score` is *strictly greater* than the agreement's `clearance_escalation_score` (default 30). A score exactly equal to the escalation score does not escalate.
3. `CONDITIONAL` when the `risk_score` is strictly greater than 0.
4. `CLEARED` when `item_count` is at least 1 and `decided_item_count` equals `item_count` — every raised item decided, and every one of them cleared.
5. `PENDING` otherwise, which covers both the review with no items at all and the review whose items are all still undecided.

Because the ladder is re-evaluated from the items each time, a review can move `PENDING → CONDITIONAL → ESCALATED → BLOCKED` as more items are decided, and a single `BLOCKED` item pins the posture at `BLOCKED` no matter what is decided afterwards. Each decision mirrors the resulting posture onto the episode's `clearance_posture` where applicable, recomputes the season's coverage figures, and increments the item's `version`.

The waiver is legal counsel's release valve on a merely conditional review. It requires a `waiver_memo`, and the review's `posture` must be exactly `CONDITIONAL`; a waiver on a `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `ESCALATED` or `BLOCKED` review — or on one already waived — is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_APPLICABLE`. An escalated or blocked review can never be waived, so the only route out of those postures is a different review over a different draft. The waiver moves `posture` to `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED`, sets `legal_waiver_granted` true, stores the `waiver_memo`, mirrors `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED` onto the episode's `clearance_posture`, recomputes the season's coverage figures — the episode now counts as a ready script — and increments the review's `version`.

### 3.8. Script Coverage and the Production Lock

The season's coverage figures are the studio's picture of whether it has enough shootable scripts. They are recomputed by the system on every transition that can change an episode's `script_readiness` or `clearance_posture` — assignment create, approve and void, the seat-release cascade, room wrap, a clearance item decision, a clearance waiver, and the lock itself — and they are never patched directly by any endpoint.

An episode counts toward `ready_script_count` only when *both* dimensions permit: its `script_readiness` is `READY` or `LOCKED`, **and** its `clearance_posture` is `CLEARED` or `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED`. An episode with an approved teleplay but a `PENDING`, `CONDITIONAL`, `ESCALATED` or `BLOCKED` clearance posture does not count, and neither does a fully cleared episode whose script is only `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Locked episodes keep counting — locking never lowers coverage. The count is order-insensitive.

From that count, `coverage_gap` is the season's `ready_script_target` minus `ready_script_count`, floored at 0, so the gap never goes negative when a season over-delivers. The `coverage_posture` is then `COVERED` when the gap is exactly 0, `AT_RISK` when the gap is 1 or 2, and `CRITICAL` for any gap above 2.

Locking an episode for production is the irreversible act that freezes a script, and it is open to a studio executive or to the season's own showrunner — a showrunner acting on another season's episode is refused with `ERR_NOT_SEASON_SHOWRUNNER` while a studio executive may lock any episode. Three business gates then apply in a fixed order. First the episode's `script_readiness` must be `READY`, otherwise `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_READY`. Second the clearance gate: the episode's `clearance_posture` must be `CLEARED` or `CONDITIONAL_WAIVED`, and a failure is reported as `ERR_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED` when the posture is `BLOCKED` and as `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_CLEARED` for `PENDING`, `CONDITIONAL` or `ESCALATED` — two distinct codes for one gate, so the caller can tell a fatal exposure from an unfinished one. Third the coverage gate: the season's `ready_script_count`, counting this episode's own contribution, must be at or above `ready_script_target`, otherwise `ERR_SCRIPT_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. The comparison is inclusive: a season whose target is 6 may lock when the count reaches exactly 6, and the episode being locked is included in the count because it is already `READY` and cleared when the gate is evaluated.

A successful lock sets `script_readiness` to `LOCKED`, stamps `locked_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the season's coverage figures and increments the episode's `version`. `LOCKED` is terminal on the readiness dimension: no rewrite, no polish, no assignment, no delivery and no void may touch the episode afterwards, and every such attempt is rejected with `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_LOCKED`. The credit dimension is unaffected — credits on a locked episode continue to move through submission, closure, arbitration and determination exactly as before.

### 3.9. Credit Determination and Guild Arbitration

The credit dimension of an episode runs `PENDING → SUBMISSIONS_OPEN → SUBMISSIONS_CLOSED → IN_ARBITRATION → DETERMINED` and never reads the script dimension beyond the single fact that an approved assignment exists. It opens on the episode's first approved assignment of any type, as described above.

A writer claims credit on their own behalf. The submitted `writer_id` must equal the acting user's id, and a claim filed for someone else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The `claim_basis` is one of `WRITTEN_BY`, `STORY_BY` or `TELEPLAY_BY`, and `claimed_contribution_pct` is an integer from 1 to 100 — it is a claim, not a share, so the claims of the several writers on one episode need not sum to anything in particular. The episode's `credit_status` must be `SUBMISSIONS_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and one writer may file only one claim per episode: a second claim on the same episode is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION`. A new submission's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, its `provisional_share_pct` is 0, its `arbitrated_share_pct` is null and its `receives_credit` is false. The episode's `version` increments.

Closing the window belongs to the season's showrunner or to a guild administrator. The episode's `credit_status` must be `SUBMISSIONS_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and the episode must have at least one submission, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBMISSIONS`. The close then applies the eligibility filter: a submission becomes `ELIGIBLE` when its writer holds at least one script assignment on that episode with status `APPROVED`, and `INELIGIBLE` otherwise. A writer who claims credit but whose script was voided, or who never held an assignment at all, is filtered out here. If the filter leaves *no* eligible submission the whole call is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBMISSIONS` and nothing is persisted — no status is written and the episode stays in `SUBMISSIONS_OPEN`.

With at least one eligible submission, provisional shares are computed by the normalisation procedure below with each writer's weight taken from their own `claimed_contribution_pct`. Ineligible submissions contribute no weight and receive no share; their `provisional_share_pct` stays 0. The normalisation is:

1. The denominator is the sum of the weights over the eligible writers.
2. For each eligible writer, the raw share is their weight divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, kept unrounded.
3. Each writer's floor share is the raw share truncated toward zero to a whole percentage point.
4. The remainder is 100 minus the sum of the floor shares.
5. The eligible writers are ordered by the fractional part of their raw share, descending, with ties broken by their submission's `created_at` ascending.
6. Exactly one percentage point is added to each of the first *remainder* writers in that order; the rest keep their floor share.

The shares are therefore always whole percentage points summing to exactly 100. For example, three eligible writers claiming 50, 30 and 25 give a denominator of 105 and raw shares of 47.619…, 28.571… and 23.809…; the floors are 47, 28 and 23, summing to 98, so the remainder is 2; ordering by fractional part descending puts the 25-claimant (0.809…) first and the 50-claimant (0.619…) second, so those two each gain a point and the final split is 48, 28 and 24.

The close writes each eligible submission's `provisional_share_pct`, writes the whole writer-id-to-share map into the episode's `provisional_credit_shares`, sets `receives_credit` on each eligible submission according to whether its provisional share is at or above the agreement's `credit_share_threshold_pct` (default 33, an inclusive comparison — a share of exactly 33 earns credit), moves `credit_status` to `SUBMISSIONS_CLOSED` and increments the episode's `version`. No writer's `on_screen_credit_count` moves at close; that counter only ever moves on determination.

Convening arbitration is a guild administrator's act. The episode's `credit_status` must be `SUBMISSIONS_CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and an episode may have at most one arbitration ever — a second is rejected with `ERR_ARBITRATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The credit-status check is the step-14 state check of §2.11 and the at-most-one rule is step-18 uniqueness, so the status check is applied first: convening a second panel on an episode the first convene already moved to `IN_ARBITRATION` reports `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The `arbiter_writer_ids` payload must hold exactly three distinct ids of existing writers, and no arbiter may be interested in the outcome: an arbiter who holds a non-void script assignment on the episode, or who has filed any credit submission on it at all — eligible or not — disqualifies the panel with `ERR_ARBITER_IS_PARTICIPANT`, as do three ids that are not distinct. The episode must also have at least one eligible submission to arbitrate over, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBMISSIONS`.

The arbitration is created in `INITIATED` and auto-advanced to `STATEMENTS_OPEN` inside the same call, so a caller never observes `INITIATED`. Its `participant_writer_ids` are the writer ids of the episode's `ELIGIBLE` submissions ordered by submission `created_at` ascending — that ordering is load-bearing, because it fixes the key set every ballot must match. `votes_cast_count` starts at 0, `vote_ledger` at an empty map, `leading_writer_id` and `determined_credit_string` at null, `concurrence_pct` at 0 and `outcome` at `NONE`. The episode moves to `IN_ARBITRATION` and its `version` increments.

Ballots are cast by the arbiters themselves, acting as writers. The acting id must appear in `arbiter_writer_ids`, otherwise `ERR_ARBITER_NOT_ON_PANEL`; the arbitration must be in `STATEMENTS_OPEN` or `PANEL_VOTING`, otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATION_CLOSED`; and an arbiter who has already appeared as a key of `vote_ledger` is refused with `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_CAST` — ballots are append-only and are never revised. The `vote_shares` payload must have keys that are exactly the `participant_writer_ids` — no missing participant, no stranger — with integer values summing to exactly 100, otherwise `ERR_VOTE_SHARES_INVALID`.

The first ballot moves the arbitration `STATEMENTS_OPEN → PANEL_VOTING`; the second and third are self-loops that leave it in `PANEL_VOTING`. Each ballot is recorded in `vote_ledger` under the arbiter's id, `votes_cast_count` increments, and two figures are recomputed over the ballots cast so far. Each participant's panel mean is the sum of that participant's share across every recorded ballot divided by `votes_cast_count`. The `leading_writer_id` is the participant with the highest panel mean, with ties broken by submission `created_at` ascending. The `concurrence_pct` is the count of recorded ballots whose share *for the leading writer* lies within `arbitration_concurrence_band_pct` percentage points (default 10, an inclusive absolute comparison) of the leading writer's panel mean, divided by `votes_cast_count`, rounded half up to three decimals. Nothing else moves — the arbitration stays in `PANEL_VOTING` however many ballots arrive, and the shares are not written to any submission. The arbitration's `version` increments.

The determination is the fork. It belongs to the guild administrator, takes an optional `policy_override` boolean, and the arbitration must not already be in `DETERMINED`, otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATION_CLOSED`.

Called without `policy_override`, or with it false, all three ballots must be in: `votes_cast_count` below 3 is refused with `ERR_PANEL_INCOMPLETE`. The branch then turns on concurrence. When `concurrence_pct` is at or above the agreement's `arbitration_concurrence_pct` (default 0.667, inclusive), the panel governs: the arbitration moves to `DETERMINED` and shares are recomputed by the same six-step normalisation as at close, but with each participant's weight taken from their *panel mean* rather than their own claim. Identical submissions therefore produce a different split whenever the panel's view differs from the claim ratio. Each eligible submission's `arbitrated_share_pct` is written, the map goes into the episode's `final_credit_shares`, and `outcome` is `UPHELD` when the arbitrated share map is identical to `provisional_credit_shares` and `REVISED` when it differs in any entry. When `concurrence_pct` falls below the threshold, the panel has failed: the arbitration moves to `DEADLOCKED`, `outcome` stays `NONE`, no `arbitrated_share_pct` is written anywhere, `final_credit_shares` stays empty and the episode remains in `IN_ARBITRATION`. The two branches share a trigger and diverge completely in what they write.

For example, with three ballots giving the leading writer 50, 45 and 40, the panel mean is 45 and all three ballots sit within a 10-point band, so `concurrence_pct` is 1.000 and the determination stands. With ballots of 70, 45 and 20 the mean is 45, only the middle ballot lies within the band, `concurrence_pct` is 0.333, and the panel deadlocks.

Called with `policy_override` true, the arbitration must be in `DEADLOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_OVERRIDE_NOT_APPLICABLE`; the same error is returned when the override is omitted or false on an arbitration that is already `DEADLOCKED`, so a deadlock can only ever be resolved by an explicit override. The override moves the arbitration `DEADLOCKED → POLICY_REVIEW`, sets `outcome` to `DEADLOCK_RESOLVED_BY_POLICY`, and applies the guild's policy fallback: each eligible submission's `arbitrated_share_pct` is set equal to its own `provisional_share_pct` and the episode's `final_credit_shares` receives the provisional map unchanged. No renormalisation occurs on this branch — the claims-based split simply becomes final.

Both terminal branches — `DETERMINED` and `POLICY_REVIEW` — then close the episode identically. The episode's `credit_status` moves to `DETERMINED`, and the `determined_credit_string` is assembled and stored on both the arbitration and the episode: it is the `legal_name` of every writer whose `receives_credit` is true, joined by the three-character separator " & ", in descending order of final share with ties broken by submission `created_at` ascending. Each eligible submission's `receives_credit` is re-evaluated against its effective final share and the agreement's `credit_share_threshold_pct`, so a writer who held provisional credit can lose it on a revised split and a writer who did not can gain it. Every writer whose `receives_credit` flips to true has their `on_screen_credit_count` incremented by one — the counter moves once, at determination, and never at close. Both the arbitration's and the episode's `version` increment. `DETERMINED` and `POLICY_REVIEW` are terminal arbitration states, and `DETERMINED` is terminal on the episode's credit dimension: no further ballot, determination or submission is possible.

## 4. API Endpoints

Every endpoint below requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1, and every request body is JSON. Resource creations return HTTP 201 with the created resource; all other successful calls — state transitions, recomputations and reads — return HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource. List endpoints accept the pagination and filter parameters described in §2.3 and return an array in `data` with the pagination fields populated in `meta`.
