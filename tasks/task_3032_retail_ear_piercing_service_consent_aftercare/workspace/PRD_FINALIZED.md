# Retail Ear Piercing Service API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Piercing a customer's ear is a regulated clinical act performed inside a retail store, and the software that supports it must satisfy two masters at once: the shop-floor pace of an intake desk handling appointments and walk-ins, and the evidentiary discipline of a health inspector auditing consent instruments, sterilization logs and adverse-reaction files years later. This document specifies the API that governs that work — the intake surface where customers and their statutory consents are captured, the roster surface where piercers are certified and recertified, the inventory surface that traces every single-use cartridge back to the autoclave cycle that released it, and the aftercare surface where healing is assessed, complications are logged and refunds are settled.

The design is deliberately conservative about three things. First, nothing at a store proceeds unless the store's derived compliance posture and the assigned piercer's live certification both allow it, so a failed sterilization cycle or an unresolved critical incident ripples outward into the operations it should stop. Second, jurisdiction is not a label but a rule source: the minimum ages at which a customer may consent alone or at all, the anatomical sites a regime permits for minors, whether a guardian must physically attend, the identification instrument that must be presented, the sterilization protocol a piercer must be certified against, and the retention horizon that decides whether an aftercare solution kit is mandatory all come from the governing jurisdiction profile and are re-evaluated against it. Third, every state change is auditable: certifications, consents, cycles, lots, appointments, screenings, sessions, incidents and healing outcomes each move through an explicit state machine, and the derived fields those transitions feed — eligibility scores, incident counts, store posture, available cartridge units — are recomputed rather than asserted.

A faithful implementation therefore has to reconcile derived state on writes, reject stale writes rather than silently overwrite them, honor an evaluator-controlled logical clock for every time-sensitive window, and keep money in integer cents. The remainder of this document is organized as a contract: the global, cross-cutting principles come first, the business workflows follow, and the endpoint and error catalogues close it out.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to the entire API surface. Where a rule names a resource field, that field is the authoritative spelling used in every request and response body.

### 2.1. Caller Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting caller and `X-User-Role` states their operational capacity. A request whose `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` header is absent or malformed is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` and HTTP 400. A request whose role is valid but is not permitted for the requested operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` and HTTP 403. `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are the only authentication inputs; no session token or bearer credential is defined.

The system recognizes eight roles, spelled exactly as follows: `IntakeAssociate`, `Piercer`, `StoreManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SterilizationTechnician`, `Customer`, `Anonymous`, and `AnyAuthenticated`.

Identity is resolved differently depending on the role named in `X-User-Role`. When `X-User-Role` is `Piercer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Piercer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Piercer` compare that resource's piercer reference to `X-User-ID`, and in particular a recertification submission is accepted only when the submitting caller's `X-User-ID` equals the certification's `piercer_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare that resource's customer reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeAssociate`, `StoreManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SterilizationTechnician`, `Anonymous` or `AnyAuthenticated`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The `Anonymous` role exists only so that an unauthenticated self-registration surface can exist, and the `AnyAuthenticated` role denotes any caller presenting a valid role header on the read-only operational surface.

Role assignment is exclusive by operation. `IntakeAssociate` owns customer registration, consent capture and verification, appointment booking and its intake-stage transitions, and aftercare credit redemption. `Piercer` owns anatomy screening, service execution, session creation, healing assessment, downgrade and re-pierce, healing-outcome downgrade and re-pierce claims, and recertification submission. `StoreManager` owns piercer registration and roster transitions, cartridge-lot registration, and refund settlement. `ComplianceOfficer` owns jurisdiction profiles, certification issuance and revocation, store creation and posture controls, sterilization cycle quarantine/release/recall, lot discard, consent revocation, screening override, the incident ladder, healing-outcome closure, customer flag clearing and closure, and the logical clock. `SterilizationTechnician` owns only opening and closing sterilization cycles. `Customer` shares only the aftercare follow-up acknowledgement. `Anonymous` shares only customer registration. `AnyAuthenticated` shares every read-only listing and retrieval endpoint. An operation attempted by a role that does not own it fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps how many items are returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and it must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` states how many leading items are skipped; when omitted it defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint description says otherwise, list endpoints return rows ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the response envelope.

### 2.4. Jurisdictional Regimes and the Statutory Consent Instrument

A `Jurisdiction` profile is the rule source for everything that happens under it. Each profile is identified by a `state_code` that must consist of exactly two uppercase letters and must be unique across `Jurisdiction` rows; a value violating either condition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A profile carries the three age boundaries that govern consent — `min_age_self_consent`, the youngest age in whole years at which a customer may sign their own consent; `min_age_with_guardian`, the youngest age at which a piercing may proceed at all, with a verified guardian consent; and `min_age_absolute`, the floor below which a piercing is refused outright no matter who consents. It additionally carries `permitted_sites`, the anatomical sites the regime permits for customers below `min_age_self_consent`, which must be a subset of `LOBE`, `UPPER_LOBE`, `HELIX`, `TRAGUS`, `INDUSTRIAL` and `NOSTRIL`; `requires_guardian_presence`, which is true when the guardian must be physically present in store and false when a notarized consent suffices; `record_retention_years`, the number of years piercing records must be retained for inspection; `consent_form_version`, the version tag of the statutory consent instrument; and `sterilization_protocol_code`, the instrument sterilization protocol code the regime mandates. A profile is created in `status` `ACTIVE` and is superseded — moving to `status` `SUPERSEDED` — by the administrative supersession operation, which takes a `successor_state_code` and returns the superseded profile. Only a jurisdiction whose `status` is `ACTIVE` may gate an appointment confirmation.

Age is measured in whole years at the service date and is evaluated in a fixed ladder. A customer whose age is below `min_age_absolute` is refused outright. A customer whose age is below `min_age_self_consent` may not self-consent and requires a consent whose `consent_type` is `GUARDIAN_PRESENT`, `GUARDIAN_NOTARIZED` or `COURT_ORDER`. A customer whose age is below `min_age_with_guardian` is rejected with `ERR_AGE_BELOW_JURISDICTION_MINIMUM` and HTTP 422. That same error and status are the rejection for the absolute floor.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Derived Aggregate Fields

Every entity in this API is created with a common set of system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and is unique across the entity's own table. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording first creation. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the last modification. `version` is an integer that increments by one on every mutation of the row. There is no resource in this system that lacks any of these four properties; `Jurisdiction`, `StoreLocation`, `Piercer`, `CertificationRecord`, `SterilizationCycle`, `CartridgeLot`, `Customer`, `ConsentRecord`, `Appointment`, `AnatomyScreening`, `PiercingSession`, `IncidentReport` and `HealingOutcome` all carry them.

A mutation is any change to a row's stored value, including a change to a derived field. A recomputation of a derived aggregate is therefore a mutation that refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`, which is what makes the concurrency rules of §2.9 meaningful. Several mutations are explicitly version-bearing and are called out here because clients depend on observing them: issuing a certification increments the target `Piercer`'s `version`; registering a cartridge lot increments the target `StoreLocation`'s `version`; recording a screening increments the target `Appointment`'s `version`; rescheduling an appointment increments that `Appointment`'s `version`; filing an incident report increments the target `PiercingSession`'s `version`; and creating a healing outcome also increments the target `PiercingSession`'s `version`.

Derived fields are server-computed and are never accepted from a client. `StoreLocation` carries `compliance_posture` and `open_incident_count`. `Piercer` carries `eligibility_score`, `active_certification_id` and `assigned_session_count`. `CertificationRecord` carries `recertification_due_at` and `attempted_practical_count`. `SterilizationCycle` carries `quarantine_scope_units`. `CartridgeLot` carries `units_available`, `units_consumed` and `units_quarantined`. `Customer` carries `aftercare_credit_cents`, `refunded_cents` and `retained_cents`. `ConsentRecord` carries `required_identification_type` and `expires_at`. `Appointment` carries `reschedule_count` and `governing_screening_id`. `AnatomyScreening` carries `refusal_reason_code`. `PiercingSession` carries `follow_up_reminder_due_at` and `net_fee_cents`. `IncidentReport` carries `refund_amount_cents`. `HealingOutcome` carries `downgrade_credited_cents`, `re_pierce_eligible` and `re_pierce_claim_count`.

Initial values are fixed at creation. `Jurisdiction.status` starts `ACTIVE`. `StoreLocation.compliance_posture` starts `OPEN`, `StoreLocation.open_incident_count` starts 0, and `StoreLocation.status` starts `OPEN`. `Piercer.eligibility_score` starts 100, `Piercer.assigned_session_count` starts 0, and `Piercer.status` starts `ACTIVE`. `CertificationRecord.attempted_practical_count` starts 0 and `CertificationRecord.status` starts `VALID`. `SterilizationCycle.quarantine_scope_units` starts 0 and `SterilizationCycle.status` starts `IN_PROGRESS`. `CartridgeLot.units_available`, `CartridgeLot.units_consumed` and `CartridgeLot.units_quarantined` start 0 and `CartridgeLot.status` starts `AVAILABLE`. `Customer.aftercare_credit_cents`, `Customer.refunded_cents` and `Customer.retained_cents` start 0 and `Customer.status` starts `ACTIVE`. `ConsentRecord.guardian_verification_status` starts `NOT_REQUIRED` and `ConsentRecord.consent_status` starts `PENDING`. `Appointment.reschedule_count` starts 0 and `Appointment.status` starts `BOOKED`. `AnatomyScreening.outcome` starts `PENDING`. `PiercingSession.follow_up_acknowledged` starts false, `PiercingSession.service_status` starts `IN_PROGRESS` and `PiercingSession.healing_status` starts `HEALING`. `IncidentReport.escalation_status` starts `LOGGED`, `IncidentReport.refund_amount_cents` starts 0 and `IncidentReport.refund_status` starts `NONE`. `HealingOutcome.healing_result` starts `PENDING_ASSESSMENT`, `HealingOutcome.downgrade_credited_cents` starts 0, `HealingOutcome.re_pierce_eligible` starts false, `HealingOutcome.re_pierce_claim_count` starts 0 and `HealingOutcome.status` starts `ASSESSED`.

### 2.6. Governing-Record Selector Policies

Three computed references are produced by named selector policies rather than by explicit client input. Each is deterministic, and in no case is `id` ever used as a tie-break.

The first is `active_certification_id` on `Piercer`, produced by selector policy `piercer_governing_certification`. Its candidate set is the `CertificationRecord` rows whose `piercer_id` equals this piercer's `id` and whose `status` is `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`. When that set is empty the candidate set falls back to the rows whose `status` is `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`. Candidates are ranked by `certification_level_order` descending, where `INSTRUCTOR` ranks 4, `SENIOR` ranks 3, `STANDARD` ranks 2 and `APPRENTICE` ranks 1; ties are broken by `status_order` descending, where `VALID` ranks 5, `EXPIRING_SOON` ranks 4, `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` ranks 3, `EXPIRED` ranks 2 and `REVOKED` ranks 1. Remaining ties are broken by `issued_at` descending and then `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. When the candidate set is empty the selector resolves to null, and every certification gate in this document then fails with `ERR_PIERCER_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

The second is `governing_screening_id` on `Appointment`, produced by selector policy `appointment_governing_screening`. Its candidate set is the `AnatomyScreening` rows whose `appointment_id` equals this appointment's `id` and whose `outcome` is `CLEARED`, `REFUSED` or `ESCALATED`. Candidates are ranked by `outcome_order` descending, where `CLEARED` ranks 3, `ESCALATED` ranks 2 and `REFUSED` ranks 1. Remaining ties are broken by `created_at` descending and then by the assessed marker order, never by `id`. When the candidate set is empty the selector resolves to null, and the transition of an appointment from `SCREENED` to `IN_SERVICE` then fails with `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED`.

The third is the cartridge lot chosen when a service begins without an explicitly supplied lot, produced by selector policy `store_available_lot`. Its candidate set is the `CartridgeLot` rows whose `store_location_id` equals the store's `id`, whose `site_type` equals the appointment's `site_requested`, whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `units_available` is at least 1, and whose `expiration_date` is later than the current clock instant. Candidates are ranked by `units_available` descending and then by `expiration_date` ascending. Remaining ties are broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. When the candidate set is empty the operation fails with `ERR_CARTRIDGE_LOT_UNAVAILABLE`.

### 2.7. Store Posture, Sterilization Assurance and Lot Traceability

A `StoreLocation` is identified by its `name` and carries `jurisdiction_id`, the regime the store operates under, and `piercing_room_count`, the number of licensed piercing rooms available for sessions. Posture is recomputed by the calculation `store_posture_computation`, which counts, over the incidents reachable through the store's sessions, those whose `escalation_status` is `LOGGED`, `TRIAGED`, `ESCALATED` or `REOPENED`, and additionally adds the store's failed-cycle lot exposure, one unit for each `CartridgeLot` bound to a `SterilizationCycle` of that store whose `status` is `FAILED`; that total is the store's `open_incident_count`. Among those same rows it counts the ones whose `severity` is `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY`, and separately it counts the store's `SterilizationCycle` rows whose `status` is `FAILED`. The posture is then decided in a pinned order: when at least one critical incident exists, or at least two failed cycles exist, the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, when the open incident count is at least 3, or at least one failed cycle exists, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. The three thresholds are fixed constants: `RESTRICTION_INCIDENT_THRESHOLD` is 3, `RESTRICTION_FAILED_CYCLE_THRESHOLD` is 1, and `FREEZE_FAILED_CYCLE_THRESHOLD` is 2. Because the freeze test is applied first, a store with both a failed cycle and a single critical incident reports `FROZEN` rather than `RESTRICTED`. The boundaries are inclusive: an open incident count of exactly 3 restricts, and a count of 2 does not. Posture is recomputed whenever any incident escalation status changes at the store, whenever any sterilization cycle status changes at the store, and whenever a cartridge lot is registered against the store. The store's posture read is itself a reconciliation point: reading a store's posture recomputes it before returning the store's `compliance_posture` and `open_incident_count`.

While a store's posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, that store may not begin a new service; the refusal is `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_SERVICE` with HTTP 409. While a store's roster `status` is `CLOSED`, no new service may begin there either. Posture transitions are driven automatically and administratively. A store whose `status` is `OPEN` moves automatically to `RESTRICTED` when its `open_incident_count` reaches 3 or when at least one bound sterilization cycle is `FAILED`; that automatic transition writes `compliance_posture` to `RESTRICTED` and recomputes the posture. A `RESTRICTED` store returns to `OPEN` only through the administrative posture-clearing operation, which requires `open_incident_count` equal to 0 and no bound `SterilizationCycle` in `IN_PROGRESS`, `FAILED` or `QUARANTINED`. Freezing reconciles the store's posture before that precondition is decided, so the freeze is refused only for a store whose recomputed posture is already `FROZEN`, and it drives a store in `status` `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` whose recomputed `compliance_posture` is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` to `compliance_posture` `FROZEN`; every appointment at that store in `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` is cascaded to `CANCELLED`, and the affected customers' `status` and `reschedule_count` are left unchanged. Unfreezing a frozen store moves it to `RESTRICTED` and never directly back to `OPEN`, so a store must always be cleared explicitly. Closing a store requires that no appointment at that store is in `CHECKED_IN`, `SCREENED` or `IN_SERVICE`; closing cascades every `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment at the store to `CANCELLED` and moves every `Piercer` whose `home_store_id` equals that store and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`.

Cartridge inventory is bound to sterilization evidence. A `SterilizationCycle` records `store_location_id`, `autoclave_unit_id`, `protocol_code`, its `cycle_start_at` and `cycle_end_at` instants, the peak chamber `temperature_celsius`, and the `biological_indicator_passed` flag. The cycle occupies the closed interval from `cycle_start_at` to `cycle_end_at`, both endpoints inclusive. A cycle starts `IN_PROGRESS` and may be closed as `PASSED` only when `temperature_celsius` is at least 134.0, `biological_indicator_passed` is true, and the closed cycle interval does not overlap another cycle on the same `autoclave_unit_id`; an overlap is rejected with `ERR_AUTOCLAVE_CYCLE_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409. Two cycles on the same autoclave unit whose closed intervals merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do still overlap and are rejected, because both endpoints of the interval are inclusive; supplied cycle bounds are truncated by flooring to the containing second before comparison. When a cycle closes as `PASSED`, every `CartridgeLot` bound to it becomes `AVAILABLE` with `units_available` equal to its `units_received`. A cycle closes as `FAILED` when `temperature_celsius` is below 134.0 or `biological_indicator_passed` is false, and the failure propagates in a four-step chain: every bound `CartridgeLot` becomes `QUARANTINED`; for each such lot the owning store's `open_incident_count` increases by one and that store's posture is recomputed; every `Piercer` whose `home_store_id` equals the lot's store and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` loses 20 points from `eligibility_score`; and for each such piercer whose `eligibility_score` has dropped below 60, every `Customer` holding an active session performed by that piercer is flagged. A `PASSED` cycle can be placed in `QUARANTINED`, which moves its bound lots to `QUARANTINED`, records the exposure in `quarantine_scope_units`, and, for any bound lot whose units were already consumed by a session in `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, files an `IncidentReport` of `severity` `MODERATE` against that session. A `QUARANTINED` cycle can be `RELEASED` only when `temperature_celsius` is at least 134.0 and `biological_indicator_passed` is true, which returns every bound `QUARANTINED` lot to `AVAILABLE`. A `RELEASED` cycle can be `RECALL`ed back to `QUARANTINED`, which pushes every bound lot to `QUARANTINED` as well, making the `RELEASED`/`QUARANTINED` pair cyclic. Quarantine exposure is measured when quarantine is applied by the calculation `quarantine_unit_exposure`: `quarantine_scope_units` is the sum of `units_available` over every `CartridgeLot` whose `sterilization_cycle_id` equals the quarantined cycle's `id` at that instant.

A `CartridgeLot` records `store_location_id`, `sterilization_cycle_id`, `lot_number`, the `site_type` it is designed for (one of `LOBE`, `UPPER_LOBE`, `HELIX`, `TRAGUS`, `INDUSTRIAL`, `NOSTRIL`), `units_received` and `expiration_date`. Its availability is governed by the calculation `cartridge_lot_availability`: `units_available` equals `units_received` minus `units_consumed` minus `units_quarantined`, floored at 0. A lot starts `AVAILABLE`, all of its received units being available once its bound cycle passes. When its bound cycle enters `FAILED` or `QUARANTINED` the lot becomes `QUARANTINED`, `units_quarantined` is set to the lot's then-current `units_available`, and `units_available` becomes 0. When its bound cycle enters `RELEASED` or `PASSED` and the lot's `expiration_date` is later than the current clock instant, the lot returns to `AVAILABLE` with `units_available` set to `units_quarantined` and `units_quarantined` reset to 0. A lot becomes `DEPLETED` automatically once `units_available` reaches 0. Discarding is an administrative act: a lot in `AVAILABLE` may be discarded when its `expiration_date` is at or before the current clock instant or when its `status` is `QUARANTINED`, and a lot in `QUARANTINED` may be discarded when its `expiration_date` is at or before the current clock instant or when its bound cycle is `FAILED` with all three practical retests exhausted. Discarding sets `status` to `DISCARDED` and, from the quarantined branch, sets `units_available` to 0. `DEPLETED` and `DISCARDED` are terminal.

### 2.8. Monetary Arithmetic and Whole-Cent Precision

All money in this system is carried in whole cents as integers. Inputs such as `base_fee_cents` are integers, and every monetary output — `net_fee_cents`, `refund_amount_cents`, `downgrade_credited_cents`, `aftercare_credit_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `retained_cents` — is an integer number of cents. Where a formula scales an amount by a rate, the scale step is floored to an integer number of cents unless the formula explicitly says to round half up, so no fractional cents are ever carried forward into a later step. The single exception is the downgrade credit in §3.6, which rounds half up per site. The rates and floors are fixed constants: an aftercare solution kit contributes 1200 cents (`AFTERCARE_KIT_FEE_CENTS`); the non-refundable retention floor is 500 cents (`AFTERCARE_RETAIN_FLOOR_CENTS`); the moderate and severe refund rates are 0.50 and 0.75 (`MODERATE_RATE`, `SEVERE_RATE`); the discount tiers turn on pre-discount subtotals of 4000 and 8000 cents (`TIER_1_FLOOR_CENTS`, `TIER_2_FLOOR_CENTS`) at rates 0.10 and 0.15 (`TIER_1_RATE`, `TIER_2_RATE`); the re-pierce discount rate is 0.20 (`RE_PIERCE_RATE`); the site minimum is 1500 cents (`SITE_MINIMUM_CENTS`); the downgrade rate is 0.25 (`DOWNGRADE_RATE`); the site-complexity surcharge is capped at 2500 cents (`MAX_SURCHARGE_CENTS`), charged 350 cents per contraindication beyond the first (`PER_CONTRAINDICATION_CENTS`) and 200 cents per requested site beyond the first (`PER_EXTRA_SITE_CENTS`).

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Six write operations in this API are version-guarded. Each requires a non-optional `expected_<parent>_version` field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent entity named below. When the supplied value does not equal the parent row's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no part of the operation is applied.

Registering an anatomy screening is guarded against the appointment it screens: the request carries `expected_appointment_version` and must match the target `Appointment`'s current `version`. Filing an incident report is guarded against the session it concerns: the request carries `expected_session_version` and must match the target `PiercingSession`'s current `version`. Creating a healing outcome is likewise guarded against the session it concerns and likewise carries `expected_session_version` and must match that `PiercingSession`'s current `version`. Rescheduling an appointment is guarded against the appointment being moved: the request carries `expected_appointment_version` and must match that `Appointment`'s current `version`. Registering a cartridge lot is guarded against the store it is registered under: the request carries `expected_store_version` and must match the target `StoreLocation`'s current `version`. Issuing a certification record is guarded against the piercer being certified: the request carries `expected_piercer_version` and must match the target `Piercer`'s current `version`.

The rejection is framed purely as a stale-version rejection: the client's observed version no longer describes the row, so the write would be applied against a superseded state, and it is refused rather than merged. No write endpoint outside the six above accepts a version field, and the version field of an entity that is not a parent in this list is never an input.

### 2.10. Certification Cadence and Eligibility Scoring

A `CertificationRecord` binds a `Piercer` to a `certification_level` — one of `APPRENTICE`, `STANDARD`, `SENIOR`, `INSTRUCTOR` — and to a `sterilization_protocol_code`, with an `issued_at` and an `expires_at`. Its `recertification_due_at` is computed as `expires_at` minus the recertification lead time of 30 days (`RECERTIFICATION_LEAD_DAYS`); the record enters `EXPIRING_SOON` exactly when the clock reaches `recertification_due_at`, and that boundary is inclusive, so at the instant the clock equals `recertification_due_at` the record is already expiring soon. A successful recertification sets `issued_at` to the current clock instant and `expires_at` to the current clock instant plus the certification validity in months (`certification_validity_months`), resets `attempted_practical_count` to 0, and recomputes `recertification_due_at`. Three practical attempts are permitted in a cycle: a failed attempt moves the record to `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` and increments `attempted_practical_count`, a record in `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` with a count below 3 may return to `EXPIRING_SOON`, and a record whose `attempted_practical_count` reaches exactly 3 moves automatically to `EXPIRED`. A certification whose `status` is `EXPIRING_SOON` and whose clock passes `expires_at` also moves automatically to `EXPIRED`. Whenever a record whose `certification_level` is `STANDARD`, `SENIOR` or `INSTRUCTOR` becomes `EXPIRED` or is revoked, the owning `Piercer` is cascaded to `SUSPENDED`.

`Piercer.eligibility_score` is recomputed by the calculation `piercer_eligibility_score` on every certification mutation and every incident mutation affecting that piercer. The score starts from a base of 100 (`BASE_SCORE`). Each `CertificationRecord` belonging to the piercer contributes, in ascending `created_at` order, 0 when its `status` is `VALID`, minus 10 when `EXPIRING_SOON`, minus 15 when `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, minus 30 when `EXPIRED`, and minus 40 when `REVOKED`. Each `IncidentReport` attributable to the piercer contributes, in ascending `reported_at` order, minus 5 when its `severity` is `MINOR`, minus 12 when `MODERATE`, minus 25 when `SEVERE`, and minus 50 when `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY`. A completion bonus of plus 8 (`COMPLETION_BONUS`) applies for each `PiercingSession` in `COMPLETED` service status performed by the piercer since that piercer's most recent such incident. The raw score is the base plus those contributions plus the bonus, and the stored `eligibility_score` is that raw score clamped last to the range 0 (`FLOOR`) to 100 (`CEILING`). The stored score is the rolling-window variant: it counts only certification rows and incident rows whose relevant timestamp falls within the trailing eligibility window of `eligibility_window_days`, with older rows contributing 0. A separate cumulative chronological variant counts every row ever recorded with no window and is reported, alongside the stored score, by the piercer eligibility audit read; the two can differ by any amount, and a piercer with two historic `SEVERE` incidents reports a lower cumulative value than the stored rolling value.

A `Piercer` moves automatically from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` when `eligibility_score` is below 60 — that low-score test is evaluated first — or when no `CertificationRecord` of that piercer declares a `certification_level` of `STANDARD`, `SENIOR` or `INSTRUCTOR` with `status` `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`; the transition writes `status` to `SUSPENDED` and recomputes `active_certification_id`. A `SUSPENDED` piercer is reinstated to `ACTIVE` administratively only when `eligibility_score` is at least 60 and at least one certification declares `certification_level` of `STANDARD`, `SENIOR` or `INSTRUCTOR` with `status` `VALID`. A piercer in `ACTIVE` or in `SUSPENDED` may be deactivated to `INACTIVE` only when `assigned_session_count` is 0; deactivation revokes every `CertificationRecord` of that piercer whose `status` is `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, moving each to `REVOKED`. `INACTIVE` is terminal.

### 2.11. Nested Resource Retrieval

Paths in this API are mounted under `/piercing-services` and several of them nest a parent identifier ahead of a child identifier, for example a store's appointments, a customer's consents, an appointment's sessions or a session's healing outcomes. When a path carries more than one resource identifier, the server validates them strictly from left to right: the first identifier that does not resolve to an existing row terminates the request with an HTTP 404 carrying the error identifier for that specific missing resource, and no later identifier is examined. A request for the sessions of a non-existent appointment therefore fails with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND` rather than any session-level error. The generic form of this error is `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, and the resource-specific forms are `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PIERCER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEALING_OUTCOME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CARTRIDGE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`. The same ordering governs creation and modification: a parent that does not exist is reported before any child-level business rule is evaluated.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence and Rule Interaction

Rules are evaluated in a fixed priority order, and the first rule that fails determines the response. Authentication is evaluated first, so missing or malformed caller context yields `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` (HTTP 400) ahead of everything else. Authorization is second, so a disallowed role yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Resource existence follows third, resolving left to right as described in §2.11 and yielding the appropriate 404. State validity is fourth: a target row that is not in a state from which the requested transition is declared yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and because state validity outranks the business-safety rules, a completed appointment belonging to an under-age customer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than with an age error. Business safety rules follow — the age gate yielding `ERR_AGE_BELOW_JURISDICTION_MINIMUM`, the permitted-site gate yielding `ERR_SITE_NOT_PERMITTED_IN_JURISDICTION`, and the contraindication gate yielding `ERR_MEDICAL_CONTRAINDICATION`, all at HTTP 422 — ahead of the business constraints, which yield `ERR_GUARDIAN_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_IDENTIFICATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PIERCER_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_CARTRIDGE_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` and `ERR_PIERCE` rejection codes at HTTP 422, and the single-active-session and posture gates at HTTP 409. Within the business constraints, the guardian gate precedes the identification gate, so a consent that fails both is reported as `ERR_GUARDIAN_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`. The contraindication gate also precedes the cartridge quarantine gate, so a contraindicated screening whose lot is quarantined reports `ERR_MEDICAL_CONTRAINDICATION`. Temporal gates come next — the slot gate yielding `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`, the room-capacity gate yielding `ERR_STORE_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, the autoclave gate yielding `ERR_AUTOCLAVE_CYCLE_OVERLAP` and the follow-up window gate yielding `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_DUE`, all at HTTP 409 — followed by the referential gates, which yield `ERR_RE_PIERCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_ALLOWABLE` at HTTP 422, then the fiscal aftercare credit gate yielding `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AFTERCARE_CREDIT` at HTTP 402, and finally the optimistic concurrency gate yielding `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. A request that is missing a required field, that is malformed, or that violates a declared field constraint — a `state_code` that is not exactly two uppercase letters or is not unique, a `permitted_sites` entry outside the enumerated site set, a `contraindication_codes` entry outside the enumerated contraindication set, or a `reaction_codes` entry outside the enumerated reaction set — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Intake: Customer Registration and the Age Ladder

The customer record is the anchor of every clinical artifact in this system. An `IntakeAssociate` — or, on the self-service surface, an `Anonymous` caller — registers a `Customer` by supplying `full_name` and `date_of_birth`, optionally together with `guardian_name` and `guardian_contact` for a minor. The `guardian_contact` is the phone number or email later used to verify a guardian consent. Registration creates the customer in `status` `ACTIVE` with `aftercare_credit_cents`, `refunded_cents` and `retained_cents` all starting at zero. `date_of_birth` is the single input that drives every age gate in the system, and the age used is the customer's age in whole years at the date of service.

At the point where a service is proposed — when an appointment is booked, when a session is opened, and when a consent is verified — the customer's age is measured against the governing jurisdiction's three boundaries and the outcome decides the intake path. Anyone below the regime's `min_age_absolute` is refused outright. Anyone at or above `min_age_absolute` but below `min_age_self_consent` may not sign for themselves and must be covered by a consent instrument executed by a guardian, which means `consent_type` `GUARDIAN_PRESENT`, `GUARDIAN_NOTARIZED` or `COURT_ORDER`; a `SELF` consent in that band does not satisfy intake. Anyone below `min_age_with_guardian` is refused with `ERR_AGE_BELOW_JURISDICTION_MINIMUM` and HTTP 422. The same rejection applies to the absolute floor. These checks guard the booking of an appointment, the opening of a session, and the verification of a consent, and they are evaluated before any other business rule on those operations.

### 3.2. Consent Instruments, Guardian Verification and Identification

A consent instrument is captured at intake as a `ConsentRecord` by an `IntakeAssociate`, who supplies `customer_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `consent_type`, `identification_type`, `identification_number_masked` and `signed_at`. The record is created with `consent_status` `PENDING`, and two derived values are set immediately. First, `required_identification_type` is resolved from a pinned mapping of the executing party to the identification instrument the regime demands: a `SELF` consent requires `GOVERNMENT_PHOTO_ID`; a `GUARDIAN_PRESENT` consent requires `BIRTH_CERTIFICATE`; a `GUARDIAN_NOTARIZED` consent requires `BIRTH_CERTIFICATE`; and a `COURT_ORDER` consent requires `PASSPORT`. The value `NONE` is derived only when the customer is at or above the jurisdiction's `min_age_self_consent` and the jurisdiction waives identification. Second, `guardian_verification_status` is initialized to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` when the `consent_type` is anything other than `SELF`, and to `NOT_REQUIRED` when it is `SELF`.

Verification of a pending consent is an `IntakeAssociate` act that activates the instrument. It requires that the consent is `PENDING`, that `guardian_verification_status` is `VERIFIED` or that the `consent_type` is `SELF`, and that the presented `identification_type` equals the derived `required_identification_type`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_IDENTIFICATION_TYPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. On success `consent_status` becomes `ACTIVE` and `expires_at` is set to `signed_at` plus the consent validity window of 30 days (`CONSENT_VALIDITY_DAYS`). That window is exclusive at the far end: a consent is usable while the clock is earlier than `expires_at`, and at the instant the clock equals `expires_at` the consent is `EXPIRED` and can no longer gate a check-in. The consent dimension progresses on its own thereafter — an `ACTIVE` consent becomes `EXPIRED` automatically once the clock reaches `expires_at`, an `ACTIVE` consent can be revoked by a `ComplianceOfficer`, which sets it to `REVOKED` and cascades every appointment for that customer and that jurisdiction whose status is `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` to `CANCELLED`, and an `EXPIRED` consent can be renewed by an `IntakeAssociate`, which returns it to `PENDING` and resets `guardian_verification_status` to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` when the `consent_type` is not `SELF` and to `NOT_REQUIRED` when it is. A `PENDING` consent may instead be rejected outright by supplying a `reason_code`, which sets `consent_status` to `REJECTED`. Both verification and rejection are available only while the consent is `PENDING`; against any other `consent_status` the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409.

Guardian identity verification is a parallel dimension that moves independently of `consent_status`. An `IntakeAssociate` records the verification outcome for a consent whose `guardian_verification_status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `FAILED` and whose `consent_type` is not `SELF`, supplying an `outcome`; the status becomes `VERIFIED` or `FAILED` according to that outcome and `consent_status` is untouched by the call. The guardian gate is enforced wherever a guardian-backed consent is used. When `consent_type` is `GUARDIAN_PRESENT` or `GUARDIAN_NOTARIZED`, `guardian_verification_status` must be `VERIFIED` before the consent can become `ACTIVE`, and additionally, when the jurisdiction's `requires_guardian_presence` is true, a `GUARDIAN_NOTARIZED` consent is rejected outright because notarization cannot substitute for an attending guardian. The refusal code is `ERR_GUARDIAN_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` with HTTP 422, and it guards both consent verification and appointment check-in. This gate outranks the identification gate, so a consent that fails both reports the guardian error.

### 3.3. Booking, Slot Discipline and Store Capacity

An `IntakeAssociate` books an `Appointment` by supplying `store_location_id`, `customer_id`, `piercer_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `intake_type`, `site_requested`, `slot_start_at` and `slot_end_at`. `intake_type` is `SCHEDULED` for a booked slot and `WALK_IN` for a customer who arrived unannounced; `site_requested` is the anatomical site the customer asked for and must be one of `LOBE`, `UPPER_LOBE`, `HELIX`, `TRAGUS`, `INDUSTRIAL` or `NOSTRIL`. The appointment is created in `status` `BOOKED` with `reschedule_count` 0, and the jurisdiction is snapshotted onto the booking so that later regime changes do not silently rewrite an existing booking's terms. The requested site must be an element of the governing jurisdiction's `permitted_sites` for the customer's consent class; a site outside that set is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_PERMITTED_IN_JURISDICTION` and HTTP 422. This check guards both booking an appointment and evaluating a screening.

Slot geometry follows a half-open convention: the occupied interval is `[slot_start_at, slot_end_at)`. Two appointments that are not `CANCELLED` may not overlap for the same `store_location_id` and the same `piercer_id`; the refusal is `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP` with HTTP 409. Adjacency is permitted — an appointment starting at the exact instant another's slot ends is fine — and every supplied slot bound is truncated by flooring to the containing minute before comparison, so sub-minute differences are invisible. A slot must also be at least 15 minutes long, with `slot_end_at` at least 15 minutes after `slot_start_at`. Separately, the store's physical capacity is enforced: the number of non-`CANCELLED` appointments at the same `store_location_id` whose half-open slots contain a common instant may not exceed that store's `piercing_room_count`, and exceeding it is refused with `ERR_STORE_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409. Both the piercer-adjacency gate and the room-capacity gate guard booking an appointment, confirming one, and rescheduling one.

Confirmation is an `IntakeAssociate` act that takes a `BOOKED` appointment to `CONFIRMED` once the slot passes its overlap and capacity checks and the snapshotted jurisdiction's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Check-in takes a `CONFIRMED` appointment to `CHECKED_IN` and requires three things: the clock is at or after `slot_start_at` minus the check-in lead of 30 minutes (`CHECK_IN_LEAD_MINUTES`), so the boundary is inclusive; the store's `status` is `OPEN`; and the customer holds at least one `ConsentRecord` whose `consent_status` is `ACTIVE`. The check-in window is the calculation `check_in_window`, and it also governs the no-show side: an appointment is marked `NO_SHOW` only once the clock has moved strictly past `slot_end_at`. The consent window is the calculation `consent_validity_window`. The same consent must also clear the guardian gate of §3.2, which is the second operation guarded by `ERR_GUARDIAN_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Rescheduling moves a `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment to `RESCHEDULED` using a new `new_slot_start_at` and `new_slot_end_at`, increments `reschedule_count` by one, and is permitted only while `reschedule_count` is below 2; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 409. Re-confirming a `RESCHEDULED` appointment returns it to `BOOKED` after the new slot passes the piercer-adjacency check, closing the cycle `CONFIRMED → RESCHEDULED → BOOKED → CONFIRMED`. Cancellation of a `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment sets it to `CANCELLED`. An appointment that is still `CONFIRMED` once the clock passes `slot_end_at` is marked `NO_SHOW` automatically, and that boundary is exclusive, so an appointment whose slot ends at the current instant is not yet a no-show; the automatic result imposes no penalty, and a `WALK_IN` slot is treated no differently from a scheduled one in this respect. Cancelling, aborting or a store-level cascade are the only paths that set `CANCELLED` besides an explicit cancellation, and `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, `NO_SHOW` and `REFUSED` are terminal.

### 3.4. Anatomy Screening and the Refusal Fork

Screening is the clinical gate between a checked-in customer and a chair. A `Piercer` records an `AnatomyScreening` against an appointment by supplying `appointment_id`, `piercer_id`, an ordered `anatomy_sites_examined` list, a `contraindication_codes` list and `expected_appointment_version`. Each entry of `contraindication_codes` must be a member of `KELOID_HISTORY`, `ACTIVE_INFECTION`, `BLOOD_THINNER_THERAPY`, `METAL_ALLERGY`, `UNCONTROLLED_DIABETES`, `PREGNANCY` or `HYPERTROPHIC_SCAR`; an entry outside that set is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The screening is created in `outcome` `PENDING`, and recording it increments the appointment's `version`, which is what makes `expected_appointment_version` on this write meaningful — a client whose observed version is stale because someone else already recorded a screening or checked the appointment in is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

The appointment's own screening step moves it from `CHECKED_IN` to `SCREENED` and may be performed by `Piercer` acting only when that piercer owns a screening for the appointment; the screening outcome is set by the screening evaluator as part of the same step. The evaluator itself is a `Piercer` act that resolves a `PENDING` screening into exactly one of three outcomes. When `contraindication_codes` is empty and the appointment's requested site is within the jurisdiction's `permitted_sites`, the screening clears and `outcome` becomes `CLEARED` with `refusal_reason_code` set to null. When the code list is non-empty and contains a code whose severity rank is at or above the escalation rank — the escalation-rank contraindications being `KELOID_HISTORY` and `BLOOD_THINNER_THERAPY` — the screening escalates and `outcome` becomes `ESCALATED`, and an `IncidentReport` of `severity` `MINOR` is filed against the session linked to the screening's appointment. When the code list is non-empty but contains no escalation-rank code, the screening is refused and `outcome` becomes `REFUSED` with `refusal_reason_code` set to the highest-severity code under the contraindication severity ranking. A screening carrying any of `KELOID_HISTORY`, `BLOOD_THINNER_THERAPY` or `ACTIVE_INFECTION` can never clear: the screening is itself resolved to `ESCALATED` when it carries `KELOID_HISTORY` or `BLOOD_THINNER_THERAPY` and to `REFUSED` otherwise, and the refusal or escalation is additionally reported with `ERR_MEDICAL_CONTRAINDICATION` and HTTP 422, so the resolution and the error are both produced by the one evaluation; the error guards both the evaluation of a screening and the start of service, and it outranks the cartridge quarantine gate. Re-evaluating a screening that already carries a terminal outcome is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_ALREADY_EVALUATED` and HTTP 409.

Escalation is not necessarily the end of the road. A `Piercer` may escalate a `REFUSED` screening to `ESCALATED` when that screening's `refusal_reason_code` is `KELOID_HISTORY` or `BLOOD_THINNER_THERAPY`, which is the edge that closes the `REFUSED`/`ESCALATED` cycle. A `ComplianceOfficer` may override an `ESCALATED` screening in one of two directions: supplying a signed medical clearance reference as `medical_clearance_ref` clears it to `CLEARED`, while overriding without that clearance refuses it to `REFUSED`; the optional `override_outcome` may be supplied to select the intended direction. Downstream, the fork that matters is at the appointment: a `SCREENED` appointment whose governing screening resolves to `CLEARED` proceeds to `IN_SERVICE`, while a `SCREENED` appointment whose governing screening resolves to `REFUSED` or `ESCALATED` is pushed to `REFUSED` by a `Piercer` supplying a `refusal_reason_code`, and that step locks the screening's `outcome` at `REFUSED`. The two branches diverge from the same `SCREENED` state and are equally specified: proceeding requires the governing screening to be `CLEARED`, and refusing requires it to be `REFUSED` or `ESCALATED`.

Starting service is the most heavily guarded transition in the system, and the checks are ordered. The appointment must be `SCREENED`, and the governing screening check is evaluated before all other business checks: when `governing_screening_id` resolves to null or to a screening whose `outcome` is not `CLEARED`, the request is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED` and HTTP 409. Next the store's `compliance_posture` must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_SERVICE` at HTTP 409. Next the assigned piercer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and the governing certification resolved through `active_certification_id` must have `status` `VALID`, otherwise `ERR_PIERCER_NOT_CERTIFIED` at HTTP 422. Finally the piercer's `assigned_session_count` must be 0, because a piercer may hold at most one session in `service_status` `IN_PROGRESS` across all stores, and a second concurrent session is refused with `ERR_PIERCER_SESSION_IN_PROGRESS` at HTTP 409. When all of these pass, the appointment becomes `IN_SERVICE`, the piercer's `assigned_session_count` increases by one, and a `PiercingSession` is created in `service_status` `IN_PROGRESS`.

### 3.5. Performing the Service: Cartridges, Certification and Fee Derivation

A `PiercingSession` is opened either implicitly by the start-of-service step or explicitly by a `Piercer` supplying `appointment_id`, `piercer_id`, `cartridge_lot_id`, `site_pierced`, `base_fee_cents`, `aftercare_kit_included` and `solution_lot_number`. `base_fee_cents` is the list fee in cents, `aftercare_kit_included` states whether the aftercare solution kit accompanied the service, and `solution_lot_number` is the traceability lot number of that solution. The mandatory-kit rule applies wherever a session is opened, not only at completion: opening a session while the governing jurisdiction mandates an aftercare kit — which it does when the jurisdiction's `record_retention_years` is at least 5 — is refused with `ERR_AFTERCARE_KIT_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422 unless `aftercare_kit_included` is true, and the session opened implicitly by the start-of-service step, which takes no client body, supplies the mandated kit itself. When `cartridge_lot_id` is omitted the lot is chosen by the `store_available_lot` selector of §2.6, preferring the lot with the most available units and then the nearest expiration, and an exhausted pool fails with `ERR_CARTRIDGE_LOT_UNAVAILABLE`. When a lot is supplied explicitly it must be `AVAILABLE`, must be bound to a `SterilizationCycle` whose `status` is `PASSED` or `RELEASED`, and must have an `expiration_date` later than the current clock instant; failing any of the three yields `ERR_CARTRIDGE_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` at HTTP 422, an error that guards both opening a session and re-piercing one. The piercer's certification must also pass: the folder resolved through `active_certification_id` must carry `status` `VALID` and a `sterilization_protocol_code` equal to the governing jurisdiction's `sterilization_protocol_code`, otherwise `ERR_PIERCER_NOT_CERTIFIED` at HTTP 422. Opening a session initializes `service_status` to `IN_PROGRESS` and `healing_status` to `HEALING`, consumes one unit from the chosen lot, increments the piercer's `assigned_session_count`, and computes `net_fee_cents`. A session may only be opened when no existing session for that appointment is already in `service_status` `IN_PROGRESS`. Opening a session is gated by the same store-posture rule that guards start-of-service: a store whose `compliance_posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` cannot open a new session, and the refusal is `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_SERVICE` at HTTP 409.

The fee is derived by the calculation `net_service_fee`, whose evaluation order is pinned so that the discount tier is selected before the re-pierce discount is applied. First the aftercare component is set to 1200 cents when `aftercare_kit_included` is true and to 0 when it is false. Second, the pre-discount subtotal is `base_fee_cents` plus that aftercare component. Third, the discount rate is selected from the pre-discount subtotal: 0.00 when the subtotal is below 4000 cents, 0.10 when it is at least 4000 cents and below 8000 cents, and 0.15 when it is at least 8000 cents. Fourth, the applied downgrade credit starts at 0. Fifth, when the session is a re-pierce claim, the re-pierce discount is the pre-discount subtotal multiplied by 0.20 and floored to whole cents. Sixth, `net_fee_cents` is the pre-discount subtotal minus the discount rate applied to that subtotal and floored to whole cents, minus the re-pierce discount, and the result is floored at the site minimum of 1500 cents for the `site_pierced` value.

A related read-only capability lets an `IntakeAssociate` or a `Piercer` quote a prospective fee without persisting anything, by supplying `base_fee_cents`, `aftercare_kit_included`, `site_pierced`, `is_re_pierce` and `downgraded_sites` and receiving back `net_fee_cents`, `discount_rate`, `re_pierce_discount_cents`, `downgrade_credited_cents` and `surcharge_cents`. The quote exercises `net_service_fee`, `downgrade_credit_amount` and `site_complexity_surcharge` read-only and writes no rows. The surcharge calculation `site_complexity_surcharge` derives a site factor — 1.0 for `LOBE`, 1.2 for `UPPER_LOBE`, 1.5 for `HELIX`, 1.6 for `TRAGUS`, 2.2 for `INDUSTRIAL` and 1.4 for `NOSTRIL` — and combines three terms: a cartilage hazard of 350 cents per contraindication beyond the first, capped at three such increments, so the count of contraindications minus one clamped into the range 0 to 3 and multiplied by 350; a quantity hazard of 200 cents per requested site beyond the first, so the requested site count minus one clamped at a minimum of 0 and multiplied by 200; and a site term equal to `base_fee_cents` multiplied by the site factor minus 1.0 and floored to whole cents. The surcharge is the sum of those three terms capped at 2500 cents. Inputs are the appointment's requested sites and the screening's contraindication codes.

Completion is a `Piercer` act that takes an appointment from `IN_SERVICE` to `COMPLETED` and requires the session's `service_status` to be `IN_PROGRESS`. It additionally requires `aftercare_kit_included` to be true whenever the jurisdiction mandates an aftercare kit, which it does when the jurisdiction's `record_retention_years` is at least 5; a missing mandatory kit is refused with `ERR_AFTERCARE_KIT_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. Completion decrements the piercer's `assigned_session_count`, moves the session's `service_status` to `COMPLETED`, consumes a further unit from the cartridge lot by adding one to `units_consumed` and subtracting one from `units_available`, recomputes `units_available` through `cartridge_lot_availability`, creates a `HealingOutcome` in `status` `ASSESSED`, and schedules the aftercare follow-up reminder at the current clock instant plus the aftercare follow-up interval of `aftercare_follow_up_days`. The session dimension mirrors this automatically: a session whose parent appointment completes moves to `service_status` `COMPLETED`, at which point `net_fee_cents` is computed by `net_service_fee` and `follow_up_reminder_due_at` is set to the current clock instant plus `aftercare_follow_up_days`.

An abort is the compensating path. A `Piercer` aborting mid-procedure moves the appointment from `IN_SERVICE` to `CANCELLED`, decrements the piercer's `assigned_session_count`, moves the session's `service_status` to `ABORTED`, and quarantines the consumed cartridge by adding one to `units_quarantined` and subtracting one from `units_available`, because a cartridge drawn mid-service is not returned to stock. The session's `healing_status` remains `HEALING`, since an aborted session is never assessed for healing, and the aborted appointment is deliberately not counted toward the piercer's eligibility score.

### 3.6. Aftercare, Healing Assessment and the Downgrade and Re-Pierce Loop

Aftercare is a scheduled obligation rather than an optional courtesy. The follow-up reminder is due at `follow_up_reminder_due_at`, and the customer may acknowledge it only within a bounded window: acknowledgement is accepted from `follow_up_reminder_due_at` inclusive until `follow_up_reminder_due_at` plus seven days exclusive. Outside that window — earlier than the due instant, or at or after the seven-day close — acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_DUE` at HTTP 409. The `Customer` role performs this acknowledgement against their own session, and the acting caller's `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of the customer that the session belongs to. A successful acknowledgement sets `follow_up_acknowledged` to true, and the acknowledgement is idempotent: a repeat acknowledgement of a session whose `follow_up_acknowledged` is already true succeeds without changing the row again.

Healing is assessed by a `Piercer` against a session whose `healing_status` is `HEALING`, by supplying `assessed_at` and a set of `failure_codes`. A healed result requires that the assessment is dated at least the healing assessment minimum of `healing_assessment_min_days` after the session's completion and that the assessed wound shows neither discharge nor embedded backing; it moves `healing_status` to `HEALED` and sets the linked `HealingOutcome` to `status` `ASSESSED` with `healing_result` `HEALED`. A failed result is declared when the assessed wound shows discharge, embedded backing, or an active infection; it moves `healing_status` to `FAILED`, sets the linked `HealingOutcome`'s `healing_result` to `FAILED` with `re_pierce_eligible` true when `re_pierce_claim_count` is 0, and files an `IncidentReport` of `severity` `MODERATE` against that session. That incident is the origin of a three-step chain: the moderate incident is triaged, escalation cascades the session's healing dimension back to `FAILED`, and the store's posture is subsequently recomputed.

A failure is recoverable once. The session moves from `FAILED` back to `HEALING` when the `HealingOutcome` has `re_pierce_eligible` true and `re_pierce_claim_count` 0; the re-pierce consumes a cartridge unit by adding one to `units_consumed` and subtracting one from `units_available`, recomputes `net_fee_cents` with the re-pierce discount of 0.20 applied per §3.5, and moves the outcome's `status` to `RE_PIERCED` with `re_pierce_claim_count` set to 1. A re-pierce claim that does not satisfy both eligibility conditions is refused with `ERR_RE_PIERCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` at HTTP 422, an error that guards both the session-level re-pierce and the outcome-level re-pierce claim. The healing-outcome record mirrors this loop on its own state machine: it moves automatically from `ASSESSED` to `RE_PIERCE_ELIGIBLE` when `healing_result` is `FAILED` and `re_pierce_claim_count` is 0, setting `re_pierce_eligible` to true; it moves from `RE_PIERCE_ELIGIBLE` to `RE_PIERCED` upon a re-pierce claim, which requires the linked session's `healing_status` to be `FAILED` and sets `re_pierce_claim_count` to 1 with `re_pierce_eligible` false; and it can return to `RE_PIERCE_ELIGIBLE` from `DOWNGRADED` upon a re-pierce claim only when the downgrade clawback leaves `downgrade_credited_cents` greater than 0, setting `re_pierce_eligible` true again.

A healed site may instead be downgraded to a lower-complexity site, a `Piercer` act that moves the session's `healing_status` to `RE_PIERCED` and sets the linked outcome's `healing_result` to `DOWNGRADED`. Downgrade is only available from `HEALED`, either at the session or at the outcome, and it credits the customer. The credit is computed by `downgrade_credit_amount`, whose nesting order is pinned: the credit is computed and rounded per site first, and only then summed. For each downgraded site the site credit is that site's `site_base_value_cents` multiplied by the downgrade rate of 0.25, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; the outcome's `downgrade_credited_cents` is the sum of those per-site credits as integers. Worked concretely, a downgrade spanning `HELIX` with a base value of 5200 cents and `TRAGUS` with a base value of 4300 cents yields a per-site rounding of 5200 times 0.25, which is 1300, and 4300 times 0.25, which is 1075, and therefore a credit of 2375 cents. The contrasting and forbidden sum-then-round order would produce the same number here, so a sharper case distinguishes them: sites whose base values are 5101 and 4301 cents round per site to 1275 and 1075, summing to 2350 cents, whereas summing first and rounding afterwards would produce 2351. The per-site-then-sum order is the one this system uses. The credited amount is added to the customer's `aftercare_credit_cents`.

Closure of a healing outcome is a `ComplianceOfficer` act with state-dependent conditions. A `RE_PIERCED` outcome may be closed only when no incident linked to the session carries an `escalation_status` of `LOGGED`, `TRIAGED`, `ESCALATED` or `REOPENED`. A `DOWNGRADED` outcome may be closed only when `downgrade_credited_cents` is 0 or the customer's `aftercare_credit_cents` is at least `downgrade_credited_cents`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.7. Adverse-Reaction Incidents, Refunds and the Escalation Cascade

An adverse reaction is recorded as an `IncidentReport` by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying `session_id`, `reported_at`, `severity`, `reaction_codes` and `expected_session_version`. `severity` is one of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` or `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY`, and each entry of `reaction_codes` must be a member of `REDNESS`, `SWELLING`, `DISCHARGE`, `EMBEDDED_BACK`, `INFECTION`, `ALLERGIC_REACTION`, `KELOID` or `NERVE_DAMAGE`; an entry outside that set is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Filing an incident creates it in `escalation_status` `LOGGED` with `refund_status` `NONE`, and increments the target session's `version`, which is what the required `expected_session_version` is checked against; a stale value is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Triage is the first rung and is performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` against a `LOGGED` incident whose referenced session exists. Triage moves `escalation_status` to `TRIAGED`, writes a provisional `refund_amount_cents`, and sets `refund_status` to `PENDING`. Triage is also the origin of a three-step chain: a triaged incident that is then escalated cascades the linked session's `healing_status` to `FAILED`, increases the owning store's `open_incident_count` once per affected session, recomputes that store's posture, and — when the recomputed posture becomes `RESTRICTED` — cancels every appointment at that store whose status is `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED`. This is a distinct cascade origin from the sterilization-failure chain of §2.7 even though both end in a posture recomputation.

Escalation and resolution split the ladder by severity. A `TRIAGED` incident may be escalated further by a `ComplianceOfficer` only when its `severity` is `SEVERE` or `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY`, which moves `escalation_status` to `ESCALATED` and runs the cascade above. A `TRIAGED` incident may instead be resolved directly, but only when its `severity` is `MINOR` or `MODERATE`, which moves `escalation_status` to `RESOLVED` and writes the final `refund_amount_cents`. An `ESCALATED` incident is resolved by a `ComplianceOfficer` who supplies both a `resolution_note` and a refund decision, which moves it to `RESOLVED` and likewise writes the final `refund_amount_cents`. Resolution is not always permanent: a `RESOLVED` incident may be reopened by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `reopen_reason`, provided `reported_at` is within the reopen window, meaning the current clock instant minus `reported_at` is at most the reopen interval of `incident_reopen_days`; reopening moves `escalation_status` to `REOPENED`. A `REOPENED` incident can be triaged again, which returns it to `TRIAGED` and sets `refund_status` back to `PENDING`, closing the `RESOLVED`/`REOPENED`/`TRIAGED` cycle.

Refunds are computed by `incident_refund_allocation`, which has two variants that share a structure but not always a value. The gross fee is built from components in a pinned order: the session's `base_fee_cents` first, then an aftercare kit component of 1200 cents when `aftercare_kit_included` is true and 0 when it is false. The refundable portion then depends on the incident's `severity`: `MINOR` yields 0; `MODERATE` yields the gross fee multiplied by 0.50 and floored to whole cents; `SEVERE` yields the gross fee multiplied by 0.75 and floored to whole cents; and `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY` yields the entire gross fee. The provisional candidate is the refundable portion minus the customer's lifetime `refunded_cents`, floored at 0 and never negative, and it is then capped so that it cannot exceed the gross fee minus the retention floor of 500 cents, with that cap itself floored at 0. Floors are applied at each tier and cap step, and no other rounding occurs. The provisional variant runs at triage, uses the session's recorded `net_fee_cents` as the gross fee and the tier rate applicable at triage time, and writes `refund_amount_cents` while setting `refund_status` to `PENDING`; it may be revised later. The final variant runs at resolution, recomputes the gross fee from `base_fee_cents` plus the aftercare kit rule, applies the retention floor against the customer's lifetime `refunded_cents`, and overwrites `refund_amount_cents`. For identical inputs the two variants differ whenever the session carried a re-pierce discount or a prior refund.

Settlement is a `StoreManager` act against a `RESOLVED` incident whose `refund_amount_cents` is greater than 0, and it may carry a `refund_destination` and an optional `requested_refund_cents`. A requested amount above the computed cap is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_ALLOWABLE` at HTTP 422: a refund may never exceed the gross fee minus the retention floor of 500 cents, and may never exceed the customer's `net_fee_cents` for that session. On success `escalation_status` becomes `REFUND_ISSUED` and `refund_status` becomes `SETTLED`; the customer's `refunded_cents` increases by `refund_amount_cents`, and the customer's `retained_cents` increases by the session's `net_fee_cents` minus `refund_amount_cents`. Customer-level state follows from incident outcomes: a customer with at least one reachable incident whose `severity` is `SEVERE` or `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY` and whose `escalation_status` is `LOGGED`, `TRIAGED`, `ESCALATED` or `REOPENED` — or whose performing piercer has dropped below an `eligibility_score` of 60 — is flagged automatically, which moves the customer to `FLAGGED` and cancels every one of their `BOOKED` or `CONFIRMED` appointments. A `ComplianceOfficer` clears the flag back to `ACTIVE` only when no reachable incident remains in `LOGGED`, `TRIAGED`, `ESCALATED` or `REOPENED` and every piercer who served the customer has an `eligibility_score` of at least 60. Closure of a customer account is also a `ComplianceOfficer` act: an `ACTIVE` customer may be closed only when no appointment of theirs is in `BOOKED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `SCREENED` or `IN_SERVICE`, and a `FLAGGED` customer may be closed only when `aftercare_credit_cents` is 0 and no reachable incident has `refund_status` `PENDING`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Aftercare Credit Redemption and the Customer Ledger

Customers accumulate store credit from downgrades and spend it at intake. An `IntakeAssociate` redeems credit by supplying an `amount_cents` against the customer's record, which reduces `aftercare_credit_cents` by that amount. The redemption may never exceed the customer's currently available `aftercare_credit_cents`; an attempt to redeem more is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AFTERCARE_CREDIT` at HTTP 402. Three customer-level amounts move independently and are never conflated: `aftercare_credit_cents` is the redeemable store credit net of redemptions, `refunded_cents` is the cumulative cash refunded across the customer's incident reports, and `retained_cents` is the cumulative non-refundable amount retained against that customer's service fees.

### 3.9. Certification, Recertification and Roster Discipline

A `ComplianceOfficer` issues a `CertificationRecord` against a `Piercer` by supplying `piercer_id`, `certification_level`, `sterilization_protocol_code`, `issued_at`, `expires_at` and `expected_piercer_version`. The record is created in `status` `VALID`, `attempted_practical_count` starts at 0, and `recertification_due_at` is computed by `certification_recertification_due` as `expires_at` minus the 30-day lead. Issuance increments the piercer's `version` — which is what `expected_piercer_version` is checked against, a stale value being refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 — recomputes the piercer's `active_certification_id` through the governing-certification selector, and recomputes `eligibility_score`. A `ComplianceOfficer` revokes a certification by supplying a `reason_code`; revocation applies while the record is `VALID`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, sets `status` to `REVOKED`, and — when the record carried a `certification_level` of `STANDARD`, `SENIOR` or `INSTRUCTOR` — cascades the owning piercer to `SUSPENDED`.

Recertification is submitted by the owning `Piercer` through a practical assessment carrying `practical_assessment_passed`. It is owner-restricted: a submission by a piercer other than the certification's owner is refused with `ERR_NOT_CERTIFICATION_OWNER` at HTTP 403. A passing assessment against an `EXPIRING_SOON` record returns it to `VALID`, sets `issued_at` to the current clock instant and `expires_at` to that instant plus `certification_validity_months`, resets `attempted_practical_count` to 0, and recomputes `recertification_due_at` — a cyclic edge, not a terminal outcome. A passing assessment against an already `EXPIRED` record performs the same late-renewal recovery and resets the attempt count. A failing assessment against an `EXPIRING_SOON` record moves it to `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` and increments `attempted_practical_count`. A record in `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` may be re-submitted back to `EXPIRING_SOON` while its attempt count remains below 3. Once `attempted_practical_count` reaches exactly 3 the record is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_RECERT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` at HTTP 409 and moves automatically to `EXPIRED`. Recertification also recomputes the piercer's `eligibility_score`, which is why a certification cycle is never merely bookkeeping: the score feeds directly into the suspension and flagging cascades described in §2.10 and §2.7.

Roster administration closes the loop. A `StoreManager` registers a piercer by supplying `full_name` and `home_store_id`, creating the piercer with `eligibility_score` 100, `assigned_session_count` 0 and `status` `ACTIVE`. A `StoreManager` reinstates a `SUSPENDED` piercer to `ACTIVE` under the conditions of §2.10, and deactivates an `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` piercer to `INACTIVE` when `assigned_session_count` is 0, revoking that piercer's `VALID` and `EXPIRING_SOON` certifications in the same act. The reading surface for this discipline exposes a piercer's certification history and an eligibility audit that returns the stored rolling score beside the cumulative chronological score, which diverge for any piercer whose history reaches back beyond the rolling window.

### 3.10. The Evaluator Clock

Every window in this document — recertification lead times, consent validity, follow-up reminders, no-show detection, check-in lead times and incident reopen windows — is measured against a logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. An administrative capability available to a `ComplianceOfficer` sets that clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant. Before any such call the clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. Time arithmetic is evaluated in UTC, at second precision for cycle intervals, minute granularity for appointment slots, and instant arithmetic for consent, recertification, follow-up and reopen windows.
