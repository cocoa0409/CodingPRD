# Contact Center Workforce Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Contact centres live or die on a single arithmetic tension: demand arrives in fifteen-, thirty- or sixty-minute bursts that nobody controls, while supply is bought in whole shifts weeks in advance under labour agreements that nobody may quietly break. A planner who over-buys burns payroll on idle agents; a planner who under-buys watches answer rates collapse, abandons climb and the marketing team's campaign land on an unstaffed queue. This service is the system of record for that trade-off. It forecasts contact volume and handle time per channel, per routing skill and per planning interval; it converts that demand into a staffing requirement under explicit service-level, occupancy and shrinkage assumptions; it runs shift bidding and schedule generation while honouring seniority and stated preferences; and it manages the intraday gap between plan and reality with overtime, voluntary time off, skill-based routing moves and outsourced partner capacity.

The product is deliberately opinionated about **auditability**. Every number a planner sees is either an input somebody typed or a derived value the server computed from a formula written down in this document — never a value a client supplied and the server trusted. Every state change is a named transition with named preconditions, and every rejection carries a stable machine-readable error identifier so that an operations dashboard can explain *why* the system said no. Two maker-checker separations are enforced structurally rather than by policy memo: the coach who adjudicates an adherence exception may not be the controller who flagged the shift, and the sourcing manager who commits an outsourced allocation may not be the one who proposed it.

The document is organised in five parts. Section 2 fixes the cross-cutting contract — identity, envelopes, pagination, precision, versioning and the cycle-wide posture gate. Section 3 walks the business itself, from opening a planning week through forecasting, bidding, publication, intraday firefighting, adherence coaching and partner settlement; every field, threshold and cascade the implementation must honour is stated there, distributed across the workflow it belongs to. Section 4 lists the HTTP surface. Section 5 catalogues every error identifier the service can emit. All resource collections are mounted at the service root — there is no versioned or namespaced prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role it acts under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. Once authentication succeeds, the role is matched against the authorised-role list of the requested endpoint as given in Section 4; a role that does not appear there is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `CapacityPlanner`, `WorkforceAnalyst`, `SchedulingManager`, `IntradayController`, `Agent`, `TeamCoach`, `SourcingManager` and `PartnerRep`.

Their remits are as follows. A `CapacityPlanner` owns the planning-cycle envelope, the labour agreements and the hiring pipeline. A `WorkforceAnalyst` owns demand: forecast groups, interval grids, marketing drivers and reforecasting. A `SchedulingManager` owns supply: the agent roster, shift templates, bid rounds, award runs and shift publication. An `IntradayController` owns real time: posting actuals, recording adherence observations and pulling the intraday levers. An `Agent` is a frontline employee acting on their own bids, exception requests and intraday offers. A `TeamCoach` adjudicates adherence exceptions and is barred from deciding a case they themselves flagged. A `SourcingManager` owns the outsourced-partner surface end to end: registration, performance reporting, allocation, commitment, revocation and settlement. A `PartnerRep` is an outsourced partner's own representative and is strictly read-only.

Identity semantics differ by role, and getting them wrong is the most common source of spurious authorisation failures.

- When `X-User-Role` is `CapacityPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `proposed_by_user_id` on a partner allocation when the transition writes it — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds verbatim for `WorkforceAnalyst`, `SchedulingManager`, `SourcingManager` and, in the recording sense, for `IntradayController` (whose identifier is stamped into `flagged_by_user_id` on a shift it flags) and `TeamCoach` (whose identifier is stamped into `decided_by_user_id` on an exception it terminally decides). For all six of these roles, no ownership comparison against a stored `id` is ever performed.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Agent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agent` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agent` compare the resource's agent foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `OutsourcePartner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `OutsourcePartner` compare the resource's partner foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership enforcement is concrete and narrow. Under the `Agent` role, five operations are ownership-scoped: submitting a bid and filing an adherence exception both require the payload's `agent_id` to equal `X-User-ID`, and withdrawing a bid, resubmitting an exception and responding to an intraday offer all require the addressed row's own agent reference to equal `X-User-ID`. A sixth scope applies to the `PartnerRep` role: listing partner allocations may only return rows whose `outsource_partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any breach of any of these six scopes yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

Separately from ownership, two segregation-of-duties comparisons guard maker-checker integrity, and both compare operator identifiers rather than entity ids. A `TeamCoach` recording a decision on an adherence exception must have an `X-User-ID` different from the `flagged_by_user_id` stored on the underlying shift. A `SourcingManager` committing a partner allocation must have an `X-User-ID` different from that allocation's `proposed_by_user_id`. Either collision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).


### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Both must be integers. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, and that ordering is stable across pages. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current page. Single-resource reads and write responses omit those four pagination fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply or choose an `id`. Every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at creation. Every one of the fifteen entity types in this system also carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`.

The `version` counter is not decoration. It is the value that optimistic-concurrency clients echo back (Section 2.7), and it advances not only when a client writes a row directly but also when a cascade from another operation mutates the row. When a partner allocation is committed, for example, the partner row's own `version` advances because the commitment moved the partner's outstanding commitment ledger.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this system — offer expiry, voluntary-time-off notice, marketing-window realisation, training start dates, graduation eligibility, horizon containment — reads a server-controlled **logical clock**, never the host's wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative test-driving capability moves it: the caller supplies `to_iso8601`, and the response reports `current_utc` (the newly effective instant) and `previous_utc` (the instant that was in force before the call). Setting the clock mutates no entity; it only changes the instant against which every subsequent request evaluates its temporal predicates. The clock never falls back to real time once set.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Units

Unless a formula explicitly names a different operation, every rounding in this system is **round half up**. Integers are produced by rounding half up to zero decimal places; the only exceptions are the places where this document explicitly names rounding *down* to the nearest whole number (a floor) or rounding *up* to the nearest whole number (a ceiling), and those exceptions are always called out at the point of use.

Fixed scales apply per field family. Ratios, rates and percentages expressed as fractions of one — service-level targets, occupancy targets, shrinkage, uplift percentages, weighted uplifts, attrition shares, adherence scores, preference-honour rates, reforecast multipliers, observed and smoothed ratios, rolling service-level attainment — carry **four decimal places**. Hour quantities and full-time-equivalent quantities — paid hours, overtime hours, voluntary-time-off hours, committed hours, scheduled hour totals, pipeline FTE, effective FTE at horizon end — carry **two decimal places**. Agent counts, interval indices, contact volumes, seconds and all monetary amounts are integers.

Money is never carried as a floating-point currency amount. Wage rates, overtime costs, budgets and settlement payables are integers **in cents**; outsourced partner pricing is an integer in **millicents** (thousandths of a cent) per handled contact, so a partner priced at 12,500 millicents per contact is priced at 12.5 cents per contact. Where a millicent price is converted to cents, the conversion divides by 1000 and rounds half up to a whole cent, and — critically — that rounding happens **per settlement line**, before any summation across lines. Summing millicents across several lines and rounding once at the end is forbidden and will produce off-by-one invoice totals.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Reforecasting and Commitment

Exactly two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only those two accept a version echo. Every other write endpoint takes no version field at all; supplying one is not part of its contract.

The first guard protects reforecasting. A reforecast run against a planning cycle requires `expected_cycle_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `PlanningCycle`. A reforecast legitimately supersedes the previous generation's smoothing multipliers for every forecast group of the cycle, so a client holding a stale view would silently overwrite a generation it never saw. If `expected_cycle_version` does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the run is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written; the client must re-read the cycle — including its advanced `reforecast_generation` — before trying again.

The second guard protects partner commitment. Committing a partner allocation requires `expected_partner_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `OutsourcePartner`. Commitment consumes partner capacity against `committed_volume_outstanding` and depends on the eligibility state that performance reports and other commitments both move, so a client whose view of the partner predates those changes is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) until it re-reads the partner.

Both rejections are evaluated as a stale-version check on the parent entity's current counter — not as a race between overlapping requests. A client that reads, waits an hour and then writes with an unchanged `version` succeeds; a client that writes one millisecond after somebody else advanced the counter fails.

### 2.8. The Planning-Cycle Posture as a System-Wide Gate

Every operational entity in this service is reachable from exactly one `PlanningCycle`, and that cycle's `posture` field acts as a gate over all of them. The five posture values are `OPEN`, `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED`, `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED`, `INTRADAY_ALERT` and `FROZEN`, and a newly created cycle starts at `OPEN`.

`FROZEN` is terminal and absolute. Once a cycle reaches `FROZEN`, every subsequent write against that cycle or against any child resource that resolves back to it — a forecast group, an interval plan, a marketing event, a shift template, a bid round, a bid, a schedule shift, an adherence exception, an intraday action, a partner allocation or a hiring class — fails with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). This check runs ahead of every business rule, so a frozen cycle masks skill mismatches, budget breaches and state errors alike. Reads are unaffected.

Two posture positions gate specific classes of work in the opposite direction. Publishing a schedule shift requires the cycle's `posture` to be one of `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED`, `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` or `INTRADAY_ALERT`; attempting it while the cycle is still `OPEN` (or after it has frozen) is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). Conversely, both demand-definition operations — creating a forecast group and generating an interval grid for a forecast group — require the cycle to be in `OPEN`; attempting either once the posture has moved past `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The same identifier guards the reverse posture move back to `OPEN`, which is declared only out of `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` and additionally requires that no shift of the cycle has yet been published.

The pair `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` and `INTRADAY_ALERT` is cyclic and machine-driven rather than operator-driven; Section 3.7 describes the threshold that flips it in both directions.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline: Recompute Chains, Reversals and Frozen Snapshots

Three cross-cutting habits govern how derived state moves.

**Recomputes are transitive and complete.** The deepest chain in the system runs from a confirmed marketing driver through interval volume, through forecast handle time, through both staffing variants, through the authoritative requirement, through the coverage gap and coverage state, through the cycle's count of short intervals, and finally through the cycle's own posture. Any operation that perturbs a link recomputes every downstream link in the same request — never lazily, never on the next read. The same discipline applies to the internal-capacity chain (staffed agents to absorbable volume to unserved volume) and to the adherence chain (a shift observation to the shift score to the agent's pooled score to the agent's coaching flag count to the agent's coaching status).

**Reversals are exact, and exactness is bought by freezing.** Where a resource can be un-done — revoking an accepted overtime offer, revoking a committed partner allocation, cancelling a confirmed marketing driver, reopening an awarded bid round — the reversal restores the ledger to precisely its pre-action value. For overtime this requires storing the priced cost on the action at acceptance time and subtracting exactly that stored number on revocation, because the tier multiplier that priced it may since have moved. For partner volume it requires subtracting exactly the allocation's `allocated_volume` from the partner's outstanding commitment.

**Every mutation touches the bookkeeping fields.** Any operation that changes a row — whether the client addressed that row directly or a cascade reached it — refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1.

### 2.10. Request Validation Order

When a single request could fail several checks, the outcome is deterministic: the checks run in a fixed precedence and the first failure is returned. That order is authentication (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then endpoint role authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then request-body shape (`ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 422 — required fields present, correct types, enum members recognised, numeric ranges and array lengths as specified in Section 3), then pagination parameter sanity on list endpoints (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`), then path-parameter existence, then body foreign-key existence, then `Agent` and `PartnerRep` ownership (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`), then segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), then the optimistic-concurrency version echo (`ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION`), then the frozen-cycle gate (`ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`), then the target resource's own state-transition legality, then the cross-entity posture gates (`ERR_CYCLE_NOT_LOCKED` or `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`), and only then the business-safety, temporal, referential, capacity and fiscal rules described throughout Section 3.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, existence is validated **left to right along the URL path**: a request addressing a nested or compound path resolves each templated identifier in the order it appears, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing produces that entity's own not-found error with HTTP 404 — so a request naming a non-existent forecast group and a malformed body reports `ERR_FORECAST_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_VALIDATION`, only after body shape has passed. Second, foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved **in the field order in which they are declared for that entity in Section 3**, and the first unresolvable one produces its own not-found error. The per-entity not-found identifiers are `ERR_PLANNING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORECAST_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKETING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERVAL_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADHERENCE_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTRADAY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OUTSOURCE_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_HIRING_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`. A forecast-group identifier arriving under any of its names — the plain group reference, the routing-target reference or the routing-source reference — resolves to `ERR_FORECAST_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`; a marketing-event identifier arriving either as the addressed event or as a superseded reference resolves to `ERR_MARKETING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable member of a bidder's ranked template list resolves to `ERR_SHIFT_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`.

When a resource is asked to make a transition that its current state does not declare — including repeating a trigger against a terminal state — the refusal is HTTP 409 and carries the most specific identifier available for that entity family: `ERR_BID_ROUND_NOT_OPEN` when a bid is submitted or withdrawn outside an open round, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_COMMITTED` when settlement is attempted on an allocation that is not committed, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_PUBLISHED` when an adherence observation or exception targets a shift that is not published, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_PENDING` when a decision targets an exception that is not awaiting decision or a resubmission targets one that was not sent back for information, `ERR_ACTION_NOT_OFFERED` when a response targets an intraday action that is no longer open or a revocation targets an already-terminal one, and the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` for every other illegal transition — cycle posture, marketing-event status, bid status, publication axis, adherence axis, partner status, allocation status, hiring-class status and agent employment status alike.

Within the final band — the business rules themselves — the precedence is likewise fixed, and the following order is exhaustive for this system. First the reopen ceiling on a bid round (`ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`). Then the four labour-safety checks against an agent's schedule, in this order: minimum rest (`ERR_MIN_REST_VIOLATION`), shift overlap (`ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`), maximum weekly hours (`ERR_MAX_WEEKLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`) and maximum consecutive days (`ERR_MAX_CONSECUTIVE_DAYS_EXCEEDED`). Then offer expiry (`ERR_ACTION_EXPIRED`). Then the temporal-window checks: horizon containment (`ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`) and marketing-event window sanity (`ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_INVALID`). Then the voluntary-time-off notice period (`ERR_VTO_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`). Then skill eligibility (`ERR_SKILL_MISMATCH`), then partner eligibility (`ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`). Then the award-run guards: exhausted template slots (`ERR_TEMPLATE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`) ahead of the uniqueness guards, duplicate bid (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`) and duplicate interval grid (`ERR_DUPLICATE_INTERVALS`), and then the preference-honour floor (`ERR_PREFERENCE_FLOOR_NOT_MET`). Then the capacity-conservation checks: coverage protection on a voluntary-time-off acceptance (`ERR_VTO_WOULD_BREACH_COVERAGE`), routing outflow against staffed agents (`ERR_ROUTING_OUTFLOW_EXCEEDS_STAFFING`), interval allocation capacity (`ERR_ALLOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`) and partner volume bounds (`ERR_PARTNER_VOLUME_OUT_OF_RANGE`). And last, the two fiscal checks: the overtime budget ceiling (`ERR_OT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`) and the settlement commitment floor (`ERR_COMMITMENT_FLOOR_BREACH`). The fiscal checks come last deliberately: a request that is both unsafe and unaffordable reports the safety failure, and a priced overtime acceptance is only budget-tested after every labour-safety rule has passed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Planning Week and Fixing the Labour Envelope

Planning in this business is weekly. A `CapacityPlanner` opens a **planning cycle** for exactly one seven-day horizon by supplying a `name` — a human label such as "W37-2026", between 1 and 80 characters and always required — together with `horizon_start_date` and `horizon_end_date`. The start date is an inclusive ISO 8601 calendar date and must be a Monday; the end date is likewise inclusive and must be exactly six days after the start date, so the horizon always spans Monday through Sunday. Four further inputs are optional and carry defaults. `interval_minutes` fixes the width of one planning interval and must be 15, 30 or 60, defaulting to 30; every interval plan the cycle ever produces uses that one width. `requirement_basis` decides which staffing variant is authoritative and must be `SERVICE_LEVEL`, `OCCUPANCY` or `MAX_OF_BOTH`, defaulting to `MAX_OF_BOTH`. `ot_budget_cents` is the hard ceiling on cumulative accepted-overtime spend for the whole cycle, an integer of zero or more, defaulting to 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand currency units. `intraday_alert_short_threshold` is the number of short intervals at or above which the cycle auto-escalates its posture, an integer of at least 1, defaulting to 3.

A freshly created cycle is initialised with `posture` at `OPEN`, `version` at 1, `reforecast_generation` at 0, and every derived aggregate at its zero value: `short_interval_count`, `total_scheduled_hours`, `ot_spend_committed_cents`, `ot_hours_committed`, `headcount_active` and `pipeline_fte` all read zero, while `requirements_locked_at` and `published_at` are null. Those two timestamps are stamped from the logical clock the first time the posture reaches `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` and `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` respectively. Reads of a cycle expose every one of these fields, and cycles can be listed with an optional `posture` filter alongside the usual pagination parameters.

Advancing the posture is a single operator capability that takes one field, `target_posture`, and runs the transition declared for the cycle's current posture. Locking requirements moves `OPEN → REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` and is the act that makes schedule publication legal; it is refused unless at least one forecast group of the cycle has an `interval_count` of 1 or more, meaning some demand grid actually exists to lock. On success it stamps `requirements_locked_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `short_interval_count` across every interval plan of the cycle, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. The reverse move `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED → OPEN` reopens forecasting after a requirements review; it is legal only while no shift of the cycle has `publication_status` equal to `PUBLISHED`, and it clears `requirements_locked_at` back to null and increments `version`. Declaring the schedule live moves `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED → SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` — the work of a `SchedulingManager` rather than a planner — and requires that at least one shift of the cycle already carries `publication_status` equal to `PUBLISHED`; it stamps `published_at` and increments `version`. Finally, freezing is available from all four live postures — `OPEN → FROZEN`, `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED → FROZEN`, `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED → FROZEN` and `INTRADAY_ALERT → FROZEN` — carries no preconditions at all, increments `version`, and is irreversible.

Underneath the cycle sits the **labour agreement**, registered by a `CapacityPlanner` and the source of every safety ceiling the scheduler must respect. Its `code` is an identifier of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique across all agreements. `min_rest_hours` is a number strictly greater than zero giving the minimum rest between the end of one shift and the start of the agent's next non-overlapping shift; the comparison passes when the observed gap is greater than or *equal to* this value. `max_weekly_hours`, also strictly positive, ceilings an agent's committed scheduled hours after any additive change. `max_consecutive_days` is an integer of at least 1 bounding how many consecutive service dates the agent may carry a non-cancelled shift across. `vto_min_notice_hours` is a number of zero or more giving the minimum lead time between the logical clock and a shift's start for a voluntary-time-off acceptance to be legal, and again equality passes. `preference_honor_floor_pct` is a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive, carried at four decimal places, giving the minimum share of the agreement's awarded bids that must land on the bidder's first-choice template for an award run to be legal. `ot_premium_multiplier` is a number of at least 1.00, defaulting to 1.00, applied on top of the cycle's overtime tier multiplier. One derived field, `enrolled_agent_count`, tracks how many agents currently reference the agreement while their employment `status` is `ACTIVE` or `LEAVE`; terminated agents drop out of the count. Agreements are readable individually and listable with an optional `code` filter.

### 3.2. Forecasting Demand and Deriving the Staffing Requirement

Demand is described by **forecast groups**, each one a single combination of contact channel and routing skill inside a cycle. A `WorkforceAnalyst` creates a group by naming its parent cycle in the payload as `planning_cycle_id`, its `channel` — one of `VOICE`, `CHAT`, `EMAIL` or `SOCIAL` — and its `skill_code`, an uppercase snake-case string of 1 to 30 characters that is later matched against an agent's skill list and against a shift template's skill. Because forecast-group creation is a demand-definition act, it is only legal while the cycle's posture is `OPEN`.

The group's shape in time comes from four inputs. `base_daily_volume` is the un-seasonalised contact count for a full service day, an integer of zero or more, and `base_aht_seconds` is the un-inflated average handle time in whole seconds, at least 1. `operating_start_minute` and `operating_end_minute` are minutes past midnight UTC bounding a half-open operating window: the start is at least 0 and strictly less than 1440, the end is strictly greater than the start and at most 1440, and both must be exact multiples of the cycle's `interval_minutes`. The intraday shape itself is `intraday_curve`, an array of strictly positive weights, one per operating interval, ordered by interval index ascending; its length must equal the operating window's width in minutes divided by the cycle's interval width, and it is normalised at read time by dividing each element by the array's sum rather than being required to sum to one. Day-of-week seasonality is `dow_multipliers`, an array of exactly seven numbers of zero or more, indexed by ISO weekday with Monday at index 0.

Four further inputs describe the service the group is meant to deliver. `service_level_target_pct` is the share of contacts to be answered within the answer-time target, strictly greater than 0 and at most 1, at four decimal places; it is the field that selects the service-level uplift band in the staffing formula. `service_level_seconds` is the paired answer-time target in whole seconds, at least 1; it is recorded for reporting and plays no part in the staffing arithmetic. `occupancy_target_pct` is the maximum share of a staffed agent's time that may be spent handling contacts, strictly greater than 0 and at most 1, at four decimal places. `shrinkage_pct` is the share of staffed time lost to breaks, training, absence and everything else, at least 0 and strictly less than 1, at four decimal places. A new group starts with `reforecast_multiplier` at 1.0000 and `interval_count` at 0. Groups are readable individually and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id`, `channel` and `skill_code` filters.

Generating the grid is a separate act, also reserved to a `WorkforceAnalyst` and also legal only while the posture is `OPEN`. The caller names one `service_date`, and the system mints one **interval plan** per interval of the group's operating window on that date — the operating window's width in minutes divided by the cycle's interval width, so a 08:00-to-20:00 window at half-hour intervals produces twenty-four rows. The rows come back ordered by `interval_index`, the zero-based position of the interval inside the operating window. Each row derives `interval_start` as the service date at the group's `operating_start_minute` plus `interval_index` multiplied by the cycle's interval width, expressed in UTC and inclusive, and `interval_end` as `interval_start` plus the cycle's interval width, exclusive. Every piece of interval arithmetic in this system — event-window overlap, shift coverage, routing adjustments — treats an interval as the half-open span from its start up to but excluding its end. The service date must fall inside the cycle horizon, from `horizon_start_date` through `horizon_end_date` inclusive; a date outside it is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_HORIZON` (HTTP 422). A grid may be generated once per group and date: a repeat attempt for the same `forecast_group_id` and `service_date` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INTERVALS` (HTTP 409). Successful generation runs the whole forecasting chain on each new row and then recomputes the group's `interval_count` and the cycle's `short_interval_count`.

**Volume forecasting.** For one interval, the system first derives the interval's share of the day: it sums every element of the group's `intraday_curve`, then divides the element at this interval's `interval_index` by that sum. It then takes the day-of-week multiplier — the element of `dow_multipliers` at the ISO weekday index of the interval's service date, with Monday at index 0 — and multiplies the group's `base_daily_volume` by that multiplier and by the interval's share. That product is the raw, driver-free expectation. It is then multiplied by one plus the interval's `applied_event_uplift_pct` (Section 3.3) and by the group's `reforecast_multiplier`, and the fully combined product is rounded half up to a whole number **exactly once** to give `volume_forecast`. There is no intermediate rounding: rounding the raw expectation before applying the uplift, or rounding after the uplift but before the multiplier, produces off-by-one volumes and is wrong.

**Handle-time forecasting.** Handle time inflates with event-driven volume but only so far. The system computes a dragged handle time as the group's `base_aht_seconds` multiplied by one plus three-tenths of the interval's `applied_event_uplift_pct`, and a ceiling as `base_aht_seconds` multiplied by 1.35 — a hard cap thirty-five percent above baseline. `aht_forecast_seconds` is the smaller of those two figures, rounded half up to whole seconds. The reforecast multiplier deliberately does **not** touch handle time; it moves volume only.

**The staffing requirement, in two variants.** The workload of an interval, in erlangs, is `volume_forecast` multiplied by `aht_forecast_seconds` and divided by the interval's length in seconds, which is the cycle's `interval_minutes` multiplied by 60. That workload is carried at full precision. From it the system always computes and stores *both* staffing variants. The service-level variant, stored in `required_service_level`, inflates the workload by a band selected from the group's `service_level_target_pct`: a target of 0.90 or higher takes an uplift of 0.35; otherwise a target of 0.80 or higher takes 0.22; otherwise a target of 0.70 or higher takes 0.12; otherwise the uplift is 0.05. The inflated workload — workload multiplied by one plus that uplift — is then divided by one minus the group's `shrinkage_pct` and rounded **up** to the next whole agent. The occupancy variant, stored in `required_occupancy_capped`, ignores the service-level band entirely: it divides the raw workload by the group's `occupancy_target_pct`, divides that by one minus `shrinkage_pct`, and likewise rounds **up** to the next whole agent. Both variants use rounding up, never rounding half up, so a fractional agent is always a whole agent. The two variants genuinely disagree — the service-level variant dominates when the answer-rate target is aggressive relative to the occupancy target, the occupancy variant dominates when the occupancy ceiling is tight — and which one is authoritative is decided by the cycle's `requirement_basis`: `SERVICE_LEVEL` selects `required_service_level`, `OCCUPANCY` selects `required_occupancy_capped`, and `MAX_OF_BOTH` selects whichever of the two is larger. The chosen number is stored in `required_agents`.

**Coverage.** Against that requirement the system counts supply. `scheduled_agents` is the number of shifts whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose template skill matches the group's `skill_code`, and whose own half-open span from `start_at` up to `end_at` **fully contains** the interval's half-open span — a shift that covers only part of the interval does not count. Routing moves adjust that headcount: `routing_inflow_agents` sums the `agents_shifted` of every accepted routing adjustment whose target group is this group and whose interval window matches this interval, and `routing_outflow_agents` does the same for accepted routing adjustments whose source group is this group. `effective_scheduled_agents` is `scheduled_agents` plus the inflow minus the outflow. `coverage_gap` is the requirement minus the effective staffing, floored at zero so it is never negative, and `coverage_state` is `COVERED` when the gap is exactly zero and `SHORT` otherwise.

**Absorbable and unserved volume.** The same interval also carries an outsourcing picture. `internal_capacity_volume` is the number of contacts the internally staffed agents can absorb: `effective_scheduled_agents` multiplied by one minus `shrinkage_pct`, multiplied by `occupancy_target_pct`, multiplied by the interval's length in seconds, divided by `aht_forecast_seconds`, and then rounded **down** to a whole number, because partial capacity never counts. `allocatable_volume` is `volume_forecast` minus that internal capacity, floored at zero. `partner_committed_volume` is the sum of `allocated_volume` over the interval's partner allocations whose status is `COMMITTED` or `SETTLED`; proposed and revoked allocations contribute nothing, which means settling a commitment leaves the interval's committed volume unchanged while revoking one releases it. `unserved_volume` is `allocatable_volume` minus `partner_committed_volume`, floored at zero. All four fields are integers, and they are recomputed whenever the interval's volume, handle time, effective staffing or partner commitment changes.

Interval plans are readable individually and listable with optional `forecast_group_id`, `service_date` and `coverage_state` filters. A separate computed read rolls the whole cycle up: it returns `planning_cycle_id`, the current `posture`, `interval_count` as the number of interval plans in the cycle, `short_interval_count`, and cycle-wide sums named `total_required_agents`, `total_effective_scheduled_agents`, `total_coverage_gap`, `total_allocatable_volume`, `total_partner_committed_volume` and `total_unserved_volume`, each the arithmetic sum of the corresponding per-interval field. An optional `service_date` query parameter narrows the rollup to a single service date.

### 3.3. Marketing Drivers and the Uplift Cascade

Campaigns move demand, and the forecast has to know about them before they land. A `WorkforceAnalyst` registers a **marketing event** against a cycle by supplying `planning_cycle_id`, a campaign label in `name` of 1 to 80 characters, the claimed `uplift_pct`, a `confidence_tier`, and the driver window as `window_start` and `window_end` — both ISO 8601 UTC instants, the start inclusive and the end exclusive, with the end required to be strictly after the start. The raw `uplift_pct` is a fraction expressed at four decimal places, strictly greater than 0 and at most 3.0000, where 0.50 means a claimed fifty-percent volume increase. `confidence_tier` must be `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW`.

Scope is declared by an exactly-one-of pair. Either the caller supplies `forecast_group_id`, in which case the driver applies to that single forecast group, or the caller supplies `channel_scope` — one of `VOICE`, `CHAT`, `EMAIL` or `SOCIAL` — in which case the driver applies to every forecast group of the cycle whose `channel` equals it. Both default to null; `channel_scope` is required exactly when `forecast_group_id` is absent and forbidden when it is present, and a payload that supplies both or neither is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. An optional `supersedes_event_id` points at another marketing event this one replaces, a self-reference within the same entity family.

A newly registered driver is created with `status` at `DRAFT`, and a draft driver perturbs nothing at all. Creation does compute `weighted_uplift_pct` immediately: the raw `uplift_pct` multiplied by the confidence weight of its tier, where `HIGH` weighs 1.0, `MEDIUM` weighs 0.6 and `LOW` weighs 0.3, stored at four decimal places (it defaults to 0.0000 before that computation). Events are readable individually, addressed by `marketing_event_id`, and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id`, `forecast_group_id`, `supersedes_event_id` and `status` filters.

Driving the lifecycle takes a single `target_status` field. **Confirmation** — the `DRAFT → CONFIRMED` move — is what injects uplift into the forecast, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. It requires the parent cycle not to be frozen and requires the event's `window_end` to be strictly after its `window_start`; a window that fails that test is refused with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). On success, every interval plan in the event's scope whose half-open interval span overlaps the event's half-open window is fully recomputed: `volume_forecast`, `aht_forecast_seconds`, `applied_event_uplift_pct`, both staffing variants, `required_agents`, `coverage_gap` and `coverage_state`, and then `internal_capacity_volume`, `allocatable_volume` and `unserved_volume` on those same intervals. The cycle's `short_interval_count` is then recomputed and the automatic posture edges described in Section 3.7 are evaluated. Finally, if `supersedes_event_id` was set and the referenced event's own `status` is `CONFIRMED`, that referenced event is transitioned `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` in the same request and its uplift is dropped from the very same recompute — so a replacement campaign never double-counts with the campaign it replaces.

The remaining three moves are narrower. Cancelling a draft — `DRAFT → CANCELLED` — requires only that the cycle is not frozen and has no side effects at all, because a draft was never contributing anything. Cancelling a confirmed driver — `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, reachable either from an operator's explicit request or from another event's supersede cascade — likewise requires only that the cycle is not frozen, and it recomputes every interval the event overlapped with the event's weighted uplift removed, then recomputes `short_interval_count` and re-evaluates the posture edges. Marking a driver `REALIZED` — the `CONFIRMED → REALIZED` move — requires that the cycle is not frozen and that the logical clock has reached or passed the event's `window_end`; requesting it earlier is refused with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_INVALID`. Realisation has no side effects, and that is the point: a realised driver **keeps** contributing its uplift to the intervals it already inflated, unlike a cancelled one, so the intervals need no recompute. `CANCELLED` and `REALIZED` are both terminal.

The uplift arithmetic itself is confidence-weighted and capped. For one interval, the system collects every in-scope driver — a marketing event of the group's own cycle whose `status` is either `CONFIRMED` or `REALIZED`, which is either scoped directly to this group or scoped by channel to the group's `channel`, and whose half-open window overlaps this interval's half-open span. Drivers in `DRAFT` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. Each in-scope driver contributes its raw `uplift_pct` multiplied by its tier's confidence weight, those contributions are added together, and the sum is capped at 1.50 — no interval may ever be inflated by more than one hundred and fifty percent of its driver-free expectation no matter how many campaigns pile onto it. The capped result is stored as the interval's `applied_event_uplift_pct` at four decimal places and is the figure the volume and handle-time formulas of Section 3.2 consume.

### 3.4. Building Supply: the Roster and the Hiring Pipeline

An agent is created by a `SchedulingManager` who names the governing agreement as `labor_agreement_id` and supplies a payroll identifier in `employee_code` — 1 to 40 characters, unique across all agents — a display name in `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and the `skills` array: a non-empty list of uppercase snake-case routing skill codes, membership in which is what makes an agent eligible for a forecast group's skill or a template's skill. `seniority_rank` is an integer of at least 1 where 1 is the most senior and, in a tie, the smaller number always wins. `weekly_contracted_hours` is a positive number recorded for reporting only, and `hourly_rate_cents` is a positive integer straight-time rate that serves as the base for every overtime price. A new agent starts with `status` at `ACTIVE`, `hiring_class_id` at null, `scheduled_hours_committed` at zero, `adherence_pct` at 1.0000, `coaching_flag_count` at zero and `coaching_status` at `NONE`; creation also recomputes the agreement's `enrolled_agent_count`. Agents are readable individually and listable with optional `labor_agreement_id`, `hiring_class_id`, `status` and `skill_code` filters, where the skill filter matches agents whose `skills` array contains the given code.

Employment state and a few mutable attributes travel through the same partial-update capability, which accepts an optional `target_status` plus optional replacement values for `skills`, `seniority_rank` and `hourly_rate_cents`. When `target_status` is present, the agent's employment state machine runs. `ACTIVE → LEAVE` and `LEAVE → ACTIVE` both carry no preconditions; each recomputes `headcount_active` on every cycle in which the agent holds a shift whose `publication_status` is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`. `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and `LEAVE → TERMINATED` each require that the agent holds no shift at all with `publication_status` equal to `PUBLISHED` — you may not terminate somebody who is still on a live schedule — and each recomputes the agreement's `enrolled_agent_count` together with cycle headcount. `TERMINATED` is terminal, and only `ACTIVE` agents are eligible for bid awards and intraday offers.

Cycle-level workforce aggregates follow from those states. `headcount_active` counts the **distinct** agents whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and who satisfy at least one of two conditions: either the agent holds a shift belonging to this cycle whose `publication_status` is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, or the agent's `hiring_class_id` points at a hiring class of this cycle whose own `status` is `GRADUATED`. An agent satisfying both conditions is still counted once. `pipeline_fte` is the sum of `effective_fte_at_horizon_end` over the cycle's hiring classes whose `status` is `CHARTERED` or `IN_TRAINING`, rounded half up to two decimal places — a graduated class has left the pipeline and a cancelled or merely planned class was never in it. `total_scheduled_hours` is the sum of `paid_hours` over the cycle's shifts whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, rounded half up to two decimal places. All three are recomputed on every shift publication change, every agent status change and every hiring-class status change.

The pipeline itself is planned by a `CapacityPlanner` as a **hiring class**. Its payload names `planning_cycle_id` (the cycle whose pipeline it feeds) and `labor_agreement_id` (the agreement every graduate is enrolled under), a `cohort_name` of 1 to 80 characters that must be unique across all classes, and a single `skill_code` of 1 to 30 characters that every graduate is minted with. `start_date` is the ISO date on which training week 1 begins. `seats` is the number of hires seated, between 1 and 200 inclusive. `training_weeks` is the number of classroom weeks during which graduates contribute nothing productive, between 1 and 12 inclusive. `nesting_weeks` is the number of post-classroom weeks during which each graduate contributes half of a full-time equivalent, between 0 and 8 inclusive. `expected_attrition_pct` is the share of seats expected not to graduate, at least 0 and strictly less than 1, at four decimal places. `graduate_hourly_rate_cents` and `graduate_weekly_hours` are the rate and contracted hours stamped onto every agent the class eventually mints, a positive integer and a positive number respectively.

Creation puts the class at `status` `PLANNED` and immediately derives its ramp. `expected_graduates` is `seats` multiplied by one minus `expected_attrition_pct`, rounded **down** to a whole number — so twenty seats at 0.1500 attrition yields seventeen expected graduates, not seventeen-point-something. `nesting_from_date` is `start_date` advanced by `training_weeks` multiplied by seven days. `productive_from_date` is `start_date` advanced by the sum of `training_weeks` and `nesting_weeks`, multiplied by seven days. `effective_fte_at_horizon_end` then evaluates the ramp at the cycle's `horizon_end_date`: if that date falls strictly before `nesting_from_date`, the class contributes 0.00; otherwise, if it falls strictly before `productive_from_date`, the class contributes `expected_graduates` multiplied by one half, rounded half up to two decimal places; otherwise the class contributes the full `expected_graduates`, expressed to two decimal places. Both comparisons are strict, so a horizon ending exactly on `productive_from_date` counts the cohort as fully productive. `graduated_agent_count` starts at zero and simply counts the agents whose `hiring_class_id` points back at this class. Classes are readable individually and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id`, `labor_agreement_id` and `status` filters.

The class lifecycle is driven by a `target_status` field for its three non-graduating moves. `PLANNED → CHARTERED` requires only that the cycle is not frozen and recomputes `effective_fte_at_horizon_end` together with the cycle's `pipeline_fte` — this is the transition that puts the cohort into the pipeline. `CHARTERED → IN_TRAINING` requires that the logical clock has reached or passed the class's `start_date` at midnight UTC, and likewise recomputes the class FTE and the cycle pipeline. Cancellation is available from three states: `PLANNED → CANCELLED` carries no preconditions and no side effects, while `CHARTERED → CANCELLED` and `IN_TRAINING → CANCELLED` also carry no preconditions but each recompute the cycle's `pipeline_fte` because the cohort is leaving the pipeline.

**Graduation** is a separate capability with no request body and the largest minting side effect in the system. `IN_TRAINING → GRADUATED` requires that the cycle is not frozen and that the logical clock has reached or passed `nesting_from_date` at midnight UTC — graduates leave the classroom into nesting, so nesting is the earliest legal graduation. On success the system mints exactly `expected_graduates` agent rows. Each is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `hiring_class_id` pointing at this class, a `skills` array containing exactly the class's single `skill_code`, `labor_agreement_id` copied from the class, `hourly_rate_cents` set from `graduate_hourly_rate_cents` and `weekly_contracted_hours` set from `graduate_weekly_hours`. Their `employee_code` values are generated from the cohort label with a three-digit zero-padded ascending index — the cohort name followed by a hyphen and `001`, then `002`, and so on up to the number of graduates — assigned in ascending index order. The class's `graduated_agent_count` is set to `expected_graduates`. The agreement's `enrolled_agent_count`, the cycle's `headcount_active` and the cycle's `pipeline_fte` are all then recomputed: in one request the cohort leaves the pipeline and its graduates enter the active headcount. `GRADUATED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.5. Shift Templates, Bid Rounds and the Award Waterfall

A **shift template** is a biddable pattern, defined by a `SchedulingManager` inside one cycle named as `planning_cycle_id`. Its `code` is a bidder-facing label such as "T-MON-AM", 1 to 40 characters, unique within its cycle. `skill_code` is the routing skill of 1 to 30 characters an agent must hold in order to bid on the template or to be scheduled against it. `start_time_local` is the shift start written as two digits, a colon and two digits, with the hour between 00 and 23 and the minute between 00 and 59; the benchmark runs a single UTC zone, so that string is interpreted directly as UTC. `duration_minutes` is the elapsed shift length including the unpaid break, at least 60 and at most 720. `unpaid_break_minutes` defaults to 30, must be at least 0, and must be strictly less than `duration_minutes`; it is the quantity subtracted from elapsed time to derive paid hours. `days_of_week` is a non-empty array of distinct ISO weekday indexes between 0 and 6 inclusive with Monday at 0, and it is the array the award cascade walks when minting shifts. `slots_total` is the number of bidders the template can absorb in one award run, at least 1, and `slots_awarded` starts at zero and counts the awarded bids currently pointing at this template. Templates are readable individually and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id` and `skill_code` filters.

A **bid round** collects the bids. A `SchedulingManager` opens one against a cycle by supplying `planning_cycle_id`, a `code` label of 1 to 40 characters unique within the cycle, and an optional `max_reopen_count` — an integer of zero or more, defaulting to 2 — bounding how many times the round may re-enter its open state after leaving it. A new round starts at `status` `DRAFT` with `reopen_count` at zero, `awarded_bid_count` and `unfilled_bid_count` at zero, and `preference_honor_rate` at null. Rounds are readable individually and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id` and `status` filters.

The round lifecycle is driven by a `target_status` field and is deliberately cyclic. `DRAFT → OPEN` requires the parent cycle's `posture` to be either `OPEN` or `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED` and requires at least one shift template to exist for the round's cycle — there is nothing to bid on otherwise; it has no further side effects. `OPEN → SCORING` requires that at least one bid of the round is in `SUBMITTED`, and its effect is to close intake: any further bid submitted against the round from that moment is refused with `ERR_BID_ROUND_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). `SCORING → OPEN` reopens intake and requires that `reopen_count` is strictly less than `max_reopen_count`; it increments `reopen_count` by 1. `AWARDED → OPEN` closes the cycle back to intake and is the most consequential reopen: it requires `reopen_count` strictly less than `max_reopen_count` and requires that no shift minted by this round has `publication_status` equal to `PUBLISHED`. Its effects are to increment `reopen_count` by 1, reset every `AWARDED` and every `UNFILLED` bid of the round back to `SUBMITTED` with `awarded_shift_template_id` and `awarded_preference_index` cleared to null, cancel every shift the round minted that is still in `DRAFT` by moving it `DRAFT → CANCELLED` on its publication axis, reset `preference_honor_rate` to null and both `awarded_bid_count` and `unfilled_bid_count` to zero, and recompute `slots_awarded` on every affected template. Whenever either reopen edge would push `reopen_count` past `max_reopen_count`, the request is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Cancellation is available from three states: `DRAFT → CANCELLED` has no preconditions and no side effects, while `OPEN → CANCELLED` and `SCORING → CANCELLED` likewise have no preconditions but each set every `SUBMITTED` bid of the round to `WITHDRAWN`. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

An `Agent` submits a **bid** into an open round, naming `bid_round_id`, their own `agent_id` (which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`) and `preference_template_ids`: a non-empty, ordered array of distinct shift-template identifiers, most preferred first, so that index 0 is the bidder's first choice. Every identifier in the list must belong to a template of the round's own cycle, and the list may hold at most ten entries. Every template named in the list must carry a `skill_code` that is a member of the bidder's `skills`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SKILL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A bidder may hold only one live bid per round: if the agent already holds a bid in the same round whose `status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, the submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID` (HTTP 409). A new bid starts at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `awarded_shift_template_id` and `awarded_preference_index` both null. Bids are readable individually, addressed by `shift_bid_id`, and listable with optional `bid_round_id`, `agent_id`, `awarded_shift_template_id` and `status` filters. Withdrawal — the `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` move — requires that the round's `status` is `OPEN` and that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the bid's own `agent_id`; a withdrawn bid is terminal and is skipped by every later award run.

**The award run.** Awarding is a single capability with no request body, exercised by a `SchedulingManager` while the round is in `SCORING`, and it produces the `SCORING → AWARDED` transition. It requires the parent cycle not to be frozen. The algorithm is a strict seniority waterfall over the round's `SUBMITTED` bids; withdrawn bids are ignored, and the templates in play are those of the round's own cycle. Bidders are ordered by their agent's `seniority_rank` ascending — remember that 1 is most senior — and ties on seniority are broken by the bid's `created_at` ascending, which is the only sanctioned tie-break. Each template starts the run with a remaining-slot count equal to its `slots_total` minus its current `slots_awarded`. Bidders are then processed one at a time in that order. For each bidder, the system walks `preference_template_ids` from index 0 forward and stops at the first template that both carries a `skill_code` the bidder holds in `skills` and still has a remaining slot count greater than zero; that bid moves `SUBMITTED → AWARDED`, its `awarded_shift_template_id` is set to that template, its `awarded_preference_index` is set to the zero-based position at which the match occurred, and the template's remaining slot count is reduced by one. A bidder whose entire preference list is exhausted by the time their turn comes moves `SUBMITTED → UNFILLED`.

Once the sweep has notionally placed everybody, the honour rate is computed: the number of awarded bids whose `awarded_preference_index` is exactly 0 divided by the total number of awarded bids, rounded half up to four decimal places. When nothing was awarded the rate is null rather than zero.

Two legality gates then run, in this order, and both run **before any bid or shift row is written**. First, a run that can place nobody is refused outright with `ERR_TEMPLATE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) — because every template referenced by the round's submitted bids has already reached its `slots_total`, or matches no bidder's skills. Running this gate first is also what guarantees the honour rate is never compared while it is null. Second, the honour floor: the system takes the strictest floor among the labour agreements of the bidders who would be awarded, which is the **smallest** `preference_honor_floor_pct` across those agreements, and refuses the run with `ERR_PREFERENCE_FLOOR_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422) when the honour rate is strictly below it. A rate exactly equal to the floor is legal. Only once both gates pass does the run commit: winning bids are written as `AWARDED` with their template and preference index, losing bids as `UNFILLED`, and one shift is minted in `DRAFT` for each awarded bid for each entry of the awarded template's `days_of_week` that falls inside the cycle horizon. The run finally recomputes each template's `slots_awarded`, the round's `awarded_bid_count`, `unfilled_bid_count` and `preference_honor_rate`.

Two system-driven bid moves complete the picture. `AWARDED → SUBMITTED` happens inside the round's reopen cascade and requires that no shift minted for that bid is `PUBLISHED`; it clears `awarded_shift_template_id` and `awarded_preference_index` and cancels the draft shifts minted for the bid. `UNFILLED → SUBMITTED` happens inside the same cascade with no preconditions and no further effects, returning the loser to the pool for the next run.

### 3.6. Publishing the Schedule Under Labour-Agreement Ceilings

A shift can arrive two ways: minted in `DRAFT` by an award run, or created directly by a `SchedulingManager` as the manual counterpart of that cascade. The manual create names `planning_cycle_id`, `agent_id`, `shift_template_id` and `service_date`, where the service date must fall inside the cycle horizon inclusive — a date outside it is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_HORIZON` (HTTP 422). From the template the system derives `start_at` as the service date at the template's `start_time_local` in UTC, `end_at` as `start_at` advanced by the template's `duration_minutes`, and `unpaid_break_seconds` as the template's `unpaid_break_minutes` multiplied by 60 and frozen at creation. `scheduled_seconds` is the difference between `end_at` and `start_at` in seconds minus `unpaid_break_seconds`, recomputed whenever `end_at` moves, and `paid_hours` is `scheduled_seconds` divided by 3600 at two decimal places. Overtime and voluntary time off both reach `paid_hours` only through `end_at`, so they can never be double-counted. `ot_hours` and `vto_hours` are two-decimal rollups of the accepted overtime and voluntary-time-off hours recorded against the shift. A shift is created with `publication_status` `DRAFT` and `adherence_status` `PENDING`, and it contributes nothing to coverage while it is in `DRAFT`.

Every shift carries **two independent state axes on one row**, and the observable state of a shift is the pair. `publication_status` moves through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and the terminal `CANCELLED`. `adherence_status` moves through `PENDING`, `ON_TRACK`, `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` and `EXCUSED`, with no terminal state at all. Movement on the publication axis never reads or writes the adherence axis, and movement on the adherence axis never reads or writes the publication axis. Shifts are readable individually and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id`, `agent_id`, `shift_template_id`, `service_date`, `publication_status` and `adherence_status` filters — either axis can be filtered on independently.

Moving the publication axis takes one field, `target_publication_status`. **Publication** is the `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` move and the most heavily guarded transition in the system. Seven preconditions must hold. The cycle's `posture` must be `REQUIREMENTS_LOCKED`, `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` or `INTRADAY_ALERT`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_LOCKED`. The agent's employment `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The template's `skill_code` must be a member of the agent's `skills`, otherwise `ERR_SKILL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The remaining four are the labour-agreement ceilings, and they are evaluated in a fixed order that matters when a candidate shift breaches several at once: first the rest rule, then the overlap rule, then the weekly-hours rule, then the consecutive-days rule.

The **rest rule** compares the gap between the end of one shift and the start of the agent's next non-overlapping shift against the agreement's `min_rest_hours`; the check passes when the gap is greater than or equal to that value, and a gap strictly below it is refused with `ERR_MIN_REST_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). A neighbouring shift whose own half-open span actually overlaps the candidate is deliberately **skipped** by the rest rule rather than being treated as a zero-hour gap — which is precisely what keeps the next rule reachable. The **overlap rule** then requires that the agent holds no other non-cancelled shift whose half-open span from `start_at` up to `end_at` overlaps the candidate's; a collision is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The **weekly-hours rule** requires that the agent's `scheduled_hours_committed` plus this shift's `paid_hours` does not exceed the agreement's `max_weekly_hours`; a breach is refused with `ERR_MAX_WEEKLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The **consecutive-days rule** requires that the agent's longest run of consecutive service dates carrying a non-cancelled shift stays at or below the agreement's `max_consecutive_days`; a longer run is refused with `ERR_MAX_CONSECUTIVE_DAYS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

A successful publication cascades four ways. The agent's `scheduled_hours_committed` — the sum of `paid_hours` over that agent's shifts whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, at two decimal places — and the cycle's `total_scheduled_hours` are both recomputed. Every interval plan the shift covers has `scheduled_agents`, `effective_scheduled_agents`, `coverage_gap`, `coverage_state`, `internal_capacity_volume`, `allocatable_volume` and `unserved_volume` recomputed. The cycle's `short_interval_count` is recomputed and the automatic posture edges are then evaluated. And the cycle's `headcount_active` is recomputed, because a draft or published shift is one of the two ways an agent enters the cycle's headcount.

**Cancellation** exists on both sides of publication and behaves differently on each. `PUBLISHED → CANCELLED` requires that the cycle is not frozen and, critically, that no intraday action against this shift is in `ACCEPTED` — a shift carrying live overtime or voluntary time off may not be pulled out from under it. On success it recomputes the agent's `scheduled_hours_committed` and the cycle's `total_scheduled_hours`, recomputes coverage on every interval the shift previously covered and re-evaluates the posture edges, and recomputes the agent's `coaching_flag_count` and `adherence_pct`, since a cancelled shift leaves the agent's scored set entirely. `DRAFT → CANCELLED` — reachable either from an operator's request or from the bid-round reopen cascade — requires only that the cycle is not frozen and triggers no recompute at all, because a draft shift was never contributing to coverage. `CANCELLED` is terminal on this axis.

### 3.7. Intraday Management: Actuals, Reforecasting and the Three Levers

Once the week is live, the plan starts being wrong, and the intraday desk's job is to notice and to respond.

**Posting actuals.** An `IntradayController` or a `WorkforceAnalyst` posts observed performance against one interval by supplying `actual_volume` and `actual_aht_seconds`, the observed contact count and the observed average handle time. Both are null until posted. Posting stores them and derives `observed_ratio` as `actual_volume` divided by `volume_forecast` at the instant of posting, at four decimal places; when the interval's `volume_forecast` is zero the ratio is stored as null rather than zero, because the division is undefined and zero would be a lie. Posting actuals deliberately does **not** move the forecast group's `reforecast_multiplier` — that only happens on an explicit reforecast run — but it does count as a perturbation of the forecast picture, so the cycle's `short_interval_count` is recomputed afterwards and the automatic posture edges are evaluated.

**The automatic posture edges.** The cycle watches its own shortfall count. Any recompute of `short_interval_count` — whether it came from a marketing-driver confirmation or cancellation, a reforecast run, an actuals posting, a routing adjustment, a shift publication or cancellation, or an intraday acceptance or revocation — is immediately followed by an evaluation of two system-driven transitions. If the cycle is in `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` and the recomputed `short_interval_count` is greater than or equal to the cycle's `intraday_alert_short_threshold`, the cycle escalates `SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED → INTRADAY_ALERT` and its `version` increments by 1. If the cycle is in `INTRADAY_ALERT` and the recomputed count is strictly below the threshold, it de-escalates `INTRADAY_ALERT → SCHEDULE_PUBLISHED` and its `version` increments by 1. The pair is cyclic and can flip repeatedly across a week; both edges happen inside the same request that perturbed the forecast, never on a later read. No operator drives either edge.

**Reforecasting.** A `WorkforceAnalyst` or a `CapacityPlanner` runs a reforecast against a cycle. The request carries `expected_cycle_version` (the stale-version guard of Section 2.7), `effective_from_interval_start` marking the boundary between history and the forward window, and optionally `forecast_group_id` to narrow the run to a single forecast group rather than every group of the cycle. The `effective_from_interval_start` instant must fall inside the cycle horizon; outside it, the run is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_HORIZON` (HTTP 422).

For each forecast group in scope the system replays posted history in chronological order. The scored input set is the group's interval plans whose `actual_volume` is not null, whose `volume_forecast` is greater than zero, and whose `interval_start` is strictly before `effective_from_interval_start` — only history is smoothed, and the forward window is what gets re-forecast. Those intervals are sorted by `interval_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. A running accumulator is seeded at 1.0 on **every** run, so the outcome depends on the chronological order of the intervals and never on the order in which the actuals happened to be posted. Stepping through the sorted intervals, the system stores each interval's `observed_ratio` as its `actual_volume` divided by its `volume_forecast` rounded half up to four decimal places, then advances the accumulator to four-tenths of that same raw ratio plus six-tenths of the accumulator's previous value, and stores the new accumulator into that interval's `smoothed_ratio`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The accumulator itself is carried at full precision between steps and rounded only when stored, so per-step rounding never compounds.

At the end of the replay, the group's `reforecast_multiplier` is the final accumulator rounded half up to four decimal places and then clamped into the band from 0.50 to 2.00 inclusive — a group whose actuals ran at triple its forecast is capped at double, and a group that collapsed to a tenth is floored at half. The system then re-runs the volume forecast, the handle-time forecast, both staffing variants and the absorbable-volume chain for every interval plan of the group whose `interval_start` is at or after `effective_from_interval_start`, increments the cycle's `reforecast_generation` by 1, recomputes `short_interval_count`, evaluates the posture edges, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

**The three levers.** An `IntradayController` creates an intraday action naming `planning_cycle_id`, the lever in `kind` — one of `OT_OFFER`, `VTO_OFFER` or `ROUTING_ADJUSTMENT` — and `expires_at`, the logical-clock instant from which a response is refused. Which of the remaining fields are required depends entirely on the lever.

An overtime offer names the counterparty in `agent_id`, the shift it extends in `schedule_shift_id`, the group whose shortfall it covers in `target_forecast_group_id`, and the hours it adds in `hours`. That target group's `skill_code` must be a member of the offered agent's `skills`, otherwise the creation is refused with `ERR_SKILL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). `hours` must be a multiple of 0.5 and lie between 0.5 and 8.0 inclusive. `source_forecast_group_id`, `agents_shifted` and `interval_start` are all null on an overtime offer.

A voluntary-time-off offer likewise names `agent_id`, `schedule_shift_id` and `hours` under the same multiple-of-a-half and 0.5-to-8.0 rules, but has no `target_forecast_group_id`, no `source_forecast_group_id`, no `agents_shifted` and no `interval_start` — it releases hours rather than covering a queue.

A routing adjustment is an operator lever rather than an offer, so it names no agent and no shift: `agent_id`, `schedule_shift_id` and `hours` are all null. It names `target_forecast_group_id` as the group receiving staffing, `source_forecast_group_id` as the group losing it, `agents_shifted` as the whole number of agents rerouted (at least 1), and `interval_start` as the interval start of the interval-plan pair the reroute applies to.

Every action is created at `status` `OFFERED` with `cost_cents` at zero, `ot_multiplier_applied` at null and `responded_by_user_id` at null. Actions are readable individually, addressed by `intraday_action_id`, and listable with optional `planning_cycle_id`, `agent_id`, `schedule_shift_id`, `target_forecast_group_id`, `source_forecast_group_id`, `kind` and `status` filters.

A routing adjustment never sits waiting: the system auto-advances it `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` inside its own creation request, leaving `responded_by_user_id` null because no agent answered. Its one precondition is that `agents_shifted` does not exceed the source group's interval's `effective_scheduled_agents` — you cannot reroute agents who are not staffed — and a breach is refused with `ERR_ROUTING_OUTFLOW_EXCEEDS_STAFFING` (HTTP 422). Acceptance recomputes `routing_inflow_agents` on the target group's matching interval and `routing_outflow_agents` on the source group's matching interval, then recomputes coverage on both, then the cycle's `short_interval_count` and the posture edges.

**Responding to an offer.** The offered `Agent` answers with a single `response` field carrying either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the action's `agent_id`. Both answers require the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at`; equality already counts as expired. If the clock has reached or passed `expires_at`, the response is refused and the action is moved `OFFERED → EXPIRED` in the very same request, returning `ERR_ACTION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409) with no ledger field moved anywhere. A decline is the simple case: `OFFERED → DECLINED` stamps `responded_by_user_id` with the acting identifier and does nothing else.

Acceptance is where the money and the schedule move. `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` requires the cycle not to be frozen, the acting identifier to match `agent_id`, and the clock to be strictly before `expires_at`. For an overtime offer it additionally requires that the cycle's overtime budget holds and that extending the shift keeps the agent's rest gap at or above the agreement's `min_rest_hours` and the agent's weekly committed total at or below the agreement's `max_weekly_hours` — the same three labour rules as publication apply here in the same relative order, so a rest breach surfaces as `ERR_MIN_REST_VIOLATION`, an overlap created by the extension surfaces as `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, and an hours breach surfaces as `ERR_MAX_WEEKLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`. For a voluntary-time-off offer it additionally requires that the notice period — the shift's `start_at` minus the current logical clock, expressed in hours — is greater than or equal to the agreement's `vto_min_notice_hours`, with shortfalls refused as `ERR_VTO_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422); and it requires that releasing the hours does not push any interval plan the shift covers from `COVERED` to `SHORT`, with breaches refused as `ERR_VTO_WOULD_BREACH_COVERAGE` (HTTP 409).

**Pricing overtime.** The cost of an accepted overtime offer is tiered on how much overtime the cycle has already committed, and the tier is read from the committed spend as it stood **before** this acceptance, so an offer never inflates its own tier. If the cycle's `ot_spend_committed_cents` before the acceptance is strictly below 20,000 cents — two hundred currency units — the tier multiplier is 1.50, the first tier. Otherwise, if it is strictly below 60,000 cents — six hundred currency units — the tier multiplier is 1.75, the second tier. Otherwise the tier multiplier is 2.00, the third tier. Both boundaries are strict, so a pre-acceptance committed spend of exactly 20,000 cents already sits in the second tier. The action's `ot_multiplier_applied` is stored as that tier multiplier multiplied by the agreement's `ot_premium_multiplier`, and `cost_cents` is the offer's `hours` multiplied by the agent's `hourly_rate_cents`, multiplied by the tier multiplier, multiplied by the agreement's `ot_premium_multiplier`, rounded half up to a whole cent. The budget gate is then evaluated with that freshly priced figure: if the cycle's `ot_spend_committed_cents` plus the new `cost_cents` would exceed the cycle's `ot_budget_cents`, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_OT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Both `ot_multiplier_applied` and `cost_cents` are frozen on the action at acceptance and are never recomputed afterwards.

An accepted overtime offer then extends the shift's `end_at` by `hours` and recomputes the shift's `scheduled_seconds`, `ot_hours` and `paid_hours`, the agent's `scheduled_hours_committed`, the cycle's `ot_hours_committed` (the two-decimal sum of `hours` over the cycle's accepted overtime actions), the cycle's `ot_spend_committed_cents` (the integer sum of `cost_cents` over the same set) and the cycle's `total_scheduled_hours`. Offers in `OFFERED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` contribute nothing to either overtime ledger. An accepted voluntary-time-off offer instead shortens `end_at` by `hours` and recomputes the shift's `scheduled_seconds`, `vto_hours` and `paid_hours`, the agent's `scheduled_hours_committed` and the cycle's `total_scheduled_hours`. Both acceptances stamp `responded_by_user_id`, then recompute coverage on every affected interval plan, the cycle's `short_interval_count` and the posture edges.

**Revocation.** An `IntradayController` may revoke an action with no request body, and revocation is available from two states: `OFFERED → REVOKED` and `ACCEPTED → REVOKED`. Both require only that the cycle is not frozen. Revoking a merely offered action has no further effect. Revoking an accepted overtime offer shortens the shift's `end_at` back by `hours`, subtracts **exactly** the frozen `cost_cents` from the cycle's `ot_spend_committed_cents` and `hours` from the cycle's `ot_hours_committed`, and recomputes the shift's `scheduled_seconds`, `ot_hours` and `paid_hours`, the agent's `scheduled_hours_committed` and the cycle's `total_scheduled_hours`. Because the cost was frozen at acceptance, the overtime ledger returns to precisely its pre-acceptance value even if the tier multiplier has since moved. Revoking an accepted voluntary-time-off offer restores `end_at` by adding `hours` back and recomputes the same shift, agent and cycle fields. Revoking an accepted routing adjustment drops both the inflow and the outflow contribution from the two intervals. Every revocation then recomputes coverage on every affected interval plan, the cycle's `short_interval_count` and the posture edges. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are all terminal on this lifecycle.

### 3.8. Adherence Monitoring, Coaching and Exception Adjudication

Adherence is the second, independent axis of a shift, and it may only be traversed while the shift's `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`; an observation or exception aimed at a shift that is not published is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). Every move on this axis also requires that the parent cycle is not frozen, and none of them touch the publication axis.

**Observations.** An `IntradayController` records an observation by supplying a single field, `out_of_adherence_seconds` — the latest observed count of non-adherent seconds. Each observation **replaces** the stored value wholesale rather than adding to it, and each observation also resets the shift's `excused_seconds` back to zero, discarding every prior excusal. That is deliberate: a fresh measurement supersedes the negotiated history.

The shift's own score follows. `adherence_pct` is the shift's `scheduled_seconds` minus its `out_of_adherence_seconds` plus its `excused_seconds`, all divided by its `scheduled_seconds`, rounded half up to four decimal places and clamped into the band from 0.0000 to 1.0000 inclusive. Recall that `scheduled_seconds` is the elapsed span from `start_at` to `end_at` in seconds minus `unpaid_break_seconds`, so overtime and voluntary time off change the denominator of this score as well as the schedule.

Where the new score lands decides which transition fires. A resulting `adherence_pct` greater than or equal to the coaching threshold of 0.8500 moves the axis to `ON_TRACK`: from `PENDING → ON_TRACK` on a first observation, or from `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE → ON_TRACK` when a better observation arrives. A resulting `adherence_pct` strictly below 0.8500 moves the axis to `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE`: from `PENDING → OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` on a first observation, from `ON_TRACK → OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` when a worse observation arrives, or from `EXCUSED → OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` when a fresh observation lands below the threshold again. The comparison is strict, so a score of exactly 0.8500 is on track and is **not** flagged. Every transition into `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` stamps `flagged_by_user_id` with the acting controller's `X-User-ID`, and that stored identifier is the value the segregation-of-duties check consumes when a coach later adjudicates. Every one of these six observation-driven transitions recomputes the shift's `adherence_pct` and then the agent's `adherence_pct`, `coaching_flag_count` and `coaching_status`.

**The agent-level rollup.** An agent's `adherence_pct` is seconds-weighted across their scored shifts, not an average of percentages. The system sums, over the scored set, each shift's `scheduled_seconds` minus its `out_of_adherence_seconds` plus its `excused_seconds`; it sums, over the same set, each shift's `scheduled_seconds`; and it divides the first sum by the second, rounding half up to four decimal places exactly once at the end so that per-shift rounding never accumulates. When the denominator is zero — an agent with no scored shift at all — the agent's `adherence_pct` is 1.0000. The scored set is the agent's shifts whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose `adherence_status` is anything other than `PENDING`; draft shifts, cancelled shifts and never-observed shifts are all excluded, which is why cancelling a published shift shifts the agent's score.

`coaching_flag_count` counts the agent's scored shifts that are **flagged**, where a shift is flagged when its `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, its `adherence_status` is `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE` and its own `adherence_pct` is strictly below 0.8500. `coaching_status` then follows a simple ratchet: it is `NONE` until the agent has ever been flagged, `COACHING_ACTIVE` while `coaching_flag_count` is 1 or more, and `CLEARED` when the count falls back to zero having previously been `COACHING_ACTIVE`. An agent who was never flagged never reaches `CLEARED`.

**Contesting a flag.** An `Agent` files an exception against their own out-of-adherence shift, naming `schedule_shift_id` and their own `agent_id` — which must equal both the shift's own agent reference and the acting `X-User-ID`. `reason_code` is the coded justification and must be one of `SYSTEM_OUTAGE`, `APPROVED_MEETING`, `COACHING_SESSION`, `MEDICAL` or `TRANSIT_DISRUPTION`. `duration_seconds` is the number of non-adherent seconds the agent asks to have excused, at least 1 and at most the shift's current `out_of_adherence_seconds` — an agent cannot ask to be excused for more than they were charged. `narrative` is free text of 1 to 500 characters. An exception's `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`. A new exception starts at `status` `SUBMITTED`, with `info_requested_count` at zero, `coach_note` at null and `decided_by_user_id` at null. Exceptions are readable individually, addressed by `adherence_exception_id`, and listable with optional `schedule_shift_id`, `agent_id` and `status` filters.

**Adjudication.** A `TeamCoach` records a branching decision by supplying `decision`, which must be `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_INFO`, plus an optional `coach_note` of free text. All three branches require the parent cycle not to be frozen, require the exception to be in `SUBMITTED` (otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_PENDING`, HTTP 409), and are subject to the segregation-of-duties check: the coach's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `flagged_by_user_id` recorded on the underlying shift, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

`SUBMITTED → APPROVED` additionally requires that the exception's `duration_seconds` is still at or below the shift's `out_of_adherence_seconds`, since a fresh observation may have moved that number since the exception was filed. Approval stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the deciding coach's identifier, then reaches across into the shift and moves its adherence axis `OUT_OF_ADHERENCE → EXCUSED`, adding the exception's `duration_seconds` to the shift's `excused_seconds`. That in turn recomputes the shift's `adherence_pct`, then the agent's `adherence_pct`, `coaching_flag_count` and `coaching_status` — so an approval on one entity can clear a coaching ratchet three hops away, setting `coaching_status` to `CLEARED` when the flag count falls to zero from `COACHING_ACTIVE`. `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` stamps `decided_by_user_id` and does nothing else; the flag stands. `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` records the `coach_note` and deliberately leaves `decided_by_user_id` null, because the branch is not terminal.

**The revision loop.** An exception sent back for information returns via a resubmission by the owning `Agent`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the exception's `agent_id` and whose cycle must not be frozen. The resubmission carries `narrative` and `duration_seconds`, both of which **replace** the stored values, and it moves the exception `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` while incrementing `info_requested_count` by 1. The three-way fork out of `SUBMITTED` reopens after every loop, so a case may be sent back more than once. `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

### 3.9. Outsourced Partner Allocation, Commitment and Settlement

When internal capacity cannot absorb a interval's forecast volume, the remainder goes to outsourced partners. A `SourcingManager` registers a partner with its `legal_name`, 1 to 120 characters and unique across all partners, and `skill_codes`, a non-empty array of uppercase snake-case routing skills the partner is certified to serve — a partner is a waterfall candidate for an interval only when this array contains the interval's forecast group's `skill_code`. `rate_per_contact_millicents` is a positive integer price per handled contact in thousandths of a cent, and it doubles as the ascending sort key of the allocation waterfall. `interval_max_volume` is the largest volume the partner will absorb in a single interval, at least 1, and is the waterfall cap while the partner is `ACTIVE`. `commitment_floor_volume` is the contractual minimum volume per committed allocation, at least 0 and at most `interval_max_volume`, and it doubles as the waterfall cap while the partner is on `PROBATION`. `performance_threshold_pct` is the rolling service-level attainment the partner must hold, strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 at four decimal places. `billing_basis` must be `COMMITMENT_FLOOR` or `DELIVERED_ONLY` and selects which settlement variant is authoritative.

A new partner starts at `status` `ACTIVE`, `version` 1, `performance_report_count` zero, `latest_sl_attainment_pcts` an empty list, `rolling_sl_attainment_pct` null, `consecutive_breach_count` zero, `committed_volume_outstanding` zero and `invoice_total_cents` zero. Partners are readable individually and listable with optional `status`, `billing_basis` and `skill_code` filters, where the skill filter matches partners whose `skill_codes` array contains the given code.

**The performance ladder.** A `SourcingManager` files a report with `period_end_date` and `sl_attainment_pct`. The pair is appended to `latest_sl_attainment_pcts` in call order — the list is append-only and never rewritten — and `performance_report_count` is incremented. The rolling attainment then reads the **last three** entries of that list in append order, using everything present when fewer than three reports exist: `rolling_sl_attainment_pct` is the sum of the attainment values in that window divided by the number of entries in it, rounded half up to four decimal places. A report **breaches** when the recomputed `rolling_sl_attainment_pct` is strictly below the partner's `performance_threshold_pct`; attainment exactly equal to the threshold is compliant. A breaching report increments `consecutive_breach_count` by one and a non-breaching report resets it to zero.

Three system-driven transitions follow from that evaluation, and each report also increments the partner's `version` by 1. `ACTIVE → PROBATION` fires on the first breach: `consecutive_breach_count` is set to 1 and the partner's waterfall cap drops from `interval_max_volume` to `commitment_floor_volume` for every subsequent allocation run. `PROBATION → ACTIVE` fires when a report does not breach: `consecutive_breach_count` resets to 0 and the full interval cap is restored. `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` fires when a second consecutive report breaches, taking `consecutive_breach_count` to 2. Suspension is terminal and drastic: every one of the partner's allocations in `PROPOSED` or `COMMITTED` is transitioned to `REVOKED`, which restores `committed_volume_outstanding` to zero and recomputes `partner_committed_volume` and `unserved_volume` on each affected interval. A suspended partner is permanently excluded from the waterfall, and any write referencing it is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409) — the same identifier used when a partner's `skill_codes` does not contain the interval's group `skill_code`.

**Proposing allocations.** A single allocation is proposed by naming `outsource_partner_id`, `interval_plan_id` and `allocated_volume`, an integer of at least 1. The allocation occupies exactly one interval plan; allocations sitting on different interval plans never aggregate against each other. The referenced partner must be eligible — not suspended, and certified for the interval's group skill — and `allocated_volume` must be at least the partner's `commitment_floor_volume` and at most the partner's applicable interval cap, which is `interval_max_volume` while the partner is `ACTIVE` and `commitment_floor_volume` while it is on `PROBATION`; a volume outside that band is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_VOLUME_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). A new allocation starts at `status` `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, `committed_by_user_id` null, `delivered_volume` null and all four settlement fields null. No ledger moves at proposal time. Allocations are readable individually, addressed by `partner_allocation_id`, and listable with optional `outsource_partner_id`, `interval_plan_id` and `status` filters — and a `PartnerRep` may only list to their own partner identifier.

**The allocation waterfall.** Rather than proposing by hand, a `SourcingManager` can ask the system to fill one interval, supplying only `interval_plan_id`. The remaining need is that interval's `allocatable_volume` minus its `partner_committed_volume`. Candidates are the partners whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` and whose `skill_codes` contains the interval's group `skill_code`; suspended partners are excluded outright. Candidates are ordered by `rate_per_contact_millicents` ascending — cheapest first — with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The system then walks that ordered list. It stops as soon as the remaining need has dropped to zero or below. For each candidate it computes a cap — `interval_max_volume` when the partner is `ACTIVE`, `commitment_floor_volume` when the partner is on `PROBATION` — and takes the smaller of that cap and the remaining need. If the amount it could take is strictly less than that partner's `commitment_floor_volume`, the partner is **skipped entirely** rather than under-filled, because the contractual minimum cannot be honoured; the walk continues to the next candidate. Otherwise it creates an allocation in `PROPOSED` with `allocated_volume` set to the taken amount and `proposed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting identifier, and reduces the remaining need by that amount. The response is the array of allocations created, in the order the waterfall created them, cheapest partner first; when no candidate qualifies the response is an empty array. The cheapest partner fills to its cap before the next is considered, and any trailing remainder is simply left unallocated where it stays visible in the interval's `unserved_volume`.

**Commitment.** `PROPOSED → COMMITTED` is the point at which partner capacity is actually consumed, and it is the most heavily guarded write on this surface. The request carries `expected_partner_version`, the stale-version echo of Section 2.7. Beyond that guard, commitment requires that the parent cycle is not frozen, that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the allocation's `proposed_by_user_id`, that the partner's `status` is not `SUSPENDED`, that `allocated_volume` is at least the partner's `commitment_floor_volume`, and that the interval's `partner_committed_volume` plus this `allocated_volume` does not exceed the interval's `allocatable_volume` — the aggregate interval capacity ceiling, breaches of which are refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). On success the system stamps `committed_by_user_id`, adds `allocated_volume` to the partner's `committed_volume_outstanding`, recomputes the interval's `partner_committed_volume` and `unserved_volume`, and increments the partner's own `version` by 1.

**Revocation.** `PROPOSED → REVOKED` requires only that the cycle is not frozen and has no ledger effect, since a proposal never moved one. `COMMITTED → REVOKED` — reachable either from an operator's explicit revocation or from the partner-suspension cascade — likewise requires only that the cycle is not frozen, and it subtracts `allocated_volume` from the partner's `committed_volume_outstanding` and recomputes the interval's `partner_committed_volume` and `unserved_volume`. Revocation restores the outstanding-commitment ledger to exactly its pre-commitment value, making commit and revoke an exactly compensating pair on a conserved quantity. `REVOKED` is terminal.

**Settlement.** `COMMITTED → SETTLED` closes the line. The request carries `delivered_volume`, the contacts the partner actually handled, and requires the cycle not to be frozen. Settlement is only legal from `COMMITTED`; attempting it from any other status is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_COMMITTED` (HTTP 409). For a partner whose `billing_basis` is `COMMITMENT_FLOOR`, `delivered_volume` must be at least that partner's `commitment_floor_volume`, and shortfalls are refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_FLOOR_BREACH` (HTTP 409).

Three amounts are always computed and always stored, regardless of which one is authoritative. `settlement_floor_cents` is `allocated_volume` multiplied by the partner's `rate_per_contact_millicents`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `settlement_delivered_cents` is `delivered_volume` multiplied by the same rate, divided by 1000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `shortfall_penalty_cents` is the under-delivered quantity — `allocated_volume` minus `delivered_volume`, floored at zero — multiplied by the rate, multiplied by one quarter, divided by 1000, rounded half up to a whole cent; it is zero whenever delivery met or exceeded the commitment. The authoritative payable, `settlement_amount_cents`, is then selected by the partner's `billing_basis`: under `COMMITMENT_FLOOR` it is `settlement_floor_cents`, so the committed volume is billed regardless of delivery and under-delivery costs the buyer full price with no penalty credited; under `DELIVERED_ONLY` it is `settlement_delivered_cents` minus `shortfall_penalty_cents`, so only handled contacts are billed and the buyer is further credited a twenty-five-percent shortfall penalty. The two variants genuinely diverge on identical data. Settlement then subtracts `allocated_volume` from the partner's `committed_volume_outstanding` and adds `settlement_amount_cents` to the partner's `invoice_total_cents`.

Rounding discipline on the invoice matters. Each settlement line is rounded to whole cents on its own, and `invoice_total_cents` is the integer sum of those already-rounded lines — never the result of summing millicents across lines and rounding once at the end. `invoice_total_cents` aggregates only allocations whose `status` is `SETTLED`, while `committed_volume_outstanding` aggregates only those whose `status` is `COMMITTED`. `SETTLED` is terminal.
