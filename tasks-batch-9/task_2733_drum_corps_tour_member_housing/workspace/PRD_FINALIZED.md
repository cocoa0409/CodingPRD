# Drum Corps Tour & Member Housing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A competitive drum corps runs one of the most logistically dense operations in youth performing arts. Between January and August a single organization auditions several hundred musicians, contracts and finances roughly one hundred and fifty of them, houses that roster on gymnasium floors at borrowed host schools during spring training, then puts the entire corps — bodies, uniforms, instruments and towed props — on the road for a sixty-day competitive tour that touches a new city almost every night. Each of those activities is today tracked in a different spreadsheet by a different volunteer, and the seams between them are where the organization loses money and, more seriously, loses track of minors.

This document specifies the Drum Corps Tour & Member Housing API: a single system of record spanning member auditions, contract negotiation and tuition financing, host-school facility agreements and sleeping arrangements, tour routing with travel-feasibility and truck-capacity checks, food service costed against dietary needs, volunteer staffing gated on background screening, and reconciliation of appearance fees and prize money against the touring circuit. The design goal is that every number the business office reports can be re-derived from primitive records, and that every child-welfare rule — chaperone coverage, minor sleeping-space segregation, athletic-trainer presence in heat — is enforced by the server rather than by a volunteer's memory.

Two constraints shape the whole specification. First, all monetary values are integer cents and all rounding points are pinned, because a two-cent drift on an installment schedule multiplied across a roster is a reconciliation the business manager cannot close. Second, the system runs on a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time: due dates, screening expiries, agreement windows and departure feasibility are all evaluated against a clock the operator sets explicitly, so that a full season can be replayed deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identifier semantics, the order in which validations fire, monetary precision, stale-view protection, cascade discipline and the segregation-of-duties doctrine.

### 2.1. Corps Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `CorpsDirector`, `MembershipCoordinator`, `TourManager`, `BusinessManager`, `VolunteerScreener`, `Member`, and `Volunteer`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string outside that set of seven, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called — the authorization column of §4 is definitive — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The `CorpsDirector` holds executive authority: opening seasons, countersigning member contracts, signing and cancelling facility agreements, and cancelling tour stops. The `MembershipCoordinator` owns the membership pipeline — auditions, contract issuance and reissue, medical postures, withdrawals, and the sleeping arrangements that house the roster. The `TourManager` owns the route and its physical resources: host schools, facility negotiation, tour stops, rehearsal blocks, and volunteer shift staffing. The `BusinessManager` owns money: installment plans, tuition posting and reversal, the billing sweep, and circuit settlements. The `VolunteerScreener` registers volunteers and records background-screening outcomes, and is deliberately barred from approving the shifts of anyone it screened.

Five of the seven roles carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `CorpsDirector`, `MembershipCoordinator`, `TourManager`, `BusinessManager`, or `VolunteerScreener`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands it — as `screened_by_user_id` on a `Volunteer`, as `approved_by_user_id` on a `StaffAssignment`, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on a `ShowSettlement` — but it never compares such an identifier against any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member record; ownership checks on any resource that references a member compare that resource's member foreign key to `X-User-ID`, so a member may sign or request revision of only a contract whose `member_id` equals their own identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting volunteer record; a volunteer may accept only a shift whose `volunteer_id` equals their own identifier. A violation of either ownership check yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` bounds the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

All thirteen resource types in this system — seasons, members, member contracts, payment plans, tuition payments, host schools, facility agreements, housing assignments, tour stops, site bookings, volunteers, staff assignments and show settlements — share four server-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade of some other request. Whenever `version` advances, `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical-clock instant of the request that caused the change. No resource in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

The **logical clock** is the sole source of "now". Every due-date comparison, screening expiry, agreement window, departure-feasibility check and future-dating rule reads it, and no rule in this specification ever consults real wall-clock time. Before any operator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must not silently fall back to the host machine's time. An operator-facing test-driving capability accepts a single `to_iso8601` field and moves the clock forward or backward to that instant. Setting the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep — it merely changes the instant against which subsequent requests are judged.

Datetime-typed inputs (`arrive_at`, `depart_at`, `show_start_at`, `block_start_at`, `block_end_at`, `shift_start_at`, `shift_end_at`, and the `recorded_at` of a remittance line) are full ISO 8601 UTC instants. Date-typed inputs (`spring_training_start_date`, `spring_training_end_date`, `tour_departure_date`, `date_of_birth`, `first_due_date`, `received_on`, `withdrawal_date`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `check_in_date`, `check_out_date`, `screening_decided_on`, `screening_expires_on`, and the `decided_on` supplied with a screening decision) are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and all arithmetic on them is whole-day arithmetic.

### 2.5. Identifier Resolution Order

The collections in this API are peers at the root of the URL space; cross-entity relationships travel as foreign keys in request bodies rather than as nested URL segments. Consequently every write path carries at most one identifier, and each read or action path carries exactly the identifier of the resource it addresses: `season_id`, `member_id`, `member_contract_id`, `payment_plan_id`, `tuition_payment_id`, `host_school_id`, `facility_agreement_id`, `housing_assignment_id`, `tour_stop_id`, `site_booking_id`, `volunteer_id`, `staff_assignment_id`, and `show_settlement_id`.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. Each collection has its own not-found error, so the response identifies which link in the chain broke: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TUITION_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOST_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOUR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_SHOW_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Each carries HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are checked in the order in which their owning resource declares them. The same family of not-found errors is reused: a body referencing a nonexistent host school reports `ERR_HOST_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, one referencing a nonexistent agreement reports `ERR_FACILITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a `previous_stop_id` that resolves to nothing reports `ERR_TOUR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to member registration, contract issuance, plan creation, payment posting, agreement drafting, housing assignment, stop planning, rehearsal-block booking, shift proposal, and settlement opening.

Every list endpoint accepts the foreign keys of its resource as optional filters, so each relationship is navigable in reverse. Members may be filtered by `season_id`, `membership_status`, `medical_status` and `is_minor`; contracts by `member_id` and `status`; plans by `member_id`, `member_contract_id` and `status`; payments by `payment_plan_id` and `status`; host schools by `state_code`; agreements by `host_school_id`, `season_id`, `status` and `usage_type`; housing assignments by `facility_agreement_id`, `member_id`, `space_index` and `status`; stops by `season_id`, `previous_stop_id`, `stop_type` and `status`; rehearsal blocks by `tour_stop_id`, `host_school_id`, `facility_agreement_id` and `status`; volunteers by `role_type` and `screening_status`; shifts by `volunteer_id`, `tour_stop_id`, `duty` and `status`; and settlements by `tour_stop_id` and `status`. Every list endpoint additionally accepts `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence: The Corps Priority Ladder

Requests frequently violate more than one rule at once. The system therefore evaluates checks in a single, fixed, globally ordered ladder, from rung 1 upward; **the first violated rung decides the response, and no side effect of the request is applied**. Text order elsewhere in this document is narrative convenience only — this ladder is the normative ordering, and where two rules could both fire, the lower-numbered rung always wins.

The ladder runs through five bands: transport and schema checks first, then existence, then ownership and stale-view protection, then the business band. Within the business band the domain ordering is pinned once and for all: **referential integrity, then member safety and compliance, then temporal feasibility, then capacity and uniqueness constraints, then money.** Safety outranks money deliberately — a corps that overfills a minor's sleeping space or rehearses at a 107-degree heat index has a child-welfare problem, not a budgeting problem.

**Rungs 1–6 — transport and schema.**

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every required body field is present, carries its declared JSON type, and every enum-typed field carries a declared member of that enum; unknown fields are ignored; integer cent fields must arrive as JSON integers and never as decimals or numeric strings. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every endpoint that has a request body.
4. Ordered date and instant pairs are strictly increasing: a season's `spring_training_end_date` must be later than its `spring_training_start_date` and its `tour_departure_date` must also be later than its `spring_training_start_date`; a facility agreement's `end_date` must be later than its `start_date`; a stop's `depart_at` must be later than its `arrive_at`; a rehearsal block's `block_end_at` must be later than its `block_start_at`; a shift's `shift_end_at` must be later than its `shift_start_at`; and a stay's `check_out_date` must be later than its `check_in_date`. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. This rung guards season creation, agreement drafting, stop planning, rehearsal-block booking, shift proposal and housing assignment.
5. Conditionally required fields obey their condition. `cdl_number` is required when and only when `role_type` is `DRIVER`; `credential_id` is required when and only when `role_type` is `ATHLETIC_TRAINER`; a stop's `show_start_at` is required when and only when `stop_type` is `SHOW`; `travel_miles_from_previous` must be exactly 0 when `previous_stop_id` is absent and greater than 0 when it is present; and `countered_nightly_rate_cents` is required on a counter-offer. A violation is `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_MISMATCH`, and this rung guards volunteer registration, stop planning and the agreement counter-offer.
6. Monetary, count and placement inputs meet their positivity floors: a payment's `amount_cents` must be greater than 0, a contract's `tuition_amount_cents` must be greater than 0, an agreement's `nightly_rate_cents` must be greater than 0, a settlement's `appearance_fee_cents` and `prize_pool_cents` must each be at least 0, `projected_placement` and `final_placement` must each be at least 1, and a host school's `sleeping_space_count` and `capacity_per_space` must each be at least 1. A violation is `ERR_AMOUNT_NOT_POSITIVE`, and this rung guards payment posting, contract issuance, agreement drafting, settlement opening, settlement finalization and host-school registration.

**Rungs 7–8 — existence.** Rung 7 resolves every identifier in the request path, left to right, reporting the per-collection not-found error listed in §2.5; it applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier. Rung 8 then resolves every foreign key in the request body, in declaration order, using the same error family.

**Rungs 9–11 — ownership and stale-view protection.** Rung 9 enforces entity-backed ownership: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` when a member signs or requests revision of a contract that is not theirs, or a volunteer accepts a shift that is not theirs. Rung 10 enforces `expected_member_version` on plan creation, rejecting a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Rung 11 enforces `expected_facility_agreement_version` on housing assignment, rejecting a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

**Rungs 12–25 — state gates.** Each of these reports HTTP 409.

12. The member's `membership_status` permits the operation: recording an audition decision requires `APPLICANT`; withdrawing requires `OFFERED`, `CONTRACTED` or `ON_TOUR`; being given a sleeping space requires `CONTRACTED` or `ON_TOUR`. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_STATE`, and the rung guards the audition decision, the withdrawal and housing assignment.
13. The contract's `status` permits the operation: signing requires `ISSUED`, requesting a revision requires `ISSUED`, reissuing requires `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and executing requires `SIGNED_BY_MEMBER`. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE` and the rung guards all four of those contract actions.
14. Plan creation requires the referenced contract's `status` to be `EXECUTED` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_EXECUTED`.
15. Payment posting requires the target plan's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`; a plan that is `SETTLED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE` accepts no further money and reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACCEPTING_PAYMENTS`. This rung deliberately outranks the fiscal over-payment check at rung 57, so a payment that is both oversized and aimed at a closed plan reports the closed plan.
16. Reversing a payment requires that payment's `status` to be `POSTED` and its plan's `status` to be anything other than `SUPERSEDED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`.
17. The agreement's `status` permits the operation: submitting requires `DRAFT` or `COUNTERED`, countering requires `SUBMITTED`, signing requires `SUBMITTED`, and cancelling requires any status other than `CANCELLED`. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE` across all four agreement actions.
18. Creating either a housing assignment or a rehearsal block requires the referenced agreement's `status` to be `SIGNED`, and its `usage_type` must cover the requested use — a housing assignment requires `SPRING_TRAINING` and a rehearsal block requires `TOUR_REHEARSAL`. Either failure reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED`.
19. Releasing a housing assignment requires its `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_HOUSING_STATE`.
20. The stop's `status` permits the operation: confirming requires `PLANNED`, completing requires `CONFIRMED`, cancelling requires `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, and creating a rehearsal block or a shift against a stop requires that stop to be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`, and this rung guards confirm, complete, cancel, rehearsal-block booking and shift proposal — five operations in all.
21. Releasing a rehearsal block requires its `status` to be `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`.
22. Recording a screening decision requires the volunteer's `screening_status` to be `PENDING`; a second decision on an already-decided volunteer reports `ERR_INVALID_SCREENING_STATE`.
23. The shift's `status` permits the operation: accepting requires `PROPOSED` and approving requires `ACCEPTED`. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_STAFF_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.
24. Finalizing a settlement requires its `status` to be `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`.
25. Both opening and finalizing a settlement require the parent stop's `status` to be `COMPLETED` and its `stop_type` to be `SHOW`; either failure reports `ERR_STOP_NOT_COMPLETED`.

**Rungs 26–29 — business referential integrity.** Rung 26 enforces cross-aggregate season coherence and reports `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH` on both housing assignment and rehearsal-block booking: a housed member's `season_id` must equal the agreement's `season_id`, and a booked stop's `season_id` must equal the agreement's `season_id`. Shifts and settlements inherit their season from their stop and need no separate check. Rung 27 requires that a plan's `member_id` equal the referenced contract's `member_id`, reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_MEMBER_MISMATCH`. Rung 28 requires that a rehearsal block's `host_school_id` equal the referenced agreement's `host_school_id`, reporting `ERR_AGREEMENT_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`. Rung 29 requires a shift's `duty` to correspond exactly to the volunteer's `role_type` — `DRIVE` with `DRIVER`, `COOK` with `COOK`, `CHAPERONE` with `CHAPERONE`, and `TRAINER` with `ATHLETIC_TRAINER` — reporting `ERR_DUTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`.

**Rungs 30–37 — member safety and compliance.** Rung 30 refuses to execute a contract unless the member's `medical_status` is `CLEARED`, reporting `ERR_MEDICAL_NOT_CLEARED`. Rung 31 refuses a sleeping arrangement that would mix minors and adults on overlapping nights, reporting `ERR_MINOR_ROOM_MIX`; it deliberately outranks the capacity check at rung 53, so an assignment that is both a minor/adult mix and over capacity reports the mix. Rungs 32, 33 and 34 guard stop confirmation with chaperone, cook and athletic-trainer coverage respectively, reporting `ERR_CHAPERONE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_COOK_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` and `ERR_TRAINER_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`. Rung 35 enforces the extreme-heat protocol on rehearsal-block booking with `ERR_EXTREME_HEAT_OUTDOOR_PROHIBITED`. Rung 36 requires a `CLEARED` screening before a shift may be approved, reporting `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED`. Rung 37 enforces segregation of duties on shift approval and settlement finalization, reporting `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

**Rungs 38–46 — temporal feasibility.** Rung 38 rejects confirming a stop the convoy cannot reach in time with `ERR_TRAVEL_TIME_INFEASIBLE`. Rung 39 rejects a rehearsal block that does not clear its neighbours at the same school with `ERR_SITE_BLOCK_CONFLICT`. Rung 40 rejects a rehearsal block falling outside its agreement's window with `ERR_BLOCK_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_WINDOW`, and rung 41 rejects a stay falling outside that window with `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_WINDOW`. Rung 42 rejects a shift falling outside its stop window with `ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_STOP_WINDOW`, and rung 43 rejects a volunteer holding two overlapping open shifts with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Rung 44 rejects any business date later than the logical-clock date — a payment's `received_on`, a screening's `decided_on`, or a member's `withdrawal_date` — with `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_INPUT`, and it guards payment posting, the screening decision and the withdrawal. Rung 45 rejects a `withdrawal_date` earlier than the season's `spring_training_start_date` with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Rung 46 rejects completing a stop before the logical clock reaches its `depart_at` with `ERR_STOP_NOT_DEPARTED`. All nine report HTTP 409.

**Rungs 47–55 — capacity and uniqueness constraints.** Rung 47 enforces email uniqueness with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` on both member registration and volunteer registration. Rung 48 allows a member at most one contract whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Rung 49 allows a stop at most one settlement, reporting `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Rung 50 caps reissues with `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` and rung 51 caps counter-offers with `ERR_MAX_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`. Rung 52 bounds `space_index` with `ERR_SPACE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE` and rung 53 enforces effective sleeping-space capacity with `ERR_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Rung 54 forbids a member from holding two overlapping active stays with `ERR_MEMBER_ALREADY_HOUSED`. Rung 55 refuses to confirm a stop whose `truck_count` falls below the computed `trucks_required`, reporting `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRUCKS`. All nine report HTTP 409.

**Rungs 56–57 — money, last.** Rung 56 caps the per-bed-night spend with `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_CAP` on both agreement drafting and the counter-offer. Rung 57 refuses a payment larger than the plan's total outstanding obligation with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Both report HTTP 409.

### 2.7. Cents, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All money in this system is expressed and stored as an integer number of cents. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request body, a response body or a stored record. Wherever a percentage or a proportion enters a computation, the intermediate product is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** — a result whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero, upward.

Rounding is not merely a precision rule but an ordering rule: every calculation in §3 states exactly how many rounding points it has and where they sit, and applying the same arithmetic with the rounding moved produces a different, wrong answer. Three specific disciplines recur. First, when a percentage applies to an aggregate, the percentage is applied to the aggregate and rounded once, never applied per-line and summed. Second, when a per-unit price is scaled by a count, the unit price is rounded first and the rounded unit is then multiplied, never the reverse. Third, when a tiered rate is selected by the size of a base amount, the base amount is fully assembled before the tier is read off it.

Non-monetary quantities have their own pinned directions. Truck counts round **up**; travel drive time rounds **up**; fuel-stop counts round **down**; installment base amounts round **down** with the shortfall redistributed as whole cents; and completed tour weeks round **down**. Bed-nights, sleeping-space counts and head counts are exact integers and never rounded at all.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Roster and Housing Writes

Two write operations in this system can be issued twice in good faith and, on their own terms, succeed both times — which means no state machine can protect the second writer from clobbering work it never observed. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the `version` of a **parent** resource.

Creating an installment plan requires a non-optional `expected_member_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the member the plan finances. Creating a second plan for the same member is legitimate under the product semantics — the new plan supersedes the prior one — so the state machine alone cannot reject a writer working from a stale view. The version check does: because a successful creation advances the member's `version`, a later request still carrying the earlier value is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` and HTTP 412 rather than silently superseding a plan its author never saw.

Creating a housing assignment requires a non-optional `expected_facility_agreement_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the facility agreement whose beds are being consumed. Adding another occupant to a `SIGNED` agreement is always legitimate on its own terms — the agreement stays `SIGNED` and only its derived occupancy figures move — so no state precondition can reject a writer whose bed-availability view is out of date. Because every assignment and every release advances the agreement's `version`, a request carrying a pre-occupancy value is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 rather than overfilling a sleeping space the writer believed was empty.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Plan creation and housing assignment are the only two writes that take one, and for both of them the field is required rather than optional.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Ledger Recomputation

A great many fields in this system are **derived**: they are never supplied by a client and are recomputed by the server whenever one of their inputs moves. The general rule is that a write which changes a primitive record also, in the same request, walks every derivation that depends on it and rewrites the dependent records — bumping each dependent record's own `version` and refreshing its own `updated_at`. Consistency is immediate; there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation anywhere in this specification.

Three cascade chains recur and are worth naming up front, because implementers routinely stop at the first link. **The housing chain**: whenever a stay starts or stops consuming beds, the owning agreement recomputes `occupied_bed_nights`, then `billable_bed_nights`, then `billable_cents`; the host school recomputes `reserved_bed_nights`; and the season recomputes `housing_cost_cents`. **The tuition chain**: whenever money moves on a plan, the plan recomputes `principal_paid_cents`, `late_fee_paid_cents`, `installments_due_count`, `due_amount_cents`, `arrears_cents` and `outstanding_cents` and re-evaluates its own `status`; the member recomputes `tuition_paid_cents`, `tuition_balance_cents` and `refund_due_cents`; and the season recomputes `tuition_collected_cents` and `late_fee_collected_cents`. **The withdrawal chain**: a member leaving the corps re-strikes their own obligation, closes or settles their plans, releases their sleeping arrangements, and through those releases re-runs the whole housing chain.

Season-level rollups are always definitions rather than running counters, and each is scoped precisely. `tuition_charged_cents` sums `tuition_charged_cents` over the season's members whose `membership_status` is `CONTRACTED`, `ON_TOUR` or `WITHDRAWN`. `tuition_collected_cents` sums `allocated_to_principal_cents` over payments whose `status` is `POSTED` and whose plan belongs to a member of that season, and `late_fee_collected_cents` sums `allocated_to_late_fee_cents` over the same population. `housing_cost_cents` sums `billable_cents` over the season's agreements whose `status` is `SIGNED`. `tour_revenue_cents` sums `final_net_cents` over settlements whose `status` is `FINALIZED` and whose stop belongs to that season.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this system are structurally split so that no single human can both prepare and bless them, and in both cases the split is enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on `X-User-Role`. A shift may not be approved by the same user identifier that recorded the volunteer's background screening, and a settlement may not be finalized by the same user identifier that prepared its draft. Because a single `BusinessManager` may prepare a settlement or finalize it but never both, closing out a show genuinely requires two distinct business-manager user identifiers. Both violations report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403, and both are evaluated at rung 37 of the ladder — after the state gates and after the screening-clearance check, so a shift for an un-screened volunteer reports the screening problem rather than the duties problem.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Season and Building the Roster

A competitive year begins when the `CorpsDirector` opens a season, supplying its `name` (a label such as "2027 Tour"), its `year` (which must be at least 2020 and at most 2100), the published per-member `tuition_base_cents` (which must be at least 0 and serves as the reference figure quoted on contracts, never as a charge in its own right), and the three dates that anchor everything downstream: `spring_training_start_date`, the date on which host-school housing begins; `spring_training_end_date`, the date spring training closes; and `tour_departure_date`, the date the corps leaves for tour. A freshly opened season starts with `billing_epoch` at 0, every ledger rollup — `tuition_charged_cents`, `tuition_collected_cents`, `late_fee_collected_cents`, `housing_cost_cents` and `tour_revenue_cents` — at 0, and `ledger_status` at `OPEN`. The only other value `ledger_status` ever takes is `RECONCILED`, and only the billing sweep may write it.

The departure date does more work than any other field in the system. It selects which of the two withdrawal-obligation variants applies to a departing member, it is the threshold the billing sweep compares against when promoting contracted members onto the touring roster, and it is the reference date against which minority is judged.

A `MembershipCoordinator` registers each auditionee with a payload carrying the `season_id` they auditioned for, their `full_name`, their `email`, their `date_of_birth`, their `section` — one of `BRASS`, `PERCUSSION` or `COLOR_GUARD` — and their `dietary_flag`, which is one of `NONE`, `VEGETARIAN`, `GLUTEN_FREE` or `SEVERE_ALLERGEN` and defaults to `NONE` when omitted. An email address may repeat across seasons but must be unique among the members of any one season; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`. Registration writes `membership_status` as `APPLICANT`, `medical_status` as `CLEARED`, every money field on the member — `tuition_charged_cents`, `tuition_paid_cents`, `tuition_balance_cents` and `refund_due_cents` — as 0, and `withdrawal_date`, `withdrawal_obligation_cents` and `audition_score` as null, with `withdrawal_obligation_basis` at `NONE`.

Minority is computed at registration and is not a live property. `is_minor` is true exactly when the member has **not** reached their eighteenth birthday on or before the season's `tour_departure_date` — equivalently, when `date_of_birth` falls later than the departure date shifted back by eighteen years. A member turning eighteen on the departure date itself is therefore an adult for every purpose in this system, including chaperone ratios and sleeping-space segregation.

Recording an audition outcome carries a `decision` of either `OFFER` or `DECLINE` together with an `audition_score`, a number that must be at least 0 and at most 100. An `OFFER` moves `membership_status` from `APPLICANT` to `OFFERED`; a `DECLINE` moves it from `APPLICANT` to `DECLINED`, which is terminal. Either way the supplied score is stored on the member and the member's `version` advances by one. Only an `APPLICANT` may receive an audition decision; a member already past that point reports `ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_STATE`.

Health posture travels on a second, entirely independent dimension. `medical_status` starts at `CLEARED` and moves among `CLEARED`, `RESTRICTED` and `SIDELINED` in any direction and any number of times: `CLEARED → RESTRICTED`, `RESTRICTED → CLEARED`, `CLEARED → SIDELINED`, `RESTRICTED → SIDELINED`, `SIDELINED → RESTRICTED`, and `SIDELINED → CLEARED` are all legal, and the dimension has no terminal state. A `MembershipCoordinator` drives it by supplying the desired `to_medical_status` and, optionally, a `note`; each such call advances the member's `version` by one and leaves `membership_status` completely untouched. The converse also holds — a `SIDELINED` member keeps their membership standing, their contract and their sleeping arrangement. Only one rule couples the dimensions, and it points in a single direction: contract execution reads `medical_status` and refuses to proceed unless it is `CLEARED`.

### 3.2. Contracting a Member: Issue, Revision, Signature and Execution

Once a member is `OFFERED`, the `MembershipCoordinator` issues a contract naming the `member_id`, the contracted `tuition_amount_cents` and the `payment_plan_type` the member has chosen, which is either `STANDARD_3` or `EXTENDED_6`. A newly issued contract has `status` `ISSUED`, `revision_round` at 0, and `revision_note`, `signature_name`, `signed_at` and `executed_at` all null. A member may hold at most one contract whose `status` is not `VOIDED`; a second issuance while a live contract exists is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXISTS`.

The contract lifecycle is deliberately cyclic, because tuition terms are negotiated. From `ISSUED` the member may push the contract back into negotiation, moving it `ISSUED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and storing the supplied `revision_note`; only the member the contract belongs to may do this, and `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's `member_id`. The coordinator closes the round by reissuing, which moves the contract `REVISION_REQUESTED → ISSUED`, increments `revision_round` by exactly one, and replaces the terms: a supplied `tuition_amount_cents` or `payment_plan_type` overwrites the prior value, and any term omitted from the reissue payload is retained unchanged.

At most three revision rounds may be completed. The cap is enforced on the reissue rather than on the request, so a reissue is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 3; a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` and the contract is left sitting in `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Note the asymmetry, which is intentional: a revision may always be requested out of `ISSUED` regardless of how many rounds have already closed, so a member who has exhausted the cap can still send a contract into `REVISION_REQUESTED` and strand it there permanently.

Signing moves the contract `ISSUED → SIGNED_BY_MEMBER`, stores the typed `signature_name`, and stamps `signed_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Like the revision request, signing is reserved to the owning member and compares `X-User-ID` against the contract's `member_id`, reporting `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` otherwise.

Execution is the `CorpsDirector`'s countersignature and the single most consequential transition in the membership pipeline. It moves the contract `SIGNED_BY_MEMBER → EXECUTED`, stamps `executed_at` with the logical-clock instant, and takes no request body at all. Three preconditions guard it: the contract must be `SIGNED_BY_MEMBER`, the member's `medical_status` must be `CLEARED` — a `RESTRICTED` or `SIDELINED` member may hold a signed contract but may not be executed onto the roster, and the attempt reports `ERR_MEDICAL_NOT_CLEARED` — and the member's `membership_status` must be `OFFERED`. On success the member moves `OFFERED → CONTRACTED`, the member's `tuition_charged_cents` is set to the contract's `tuition_amount_cents`, the member's `tuition_balance_cents` is recomputed as the charged amount less the paid amount but never below 0, the season's `tuition_charged_cents` rollup is recomputed, and both the contract's and the member's `version` advance by one.

Withdrawal from `OFFERED` voids any contract that never reached execution. A contract sitting in `ISSUED`, in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or in `SIGNED_BY_MEMBER` moves to `VOIDED` — a terminal status, alongside `EXECUTED` — with its `version` advanced by one. The three void transitions are identical in effect; which source status the contract happened to occupy has no bearing on the outcome.

### 3.3. Financing Tuition: Installment Plans and Posted Payments

An executed contract does not by itself collect money; the `BusinessManager` must build the installment plan that finances it. Plan creation carries the `member_id`, the `member_contract_id` of the executed contract, the `first_due_date` on which installment one falls due, and the `expected_member_version` described in §2.8. The referenced contract must have `status` `EXECUTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_EXECUTED`, and the supplied `member_id` must be the very member the contract names, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_MEMBER_MISMATCH`.

Creation copies two values off the contract and never lets a client override them: `plan_type` is copied from the contract's `payment_plan_type`, and `principal_cents` is copied from the contract's `tuition_amount_cents`. From `plan_type` follows `installment_count`, which is 3 when the type is `STANDARD_3` and 6 when it is `EXTENDED_6`.

The **installment schedule** is then struck in three ordered steps, and the order is what makes the arithmetic reproducible. Step one applies the financing surcharge to the whole principal exactly once: the surcharge rate is zero for a `STANDARD_3` plan and two and one-half percent for an `EXTENDED_6` plan, and `surcharge_cents` is the principal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. A `STANDARD_3` plan therefore always carries a `surcharge_cents` of 0. `financed_total_cents` is the principal plus that surcharge. Step two splits the financed total — never the bare principal — across the installments: `installment_base_cents` is the financed total divided by the installment count and rounded down to a whole cent, and `installment_remainder_cents` is whatever the financed total exceeds the base amount multiplied by the installment count. Step three distributes that remainder one cent at a time onto the earliest installments in due-date order, so installment number one through installment number `installment_remainder_cents` each carry the base amount plus one cent and every later installment carries the base amount exactly. Because of this, the installment amounts always sum to the financed total to the cent, with no residue anywhere.

Due dates follow mechanically: installment number one falls due on `first_due_date`, and installment number *k* falls due thirty calendar days after installment number *k* minus one — that is, `first_due_date` advanced by thirty days multiplied by one less than the installment number. There is no calendar-month arithmetic anywhere; every gap is exactly thirty days.

A new plan lands with `status` `ACTIVE`, `delinquency_strikes` at 0, `principal_paid_cents`, `late_fee_accrued_cents`, `late_fee_paid_cents`, `installments_due_count`, `due_amount_cents` and `arrears_cents` all at 0, and `outstanding_cents` equal to the financed total. Creating a plan for a member who already has one is legitimate and supersedes the old arrangement: any prior plan of that member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal. Both source statuses behave identically in one respect and identically in another: in both cases the superseded plan's `version` advances by one and its accumulated `late_fee_accrued_cents` and `late_fee_paid_cents` remain on it rather than migrating, and in both cases the replacement plan starts life with `late_fee_accrued_cents` at 0. Plan creation also advances the financed member's `version` by one, which is precisely why a second creation working from a stale view is caught by `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION`.

Four derived figures on a plan are re-derived against the logical clock on every read as well as on every write, so a plan fetched after the clock has advanced reports live arrears without any sweep having run. `installments_due_count` is the number of installments whose due date falls on or before the logical-clock date, capped at `installment_count`. `due_amount_cents` is the sum of the installment amounts for installments one through that count. `arrears_cents` is the amount due less the principal already paid, floored at 0. And `outstanding_cents` is the financed total less the principal paid, plus the late fees accrued less the late fees paid.

Posting money is the `BusinessManager`'s daily work. A payment names its `payment_plan_id`, the gross `amount_cents` tendered, the `received_on` business date on which the funds arrived, and the `method`, which is `CHECK`, `ACH` or `CARD`. The receipt date may not be later than the logical-clock date, or the posting is refused with `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_INPUT`. The target plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`; a `SETTLED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE` plan accepts nothing further and reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACCEPTING_PAYMENTS`. And the tendered amount may not exceed the plan's total outstanding obligation — the accrued late fees less the late fees already paid, plus the financed total less the principal already paid — or the posting is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, because the ledger never holds unallocated money.

Accepted money runs through a strict two-bucket **waterfall**. The first bucket is unpaid late fees: `allocated_to_late_fee_cents` is the smaller of the tendered amount and the outstanding late-fee balance, where that balance is `late_fee_accrued_cents` less `late_fee_paid_cents`. The second bucket is tuition principal, and it receives only what the first bucket did not absorb: `allocated_to_principal_cents` is the smaller of the tendered amount less the late-fee allocation, and the outstanding principal, where that is `financed_total_cents` less `principal_paid_cents`. There is no third bucket, and because the over-payment rule above already forbids a tender larger than the two buckets combined, the two allocations always sum exactly to the tendered amount. A newly posted payment carries `status` `POSTED`, with `reversal_reason` and `reversed_at` null.

The two allocations then drive the cascade of §2.9: the plan's `principal_paid_cents` and `late_fee_paid_cents` absorb them, the plan's derived arrears figures are refreshed, the member's `tuition_paid_cents` is re-summed over principal allocations of `POSTED` payments only — late-fee allocations are excluded from that figure entirely — the member's `tuition_balance_cents` and `refund_due_cents` are recomputed as the charged amount less the paid amount and the paid amount less the charged amount respectively, each floored at 0, and the season's `tuition_collected_cents` and `late_fee_collected_cents` are recomputed.

Only then is the plan's own status re-evaluated, and the branches are checked in this order. If `arrears_cents` is 0, `principal_paid_cents` equals `financed_total_cents` and `late_fee_paid_cents` equals `late_fee_accrued_cents` — equivalently, if `outstanding_cents` has reached 0 — the plan moves to `SETTLED`, and it does so from `ACTIVE` and from `DELINQUENT` alike. Otherwise, if `arrears_cents` has reached 0 while something remains outstanding and the plan was `DELINQUENT`, the plan is cured and moves `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`; curing never touches `delinquency_strikes`, which only ever increases. Otherwise the plan's status is left exactly as it was.

Bad tender must be unwound. Reversing a payment stores the supplied `reversal_reason`, stamps `reversed_at` with the logical-clock instant, and moves the payment `POSTED → REVERSED`, which is terminal. A reversal is permitted only while the payment is `POSTED` and only while its plan's `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`; otherwise it reports `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`. The unwind is exact: the plan's `principal_paid_cents` is reduced by that payment's `allocated_to_principal_cents` and its `late_fee_paid_cents` by that payment's `allocated_to_late_fee_cents`, the member's `tuition_paid_cents`, `tuition_balance_cents` and `refund_due_cents` are recomputed, and the season's `tuition_collected_cents` and `late_fee_collected_cents` return to their pre-payment values. A `REVERSED` payment contributes nothing to either waterfall bucket or to any rollup thereafter. Finally the plan is re-evaluated: if the unwind re-opens arrears on a plan that was `ACTIVE`, the plan moves `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` as a compensating action, while a plan already `SETTLED` is never re-opened.

### 3.4. The Billing Sweep: Late Fees, Delinquency, Promotion and Expiry

Nothing in this system ages on its own. Time-driven consequences are applied only when the `BusinessManager` runs a **billing sweep** against a season — a capability that takes no request body, returns the season, and performs, in one pass, five distinct jobs. Every sweep increments the season's `billing_epoch` by exactly one, so the epoch is a reliable count of how many sweeps a season has seen.

**Job one: late-fee assessment.** The sweep visits every payment plan belonging to a member of the season whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`. Plans that are `SETTLED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE` are skipped entirely and accrue nothing further, ever. For each visited plan the sweep first refreshes `installments_due_count` against the logical-clock date, recomputes `due_amount_cents` as the sum of the installment amounts up to that count, and recomputes `arrears_cents` as the amount due less `principal_paid_cents`, floored at 0.

If a plan's arrears are 0, no fee is charged and no strike is recorded; a plan sitting in `DELINQUENT` whose arrears have cleared returns to `ACTIVE`. If arrears are greater than 0, the fee is computed as one and one-half percent of the arrears, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then clamped — first up to a floor of twenty-five dollars, then down to a ceiling of one hundred and fifty dollars, in that order. Because the floor is twenty-five dollars, any plan with modest arrears is charged the flat floor rather than the percentage; the ceiling only begins to bite once arrears pass ten thousand dollars. The charged fee is added to `late_fee_accrued_cents`, `delinquency_strikes` is increased by one, `outstanding_cents` is recomputed, and the plan's `version` advances by one.

The strike count drives a three-step escalation. A plan in `ACTIVE` observed with arrears moves `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`. A plan already in `DELINQUENT` observed with arrears while its `delinquency_strikes` stands below 2 stays in `DELINQUENT` — a self-transition that still charges a fee and still increments the strike count. A plan in `DELINQUENT` observed with arrears while its `delinquency_strikes` already stands at exactly 2 takes its third strike and moves `DELINQUENT → CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE`, a terminal status. That third strike is the first link of a four-link cascade, and every link must fire in the same sweep.

Link one closes the plan: the strike count reaches 3, the late fee for that observation is still charged, and `status` becomes `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE`. Link two, caused by link one, ejects the member: a member sitting in `CONTRACTED` moves `CONTRACTED → WITHDRAWN`, the member's `withdrawal_date` is set to the logical-clock date, the withdrawal-obligation calculation of §3.5 runs, `tuition_charged_cents` is replaced by the resulting `withdrawal_obligation_cents`, and `tuition_balance_cents` and `refund_due_cents` are recomputed. Link three, caused by link two, releases beds: every housing assignment of that member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `MEMBER_WITHDRAWN`. Link four, caused by link three, re-runs the housing chain: each affected agreement recomputes `occupied_bed_nights`, `billable_bed_nights` and `billable_cents`, each affected host school recomputes `reserved_bed_nights`, and the season recomputes `housing_cost_cents`. One thing that pointedly does **not** happen: the plan's own status is not re-derived after link two, because `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE` is terminal and a member's withdrawal cannot resurrect it into `SETTLED`.

**Job two: roster promotion.** Once the logical-clock date has reached or passed the season's `tour_departure_date`, the sweep promotes each member whose `membership_status` is `CONTRACTED` onto the touring roster, moving them `CONTRACTED → ON_TOUR` and advancing their `version` by one. Two conditions gate the promotion: the member must hold no payment plan whose `status` is `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE`, and the member's `medical_status` must be `CLEARED`. Promotion matters well beyond bookkeeping, because head counts, chaperone ratios, cook ratios and food-service cost all count `ON_TOUR` members and nobody else.

**Job three: screening expiry.** A volunteer whose `screening_status` is `CLEARED` and whose `screening_expires_on` falls strictly before the logical-clock date moves `CLEARED → EXPIRED`, a terminal posture. Expiry is not silent: every shift of that volunteer whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED` or `APPROVED` **and** whose `shift_end_at` falls after the current logical-clock instant is moved to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `SCREENING_EXPIRED`. Shifts that have already finished are left alone, since releasing them would rewrite history. The volunteer's `assignment_count` is then recomputed over its remaining `APPROVED` shifts, and the volunteer's `version` advances by one.

**Job four: ledger rollups.** Every season total defined in §2.9 is recomputed from primitives.

**Job five: ledger closure.** `ledger_status` remains `OPEN` until a sweep observes that every tour stop of the season sits in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` **and** that every settlement belonging to those stops sits in `FINALIZED`; at that point, and only inside a sweep, it becomes `RECONCILED`. Completing a stop never sets `RECONCILED` by itself, and finalizing the last settlement of the season never does either — the season closes on the next sweep after the last piece falls into place, not before.

### 3.5. Withdrawal and the Obligation Ladder

Members leave. Some decline before spring training ends, some quit three weeks into tour, and a few are ejected by the billing sweep. Every one of those exits runs through the same withdrawal machinery, driven by the `MembershipCoordinator` with a payload carrying the `withdrawal_date` on which the departure takes effect and, optionally, a free-text `reason`.

Three temporal and state conditions gate a withdrawal. The member's `membership_status` must be `OFFERED`, `CONTRACTED` or `ON_TOUR`; an `APPLICANT`, a `DECLINED` member or an already-`WITHDRAWN` member reports `ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_STATE`. The `withdrawal_date` may not fall after the logical-clock date, or the request reports `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_INPUT`. And the `withdrawal_date` must fall on or after the season's `spring_training_start_date`, because a departure cannot be back-dated to before the season's housing even opened; an earlier date reports `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

A member withdrawing from `OFFERED` has never been charged anything, so no obligation arises: `withdrawal_obligation_cents` is written as 0 and `withdrawal_obligation_basis` stays `NONE`. The only other consequence is contractual — their contract, if it sits in `ISSUED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SIGNED_BY_MEMBER`, moves to `VOIDED`. The member's `version` advances by one.

A member withdrawing from `CONTRACTED` or from `ON_TOUR` triggers the full **withdrawal obligation** calculation, and the two source statuses behave identically — the same obligation arithmetic, the same plan closure, the same bed release, and the same downstream housing recomputation apply to both. The calculation reads only the departing member, their own `tuition_charged_cents` and their season's two anchor dates; no other member is touched.

Let the **spring window** be the number of days from the season's `spring_training_start_date` up to its `tour_departure_date`, and let the **contracted tuition** be the member's current `tuition_charged_cents`, which is 0 when no contract was ever executed. Three branches follow, tested in this order.

If the contracted tuition is 0, nothing was ever charged, so the obligation is 0 and `withdrawal_obligation_basis` is written as `NONE`.

Otherwise, if the `withdrawal_date` falls strictly before the `tour_departure_date`, the basis is `PRORATED_SPRING` and the member pays only for the fraction of spring training they actually completed. Days completed is the number of days from `spring_training_start_date` to the `withdrawal_date`, clamped below at 0 and above at the length of the spring window. The tuition component is the contracted tuition multiplied by days completed and divided by the length of the spring window, rounded half up to a whole cent. A flat administrative fee of one hundred and fifty dollars is then added to that rounded figure — after the rounding, never folded in before it — and the sum is the obligation. Under this branch a member who quits on the day before departure owes very nearly the whole tuition, because the completed fraction is one day short of the full window.

Otherwise the `withdrawal_date` falls on or after the `tour_departure_date`, the basis is `LIQUIDATED_LADDER`, and proration is abandoned for a step ladder keyed on completed tour weeks. Completed weeks are the number of days from the `tour_departure_date` to the `withdrawal_date` divided by seven and rounded down to a whole number. That week number selects a percentage from a five-rung ladder: zero completed weeks charge seventy-five percent, one completed week charges eighty-five percent, two charge ninety-two percent, three charge ninety-seven percent, and four or more charge the full hundred percent — any week number above four is treated as four. The tuition component is the contracted tuition multiplied by the selected percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the same one-hundred-and-fifty-dollar administrative fee is added afterwards. The discontinuity at the departure date is deliberate and steep: the identical contract owes very nearly all of its tuition on the last day before departure but only seventy-five percent plus the admin fee on the first day of tour.

Whichever branch fires, the outcome is written to `withdrawal_obligation_cents` and `withdrawal_obligation_basis`, and the obligation then **replaces** the contracted figure — the member's `tuition_charged_cents` is overwritten with the obligation. `tuition_balance_cents` becomes the obligation less `tuition_paid_cents`, floored at 0, and `refund_due_cents` becomes `tuition_paid_cents` less the obligation, floored at 0. Because the season's charged-tuition rollup counts members in `CONTRACTED`, `ON_TOUR` and `WITHDRAWN` alike, the season's `tuition_charged_cents` moves as soon as the obligation is struck.

Two cascades follow the obligation. First, every payment plan of that member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` is closed out against the recharged balance: if `outstanding_cents` has fallen to 0 or below after the recharge, the plan moves to `SETTLED` — and any overpayment simply sits on the member as `refund_due_cents` — while if anything remains outstanding, the plan moves to `CLOSED_WITH_BALANCE`. Both source statuses close symmetrically, so an `ACTIVE` plan and a `DELINQUENT` plan with identical balances reach identical destinations, each advancing its own `version` by one. Second, every housing assignment of that member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `MEMBER_WITHDRAWN`, and each release re-runs the housing chain: the owning agreement recomputes `occupied_bed_nights`, `billable_bed_nights` and `billable_cents`, the host school recomputes `reserved_bed_nights`, and the season recomputes `housing_cost_cents`. The member's own `version` advances by one for the withdrawal itself.

### 3.6. Host Schools, Facility Agreements and Sleeping Arrangements

Spring training and mid-tour rehearsals both happen on borrowed property, so the `TourManager` first catalogues each host school: its `name`, its `city`, its `state_code` — exactly two uppercase letters — the `sleeping_space_count` of distinct sleeping spaces such as gymnasia and classrooms it offers, the `capacity_per_space` nominal bunk capacity of each of those spaces before any chaperone reservation, the `rehearsal_field_count` of fields it can give the corps, and its `has_air_conditioning` posture. The space count and the per-space capacity must each be at least 1, while the field count may be 0 — such a school can house the corps overnight but cannot host a rehearsal block, since a block needs at least one rehearsal field. A new school begins with `booked_block_count` and `reserved_bed_nights` both at 0; both are derived counters the server maintains, the first counting the school's rehearsal blocks whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` and the second summing the nights of active stays across all of the school's agreements.

Occupancy is authorized by a **facility agreement**, drafted by the `TourManager` with the `host_school_id` being contracted, the `season_id` it belongs to, a `usage_type` of either `SPRING_TRAINING` or `TOUR_REHEARSAL`, the inclusive `start_date` and the **exclusive** `end_date` of the window, the corps-proposed per-bed-night `nightly_rate_cents`, and the `minimum_billable_beds` floor the school insists on billing against, expressed in beds per night and permitted to be 0. Because the end date is exclusive, the agreement's night count is simply the number of days from `start_date` to `end_date`. The usage type is a hard partition of what the agreement can carry: a `SPRING_TRAINING` agreement carries sleeping arrangements and a `TOUR_REHEARSAL` agreement carries rehearsal blocks, and mixing them reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED`. A draft lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `counter_round` at 0, `countered_nightly_rate_cents` and `effective_nightly_rate_cents` null, and `occupied_bed_nights`, `billable_bed_nights` and `billable_cents` all at 0.

The corps board caps per-bed-night spend at fifteen dollars, and the cap is checked on both the initial proposal and every counter: a `nightly_rate_cents` or a `countered_nightly_rate_cents` above 1500 cents is refused with `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_CAP`.

Negotiation is a genuine loop. Submitting sends the agreement to the school for signature and is legal out of `DRAFT` and out of `COUNTERED` alike — `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and `COUNTERED → SUBMITTED` — each merely advancing `version` by one. From `SUBMITTED` the agreement forks two ways, and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric in effect.

The **counter** branch, `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED`, records the school's counter-offer: the supplied `countered_nightly_rate_cents` replaces whatever was there, `counter_round` increases by exactly one, and `version` advances. Critically, no rate is pinned and no billing figure is computed on this branch — that is precisely what distinguishes it from the signature branch. At most two counter rounds may be recorded, so a counter is permitted only while `counter_round` is strictly less than 2; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_MAX_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` and leaves the agreement sitting in `SUBMITTED`.

The **signature** branch, `SUBMITTED → SIGNED`, is the `CorpsDirector`'s act and it pins economics. `effective_nightly_rate_cents` is pinned at signing to the `countered_nightly_rate_cents` whenever `counter_round` is greater than 0, and to the originally proposed `nightly_rate_cents` otherwise; it stays null until the agreement is signed. `billable_bed_nights` is then computed as the greater of the agreement's `occupied_bed_nights` and the product of `minimum_billable_beds` with the agreement's night count, and `billable_cents` is that bed-night figure multiplied by the pinned effective rate. The season's `housing_cost_cents` is recomputed, and `version` advances. An unsigned agreement bills nothing: `billable_cents` stays 0 until the signature lands.

Cancellation is available from every non-terminal status and always stores the supplied `cancel_reason`. Out of `DRAFT`, out of `SUBMITTED` and out of `COUNTERED` the cancellation is inert beyond that — status becomes `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and `version` advances by one. Out of `SIGNED` it is a demolition. Every housing assignment on the agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_CANCELLED`. Every rehearsal block on the agreement whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`, also with `released_reason` `AGREEMENT_CANCELLED`, and the owning school's `booked_block_count` is decremented once per released block. The agreement's `occupied_bed_nights` is set to 0 and its `billable_cents` to 0 — a cancelled agreement bills nothing at all — while `billable_bed_nights` settles at the product of `minimum_billable_beds` with the agreement's night count, floored at 0. Finally each affected school recomputes `reserved_bed_nights` and the season recomputes `housing_cost_cents`. Attempting to cancel an already-`CANCELLED` agreement reports `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

Bedding the roster down is the `MembershipCoordinator`'s job, and it is the most heavily guarded write in the system. The payload names the `facility_agreement_id` whose beds are consumed, the `member_id` being housed, the one-based `space_index` of the sleeping space, the inclusive `check_in_date` of the first night occupied, the **exclusive** `check_out_date` on which the member departs, and the `expected_facility_agreement_version` of §2.8. The check-out night itself is never occupied, so `nights` is computed as the number of days from check-in to check-out, and two stays that merely touch — one member checking out on the morning another checks in — do not overlap at all, because the half-open comparison leaves a gap of exactly zero.

The referenced agreement must be `SIGNED` and its `usage_type` must be `SPRING_TRAINING`, or the request reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED`. The member's `season_id` must equal the agreement's `season_id`, or the request reports `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. The member's `membership_status` must be `CONTRACTED` or `ON_TOUR`, or the request reports `ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_STATE`. The whole stay must sit inside the agreement's window — check-in on or after `start_date` and check-out on or before `end_date` — or the request reports `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_WINDOW`. The `space_index` must be at least 1 and at most the host school's `sleeping_space_count`, or the request reports `ERR_SPACE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`. And the member may not already hold an active stay whose dates overlap the requested ones — in any space, under any agreement — or the request reports `ERR_MEMBER_ALREADY_HOUSED`.

The two remaining checks operate on the **overlap set**: the housing assignments whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, that sit on the same `facility_agreement_id` and the same `space_index`, and whose date range genuinely overlaps the candidate's — that is, whose existing check-in falls strictly before the candidate's check-out and whose existing check-out falls strictly after the candidate's check-in. Released assignments free their beds immediately and are excluded from the overlap set and from every bed-night sum, which is what makes a released bed instantly re-assignable.

Minor supervision comes first. `occupant_is_minor` is snapshotted from the member's `is_minor` at the moment the assignment is created and never re-read afterwards. If any member of the overlap set carries an `occupant_is_minor` value different from the candidate's, the sleeping space would mix minors and adults on overlapping nights and the assignment is refused with `ERR_MINOR_ROOM_MIX`. Only once the space is confirmed to be uniformly minors or uniformly adults does capacity matter: the space's effective capacity is `capacity_per_space` reduced by exactly one bunk whenever a minor occupies the space on an overlapping night — that single reserved bunk is the chaperone's — and the size of the overlap set plus the candidate must not exceed that effective capacity, or the assignment is refused with `ERR_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A twelve-bunk gymnasium therefore holds twelve adults but only eleven minors. Because the minor-mix check outranks the capacity check, an assignment that is simultaneously a minor/adult mix and over capacity always reports the mix.

A successful assignment lands with `status` `ACTIVE` and `released_reason` null, then runs the housing chain in full: the agreement's `occupied_bed_nights` is re-summed over its active stays, `billable_bed_nights` is re-derived as the greater of that occupancy and the minimum-bed floor multiplied by the agreement's night count, `billable_cents` is re-derived from the pinned effective rate, the school's `reserved_bed_nights` is re-summed across all of its agreements, and the season's `housing_cost_cents` is re-summed over `SIGNED` agreements. The agreement's own `version` advances by one as part of the same request — the fact that makes stale-view rejection possible at all.

Releasing a stay by hand sets `released_reason` to `MANUAL`, moves the assignment `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, and runs exactly the same recomputation in reverse: the agreement's occupancy drops by that stay's `nights`, its billing figures are re-derived, the school's `reserved_bed_nights` drops by the same nights, and the season's housing cost is re-summed. A release requires the assignment to be `ACTIVE`; a second release reports `ERR_INVALID_HOUSING_STATE`. The `released_reason` recorded on a stay is `MANUAL` for an operator release, `MEMBER_WITHDRAWN` when the withdrawal cascade freed it, and `AGREEMENT_CANCELLED` when a signed agreement was demolished; the recomputation is identical in all three cases and only the recorded reason differs.

### 3.7. Routing the Tour: Stops, Truck Load, Travel Time and Rehearsal Blocks

The route is a chain of stops, each planned by the `TourManager`. A stop declares the `season_id` being toured, the `previous_stop_id` of the stop the convoy departs from — omitted for the first stop of a leg and otherwise a self-reference into the same collection — a `stop_type` of either `SHOW` or `REHEARSAL_ONLY`, the `venue_name`, the `city` and the two-letter `state_code`, the convoy's `arrive_at` and `depart_at` instants, and, when and only when the stop is a `SHOW`, the `show_start_at` competition instant. A `REHEARSAL_ONLY` stop must carry no `show_start_at` at all; either mismatch reports `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_MISMATCH`, as does a `travel_miles_from_previous` that is anything other than 0 when no predecessor is named, or that is 0 or negative when one is.

The same payload declares what has to be trucked and what the weather will do: `uniform_trunks`, `instrument_cases` and `prop_units` are the counts of uniform trunks, instrument cases and prop units (floor sections, towers) bound for the stop, `truck_count` is the number of tractor-trailers actually available for the move, `rehearsal_minutes` is the planned on-site rehearsal time, and `heat_index_f` is the forecast heat index in degrees Fahrenheit, which must be at least 0 and at most 140. All four count fields must be at least 0. A stop that references a `previous_stop_id` which resolves to nothing reports `ERR_TOUR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`.

Two calculations run at planning time and are re-run when the stop is confirmed. The **transport plan** first converts the declared load into two totals. `total_load_cuft` is twenty-two cubic feet per uniform trunk, plus fourteen cubic feet per instrument case, plus forty-six cubic feet per prop unit. `total_load_lbs` is one hundred and eighty pounds per uniform trunk, plus ninety-five pounds per instrument case, plus two hundred and sixty pounds per prop unit. Four independent dimensions then combine in a pinned order. A volume figure is the total cubic feet divided by the eighteen-hundred-cubic-foot capacity of one trailer, rounded **up** to a whole trailer. A weight figure is the total pounds divided by the twenty-six-thousand-pound payload of one trailer, also rounded up. `trucks_required` is the largest of those two figures and 1 — even an empty load requires one truck. Only then do the two additive surcharges apply, and they apply to that maximum rather than being folded into either ceiling: one extra trailer is added whenever `prop_units` exceeds twenty-four, to escort oversize props, and one further trailer is added whenever `travel_miles_from_previous` is at least three hundred and fifty, to carry a relief tractor. A stop is therefore capable of needing four trucks where a stop one prop unit and three miles smaller needs only two.

The **travel feasibility** calculation produces `required_travel_minutes`, the convoy time the route demands. When no `previous_stop_id` is named the figure is 0. Otherwise the drive component is `travel_miles_from_previous` divided by an average convoy speed of forty-five miles per hour and converted to minutes, rounded **up** to a whole minute; the fuel component is `travel_miles_from_previous` divided by two hundred miles, rounded **down** to a whole number of stops, each stop costing thirty minutes; and `required_travel_minutes` is the drive component plus the fuel component. The fuel stop appears exactly *at* two hundred miles rather than beyond it, so a two-hundred-mile leg carries one fuel stop and a one-hundred-and-ninety-nine-mile leg carries none. A newly planned stop lands with `status` `PLANNED`, `settlement_state` `NONE`, `meal_cost_cents` at 0, and `roster_head_count`, `minor_head_count`, `required_chaperone_count` and `required_cook_count` all at 0 until confirmation snapshots them.

Between performances the corps rehearses on borrowed fields, which the `TourManager` reserves as **rehearsal blocks**. A block names the `tour_stop_id` it belongs to, the `host_school_id` being occupied, the `facility_agreement_id` authorizing the occupation, the inclusive `block_start_at` and exclusive `block_end_at` instants, and an `indoor` flag stating whether the block is held inside. The parent stop must be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, or the booking reports `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`. The agreement must be `SIGNED` with `usage_type` `TOUR_REHEARSAL`, or the booking reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SIGNED`. The supplied `host_school_id` must be the very school the agreement names, or the booking reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`. The stop's `season_id` must equal the agreement's `season_id`, or the booking reports `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. And the block must fall wholly inside the agreement's window, read as running from midnight UTC on `start_date` inclusive to midnight UTC on `end_date` exclusive; a block poking outside it reports `ERR_BLOCK_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_WINDOW`.

A host school can host exactly one rehearsal block at a time, and it needs load-in and load-out time on **both** sides of every block. The buffer is ninety minutes per side, and each of the two blocks contributes its own ninety minutes, so a candidate block conflicts with an existing block at the same school — one whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` — whenever the candidate starts earlier than that block's end pushed one hundred and eighty minutes later, and the candidate ends later than that block's start pulled one hundred and eighty minutes earlier. Equivalently, two blocks at one school must be separated by a clear gap of at least one hundred and eighty minutes; a gap of exactly one hundred and eighty minutes is accepted because the comparison is strict, and one of a hundred and seventy-nine minutes is refused with `ERR_SITE_BLOCK_CONFLICT`. Released blocks impose no buffer at all, which is what makes a released slot immediately re-bookable.

Heat is the other gate. When the parent stop's `heat_index_f` stands at 105 or above, a rehearsal block may be created only if it is both indoors and at an air-conditioned school — `indoor` must be true **and** the school's `has_air_conditioning` must be true. An outdoor block, or an indoor block at a school without air conditioning, is prohibited under the extreme-heat protocol and reports `ERR_EXTREME_HEAT_OUTDOOR_PROHIBITED`.

A new block lands with `status` `HELD`, `released_reason` null, and the owning school's `booked_block_count` increased by one. A held block is promoted `HELD → CONFIRMED` automatically when its parent stop is confirmed. The `TourManager` may hand a block back to the school at any point out of `HELD` or out of `CONFIRMED`, which sets `released_reason` to `MANUAL`, moves the block to `RELEASED`, and decrements the school's `booked_block_count` by one; the two source statuses release identically. Releasing an already-`RELEASED` block reports `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The server also releases blocks on its own initiative out of both `HELD` and `CONFIRMED` — with `released_reason` `STOP_CANCELLED` when the parent stop is cancelled and `AGREEMENT_CANCELLED` when the authorizing signed agreement is cancelled — and in each of those cases too the school's `booked_block_count` is decremented once per released block.

### 3.8. Volunteer Screening, Shift Staffing and the Life of a Stop

The corps runs on adult volunteers, and every one of them passes through the `VolunteerScreener` before touching a bus or a kitchen. Registration carries the volunteer's `full_name`, their `email` — which must be unique across all volunteers in the system, not merely within a season, with collisions reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` — and their `role_type`, which is one of `DRIVER`, `COOK`, `CHAPERONE` or `ATHLETIC_TRAINER`. Two credential fields are conditional: `cdl_number` must be supplied when and only when the role type is `DRIVER`, and `credential_id` must be supplied when and only when the role type is `ATHLETIC_TRAINER`. Supplying either outside its condition, or omitting it inside its condition, reports `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_MISMATCH`. A new volunteer starts with `screening_status` `PENDING`, `assignment_count` at 0, and `screening_decided_on`, `screening_expires_on` and `screened_by_user_id` all null.

Recording a background-screening outcome carries a `decision` of either `CLEAR` or `REJECT` together with the `decided_on` date on which the decision was reached, which may not be later than the logical-clock date; a future-dated decision reports `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_INPUT`. The volunteer's `screening_status` must be `PENDING`, so a second decision on an already-decided volunteer reports `ERR_INVALID_SCREENING_STATE`. Both outcomes store the supplied date as `screening_decided_on`, stamp `screened_by_user_id` with the deciding user's `X-User-ID`, and advance `version` by one; the two branches differ in exactly one respect. A clearance moves the volunteer `PENDING → CLEARED` and computes `screening_expires_on` as `screening_decided_on` advanced by three hundred and sixty-five days. A rejection moves the volunteer `PENDING → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and leaves `screening_expires_on` null.

Shifts are proposed by the `TourManager`, naming the `volunteer_id`, the `tour_stop_id` being staffed, the `duty` performed — `DRIVE`, `COOK`, `CHAPERONE` or `TRAINER` — and the inclusive `shift_start_at` and exclusive `shift_end_at` instants. The parent stop must be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, or the proposal reports `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`. The duty must correspond exactly to the volunteer's `role_type`: `DRIVE` pairs with `DRIVER`, `COOK` with `COOK`, `CHAPERONE` with `CHAPERONE`, and `TRAINER` with `ATHLETIC_TRAINER`; any other pairing reports `ERR_DUTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`. The shift must sit inside the stop's window, starting no earlier than the stop's `arrive_at` and ending no later than its `depart_at`, or the proposal reports `ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_STOP_WINDOW`. And one volunteer may not hold two open shifts whose windows overlap: for the same volunteer, no shift whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED` or `APPROVED` may have a start strictly earlier than the candidate's end while also having an end strictly later than the candidate's start. Touching shifts — one ending exactly when the next begins — are legal; genuine overlap reports `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. A new shift lands in `status` `PROPOSED` with `approved_by_user_id` and `released_reason` null.

Staffing then runs a three-party chain. The volunteer accepts their own shift, moving it `PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`; `X-User-ID` must equal the shift's `volunteer_id`, and a volunteer reaching for someone else's shift reports `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The `TourManager` then approves it, moving it `ACCEPTED → APPROVED`, stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the approving user's `X-User-ID`, and increasing the volunteer's `assignment_count` by one. Approval carries two gates beyond the status gate: the volunteer's `screening_status` must be `CLEARED`, so a `PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` screening reports `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED`; and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the volunteer's `screened_by_user_id`, so the screener may never bless their own subject and an attempt reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Since the screening gate is evaluated ahead of the duties gate, a shift for an unscreened volunteer reports the screening problem even when the approver is also the screener.

Confirming a stop is the moment every one of these threads is pulled tight, and it is the `TourManager`'s call. The stop must be `PLANNED`. Its predecessor, when one is named, must itself have reached `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` — a route cannot be locked in ahead of the leg feeding it, and a predecessor still sitting in `PLANNED` blocks the confirmation with `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`. The convoy must be able to make the drive: the predecessor's `depart_at` advanced by this stop's `required_travel_minutes` must fall no later than this stop's `arrive_at`, or the confirmation reports `ERR_TRAVEL_TIME_INFEASIBLE`. The fleet must be adequate: `truck_count` must be at least the computed `trucks_required`, or the confirmation reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRUCKS`.

Three staffing-coverage gates then fire, each counting only shifts on this stop whose `status` is `APPROVED`. Chaperone coverage requires at least one approved `CHAPERONE` shift for every ten minors on the touring roster — the minor head count divided by ten and rounded **up** — reported as `ERR_CHAPERONE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` when short. Cook coverage requires at least one approved `COOK` shift for every forty members on the touring roster — the roster head count divided by forty and rounded up — reported as `ERR_COOK_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` when short. Athletic-trainer coverage requires at least one approved `TRAINER` shift whenever the stop is strenuous or hot: whenever `rehearsal_minutes` is at least two hundred and forty, that is four hours or more of on-site rehearsal, **or** `heat_index_f` is at least 95. Either condition alone suffices to require the trainer, and its absence reports `ERR_TRAINER_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`. All three ratios are computed against the live touring roster at the instant of confirmation.

Confirmation's side effects are extensive. `roster_head_count` is snapshotted as the number of members of the stop's season whose `membership_status` is `ON_TOUR`, and `minor_head_count` as the number of those same members whose `is_minor` is true. `required_chaperone_count` is snapshotted as the minor head count divided by ten and rounded up, and `required_cook_count` as the roster head count divided by forty and rounded up. The transport plan and the travel-feasibility figures are re-run and re-written, so `total_load_cuft`, `total_load_lbs`, `trucks_required` and `required_travel_minutes` all reflect the load as it stands at confirmation. Every rehearsal block on the stop whose `status` is `HELD` is promoted to `CONFIRMED`. The stop moves `PLANNED → CONFIRMED` and its `version` advances by one.

Confirmation also strikes the food-service cost, snapshotted into `meal_cost_cents`. The population is exactly the members of the stop's season whose `membership_status` is `ON_TOUR`, grouped by `dietary_flag`; members still in `APPLICANT`, `OFFERED` or `CONTRACTED`, and members who have `WITHDRAWN`, are all excluded. The arithmetic has two steps and the order between them is what makes it reproducible. Step one computes a per-member unit cost **once per dietary class**, as a base meal cost of eleven dollars and seventy-five cents multiplied by that class's dietary multiplier and rounded half up to a whole cent: the multiplier is one for `NONE`, giving a unit of 1175 cents; one and eight hundredths for `VEGETARIAN`, giving 1269 cents; one and twenty-two hundredths for `GLUTEN_FREE`, giving 1434 cents; and one and forty-five hundredths for `SEVERE_ALLERGEN`, giving 1704 cents. Step two multiplies each **already-rounded** unit by that class's head count and by the three meals served per stop, then sums those products across the four classes. Rounding a grand total assembled from unrounded products would produce a different and incorrect figure; the rounding happens per dietary class, before any multiplication or summation. A stop that has not been confirmed carries `meal_cost_cents` at 0.

Completion closes a stop out. It moves the stop `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` and requires that the logical-clock instant have reached or passed the stop's `depart_at`; completing early reports `ERR_STOP_NOT_DEPARTED`. A stop whose `stop_type` is `SHOW` has its `settlement_state` moved `NONE → PENDING`, opening it for circuit reconciliation, while a `REHEARSAL_ONLY` stop leaves `settlement_state` at `NONE` — there is nothing for the circuit to settle. The season's `ledger_status` is deliberately **not** re-evaluated here; it moves only inside a billing sweep.

Cancellation is the `CorpsDirector`'s and is available out of `PLANNED` and out of `CONFIRMED`, with identical effects from both. The supplied `cancel_reason` is stored and the stop moves to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal alongside `COMPLETED`. The first cascade link releases rehearsal space: every rehearsal block on the stop whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `STOP_CANCELLED`. The second link, caused by the first, decrements each affected school's `booked_block_count` once per released block. The third releases people: every shift on the stop whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `STOP_CANCELLED`, and for each shift that was `APPROVED` at the moment of release, that volunteer's `assignment_count` is decremented by one — shifts that never reached approval never contributed to the count and so do not decrement it. The stop's `version` advances by one.

### 3.9. Circuit Settlement and Prize Reconciliation

Every competitive show generates money in two directions: the touring circuit owes the corps an appearance fee and a share of the prize pool, and it deducts a circuit fee, occasional sanction charges and, sometimes, a rebate of part of the fee. Reconciling that is the `BusinessManager`'s closing act, and it happens twice for every show — once optimistically, at the moment the settlement is opened, and once definitively, when the circuit's certified results and remittance advice arrive.

Opening a settlement names the `tour_stop_id` being reconciled, the contracted `appearance_fee_cents` the circuit owes, the total `prize_pool_cents` the circuit posted for the show, and the `projected_placement` the corps registered as its own projection, which must be at least 1 and at most 12. The parent stop must have `status` `COMPLETED` and `stop_type` `SHOW`, or the request reports `ERR_STOP_NOT_COMPLETED`; a stop may carry at most one settlement, so a second attempt reports `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The draft lands with `status` `DRAFT`, `prepared_by_user_id` stamped with the opening user's `X-User-ID`, `finalized_by_user_id` and `final_placement` null, `remittance_line_count` at 0, and every one of `final_prize_cents`, `final_gross_cents`, `final_circuit_fee_cents`, `sanctions_charged_cents`, `rebate_applied_cents`, `final_net_cents` and `variance_cents` null until finalization.

Two rate schedules govern both passes. The **placement prize share** is thirty percent of the prize pool for a first-place finish, twenty-two percent for second, sixteen percent for third, twelve percent for fourth and nine percent for fifth; any placement of sixth or worse earns a flat five percent. The **circuit fee rate** is tiered on the *full* gross rather than marginally: four percent when the gross falls below fifteen thousand dollars, five and one-half percent when it falls below thirty thousand dollars, and seven percent at thirty thousand dollars or above.

The **provisional pass** runs when the draft is opened and reads the corps' own projection. `provisional_prize_cents` is the prize pool multiplied by the share belonging to `projected_placement`, rounded half up to a whole cent. `provisional_gross_cents` is the appearance fee plus that prize. `provisional_circuit_fee_cents` is that gross multiplied by the fee rate the *same* gross selects, rounded half up. And `provisional_net_cents` is the gross less the fee. No remittance lines exist at this point, so no sanction and no rebate figure into the provisional net; it is the optimistic number the tour budget carries.

The **final pass** runs at finalization, which carries the circuit's certified `final_placement` — also at least 1 and at most 12 — together with `remittance_lines`, an array of objects each carrying a `kind` of `PRIZE_ADJUSTMENT`, `SANCTION_FEE` or `FEE_REBATE`, an `amount_cents` integer, and a `recorded_at` ISO 8601 UTC instant. Finalization requires the settlement to be `DRAFT`, or it reports `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`; requires the parent stop still to be `COMPLETED` and a `SHOW`, or it reports `ERR_STOP_NOT_COMPLETED`; and requires the finalizing `X-User-ID` to differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, or it reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The final pass re-strikes the prize on the certified placement and then assembles the gross before touching the fee — this evaluation order is pinned, and reading the tier off the appearance fee alone, or applying the fee before the adjustments, gives a different and wrong answer. `final_prize_cents` is the prize pool multiplied by the share belonging to `final_placement`, rounded half up. `final_gross_cents` is the appearance fee, plus that final prize, plus the sum of the `amount_cents` of every line whose `kind` is `PRIZE_ADJUSTMENT`. Only once that gross stands complete is the fee tier read off it, and `final_circuit_fee_cents` is the final gross multiplied by that tier's rate, rounded half up. Because prize adjustments move the gross, they can move the fee tier along with it.

The remaining lines are then **replayed chronologically**, and the replay order is the whole point of the algorithm. Lines are sorted by `recorded_at` ascending and, for lines sharing an instant, by their index in the submitted array ascending; no identifier ever participates in the ordering. `PRIZE_ADJUSTMENT` lines are excluded from the replay, having already been consumed by the gross. Two running quantities carry through it: a running net that starts at the final gross less the final circuit fee, and a rebate allowance that starts at exactly the final circuit fee, because a rebate can never give back more than the fee actually charged. `sanctions_charged_cents` and `rebate_applied_cents` both start at 0.

For each `SANCTION_FEE` line, a per-line cap is computed as fifteen percent of the running net **as it stands at that moment**, rounded half up; the amount actually charged is the smaller of the line's `amount_cents` and that cap; the running net is reduced by the charged amount; and `sanctions_charged_cents` accumulates it. For each `FEE_REBATE` line, the amount actually applied is the smaller of the line's `amount_cents` and the remaining rebate allowance; the allowance is reduced by the applied amount; the running net is increased by it; and `rebate_applied_cents` accumulates it. Because every sanction cap is a percentage of the net at that instant rather than of the original gross, two sanction lines carrying the same pair of amounts in the opposite order produce genuinely different final nets on otherwise identical data — which is exactly why the ordering is pinned rather than left to the implementation.

When the replay ends, `final_net_cents` is the running net and `variance_cents` is the final net less the provisional net — a negative variance being the ordinary outcome when the circuit's certified placement is worse than the projection or the sanctions bite. `remittance_line_count` is written as the number of remittance lines submitted with the finalization, counting the `PRIZE_ADJUSTMENT` lines as well as the replayed sanction and rebate lines.

Finalization then closes the chain. The settlement moves `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, `finalized_by_user_id` is stamped with the finalizing user's `X-User-ID`, and the settlement's `version` advances by one. The parent stop's `settlement_state` moves `PENDING → SETTLED`. And because that stop now carries a finalized settlement, the season's `tour_revenue_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `final_net_cents` over the `FINALIZED` settlements of that season's stops. The season's `ledger_status` is not touched here — it turns `RECONCILED` only on a later billing sweep that finds every stop closed and every settlement finalized.

Collections sit at the root of the URL space and are addressed as peers; relationships travel as foreign keys in request bodies rather than as nested path segments. Operations that bring a new resource into existence return `201 Created`.
