# NIL Collective Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A name-image-and-likeness collective sits in an uncomfortable place. It is a fundraising organisation that must court donors with membership tiers, recurring subscriptions and major-gift solicitation; it is a contracting party that must promise student-athletes money in exchange for appearances, social posts, charity work and autograph sessions; and it is a reporting entity that must answer to an institution's compliance office, to state disclosure law, to an athletics association's rulebook, and to the tax authorities on both sides of the transaction. Today most collectives run these four jobs on spreadsheets, and the failure modes are predictable: money is promised before it is raised, a payout is released against work nobody documented, a coach discusses deal terms on a channel the institution never sees, and an athlete who entered the transfer portal keeps drawing on a contract that should have been frozen.

This document specifies the NIL Collective Administration API, the system of record that closes those gaps. Its central design conviction is that **money-out is never a matter of intent — it is a matter of evidence and headroom**. Every dollar released must trace back to a settled, correctly designated contribution; to a deliverable whose documentation of services a compliance officer actually reviewed; to a contract whose disclosure the institution actually adjudicated; and to an athlete whose eligibility is current and whose taxpayer certification is on file. A second conviction follows from the first: the person who verifies work may not be the person who pays for it. Segregation of duties is not advice here, it is an enforced rejection.

The API is mounted under `/nil`. It is deliberately deterministic: there is no wall-clock time, no random ordering, no partial batch success, and no place where a caller's own identifier decides how a computation is ordered. Every figure in this specification is an integer number of cents, every ordering is pinned to an explicit key, and every rejection carries a stable machine-readable code so that a collective's back office can reconcile a refusal without a support ticket.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must present two headers: `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role, and `X-User-ID`, a non-empty actor handle. If either header is absent, or if either header is present but empty — an empty `X-User-Role` counts as absent rather than as an unrecognised role — the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is present and non-empty but is not one of the six recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 400). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles permitted on the addressed endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `CollectiveDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `InstitutionReviewer`, `Athlete`, and `TreasuryOfficer`. `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform: it onboards collectives, changes a collective's tax entity structure, and drives the logical clock. `CollectiveDirector` runs fundraising and contracting — tiers, donors, commitments, contributions, athletes, individual and roster-wide contracts, deliverables and communication records. `ComplianceOfficer` owns eligibility, taxpayer documentation and deliverable review, and is the only role permitted to record a deliverable review decision or an athlete eligibility change. `InstitutionReviewer` represents the institution's compliance office: it is the only role permitted to adjudicate a contract disclosure, and it may otherwise only read. `Athlete` is the student-athlete's own login. `TreasuryOfficer` moves money — contribution settlements, refunds, billing runs, payment releases and reversals.

Five of the six roles are **opaque service roles**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `CollectiveDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `InstitutionReviewer`, or `TreasuryOfficer`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it against any row's `id`. It is nonetheless load-bearing, because the server records it in two places: a compliance officer's `X-User-ID` is stored as `verified_by_user_id` on a deliverable when that officer verifies or waives it, and a treasury officer's `X-User-ID` is stored as `initiated_by_user_id` on every payment release that officer creates. The segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.11 compares exactly those two recorded strings.

The `Athlete` role alone is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Athlete`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `portal_user_id` of the acting `Athlete` row; ownership checks compare that handle against the athlete named by the `athlete_id` of the deal contract that owns the addressed deliverable. Concretely, an athlete may record documentation of services and may submit a deliverable, but only for a deliverable whose parent contract names the athlete whose `portal_user_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`; any other deliverable yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403). The `Athlete` role is additionally barred from every donor-facing read — the donor member, commitment and contribution reads, both in detail and list form — and from every write other than deliverable evidence and deliverable submission; each such attempt is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty further characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in this specification taken from real server time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it advances between requests even while the logical clock stands still.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1, and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Successful list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer literal is rejected the same way, while an empty-string value for either is treated as absent and takes its default. Unknown query parameters are ignored; a declared enum filter carrying an undeclared value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an empty-string filter value is treated as absent; and an identifier-valued filter naming a row that does not exist is not an error at all — it simply matches nothing and yields an empty page.

List results are returned in insertion order — ascending `sequence_no` of the listed entity type, as described in §2.4. Because the logical clock described in §2.5 is static between explicit clock moves and may be moved backward, ascending `created_at` is not a total order and must not be used as the sort key; `sequence_no` ascending is the sort order for every list endpoint in this specification — with the single exception of the collective list, whose entity type carries no `sequence_no` and which is therefore returned in ascending order of collective creation, the same insertion order a `sequence_no` would express.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Insertion Order and Row Versions

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the instant the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation that the specification says bumps it. Collectives, membership tiers, donor members, commitments, contributions, athletes, deal contracts, deliverables and payment releases all carry `updated_at` and `version`. The communication log is the sole exception: a communication record carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, because it is append-only — there is no endpoint that modifies a recorded touchpoint, and it can therefore never serve as a stale-version parent.

Ordering in this system is never left to chance. Every entity type except the collective carries `sequence_no`, a monotonic per-entity-type insertion counter that starts at 1, is assigned when the row is created, is never reused, and is entirely independent of the logical clock. `sequence_no` is the primary sort key or the final tie-break for every order-sensitive rule in this document: list ordering, cascade ordering, allocation remainder distribution, deduction attribution and batch replay. The `id` is used for identity only. It is never a selector for business logic, never a ranking input, never a tie-break, never a cascade order and never an allocation order.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Tax Years and Calendar Arithmetic

The server holds a single logical UTC clock, and every timestamp this system writes is stamped from it: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `received_at`, `released_at`, `verified_at`, `submitted_at`, `logged_at`, `recorded_at`, `executed_at`, `structure_effective_at` and `disclosure_submitted_at`. At startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a `PlatformAdmin` moves it the server must report that default rather than falling back to real wall-clock time. The clock does not tick as requests arrive: it is static between explicit moves.

`POST /nil/_clock/set_current_utc` is the only way to move it. The request supplies `current_utc`, the instant to move to, and the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside `previous_utc`, the instant the clock read before the call. The clock may be moved backward as well as forward. This endpoint exists because every window in this specification — the disclosure window, deliverable due dates, the tax-year boundary and the reversal window — is expressed against the logical clock, and no business endpoint can reach those boundaries on its own.

Wherever this document says "tax year" or "year to date", it means the four-digit UTC calendar year of the current logical clock reading. Wherever it says a stored timestamp falls in the current tax year, it means the four-digit UTC calendar year of that timestamp equals the four-digit UTC calendar year of the current clock reading.

### 2.6. Money, Integer Cents and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents; no endpoint accepts or returns a fractional currency amount. Two rounding operations appear, and each calculation states which one it uses and in what order:

- **Round half up** means the exact rational quotient is rounded to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half rounded away from zero.
- **Floor** means the exact rational quotient is truncated toward negative infinity.

Percentage and basis-point tests are always evaluated by integer cross-multiplication, never by first dividing to produce a percentage figure, so that boundary comparisons are exact rather than dependent on an intermediate rounding. Where a calculation applies more than one rounding step, the order of those steps is stated explicitly in that calculation's narrative and must be followed as written.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Parent Records

Four write operations mutate a child row while depending on the state of a parent row, and each requires the caller to echo the parent `version` it last observed. The version field is a required input on these operations, not an optional one, and its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

- `POST /nil/commitments` requires `expected_donor_member_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `DonorMember`. If that integer does not equal the donor member's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DONOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. On success the donor member's `version` is incremented by exactly 1.
- `POST /nil/deal-contracts/roster-allocations` requires `expected_collective_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Collective`. A mismatch with the collective's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COLLECTIVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no contract is minted. On success the collective's `version` is incremented by exactly 1 for the whole batch, however many contracts the batch created.
- `PATCH /nil/collectives/{id}/entity-structure` requires `expected_collective_version`. A mismatch with the collective's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COLLECTIVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and no part of the restructure cascade runs. On success the collective's `version` is incremented by exactly 1.
- `POST /nil/payment-releases` requires `expected_deal_contract_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `DealContract`. A mismatch with the contract's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no release row is created. On success the contract's `version` is incremented by exactly 1.

The purpose in each case is the same: a client that read a parent, then acted on a stale picture of it, must be told so rather than allowed to write against a lifecycle, payment or disclosure position it never observed. No endpoint outside this list of four accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere is simply ignored as an unknown body field.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Fails

Validation is strictly ordered. The server evaluates checks in ascending priority and the **first** violated check decides the response; no later check can mask an earlier one, and nothing is written when any check fails. Callers and implementers alike depend on this ladder, so it is stated here once in full:

1. Authentication headers present — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`, on every endpoint.
2. The presented role is a recognised role — `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, on every endpoint.
3. The presented role appears in the addressed endpoint's permitted roles — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, on every endpoint.
4. Entity-backed ownership: an athlete caller may only act on their own deliverables — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`, on deliverable evidence recording and deliverable submission.
5. Request body and query shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, on every write endpoint and every list endpoint.
6. Every entity addressed by a path placeholder exists — the matching not-found code, on every endpoint with a path placeholder.
7. Every foreign key supplied in a request body references an existing row — the matching not-found code, on donor member creation, commitment creation, contribution creation, athlete creation, individual contract creation, payment release creation and communication log creation.
8. All entities named in one request belong to the same collective — `ERR_CROSS_COLLECTIVE_REFERENCE`, on donor member creation, individual contract creation and communication log creation.
9. A named child belongs to the named parent — `ERR_DELIVERABLE_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` or `ERR_COMMITMENT_DONOR_MISMATCH`, on payment release creation and contribution creation.
10. Institution code uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_IPEDS_CODE`, on collective creation.
11. Tier code uniqueness within a collective — `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`, on membership tier creation.
12. Donor email and athlete portal handle uniqueness within a collective — `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_HANDLE`, on donor member creation and athlete creation.
13. Membership tier state — `ERR_TIER_NOT_DRAFT` or `ERR_TIER_NOT_OPEN`, on tier transitions, donor member creation and commitment transitions.
14. Commitment state — `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_CHARGEABLE`, on commitment transitions, commitment restructuring and contribution creation.
15. Contribution state — `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_STATE_INVALID`, on contribution settlement and refund.
16. Athlete eligibility state — `ERR_ATHLETE_STATE_INVALID`, on athlete eligibility changes.
17. Contract lifecycle state — `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`, on contract activation, compliance submission, compliance decision, termination, deliverable creation, deliverable submission and payment release creation.
18. Contract disclosure state — `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_SUBMITTED` or `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_CLEARED`, on compliance submission, compliance decision and activation.
19. Deliverable state — `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`, on deliverable submission, deliverable review and evidence recording.
20. Payment release state — `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_RELEASED`, on release reversal.
21. Parent stale-version guards — `ERR_STALE_DONOR_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_COLLECTIVE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`, on the four operations listed in §2.7.
22. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on payment release creation. This check deliberately sits after existence, referential and state checks because it can only be evaluated once the named deliverable has been resolved and confirmed to belong to the contract.
23. Funding posture gate on individual contract creation — `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`.
24. Funding posture gate on roster allocation — `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_RESTRICTED` or `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`.
25. Funding posture gate on payment release creation — `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`.
26. The athlete is enrolled, for contracting — `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_ENROLLED`, on individual contract creation and contract activation.
27. A roster batch resolves to at least one eligible athlete — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ATHLETES`.
28. Exclusivity window non-overlap — `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on individual contract creation and roster allocation.
29. A deliverable's due instant falls inside the contract window — `ERR_DUE_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`.
30. A reversal stays inside the tax year of the release — `ERR_REVERSAL_ACROSS_TAX_YEARS`.
31. Minimum documentation of services before submission — `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`.
32. Documented charity hours meet the requirement — `ERR_CHARITY_HOURS_SHORTFALL`.
33. Submission attempt limit — `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`.
34. Tier dues increase strictly with benefit rank — `ERR_TIER_RANK_DUES_INVERSION`, on tier transitions.
35. Entity-structure change budget and change-of-value — `ERR_STRUCTURE_CHANGE_LIMIT` or `ERR_STRUCTURE_UNCHANGED`.
36. A deliverable payout names a verified or waived deliverable — `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_VERIFIED`.
37. A release stays inside the contract's face value — `ERR_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT_VALUE`.
38. Every roster share is at least one cent — `ERR_ROSTER_SHARE_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
39. The athlete's annual committed value stays inside the cap — `ERR_ATHLETE_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`, on individual contract creation and roster allocation.
40. Billing interval matches commitment kind — `ERR_BILLING_INTERVAL_MISMATCH`.
41. Contribution source kind pairs correctly with a commitment — `ERR_SOURCE_KIND_COMMITMENT_MISMATCH`.
42. A release draws on a matching designation — `ERR_FUNDING_DESIGNATION_MISMATCH`.
43. The designated pool holds enough unreleased settled funds — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DESIGNATED_FUNDS`.
44. A nonresident-alien payee has the required certification — `ERR_TAXPAYER_DOC_REQUIRED`.
45. A deliverable payout stays inside the deliverable's eligible amount — `ERR_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_PAYOUT_ELIGIBLE`.
46. Roster batch overlap capacity on the collective-and-sport resource — `ERR_BATCH_OVERLAP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This rule is deliberately evaluated last, so that a batch which also violates any cheaper rule reports that cheaper rule instead.

Shape validation at priority 5 covers all of the following in one pass: every field the endpoint declares required is present and of the declared type; every enum field carries a declared value; `annual_dues_cents`, `amount_cents`, `gross_amount_cents`, `total_value_cents`, `unit_value_cents` and `requested_amount_cents` are each strictly greater than zero; `annual_agi_cents` and `required_charity_hours_tenths` are each at least zero; `roster_weight` is between 1 and 10 inclusive; `benefit_rank` is between 1 and 5 inclusive; `class_year` is between 2026 and 2031 inclusive; `installments_total` is at least 1; `home_state_code` is one of `AL`, `CA`, `FL`, `OH`, `TX`; `effective_end_at` is strictly after `effective_start_at`; `required_charity_hours_tenths` is strictly greater than zero when `deliverable_kind` is `CHARITY_WORK` and exactly zero otherwise; `limit` is between 1 and 100 and `offset` is at least zero; and every supplied timestamp is an ISO 8601 UTC instant, read leniently — the trailing `Z` may be omitted and the value is then read as UTC, a non-zero UTC offset is accepted and normalised to UTC, the seconds component may be omitted, and fractional digits beyond the third are accepted on input and truncated rather than rounded to three digits when the instant is rendered back. Unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected. An absent request body is treated as an empty JSON object, so each required field is reported missing in turn; a body that is present but is not a JSON object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field explicitly set to `null` is treated as absent — it takes its declared default where the field has one and fails the required-field check where it does not. A required string field must be a non-empty, non-whitespace string, and an optional string field that is supplied must clear the same bar — the single exception is a communication record's free-text `note`, which may be empty or whitespace and is stored exactly as supplied. A JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared.

### 2.9. Nested Addressing and Referential Integrity

Only two URL paths nest: membership tiers hang off a collective, and deliverables hang off a deal contract. Nested paths are validated left to right — the outer entity is resolved first, so addressing a tier collection under an unknown collective yields `ERR_COLLECTIVE_NOT_FOUND` and addressing a deliverable collection under an unknown contract yields `ERR_DEAL_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, in both cases before any body field is examined, and before the pagination and filter parameters of a nested list endpoint are validated as well. Everything else in this system is peer-addressed: the relationship is carried in the request body as a foreign key, and a body key naming a row that does not exist yields the matching not-found code — `ERR_COLLECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DONOR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COMMUNICATION_LOG_NOT_FOUND`. Where one payload names more than one foreign key, they are resolved in the order this document lists them — on donor member creation the `collective_id` before the `membership_tier_id` — so a payload naming two unknown rows reports the first of them.

Two further integrity rules follow. First, all entities named in a single request must belong to the same collective: a donor member's `membership_tier_id` must name a tier of the collective given by its `collective_id`, a deal contract's `athlete_id` must name an athlete of the collective given by its `collective_id`, and a communication record's `athlete_id`, when supplied, must name an athlete of the collective given by its `collective_id`. A violation is `ERR_CROSS_COLLECTIVE_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409). Second, a named child must belong to the named parent: a payment release's `deliverable_id` must name a deliverable whose `deal_contract_id` equals the release's `deal_contract_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`; and a contribution's `commitment_id` must name a commitment whose `donor_member_id` equals the contribution's `donor_member_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_COMMITMENT_DONOR_MISMATCH`. Both are HTTP 409.

Foreign keys are immutable once written. A membership tier's `collective_id`, a donor member's `collective_id`, a commitment's `donor_member_id`, a contribution's `donor_member_id`, an athlete's `collective_id`, a deal contract's `collective_id` and `athlete_id`, a deliverable's `deal_contract_id`, a payment release's `deal_contract_id` and a communication record's `collective_id` are all fixed at creation and no endpoint changes them.

### 2.10. The Funding Posture Gate

Each collective carries a derived spending posture, `funding_posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is not driven by any trigger endpoint. Instead it is re-derived and persisted at the end of every request that changes the collective's `settled_net_funds_cents` or its `committed_unreleased_cents`, and that persistence refreshes the collective's `updated_at` only when the re-derivation actually moves something: the four ledger figures and the posture are compared against the values already stored, and if every one of the five comes back identical the row is left alone and its `updated_at` does not advance. A re-derivation never bumps the collective's `version` — only the two operations of §2.7 that name `expected_collective_version` do that. Any posture may move to any other posture, including back to itself, as the underlying figures move.

The derivation works on three collective-level figures. `settled_net_funds_cents` is the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the collective's contributions whose status is `SETTLED`, and over those rows only: a contribution in `PENDING`, `FAILED` or `REFUNDED` contributes nothing to the sum and is never subtracted from it either, so a refund lowers the figure purely by dropping its row out of the `SETTLED` set rather than by deducting that row a second time. `released_total_cents` is the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over the collective's payment releases with status `RELEASED`; `QUEUED`, `VOIDED` and `REVERSED` releases contribute nothing. `reversed_total_cents` is the corresponding sum over releases with status `REVERSED`. `committed_unreleased_cents` is the sum, over the collective's deal contracts whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, of the contract's `total_value_cents` less that contract's own released total; it is never negative, and contracts in `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` or `VOIDED` are excluded entirely.

From those, let the available balance be `settled_net_funds_cents` minus `released_total_cents`, and let the committed figure be `committed_unreleased_cents`. If the committed figure is zero, the posture is `OPEN`. Otherwise, if the available balance is greater than or equal to the committed figure, the posture is `OPEN`. Otherwise, if the available balance multiplied by 100 is greater than or equal to the committed figure multiplied by 75, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. In every remaining case the posture is `FROZEN`. That comparison is performed in cross-multiplied integer form and never by dividing to produce a coverage percentage, so a collective whose available balance covers exactly three quarters of its commitments lands on `RESTRICTED` rather than on `FROZEN`.

Three operations consult the posture, and all three read the value **as it stood before the current request's mutation**; the value stored on the row is the post-mutation posture. Creating an individual deal contract is refused with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN` while the pre-request posture is `FROZEN`. A roster-wide allocation batch requires a pre-request posture of `OPEN`: a `RESTRICTED` posture refuses it with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_RESTRICTED` and a `FROZEN` posture with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`. Creating a payment release is refused with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN` while the pre-request posture is `FROZEN`. Contract activation is deliberately **not** gated on the posture: a contract that is already counted as committed can always be activated.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The collective's control environment rests on one non-negotiable separation: the person who attests that work was performed may not be the person who pays for it. When a payment release with `release_kind` of `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT` is created, the caller's `X-User-ID` is compared against the `verified_by_user_id` recorded on the named deliverable when a compliance officer verified or waived it. If the two strings are equal, the release is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). This is why `X-User-ID` matters even for opaque roles, and why the verifier's handle is persisted on the deliverable rather than merely logged.

### 2.12. Cascades, Bubble-Ups and Ledger Re-derivation

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Three distinct shapes recur, and each is deterministic.

**Downward cascades** propagate from parent to children, always processed in ascending `sequence_no` of the child entity type. An entity-structure change closes tiers, then pauses commitments on those tiers, then lapses the affected donors — three levels, each level fully processed in `sequence_no` order before the next begins. A contract entering `TERMINATED` or `VOIDED` forfeits its non-terminal deliverables in `sequence_no` order and then voids its queued releases in `sequence_no` order. An athlete completing a transfer or being declared ineligible terminates that athlete's `DRAFT` and `ACTIVE` contracts in contract `sequence_no` order, and each of those terminations then runs its own deliverable and release cascade.

**Bubble-ups** propagate from child to parent. A settled contribution can move its donor member to `ACTIVE`; a paused, cancelled or fulfilled commitment can move its donor member to `LAPSED`; a released payment can move its contract's payment dimension forward; and a fully paid contract whose deliverables are all terminal can move its lifecycle dimension to `COMPLETED`.

**Ledger re-derivation** is the third shape. Any change to a settled contribution, a released or reversed payment, or a contract's lifecycle position re-derives the affected collective figures and then re-derives `funding_posture` from them. Likewise, a released or reversed payment re-derives the athlete's `tax_year_gross_paid_cents`, `tax_year_withheld_cents` and `tax_form_required`, and a change to a contract's deliverable set re-derives `deliverable_value_total_cents` and re-scores the affected deliverables. Derived figures are always recomputed from the underlying rows rather than adjusted incrementally, so a compensating operation such as a refund or a reversal leaves exactly the figures a fresh computation would produce.

Wherever an operation is stated to bump a `version`, that bump is by exactly 1 and it applies to the row named in that statement. Operations that do not say so leave `version` alone — a billing run, for example, touches neither the collective's `version` nor any optimistic-concurrency field, which is why no version input is accepted there.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Founding a Collective and Policing Program Contact

A collective enters the system when a `PlatformAdmin` onboards it. The payload states the collective's `name`, the `institution_name` of the school it supports, the `institution_ipeds_code` that identifies that school — conventionally six characters, though any non-empty string is accepted and its length is never validated — the two-letter uppercase `home_state_code` whose disclosure law governs the collective (one of `AL`, `CA`, `FL`, `OH` or `TX`), and the `association_ruleset` edition in force — `NCAA_2024`, `NCAA_2026` or `NAIA`. The institution is modelled as attributes of the collective rather than as a separate resource. Two further inputs are optional: `entity_structure`, which defaults to `FOR_PROFIT_LLC` and may otherwise be `NONPROFIT_501C3` or `DONOR_ADVISED_CONDUIT`; and `communication_policy`, which defaults to `STRICT_SEPARATION` and may otherwise be `MONITORED_CONTACT`. The `institution_ipeds_code` must be unique across all collectives, compared as an exact case-sensitive string; a second collective claiming the same code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IPEDS_CODE`.

Upon creation the row is stamped with `version` 1, a `structure_effective_at` equal to the current logical clock reading, a `structure_change_count` of 0, and a `funding_posture` of `OPEN` — trivially so, because nothing is committed yet. The `charitable_deduction_enabled` flag is computed, never supplied: it is true if and only if `entity_structure` is `NONPROFIT_501C3`, and any value a client submits for it is ignored. That single boolean is what decides whether donors see any deductible amount at all, so the tax-structure decision made at onboarding reaches all the way into every contribution receipt. The four ledger figures — `settled_net_funds_cents`, `committed_unreleased_cents`, `released_total_cents` and `reversed_total_cents` — all begin at zero and are thereafter derived rather than written. The collective listing may be narrowed by `funding_posture` and by `entity_structure`; addressing a collective that does not exist yields `ERR_COLLECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`.

Alongside fundraising, the collective must be able to prove where the line between the collective and the athletics program was drawn. Every coach or program touchpoint is recorded as an immutable communication record, created by a `CollectiveDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer`. The payload names the `collective_id`, the `actor_role` of whoever spoke (`HEAD_COACH`, `PROGRAM_STAFF`, `COLLECTIVE_DIRECTOR` or `COMPLIANCE_OFFICER`), the `channel` used (`DIRECT_MESSAGE`, `TEAM_MEETING`, `PUBLIC_POST` or `INSTITUTION_PORTAL`), and the `topic` discussed (`DEAL_TERMS`, `DEAL_SOLICITATION`, `GENERAL_PROGRAM` or `COMPLIANCE_NOTICE`). An `athlete_id` may optionally identify the athlete the touchpoint concerned, and a free-text `note` may optionally accompany it; either one omitted is stored and reported as null. The `actor_role` recorded on the touchpoint is entirely independent of the caller's own role: it is the recorded subject of the conversation, not the identity of whoever is filing the record. Each record is stamped with `logged_at` from the logical clock, receives the next communication-log `sequence_no`, and is thereafter immutable — no endpoint modifies it, which is why it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

At write time the server decides both the `verdict` and the `rationale_code` explaining that verdict. Four clauses are evaluated **in this order, and the first one that matches decides both fields**:

1. If an `athlete_id` was supplied and that athlete's `eligibility_status` is anything other than `ENROLLED`, the `verdict` is `PROHIBITED` and the `rationale_code` is `ATHLETE_NOT_ENROLLED`.
2. Otherwise, if the `actor_role` is `HEAD_COACH` or `PROGRAM_STAFF` **and** the `topic` is `DEAL_TERMS` or `DEAL_SOLICITATION`, the collective's `communication_policy` decides among three sub-cases. Under `STRICT_SEPARATION` the `verdict` is `PROHIBITED` with `rationale_code` `COACH_DEAL_TERMS_BARRED`. Under `MONITORED_CONTACT` where the `channel` is `INSTITUTION_PORTAL`, the `verdict` is `PERMITTED_WITH_DISCLOSURE` with `rationale_code` `MONITORED_CHANNEL_REQUIRED`. Under `MONITORED_CONTACT` where the `channel` is anything other than `INSTITUTION_PORTAL`, the `verdict` is `PROHIBITED`, again with `rationale_code` `MONITORED_CHANNEL_REQUIRED`.
3. Otherwise, if the `actor_role` is `COLLECTIVE_DIRECTOR` and the `topic` is `DEAL_TERMS` and the `channel` is `PUBLIC_POST`, the `verdict` is `PERMITTED_WITH_DISCLOSURE` with `rationale_code` `MONITORED_CHANNEL_REQUIRED`.
4. Otherwise the `verdict` is `PERMITTED` with `rationale_code` `POLICY_ALLOWS`.

Because clause 1 outranks clause 2, a head coach discussing deal terms with an athlete who has entered the transfer portal is `PROHIBITED` on the eligibility ground even under `MONITORED_CONTACT` over the institution portal — the recorded rationale is `ATHLETE_NOT_ENROLLED`, not `MONITORED_CHANNEL_REQUIRED`. And a `PROHIBITED` verdict is still recorded, with HTTP 201: this ledger is an audit trail, not an authorization gate. Its purpose is to make an out-of-bounds conversation visible to the institution rather than to pretend it did not happen. Communication records may be listed with filters on `collective_id`, `athlete_id`, `actor_role` and `verdict`; a record addressed by an unknown identifier yields `ERR_COMMUNICATION_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, and a supplied `athlete_id` naming a nonexistent athlete yields `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_FOUND` while one naming an athlete of a different collective yields `ERR_CROSS_COLLECTIVE_REFERENCE`.

### 3.2. Restructuring the Tax Entity and Its Three-Level Cascade

Occasionally a collective converts — a for-profit vehicle reorganises as a charitable entity, or a donor-advised conduit is unwound. Only a `PlatformAdmin` may record that conversion, by supplying the new `entity_structure` together with `expected_collective_version`. Because a conversion rewrites tax treatment and disturbs the whole donor base, three cheap checks guard it before anything is written. The stale-version guard of §2.7 comes first among them in the ordering of §2.8: a mismatch between `expected_collective_version` and the collective's stored `version` yields `ERR_STALE_COLLECTIVE_VERSION` and no part of the cascade runs. Then the change budget: a collective may accept at most **two** entity-structure changes over its lifetime, so a third attempt — one made when `structure_change_count` already reads 2 — is refused with `ERR_STRUCTURE_CHANGE_LIMIT`. Finally the change must actually be a change: submitting the `entity_structure` the collective already holds is refused with `ERR_STRUCTURE_UNCHANGED`.

An accepted conversion writes the new `entity_structure`, re-derives `charitable_deduction_enabled` from it, stamps `structure_effective_at` with the current logical clock reading, increases `structure_change_count` by 1, and increases the collective's `version` by 1. It then runs a three-level cascade, each level completed in full before the next begins:

- **Level 1.** Every membership tier of the collective whose `tier_status` is `DRAFT` or `OPEN` becomes `CLOSED`, processed in ascending tier `sequence_no`. A tier already `CLOSED` is left alone.
- **Level 2.** Every commitment whose owning donor member's `membership_tier_id` is one of those tiers and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE` becomes `PAUSED`, with `pause_reason` set to `STRUCTURE_RESTRUCTURE`, processed in ascending commitment `sequence_no`. This is the one path by which a commitment is paused for a reason other than the donor asking.
- **Level 3.** Every donor member that is left holding at least one commitment, all of whose commitments are now in `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED` or `FULFILLED`, becomes `LAPSED`, processed in ascending donor member `sequence_no`. A donor with no commitments at all is not lapsed by this rule.

One thing a restructure explicitly does **not** do is rewrite history. Deductible amounts already stored on settled contributions are left exactly as they are, even though the collective's deductibility flag may have just flipped; only the year-end tax recompute described in §3.6 rewrites those figures.

### 3.3. The Dues Ladder and the Donor Roll

A `CollectiveDirector` builds the membership offering as a ladder of tiers under a named collective. Each new tier states its `tier_code`, a human-readable `tier_name`, the `annual_dues_cents` a member at that tier pays each year, and a `benefit_rank` between 1 and 5 where 5 is the richest bundle. The `tier_code` must be unique among that collective's tiers **including tiers that have already been closed**, so a retired code cannot be recycled; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`. The collective is taken from the addressed path and stored as the tier's `collective_id`; a new tier begins life in `tier_status` `DRAFT` and receives the next tier `sequence_no`.

Tiers move through their lifecycle by an explicit `action` supplied by a `CollectiveDirector` — either `OPEN` or `CLOSE`. Opening a tier advances it `DRAFT → OPEN` and requires two things: the tier must currently be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TIER_NOT_DRAFT`; and the ladder must stay monotonic. Monotonicity means the tier's `annual_dues_cents` must be strictly greater than the `annual_dues_cents` of every `OPEN` tier in the same collective whose `benefit_rank` is lower, and strictly less than that of every `OPEN` tier whose `benefit_rank` is higher. Two `OPEN` tiers sharing the same `benefit_rank` are never compared with each other, so their `annual_dues_cents` may stand in any relation, equal figures included. A tier that would price a richer bundle at or below a poorer one is refused with `ERR_TIER_RANK_DUES_INVERSION`. Closing a tier advances it `OPEN → CLOSED` and requires the tier to be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TIER_NOT_OPEN`. `CLOSED` is terminal — a closed tier is never reopened. Importantly, closing a tier by explicit action does **not** pause anyone's commitments; only the entity-structure restructure cascade of §3.2 does that, and the difference matters when a director is merely retiring an offering rather than reorganising the entity.

Each tier reports `active_commitment_count`, the number of commitments whose owning donor member sits on that tier and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`. Tier listings may be filtered by `tier_status`, and an unknown tier identifier yields `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_TIER_NOT_FOUND`.

Donors are enrolled onto that ladder by a `CollectiveDirector`. The payload names the `collective_id` and the `membership_tier_id` the donor is joining at, a `display_name`, and an `email`. Optionally it states `donor_kind` — `INDIVIDUAL` by default, or `ENTITY` for a corporate donor — and `annual_agi_cents`, the donor's stated adjusted gross income for the current tax year in cents, defaulting to 0 and never negative. The named tier must belong to the named collective, or the request is `ERR_CROSS_COLLECTIVE_REFERENCE`, and it must be in `tier_status` `OPEN` at the moment of enrolment, or the request is `ERR_TIER_NOT_OPEN`; a donor cannot be signed up at a draft or retired price point. The `email` must be unique within the collective, compared as an exact case-sensitive string and never checked for address syntax — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_HANDLE`. A new donor row starts at `version` 1 with `status` `PROSPECT` and the next donor `sequence_no`.

Donor lifecycle is driven entirely by consequence, never by an explicit transition endpoint. A donor in `PROSPECT` becomes `ACTIVE` the moment any contribution of theirs reaches `SETTLED`, and a donor in `LAPSED` returns to `ACTIVE` on the same event; both transitions also re-derive `lifetime_settled_cents`, the sum of `net_amount_cents` over that donor's `SETTLED` contributions, and `lifetime_deductible_cents`, the sum of `deductible_amount_cents` over the same rows; that re-derivation refreshes the donor's `updated_at` only when at least one of the two figures actually moves, and it never bumps the donor's `version`. In the other direction, any request that leaves a donor holding at least one commitment with **every** one of that donor's commitments in `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED` or `FULFILLED` moves the donor from `ACTIVE` to `LAPSED`.

A donor's deductibility inputs can be corrected after the fact: a `CollectiveDirector` may update `donor_kind` and `annual_agi_cents` — both are optional inputs, so either may be supplied on its own and a request that supplies neither is still accepted — which bumps the donor's `version` by 1. Correcting either figure does **not** retroactively rewrite the `deductible_amount_cents` already stored on that donor's settled contributions — only a year-end tax recompute does that. Donor listings may be filtered by `collective_id`, `membership_tier_id` and `status`, and an unknown donor identifier yields `ERR_DONOR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.4. Commitments: Subscriptions, Pledges and the Supersession Chain

A commitment is a donor's forward promise, and it comes in two shapes. A `CollectiveDirector` creates one by naming the `donor_member_id`, the `kind` — `RECURRING_SUBSCRIPTION` for dues charged by billing cycle, or `MAJOR_GIFT_PLEDGE` for a pledge charged by explicit installments — the per-installment `amount_cents`, the `billing_interval`, and optionally `installments_total`, which defaults to 1 and is at least 1. For a recurring subscription, `installments_total` is the number of cycles to run before the commitment is considered discharged. The `billing_interval` must be consistent with the `kind`: it must be `NONE` when the `kind` is `MAJOR_GIFT_PLEDGE`, and it must be `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL` — never `NONE` — when the `kind` is `RECURRING_SUBSCRIPTION`. An inconsistent pairing is refused with `ERR_BILLING_INTERVAL_MISMATCH`. Creation also requires `expected_donor_member_version` per §2.7; on success the donor member's `version` rises by exactly 1, so a client that creates two commitments in a row must re-read the donor between them. The new commitment starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `installments_charged` 0, `pause_reason` `NONE`, a null `superseded_by_commitment_id`, and the next commitment `sequence_no`.

Three explicit actions drive a commitment, each supplied as an `action` by a `CollectiveDirector`. `PAUSE` advances `ACTIVE → PAUSED` or `PAST_DUE → PAUSED` and requires the commitment to currently be `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`; it sets `pause_reason` to `DONOR_REQUEST`. `RESUME` advances `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, requires the commitment to currently be `PAUSED`, and additionally requires the owning donor's `membership_tier_id` to reference a tier whose `tier_status` is `OPEN` — resuming onto a retired price point is refused with `ERR_TIER_NOT_OPEN`. A successful resume clears `pause_reason` back to `NONE`. `CANCEL` advances `ACTIVE → CANCELLED`, `PAST_DUE → CANCELLED` or `PAUSED → CANCELLED`, and requires the commitment to currently be `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE` or `PAUSED`. Any action attempted from a state its rule does not permit is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Each of the three actions then re-evaluates the owning donor member for the `ACTIVE → LAPSED` bubble-up described in §3.3.

Two more commitment transitions happen as consequences of money rather than as commands. When a contribution attributed to a commitment fails, the commitment moves `ACTIVE → PAST_DUE`. When a contribution attributed to it settles, a commitment in `PAST_DUE` returns to `ACTIVE`, and `installments_charged` rises by 1 on every settling contribution attributed to the commitment — whichever state the commitment stood in beforehand, from `ACTIVE` exactly as from `PAST_DUE`. And once `installments_charged` reaches `installments_total` on a settling contribution, a commitment standing in `ACTIVE` at that point advances `ACTIVE → FULFILLED` and the owning donor is re-evaluated for the lapse bubble-up. A one-installment subscription is therefore discharged by its very first settled charge, and a later charge cycle skips it because a `FULFILLED` commitment is no longer chargeable. `FULFILLED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Restructuring is the one operation that produces the self-referential link. When a pledge or subscription is renegotiated, a `CollectiveDirector` supplies a new `amount_cents` and optionally a new `installments_total`; the commitment must currently be `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE` or `PAUSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A brand-new commitment row is created on the same `donor_member_id`, carrying the same `kind` and the same `billing_interval` as the original, in `status` `ACTIVE`, with `pause_reason` `NONE` however the original stood, with `installments_charged` reset to 0 and the next `sequence_no` — and it is that **new** row that the response returns. An `installments_total` omitted from the restructure payload is inherited from the original commitment rather than reset to 1, and the original's own `amount_cents` and `installments_total` are left exactly as they were. The original row's `superseded_by_commitment_id` is set to the new row's identifier and its own `status` becomes `CANCELLED`. A commitment may never supersede itself and a supersession chain may never contain a cycle, which the rule guarantees structurally: the new row's `superseded_by_commitment_id` is always null at creation. The donor is then re-evaluated for the lapse bubble-up, which the freshly `ACTIVE` replacement normally prevents. Commitment listings may be filtered by `donor_member_id`, by `superseded_by_commitment_id` — the way to walk a restructuring chain forward — and by `status` and `kind`; an unknown commitment identifier yields `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.5. Money In: Contributions, Processing Fees and Settlement Outcomes

Every dollar that arrives is a contribution row, recorded by either a `CollectiveDirector` or a `TreasuryOfficer`. The payload names the `donor_member_id`, the `gross_amount_cents` charged, and the `source_kind` — `SUBSCRIPTION_CHARGE` for a dues cycle, `MAJOR_GIFT_INSTALLMENT` for a pledge installment, or `ONE_TIME_GIFT` for an unattached gift. Three inputs are optional: `commitment_id`, naming the commitment the money is attributed to; `designation`, the donor's restriction on the use of the funds, which defaults to `UNRESTRICTED` and may otherwise be `ROSTER_POOL` or `CHARITY_PROGRAM`; and `processor_outcome`, a deterministic stand-in for the payment processor's answer, which defaults to `APPROVE` and may otherwise be `DECLINE` or `DEFER`.

The pairing between `source_kind` and `commitment_id` is strict. A `SUBSCRIPTION_CHARGE` or a `MAJOR_GIFT_INSTALLMENT` **must** carry a `commitment_id`; a `ONE_TIME_GIFT` **must not** carry one. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_KIND_COMMITMENT_MISMATCH`. When a `commitment_id` is present, the named commitment must belong to the named donor — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_DONOR_MISMATCH` — and it must be in `status` `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`, since a paused, cancelled or fulfilled promise cannot be charged; a commitment in any other state yields `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_CHARGEABLE`. Those two rules are evaluated whenever a `commitment_id` is present at all, and by the ladder of §2.8 they outrank the pairing rule, so a `ONE_TIME_GIFT` that names a cancelled commitment of its own donor reports `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_CHARGEABLE` rather than `ERR_SOURCE_KIND_COMMITMENT_MISMATCH`. The row is created with `received_at` stamped from the logical clock, an initial `status` of `PENDING`, and the next contribution `sequence_no`, which is also the primary attribution key described in §3.6. A row still standing in `PENDING` reports `processing_fee_cents`, `net_amount_cents` and `deductible_amount_cents` of 0 and a null `deduction_variant`. The submitted `processor_outcome` is itself persisted on the row and echoed on every read of it, and a later settlement decision records its own `outcome` without rewriting that stored value.

The `processor_outcome` is then resolved inside the very same request. `APPROVE` settles the contribution immediately, advancing it `PENDING → SETTLED`. `DECLINE` fails it, advancing it `PENDING → FAILED`. `DEFER` leaves it in `PENDING`, to be resolved later by a settlement decision. That later decision — available to a `CollectiveDirector` or a `TreasuryOfficer` — supplies an `outcome` of `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` and requires the contribution to still be `PENDING`; a contribution already settled, failed or refunded is refused with `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_STATE_INVALID`. That later decision does **not** re-check the attributed commitment's chargeability — a commitment that has since been paused, cancelled or fulfilled still takes the `installments_charged` increment of §3.4 on a settling contribution while its own `status` is left exactly where it stands. Whichever route it takes, an inline settlement and a deferred settlement have identical consequences.

Settlement is where the fee band is applied. Band selection is by **average** rate on the whole gross amount, not marginal: exactly one band applies to the entire `gross_amount_cents`, and both band boundaries are inclusive.

- If `gross_amount_cents` is at most 99,999 cents — that is, at most $999.99 — the rate is 290 basis points and the fixed component is 30 cents.
- Otherwise, if `gross_amount_cents` is at most 999,999 cents — at most $9,999.99 — the rate is 250 basis points and the fixed component is 30 cents.
- Otherwise the rate is 210 basis points and there is no fixed component at all.

The `processing_fee_cents` is then the `gross_amount_cents` multiplied by the band's basis-point figure, divided by 10,000, with that quotient rounded half up to a whole cent, and the band's fixed component added afterwards. The `net_amount_cents` is the `gross_amount_cents` less the `processing_fee_cents`. Because the boundaries are inclusive, a gross of exactly 99,999 cents is a first-band charge while 100,000 cents is a second-band charge, and a gross of exactly 999,999 cents is a second-band charge while 1,000,000 cents is a third-band charge. A contribution that fails carries a `processing_fee_cents` of 0 and a `net_amount_cents` of 0.

A settling contribution fans out in four directions. Its `processing_fee_cents`, `net_amount_cents` and `deductible_amount_cents` are computed and its `deduction_variant` is stamped `AT_RECEIPT`. That attribution is not confined to the settling row: the whole `AT_RECEIPT` pass of §3.6 is re-run across every settled current-tax-year contribution of the owning donor, so an earlier row's stored `deductible_amount_cents` may move and any such row previously stamped `YEAR_END_RECOMPUTE` is restamped `AT_RECEIPT`. The collective's `settled_net_funds_cents` is re-derived and `funding_posture` re-derived from it. The owning donor member bubbles up to `ACTIVE` and both lifetime figures are re-derived. And when a `commitment_id` is present, the commitment advances — back to `ACTIVE` from `PAST_DUE`, with its `installments_charged` incremented on this settlement whichever of those two states it was in, and onward to `FULFILLED` once that counter reaches `installments_total`. A **failing** contribution zeroes its fee, net and deductible figures — leaving its `deduction_variant` null, since no attribution pass ever reached it — and, when a `commitment_id` is present, moves the commitment `ACTIVE → PAST_DUE`, leaving a commitment already standing in `PAST_DUE` untouched.

Refunds are the compensating move and belong exclusively to the `TreasuryOfficer`. A refund requires the contribution to be `SETTLED`, advancing it `SETTLED → REFUNDED`; a refund attempted against a pending, failed or already-refunded row is `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_STATE_INVALID`. The refund re-derives the collective's `settled_net_funds_cents` from the underlying rows exactly as §2.10 defines it — which lowers the figure by that row's `net_amount_cents`, once and only once, because the row has just left the `SETTLED` set — and re-derives `funding_posture`, and it re-derives the donor's `lifetime_settled_cents` and `lifetime_deductible_cents`. A refunded row is excluded from deductibility entirely, which frees its share of the donor's ceiling for the remaining rows the next time attribution is computed — but no attribution pass runs inside the refunding request itself, so the surviving rows keep the figures they already carried and the refunded row keeps its own stored `processing_fee_cents`, `net_amount_cents`, `deductible_amount_cents` and `deduction_variant` unchanged; only its exclusion from the `SETTLED` set removes it from the donor and collective totals. A refund likewise leaves the attributed commitment alone: its `status`, `installments_charged` and `pause_reason` are untouched. A refund declares no body field whatsoever, and the request body is never parsed on this endpoint: whatever accompanies the call — an object, an array, a bare literal or nothing at all — is ignored rather than measured against the priority-5 shape pass, so no body can make this endpoint answer `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Contribution listings may be filtered by `donor_member_id`, `commitment_id`, `status`, `designation` and `deduction_variant`; an unknown contribution identifier yields `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.6. Deductibility Attribution, and the Two Kinds of Billing Run

Charitable deductibility is the most delicate figure the collective reports, because it is not a per-gift property — it is an allocation of a per-donor annual ceiling across that donor's gifts, and the answer depends on the order in which the gifts are walked. The rule is scoped to one donor member at a time and to the current tax year.

First the gate: if the owning collective's `charitable_deduction_enabled` is false, then `deductible_amount_cents` is 0 for every contribution of every donor of that collective and the remainder of the rule is skipped entirely. Concretely, an attribution pass on a non-deductible collective zeroes the `deductible_amount_cents` of exactly the rows it would otherwise have walked — that donor's `SETTLED` contributions whose `received_at` falls in the current tax year — refreshing the `updated_at` of only those rows whose stored figure was not already 0, and it leaves each row's `deduction_variant` exactly as it stood rather than stamping the pass's own variant onto it. Rows outside that input set are not rewritten at all, so a settled contribution belonging to an earlier tax year keeps whatever `deductible_amount_cents` it was assigned while the flag still stood true. A for-profit vehicle or a donor-advised conduit therefore reports no deduction at all, no matter how large the gift or how high the donor's income.

Otherwise, for one donor member: the applicable percentage is 60 when the donor's `donor_kind` is `INDIVIDUAL` and 100 when it is `ENTITY`. The donor's ceiling is the `annual_agi_cents` multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, with the quotient floored — truncated toward negative infinity — to a whole cent. A remaining-headroom figure starts at that ceiling. The input set is every contribution of that donor whose `status` is `SETTLED` and whose `received_at` falls in the current tax year; rows in `PENDING`, `FAILED` or `REFUNDED` are excluded outright. Those rows are then walked in the order the active variant prescribes, and for each row in turn the row's `deductible_amount_cents` is set to whichever is smaller — that row's `net_amount_cents`, or the remaining headroom — after which the remaining headroom is reduced by the amount just assigned. Rows reached after the headroom is exhausted therefore receive 0.

Two variants differ **only** in the walk order, and the variant used is recorded on each row as its `deduction_variant`:

- The `AT_RECEIPT` variant runs whenever a contribution settles, and it walks the donor's qualifying rows in ascending contribution `sequence_no` — the order in which this server recorded them.
- The `YEAR_END_RECOMPUTE` variant runs only during a year-end tax recompute, and it walks the same rows in ascending `received_at`, using ascending `sequence_no` as the tie-break — the order in which the money was actually received according to the logical clock.

Because the two orders differ whenever gifts were back-dated relative to the order they were keyed in, the variants always agree on the donor's **total** deductible amount — which is the smaller of the ceiling and the sum of the qualifying nets — but they disagree on the **attribution** across rows whenever the ceiling binds. That divergence is the entire point of the year-end pass: it restates each receipt against the true chronology before the donor's tax documents are produced.

Both kinds of bulk run are triggered by a `TreasuryOfficer` against a named collective, supplying a `run_kind` of either `SUBSCRIPTION_CHARGE_CYCLE` or `YEAR_END_TAX_RECOMPUTE`. A `SUBSCRIPTION_CHARGE_CYCLE` walks every `RECURRING_SUBSCRIPTION` commitment of that collective whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`, in ascending commitment `sequence_no`, and records one contribution per commitment with a `gross_amount_cents` equal to that commitment's per-installment `amount_cents`, a `source_kind` of `SUBSCRIPTION_CHARGE`, a `designation` of `UNRESTRICTED` and a `processor_outcome` of `APPROVE`, each settled inline with all the fan-out of §3.5; the collective ledger and `funding_posture` are re-derived once at the end of the run. The set of commitments to charge is resolved in full before the first charge is recorded, so a commitment that its own charge discharges to `FULFILLED` is still charged exactly once and no commitment created by the run itself is picked up by it. A `YEAR_END_TAX_RECOMPUTE` re-runs the attribution above under the `YEAR_END_RECOMPUTE` ordering for every settled contribution of every donor of the collective in the current tax year, stamping each touched row's `deduction_variant` as `YEAR_END_RECOMPUTE` — every row the pass walks ends up carrying that variant, whether or not its own `deductible_amount_cents` moved; it is the only producer of that variant. That stamping is nonetheless subject to the deductibility gate above, so on a collective whose `charitable_deduction_enabled` is false the pass only zeroes the amounts and every walked row keeps whatever `deduction_variant` it already carried, `AT_RECEIPT` included, while `recomputed_contribution_count` still counts those rows and `deduction_delta_cents` still reports the zeroing. Each donor the recompute walks also has `lifetime_settled_cents` and `lifetime_deductible_cents` re-derived once its rows have been restated, and a donor with no qualifying row at all is skipped entirely rather than walked to no effect. Neither run kind touches the collective's `version`, and neither accepts a stale-version field.

The run's response is a report rather than an entity: it echoes the `collective_id` it ran against and the `run_kind`, states the `executed_at` instant taken from the logical clock, and reports `charged_commitment_count`, `settled_contribution_count`, `failed_contribution_count`, `recomputed_contribution_count` and `deduction_delta_cents` — the signed sum of the per-row changes the recompute produced, which nets to zero across a donor even when individual rows move by large amounts. Every counter is always present, and the counters that do not apply to the run kind report 0: a charge cycle reports `recomputed_contribution_count` 0 and `deduction_delta_cents` 0, while a year-end recompute reports all three charge counters 0. Because each charge is recorded with an `APPROVE` outcome, `failed_contribution_count` is 0 on a charge cycle as well and `settled_contribution_count` equals `charged_commitment_count`. `recomputed_contribution_count` counts every settled current-tax-year contribution the pass walked, whether or not that row's stored figure actually moved.

### 3.7. The Athlete Roster: Eligibility, Portal Movement and Taxpayer Documentation

Athletes are added to a collective's roster by a `CollectiveDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer`. The payload names the `collective_id`, the athlete's `full_name`, the `portal_user_id` that the athlete will present as their login handle, the `sport` — one of `FOOTBALL`, `MENS_BASKETBALL`, `WOMENS_BASKETBALL`, `BASEBALL` or `SOFTBALL` — and the `class_year`, an academic year from 2026 through 2031 inclusive. Optionally the payload states `roster_weight`, an integer from 1 through 10 inclusive that defaults to 1 and is used by weighted roster allocation, and `tax_person_kind`, which defaults to `US_PERSON` and may otherwise be `NONRESIDENT_ALIEN`. The `portal_user_id` must be unique within the collective, compared as an exact case-sensitive string, since it is the handle an `Athlete` caller presents in `X-User-ID`; a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_HANDLE`. The same handle may be reused freely on a different collective. `tax_person_kind` is the only tax field a creation payload may set: `taxpayer_doc_on_file` and `treaty_exempt_claimed` are never accepted here and any submitted values for them are ignored. A new athlete begins in `eligibility_status` `ENROLLED` with `taxpayer_doc_on_file` `NONE`, `treaty_exempt_claimed` false, and the next athlete `sequence_no`, which serves as the final tie-break in allocation ordering.

Eligibility changes belong exclusively to the `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `change` of `ENTER_PORTAL`, `WITHDRAW_FROM_PORTAL`, `COMPLETE_TRANSFER`, `DECLARE_INELIGIBLE` or `REINSTATE`. Each is a declared transition out of a specific current state, and a change requested from any other state is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_STATE_INVALID`:

- `ENTER_PORTAL` moves `ENROLLED → PORTAL_ENTERED` and requires the athlete to be `ENROLLED`. Entering the portal does **not** terminate any contract: it only holds money. Concretely, a payment release created while the athlete is `PORTAL_ENTERED` stays in `QUEUED` instead of releasing.
- `WITHDRAW_FROM_PORTAL` moves `PORTAL_ENTERED → ENROLLED` and requires the athlete to be `PORTAL_ENTERED`. It then releases the held money: every payment release still in `QUEUED` on an `ACTIVE` contract of this athlete advances `QUEUED → RELEASED`, processed in ascending payment-release `sequence_no`, each with all the release-time consequences of §3.9.
- `COMPLETE_TRANSFER` moves `PORTAL_ENTERED → TRANSFERRED_OUT` and requires the athlete to be `PORTAL_ENTERED`. Every contract of the athlete whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` is terminated with `termination_reason` `TRANSFER_OUT`, processed in ascending contract `sequence_no`, and each of those terminations then runs its own deliverable-forfeit and release-void cascade and computes its own clawback. `TRANSFERRED_OUT` is terminal.
- `DECLARE_INELIGIBLE` moves `ENROLLED → INELIGIBLE` and requires the athlete to be `ENROLLED`. It terminates the same set of `DRAFT` and `ACTIVE` contracts in the same ascending contract `sequence_no` order, with `termination_reason` `INELIGIBILITY`, with the same downstream cascades.
- `REINSTATE` moves `INELIGIBLE → ENROLLED` and requires the athlete to be `INELIGIBLE`. Reinstatement restores the athlete's ability to contract but does **not** revive anything: contracts already terminated stay terminated, because termination is terminal.

Taxpayer documentation is recorded separately, by either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `TreasuryOfficer`, who supplies `taxpayer_doc_on_file` — `NONE`, `W9` or `W8BEN`, a required input on this operation — and optionally `treaty_exempt_claimed`, which defaults to false. Because the recording overwrites both fields outright rather than patching only what was named, omitting `treaty_exempt_claimed` stores false and thereby clears a claim that previously stood true. A `treaty_exempt_claimed` of true is only meaningful alongside a `W8BEN` and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when `taxpayer_doc_on_file` is anything else; the flag is only consulted for a `NONRESIDENT_ALIEN`. Recording documentation re-derives `tax_form_required` and bumps the athlete's `version` by 1.

Three derived figures describe an athlete's money. `annual_committed_value_cents` is the sum of `total_value_cents` over that athlete's contracts whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; completed, terminated and voided contracts are excluded, so terminating a contract frees cap headroom for a new one. `tax_year_gross_paid_cents` is the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over the athlete's payment releases with `status` `RELEASED` whose `released_at` falls in the current tax year, and `tax_year_withheld_cents` is the corresponding sum of `withholding_amount_cents`; queued, voided and reversed releases contribute nothing to either.

From the gross figure the information-reporting obligation follows. If `tax_year_gross_paid_cents` is zero, `tax_form_required` is `NONE`. Otherwise, if the athlete's `tax_person_kind` is `NONRESIDENT_ALIEN`, `tax_form_required` is `FORM_1042_S` — a nonresident alien requires that form at any nonzero amount, with no threshold at all. Otherwise, if `tax_year_gross_paid_cents` is at least 60,000 cents — six hundred dollars — `tax_form_required` is `FORM_1099_NEC`. In every remaining case it is `NONE`. The threshold comparison is inclusive, so a US person paid exactly six hundred dollars in the year requires the form while one paid a cent less does not, and reversing the release that carried them over the line drops them back to `NONE`. The figure is re-derived whenever a release for the athlete reaches `RELEASED` or `REVERSED` and whenever the athlete's tax profile changes. Athlete listings may be filtered by `collective_id`, `sport`, `eligibility_status` and `tax_form_required`; an unknown athlete identifier yields `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.8. Contracting: Individual Deals, Roster-Wide Allocation, Disclosure and Activation

A deal contract is the join between the collective's money and one athlete's name, image and likeness. Every contract carries **three independent state dimensions on the same row**: `lifecycle_status`, tracking the agreement itself; `payment_status`, tracking money paid against it; and `disclosure_status`, tracking the institution's adjudication of it. The three advance independently except where a rule explicitly reads across dimensions, and this section covers the first and third; §3.9 covers the second.

**Individually negotiated contracts.** A `CollectiveDirector` creates one by naming the `collective_id` and the `athlete_id`, the contract's face value as `total_value_cents`, and the effective window as `effective_start_at` (inclusive) and `effective_end_at` (exclusive, and strictly after the start). Optionally the payload states `funding_designation`, naming which designated pool of settled contributions the contract draws on — `UNRESTRICTED` by default, or `ROSTER_POOL` or `CHARITY_PROGRAM` — and `exclusivity_category`, the product exclusivity claimed, which defaults to `NONE` and may otherwise be `APPAREL`, `BEVERAGE` or `AUTOMOTIVE`. The `deal_kind` and `allocation_batch_code` are system-assigned and any submitted values are ignored: an individually negotiated contract is stamped `deal_kind` `INDIVIDUAL` with a null `allocation_batch_code`. The new row starts at `version` 1 in `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT` and `payment_status` `UNPAID`, with `termination_reason` `NONE` and the next contract `sequence_no`, which is the deterministic cascade order for contract-level fan-out.

Four business checks guard an individual creation, in the order given by §2.8. The athlete must belong to the named collective, or the request is `ERR_CROSS_COLLECTIVE_REFERENCE`. The collective's pre-request `funding_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`. The athlete's `eligibility_status` must be `ENROLLED`, or the request is `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_ENROLLED` — a collective may not sign an athlete who is in the portal, has transferred out, or has been declared ineligible. Then exclusivity: two contracts for the same athlete carrying the same non-`NONE` `exclusivity_category` may not have overlapping effective windows, where each window is the half-open interval running from `effective_start_at` up to but not including `effective_end_at`, and only contracts whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` are considered. Adjacency is explicitly permitted — one window's `effective_end_at` exactly equal to the other's `effective_start_at` is not an overlap — while any true overlap is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Finally the athlete cap: after the request, the athlete's `annual_committed_value_cents` must not be **strictly greater** than 5,000,000 cents, that is fifty thousand dollars. A post-creation total of exactly fifty thousand dollars is allowed; one cent more is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

A successful creation re-derives the athlete's `annual_committed_value_cents` and re-derives the collective's `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture` — note that a merely `DRAFT` contract already counts as committed, so drafting a large deal can move a collective's posture on its own. Contract listings may be filtered by `collective_id`, `athlete_id`, `allocation_batch_code`, `deal_kind`, `lifecycle_status`, `payment_status` and `disclosure_status`; an `allocation_batch_code` together with an `athlete_id` is the way to address one share of a roster batch without treating any identifier as a business selector. An unknown contract identifier yields `ERR_DEAL_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`.

**Roster-wide allocation.** A `CollectiveDirector` may instead mint one contract per eligible athlete in a single batch. The payload names the `collective_id`, the `allocation_batch_code` that every contract in the batch will share, the target `sport`, the `pool_amount_cents` to divide, the `allocation_mode` — either `EQUAL_SHARE` or `WEIGHTED_BY_ROSTER_WEIGHT` — and the shared `effective_start_at` and `effective_end_at`, optionally with a shared `funding_designation` and `exclusivity_category`. It also requires `expected_collective_version` per §2.7. The batch's input set is every athlete of that collective whose `sport` equals the target sport and whose `eligibility_status` is `ENROLLED`; athletes in `PORTAL_ENTERED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT` or `INELIGIBLE` are excluded from the batch entirely and receive no contract. If the input set resolves to no athlete at all, the batch is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ATHLETES`.

The division proceeds in four pinned steps. Each athlete in the input set is given a weight: exactly 1 under `EQUAL_SHARE`, or that athlete's `roster_weight` under `WEIGHTED_BY_ROSTER_WEIGHT`. Let the total weight be the sum of those weights over the input set. **Step one, the floor pass:** each athlete's base share is the `pool_amount_cents` multiplied by that athlete's weight, divided by the total weight, with the quotient floored to a whole cent. **Step two, the remainder:** the remainder is the `pool_amount_cents` less the sum of the base shares, and it is always at least zero and strictly less than the number of athletes in the input set. **Step three, distribution:** the athletes are ordered by descending weight, then by ascending athlete `sequence_no`, and exactly one additional cent is added to each of the first athletes in that order until the remainder is exhausted. An athlete identifier is never an ordering input here. **Step four:** one contract is minted per athlete, stamped `deal_kind` `ROSTER_SHARE`, carrying the shared `allocation_batch_code`, with `total_value_cents` set to that athlete's allocated amount; consecutive contract `sequence_no` values are assigned in the step-three distribution order. A submitted `total_value_cents` has no meaning in a batch and is ignored — the allocation assigns it.

Every allocated amount must be at least one cent. If any athlete's share would fall below that, the **whole batch** is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_SHARE_BELOW_MINIMUM` and no contract is minted; the batch is all-or-nothing and there is no partial success. The `pool_amount_cents` is itself not one of the strictly-positive money fields of §2.8, so a pool of zero or a negative pool is not an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at all — it is accepted by the shape pass, driven through the same division, and then caught here, because every share it produces falls below one cent. The same all-or-nothing rule governs every other check: the collective's pre-request `funding_posture` must be `OPEN`, so a `RESTRICTED` posture refuses the batch with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_RESTRICTED` and a `FROZEN` posture with `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`; the exclusivity-window rule is applied to every athlete in the batch and a single conflict yields `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` for the whole batch; and the fifty-thousand-dollar annual cap is applied to every athlete in the batch, a single failure yielding `ERR_ATHLETE_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

One further constraint applies only to batches, and it is deliberately evaluated **last** of all so that a batch which also breaks any cheaper rule reports that cheaper rule instead. It is an aggregate-capacity limit on the collective-and-sport pair, and its interval semantics differ on purpose from the exclusivity rule above: here the windows are treated as **closed** intervals, so two windows collide when the first's start is at or before the second's end **and** the second's start is at or before the first's end. Windows that merely touch at an endpoint therefore **do** collide here, whereas the same touching windows do not conflict under the half-open exclusivity rule. A new batch is refused with `ERR_BATCH_OVERLAP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when the number of **distinct** existing `allocation_batch_code` values on the same collective and the same sport whose windows collide with the new batch's window already stands at two or more, counting only contracts whose `lifecycle_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`.

A successful batch reports the shared `allocation_batch_code`, the `created_contract_count`, the `allocated_total_cents` — which always equals the `pool_amount_cents`, since the remainder is fully distributed — the `remainder_cents` reporting how many one-cent units step three had to hand out, and the `min_share_cents` and `max_share_cents` of the shares produced. It also echoes back the `collective_id` it ran against together with the `sport`, the `allocation_mode` and the `pool_amount_cents` it was given, and it carries a `deal_contracts` array holding every contract the batch minted, each rendered exactly as a single-contract read would render it and listed in the step-three distribution order. The whole report travels as a single `object` response rather than an `array` one, notwithstanding that embedded list, and it therefore carries no pagination fields in `meta`. It increments the collective's `version` by exactly 1 once for the entire batch, re-derives the collective's `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture`, and re-derives each affected athlete's `annual_committed_value_cents`.

**Disclosure.** Whether a contract needs to be disclosed is decided at creation from its own face value, which means a single roster batch can produce a mix of outcomes. A contract whose `total_value_cents` is at least 60,000 cents — six hundred dollars or more — starts in `disclosure_status` `DISCLOSURE_PENDING` with a `disclosure_due_at` equal to its `created_at` plus 168 hours, that is seven days on the logical clock. A contract below that face value starts in `DISCLOSURE_NOT_REQUIRED` with a null `disclosure_due_at`. Its `disclosure_submitted_at` is null until the first submission and `disclosure_return_count` starts at 0.

Filing the disclosure is available to a `CollectiveDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, who may optionally cite the governing authority via `state_law_citation` and `association_rule_citation`. Filing requires the contract's `lifecycle_status` to be `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL` for a contract already in a terminal lifecycle state — and requires `disclosure_status` to be `DISCLOSURE_PENDING` or `DISCLOSURE_RETURNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`. It always stamps `disclosure_submitted_at` with the current logical clock reading and always bumps the contract's `version` by 1, and the resulting disclosure state depends purely on timing: when the clock reads at or before `disclosure_due_at`, the dimension lands on `DISCLOSURE_PENDING` — which is where it stays from `DISCLOSURE_PENDING`, and where it returns to from `DISCLOSURE_RETURNED`; when the clock reads strictly after `disclosure_due_at`, the dimension moves to `DISCLOSURE_LATE` from either `DISCLOSURE_PENDING` or `DISCLOSURE_RETURNED`.

Adjudication belongs to the `InstitutionReviewer` alone, who records a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` and may attach a `reviewer_note`. Either decision requires the contract's `disclosure_submitted_at` to be set — adjudicating a disclosure nobody filed is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and requires `disclosure_status` to be `DISCLOSURE_PENDING` or `DISCLOSURE_LATE`, or the request is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`; it also requires the contract's `lifecycle_status` to still be `DRAFT`, and by the ladder of §2.8 that lifecycle condition is evaluated ahead of both disclosure conditions — a contract standing in `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT` and one already `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` or `VOIDED` with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`, before the filing or the disclosure position is looked at. Either decision bumps the contract's `version` by 1.

An `ACCEPT` advances `DISCLOSURE_PENDING → DISCLOSURE_ACCEPTED` or `DISCLOSURE_LATE → DISCLOSURE_ACCEPTED`. Accepting a late filing is still an acceptance: activation is unblocked exactly as it would be for a timely filing, and the fact that the filing was late is preserved in the relationship between `disclosure_submitted_at` and `disclosure_due_at` rather than in the state name. A `RETURN` advances `DISCLOSURE_PENDING → DISCLOSURE_RETURNED` or `DISCLOSURE_LATE → DISCLOSURE_RETURNED` and increments `disclosure_return_count` by 1. The **second** return is fatal: when the increment brings `disclosure_return_count` to 2, the lifecycle dimension crosses over and moves `DRAFT → VOIDED` with `disclosure_status` `DISCLOSURE_RETURNED` and `disclosure_return_count` 2, every deliverable of the contract in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` becomes `FORFEITED` in ascending deliverable `sequence_no`, and the collective's `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture` are re-derived because a voided contract is no longer committed. That crossover rides on the single `version` bump the `RETURN` itself already made and adds no second one; it leaves `termination_reason` at `NONE` and computes no clawback, since only a `TERMINATED` contract reports a `clawback_amount_cents` other than 0. `DISCLOSURE_ACCEPTED` and `DISCLOSURE_NOT_REQUIRED` are the terminal disclosure positions.

**Activation.** A `CollectiveDirector` activates a contract, advancing `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and bumping the contract's `version` by 1. Three preconditions hold. The contract's `lifecycle_status` must be `DRAFT`, or the request is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`. Its `disclosure_status` must be `DISCLOSURE_NOT_REQUIRED` or `DISCLOSURE_ACCEPTED` — a contract still pending, late or returned on disclosure is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_CLEARED`. And the athlete's `eligibility_status` must be `ENROLLED`, or the request is `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_ENROLLED`. Deliberately absent from that list is the funding posture: activation is never gated on it, because the contract's value was already counted as committed the moment it was drafted, so a collective whose posture has since tightened can still activate what it has already promised. Activation declares no body field whatsoever, and the request body is never parsed on this endpoint: whatever accompanies the call — an object, an array, a bare literal or nothing at all — is ignored rather than measured against the priority-5 shape pass, so no body can make this endpoint answer `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The other end of the lifecycle also runs by consequence. Any request that leaves a contract holding at least one deliverable, with **every** deliverable in a terminal status of `VERIFIED`, `WAIVED` or `FORFEITED`, and with `payment_status` `FULLY_PAID`, advances the lifecycle `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and re-derives the collective's `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture`, since a completed contract no longer counts as committed. `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` and `VOIDED` are the terminal lifecycle positions, and any operation attempted against a contract in one of them reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`.

### 3.9. Documented Services, Verified Payouts, Reversal and Clawback

**Promising the work.** A `CollectiveDirector` adds deliverables to a named contract, one per promised service. Each states its `deliverable_kind` — `APPEARANCE`, `SOCIAL_POST`, `CHARITY_WORK` or `AUTOGRAPH_SESSION` — the stand-alone `unit_value_cents` of that service, and the `due_at` instant by which the service must have been performed. Optionally it states `required_charity_hours_tenths`, the charity hours required expressed in integer tenths of an hour, so a value of 25 means two and a half hours; this figure must be strictly greater than zero when the `deliverable_kind` is `CHARITY_WORK` and exactly zero for every other kind. The `due_at` must fall inside the parent contract's effective window — at or after `effective_start_at` and strictly before `effective_end_at` — or the request is refused with `ERR_DUE_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`. Deliverables may be added while the contract is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; a contract in a terminal lifecycle position reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`.

A new deliverable takes its `deal_contract_id` from the addressed path and begins in `status` `PENDING` with an empty `evidence_records` list, a `submission_count` of 0, a `late_flag` of false, a `charity_hours_tenths` of 0, null `performed_at`, `submitted_at`, `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at`, and the next deliverable `sequence_no`, which is the deterministic cascade order for deliverable fan-out. Its `payout_basis` is never null and never waits for a review: the field is read straight off the parent contract's `deal_kind` on every serialization, so a brand-new `PENDING` deliverable already reports `STRICT_UNIT_BASIS` or `CONTRACT_SHARE_BASIS`, and a `FORFEITED` one goes on reporting its basis too — while `payout_eligible_cents` reports 0 for any status other than `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`. Neither figure is stored on the row; both are computed from the current rows at read time, which is why a change anywhere in the contract's deliverable set shows up on the next read without touching any deliverable's `updated_at`. Adding it re-derives the parent contract's `deliverable_value_total_cents` — the sum of `unit_value_cents` over that contract's deliverables whose `status` is anything other than `FORFEITED` — which in turn re-scores every already-verified or already-waived sibling that is scored on a contract-share basis. Deliverable listings under a contract may be filtered by `status` and `deliverable_kind`; an unknown deliverable identifier yields `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`.

**Documenting the work.** Documentation of services is recorded one item at a time, by the athlete themselves or by a `CollectiveDirector`, and it is append-only: nothing is ever removed. Each item states its `evidence_kind` — `ANALYTICS_SCREENSHOT`, `SIGNED_ATTENDANCE_SHEET`, `PHOTO_SET` or `ORGANISATION_LETTER` — and a `reference_label` identifying it. The item is appended to the deliverable's `evidence_records` list as an object carrying that `evidence_kind` and `reference_label` together with a `recorded_at` stamped from the logical clock and its own within-deliverable `sequence_no`, after which `evidence_count`, the length of the list, is re-derived. Evidence may only be added while the deliverable is in a non-terminal status; a deliverable already `VERIFIED`, `WAIVED` or `FORFEITED` reports `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. An athlete caller may only do this for their own deliverables, per §2.1.

**Submitting the work.** Submission is likewise open to the athlete or to a `CollectiveDirector`, and it supplies the `performed_at` instant the athlete asserts the service was performed, optionally the `charity_hours_tenths` actually documented, and optionally a free-text `submission_note`. Submission advances `PENDING → SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED`, and five preconditions guard it. The deliverable's `status` must be `PENDING` or `REJECTED`, or the request is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. The parent contract's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE` — a draft contract's deliverables cannot be submitted against — or the request is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The deliverable's `evidence_count` must be at least one documentation-of-services record, or the request is `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`. For a `CHARITY_WORK` deliverable, the submitted `charity_hours_tenths` must be greater than or equal to `required_charity_hours_tenths`, or the request is `ERR_CHARITY_HOURS_SHORTFALL`. And `submission_count` must be strictly below three, so a deliverable may be submitted at most three times counting the first: a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED` and the deliverable stays `REJECTED`.

A successful submission sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, increases `submission_count` by 1, records the supplied `performed_at`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, overwrites `charity_hours_tenths` from this submission, and sets `late_flag` to true exactly when the supplied `performed_at` is strictly after the deliverable's `due_at` and to false otherwise. A resubmission overwrites `performed_at`, `submitted_at`, `late_flag` and `charity_hours_tenths` from the newest submission, so a late first attempt followed by a timely resubmission clears the flag.

**Reviewing the work.** A `ComplianceOfficer` — and only a `ComplianceOfficer` — records a `decision` of `VERIFY`, `REJECT` or `WAIVE`, optionally with a `review_note`. `VERIFY` advances `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and requires the deliverable to currently be `SUBMITTED`. `REJECT` advances `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and likewise requires `SUBMITTED`; it sets `payout_eligible_cents` to 0 and sends the work back for another attempt within the three-submission budget. `WAIVE` advances `PENDING → WAIVED`, `SUBMITTED → WAIVED` or `REJECTED → WAIVED`, and requires the deliverable to be `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`. Any decision attempted from a state its rule does not permit is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. No condition on the parent contract guards a review at all — neither its `lifecycle_status` nor its disclosure position is consulted — so a `WAIVE` recorded against a `PENDING` deliverable of a merely `DRAFT` contract is accepted; what keeps a terminal contract's deliverables out of reach is not a lifecycle check but the forfeiture cascade, which has already parked them in a terminal status of their own. Both `VERIFY` and `WAIVE` record the reviewing officer's `X-User-ID` as `verified_by_user_id`, stamp `verified_at` from the logical clock, compute `payout_basis` and `payout_eligible_cents`, and re-evaluate the parent contract's lifecycle dimension for the `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` bubble-up. `VERIFIED`, `WAIVED` and `FORFEITED` are the terminal deliverable positions.

**What the work is worth.** A deliverable's `payout_eligible_cents` is 0 unless its `status` is `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`. Otherwise the figure is built in a pinned two-stage order, and the first stage depends on which basis the parent contract selects — recorded on the deliverable as `payout_basis`, and never chosen by a caller:

- When the parent contract's `deal_kind` is `INDIVIDUAL`, the `payout_basis` is `STRICT_UNIT_BASIS` and the base is simply the deliverable's own `unit_value_cents`. Each deliverable is scored from its own stated value and its siblings are irrelevant.
- When the parent contract's `deal_kind` is `ROSTER_SHARE`, the `payout_basis` is `CONTRACT_SHARE_BASIS` and the base is a proportional share of the contract's face value: the contract's `total_value_cents` multiplied by this deliverable's `unit_value_cents`, divided by the contract's `deliverable_value_total_cents`, with the quotient floored to a whole cent. If the contract's `deliverable_value_total_cents` is zero the base is 0. Because forfeited siblings are excluded from `deliverable_value_total_cents`, forfeiting one deliverable re-scores every surviving deliverable on a roster-share contract upward.

Then, and only then, the lateness penalty is applied to that base: if the deliverable's `late_flag` is true, the `payout_eligible_cents` is the base less the base multiplied by 15, divided by 100, with that quotient rounded half up to a whole cent; if the `late_flag` is false, the `payout_eligible_cents` is the base unchanged. The penalty is applied strictly **after** the share floor, never before, and the figure is re-derived whenever the parent contract's `deliverable_value_total_cents` changes.

**Releasing money.** Only a `TreasuryOfficer` may create a payment release. The payload names the `deal_contract_id`, the `release_kind` — `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT` for a payout against documented services, or `CONTRACT_ADVANCE` for an advance against the contract's face value — the gross `requested_amount_cents` to release, and `expected_deal_contract_version` per §2.7. A `deliverable_id` is required when the `release_kind` is `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT` and must be absent when it is `CONTRACT_ADVANCE` — that pairing belongs to the priority-5 shape pass, so violating it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` reported before either the contract or the deliverable has been resolved — and when present it must name a deliverable belonging to the named contract, or the request is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`.

Nine substantive checks then apply, in the order §2.8 fixes. The contract's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The stale-version guard runs next. Then segregation of duties: for a `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the named deliverable's `verified_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then the posture gate: the collective's pre-request `funding_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is `ERR_COLLECTIVE_FUNDING_FROZEN`. Then, for a `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT`, the named deliverable's `status` must be `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`, or the request is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_VERIFIED`. Then the contract ceiling: the `requested_amount_cents` plus the contract's `released_total_cents` — the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over that contract's releases with `status` `RELEASED` — must not exceed the contract's `total_value_cents`, or the request is `ERR_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_CONTRACT_VALUE`. Then the designation match: a release may only draw on contributions carrying the same `designation` as the contract's `funding_designation`, so a contract whose designation has no matching settled contributions at all cannot be paid and is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_DESIGNATION_MISMATCH`. Then the designated-pool ceiling: the `requested_amount_cents` must not exceed the collective's unreleased settled net funds carrying that designation, computed as the sum of `net_amount_cents` over settled contributions with that designation less the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over released payments on contracts carrying that same designation; a shortfall is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DESIGNATED_FUNDS`. Then the nonresident-alien documentation rule: paying an athlete whose `tax_person_kind` is `NONRESIDENT_ALIEN` requires `taxpayer_doc_on_file` to be `W8BEN`, or the request is `ERR_TAXPAYER_DOC_REQUIRED`. And finally, for a `DELIVERABLE_PAYOUT`, the `requested_amount_cents` must not exceed the named deliverable's `payout_eligible_cents`, or the request is `ERR_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_PAYOUT_ELIGIBLE`.

**Withholding.** Before the row is queued or released, the withholding regime is decided from the athlete on the parent contract, and recorded as `withholding_basis`. If the athlete's `tax_person_kind` is `NONRESIDENT_ALIEN`, then when `treaty_exempt_claimed` is true **and** the `requested_amount_cents` is at most 300,000 cents — three thousand dollars — the `withholding_basis` is `NONE` and `withholding_amount_cents` is 0; in every other nonresident case the `withholding_basis` is `NRA_STATUTORY` and `withholding_amount_cents` is the `requested_amount_cents` multiplied by 30, divided by 100, with the quotient rounded half up to a whole cent. If instead the athlete is a `US_PERSON`, then when `taxpayer_doc_on_file` is anything other than `W9` the `withholding_basis` is `BACKUP` and `withholding_amount_cents` is the `requested_amount_cents` multiplied by 24, divided by 100, rounded half up; when a `W9` is on file the `withholding_basis` is `NONE` and `withholding_amount_cents` is 0. In every case `net_payable_cents` is the `requested_amount_cents` less the `withholding_amount_cents`. The treaty ceiling comparison is inclusive: a release of exactly three thousand dollars to a treaty-claiming nonresident is fully exempt, while one cent more is withheld on the **whole** amount rather than on the excess.

**Queued or released.** Every release row is created in `status` `QUEUED` with `initiated_by_user_id` set to the creating officer's `X-User-ID`, a null `released_at`, `reversal_reason` `NONE`, and the next release `sequence_no`; creating it bumps the parent contract's `version` by 1. Whether it stays queued depends on the athlete: when the athlete's `eligibility_status` is `ENROLLED`, the row advances `QUEUED → RELEASED` in the very same request with `released_at` stamped from the logical clock; when the athlete is `PORTAL_ENTERED`, the row remains `QUEUED` until the athlete withdraws from the portal, at which point the withdraw cascade of §3.7 advances it. Releasing a payment always requires the parent contract to be `ACTIVE` and the athlete to be `ENROLLED`, whether the advance happens inline or through that cascade.

The moment a release reaches `RELEASED`, four things follow. The contract's `payment_status` advances: from `UNPAID` to `PARTIALLY_PAID` when the contract's `released_total_cents` now stands strictly between zero and its `total_value_cents`; from `UNPAID` straight to `FULLY_PAID`, or from `PARTIALLY_PAID` to `FULLY_PAID`, when that total now equals `total_value_cents`. Reaching `FULLY_PAID` re-evaluates the lifecycle dimension for the `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` bubble-up, while reaching `PARTIALLY_PAID` re-derives the collective's `released_total_cents` and `committed_unreleased_cents`. Second, the collective's `released_total_cents`, `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture` are re-derived. Third, the athlete's `tax_year_gross_paid_cents`, `tax_year_withheld_cents` and `tax_form_required` are re-derived. Release listings may be filtered by `deal_contract_id`, `deliverable_id`, `status`, `release_kind` and `withholding_basis`; an unknown release identifier yields `ERR_PAYMENT_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`.

**Reversal.** A `TreasuryOfficer` may reverse a released payment in full, supplying a `reversal_reason` of `ERRONEOUS_PAYMENT` or `CLAWBACK`; partial reversals do not exist. The release's `status` must be `RELEASED` — a queued, voided or already-reversed row is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_RELEASED` — and the calendar year of its `released_at` must equal the current tax year, or the reversal is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_ACROSS_TAX_YEARS`, so that the athlete's tax ledger for a closed year is never rewritten. A reversal advances `RELEASED → REVERSED`, records the `reversal_reason`, and bumps the parent contract's `version` by 1. The contract's `payment_status` steps back to whatever the surviving released total now implies: to `PARTIALLY_PAID` when the reversal leaves `released_total_cents` strictly between zero and `total_value_cents`, and to `UNPAID` when it leaves that total at zero — including straight from `FULLY_PAID` to `UNPAID` when the reversed row was the contract's only released payment. A dimension already standing at `CLAWBACK_DUE` is left exactly there, that position being terminal. Nothing about the contract's `lifecycle_status` gates a reversal, so a release on a contract that has since reached `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` may still be reversed and its payment dimension still steps back while its lifecycle dimension stays put; a reversal never re-evaluates the `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` bubble-up and never recomputes a clawback. The collective's `released_total_cents`, `reversed_total_cents`, `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture` are all re-derived, and so are the athlete's `tax_year_gross_paid_cents`, `tax_year_withheld_cents` and `tax_form_required` — a reversed release contributes nothing to either figure, which is precisely how a US person can drop back below the six-hundred-dollar reporting threshold. `REVERSED` and `VOIDED` are terminal release positions, and a `VOIDED` release moves no money at all: neither the collective ledger nor the athlete's tax ledger is affected by one.

**Termination and clawback.** A `CollectiveDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may terminate a contract directly, supplying a `termination_reason` of `TRANSFER_OUT`, `INELIGIBILITY`, `BREACH` or `MUTUAL_RELEASE`; the athlete-eligibility cascade of §3.7 reaches the same transition with a cascade-derived reason. Termination requires the contract's `lifecycle_status` to be `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, advancing either `DRAFT → TERMINATED` or `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`; a contract already in a terminal position reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMINAL`. The transition records the supplied or cascade-derived `termination_reason`, bumps the contract's `version` by 1, then runs its cascades in a fixed order: every deliverable of the contract whose `status` is `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` becomes `FORFEITED` with `payout_eligible_cents` set to 0 in ascending deliverable `sequence_no`, and the contract's `deliverable_value_total_cents` is re-derived with those forfeited rows excluded; then every payment release of the contract still in `QUEUED` becomes `VOIDED` in ascending release `sequence_no`. Finally the collective's `committed_unreleased_cents` and `funding_posture` are re-derived and the athlete's `annual_committed_value_cents` is re-derived, freeing cap headroom.

The clawback figure is computed as part of that transition, and crucially it is taken **after** the deliverable forfeiture cascade has run, so work that was never verified never counts as earned. If the `termination_reason` is `MUTUAL_RELEASE`, the `clawback_amount_cents` is 0 outright — a negotiated parting demands nothing back regardless of the ruleset. Otherwise the earned figure is the sum of `payout_eligible_cents` over the contract's deliverables whose `status` is `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`, and the unearned figure is the contract's `released_total_cents` less that earned figure. A cap is then applied, and which cap depends on the collective's `association_ruleset`: 100 percent of `released_total_cents` under `NCAA_2024`, 50 percent under `NCAA_2026`, and 75 percent under `NAIA`. The cap itself is the `released_total_cents` multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, with the quotient floored to a whole cent. The `clawback_amount_cents` is then the smaller of the unearned figure and the cap, and never below zero. While a contract's `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `TERMINATED` its `clawback_amount_cents` is 0 and its `termination_reason` is `NONE`. When the computed `clawback_amount_cents` is strictly greater than zero, the payment dimension moves to `CLAWBACK_DUE` — from `UNPAID`, from `PARTIALLY_PAID` or from `FULLY_PAID` alike — and `CLAWBACK_DUE` is terminal on that dimension. Recovering the money then happens through a reversal carrying `reversal_reason` `CLAWBACK`.
