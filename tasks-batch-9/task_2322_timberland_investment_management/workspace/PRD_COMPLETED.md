# Timberland Investment Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Institutional timberland is one of the few asset classes where the accounting unit is a biological one. A closed-end timberland fund does not simply hold land: it holds standing merchantable tonnage that grows every year, and that same tonnage is claimed simultaneously by the harvest schedule, by carbon permanence commitments, and by land parcels being sold out from under it for higher-and-better use. The organization that manages this portfolio must be able to answer, at any instant, how many tons on a given stand are genuinely free to allocate — and it must refuse allocations that would double-spend the same wood.

This document specifies the Timberland Investment Management API, the system of record for that discipline. It governs acquisition underwriting against timber cruise inventory and growth models, harvest scheduling reconciled against biological growth, price expectations and the investor cash-yield mandate, sustainable-forestry certification and audit corrective-action cycles, higher-and-better-use land disposition, hunting and recreational lease revenue, carbon offset project development and inventory dedication, wildfire, insect and weather risk assessment with insurance and salvage planning, and investor reporting backed by independent appraisal.

Two design pressures run through every requirement below. The first is conservation: tonnage moved into a reservation, a carbon dedication or a sold parcel leaves the allocatable pool exactly once, and when a wildfire destroys wood the system must claw the shortfall back from the claimants in a fixed, auditable order rather than letting the books drift. The second is segregation of duties: the analyst who underwrites a tract may not approve it, the appraiser who submits a valuation may not countersign it, and the forester who writes a corrective action may not verify its closure. All API paths described here are mounted under `/timberland`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. The system recognizes exactly seven roles: `FundAdmin`, `AcquisitionAnalyst`, `InvestmentCommittee`, `ForestManager`, `CertificationAuditor`, `CarbonDeveloper`, and `IndependentAppraiser`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven exact strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not authorized for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Authorization is evaluated at action granularity where an endpoint multiplexes several operations behind an `action` discriminator. The corrective-action cycle on an audit finding is the sharpest case: submitting a corrective action is permitted only to `ForestManager`, while rejecting a corrective action and verifying its closure are permitted only to `CertificationAuditor`. A `CertificationAuditor` attempting to submit a corrective action, or a `ForestManager` attempting to verify one, is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even though both roles are listed on that endpoint. Because authorization outranks path existence in §2.9, the `action` discriminator is read straight out of the body before the path identifier is resolved: a `CertificationAuditor` submitting a corrective action is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even when the finding identifier in the path names nothing, and an `action` value outside the declared set carries no action-level restriction of its own and falls through to the later shape check.

None of the seven roles is backed by a persisted entity. For each of `FundAdmin`, `AcquisitionAnalyst`, `InvestmentCommittee`, `ForestManager`, `CertificationAuditor`, `CarbonDeveloper`, and `IndependentAppraiser`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any stored record. The server records that identifier wherever a scaffolded transition captures custody — as `authored_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an underwriting case, as `appraisal_submitted_by_user_id` and `appraisal_signed_by_user_id` on a property, as `measured_by_user_id` on a cruise plot, and as `car_submitted_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on an audit finding — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. Ownership is never derived from the caller; the only comparisons the system makes on `X-User-ID` are the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.10, which compare the caller against a previously recorded custody field.

Read access is broad: all seven roles may call every read endpoint, with one exception. The investor reporting pack is restricted to `FundAdmin`, `InvestmentCommittee`, and `IndependentAppraiser`; the other four roles receive `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` on it. All seven roles may set the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty random uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and it is the one timestamp in the system read from the host wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Requests to an unroutable path, or to a routed path with an unsupported method, answer in this same envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and the transport status the router assigned (404 or 405) rather than 400.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted the server uses 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0, its minimum being 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or that falls outside its declared range, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, and because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, records created without an intervening clock move share a timestamp and are then ordered by the sequence in which the server created them. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full result set before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Each persisted resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a stand identifier and a harvest block identifier are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must not attempt to infer type from the string. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits and read from the logical clock of §2.5, never from the host wall clock.

Funds, properties, stands, underwriting cases, harvest blocks, carbon projects, carbon dedications, HBU parcels, recreational leases, certification audits, audit findings and hazard events additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation and likewise read from the logical clock, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that resource — including mutations that only touch derived fields. The unit of mutation is the request: a single request advances a given resource's `version` by exactly one and refreshes its `updated_at` once, however many distinct effects inside that request happen to touch it. Cruise plots are the single exception: a cruise plot is immutable once recorded, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all.

Reconciliation counts as mutation. When a hazard event cuts a harvest block's `planned_tons`, when an audit finding closure recomputes a property's `certification_posture`, or when a completed harvest refreshes a fund's `cash_yield_bps`, each affected resource has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented independently, even though the client's request named only one of them. Recomputing a purely derived figure while serving a read is not a mutation and leaves both fields alone — a stand's four claim buckets and `available_tons`, an audit's three finding counts, a project's `dedicated_tons_total`, and a fund's `nav_usd`, `cash_yield_bps`, `harvest_acceleration_required` and two exposure counters are all rebuilt on demand without bumping anything. The two lazy transitions of §2.5 are different: they overwrite a stored status, so they do refresh `updated_at` and increment `version` on the resource they move, even though the client only issued a read.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

Every date- and time-dependent rule in this specification reads a server-held **logical clock**, never the host machine's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. An administrative test-driving capability, available to all seven roles, sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; `current_utc` is required and may be any ISO 8601 instant — a supplied offset is normalised to UTC and a missing offset is read as UTC — the clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards, and the call answers 200 with an empty `data` object rather than any resource. The rules that consult it are the harvest re-entry buffer, lease proration and expiry, carbon permanence locks, appraisal staleness, the years-to-start projection horizon, and the trailing claim window used for experience-rated insurance.

Interval semantics differ by resource and are deliberately not uniform. A harvest block's operating window, spanning `planned_start_date` to `planned_end_date`, is **closed at both ends** — both endpoint days belong to the window. A recreational lease term, spanning `term_start_date` to `term_end_date`, is **half-open**: the start date belongs to the term and the end date does not. A hunting-season blackout, spanning `blackout_start_date` to `blackout_end_date`, is likewise half-open. Two lease terms whose endpoints merely touch — one ending on the very day another begins — do not intersect. A year is 365 days everywhere a year-length constant is needed.

Advancing the clock does not itself sweep the database. Two transitions are applied lazily instead, evaluated when the affected resource is next read: a signed appraisal becomes stale, and an active lease expires. Both are described in the workflows that own them.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Monetary amounts are held and returned at two decimal places. Tonnage and acreage are held at two decimal places, with one deliberate exception: per-plot net tons per acre and the stand-level mean of those per-plot figures are held at **three** decimal places, and the per-plot rounding happens before the mean is taken, not after. Basis-point yields are integers, rounded to zero decimal places. An effective stumpage price is rounded to four decimal places before it multiplies tonnage, and an insurance loss ratio is rounded to four decimal places before it loads a premium. Discount factors are never rounded. Counts of properties, plots, findings and revision rounds are integers and are not rounded at all.

Where a formula names an intermediate step, that step's rounding is part of the contract. Computing an unrounded chain and rounding only at the end produces different answers from the ones this specification requires, and clients should expect the stored figures to reflect the stepwise discipline.

### 2.7. The Stand Inventory Partition

A stand's merchantable wood is tracked as one pool and four claims against it. `net_merch_tons` is the standing merchantable inventory. Against it sit `reserved_tons` (the sum of `planned_tons` over the stand's harvest blocks in `RESERVED`, rounded half-up to two decimals), `committed_tons` (the same sum over blocks in `COMMITTED`), `dedicated_tons` (the sum, over the stand's carbon dedications in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `REDUCED`, of each dedication's `dedicated_tons` less its `reduced_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals), and `hbu_withdrawn_tons` (the sum of `withdrawn_tons` over the stand's HBU parcels in `CLOSED`, rounded half-up to two decimals).

`available_tons` is the residue: `net_merch_tons` less `reserved_tons`, less `committed_tons`, less `dedicated_tons`, less `hbu_withdrawn_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals. This identity is the conserved quantity of the whole system. Harvest reservations, carbon dedications and HBU withdrawals are independent claimants against the same wood — none of them owns the stand — and every allocation, release, cut and claw-back described in §3 is expressed as a movement between these five figures. A stand also records `plot_count`, `mean_net_tons_per_acre`, `cruise_confidence` and `gross_merch_tons` from its cruise, and may reference the stand it was carved out of through `split_from_stand_id`. The four claim buckets and `available_tons` are not carried as independent counters: they are rebuilt from the claiming rows themselves — the stand's harvest blocks, carbon dedications and HBU parcels — every time the stand is serialized, so no bucket can drift from the rows that claim the wood, and a movement between buckets needs no separate write.

Moving tons between the reserved and committed buckets does not change `available_tons`. Only cruise closure, hazard damage, harvest completion, new reservations, new dedications, releases, cancellations and HBU closings change it.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Inventory and Capital Writes

Four write operations require the client to declare the version of a *parent* resource it last observed. These are not optional fields and they are not offered anywhere else: an endpoint not named below accepts no version field at all, and the server performs no concurrency check on it.

Reserving harvest tonnage requires `expected_stand_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target stand. If it does not equal the stand's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STAND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A reservation is legitimate on its own terms, so nothing in the block's state preconditions would stop a writer whose view of the stand predates an intervening reservation, dedication, hazard loss or HBU withdrawal from quietly over-allocating tons already spoken for. The stale version is what the system detects.

Creating a carbon dedication requires the same field, `expected_stand_version`, against the same stand `version`, and a mismatch is likewise rejected with `ERR_STALE_STAND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Dedications and reservations draw on the same allocatable pool, so a dedication written against a stale `available_tons` would satisfy its own check and break the partition identity.

Opening an underwriting case requires `expected_property_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the property being underwritten. A mismatch with the property's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new case legitimately supersedes prior cases on approval, so no case-level state check would stop a writer whose cruise, appraisal, risk-review or market-access view of the property is out of date from underwriting a value that no longer holds.

Closing an acquisition requires `expected_fund_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning fund. A mismatch with the fund's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every approved case is individually fundable, so a writer whose view of `cash_balance_usd` predates another acquisition, a premium debit, a lease refund or a buffer cure would commit capital the fund no longer has under a check its own stale numbers satisfy.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and Validation Precedence

When a URL contains a resource identifier, the server resolves it before it looks at anything in the body, and when a path nests identifiers it resolves them left to right, failing on the first one that does not exist. Each resource type has its own 404: `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNDERWRITING_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HARVEST_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARBON_DEDICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HBU_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECREATIONAL_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_HAZARD_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys inside a request body are resolved the same way — left to right, in the order the fields are declared for the entity being created — and the response carries the matching not-found code for the *first* reference that fails to resolve. Creating a carbon dedication therefore reports a bad `carbon_project_id` as `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` even when `stand_id` is also unresolvable, because the project reference is declared first. Body foreign-key resolution applies to the creation of properties, stands, cruise plots, underwriting cases, harvest blocks, carbon projects, carbon dedications, HBU parcels, recreational leases, certification audits and hazard events, and to the acquisition closing (which cites `underwriting_case_id` in its body).

The complete evaluation order for any request is fixed, and the first failure encountered is the one reported:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's role list and, on endpoints with an `action` discriminator, is permitted for the requested action, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** The resource named by the path identifier exists, else its type-specific not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
4. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key in the body resolves, left to right, else the matching not-found code for the first unresolved reference (404).
5. **Request shape.** All required input fields present; enum values inside their declared sets; numerics inside their declared ranges and decimal precision; dates well formed. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Computed and system-managed fields that appear in a request body are silently ignored rather than rejected. Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Stand version guard.** `expected_stand_version` equals the stand's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_STAND_VERSION` (412). Applies to harvest block creation and carbon dedication creation.
7. **Property and fund version guards.** `expected_property_version` equals the property's `version` (`ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION`, 412) on underwriting case creation; `expected_fund_version` equals the fund's `version` (`ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION`, 412) on the acquisition closing.
8. **Segregation of duties**, as specified in §2.10: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to the committee decision, the appraisal actions, and the audit-finding transitions.
9. **State legality.** The requested transition is declared for the entity's current state, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint. At the same rank sit the domain state gates: `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`, `ERR_CRUISE_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_UNDERWRITING_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_OWNED`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_SIGNED`, `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_DEDICATION`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_CARBON_PERMANENCE_LOCK`, and `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_BLOCK_CLOSURE` (all 409).
10. **Business-safety gates.** Certification suspension (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`, 409) and the cash-yield cancellation lock (`ERR_YIELD_SHORTFALL_LOCK`, 409).
11. **Temporal shape.** Window and term validity: `ERR_HARVEST_WINDOW_INVALID` and `ERR_LEASE_TERM_INVALID` (both 422).
12. **Temporal conflict.** The harvest re-entry buffer (`ERR_REENTRY_BUFFER_CONFLICT`, 409) and the lease blackout collision (`ERR_LEASE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`, 409).
13. **Structural constraints.** Plot-number uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PLOT_NUMBER`, 409), the requirement of at least one cruise plot (`ERR_NO_CRUISE_PLOTS`, 422), appraisal input completeness (`ERR_APPRAISAL_INPUTS_INCOMPLETE`, 422), and the retained-acreage floor (`ERR_HBU_ACRES_EXCEED_STAND`, 422).
14. **Referential agreement.** `ERR_STAND_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` and `ERR_CASE_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` (both 422).
15. **Fiscal checks, last.** `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_TONS`, `ERR_ACTUAL_TONS_EXCEED_PLANNED`, `ERR_LEASE_ACREAGE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_FUND_CASH_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_UNDERWRITTEN_VALUE`, `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_INDEMNITY`, `ERR_SALVAGE_EXCEEDS_RECOVERABLE`, `ERR_CARBON_NO_DEDICATIONS`, and `ERR_BUFFER_CURE_INSUFFICIENT` (all 422).

The ordering matters in practice. A harvest reservation submitted with a stale stand version *and* insufficient available tons reports the stale version, because the version guard sits above the fiscal check — the client's numbers cannot be trusted until its view of the stand is confirmed current. A committee decision issued by the case's own author on a case that has exhausted its revision rounds reports the segregation-of-duties violation, because duty separation is evaluated before state legality.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of duties may never be performed by the same `X-User-ID`, and all three failures share one code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

First, the committee member deciding an underwriting case must not be the analyst who authored it: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `authored_by_user_id`. Second, the appraiser countersigning a valuation must be neither the appraiser who submitted it nor an author of underwriting work on the same tract: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `appraisal_submitted_by_user_id`, and must not equal the `authored_by_user_id` of *any* underwriting case on that property, whatever that case's state. Third, the auditor verifying and closing a nonconformance must not be the person who submitted the corrective action: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `car_submitted_by_user_id`.

### 2.11. Fund Cash, Operating Cash and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A fund's `cash_balance_usd` is its running treasury. It is seeded at inception from `opening_cash_usd` and is thereafter credited by completed-harvest net revenue, HBU closing net proceeds, activation rent on recreational leases, and hazard claim settlements; it is debited by property acquisition prices, lease termination refunds, insurance premiums, and carbon buffer cure costs. Before any of those debits is posted, the server confirms that `cash_balance_usd` is greater than or equal to the amount about to be withdrawn; if it is not, the request fails with `ERR_FUND_CASH_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). That guard applies to exactly four operations: the acquisition closing, a carbon buffer cure, a property risk review (which posts the premium), and a lease termination (which posts the refund).

`net_operating_cash_usd` is a second, narrower accumulator: inception-to-date operating cash only. It moves with every cash event listed above **except** the acquisition price, which is a capital call and is deliberately excluded. From it the system derives `cash_yield_bps` as `net_operating_cash_usd` divided by `committed_capital_usd`, multiplied by 10,000, rounded half-up to zero decimal places — an integer basis-point figure. `harvest_acceleration_required` is true precisely when `cash_yield_bps` is **strictly less than** `target_cash_yield_bps`; equality leaves it false. That single boolean is what locks scheduled volume in place, as §3.4 describes.

Two exposure counters live on the fund alongside those figures. `replan_property_count` is the number of the fund's properties whose `harvest_plan_status` reads `REPLAN_REQUIRED`. `valuation_basis_stale_count` is the number of the fund's properties whose `status` is `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED` and whose `appraisal_status` is anything other than `SIGNED`. Both are recomputed whenever the fund or the investor report is read, and both are also maintained incrementally by the transitions that cause them to move. Recomputing them — or `nav_usd`, `cash_yield_bps` or `harvest_acceleration_required` — never by itself increments the fund's `version`: only an actual movement of `cash_balance_usd` or `net_operating_cash_usd` marks the fund as mutated, which is why a hazard report, a posture change or an appraisal action can shift the fund's derived figures while leaving its `version` where it was.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Fund Formation, Tract Registration and the Timber Cruise

A `FundAdmin` opens the portfolio by creating a closed-end fund with a `name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, a `vintage_year` between 2000 and 2100, the total limited-partner commitments as `committed_capital_usd` (strictly positive, two decimals), the investor-mandated `target_cash_yield_bps` between 0 and 5000, and the drawn cash at inception as `opening_cash_usd` (zero or more, two decimals). At creation `cash_balance_usd` is set equal to `opening_cash_usd`, `net_operating_cash_usd` is 0.00, `cash_yield_bps` is 0, `nav_usd` equals `cash_balance_usd`, and `replan_property_count` and `valuation_basis_stale_count` are both 0. `harvest_acceleration_required` is evaluated immediately from the strict comparison of the two yield figures: a fund created with any positive `target_cash_yield_bps` therefore begins life already requiring harvest acceleration, while a fund with a target of 0 does not.

An `AcquisitionAnalyst` registers each prospective tract under a fund, supplying the owning `fund_id`, a `name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, a `region_code` of `SOUTH`, `WEST` or `NORTHEAST`, the deeded `total_acres` (strictly positive, two decimals), the `haul_distance_miles` from the tract centroid to the nearest merchandising mill (an integer from 0 to 600), the `insured_value_usd` that will serve as the premium base (zero or more, two decimals), the acreage judged higher-and-better-use at underwriting as `hbu_acres` with its expected `hbu_price_per_acre_usd`, the stabilised `annual_rec_income_usd` expected from recreational leasing — those three each zero or more at two decimals, and `hbu_acres` deliberately unconstrained by `total_acres` — and the `certification_scheme` the tract is enrolled in — `SFI`, `FSC` or `NONE`. The `fund_id` is immutable once set.

Registration derives the tract's market access from haul distance alone. `market_access_tier` reads `TIER_A` when `haul_distance_miles` is at most 40, `TIER_B` from 41 through 80 inclusive, `TIER_C` from 81 through 140 inclusive, and `TIER_D` at 141 or more. The companion `market_access_discount_pct` is 0.0 for `TIER_A`, 4.5 for `TIER_B`, 9.0 for `TIER_C` and 15.0 for `TIER_D`; that percentage is the haircut applied to every stumpage revenue projection in the tract's underwriting. A new property begins with `status` `PROSPECT`, `appraisal_status` `NONE`, and `harvest_plan_status` `ALIGNED`. Its `certification_posture` begins as `CERTIFIED`, except that a tract registered with `certification_scheme` of `NONE` is immediately derived to `UNCERTIFIED` — an absorbing value for such tracts, whose audits, if any are scheduled at all, can never leave `SCHEDULED`.

A property carries four independent state dimensions, and a reader must be able to observe any combination of them. `status` tracks acquisition and disposition through `PROSPECT`, `UNDER_UNDERWRITING`, `APPROVED_FOR_BID`, `PASSED`, `OWNED` and `HBU_MARKETED`, with `PASSED` terminal. `appraisal_status` tracks independent valuation through `NONE`, `SUBMITTED`, `SIGNED` and `STALE`. `certification_posture` is derived from audit findings and takes `CERTIFIED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `SUSPENDED` or `UNCERTIFIED`. `harvest_plan_status` is either `ALIGNED` or `REPLAN_REQUIRED` and is driven by inventory-loss events. Neither `status` nor `appraisal_status` gates the other's transitions; they advance on disjoint triggers.

A `ForestManager` then lays out **stands** on the tract, each created against a `property_id` with an operator-assigned `stand_code` of one to forty characters, its `acres` (strictly positive, two decimals), a dominant `species_code` of `LOBLOLLY_PINE`, `DOUGLAS_FIR` or `NORTHERN_HARDWOOD`, an `age_years` from 0 to 200, and a `site_index` productivity figure from 20 to 140. A newly created stand is `CRUISE_OPEN`, its `plot_count` is 0, its `cruise_confidence` is `NONE`, its `mean_net_tons_per_acre` is unset, and all five inventory figures — `gross_merch_tons`, `net_merch_tons`, `reserved_tons`, `committed_tons`, `dedicated_tons`, `hbu_withdrawn_tons` and `available_tons` — read 0.00. Creating a stand mutates the parent property, incrementing its `version`. A stand's `split_from_stand_id` is null unless the system minted it from an HBU closing.

While the stand remains `CRUISE_OPEN`, the `ForestManager` records **cruise plots** against it. Each plot cites its `stand_id`, an integer `plot_number` of at least 1 that must be unique within that stand, the gross measured `tons_per_acre` (strictly positive, two decimals), the observed `defect_pct` from 0 through 100 at two decimals, and the field `measured_at` instant in ISO 8601 UTC. A duplicate `plot_number` on the same stand is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLOT_NUMBER`, and any attempt to add a plot to a stand that has left `CRUISE_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CRUISE_ALREADY_CLOSED`. On acceptance the server stamps `measured_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, increments the stand's `plot_count`, and computes the plot's own `net_tons_per_acre` as `tons_per_acre` multiplied by the quantity one minus `defect_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to **three** decimal places. Recording a plot is bookkeeping on the sample rather than a mutation of the stand: `plot_count` rises, but the stand's `updated_at` and `version` are left untouched, so a client's `expected_stand_version` survives a whole round of plotting. Plots are immutable thereafter, and `measured_at` is echoed back exactly as it was supplied.

Closing the cruise advances the stand from `CRUISE_OPEN` to `INVENTORIED` and expands the plot sample to a stand-level inventory. The stand must still be `CRUISE_OPEN`, or the request fails with `ERR_CRUISE_ALREADY_CLOSED`, and it must have at least one recorded plot, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_CRUISE_PLOTS`. The expansion consumes every cruise plot on the stand regardless of its `measured_at` value, and because plots cannot be added afterwards the sample is frozen at that moment. The rounding order is part of the contract and is not negotiable: each plot's `net_tons_per_acre` is already rounded to three decimals; `mean_net_tons_per_acre` is the sum of those rounded per-plot figures divided by `plot_count`, rounded half-up to three decimals; and `gross_merch_tons` is that rounded mean multiplied by the stand's `acres`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Averaging raw measurements, or expanding each plot to tons before averaging, produces a different and incorrect result.

Sample size then decides whether a haircut applies. `cruise_confidence` becomes `NORMAL` when `plot_count` is at least 5 at the moment of closure, and `LOW` otherwise. Under `NORMAL` confidence, `net_merch_tons` equals `gross_merch_tons` exactly. Under `LOW` confidence, `net_merch_tons` is `gross_merch_tons` multiplied by the quantity one minus eight divided by one hundred — a thinning of eight percent for a thin sample — rounded half-up to two decimals. Because no claim has yet been made against the stand, `available_tons` is set equal to `net_merch_tons` and all four claim buckets remain 0.00. Closing the cruise increments the stand's `version`.

### 3.2. Underwriting a Tract and the Committee Review Cycle

An `AcquisitionAnalyst` opens an **underwriting case** on a property, supplying the `property_id`, a `growth_variant` of either `YIELD_TABLE` or `MAI_LINEAR`, a `discount_rate_bps` from 1 to 5000, the price expectation `stumpage_price_usd_per_ton` (strictly positive, two decimals) that applies uniformly across all three projection periods, the `bid_price_usd` the committee will be asked to approve (strictly positive, two decimals), and the `expected_property_version` described in §2.8. The case is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0 and `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` unset, the server stamps `authored_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, and the property's `version` is incremented.

The valuation stack is computed at creation and recomputed from scratch at every submission, so that a case resubmitted after a cruise or a hazard event reflects the tract as it stands. Only the property's stands whose `status` is `INVENTORIED` contribute: their `net_merch_tons` are summed into a base tonnage and their `acres` into a base acreage. Stands still `CRUISE_OPEN` (never cruised), stands in `SALVAGE_PENDING` (loss not yet settled) and stands in `DISPOSED` (sold as HBU land) are all excluded. The species that keys the growth constants is the property's dominant species — the `species_code` of the contributing stand with the largest `acres`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. When no stand contributes at all there is no dominant species, and the projection leaves the (zero) base tonnage untouched rather than applying any growth constant.

**Growth projection** is the variant-dependent heart of the model, and it is used in two places: here, once per harvest period, and again when a harvest block records its projection horizon. Under `YIELD_TABLE`, the projected volume is the base tonnage multiplied by the factor one plus the species' periodic growth percentage divided by one hundred, that factor raised to the power of the whole number of years being projected, and the single result rounded half-up to two decimal places. The periodic growth percentages are 4.2 for `LOBLOLLY_PINE`, 3.1 for `DOUGLAS_FIR` and 2.4 for `NORTHERN_HARDWOOD`. Under `MAI_LINEAR`, the projected volume is the base tonnage plus the species' mean annual increment per acre per year multiplied by the stand acreage and by the whole number of years, again rounded half-up to two decimal places. Those increments are 2.1 tons per acre per year for `LOBLOLLY_PINE`, 1.6 for `DOUGLAS_FIR` and 0.9 for `NORTHERN_HARDWOOD`. Rounding occurs once, at the end; there is no per-year re-rounding. The two variants are genuinely different curves — the geometric form compounds off the standing volume and therefore outruns the arithmetic form on well-stocked stands while falling behind it on lightly stocked ones — so the same inputs under different variants must be expected to produce different values.

The timber present value accumulates over exactly three annual harvest periods, numbered one through three. For each period the projected volume is computed for that many years of growth under the case's `growth_variant`; the period's harvestable tonnage is that volume multiplied by twenty-five percent — a quarter of standing volume per period — rounded half-up to two decimals; the period's revenue is that tonnage multiplied by the case's `stumpage_price_usd_per_ton` and then by the quantity one minus the property's `market_access_discount_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals; the period's discount factor is one plus `discount_rate_bps` divided by 10,000, raised to the power of the period number less one half, reflecting a mid-period cash flow convention and never itself rounded; and the period's present value is the revenue divided by that discount factor, rounded half-up to two decimals. `timber_pv_usd` is the sum of the three per-period present values, rounded half-up to two decimals.

Two non-timber components join it. `hbu_premium_usd` is the property's `hbu_acres` multiplied by its `hbu_price_per_acre_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals. `rec_income_pv_usd` is the property's `annual_rec_income_usd` multiplied by six and one half, rounded half-up to two decimals — a six-and-a-half-times multiple on stabilised recreational income. `pre_fee_value_usd` is the sum of `timber_pv_usd`, `hbu_premium_usd` and `rec_income_pv_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals.

The acquisition fee is tiered, and the tier is keyed on `pre_fee_value_usd` — never on the post-fee figure. `acquisition_fee_pct` is 1.50 when `pre_fee_value_usd` is below 1,500,000.00; 1.00 when it is at least 1,500,000.00 but below 3,250,000.00; and 0.75 when it is at or above 3,250,000.00. `acquisition_fee_usd` is `pre_fee_value_usd` multiplied by `acquisition_fee_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `underwritten_value_usd` is `pre_fee_value_usd` less `acquisition_fee_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Selecting the tier from `underwritten_value_usd` is the natural mistake and is wrong: subtracting the fee can drop the value back below a breakpoint that the pre-fee figure cleared.

Submitting the case moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, or resubmits it from `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `SUBMITTED`, and simultaneously drives the property's `status` to `UNDER_UNDERWRITING`. Submission requires the case to be in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, requires the property's `status` to be `PROSPECT` or `UNDER_UNDERWRITING` — either check failing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and requires, after the valuation stack has been recomputed against current inventory, that `bid_price_usd` be no greater than `underwritten_value_usd`; a bid above the underwritten value is refused with `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_UNDERWRITTEN_VALUE`. Because the stack is rebuilt before the bid is tested, the recomputed figures are retained on the case even when that test then fails: a refused submission leaves the case in its old `status` at its old `version` but carrying the fresh valuation stack. A resubmission out of `REVISION_REQUESTED` clears the previous `decision_note`. Every submission increments the property's `version`.

The `InvestmentCommittee` then issues a decision on a `SUBMITTED` case, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_REVISION` and an optional `decision_note` recording the rationale — free text of one or more characters with no upper bound, and when it is omitted the case keeps whatever `decision_note` it already carried, since only a resubmission out of `REVISION_REQUESTED` clears that field. Whatever the decision, the caller must not be the case's author (§2.10) and the server stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` and increments the property's `version`.

The three branches are specified with equal force. **Approval** moves the case to `APPROVED` and the property from `UNDER_UNDERWRITING` to `APPROVED_FOR_BID`; it also withdraws every sibling case on the same property that is still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, moving each to `WITHDRAWN` and writing the `decision_note` `superseded by approved case` on each. **Rejection** moves the case to `REJECTED` and the property from `UNDER_UNDERWRITING` to the terminal `PASSED`. **A revision request** moves the case to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments its `revision_count` by one, while the property stays in `UNDER_UNDERWRITING` — a self-loop on the property dimension while the case cycles. A revision request is permitted only while `revision_count` is strictly less than two; a third request against a case that has already absorbed two rounds is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal for a case.

### 3.3. Independent Appraisal and the Acquisition Closing

Valuation for investor reporting comes from a third party, and the `IndependentAppraiser` role drives a dimension of its own on the property. The appraisal endpoint takes an `action` of `SUBMIT`, `SIGN` or `INVALIDATE`, together with whichever inputs the chosen methodology needs, and every appraisal action increments the property's `version`.

A submission carries an `appraisal_methodology` of `INCOME`, `MARKET` or `COST` and moves `appraisal_status` from `NONE` to `SUBMITTED`, or from `STALE` back to `SUBMITTED` when a season's revaluation is under way. Each methodology has its own required input set, and a missing input for the **chosen** methodology is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_INPUTS_INCOMPLETE`; inputs belonging to the other two methodologies are ignored when supplied rather than rejected. All five inputs are numbers at two decimals — `cap_rate_bps` strictly positive, the other four zero or more — and an input that is *present but* malformed, non-numeric or out of range is the rank-5 shape failure `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whichever methodology it belongs to, while an input that is simply absent is the rank-13 completeness failure. Under `INCOME`, the required inputs are `stabilized_noi_usd` and `cap_rate_bps`, and `appraised_value_usd` is `stabilized_noi_usd` divided by the quantity `cap_rate_bps` divided by 10,000 — direct capitalisation — rounded half-up to two decimals. Under `MARKET`, the required input is `comparable_price_per_acre_usd`, and `appraised_value_usd` is the property's `total_acres` multiplied by that per-acre comparable, rounded half-up to two decimals. Under `COST`, the required inputs are `bare_land_value_per_acre_usd` and `timber_unit_value_usd_per_ton`, and `appraised_value_usd` is the property's `total_acres` multiplied by the bare-land value per acre, plus the timber basis tonnage multiplied by the timber unit value per ton, the whole sum rounded half-up to two decimals; the timber basis tonnage is the sum of `net_merch_tons` over the property's stands whose `status` is `INVENTORIED`. Submission also stamps `appraisal_submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`.

Countersigning moves `appraisal_status` from `SUBMITTED` to `SIGNED`. The signer must satisfy both segregation-of-duties comparisons of §2.10, and on success the server stamps `appraisal_signed_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, records `appraisal_signed_at` as the current logical-clock instant, and recomputes the fund's `valuation_basis_stale_count`.

Invalidation has two distinct outcomes depending on where the dimension stands, and both matter. Invalidating a `SUBMITTED` appraisal returns `appraisal_status` to `NONE` and discards the work: `appraised_value_usd`, `appraisal_methodology` and `appraisal_submitted_by_user_id` are all cleared. Invalidating a `SIGNED` appraisal instead moves `appraisal_status` to `STALE`, which erases nothing at all — `appraised_value_usd`, `appraisal_methodology`, `appraisal_submitted_by_user_id`, `appraisal_signed_by_user_id` and `appraisal_signed_at` are every one of them retained — but subjects the property's contribution to fund net asset value to a twelve percent haircut, and recomputes `valuation_basis_stale_count`. The same `SIGNED` to `STALE` step also happens without any client action: whenever the property, its fund, or the investor report is read, a signed appraisal whose `appraisal_signed_at` lies more than 365 days before the logical clock is lazily staled, with the identical haircut and counter effects. Those three reads are the only triggers — reading the fund's property list, or any other endpoint, leaves an overdue appraisal reading `SIGNED` until one of them runs. The dimension therefore cycles `SIGNED` to `STALE` to `SUBMITTED` to `SIGNED` once per appraisal season.

With an approved case and a signed appraisal in hand, a `FundAdmin` closes the acquisition, citing the `underwriting_case_id` and the `expected_fund_version` of §2.8. The cited case must be `APPROVED`, or the closing is refused with `ERR_UNDERWRITING_NOT_APPROVED`; the property's own `status` must be `APPROVED_FOR_BID`, or the closing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the property's `appraisal_status` must be `SIGNED`, or the closing is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_SIGNED`; the case's `property_id` must equal the property named in the path, or the closing is refused with `ERR_CASE_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`; and the fund's `cash_balance_usd` must be at least the case's `bid_price_usd`, or the closing is refused with `ERR_FUND_CASH_INSUFFICIENT`. Those five gates are evaluated in exactly that written order, which is the precedence of §2.9: the fund version guard first of all, then the state gates, then the referential agreement, then the fiscal check last. On success the property moves from `APPROVED_FOR_BID` to `OWNED`, its `acquisition_price_usd` is set to the case's `bid_price_usd`, the same amount is debited from the fund's `cash_balance_usd`, the fund's `nav_usd` is recomputed, and the fund's `version` is incremented. `net_operating_cash_usd` is deliberately **not** touched: the bid is a capital call, not operating cash, and including it would understate the fund's yield against its mandate for the life of the fund.

Net asset value is recomputed on every read of a fund and of the investor report, and after every cash movement. Only properties whose `status` is `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED` carry value; those in `PROSPECT`, `UNDER_UNDERWRITING`, `APPROVED_FOR_BID` and `PASSED` contribute 0.00. A carrying property whose `appraisal_status` is `SIGNED` contributes its `appraised_value_usd` in full. One whose `appraisal_status` is `STALE` contributes `appraised_value_usd` multiplied by the quantity one minus twelve divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals. One whose `appraisal_status` is `NONE` or `SUBMITTED` falls back to cost basis and contributes its `acquisition_price_usd`. `nav_usd` is the fund's `cash_balance_usd` plus the sum of those carrying values, rounded half-up to two decimals.

The investor reporting pack is assembled from the same pass and is available to `FundAdmin`, `InvestmentCommittee` and `IndependentAppraiser` only. It reports `nav_usd`, `cash_balance_usd`, `net_operating_cash_usd`, `cash_yield_bps`, the mandated `target_cash_yield_bps`, the `harvest_acceleration_required` flag, `replan_property_count`, `valuation_basis_stale_count`, `owned_property_count` (the number of the fund's properties whose `status` is `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED`), `appraised_value_total_usd` (the sum of the carrying values over those same properties, rounded half-up to two decimals), and `standing_inventory_tons`. That last figure has a deliberately wider scope than the others: it is the sum of `net_merch_tons` over every stand of every property of the fund whose stand `status` is anything other than `DISPOSED`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and the owning property's `status` is irrelevant to it. Reading the fund or the report also applies any pending lazy staling of signed appraisals before the figures are computed.

### 3.4. Harvest Scheduling, Commitment and Completion

A `ForestManager` reserves stand inventory by creating a **harvest block**, supplying the `stand_id`, the operating window as `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the `planned_tons` to reserve (strictly positive at creation, two decimals), a `product_mix` of `SAWTIMBER`, `MIXED` or `PULPWOOD`, the `expected_stumpage_price_usd_per_ton` base price expectation (strictly positive, two decimals), a `price_index_factor` between 0.10 and 3.00 at two decimals representing the expected regional price index at planning time, the `expected_haul_distance_miles` for this block from 0 to 600, a `growth_variant` of `YIELD_TABLE` or `MAI_LINEAR`, and the `expected_stand_version` of §2.8.

Several gates guard the reservation. The stand must be `INVENTORIED` — a stand still `CRUISE_OPEN` or sitting in `SALVAGE_PENDING` yields `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`. The window must be coherent: `planned_end_date` must be on or after `planned_start_date`, since the window is closed at both ends, and a window that ends before it starts yields `ERR_HARVEST_WINDOW_INVALID`. The **re-entry buffer** forbids returning to the same ground too soon: for every block on the same stand whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `COMMITTED` or `COMPLETED`, the new block's `planned_start_date` must fall strictly after that prior block's `planned_end_date` advanced by twenty-four calendar months, the day of month clamped down when the target month is short — two full growing years — and a start date landing exactly on that buffer boundary is itself a conflict, refused with `ERR_REENTRY_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Finally, `planned_tons` must be no greater than the stand's `available_tons`, with equality permitted; an over-reservation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_TONS`.

On acceptance the block is created in `RESERVED`, its `planned_tons` is added to the stand's `reserved_tons` and subtracted from `available_tons`, and the stand's `version` is incremented. The server records `years_to_start` as the whole number of days between the logical clock date and `planned_start_date` divided by 365 and taken down to the next whole number — a window that has already opened at planning time therefore records a negative `years_to_start`, which is stored as it stands and fed to the projection unchanged — and records `projected_available_tons` as a snapshot of headroom at that future date: the projected volume computed for `years_to_start` years under this block's own `growth_variant`, keyed on the stand's own `species_code` and `acres`, off the stand's current `net_merch_tons`, less the stand's `reserved_tons`, less `committed_tons`, less `dedicated_tons`, less `hbu_withdrawn_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals. That snapshot is taken **before** this block's own reservation is applied. Creating a block on any of a property's stands also returns the property's `harvest_plan_status` from `REPLAN_REQUIRED` to `ALIGNED` and decrements the fund's `replan_property_count` accordingly — filing a fresh block is how a planner signals that the plan has been repaired. The property's own `version` moves only when that flag actually changes; filing a block against a property already `ALIGNED` leaves the property's `version` where it was.

Block creation also prices the operation provisionally. **Net revenue** is computed the same way in both variants, differing only in which tonnage and which index feed it. The effective price is the block's stumpage expectation multiplied by the index factor and then by the product-mix factor — 1.00 for `SAWTIMBER`, 0.82 for `MIXED`, 0.61 for `PULPWOOD` — rounded half-up to four decimal places. Gross revenue is the tonnage multiplied by that effective price, rounded half-up to two decimals. Logging cost is the tonnage multiplied by twelve dollars seventy-five cents per ton, rounded half-up to two decimals. Haul cost applies a nested cap: the billable mileage is the lesser of `expected_haul_distance_miles` and ninety miles, with any distance beyond ninety absorbed by the contractor, and the cost is the tonnage multiplied by fourteen and one half cents per ton-mile multiplied by those billable miles, rounded half-up to two decimals. Severance tax applies a nested floor: it is the greater of two hundred fifty dollars and gross revenue multiplied by two and one half percent rounded half-up to two decimals, and the winner is stored in `severance_tax_usd`. Net revenue is gross revenue less logging cost, less haul cost, less severance tax, rounded half-up to two decimals. At creation the tonnage is `planned_tons` and the index is `price_index_factor`, and the result is written to `provisional_net_revenue_usd`; the provisional figure never touches fund cash. Because the severance-tax floor stands at two hundred fifty dollars however small the harvest and the logging and haul costs are charged per ton regardless, net revenue is not floored at zero: a thin harvest can produce a negative figure, which is stored as it stands and, on completion, credited as it stands — reducing both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`.

Committing a reserved block advances it from `RESERVED` to `COMMITTED`. Its gates are evaluated in the written order below, which is the precedence of §2.9: the block's own `status` must be `RESERVED`, or the commit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the stand must be `INVENTORIED`, or the commit is refused with `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`; the property's `certification_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the commit is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`; and the block's closed window must not intersect the half-open hunting blackout of any `ACTIVE` recreational lease on the property, or the commit is refused with `ERR_LEASE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`. Commitment moves `planned_tons` out of the stand's `reserved_tons` and into its `committed_tons`, leaving `available_tons` unchanged, and increments the stand's `version`.

Closing a block takes an `action` of `COMPLETE` or `CANCEL`. **Completion** applies to a `COMMITTED` block and carries the scaled `actual_tons` actually removed together with the `realized_price_index_factor` the market actually delivered. Both are required on a `COMPLETE` close and both are numbers of at least 0 at two decimals; unlike the planning-time `price_index_factor`, the realized index is not confined to the 0.10-to-3.00 band and any non-negative value is accepted. The three gates are then evaluated in the written order below, which is the precedence of §2.9: the block's `status` must be `COMMITTED`, or the close is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the property's `certification_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or it is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`; and `actual_tons` must be no greater than the block's current `planned_tons`, or the close is refused with `ERR_ACTUAL_TONS_EXCEED_PLANNED`. The final revenue variant runs the identical arithmetic described above with `actual_tons` as the tonnage and `realized_price_index_factor` as the index, writing the result to `final_net_revenue_usd` and refreshing `severance_tax_usd` from that same pass. The block moves to `COMPLETED`, `actual_tons` is subtracted from the stand's `net_merch_tons`, the block's `planned_tons` leaves the stand's `committed_tons`, `final_net_revenue_usd` is credited to both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`, and `cash_yield_bps` and `harvest_acceleration_required` are recomputed. The stand's `version` is incremented.

**Cancellation** applies to a block in either `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, and both branches carry the same guard and the same cash-yield logic; a `CANCEL` close reads no tonnage or price fields at all, so an `actual_tons` or `realized_price_index_factor` sent alongside it is ignored and neither is recorded. A cancellation is permitted only while the fund's `harvest_acceleration_required` is false; when the fund is running behind its investor cash-yield mandate the scheduled volume is locked in place and the cancellation is refused with `ERR_YIELD_SHORTFALL_LOCK`. Cancelling a `RESERVED` block moves its `planned_tons` out of the stand's `reserved_tons` and back into `available_tons`; cancelling a `COMMITTED` block moves its `planned_tons` out of `committed_tons` and back into `available_tons`. Either way the block becomes `CANCELLED` and the stand's `version` is incremented.

Two further movements on a block are never client-initiated. When the property's `certification_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED`, every `COMMITTED` block on that property's stands reverts from `COMMITTED` to `RESERVED`, moving its `planned_tons` back out of `committed_tons` and into `reserved_tons`; because `planned_tons` itself is untouched, the property's `harvest_plan_status` is left exactly as it was by this revert. Each reverted block's own `version` is incremented; the stands are not, since their buckets are derived from the blocks rather than stored. That cycle between `RESERVED` and `COMMITTED` repeats across as many suspension and reinstatement rounds as the audit history produces. Separately, when an inventory-loss waterfall cuts a block's `planned_tons` all the way to 0.00, the block moves from `RESERVED` or from `COMMITTED` to the terminal `VOIDED`, its `voided_reason` is set to `HAZARD_LOSS` or `HBU_WITHDRAWAL` according to which waterfall consumed it, and the property's `harvest_plan_status` becomes `REPLAN_REQUIRED`. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal.

### 3.5. Carbon Project Development and Permanence Obligations

A `CarbonDeveloper` opens an improved-forest-management **carbon project** on a tract the fund already owns, supplying the `property_id`, a `registry` of `ACR`, `CAR` or `VERRA`, a `crediting_period_years` from 1 to 40, a `permanence_term_years` from 1 to 100, and a `baseline_tons_per_acre` carbon stocking assumption (strictly positive, two decimals). The property must be `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_OWNED`. The project is created in `DRAFT` with `dedicated_tons_total`, `buffer_pool_tons` and `buffer_deficit_tons` all 0.00.

Inventory is then pledged one stand at a time through **carbon dedications**, each citing the `carbon_project_id`, the `stand_id` whose inventory is being pledged, the `dedicated_tons` to withdraw from the allocatable pool (strictly positive, two decimals), and the `expected_stand_version` of §2.8. Its gates are evaluated in the written order below, which is the precedence of §2.9. After the stand version guard: the project must still be in `DRAFT` or `VALIDATION`, or the dedication is refused with `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_DEDICATION`. The stand must be `INVENTORIED`, or it is refused with `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`. The stand must belong to the project's own property, or the dedication is refused with `ERR_STAND_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` — so a stand on the wrong tract that is also not yet inventoried answers `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`. And `dedicated_tons` must be no greater than the stand's `available_tons`, equality permitted, or it is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_TONS` — the same ceiling a harvest reservation faces, drawn against the same pool. A new dedication is `PENDING` with `reduced_tons` 0.00, `permanence_end_date` unset and `release_reason` unset; its tonnage is added to the stand's `dedicated_tons` and subtracted from `available_tons`, the project's `dedicated_tons_total` is recomputed, and the stand's `version` is incremented. `dedicated_tons_total` is at all times the sum, over the project's dedications in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `REDUCED`, of each dedication's `dedicated_tons` less its `reduced_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals.

The registry lifecycle is driven by an `action` of `SUBMIT_VALIDATION`, `REGISTER`, `CURE_BUFFER` or `TERMINATE`. Submitting for validation moves the project from `DRAFT` to `VALIDATION`; it requires at least one dedication in `PENDING`, and a project with nothing pledged is refused with `ERR_CARBON_NO_DEDICATIONS`. Validation freezes nothing: a project sitting in `VALIDATION` still accepts fresh dedications, and `dedicated_tons_total` keeps tracking them. Registration moves the project from `VALIDATION` to `REGISTERED` and requires both that the property's `certification_posture` not be `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED` — and that `dedicated_tons_total` be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_CARBON_NO_DEDICATIONS`. Registration activates every `PENDING` dedication of the project, moving each to `ACTIVE` and stamping each with a `permanence_end_date` equal to the logical clock date advanced by the project's `permanence_term_years` calendar years, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. It also fixes `buffer_pool_tons` at `dedicated_tons_total` multiplied by eighteen percent, rounded half-up to two decimals — an eighteen percent insurance buffer against future reversal.

Reversal is what makes a carbon project fragile. When a hazard waterfall claws tonnage back out of an `ACTIVE` dedication of a registered project, that dedication moves from `ACTIVE` to `REDUCED` (its `reduced_tons` having risen above zero while still remaining below its `dedicated_tons`), the project's `buffer_deficit_tons` accumulates eighteen percent of the tonnage cut, and the project moves from `REGISTERED` to `REVERSAL_PENDING`. Curing the buffer moves it back: a cure supplies `cure_tons`, a required number of at least 0 at two decimals, which must be at least the project's current `buffer_deficit_tons`, or the cure is refused with `ERR_BUFFER_CURE_INSUFFICIENT`. A successful cure adds `cure_tons` to `buffer_pool_tons`, resets `buffer_deficit_tons` to 0.00, debits `cure_tons` multiplied by fourteen dollars per ton from both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`, and returns the project to `REGISTERED`. That cycle between `REGISTERED` and `REVERSAL_PENDING` repeats once per reversal event, and the debit is subject to the fund cash guard of §2.11.

Termination is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `VALIDATION`, from `REVERSAL_PENDING` and from `REGISTERED`, and the branches differ. From `DRAFT` and from `VALIDATION` there are no preconditions: every dedication in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `REDUCED` moves to `RELEASED` and its tonnage returns to the stand's `available_tons`. From `REVERSAL_PENDING` there are likewise no preconditions: the dedications are released the same way and the outstanding `buffer_deficit_tons` is written off unfunded — written off in the sense that no cash is ever demanded for it, not zeroed out, so the figure remains on the terminated project exactly as the reversal left it. A dedication released by a project termination carries no `release_reason`; that field stays unset. From `REGISTERED`, termination is permitted only when no `ACTIVE` dedication still has a `permanence_end_date` in the future; a premature termination of a registered project is refused with `ERR_CARBON_PERMANENCE_LOCK`, and when it does succeed every dedication in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `REDUCED` moves to `RELEASED`. `TERMINATED` is terminal.

An individual dedication can also be released on its own, with an optional `release_reason` of at least one character and no upper bound recorded; omitting it leaves the field unset rather than writing a blank. The three source states behave differently and each rule stands on its own. A `PENDING` dedication is not yet permanence-locked and releases freely, provided its parent project is still in `DRAFT` or `VALIDATION`; if the project has moved on, the release is refused with `ERR_CARBON_PROJECT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_DEDICATION`. On success its tonnage returns to the stand's `available_tons` and the stand's `version` is incremented. An `ACTIVE` dedication is locked: it may be released only once the logical clock date has reached or passed its `permanence_end_date`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_CARBON_PERMANENCE_LOCK`; on release its tonnage returns to `available_tons` and the stand's `version` is incremented. A `REDUCED` dedication carries the same permanence lock, and on release only its remaining tonnage — its `dedicated_tons` less its `reduced_tons` — returns to `available_tons`. In every case the dedication becomes `RELEASED`, which is terminal, the stand's `dedicated_tons` falls by the tonnage returned, and the project's `dedicated_tons_total` is recomputed. A dedication already `RELEASED` is neither of those three source states, so a second release attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.6. Recreational Leasing and Hunting-Season Blackouts

Hunting and recreational access is a real cash line on a timberland fund, and a `FundAdmin` records each offer as a **recreational lease** against a `property_id`, naming the hunting club or lessee in `lessee_name` (one to one hundred twenty characters), the `leased_acres` under the lease (strictly positive, two decimals), the `rent_per_acre_usd` (strictly positive, two decimals), the half-open term as `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, and the hunting-season blackout as `blackout_start_date` and `blackout_end_date`. The term must be non-empty — `term_end_date` strictly after `term_start_date` — and the blackout must be non-empty and must sit entirely inside the term; a term or blackout that violates any of those conditions is refused with `ERR_LEASE_TERM_INVALID`.

A new lease is `PENDING`. At creation the system computes `annual_rent_usd` as `leased_acres` multiplied by `rent_per_acre_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `term_days` as the whole number of days from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` under half-open counting, which excludes the end date. `first_year_prorated_rent_usd` and `termination_effective_date` are both unset until the events that produce them, while `earned_rent_usd` and `refunded_rent_usd` both start at 0.00. Recording an offer imposes no condition on the property's `status` — a lease may be filed against a tract at any stage of its life, and the ownership gate is deferred to activation. A `PENDING` lease already occupies acreage against the property's coverage cap even though no rent has been charged.

Activation moves the lease from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` and is where both the coverage cap and the money land. The property must be `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED`, or activation is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_OWNED`. The **coverage capacity** of a property is its `total_acres` multiplied by eighty percent, rounded half-up to two decimals — no more than four fifths of a tract may be under recreational lease at once. The set that counts against that capacity is the property's leases in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` whose half-open term intersects the candidate's half-open term; terms that merely touch at an endpoint do not intersect and do not count. The sum of those overlapping leases' `leased_acres` plus the candidate's own `leased_acres`, rounded half-up to two decimals, must be less than or equal to capacity; equality is allowed and only a strictly greater total is refused, with `ERR_LEASE_ACREAGE_EXCEEDED`.

On activation the lessee is billed `first_year_prorated_rent_usd`, computed as `annual_rent_usd` multiplied by the lesser of `term_days` and 365, divided by 365, rounded half-up to two decimals — a term shorter than a year pays pro rata, and a term longer than a year pays one full year up front. That amount is credited to both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`, and `cash_yield_bps` and `harvest_acceleration_required` are recomputed. From activation onward the lease's blackout window begins blocking harvest commitments on the property, as §3.4 describes.

Termination takes a `termination_effective_date`, required on both branches and recorded on the lease in both, and behaves differently depending on where the lease stands, and both branches are specified. Terminating an `ACTIVE` lease requires the `termination_effective_date` to be on or after `term_start_date` and strictly before `term_end_date`; a date outside that half-open window is refused as an undeclared transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than as a term-shape error. Earned rent is measured to that date exclusive: `earned_rent_usd` is `annual_rent_usd` multiplied by the whole number of days from `term_start_date` to `termination_effective_date`, divided by 365, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `refunded_rent_usd` is `first_year_prorated_rent_usd` less `earned_rent_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals. The refund is debited from both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`, subject to the cash guard of §2.11, and the leased acreage is released from the coverage cap along with the blackout from the commit gate. Because a term longer than a year bills only one year up front while earned rent keeps accruing past that point, the refund can come out negative; it is stored as it stands and debited as it stands, which adds back to both accumulators, and the cash guard passes trivially in that case. Terminating a `PENDING` lease has no date preconditions at all and moves no money: because no rent was ever charged, `refunded_rent_usd` stays 0.00 and `earned_rent_usd` stays 0.00. Either way the lease becomes `TERMINATED`, which is terminal.

A lease that simply runs its course is never terminated by a client. Whenever the lease itself, or the property's list of leases, is read, an `ACTIVE` lease whose `term_end_date` has been reached or passed by the logical clock date is lazily moved to `EXPIRED`, and its acreage is released from the coverage cap at that moment. Those two reads are the only triggers: activation and termination do not run the sweep, so terminating a lease whose term has already run out still takes the `ACTIVE` branch and still settles rent. `EXPIRED` is terminal. `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` leases release coverage immediately and impose no blackout; only `ACTIVE` leases impose blackouts.

### 3.7. Sustainable-Forestry Certification: Audits, Corrective Actions and Suspension

Certification is what keeps mills buying the fund's wood, and the `CertificationAuditor` role is deliberately independent of the people who manage the forest. An auditor schedules an audit on an owned tract, supplying the `property_id`, an `audit_type` of `SURVEILLANCE` or `RECERTIFICATION`, and a `scheduled_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The property must be `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED`, or the scheduling is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_OWNED`; enrolment is not checked at this point, so a tract whose `certification_scheme` is `NONE` may perfectly well carry a `SCHEDULED` audit and the scheme gate bites only when field work begins. A new audit is `SCHEDULED` with `major_finding_count`, `open_major_count` and `open_minor_count` all 0 — those three counters are never stored but derived from the audit's findings on every read, as §2.11 requires — and scheduling recomputes the property's `certification_posture`, bumping the property's `version` only if that recomputation actually moves the posture.

The audit lifecycle is driven by an `action` of `START_FIELD_WORK`, `ISSUE_FINDINGS`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `CLOSE`, and every one of those actions recomputes the property's `certification_posture` and increments the property's `version`. Starting field work moves the audit from `SCHEDULED` to `FIELD_WORK` and requires the property's `certification_scheme` to be something other than `NONE`; a tract enrolled in no scheme cannot be audited, and the attempt is refused as an undeclared transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Issuing findings moves the audit from `FIELD_WORK` to `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and recomputes the posture from the finding counts then standing.

Nonconformances are raised against an audit while it is in `FIELD_WORK` or `FINDINGS_ISSUED`; raising one against an audit in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Each finding records the parent audit it belongs to in `certification_audit_id`, taken from the audit named in the request path rather than from the body, and carries a registry `finding_code` of one to forty characters, a `severity` of `MAJOR`, `MINOR` or `OBSERVATION`, the standard `indicator_ref` it cites (one to forty characters), and a corrective-action `due_date`. A new finding is `OPEN` with `rejection_count` 0 and `car_text`, `car_submitted_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` all unset. Raising it recomputes the audit's counts and then the property's posture — and it is the *second* open `MAJOR` that suspends the tract and reverts its committed harvest volume. Raising a finding leaves the parent audit's own `version` untouched, since the counters it exposes are derived, and moves the property's `version` only when the posture recomputation actually changes the posture.

The audit's three counters are defined precisely and differently. `major_finding_count` counts the audit's findings whose `severity` is `MAJOR`, in any status. `open_major_count` counts those whose `severity` is `MAJOR` **and** whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CAR_SUBMITTED`. `open_minor_count` counts those whose `severity` is `MINOR` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CAR_SUBMITTED`. Findings of severity `OBSERVATION` never influence the posture in any status, and findings in `CLOSED` are excluded from the open counts whatever their severity.

**Posture derivation** looks across all of a property's audits at once, not just the one being acted on. Let the property's open major count be the number of findings across every audit of the property with `severity` `MAJOR` and `status` `OPEN` or `CAR_SUBMITTED`, and the open minor count be the same for `MINOR`. The posture is then decided in strict order: if the property's `certification_scheme` is `NONE`, the posture is `UNCERTIFIED`; otherwise, if any audit of the property has `status` `SUSPENDED`, the posture is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise, if the open major count is at least two, the posture is `SUSPENDED` — the threshold is two, not one; otherwise, if the open major count is exactly one or the open minor count is at least one, the posture is `CONDITIONAL`; otherwise the posture is `CERTIFIED`. This derivation runs on audit scheduling, on every audit advance, on every finding creation, and on every finding transition. Its named transitions are therefore `CERTIFIED` to `CONDITIONAL` when the property picks up its first open major or any open minor, `CONDITIONAL` to `SUSPENDED` when the open major count reaches two, `SUSPENDED` back to `CONDITIONAL` when verification brings the open major count down to one, `CONDITIONAL` back to `CERTIFIED` once the property has zero open majors and zero open minors and at least one audit closed conforming, and `CERTIFIED` to `UNCERTIFIED` at property registration when the scheme is `NONE`.

Any transition **into** `SUSPENDED` carries a cascade beyond the posture field itself: every harvest block on the property's stands whose `status` is `COMMITTED` reverts to `RESERVED`, with its `planned_tons` moving from the stand's `committed_tons` back to `reserved_tons`. `available_tons` and `planned_tons` are untouched by that revert, and so is the property's `harvest_plan_status` — a suspension does not create a replanning obligation, it only unwinds commitments. While the posture reads `SUSPENDED`, harvest commitment, harvest completion and carbon registration are all refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`.

An auditor may also suspend an audit explicitly, moving it from `FINDINGS_ISSUED` to `SUSPENDED`; that action requires `open_major_count` of at least two and drives the property's posture to `SUSPENDED` with the same committed-block revert. Reinstatement moves the audit from `SUSPENDED` back to `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and requires `open_major_count` of at most one, then recomputes the posture. Both of those count thresholds are part of the transition's own admissibility, so a suspension attempted below two open majors and a reinstatement attempted above one open major are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, exactly as a wrong source state would be. Closure moves the audit to the terminal `CLOSED_CONFORMING` and is reachable from `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and from `CAR_REVIEW`; both branches require `open_major_count` and `open_minor_count` to be exactly zero, and a closure attempted with any open major or minor finding outstanding is refused with `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_BLOCK_CLOSURE`.

The corrective-action cycle sits underneath, driven by an `action` of `SUBMIT_CAR`, `REJECT_CAR` or `VERIFY_CLOSE` on an individual finding. A `ForestManager` — and only a `ForestManager` — submits the corrective action, supplying the `car_text` narrative, which must be present and at least one character long with no upper bound on its length; the finding must be `OPEN` and its parent audit must be in `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `SUSPENDED`. Submission moves the finding from `OPEN` to `CAR_SUBMITTED` and stamps `car_submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`. When that submission leaves no finding of the audit in `OPEN`, and the audit has at least one open major or minor finding in total, the audit itself advances from `FINDINGS_ISSUED` to `CAR_REVIEW`.

A `CertificationAuditor` — and only a `CertificationAuditor` — resolves the submission. Rejecting it requires the finding to be in `CAR_SUBMITTED`, returns the finding to `OPEN`, increments its `rejection_count` by one, and drags the parent audit back from `CAR_REVIEW` to `FINDINGS_ISSUED`. That cycle between `OPEN` and `CAR_SUBMITTED`, and correspondingly between `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and `CAR_REVIEW`, may repeat without limit — there is no cap on `rejection_count`. Verifying and closing moves the finding from `CAR_SUBMITTED` to the terminal `CLOSED`, requires the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `car_submitted_by_user_id` (§2.10), imposes no condition of its own on the parent audit's state and leaves that audit's `status` where it stands, stamps `closed_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, and recomputes the audit's open counts and then the property's posture — which is how a suspended tract works its way back to `CONDITIONAL` and eventually to `CERTIFIED`.

### 3.8. Hazard Review, Insurance Pricing, Loss Reallocation and Salvage

Insurance is priced from a periodic hazard review. A `FundAdmin` records one by supplying an `insurance_rating_basis` of `ACTUARIAL` or `EXPERIENCE_RATED`, together with the three raw indices from the field: `wildfire_hazard_index`, `insect_hazard_index` and `weather_hazard_index`, each from 0 through 100 at two decimals. The review stores those raw values on the property, computes `composite_risk_score` and `insurance_premium_usd`, debits the premium from the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and from `net_operating_cash_usd` under the cash guard of §2.11, recomputes `cash_yield_bps` and `harvest_acceleration_required`, and increments the property's `version`. A review may be recorded whatever the property's `status`: there is no ownership or lifecycle gate on it, so a tract still `IDENTIFIED` or already `DISPOSED` can be priced, and repeated reviews simply overwrite the stored figures.

The composite score is a weighted hazard blend with two region- and species-specific adjustments, and both adjustments are capped at 100.00. The wildfire component is `wildfire_hazard_index` multiplied by the region's wildfire multiplier — 1.00 for `SOUTH`, 1.25 for `WEST`, 0.80 for `NORTHEAST` — rounded half-up to two decimals, then held down to at most 100.00. The insect component is `insect_hazard_index` multiplied by the species insect multiplier of the property's dominant species — 1.15 for `LOBLOLLY_PINE`, 1.00 for `DOUGLAS_FIR` and 1.00 for `NORTHERN_HARDWOOD`, and 1.00 when the property has no stand at all — rounded half-up to two decimals, then likewise held down to at most 100.00. Dominant species here means the `species_code` of the property's stand with the largest `acres`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`, and every stand of the property counts whatever its `status` — a `CRUISE_OPEN` stand, a `SALVAGE_PENDING` one, and a `DISPOSED` stand derived from an HBU carve-out are all eligible to be the dominant one. The weather component enters unadjusted as the raw `weather_hazard_index`. `composite_risk_score` is then forty-five hundredths of the adjusted wildfire component, plus thirty hundredths of the adjusted insect component, plus twenty-five hundredths of the weather index, rounded half-up to two decimals. It is stored under both rating bases, even though only one of them prices the policy.

The base premium is the property's `insured_value_usd` multiplied by thirty-eight basis points — that is, divided by 10,000 and multiplied by 38 — rounded half-up to two decimals. Under `ACTUARIAL`, where no credible claim history exists, the premium loads the base by the composite hazard: it is the base premium multiplied by the quantity one plus `composite_risk_score` divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals, and then floored at one thousand five hundred dollars, `insurance_premium_usd` taking whichever of the two is greater. Under `EXPERIENCE_RATED`, the composite score is ignored entirely and the loading comes from realised losses: the loss ratio is the trailing settled claims divided by `insured_value_usd`, rounded half-up to four decimals, and taken to be 0.0000 outright when `insured_value_usd` is 0.00 so that the division is never attempted; the ratio is an intermediate figure only and is never stored on the property. The premium is the base premium multiplied by the quantity one plus that loss ratio, rounded half-up to two decimals, again floored at one thousand five hundred dollars. Trailing settled claims are the sum of `settled_claim_usd` over the hazard events on the property's stands whose event `status` is `SETTLED` and whose `occurred_at` is at or after the logical clock instant less 1,095 days. That three-year window is open at its top end — it has a floor and no ceiling — so an event stamped with a future `occurred_at` still counts. Events still in `REPORTED` contribute 0.00 to that sum however large their computed claim.

When a peril actually strikes, a `ForestManager` reports a **hazard event** against the `stand_id`, with an `event_type` of `WILDFIRE`, `INSECT`, `WIND_STORM` or `ICE_STORM`, the `occurred_at` instant in ISO 8601 UTC, and the merchantable `damaged_tons` destroyed or degraded (strictly positive, two decimals). The stand must be `INVENTORIED`, or the report is refused with `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`, and only then is `damaged_tons` checked against the stand's current `net_merch_tons`; a report claiming more damage than there was wood is rejected as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That ceiling is the one shape complaint in the system deliberately evaluated *after* a state gate rather than at rank 5 of §2.9, because it is a comparison against stored inventory rather than a property of the body alone: a report of impossible tonnage against a stand that is not `INVENTORIED` answers `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`. A new event is `REPORTED`, with `settled_claim_usd` and `salvage_sold_tons` unset, `inventory_shortfall_tons` 0.00 and an empty `reallocation_log`.

Acceptance computes two figures and then runs the waterfall. `salvageable_tons` is `damaged_tons` multiplied by the peril's salvage recovery percentage — thirty-five percent for `WILDFIRE`, fifty-five for `INSECT`, seventy for `WIND_STORM`, sixty for `ICE_STORM` — rounded half-up to two decimals. `insurance_claim_usd` is `damaged_tons` multiplied by an indemnity rate of twenty-two dollars fifty cents per ton and then by an indemnity share of sixty percent, rounded half-up to two decimals; it is the ceiling on what the carrier can be made to pay. The stand's `net_merch_tons` is then reduced by `damaged_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and the stand moves from `INVENTORIED` to `SALVAGE_PENDING`. The stand's `version` is incremented.

The reduction may leave the stand over-allocated, and the **inventory-loss waterfall** is how the books are brought back into line. Allocated tonnage is the sum of `reserved_tons`, `committed_tons`, `dedicated_tons` and `hbu_withdrawn_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals; `inventory_shortfall_tons` is the greater of zero and that allocated total less the stand's post-damage `net_merch_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals. The shortfall is then clawed back from three ordered buckets, cheapest claim to break first: **bucket one** is the stand's harvest blocks whose `status` is `RESERVED`, taken in ascending `created_at` order; **bucket two** is the blocks whose `status` is `COMMITTED`, also in ascending `created_at` order; **bucket three** is the stand's carbon dedications, `ACTIVE` ones first and then `PENDING` ones, each group in ascending `created_at` order. `hbu_withdrawn_tons` is never clawed back — that land has been sold — and if the shortfall survives all three buckets the remainder is absorbed implicitly against the withdrawn bucket: nothing further is adjusted and the stand's derived `available_tons` is simply left reading negative until inventory or withdrawals change.

Walking the buckets in that order, the waterfall cuts each target by the lesser of the remaining shortfall and that target's own remaining tonnage, stopping as soon as the remaining shortfall reaches zero. Cutting a harvest block reduces its `planned_tons` by the cut amount, rounded half-up to two decimals, and sets the property's `harvest_plan_status` to `REPLAN_REQUIRED` — any cut does this, whether or not the block survives — which in turn increments the fund's `replan_property_count`; a block reduced all the way to 0.00 additionally becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `HAZARD_LOSS`. Cutting a carbon dedication adds the cut amount to its `reduced_tons` and moves it to `REDUCED`, adds eighteen percent of the cut amount (rounded half-up to two decimals) to the parent project's `buffer_deficit_tons`, and moves that project to `REVERSAL_PENDING`. Every cut appends an ordered record to the event's `reallocation_log`, each record carrying `target_type` (either `HARVEST_BLOCK` or `CARBON_DEDICATION`), `target_id`, `tons_cut` and `resulting_status` — the target's status as it stands *after* the cut, which for a survivor is still `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` and for a dedication is always `REDUCED` — and the remaining shortfall is reduced by the cut, rounded half-up to two decimals, before the next target is considered. Only blocks in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` and dedications in `ACTIVE` or `PENDING` participate: blocks already `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` and dedications already `RELEASED` or already `REDUCED` are off the pool and are skipped entirely, so a dedication a previous reversal has already cut is never revisited by a later one.

Settlement closes the episode. A `FundAdmin` settles a `REPORTED` event by supplying the carrier's `settled_claim_usd` and the `salvage_sold_tons` actually merchandised out of the salvage operation; both are required numbers of at least 0 at two decimals. The settlement may not exceed the event's computed `insurance_claim_usd`, or it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_INDEMNITY`, and the sold volume may not exceed the event's computed `salvageable_tons`, or it is refused with `ERR_SALVAGE_EXCEEDS_RECOVERABLE`. On success the event moves from `REPORTED` to the terminal `SETTLED`, the stand moves back from `SALVAGE_PENDING` to `INVENTORIED`, `settled_claim_usd` is credited to both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`, and `cash_yield_bps` and `harvest_acceleration_required` are recomputed. `salvage_sold_tons` is recorded for the file and nothing else: it returns no tonnage to the stand and carries no cash of its own, the whole of the settlement's money being the claim. From that moment the settled claim counts toward the trailing claim window used for experience-rated premiums. A stand may be damaged and settled repeatedly, cycling between `INVENTORIED` and `SALVAGE_PENDING` as often as the weather requires.

### 3.9. Higher-and-Better-Use Disposition

Some acreage is worth more as land than as timber, and the disposition program carves it out. A `FundAdmin` identifies an **HBU parcel** inside a stand, supplying the `stand_id`, an operator-assigned `parcel_code` of one to forty characters, the parcel `acres` (strictly positive, two decimals), and the listing `ask_price_usd` (strictly positive, two decimals). The stand must be `INVENTORIED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STAND_NOT_INVENTORIED`, and the carve-out must leave at least five acres behind on the parent stand: `acres` must be no greater than the stand's `acres` less five, and a parcel that would leave less is refused with `ERR_HBU_ACRES_EXCEED_STAND`. A new parcel is `IDENTIFIED` with `withdrawn_tons` 0.00 and `contract_price_usd`, `closing_costs_usd`, `commission_usd`, `net_proceeds_usd` and `derived_stand_id` all unset. Identifying a parcel is a pure record: it moves no acreage and no tonnage, so the parent stand is not mutated and its `version` is left where it was.

Disposition proceeds through an `action` of `LIST`, `CONTRACT`, `RELIST`, `CLOSE` or `WITHDRAW`. Listing moves the parcel from `IDENTIFIED` to `LISTED` and requires the property to be `OWNED` or `HBU_MARKETED` — otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_OWNED` — and its `appraisal_status` to be `SIGNED`, since land is not marketed without an independent valuation behind it; an unsigned appraisal is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_SIGNED`. Listing also drives the property's `status` from `OWNED` to `HBU_MARKETED`. Contracting moves the parcel from `LISTED` to `UNDER_CONTRACT`, requires a `contract_price_usd` that is present and strictly positive, and records both that price and the resulting `commission_usd`, which is `contract_price_usd` multiplied by three and one half percent, rounded half-up to two decimals. Relisting moves the parcel back from `UNDER_CONTRACT` to `LISTED` on buyer fallout and clears both `contract_price_usd` and `commission_usd`; that cycle between `LISTED` and `UNDER_CONTRACT` may repeat any number of times.

Withdrawal is reachable from `IDENTIFIED`, `LISTED` and `UNDER_CONTRACT`, and the three branches differ only in their downstream effect on the property. Withdrawing an `IDENTIFIED` parcel has no side effect at all, because such a parcel never put the property on the market. Withdrawing a `LISTED` or an `UNDER_CONTRACT` parcel returns the property's `status` from `HBU_MARKETED` to `OWNED` **when no parcel of that property remains in `LISTED` or `UNDER_CONTRACT`**. The property therefore cycles between `OWNED` and `HBU_MARKETED` as often as the disposition pipeline opens and empties. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Closing is the consequential branch. It moves the parcel from `UNDER_CONTRACT` to the terminal `CLOSED` and requires `closing_costs_usd` to be present as a number of at least 0 at two decimals, the parent stand to be `INVENTORIED`, and the stand's `acres` less the parcel's `acres` to be at least five acres. Money settles first: `net_proceeds_usd` is `contract_price_usd` less `commission_usd` less `closing_costs_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and is credited to both the fund's `cash_balance_usd` and its `net_operating_cash_usd`. Closing costs are not capped against the contract price, so a costly closing can drive `net_proceeds_usd` negative; the negative figure is stored and credited as it stands, which reduces both accumulators.

The inventory split is where the conservation discipline shows. `withdrawn_tons` is the parent stand's `net_merch_tons` multiplied by the parcel's `acres` and divided by the parent stand's `acres` — a pro-rata share of standing wood measured against the pre-split acreage — rounded half-up to two decimals. Acreage is conserved exactly: a **derived stand** is minted carrying acreage equal to the parcel's `acres`, and the parent stand's `acres` is reduced by the parcel's `acres`, rounded half-up to two decimals, so that parent and derived together equal the original. The derived stand references its origin through `split_from_stand_id` and inherits the parent's descriptive attributes outright — the same `stand_code`, `species_code`, `age_years`, `site_index`, `mean_net_tons_per_acre` and `cruise_confidence` — while its `plot_count` is 0, because no plots were ever cruised against it, and both its `gross_merch_tons` and its `net_merch_tons` equal `withdrawn_tons`. Its four allocation buckets and therefore `available_tons` all derive to 0.00, nothing being reserved, committed, dedicated or withdrawn against a stand that has just been disposed of. It is created already reading the terminal `DISPOSED` at `version` 1 — it inherits a completed cruise and its whole life happens inside this one chain of effects, so it is never observable in `INVENTORIED` and its version never reflects a transition; no client-facing operation reaches `DISPOSED` by any other route. The parcel's `derived_stand_id` is set to that new stand's identifier.

The parent stand's `net_merch_tons` is deliberately **not** reduced by the withdrawal. Instead the tonnage is parked in the `hbu_withdrawn_tons` bucket, which rises by `withdrawn_tons`. Keeping the pool intact and moving the claim is what preserves the partition identity of §2.7 exactly and gives the reallocation logic a single source of truth. It does, however, mean the stand can now be over-allocated, so the closing runs the identical ordered waterfall described in §3.8 — `RESERVED` blocks in ascending `created_at` order first, then `COMMITTED` blocks in the same order, then carbon dedications with `ACTIVE` before `PENDING` — against a shortfall computed as the greater of zero and the sum of `reserved_tons`, `committed_tons`, `dedicated_tons` and `hbu_withdrawn_tons` less the stand's `net_merch_tons`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Every block whose `planned_tons` is cut sets the property's `harvest_plan_status` to `REPLAN_REQUIRED`, and a block cut to 0.00 becomes `VOIDED` — here with `voided_reason` `HBU_WITHDRAWAL` rather than `HAZARD_LOSS`. Dedications cut by this waterfall behave exactly as they do under a hazard loss, moving to `REDUCED` and driving their project to `REVERSAL_PENDING` with an eighteen percent buffer deficit on the tonnage cut. What this waterfall does not do is leave a log: the ordered record of cuts is a field on a hazard event, and an HBU closing has nowhere to put one, so its cuts are visible only on the targets themselves. Finally, closing returns the property's `status` from `HBU_MARKETED` to `OWNED` when no parcel of the property remains in `LISTED` or `UNDER_CONTRACT`, and increments the stand's `version`.

A property left with `harvest_plan_status` `REPLAN_REQUIRED` — whether by a hazard waterfall or by an HBU withdrawal — stays that way until a `ForestManager` files a fresh harvest block on one of that property's stands, which returns the flag to `ALIGNED` and decrements the fund's `replan_property_count`. A second loss re-raises it, and the exposure counter tracks the fund's total replanning obligation for investor reporting.
