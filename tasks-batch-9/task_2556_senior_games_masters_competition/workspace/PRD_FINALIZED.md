# Senior Games & Masters Competition API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every two years a state senior games organizing committee has to turn a spreadsheet-driven volunteer effort into something closer to a small Olympic Games. Several thousand masters athletes aged fifty and above register across dozens of sports, each one contested in five-year age bands, each band carrying its own equipment weights and format modifications. The committee must decide who qualified and from which meet, place every event in a venue that can medically support it, keep multi-sport athletes from being scheduled into two fields of play at once, screen participants for cardiac risk before they compete, staff each competition block with certified officials who are not double-booked, ratify age-group records without letting the same person both verify and ratify a mark, house visiting athletes in a village with a finite number of beds, and finally report back to the host municipality and its sponsors what the games were actually worth to the local economy.

This document specifies the Senior Games & Masters Competition API, the backend service that carries all of that state. Every route described here is mounted beneath the `/senior-games` prefix. The service is deliberately opinionated about arithmetic and ordering: registration fees, sponsorship recognition, medical risk scores and economic impact figures are all derived by pinned formulas whose evaluation order changes the answer, and capacity counters for event entries, venue areas, official minutes and bed-nights are conserved quantities that must be debited and credited in exact compensating pairs. Where a single write ripples outward — closing a venue, withdrawing an athlete, placing a screening hold — the ripple is part of the contract, not an implementation detail.

Two design constraints shape the whole surface. First, time is supplied to the server, not read from the host: deadlines, competition windows, stay windows, session intervals and expiry sweeps all evaluate against a logical clock that a test harness can position. Second, most forward motion of the games edition itself happens through an explicit operations checkpoint rather than a background scheduler, so the committee — and the test suite — can step the games forward one phase at a time and observe exactly what each step changed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold everywhere: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity, precision, concurrency guards, cascade discipline, and the order in which rejections are decided.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, read or write, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven role names the system recognises. There is no anonymous access tier.

The seven roles are `GamesAdmin`, `RegistrationOfficer`, `Athlete`, `MedicalDirector`, `SportOfficial`, `RecordsRatifier` and `SponsorLiaison`. `GamesAdmin` is organizing-committee operations staff: it builds the edition, the sport catalogue, venues and the schedule, recruits personnel, runs the operations checkpoint, and drives the logical clock in test harnesses. `RegistrationOfficer` staffs the entry desk and the results desk: it reviews entries against qualification and age-group rules, seeds events, and verifies, publishes and adjudicates marks produced by officials. `Athlete` is a masters competitor acting on their own registration: paying fees, filing and revising entries, booking a village bed, protesting a mark, and withdrawing. `MedicalDirector` records screening recommendations from the risk model and hears appeals against a screening hold. `SportOfficial` is a certified official or volunteer acting as themselves: confirming their own assignment and submitting marks for the sessions they officiate. `RecordsRatifier` is the national-body records officer who files age-group record claims and ratifies or rejects them. `SponsorLiaison` books sponsorship pledges, allocates recognition across buckets, and finalizes the edition's economic-impact report. Every single-resource read and every list read is available to all seven roles; write access is per-endpoint, and a role that is not listed for the endpoint it calls is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the seven roles are backed by persisted entities, and for those two `X-User-ID` is an entity identifier that ownership checks compare against. When `X-User-Role` is `Athlete`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Athlete` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Athlete` compare the resource's athlete foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and on athlete-scoped action paths the athlete identifier in the path must itself equal `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SportOfficial`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Personnel` row; a personnel assignment is owned by the person named in its `personnel_id`, and a competition result is owned by the person named in its `submitted_by_personnel_id`. A violation of either ownership check is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining five roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `GamesAdmin`, `RegistrationOfficer`, `MedicalDirector`, `RecordsRatifier` or `SponsorLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any row's `id`. The server does, however, record it where a transition writes it down: `verified_by_user_id` on a competition result captures the `RegistrationOfficer` who verified it, `ratified_by_user_id` on an age-group record captures the `RecordsRatifier` who ratified or rejected it, and `screened_by_user_id` on a medical screening is supplied in the request body by the acting `MedicalDirector`. Those recorded operator strings are the basis of the segregation-of-duties rule: ratification compares the acting `X-User-ID` against both `claimed_by_user_id` and the cited result's `verified_by_user_id`, and a match on either side is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many rows to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Games Calendar

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every deadline comparison, competition-window test, village-window test, session-interval evaluation, expiry sweep and stay completion evaluates against a single server-held logical clock. On process startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a harness moves it, that is the instant every rule sees; falling back to real time is a defect. A `GamesAdmin` may reposition the clock to any instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; the response reports the resulting instant as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity row — it changes only what subsequent rule evaluations observe.

Calendar dates and instants play different roles and must not be conflated. `competition_start_date`, `competition_end_date`, `age_reference_date`, `meet_date`, `check_in_date` and `check_out_date` are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. `registration_deadline_utc`, `start_utc`, `end_utc`, `seeded_at_utc` and `screened_at_utc` are instants. Where a rule compares the clock against a calendar date — for example the edition advancing into competition, or a stay completing — the comparison uses the clock's UTC calendar date; where a rule compares against an instant, the comparison is on the full instant.

An edition's own calendar is constrained at creation. `competition_end_date` must be on or after `competition_start_date`, and `age_reference_date` must be on or after `competition_end_date`. A violation of either is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Rows

Most resources carry the same four server-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only touch a derived counter such as `accepted_entry_count`, `confirmed_official_count`, `assigned_minutes_total`, `reserved_bed_nights` or `sponsorship_recognized_cents`. Every resource that has a lifecycle also exposes a `status` field naming the state it currently occupies, and the corresponding list read may be narrowed by a `status` filter; the athlete is the one exception, splitting its lifecycle across two separately named dimensions instead of a single `status`.

Two resources are immutable once written and therefore carry a reduced property set. A `QualifyingResult` and a `MedicalScreening` each expose only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources entirely, and no endpoint modifies them after creation. Every other resource in the system — games editions, sports, sport events, venues, session slots, athletes, sport entries, personnel, personnel assignments, competition results, age-group records, accommodation blocks, village stays and sponsorship commitments — carries all four properties.

### 2.6. Identifier Resolution and Referential Integrity

Identifiers supplied in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. The path identifier names the system recognises are `games_edition_id`, `sport_id`, `sport_event_id`, `venue_id`, `session_slot_id`, `athlete_id`, `qualifying_result_id`, `sport_entry_id`, `medical_screening_id`, `personnel_id`, `personnel_assignment_id`, `competition_result_id`, `age_group_record_id`, `accommodation_block_id`, `village_stay_id` and `sponsorship_commitment_id`. Each has a dedicated HTTP 404 error: `ERR_GAMES_EDITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPORT_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFYING_RESULT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPORT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEDICAL_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERSONNEL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPETITION_RESULT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGE_GROUP_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VILLAGE_STAY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign-key identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after all path identifiers, and among themselves they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields — not in alphabetical order and not in whatever order a map happens to iterate. A body foreign key that does not resolve yields the same dedicated 404 error as its path counterpart; `submitted_by_personnel_id` resolves against `Personnel` and therefore answers `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`.

Relationships in this system are carried by identifier fields on the rows themselves rather than by deep URL nesting: no path descends more than one action segment below its collection. A session slot names its `venue_id` and its `sport_event_id`; a sport entry names its `athlete_id` and its `sport_event_id`; a competition result names its `sport_entry_id`, its `session_slot_id` and its `submitted_by_personnel_id`; an age-group record names its `sport_event_id` and its `competition_result_id` and derives `holder_athlete_id` and `superseded_record_id`; a village stay names its `athlete_id` and its `accommodation_block_id`; and editions own their venues, athletes, personnel, accommodation blocks and sponsorship commitments through `games_edition_id`.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Shared Parents

Three write operations act on state that is genuinely shared across sibling rows, and for those three the client must prove it has observed the parent's latest revision. Each takes a mandatory version field in its request body — the field is not optional, and omitting it is a malformed request answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The value supplied must equal the parent row's current `version` at the moment the write is evaluated; if the parent's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the write is rejected rather than applied.

Filing an age-group record claim requires `expected_sport_event_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SportEvent`. A mismatch against the event's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SPORT_EVENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because another writer may already have filed and ratified a claim in the same event scope, which legitimately supersedes the prior record and rewrites the event's `current_record_id`; a client working from an older view of the event would compute the wrong `superseded_record_id`.

Reserving a village stay requires `expected_block_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `AccommodationBlock`. A mismatch against the block's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Bed-night occupancy is an aggregate over sibling stays, so an earlier writer that consumed beds leaves the current writer's per-night availability view stale even when beds still remain and the reservation would otherwise succeed on its own merits.

Allocating recognition for a sponsorship commitment requires `expected_games_edition_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `GamesEdition`. A mismatch against the edition's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GAMES_EDITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The recognition ledger `sponsorship_recognized_cents` is a shared running total, so an allocation computed against a stale edition view would post onto a balance the client never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three carry no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is an unknown body field.

### 2.8. Money, Counters and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities in this system are integers denominated in cents; there are no decimal currency fields. `municipal_support_cents`, `entry_fee_cents`, `nightly_rate_cents`, `pledged_cents`, `fee_total_cents`, `fee_paid_cents`, `recognized_cents`, `sponsorship_recognized_cents`, `provisional_impact_cents`, `final_impact_cents` and `total_charge_cents` are all whole cents, and every rounding step named in this document uses the round-half-up convention — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero.

Where a formula produces money, the number and placement of rounding steps is part of the specification and not a matter of taste. The registration fee settlement rounds exactly once, on the discount amount, and never again. Sponsorship recognition rounds four times, once per recognition bucket, before the buckets are summed; rounding the multiplied total once instead is a different and incorrect answer. Economic impact rounds twice, once when direct visitor spend is computed and once when the multiplier is applied to it. The single non-monetary rounded quantity in the system is the medical `risk_score`, which is carried to one decimal place, rounded half up, and clamped so it can never exceed 100.0.

Derived counters are conserved quantities, maintained incrementally rather than recomputed by scan, and every debit has an exactly compensating credit. `accepted_entry_count` and `available_slots` on a sport event always sum to `capacity`, and `available_slots` is never negative. `confirmed_official_count` on a session slot rises and falls with confirmed official assignments and is reset to zero when the slot's staffing is torn down. `assigned_minutes_total` on a person rises by an assignment's `duration_minutes` when the assignment is created and falls by exactly that same figure when the assignment is released or marked a no-show. `reserved_bed_nights` on an accommodation block rises by a stay's `nights` on reservation and falls by exactly that figure on cancellation. `sponsorship_recognized_cents` on an edition rises by a commitment's `recognized_cents` on allocation and falls by exactly that figure when an allocated commitment is withdrawn.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation

A single write frequently changes more than one row, and the full ripple is part of the contract. Closing a venue displaces its scheduled and running slots, which releases the personnel assignments on those slots, which debits each affected person's served-minutes ledger, which may in turn put the slots' sport events on schedule hold. Withdrawing an athlete withdraws their entries, which restores capacity on the affected events, which may drive those events undersubscribed, which releases the slots of those events, which releases the personnel on those slots. Placing a screening hold on an athlete pulls their accepted entries onto medical hold, which restores event capacity, which may drive events undersubscribed. Each of these steps is a real transition on a real row: it sets that row's status, refreshes that row's `updated_at`, and increments that row's `version` independently of every other row touched in the same request.

The server is expected to perform these transitions on its own initiative, without a client request naming the dependent row. Some cascades are deliberately one-way. Session slots released when an event falls undersubscribed are not restored when the event recovers — they must be created afresh. Personnel assignments released by a venue closure are not restored when the slot is rescheduled. `provisional_impact_cents` is never overwritten by finalization, so an edition can be read after finalization with both the forward-looking projection and the chronological actual sitting side by side on the same row.

### 2.10. Order of Validation Precedence

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure decides the response.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint called — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. On every POST endpoint, each required body field is present, correctly typed, inside its declared numeric range, and for enum fields a declared member; unknown body fields are rejected — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. On every list read, `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is a non-negative integer — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. Every identifier in the request path resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found error.
7. Every foreign-key identifier in the request body resolves, in the order the endpoint declares its inputs — otherwise the matching not-found error. This applies to the creation endpoints for sport events, venues, session slots, athletes, qualifying results, sport entries, medical screenings, personnel, personnel assignments, competition results, age-group records, accommodation blocks, village stays and sponsorship commitments.
8. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` matches the owning identifier of the target — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This guard covers ten operations: athlete fee payment, athlete withdrawal, sport-entry creation, sport-entry resubmission, sport-entry withdrawal, competition-result creation, competition-result protest, personnel-assignment confirmation, village-stay reservation and village-stay cancellation.
9. The version guard field equals the parent's current `version` on the three guarded writes — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SPORT_EVENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_GAMES_EDITION_VERSION` as described in §2.7. The three guarded writes are age-group record creation, village-stay creation and sponsorship allocation.
10. The governing games edition is in a status that permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_EDITION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Registration writes — athlete creation, fee payment and sport-entry creation — require `REGISTRATION_OPEN`; athlete check-in requires `REGISTRATION_CLOSED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; impact finalization requires `COMPLETED`; sponsorship allocation requires any status other than `IMPACT_FINALIZED`. Opening registration is governed by the edition's own origin state `DRAFT`, which is decided at step 11.
11. The target row's own current status is a declared origin state of the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This applies to every action endpoint, that is, every operation that opens registration, runs the operations checkpoint, seeds, cancels, closes, reschedules, advances, pays fees, checks in, withdraws, hears a medical appeal, reviews, resubmits, confirms, releases, verifies, publishes, protests, resolves a protest, ratifies, allocates or finalizes impact.
12. The target row is not already sitting in a terminal state of its own machine — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The terminal states for this purpose are `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` on a sport entry, `VOIDED` on a competition result, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` on an age-group record, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` on a village stay, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` on an athlete's registration dimension, and `WITHDRAWN` on a sponsorship commitment. Step 11 is evaluated strictly before this check and is never skipped, so a repeat command whose target now sits in a terminal state that is not a declared origin of that command is refused at step 11 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than here — a second cancellation of a `CANCELLED` village stay, a second withdrawal of a `WITHDRAWN` sponsorship commitment, and a second withdrawal, a resubmission or a review decision on a `WITHDRAWN` sport entry all answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` therefore surfaces only where the status inspected at step 11 and the terminal status inspected here are different dimensions of the same row, as on a medical appeal, whose origin is read from the athlete's `medical_status` while the terminal check reads the athlete's registration dimension. A games edition is deliberately not covered by this check: its post-finalization guard lives at step 26 instead, so an operations checkpoint on an `IMPACT_FINALIZED` edition answers `ERR_IMPACT_ALREADY_FINALIZED` while a second finalization attempt answers `ERR_EDITION_STATE_INVALID` from step 10.
13. The entering athlete's `registration_status` is `REGISTERED` when an entry is accepted — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 409).
14. The venue named for a session slot is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). This guards slot creation, slot rescheduling and slot advancement.
15. The competition result cited by a record claim is `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED` — otherwise `ERR_RESULT_NOT_RECORD_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). This guards both record-claim creation and record ratification.
16. The slot cited by a competition result is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` at submission, and `COMPLETED` at verification — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_NOT_COMPETED` (HTTP 409).
17. The athlete's medical dimension does not block competition — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_MEDICAL_HOLD` (HTTP 422). `medical_status` must not be `SCREENED_HOLD` when an entry is submitted or reviewed, and must be one of `SCREENED_CLEARED`, `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` or `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL` at check-in. The guard therefore covers three operations: sport-entry creation, sport-entry review and athlete check-in.
18. A slot for a high-contact event sits at a venue with a staffed medical bay — otherwise `ERR_MEDICAL_COVERAGE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). This guards slot creation and slot rescheduling.
19. A slot for an older age band sits at a venue with cardiac-grade emergency protocol — otherwise `ERR_EMERGENCY_PROTOCOL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). This guards slot creation and slot rescheduling.
20. Ratification satisfies segregation of duties — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
21. The cited qualifying mark supports the entry — otherwise `ERR_QUALIFYING_RESULT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). This guards sport-entry creation and sport-entry resubmission.
22. The event's age band and the sport's minimum age admit the athlete — otherwise `ERR_AGE_GROUP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This guards sport-entry creation and sport-entry resubmission.
23. An official's certification covers the slot's sport at the required level — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).
24. The person is not already committed in a conflicting window — otherwise `ERR_PERSONNEL_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409).
25. Every pair of an athlete's scheduled blocks is separated by the recovery gap — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). This guards entry acceptance, slot creation and slot rescheduling.
26. The logical clock is strictly before the edition's `registration_deadline_utc` — otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). This guards athlete creation, sport-entry creation, sport-entry resubmission and opening registration.
27. A village stay lies inside the village window and `check_out_date` is strictly after `check_in_date` — otherwise `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_VILLAGE_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
28. The athlete holds no other live entry on the same event — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTRY` (HTTP 409).
29. The candidate slot window leaves a free competition area at the venue — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). This guards slot creation and slot rescheduling.
30. Every night of the candidate stay is below the block's bed capacity — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_NIGHT_FULL` (HTTP 409).
31. The assignment keeps the person's same-date assigned minutes within the daily ceiling — otherwise `ERR_PERSONNEL_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
32. The entry's revision budget is not exhausted — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). This guards a review decision requesting revision and an athlete resubmission.
33. The result has not been protested before — otherwise `ERR_PROTEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422).
34. Seeding has at least one accepted entry to work from — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ENTRIES` (HTTP 422).
35. Seeding meets the event's staging floor — otherwise `ERR_MIN_ENTRIES_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).
36. Uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_SPORT_CODE_TAKEN`, `ERR_ATHLETE_EMAIL_TAKEN`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DUPLICATE` or `ERR_RESULT_ALREADY_RECORDED` (all HTTP 409), guarding sport creation, athlete creation, personnel-assignment creation and competition-result creation respectively.
37. The athlete meets the games-wide minimum age — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_UNDER_MINIMUM_AGE` (HTTP 422).
38. Structural arithmetic on writes holds: an event's `capacity` is at least its `min_entries_to_stage` at creation, and `end_utc` is strictly after `start_utc` on any slot write — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
39. The event has a free entry slot when an entry is accepted — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_FULL` (HTTP 409).
40. The tendered payment equals the settled fee — otherwise `ERR_FEE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
41. The impact ledger is not already frozen — otherwise `ERR_IMPACT_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409). This guards both impact finalization and any further operations checkpoint once `final_impact_cents` is non-null.

The practical consequence of this ordering is that structural and authorisation failures always mask business failures, cross-entity state gates are decided before safety gates, safety gates are decided before eligibility arithmetic, and capacity and fiscal checks are decided last. A screening hold on an athlete therefore surfaces as `ERR_ATHLETE_MEDICAL_HOLD` even when the event is also full, and a closed venue surfaces as `ERR_VENUE_CLOSED` even when the requested window would also have exceeded the venue's competition-area count.

### 2.11. Interval and Boundary Conventions

Four distinct occupancy and conflict models operate in this system, and they do not share boundary semantics. Venue-area occupancy treats a slot's footprint as half-open — the start instant is included, the buffered end instant is excluded — so two slots whose footprints merely touch do not collide, and it compares that footprint against an aggregate per-venue area count. Personnel conflict treats a slot's footprint as closed on both ends, expands it symmetrically by a travel buffer whose size depends on whether the two slots share a venue, and has an implicit capacity of one because a person cannot be in two places; it additionally enforces a separate per-calendar-date minutes ceiling. The multi-sport athlete recovery rule expands nothing at all: it measures the gap between two blocks and requires it to reach a minimum, with a gap exactly equal to the minimum accepted. Bed-night occupancy works at date granularity, half-open on the departure date, and is evaluated night by night rather than against one merged window, so a long stay can fail on a single night in the middle while every other night has room. The exact arithmetic of each model is given with the workflow it governs in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Edition and Its Competition Catalogue

A `GamesAdmin` begins by creating the **games edition** — the container every other row in the system ultimately hangs from. The edition is described by its public `name`, the `host_city` whose economic impact will eventually be reported, and the `host_state_code`, given as exactly two uppercase letters, which is the reference point for residency-based qualification exemptions. Its calendar is fixed by `competition_start_date`, `competition_end_date` and the masters `age_reference_date`, and its registration cut-off by the instant `registration_deadline_utc`. Two inputs are optional: `age_basis`, which selects which of the two age computations drives band assignment and defaults to `REFERENCE_DATE` with `FIRST_COMPETITION_DAY` as the alternative; and `municipal_support_cents`, the cash the host municipality has pledged, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A newly created edition is `DRAFT`, its `ops_epoch` counter reads 0, both `sponsorship_recognized_cents` and `provisional_impact_cents` read 0, and `final_impact_cents` is null.

Before registration can open, the committee has to describe what will actually be contested. A **sport** is registered with a `sport_code` — two to eight uppercase letters, unique across the whole catalogue, with a collision refused as `ERR_SPORT_CODE_TAKEN` — a human-readable `name`, a `contact_class` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and a `mark_direction` of either `LOWER_IS_BETTER` or `HIGHER_IS_BETTER` that determines throughout the system whether a numerically smaller or larger mark is the better performance. Each sport also declares the `required_certification_level` an official must hold to work it, an integer from 0 through 4. Three inputs are optional: `min_age`, the sport-specific minimum basis age, which defaults to 50 and may not be set below 50; and the two age-keyed modification maps, `equipment_profile` and `format_modifications`, which default to empty objects. Those two maps are how age-based adaptation is expressed — an implement weight for the 70-74 band, a shortened heat length for the 80-84 band — and the service treats them as opaque payloads: it stores them and echoes them back on read without interpreting their contents.

A **sport event** is one age band of one division of one sport within one edition. It is created against a `sport_id` and a `games_edition_id`, and carries an `event_code` such as a distance or a doubles designation, an `age_group_code` that is either a five-year band written as two ages joined by a hyphen — the lower bound in the fifties through nineties, the upper bound likewise — or the literal open band `100+`, a `gender_division` of `MEN`, `WOMEN` or `MIXED`, an `entry_fee_cents` figure of zero or more, and a `capacity` of at least one accepted entry. Two inputs are optional: `min_entries_to_stage`, the accepted-entry floor below which the event cannot be staged, which defaults to 3 and must be at least 1; and `national_qualifying_standard`, the mark a verified performance must reach for national qualification, which defaults to null and when null disables national qualification for that event entirely. An event whose `capacity` is smaller than its `min_entries_to_stage` is a contradiction and is refused as a malformed request. A fresh event is `OPEN`, with `accepted_entry_count` at 0, `available_slots` equal to its `capacity`, `current_record_id` null and `seeded_at_utc` null.

With at least one `OPEN` event in place, a `GamesAdmin` may open registration, moving the edition `DRAFT` → `REGISTRATION_OPEN`. The transition requires all three of: the edition currently `DRAFT`, at least one sport event belonging to that edition currently `OPEN`, and a logical clock strictly before `registration_deadline_utc` — a clock already at or past the deadline is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, while an edition that is not `DRAFT` and an edition with no `OPEN` sport event of its own are both refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, decided at step 11 and therefore before the deadline is inspected.

Once entries have accumulated, a `RegistrationOfficer` seeds an event, advancing it `OPEN` → `SEEDED` and stamping `seeded_at_utc` with the current logical clock. Seeding demands both that the event's `accepted_entry_count` has reached its `min_entries_to_stage` — a shortfall is refused with `ERR_MIN_ENTRIES_NOT_MET` — and that the set of entries on the event currently `ACCEPTED` is not empty, an empty set being refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ENTRIES`.

The event's remaining lifecycle is driven by capacity and by the schedule rather than by direct commands. Whenever an accepted entry leaves the `ACCEPTED` state and the resulting `accepted_entry_count` falls below `min_entries_to_stage`, the event moves to `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` — from `OPEN` as `OPEN` → `UNDERSUBSCRIBED`, and from `SEEDED` as `SEEDED` → `UNDERSUBSCRIBED`. Both branches release every session slot of that event that was still `SCHEDULED`, and the seeded branch additionally resets `seeded_at_utc` back to null. Recovery is symmetric but partial: when a waitlist promotion or a fresh acceptance lifts `accepted_entry_count` back to at least `min_entries_to_stage`, the event returns `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` → `OPEN`, closing that cycle — but the session slots released on the way down are not restored and must be created again. An event that has lost its whole schedule to a venue closure moves `SEEDED` → `SCHEDULE_HOLD`, which requires that the event has at least one session slot and that none of its slots is currently `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; rescheduling any one of those displaced slots back into a scheduled state returns the event `SCHEDULE_HOLD` → `SEEDED`. When the first slot of a seeded event actually starts, the event follows it `SEEDED` → `IN_PROGRESS`; when no slot of a running event remains `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `DISPLACED`, the event closes `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETED`.

Cancellation is available only from the undersubscribed state. A `GamesAdmin` supplying a `reason` moves the event `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` → `CANCELLED`, which withdraws every entry on that event still sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MEDICAL_HOLD`, and resets the event's capacity accounting outright — `accepted_entry_count` back to 0 and `available_slots` back to the full `capacity`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are the two terminal states of an event.

### 3.2. Athlete Registration, Age Bands and Fee Settlement

A `GamesAdmin` or a `RegistrationOfficer` registers an **athlete** into an edition by supplying the `games_edition_id`, the athlete's `full_name`, a contact `email`, a `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `residency_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters. Email addresses are unique per edition: a second athlete row pairing the same `games_edition_id` with the same `email` is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_EMAIL_TAKEN`. Residency is the reference point for qualification exemptions — an athlete whose `residency_state_code` differs from the edition's `host_state_code` is an out-of-state competitor and is expected to be entered by organizer invitation on every event they contest. Registration is only accepted while the edition is `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and while the logical clock is strictly before `registration_deadline_utc`.

Athletes carry **two independent status dimensions** that never gate one another mechanically. `registration_status` tracks the commercial and attendance dimension and starts at `PENDING_PAYMENT`, with `REGISTERED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` as its other values and the last two terminal. `medical_status` tracks the clinical dimension and starts at `NOT_SCREENED`, with `SCREENED_CLEARED`, `SCREENED_RESTRICTED`, `SCREENED_HOLD` and `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL` as its other values and none of them terminal. An athlete may perfectly well be `REGISTERED` while still `NOT_SCREENED`; the dimensions only meet where a specific rule names both. A newly created athlete also reads `fee_total_cents` 0, `fee_paid_cents` 0 and an empty `fee_breakdown`.

Two ages are computed for every athlete at creation, and both are persisted. Whole years elapsed between a birth date and a target date are counted as the difference of the two calendar years, reduced by one when the target date's month-and-day pair falls earlier in the year than the birth date's month-and-day pair; a birthday landing exactly on the target date therefore counts as having been reached. `age_at_reference` applies that count from `date_of_birth` to the edition's `age_reference_date`, and `age_at_first_day` applies it from `date_of_birth` to the edition's `competition_start_date`. Which of the two becomes the athlete's **basis age** is decided by the edition's `age_basis`: `REFERENCE_DATE` selects `age_at_reference`, `FIRST_COMPETITION_DAY` selects `age_at_first_day`. The reference-date basis is also always the basis used for record ratification, regardless of what the edition selected for registration.

The basis age then produces `age_group_code`. A basis age below the games-wide floor of 50 is not eligible at all and the registration is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_UNDER_MINIMUM_AGE`. A basis age of 100 or more lands in the open band, written as the literal `100+`. Otherwise the band floor is 50 plus five years for each complete five-year step the basis age sits above 50, and the code is that floor joined by a hyphen to the floor plus four — so a basis age of 67 gives the floor 65 and the code `65-69`. Because the two age computations can straddle a birthday, the choice of `age_basis` is materially observable on the same row: an athlete born on 20 November 1971, in an edition whose `competition_start_date` is 10 September 2026 and whose `age_reference_date` is 31 December 2026, is 54 and lands in `50-54` under the first-competition-day basis but is 55 and lands in `55-59` under the reference-date basis. At the eligibility boundary the same divergence decides admission outright: an athlete who is 49 on the first competition day but turns 50 before the reference date is refused under the first-competition-day basis and admitted under the reference-date basis.

Money enters the picture when the athlete pays. Fee settlement is recomputed from scratch at payment time — never at entry time — and its six steps are evaluated in a pinned order, because the discount tier keys on the number of entries while the surcharge tier keys on the amount the discount itself produces. **Step one** counts the athlete's live entries: those in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED`, which becomes `entry_count`; entries on `MEDICAL_HOLD`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded. **Step two** sums the `entry_fee_cents` of the sport event behind each of those same entries into `event_fees_cents`, in whole cents with no rounding. **Step three** adds the base registration fee — ninety-five dollars, that is 9500 cents — to `event_fees_cents` to give `subtotal_cents`. **Step four** picks the multi-event `discount_pct` from `entry_count`: zero for a single entry or none at all, five percent for two or three entries, twelve percent for four or five entries, and twenty percent for six or more; `discount_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage and rounded half up to whole cents, and this is the only rounding step in the whole settlement; `discounted_cents` is the subtotal less the discount. **Step five** picks `surcharge_cents` from `discounted_cents`: nothing at all below two hundred and fifty dollars, fifteen dollars — 1500 cents — from two hundred and fifty dollars up to but not including six hundred dollars, and forty dollars — 4000 cents — at six hundred dollars and above. **Step six** sets `fee_total_cents` to `discounted_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`, and pins the seven intermediate values into `fee_breakdown` under the keys `entry_count`, `event_fees_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `discount_pct`, `discount_cents`, `discounted_cents` and `surcharge_cents`.

Payment itself is a single tendered figure. An `Athlete` acting on their own registration, or a `GamesAdmin` acting for them, submits `amount_cents`, and the settlement is run; if the tendered `amount_cents` differs by even one cent from the `fee_total_cents` the settlement produces, the request is refused with `ERR_FEE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` and nothing is written. On success the recomputed `fee_total_cents` and `fee_breakdown` are stored, `fee_paid_cents` is set equal to `fee_total_cents`, and the registration dimension advances `PENDING_PAYMENT` → `REGISTERED`. The edition must be `REGISTRATION_OPEN` for the payment to be accepted.

On-site arrival is the next step and it is the one place the two dimensions meet. A `GamesAdmin` checks an athlete in, advancing `REGISTERED` → `CHECKED_IN`, but only when the athlete's `medical_status` is one of `SCREENED_CLEARED`, `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` or `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL` — a `NOT_SCREENED` or `SCREENED_HOLD` athlete is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_MEDICAL_HOLD` — and only while the edition is `REGISTRATION_CLOSED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Check-in also pulls the athlete's accommodation forward: every village stay of that athlete still `RESERVED` whose `check_in_date` is on or before the logical clock's calendar date moves `RESERVED` → `CHECKED_IN`.

Withdrawal is available from three registration states, and the size of the unwind differs by state. From `PENDING_PAYMENT`, withdrawal moves the athlete `PENDING_PAYMENT` → `WITHDRAWN`, withdraws that athlete's entries in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED` — none of which hold event capacity — and cancels every village stay of theirs still `RESERVED`, each cancellation debiting that stay's `nights` from its accommodation block's `reserved_bed_nights`. From `REGISTERED`, withdrawal fires the full five-link cascade: the athlete moves `REGISTERED` → `WITHDRAWN`; every entry of theirs in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MEDICAL_HOLD` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and each entry leaving `ACCEPTED` decrements its event's `accepted_entry_count` by one and increments that event's `available_slots` by one; each such event whose `accepted_entry_count` has fallen below its `min_entries_to_stage` moves to `UNDERSUBSCRIBED`; each event so moved releases every session slot of that event still `SCHEDULED`; and each released slot releases every personnel assignment on it in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED`, each release debiting that person's `assigned_minutes_total` by the assignment's `duration_minutes`. Alongside the five links, every village stay of the athlete in `RESERVED` or `CHECKED_IN` is cancelled, each cancellation debiting that stay's `nights` from the block's `reserved_bed_nights`. From `CHECKED_IN`, withdrawal moves the athlete `CHECKED_IN` → `WITHDRAWN` and fires exactly the same five-link cascade and the same stay cancellations, but it carries one additional precondition: no competition result in `VERIFIED`, `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED` may exist for any entry of that athlete. An athlete whose mark has already been verified has entered the results record and can no longer be unwound. An optional `reason` may accompany any withdrawal.

The fourth exit from registration is not a command at all. An athlete still `PENDING_PAYMENT` when the operations checkpoint observes a logical clock at or past `registration_deadline_utc`, with `fee_paid_cents` short of `fee_total_cents`, moves `PENDING_PAYMENT` → `EXPIRED`; that expiry withdraws every entry of theirs in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MEDICAL_HOLD`, and each entry leaving `ACCEPTED` decrements its event's `accepted_entry_count`.

### 3.3. Qualification, Entry Review and the Waitlist

Most masters events are contested from a qualifying mark rather than open entry, so the committee records those marks first. A **qualifying result** is filed by a `GamesAdmin` or a `RegistrationOfficer` naming the `athlete_id` who produced it, the `sport_id` the meet was contested in, the `event_code` used at that meet, the `mark_value` achieved — which must be strictly greater than zero — the `meet_date` on which the meet was held, a `meet_level` of either `LOCAL` or `STATE`, and a boolean `sanctioned` flag recording whether the state association sanctioned the meet. The row is immutable once written: it is never edited and, as noted in §2.5, it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`.

An **entry** binds an athlete to one sport event. It is filed by the `Athlete` themselves or by a `RegistrationOfficer` and names the `athlete_id`, the `sport_event_id`, and a `qualification_source` of `STATE_QUALIFIER`, `LOCAL_QUALIFIER`, `AGE_EXEMPT` or `ORGANIZER_INVITE`, with `qualifying_result_id` supplied only when a qualifying mark is being cited. Entries begin `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0 and `review_note` null, and two fields are derived on the spot. `qualification_priority` is the rank weight of the source — 1 for `STATE_QUALIFIER`, 2 for `LOCAL_QUALIFIER`, 3 for `AGE_EXEMPT` and 4 for `ORGANIZER_INVITE`, so a lower number is a stronger claim. `qualifying_mark` is copied from the cited qualifying result's `mark_value`, and stays null for `AGE_EXEMPT` and `ORGANIZER_INVITE` entries, which cite no mark at all.

Filing an entry is the most heavily guarded write in the system. The edition must be `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and the logical clock strictly before `registration_deadline_utc`. An `Athlete` may only file for themselves. The athlete's `medical_status` must not be `SCREENED_HOLD`. The athlete must not already hold another entry on the same `sport_event_id` in any of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MEDICAL_HOLD`, a second live entry being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTRY`. And the citation itself must hold together: `qualifying_result_id` must be supplied exactly when `qualification_source` is `STATE_QUALIFIER` or `LOCAL_QUALIFIER`, and must be absent exactly when it is `AGE_EXEMPT` or `ORGANIZER_INVITE`; the cited result must belong to the entering athlete, must have been contested in the same sport as the target event, must carry an `event_code` equal to the target event's `event_code`, must have `sanctioned` true, and must have `meet_level` of `STATE` when the source is `STATE_QUALIFIER` while a `LOCAL_QUALIFIER` source accepts either `LOCAL` or `STATE`. Any failure in that citation cluster is refused with `ERR_QUALIFYING_RESULT_INVALID`.

Age fit is checked separately. The target event's `age_group_code` must equal the athlete's computed `age_group_code`, and the sport's `min_age` must be no greater than the athlete's basis age; an `AGE_EXEMPT` entry additionally demands a basis age of at least 75, the age-exemption floor. Any of those three failures is refused with `ERR_AGE_GROUP_MISMATCH`.

Review is the `RegistrationOfficer`'s desk work, driven by a single `decision` field with five admissible values and an optional `review_note` that is stored on the entry by every decision that carries one. `TAKE_UP` moves the entry `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` and requires the target event to be `OPEN` or `UNDERSUBSCRIBED`. `ACCEPT` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW` → `ACCEPTED`, and is the transition that consumes capacity: it requires the athlete's `registration_status` to be `REGISTERED` — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_NOT_REGISTERED` — the athlete's `medical_status` to be one of `SCREENED_CLEARED`, `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` or `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL`, the event's `available_slots` to be greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_FULL` — and the athlete's already-scheduled competition blocks to still satisfy the recovery-gap rule described in §3.5. On success it stores the `review_note`, increments the event's `accepted_entry_count` by one, decrements the event's `available_slots` by one, and lifts the event `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` → `OPEN` when the acceptance brings `accepted_entry_count` up to `min_entries_to_stage`. `WAITLIST` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW` → `WAITLISTED` and stores the `review_note`, consuming no capacity. `REJECT` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REJECTED` and stores the `review_note`, which is terminal. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` and stores the `review_note`, but only while the entry's `revision_round` is still below the two-round revision budget; a third request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

The revision cycle closes on the athlete's side. An `Athlete` answering a revision request resubmits with a corrected `qualification_source` and, where the new source demands it, a `qualifying_result_id`; the entry moves `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, the two supplied fields overwrite what was there, `qualification_priority` and `qualifying_mark` are recomputed from the new source and citation, and `revision_round` is incremented by one. Resubmission requires `revision_round` still below the budget of two — a third resubmission is likewise refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — and requires the logical clock strictly before `registration_deadline_utc`. The whole loop `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED` may therefore run at most twice before the entry has to be decided on its merits.

Withdrawal of a single entry is available to the `Athlete` or to a `RegistrationOfficer`, takes an optional `reason`, and is admissible from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `MEDICAL_HOLD`. From the four states that hold no capacity the move to `WITHDRAWN` is bookkeeping only. From `ACCEPTED` it is not: the withdrawal decrements the event's `accepted_entry_count` by one, increments the event's `available_slots` by one, and drives the event to `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` when `accepted_entry_count` has fallen below `min_entries_to_stage`; it also carries the precondition that no competition result in `VERIFIED`, `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED` cites the entry.

Freed capacity is filled by promotion, not by luck, and the promotion order is fully determined. At each operations checkpoint the sport events of the edition whose status is `OPEN`, `SEEDED` or `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` and whose `available_slots` is greater than zero are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Within each event, the candidate set is that event's entries currently `WAITLISTED` whose athlete is `REGISTERED` and whose athlete's `medical_status` is one of `SCREENED_CLEARED`, `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` or `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL`. Candidates are then ranked ascending on a three-part key, compared part by part in order. The first part is `qualification_priority`, so a state qualifier outranks a local qualifier, which outranks an age exemption, which outranks an organizer invitation. The second part is the mark: where the sport's `mark_direction` is `LOWER_IS_BETTER` the smaller `qualifying_mark` ranks ahead, where it is `HIGHER_IS_BETTER` the larger `qualifying_mark` ranks ahead, and entries whose `qualifying_mark` is null sort after every entry that has one. The third and final tie-break is the entry's own `created_at`, ascending — never its `id`. Candidates are promoted in that rank order while the event still has `available_slots` greater than zero; each promotion moves the entry `WAITLISTED` → `ACCEPTED`, increments `accepted_entry_count` by one, decrements `available_slots` by one, and lifts the event `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` → `OPEN` when the floor is reached. An event with no eligible candidate is simply skipped: that is not an error, and the checkpoint carries on.

### 3.4. Medical Screening, Risk Bands and Appeals

Masters competition carries real cardiac risk, so the games medical director screens participants before they compete. A `MedicalDirector` records a **screening** naming the `athlete_id`, their own operator string as `screened_by_user_id`, and three vitals: `systolic_bp`, the resting systolic blood pressure in mmHg, accepted only in the range 60 through 260; `resting_hr`, the resting heart rate in bpm, accepted only in the range 30 through 200; and `prior_cardiac_event`, a boolean recording whether the athlete reports a previous cardiac event. The screening row is stamped with `screened_at_utc` from the logical clock and is then immutable — like a qualifying result it carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Three values are derived at screening time. `contact_sport_count` snapshots how much high-contact competition the athlete is signed up for: it counts the athlete's entries in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED` whose event belongs to a sport whose `contact_class` is `HIGH`. `risk_score` is a composite of four point terms scaled by a cardiac multiplier. `recommendation` is a band read off the score.

The four point terms are these. **Age points** take the athlete's `age_at_reference` — always the reference-date age, whatever the edition's `age_basis` says — subtract the age floor of 55, treat any negative result as zero, multiply by one and a half points per year, and cap the term at 30 points, which is reached at 75 years of age. **Blood-pressure points** contribute nothing while `systolic_bp` is below 130, contribute 12 points once `systolic_bp` is at least 130 and still below 150, and contribute 25 points once `systolic_bp` reaches 150. **Heart-rate points** take `resting_hr`, subtract the floor of 70 bpm, treat any negative result as zero, and multiply by four tenths of a point per bpm, with no cap. **Load points** multiply `contact_sport_count` by 7 points per high-contact entry and cap the term at 20 points, so a third and fourth high-contact entry add nothing beyond the third's partial contribution to the ceiling. The four terms are summed, and the sum is multiplied by 1.6 when `prior_cardiac_event` is true and by 1.0 when it is false. That product is then clamped so it can never exceed 100.0 and rounded half up to one decimal place to give `risk_score`. Nothing else participates: earlier screenings of the same athlete are not inputs, and a second screening is computed from the current vitals and current entry set alone.

`recommendation` follows directly from `risk_score`: below 25.0 it is `CLEARED`; from 25.0 up to but not including 60.0 it is `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTION`; at 60.0 and above it is `HOLD_PENDING_PHYSICIAN_REVIEW`. Each recommendation drives the athlete's medical dimension out of `NOT_SCREENED`, and the three branches differ sharply in what they do to the athlete's entries.

A `CLEARED` recommendation moves the athlete `NOT_SCREENED` → `SCREENED_CLEARED` and touches nothing else; the athlete's entries stand as they are. A `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTION` recommendation moves the athlete `NOT_SCREENED` → `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` and pulls back only their high-contact commitments: every entry of that athlete currently `ACCEPTED` whose event belongs to a sport whose `contact_class` is `HIGH` moves `ACCEPTED` → `MEDICAL_HOLD`, and each such move decrements that event's `accepted_entry_count` by one, increments that event's `available_slots` by one, and drives the event to `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` when `accepted_entry_count` falls below `min_entries_to_stage`. A `HOLD_PENDING_PHYSICIAN_REVIEW` recommendation moves the athlete `NOT_SCREENED` → `SCREENED_HOLD` and pulls back everything: every entry of that athlete currently `ACCEPTED`, regardless of contact class, moves `ACCEPTED` → `MEDICAL_HOLD`, and each such move decrements that event's `accepted_entry_count` by one, increments that event's `available_slots` by one, and drives the event to `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` when `accepted_entry_count` falls below `min_entries_to_stage`.

An athlete who believes a hold or a restriction is wrong may have it reviewed, and the `MedicalDirector` rules on the appeal with a `decision` of either `GRANT` or `DENY` plus an optional `note`. A granted appeal from a hold moves the athlete `SCREENED_HOLD` → `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL` and requires that at least one screening exists for that athlete. A granted appeal from a restriction moves the athlete `SCREENED_RESTRICTED` → `CLEARED_BY_APPEAL`. Both grants restore the athlete's suspended entries the same way: every entry of that athlete currently `MEDICAL_HOLD` moves `MEDICAL_HOLD` → `ACCEPTED` where its event's `available_slots` is greater than zero, each such restoration incrementing that event's `accepted_entry_count` by one and decrementing its `available_slots` by one, while an entry whose event has `available_slots` at zero moves `MEDICAL_HOLD` → `WAITLISTED` instead and waits for the promotion pass to reach it. That closes the `ACCEPTED` → `MEDICAL_HOLD` → `ACCEPTED` cycle through the medical dimension. A denied appeal from a hold leaves the athlete in `SCREENED_HOLD` — the dimension is genuinely unchanged — but the decision is nonetheless recorded as a mutation of the athlete row, refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`.

### 3.5. Venues, Competition Blocks and Schedule Disruption

A **venue** is created by a `GamesAdmin` against a `games_edition_id` with a `name`, a `competition_area_count` of at least one — the number of independently usable fields of play, which is the venue's aggregate concurrency capacity — and a `spectator_capacity` of zero or more, which is carried into the economic impact packet rather than used by any gate. Each venue declares the emergency tier in force on site as `emergency_protocol_code`, one of `EP_BASIC`, `EP_ADVANCED` or `EP_CARDIAC`, and may optionally declare `has_onsite_medical_bay`, which defaults to false. Venues start `OPEN` with `closed_reason` null; `CLOSED` is terminal.

A **session slot** is one competition block: one event, at one venue, over one interval. It names its `venue_id` and its `sport_event_id` and is bounded by `start_utc`, which is inclusive, and `end_utc`, which is exclusive and must be strictly later than `start_utc`. Two inputs are optional: `teardown_buffer_minutes`, the minutes of field-of-play teardown appended to the block when venue occupancy is tested, which defaults to 15 and is accepted only in the range 0 through 120; and `required_official_count`, the number of confirmed official assignments the slot needs before it may start, which defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. A new slot is `SCHEDULED` with `confirmed_official_count` at 0.

Placing a slot runs four independent gates beyond the basic requirement that the named venue be `OPEN`. The **medical-coverage gate** refuses a slot whose event belongs to a sport whose `contact_class` is `HIGH` unless the venue's `has_onsite_medical_bay` is true, answering `ERR_MEDICAL_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`. The **emergency-protocol gate** looks at the lower bound of the event's `age_group_code` — 75 for `75-79`, and 100 for the open band `100+` — and refuses any slot whose event's band floor is 75 or above unless the venue's `emergency_protocol_code` is exactly `EP_CARDIAC`, answering `ERR_EMERGENCY_PROTOCOL_INSUFFICIENT`. The **venue-area gate** and the **athlete recovery gate** are arithmetic and are set out below.

Venue-area occupancy asks whether the venue still has a free field of play across the candidate block. A slot's occupied window runs from its `start_utc`, included, to its `end_utc` advanced by its `teardown_buffer_minutes`, excluded — the buffer extends the end only and is never subtracted from the start. Two windows intersect when the first begins strictly before the second ends and the second begins strictly before the first ends, which means windows that merely touch at an endpoint do not intersect: a block ending at exactly the instant another begins is legal. The candidate window is built the same way from the requested `start_utc`, `end_utc` and `teardown_buffer_minutes`. The service then counts the slots at the requested `venue_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose occupied window intersects the candidate window, excluding — on a reschedule — the slot being moved from its own comparison. If that count has already reached the venue's `competition_area_count`, there is no free area and the request is refused with `ERR_VENUE_AREA_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Slots that are `DISPLACED`, `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` occupy no area at all.

The athlete recovery gate protects the multi-sport competitor, and it neither expands intervals nor counts capacity — it measures the distance between blocks. For the athlete in question, gather the session slots whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose sport event is an event on which that athlete holds an `ACCEPTED` entry; entries that are `WAITLISTED`, on `MEDICAL_HOLD` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute no slots. Then take every unordered pair of two distinct slots from that set and measure the gap in whole minutes from the earlier of the two blocks' `end_utc` values to the later of the two blocks' `start_utc` values. A negative gap means the two blocks overlap outright. If any pair's gap is less than the recovery minimum of half an hour, the request is refused with `ERR_ATHLETE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`; a gap of exactly thirty minutes is acceptable. This gate is evaluated when an entry is accepted, and also when a slot is created or rescheduled for an event that already holds accepted entries — in the latter case for every `ACCEPTED` entry of that event.

Slots are driven forward by a `GamesAdmin` supplying a `to_status` of either `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`. Starting a block moves it `SCHEDULED` → `IN_PROGRESS` and requires the slot's venue to be `OPEN`, the slot's sport event to be `SEEDED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, the slot's `confirmed_official_count` to have reached its `required_official_count`, and the logical clock to be at or past the slot's `start_utc`. A slot that is short of officials or whose start instant has not yet arrived cannot be started. On success the slot's event follows it `SEEDED` → `IN_PROGRESS` if it was still merely seeded. Closing a block moves it `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETED` and settles that block's staffing: every personnel assignment on the slot that was `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED` → `COMPLETED`, and every assignment that was still merely `ASSIGNED` moves `ASSIGNED` → `NO_SHOW`, each no-show incrementing that person's `no_show_count` by one and decrementing their `assigned_minutes_total` by the assignment's `duration_minutes`. Closing the block also closes its event `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETED` when no sibling slot of that event remains `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `DISPLACED`.

Two forces take slots off the schedule involuntarily. When a slot's event falls `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` or is cancelled, each of that event's still-`SCHEDULED` slots moves `SCHEDULED` → `RELEASED`, freeing the venue-area interval it held; every personnel assignment on that slot in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`, each release debiting that person's `assigned_minutes_total` by the assignment's `duration_minutes`; and the slot's `confirmed_official_count` is reset to 0. When the venue itself closes, the disruption is broader. A `GamesAdmin` closing a venue supplies a `reason`, which is stored as the venue's `closed_reason` as the venue moves `OPEN` → `CLOSED`, and three links then fire. First, every slot at that venue whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `DISPLACED`. Second, each displaced slot releases every personnel assignment on it in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED`, and each of those releases debits that person's `assigned_minutes_total` by the assignment's `duration_minutes`; a slot displaced from `SCHEDULED` additionally resets its `confirmed_official_count` to 0, while a slot displaced out of `IN_PROGRESS` releases its assignments and debits the minutes but leaves `confirmed_official_count` as it stood. Third, each displaced slot re-evaluates its sport event, and an event whose slot set no longer contains any `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` slot moves `SEEDED` → `SCHEDULE_HOLD`.

Recovery from displacement is explicit. A `GamesAdmin` reschedules a `DISPLACED` slot by supplying a new `venue_id`, a new `start_utc` and a new `end_utc`, with an optional `teardown_buffer_minutes`. The target venue must be `OPEN`, `end_utc` must be strictly later than `start_utc`, and the new interval must clear the venue-area gate at the target venue — with the slot being moved excluded from its own comparison set — the medical-coverage gate, the emergency-protocol gate and the athlete recovery gate for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the slot's event. On success the slot's `venue_id`, `start_utc` and `end_utc` are overwritten, the slot moves `DISPLACED` → `SCHEDULED`, and the slot's event returns `SCHEDULE_HOLD` → `SEEDED` if it was on hold. That closes the `SCHEDULED` → `DISPLACED` → `SCHEDULED` cycle — but the personnel released when the slot was displaced are not restored, and the slot must be staffed again from scratch.

### 3.6. Officials, Volunteers and Staffing a Block

Competition workforce is recruited per edition. A `GamesAdmin` registers a person against a `games_edition_id` with a `full_name` and a `personnel_type` of either `OFFICIAL` or `VOLUNTEER`, and may supply a `certification_level`, an integer from 0 through 4 that defaults to 0, together with `certified_sport_codes`, a list of the sport codes that person is badged to officiate, which defaults to empty. Two ledgers accompany every person and both start at zero: `assigned_minutes_total`, the sum of the `duration_minutes` of that person's assignments whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`; and `no_show_count`, the number of that person's assignments that ended in `NO_SHOW`.

An **assignment** binds one person to one session slot. A `GamesAdmin` creates it by naming `personnel_id`, `session_slot_id` and the `role_at_event` that person will fill, one of `CHIEF_JUDGE`, `JUDGE`, `TIMER`, `MARSHAL` or `RUNNER`. The service derives `duration_minutes` from the slot's own interval — the whole minutes from the slot's `start_utc` to its `end_utc`, with the teardown buffer playing no part — and credits the person's `assigned_minutes_total` by that figure. The new row is `ASSIGNED`. The person and the slot must belong to the same edition; a person may not hold two live assignments on the same slot, so the pair of `personnel_id` and `session_slot_id` must be unique across assignments whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_DUPLICATE`.

Officials must be qualified for the work. When the person's `personnel_type` is `OFFICIAL`, their `certification_level` must be at least the `required_certification_level` of the sport that owns the slot's event, and that sport's `sport_code` must appear in their `certified_sport_codes`; failing either half is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. Volunteers are exempt from both halves of that gate — a volunteer with level 0 and an empty badge list may be assigned to any block — but volunteers do not count toward a slot's confirmed official coverage.

Nobody can be in two places at once, so the assignment gate also runs a conflict scan whose capacity is always one. Look at each of the target person's existing assignments whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose slot is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; assignments that are `RELEASED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, and assignments hanging off slots in any other state, are ignored. For each such existing slot, decide the travel buffer: it is zero minutes when that slot is at the same `venue_id` as the candidate slot, and three quarters of an hour when the two are at different venues. Expand that existing slot symmetrically — its low bound is its `start_utc` moved earlier by the buffer, its high bound is its `end_utc` moved later by the buffer — and then compare inclusively on both ends: there is a collision when the expanded low bound is at or before the candidate slot's `end_utc` and the candidate slot's `start_utc` is at or before the expanded high bound. Because the comparison is inclusive, two same-venue blocks that merely touch — one ending exactly when the next begins — do collide here even though they were perfectly legal for the venue's own area count. Any collision is refused with `ERR_PERSONNEL_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

A second, independent test caps how much a person may work in one day. Take the UTC calendar date on which the candidate slot's `start_utc` falls, sum the `duration_minutes` of that person's assignments whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose slots start on that same UTC date, and add the candidate slot's own `duration_minutes`. If the total exceeds the daily ceiling of eight hours of assigned time — four hundred and eighty minutes — the request is refused with `ERR_PERSONNEL_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A total that lands exactly on the ceiling is allowed. Released, completed and no-show assignments contribute nothing to this sum, so releasing a person from one block genuinely frees their day.

Assignments are accepted by the person who holds them, not by the administrator who created them. A `SportOfficial` confirms their own assignment, and the confirmation is refused unless the caller's `X-User-ID` is the assignment's own `personnel_id` — a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The assignment must still be `ASSIGNED` and its slot must still be `SCHEDULED`, so confirmations close the moment a block starts. Confirming moves the assignment `ASSIGNED` → `CONFIRMED` and, when the person's `personnel_type` is `OFFICIAL`, increments the slot's `confirmed_official_count` by one, which is the counter the slot's own start gate reads.

Releases and completions unwind the two ledgers with exact symmetry. A `GamesAdmin` may release an assignment, optionally recording a `reason`; the same release also happens as a consequence of a slot being released or displaced. Releasing an `ASSIGNED` assignment moves it to `RELEASED` and debits the person's `assigned_minutes_total` by that assignment's `duration_minutes`. Releasing a `CONFIRMED` assignment does the same and additionally decrements the slot's `confirmed_official_count` by one when the person is an `OFFICIAL`; it further requires that the slot not be `IN_PROGRESS`, so a confirmed official cannot be pulled out of a block that is already under way — unless the release arrives as part of the venue-close cascade, which overrides that protection because the block is being abandoned anyway. When a block closes normally, a `CONFIRMED` assignment moves to `COMPLETED` and the person's `assigned_minutes_total` is deliberately left alone, because completed service still counts as minutes served; an assignment still sitting at `ASSIGNED` when the block closes moves to `NO_SHOW`, which raises `no_show_count` by one and debits `assigned_minutes_total` by that assignment's `duration_minutes`, since minutes that were never worked should not appear on the ledger. `COMPLETED`, `RELEASED` and `NO_SHOW` are all final; an assignment in any of them admits no further action.

### 3.7. Marks, Protests and Age-Group Records

A **competition result** is one athlete's mark in one block. The officiating `SportOfficial` records it by naming the `sport_entry_id` it belongs to, the `session_slot_id` in which it was contested, their own `submitted_by_personnel_id`, the `mark_value` — a positive figure whose unit is the sport's own `mark_unit` and whose better direction is the sport's `mark_direction` — and the `placement`, a finishing position of one or better. The recording official may only file marks under their own identity: `submitted_by_personnel_id` must match the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The cited block must actually have been contested, meaning its status is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` when the mark is filed — anything earlier answers `ERR_SLOT_NOT_COMPETED`. One entry may hold only one live mark: at most one result per `sport_entry_id` may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED`, and a second filing is refused with `ERR_RESULT_ALREADY_RECORDED`. A fresh mark is `SUBMITTED` with `verified_by_user_id` null, `national_qualifying` false and `protest_round` at zero.

Verification is where the workflow forks, and both prongs require the block to have finished — the cited slot's status must be `COMPLETED`, not merely under way, or the verification answers `ERR_SLOT_NOT_COMPETED`. A `RegistrationOfficer` verifies the mark, and the service compares it against the standing age-group record of the event: the record branch applies when the event's `current_record_id` is null, so that no record yet stands, or when the mark is better than the `mark_value` of the record named by `current_record_id` judged by the sport's `mark_direction` — smaller is better where the direction is `LOWER_IS_BETTER`, larger is better where it is `HIGHER_IS_BETTER`. On that branch the mark moves `SUBMITTED` → `RECORD_PENDING`, which makes it eligible for a record claim but explicitly does not publish it. The non-record branch applies when the mark does not beat the standing record, and it moves the mark `SUBMITTED` → `VERIFIED` and confers no record eligibility. Both branches stamp `verified_by_user_id` with the verifying caller's `X-User-ID` and both settle `national_qualifying`.

National qualification is decided independently of records, against the event's own `national_qualifying_standard`. When that standard is null the event has no national pathway and `national_qualifying` is false. Otherwise the sport's `mark_direction` decides the comparison: under `LOWER_IS_BETTER` the flag is true when the `mark_value` is at or below the standard, and under `HIGHER_IS_BETTER` it is true when the `mark_value` is at or above the standard. Meeting the standard exactly qualifies in both directions. The flag stays false for as long as a mark is merely `SUBMITTED`; it is only computed at verification, on both branches alike.

Publication is the join point where the fork converges. A `RegistrationOfficer` publishes the mark, moving `VERIFIED` → `PUBLISHED` with no further condition. The record branch is stricter: a `RECORD_PENDING` mark may only move `RECORD_PENDING` → `PUBLISHED` when a record claim citing it exists in `PENDING_RATIFICATION` or `RATIFIED`, so a record-beating mark cannot appear in the published results until the record paperwork has at least been filed.

Athletes may dispute their own mark exactly once. The athlete supplies the `grounds` for the dispute, and the service refuses the protest unless the caller's `X-User-ID` is the `athlete_id` of the entry behind the mark. The mark's `protest_round` must still be zero; a second protest is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_LIMIT_REACHED`, and that rule — not the terminal-state check — is what ultimately locks a published result down, because `PUBLISHED` is a settled but re-openable state rather than a final one. A `VERIFIED` mark moves `VERIFIED` → `PROTESTED` with no collateral effect. A `PUBLISHED` mark moves `PUBLISHED` → `PROTESTED` and additionally pulls down the paperwork built on it: every record claim citing that mark which is still `PENDING_RATIFICATION` moves to `REJECTED`.

A `RegistrationOfficer` resolves the protest with a `decision`. A decision of `REMEASURE` accepts a corrected `mark_value` and `placement`, rewrites both onto the result, returns it `PROTESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, resets `verified_by_user_id` to null and `national_qualifying` to false so the mark must be verified again from scratch, and increments `protest_round` by one. That closes the loop from `SUBMITTED` through verification and protest back to `SUBMITTED`, and because the protest gate demands a `protest_round` of zero, the loop can be travelled at most once. A decision of `VOID` moves the mark `PROTESTED` → `VOIDED`, which is genuinely final, and unwinds everything derived from it: every record claim citing the mark in `PENDING_RATIFICATION` or `RATIFIED` moves to `REJECTED`, and where a `RATIFIED` claim is destroyed this way, the event's `current_record_id` is reset to that claim's `superseded_record_id`, so the record book falls back to whatever stood before.

An **age-group record claim** is filed by a `RecordsRatifier` against a record-eligible mark. The claim names its `sport_event_id`, the `competition_result_id` it cites, the `claimed_by_user_id` of the person filing it, and an `expected_sport_event_version` that must match the event's current `version` — a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_SPORT_EVENT_VERSION`, and a successful filing increments that event's `version`. The cited mark must be `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED`, and citing anything else is refused with `ERR_RESULT_NOT_RECORD_ELIGIBLE`. The service derives the rest of the row rather than trusting the client: `holder_athlete_id` from the athlete behind the cited mark's entry, `age_group_code` copied from the event, `mark_value` copied from the cited mark, and `superseded_record_id` set to whatever record the event's `current_record_id` currently names — the record this claim would displace — or null when no record yet stands. The claim starts `PENDING_RATIFICATION` with `ratified_by_user_id` null.

Ratification is separated from both claiming and verifying, because no single person should be able to manufacture a record end to end. A `RecordsRatifier` rules on the claim with a `decision`. Under `RATIFY` the cited mark must still be `RECORD_PENDING` or `PUBLISHED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be neither the claim's own `claimed_by_user_id` nor the `verified_by_user_id` of the cited mark; either coincidence is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the claim moves `PENDING_RATIFICATION` → `RATIFIED`, `ratified_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting caller, the event's `current_record_id` is repointed at this claim, and the prior record named by this claim's `superseded_record_id`, where that pointer is not null, moves `RATIFIED` → `SUPERSEDED`. Under `REJECT` the claim moves `PENDING_RATIFICATION` → `REJECTED` and `ratified_by_user_id` records the rejecting caller — which is precisely how a deliberate rejection is distinguishable afterwards from a claim swept away by the protest or void cascade, since a cascade-driven rejection leaves `ratified_by_user_id` null.

Two of the record states are final and neither admits any further action: `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED`. `RATIFIED`, by contrast, is not final. A record that has been superseded by a later ratification can even be revived: when the mark behind a `RATIFIED` record is voided, that record moves `RATIFIED` → `REJECTED`, the event's `current_record_id` is set back to that record's `superseded_record_id`, and the record named by that pointer, where it is not null, moves back out of `SUPERSEDED` and into `RATIFIED` — the record book restores the entry that the discredited mark had displaced.

### 3.8. The Athlete Village

Housing is organised into **accommodation blocks**, each created by a `GamesAdmin` against a `games_edition_id` with a `name`, a `bed_capacity` of at least one bed, a `nightly_rate_cents` of zero or more, and an optional `accessible_units` count of zero or more that records how many of the block's units are step-free. A block carries one ledger, `reserved_bed_nights`, which starts at zero and holds the sum of the `nights` of every stay in that block whose status is `RESERVED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`.

A **village stay** is one athlete's booking in one block. It is reserved either by the athlete themselves or by a `GamesAdmin`, naming `athlete_id`, `accommodation_block_id`, a `check_in_date`, a `check_out_date` strictly later than the check-in date, and an `expected_block_version` that must equal the block's current `version`; a stale figure is refused with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION`, and a successful reservation increments the block's `version`. The stay must sit inside the village window, which opens two days before the edition's `competition_start_date` and closes two days after its `competition_end_date`: both the `check_in_date` and the `check_out_date` must fall within that inclusive span, and a booking that runs outside it in either direction is refused with `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_VILLAGE_WINDOW`.

Bed capacity is tested one night at a time rather than against a single merged window, which means a long booking can fail on a single congested night even though every other night is wide open. Enumerate the nights of the candidate stay: each calendar date from the `check_in_date`, included, up to but not including the `check_out_date` — so the night of departure is never occupied and a stay of one night touches exactly one date. For each such date, count the existing stays on the same `accommodation_block_id` whose status is `RESERVED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED` and whose own interval covers that date under the same rule, its check-in date at or before the date and its check-out date strictly after it. If on any single one of the candidate's nights that count has already reached the block's `bed_capacity`, the reservation is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NIGHT_FULL`. Cancelled stays occupy nothing and are invisible to this count.

Where the booking is accepted, the service derives the rest: `nights` is the whole number of days from the `check_in_date` to the `check_out_date`, `total_charge_cents` is that night count multiplied by the block's `nightly_rate_cents` — snapshotted at reservation so a later change to the block's nightly rate never rewrites an existing stay's charge — and the block's `reserved_bed_nights` is increased by the same night count. The stay opens `RESERVED`.

Stays advance in step with the athlete and the calendar. When an athlete checks in to the games, each of their stays that is still `RESERVED` and whose `check_in_date` has arrived — meaning it is at or before the logical clock's date — moves `RESERVED` → `CHECKED_IN`; the block's `reserved_bed_nights` is deliberately unchanged, because a reserved bed and an occupied bed both consume the same bed-nights. When the clock's date reaches or passes a `CHECKED_IN` stay's `check_out_date`, the ops-run checkpoint closes it `CHECKED_IN` → `COMPLETED`, again leaving `reserved_bed_nights` untouched, since the nights were genuinely consumed. Cancellation is the only path that gives capacity back: either the athlete or a `GamesAdmin` may cancel a `RESERVED` or a `CHECKED_IN` stay with an optional `reason`, and the athlete-withdrawal cascade cancels stays the same way. A cancellation debits the block's `reserved_bed_nights` by exactly the stay's own `nights` — the precise compensating action of the reservation — and zeroes the stay's `total_charge_cents`, so a cancelled booking bills nothing. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both final and admit no further action.

### 3.9. Checkpoints, Sponsorship Recognition and Economic Impact

The games edition is advanced by a **checkpoint run**, invoked by a `GamesAdmin`, and its work is done in a pinned order that the implementation must reproduce exactly, because each phase reads state the previous phase has already changed. First, the edition's `ops_epoch` is incremented by one, stamping the run. Second, unpaid registrations are expired: every athlete still in `PENDING_PAYMENT` past the edition's `registration_deadline_utc` moves to `EXPIRED`, releasing that athlete's live entries and their held capacity. Third, waitlisted entries are promoted into whatever capacity now exists, event by event and candidate by candidate in the promotion ranking. Fourth, village stays whose departure date has arrived are completed. Fifth, the edition's own status is advanced — by at most one step, however far the clock has run. Sixth, and only now that heads and stays have settled, `provisional_impact_cents` is recomputed. The run also increments the edition's `version` by one. Once an edition's `final_impact_cents` has been set, the books are closed and any further checkpoint run is refused with `ERR_IMPACT_ALREADY_FINALIZED`.

The one-step rule in the fifth phase is deliberate and observable. `DRAFT` is never advanced by a checkpoint run at all — only the explicit act of opening registration moves an edition out of `DRAFT`. From `REGISTRATION_OPEN` the run moves the edition to `REGISTRATION_CLOSED` when the logical clock has reached the `registration_deadline_utc`. From `REGISTRATION_CLOSED` it moves the edition to `IN_PROGRESS` when the clock's date has reached the `competition_start_date`. From `IN_PROGRESS` it moves the edition to `COMPLETED` when the clock's date is past the `competition_end_date`, and that closing step also finishes competition on the ground: every session slot still `IN_PROGRESS` anywhere in the edition moves to `COMPLETED`, with all the staffing consequences a block closure carries. Each of those three steps needs its own checkpoint call: an operator who sets the clock far into the future and then runs a single checkpoint gets exactly one advance, not a cascade to `COMPLETED`.

A **sponsorship commitment** is recorded by a `SponsorLiaison` against a `games_edition_id` with a `sponsor_name`, a `sponsor_kind` of `CORPORATE`, `MUNICIPAL` or `FOUNDATION`, a `tier` of `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `PLATINUM`, and a `pledged_cents` of at least one cent. It opens `PLEDGED` with `recognized_cents` at zero and an empty `allocation_map`.

Allocating a commitment converts the pledge into recognised value spread across four programme buckets, and the arithmetic order matters to the cent. The tier sets a recognition multiplier: `BRONZE` recognises the pledge at par, one times the pledged amount; `SILVER` at one and five hundredths; `GOLD` at one and twelve hundredths; and `PLATINUM` at one and a quarter. The four buckets take fixed shares of the pledge and are filled in this declared order: `VENUE_SIGNAGE` takes forty percent, `ATHLETE_SERVICES` thirty percent, `MEDICAL_COVERAGE` twenty percent and `SOCIAL_PROGRAM` the remaining ten percent. Each bucket's figure is computed independently as the `pledged_cents` multiplied by that bucket's share and by the tier multiplier, and each is then rounded to a whole number of cents on its own, with a half cent rounding upward. Only after all four buckets have been rounded are they added together, and that sum — not a separately rounded product of pledge and multiplier — becomes the commitment's `recognized_cents`. Rounding the pledge-times-multiplier total once and splitting it afterwards is not equivalent and will differ from the required figure by a cent or two on many pledges. The four rounded figures are stored as the `allocation_map`, keyed by bucket name, and `recognized_cents` is added to the edition's `sponsorship_recognized_cents` ledger.

Allocation is guarded on both sides. The commitment must still be `PLEDGED`, the edition must not yet be `IMPACT_FINALIZED`, and the caller must supply an `expected_games_edition_version` matching the edition's current `version`, since the allocation writes to the shared edition ledger; a stale figure is refused with `ERR_STALE_GAMES_EDITION_VERSION`. Success moves the commitment `PLEDGED` → `ALLOCATED` and increments both the edition's `version` and the commitment's own `version`. A sponsor may also walk away. Withdrawing a commitment that is still `PLEDGED` moves it `PLEDGED` → `WITHDRAWN` and touches no ledger, because nothing had been recognised. Withdrawing one that is `ALLOCATED` moves it `ALLOCATED` → `WITHDRAWN`, requires the edition not to be `IMPACT_FINALIZED`, debits the edition's `sponsorship_recognized_cents` by exactly the commitment's `recognized_cents`, resets that `recognized_cents` to zero and empties the `allocation_map` back to nothing, and increments both versions. `WITHDRAWN` is final.

Economic impact is projected from heads, nights and spend, and it exists in two variants that share their constants but differ in every input. Four figures are fixed for both: each competing athlete is assumed to bring accompanying visitors at a factor of one point eight heads per athlete; a projected stay is four nights; each head is assumed to spend eighteen thousand five hundred cents per day; and the regional output multiplier applied to direct spend is one point four five.

The provisional variant is what the checkpoint run recomputes into `provisional_impact_cents`. Its head count, reported as `registered_heads`, is the number of athletes in the edition whose `registration_status` is `PENDING_PAYMENT`, `REGISTERED` or `CHECKED_IN` — everyone who has entered and not fallen away. Visitor heads are that count multiplied by one point eight, and this intermediate figure is deliberately left unrounded and may be fractional. Direct spend is the sum of athlete heads and visitor heads, multiplied by the daily spend of eighteen thousand five hundred cents and by the projected four nights, and that product is rounded to whole cents with a half cent going up. `provisional_impact_cents` is then that rounded direct spend multiplied by one point four five and rounded again to whole cents, half up.

The final variant is computed once, when a `SponsorLiaison` finalises the impact of a `COMPLETED` edition, and it replaces projections with observed data. Its head count, reported as `checked_in_heads`, counts only athletes whose `registration_status` is `CHECKED_IN` — nobody who merely registered. Instead of assuming four nights it uses `actual_room_nights`, the sum of the `nights` of every village stay whose accommodation block belongs to this edition and whose status is `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`, accumulated over the stays in ascending `check_in_date` order and, where two stays share a check-in date, in ascending `created_at` order. Nights per head is `actual_room_nights` divided by the head count, or by one where the head count is zero so that the division is always defined; that quotient is kept as an exact fraction and is not rounded before it is used. Visitor heads are again the head count multiplied by one point eight, unrounded. Direct spend is the sum of athlete heads and visitor heads, multiplied by eighteen thousand five hundred cents and by that exact nights-per-head quotient, rounded to whole cents half up; and `final_impact_cents` is that figure multiplied by one point four five, rounded to whole cents half up.

Finalisation is a one-way door. The edition must be `COMPLETED` and its `final_impact_cents` must still be null; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_EDITION_STATE_INVALID`, because the edition is by then `IMPACT_FINALIZED`. The finalising call moves the edition `COMPLETED` → `IMPACT_FINALIZED`, writes `final_impact_cents`, and deliberately leaves `provisional_impact_cents` exactly as the last checkpoint run left it, so the projection and the settled figure remain side by side for comparison. After finalisation the edition accepts no further checkpoint run, no sponsorship allocation and no sponsorship withdrawal.

Both variants, together with the money and head figures behind them, are exposed as a single computed reading on the edition. That reading reports the `provisional_impact_cents` and `final_impact_cents` figures, the `sponsorship_recognized_cents` and `municipal_support_cents` ledgers, and the three underlying counts — `registered_heads`, `checked_in_heads` and `actual_room_nights` — so that the projection can be audited without recomputing it. It is a pure read: it never advances a status, never recomputes a stored ledger and never bumps a version.

Every other operation, including all reads and every action that advances a status, settles money, allocates, releases, ratifies or reschedules an existing resource, returns `200 OK`.
