# Grief & Bereavement Support API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hospice bereavement programme carries a peculiar operational burden: the people it serves arrive at the worst moment of their lives, the services offered to them are scarce and scheduled weeks in advance, and almost every dollar spent is somebody else's restricted grant money that must be accounted for line by line. Today the programme runs on a patchwork of spreadsheets — one for the intake queue, one for the group-session calendar, one for the counselling waitlist, one for volunteer training hours, and a shared drive full of quarterly funder reports that nobody can reproduce six months later. When a counsellor leaves the roster, the referrals assigned to them are silently orphaned. When a support group fails to fill, the money committed to it stays committed. When a family stops answering the six-month anniversary card, nothing escalates.

This document specifies the API that replaces that patchwork. The service tracks a bereaved person from the moment a hospice, a funeral home, or the person themselves files an intake record, through needs assessment and risk tiering, into group cohorts and individual counselling, alongside a trained volunteer companion, and out again through discharge or withdrawal — while a parallel outreach cadence keeps contact at the one-month, three-month, six-month and first-anniversary milestones. Layered over that clinical spine is a fiscal spine: remembrance events and group series draw money from grant awards, allocations are committed forward and settled on what actually happened, and reporting periods roll up those allocations into funder-facing totals that must survive an audit.

Every route described here is mounted beneath `/bereavement`. Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **time is logical, not physical**: no rule in this system reads the wall clock, and every deadline, expiry, cadence window and eligibility date is evaluated against a server-held logical clock that an operator repositions explicitly. Second, **counters are conserved, not recomputed on demand**: a cohort's seats and an award's cents are partitioned pools whose parts must always sum to the declared whole, and every operation that moves a record between states is responsible for moving the corresponding units between pool compartments in the same write.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting as. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else, including before the server looks at whether the addressed resource exists.

The seven roles are `ProgramDirector`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `ReferralSource`, `Counselor`, `VolunteerCompanion`, `Participant`, and `GrantOfficer`. A `ProgramDirector` runs the programme: meeting spaces, cohorts, counsellor roster, volunteer roster, remembrance events, grant money, and the operational sweep. An `IntakeCoordinator` staffs the front door: screening and accepting referrals, recording assessments, working the group and counselling queues, drafting invitations and driving outreach. A `ReferralSource` is a hospice team, a funeral-home partner, or a self-referring bereaved person, and may do exactly one thing — file an intake record. A `Counselor` is a licensed individual-counselling provider. A `VolunteerCompanion` is a trained lay companion. A `Participant` is the bereaved person receiving services. A `GrantOfficer` is a funder-side reviewer who reads the money surface and rules on submitted reports but never writes programme data. When the role named in `X-User-Role` does not appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being addressed (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

Four of the seven roles are **opaque**: `ProgramDirector`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `ReferralSource` and `GrantOfficer`. For these, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity — the server never compares it to any record's `id`. It may still be recorded: when a `ProgramDirector` submits a grant report, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored on that report as `submitted_by_user_id`, and that stored value is later compared against the `X-User-ID` of the `GrantOfficer` who attempts to accept the report.

The remaining three roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the `id` of a persisted record and ownership is checked against it. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant`; ownership checks compare it against the participant named by the path, or against the `participant_id` of the enrollment, invitation or anniversary touchpoint named by the path. When `X-User-Role` is `Counselor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Counselor`, and ownership checks compare it against the `counselor_id` of the counselling referral being acted on. When `X-User-Role` is `VolunteerCompanion`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `VolunteerCompanion`, and ownership checks compare it against the volunteer id in the path, or against the `companion_id` of the companion match being acted on; for sending or acknowledging an anniversary touchpoint, a volunteer qualifies when they are the `companion_id` of an `ACTIVE` companion match whose `participant_id` equals the touchpoint's `participant_id`. Any of these comparisons failing yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403.

One additional authorisation rule stands apart from ownership. Accepting a submitted grant report is a segregation-of-duties boundary: the acting `GrantOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded on the report. If they are equal, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected under the request-shape rule described in §2.5.

Unless a subsection of §3 explicitly nominates a different ordering for a particular collection, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. When a list endpoint responds, its `meta` object carries the four pagination fields alongside the two request-identification fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports how many records matched the filter before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system is identified by a server-minted UUID exposed as `id`. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by a client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the record was first persisted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that record. There is one deliberate exception: an assessment record is an immutable clinical observation. Assessment rows carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because nothing in the system ever modifies an assessment after it is written.

The logical clock deserves careful reading, because it is the single time source in the system. Before an operator ever repositions it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. A `ProgramDirector` repositions it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep — it only moves the reference point. Every deadline, expiry window, cadence due date, eligibility comparison and effective-date test described in this document reads the logical clock and nothing else. Where a rule speaks of "the logical clock date", it means the calendar date component of that instant.

### 2.5. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response must carry the error of the *first* rule that fails, evaluated in the fixed order below. This ordering is part of the contract: an implementation that reports a capacity violation on a request that also carries a bad enum value is wrong, because the request-shape rule sits higher on the ladder. The fifteen rungs, from first-evaluated to last, are:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Resource ownership.** For entity-backed roles acting on a resource they must own, `X-User-ID` matches the owning identifier as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list contains an entity-backed role.
4. **Segregation of duties.** On grant-report acceptance only, the acting `GrantOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. **Path existence.** Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated from left to right, so the leftmost missing segment determines the reported error — otherwise the corresponding not-found error (404). Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
6. **Request shape.** The body conforms to the endpoint's declared input: every required field is present, every enum value is a declared member, every integer lies inside its declared range, and every array-typed field is an array. This rung also carries four conditional shape rules: `source_org_name` is required when `source_type` is anything other than `SELF` and must be omitted or null when `source_type` is `SELF`; an allocation's `cohort_id` is non-null exactly when its `category` is `GROUP_PROGRAM` and its `event_id` is non-null exactly when its `category` is `REMEMBRANCE_EVENT`; a cohort's `series_start_date` must fall on the weekday named by its `weekday` field; and a cohort's `min_viable_enrollment` must not exceed its `seats_total`. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint with a request body.
7. **Body-referenced existence.** Every entity identifier named inside the request body resolves to an existing record — otherwise the corresponding not-found error (404). This rung guards enrollment creation, counselling-referral creation, counselling-referral assignment, companion-match creation, cohort creation, remembrance-event creation, invitation creation, funding-allocation creation and grant-report creation.
8. **Stale-parent rejection.** On the three version-guarded writes described in §2.6, the supplied expected version equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise the rule's declared stale-version error (412).
9. **State legality.** The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared. This rung carries the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and the specific state errors `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SCREENED`, `ERR_COHORT_NOT_ENROLLING`, `ERR_COUNSELOR_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_COMPANION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_GRANT_CLOSED`, `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FUNDABLE`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FUNDABLE`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN` and `ERR_OUTREACH_NOT_DUE`, all at HTTP 409. Applies to every state-transition endpoint.
10. **Clinical safety.** The three safety gates described in §3.4 and §3.6 — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SUICIDALITY_REQUIRES_COUNSELING` and `ERR_COMPANION_TRAINING_INSUFFICIENT`, all 422 — are evaluated here, ahead of every referential, temporal, capacity and fiscal rule. They guard exactly two operations: enrollment creation and companion-match creation.
11. **Cross-entity attribute agreement.** The five matching rules — `ERR_LOSS_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SPECIALTY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_LOSS_TYPE_NOT_ENDORSED`, `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_ALLOWED` and `ERR_ALLOCATION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`, all 422 — guard exactly four operations: enrollment creation, counselling-referral assignment, companion-match creation and funding-allocation creation.
12. **Time geometry.** The five temporal rules — `ERR_SPACE_TIME_CONFLICT` (409), `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_PAST` (422), `ERR_DECEASED_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422), `ERR_OUTSIDE_GRANT_PERIOD` (422) and `ERR_RSVP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) — guard exactly six operations: cohort creation, remembrance-event creation, referral filing, funding-allocation creation, invitation sending and invitation responding.
13. **Capacity and uniqueness.** The twelve constraint rules enumerated in §3.4, §3.5, §3.6, §3.7, §3.8 and §3.9. All of them report HTTP 409 except `ERR_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`, which report 422. They guard exactly ten operations: enrollment creation, counselling-referral assignment, companion-match creation, cohort creation, remembrance-event creation, grant-award creation, grant-report creation, cohort resume, referral filing and assessment recording.
14. **Fiscal sufficiency.** On funding-allocation creation only, the award's `available_cents` is greater than or equal to the `provisional_cents` about to be committed. Equality passes. A shortfall yields `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
15. **Outcome-summary precondition.** Reading a participant's outcome summary requires at least one `BASELINE` assessment on file for that participant; otherwise `ERR_NO_BASELINE_ASSESSMENT` (422).

Because rung 3 precedes rung 5, an entity-backed principal addressing a resource they do not own is told they are not the owner rather than being told the record is missing only when the record exists; when the path names a record that does not exist at all, rung 5 reports the not-found error. Because rung 10 precedes rung 13, a participant with no needs assessment attempting to enrol into a full cohort is told an assessment is required, not that the cohort is full.

### 2.6. Stale-Parent Protection on Contended Writes

Three writes in this system read a parent record's counters, make a decision from them, and then commit against that parent. All three are guarded by an explicit version check so that a client can never commit a decision based on a snapshot it no longer holds.

Creating an enrollment requires a non-optional `expected_cohort_version` field carrying the `version` of the target cohort as the client last observed it. If that value differs from the cohort's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COHORT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — the seat pool the client reasoned about (`seats_open`, `seats_held`, `seats_filled`) has moved, so the hold is refused rather than silently drawn from a pool the client never saw.

Assigning a counselling referral requires a non-optional `expected_counselor_version` field carrying the `version` of the named counsellor as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the counsellor's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COUNSELOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the caseload and weekly-minute figures the client used to pick that counsellor are no longer current.

Creating a funding allocation requires a non-optional `expected_grant_award_version` field carrying the `version` of the target grant award as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the award's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the `available_cents` figure the commitment was sized against has moved.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Where an endpoint is not named above, supplying an expected-version value is neither required nor meaningful.

### 2.7. Conserved Pools, Money and Rounding

Two resources in this system are modelled as strictly partitioned pools, and the partition identity must hold after every single write.

A cohort's seat pool satisfies, at all times, that `seats_total` equals `seats_open` plus `seats_held` plus `seats_filled`. A held enrollment occupies one unit of `seats_held`; a confirmed or attending enrollment occupies one unit of `seats_filled`; every other seat sits in `seats_open`. Every enrollment transition that changes which compartment a seat belongs to must move exactly one unit and leave the sum untouched.

A grant award's money pool satisfies, at all times, that `awarded_cents` equals `available_cents` plus `committed_cents` plus `spent_cents`. `committed_cents` is the sum of `provisional_cents` over that award's allocations in status `COMMITTED`; `spent_cents` is the sum of `settled_cents` over that award's allocations in status `SETTLED`; `available_cents` is what remains after subtracting both from `awarded_cents`.

All monetary quantities in this system are whole cents. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — a value exactly on the half boundary rounds away from zero. Scores and ratios carry their own precision: a normalised assessment score, a composite need index, a counselling queue priority and a companion match score are each rounded half up to two decimal places, while an enrollment's `attendance_ratio` is rounded half up to four decimal places. Where a formula in §3 pins an order of rounding operations, that order is normative and an implementation that rounds at a different point produces a different — and incorrect — answer.

### 2.8. Nested Paths and Not-Found Reporting

Several routes address a child through its parent. Path parameters are resolved strictly from left to right: the parent is looked up first, and if it does not exist the request fails immediately with that parent's not-found error, without any attempt to resolve the remaining segments. Each entity has its own dedicated code — `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNSELOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNSELING_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPANION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_OUTREACH_NOT_FOUND` — all reported at HTTP 404. The same codes are reused when an identifier supplied inside a request body fails to resolve.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

A number of operations in this system reach far beyond the record named in the path. Withdrawing a participant unwinds their enrollments, counselling referrals, companion matches and outreach touchpoints in one write. Deactivating a counsellor returns their whole caseload to the waitlist, which in turn re-tiers participants, which in turn re-channels their outreach. Completing a cohort closes out enrollments, advances participants and settles grant money against the award. Cancelling a cohort or an event releases money back to the award.

Three rules govern all of these. First, a cascade is atomic with its trigger: either the whole chain lands or none of it does. Second, every record touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and — where the entity carries one — its `version` incremented by one, exactly as if it had been written directly. Third, derived counters are recomputed as part of the same write, never lazily: `seats_open`, `seats_held`, `seats_filled`, `active_caseload`, `committed_minutes`, `active_match_count`, `has_active_companion`, `invitations_sent`, `invitations_accepted`, `confirmed_headcount`, `committed_cents`, `spent_cents` and `available_cents` are all expected to be correct in the very response that caused them to change.

### 2.10. The Operational Sweep

Clock-driven transitions do not fire on their own. A `ProgramDirector` advances them by invoking the operational sweep, which walks the whole data set once and applies every overdue clock-driven transition in a **fixed five-step order**: first, held enrollments whose hold deadline has passed are expired; second, sent invitations past their RSVP deadline are expired; third, certified volunteers whose certification has expired are lapsed, with their active matches ended; fourth, scheduled anniversary touchpoints whose due date has arrived are marked due; fifth, sent anniversary touchpoints that have gone unanswered long enough are marked as having received no response. The order matters because later steps may observe records that earlier steps have already moved.

The sweep reports what it did through six counters: `enrollments_expired`, `invitations_expired`, `companions_lapsed`, `matches_ended`, `outreaches_due` and `outreaches_no_response`. Each counts the records that step actually moved during this invocation; a sweep that finds nothing overdue reports zeros across the board and mutates nothing. The precise threshold for each step is stated in the §3 subsection that owns the corresponding workflow.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Intake: From Referral to Participant

A referral is the programme's front door, and it can be pushed through that door from three directions. The `source_type` recorded on an intake record is one of `HOSPICE`, `FUNERAL_HOME` or `SELF`. The first two are institutional referrals and must name the referring organisation in `source_org_name`; a self-referral carries no organisation, and supplying one is a shape violation. Alongside the source, the filer records who died (`decedent_name`), when they died (`date_of_death`, expressed as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form), how the bereaved person stood in relation to them (`relationship_to_deceased`, one of `SPOUSE`, `CHILD`, `PARENT`, `SIBLING`, `OTHER_FAMILY`, `FRIEND` or `CAREGIVER`), the clinical loss category (`loss_type`, one of `ANTICIPATED_ILLNESS`, `SUDDEN_DEATH`, `PERINATAL`, `SUICIDE`, `OVERDOSE` or `PEDIATRIC`), and how to reach the bereaved contact (`contact_name` and `contact_email`). Both an `IntakeCoordinator` and a `ReferralSource` may file one.

Two guards sit on filing. A death cannot be recorded in the future: if `date_of_death` is later than the logical clock date, the filing is refused with `ERR_DECEASED_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. And the same bereavement must not be filed twice: if a referral already exists in status `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED` or `ACCEPTED` carrying the same `contact_email` and the same `date_of_death`, the filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REFERRAL`. A referral that was declined does not block a re-file. A newly filed referral lands in status `RECEIVED` with `screening_band` and `days_since_death` both null, `eligibility_note`, `decline_reason` and `participant_id` all null.

Screening is the `IntakeCoordinator`'s first clinical act, and it moves the intake record `RECEIVED → SCREENED`. It is permitted only from `RECEIVED`, and it re-checks that `date_of_death` is not later than the logical clock date. Screening computes `days_since_death` as the whole number of days between `date_of_death` and the logical clock date, and from that number it bands the referral into `screening_band`. A death within the last month — thirty days or fewer since it occurred — bands as `URGENT`. A death older than that but no more than six months ago, meaning up to and including one hundred and eighty days, bands as `PRIORITY`. Anything older bands as `ROUTINE`. Both thresholds are inclusive: exactly thirty days is still `URGENT`, and exactly one hundred and eighty days is still `PRIORITY`. The coordinator may attach a free-text `eligibility_note` at screening, which is stored on the referral as supplied.

Declining is available from either `RECEIVED` or `SCREENED` and moves the record to `DECLINED`, recording the supplied `decline_reason` — one of `OUT_OF_AREA`, `DUPLICATE`, `DECLINED_SERVICES` or `INELIGIBLE_TIMING`. Accepting, by contrast, is available only from `SCREENED`; attempting it against a referral in any other status is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SCREENED`. Both `ACCEPTED` and `DECLINED` are terminal for a referral: any further transition attempt out of either is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Acceptance is where a referral becomes a person the programme is responsible for. In one write, the intake record moves `SCREENED → ACCEPTED`, a participant record is minted, and the referral's `participant_id` is set to the new participant's `id` so the two remain linked. The new participant carries `referral_id` pointing back at the intake record, and copies four values verbatim from it: `contact_name` becomes the participant's `display_name`, and `loss_type`, `relationship_to_deceased` and `date_of_death` are carried across unchanged. The month-and-day slice of `date_of_death` is stored as `anniversary_month_day` in `MM-DD` form. The participant starts with `service_status` `INTAKE`, `outreach_status` `SCHEDULED`, `risk_tier` `LOW`, `composite_need_index` 0, `outreach_cadence_days` 90, `waitlist_reentry_count` 0, `sessions_attended_total` 0, `has_active_companion` false, and both `baseline_score` and `latest_score` null. The same write also lays down the participant's outreach cadence plan, described in §3.8. Because a person is being created rather than an existing record edited, acceptance answers with HTTP 201.

Intake records can be listed and filtered by `status`, by `source_type`, or by the `participant_id` they produced. Participants can be listed and filtered by the originating `referral_id`, by `service_status`, by `risk_tier` or by `loss_type`.

### 3.2. Assessment, Risk Tiering and Outcome Measurement

Clinical assessments are recorded against a participant by an `IntakeCoordinator` or a `Counselor`, and each one is an immutable observation. An assessment declares its `kind` — `NEEDS`, `BASELINE`, `MIDPOINT` or `EXIT` — and the `instrument` used to collect it, either `ICG_SF` or `PG13`. Three sub-scores are supplied: `grief_severity`, an integer from 0 to 40 inclusive; `isolation_index`, an integer from 0 to 20 inclusive; and `functional_impairment`, an integer from 0 to 20 inclusive. A `suicidality_flag` boolean may be supplied and defaults to false when omitted. The server stamps `recorded_at` from the logical clock and assigns `sequence_index`, a one-based counter of that participant's assessments across all kinds in creation order — the first assessment ever recorded for a participant carries sequence index 1, regardless of its kind. Recording an assessment answers with HTTP 201.

Every assessment, whatever its kind, gets two computed figures. `raw_total` is the sum of the three sub-scores. `normalized_score` expresses that total as a percentage of the eighty points the three sub-scores can jointly reach: divide `raw_total` by eighty, multiply by one hundred, and round half up to two decimal places. Outcome measurement runs on `MIDPOINT` and `EXIT` rows, and both of those require groundwork: recording an assessment of kind `MIDPOINT` or `EXIT` for a participant who has no `BASELINE` assessment on file is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`.

A `NEEDS` assessment does more than record a score — it re-tiers the participant. The composite need index is a weighted blend of the three sub-scores, each first expressed as a fraction of its own maximum: grief severity out of forty carries half the weight, functional impairment out of twenty carries three tenths of the weight, and social isolation out of twenty carries the remaining two tenths. That blend is multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places to give the composite. Only then — never before the rounding — does the safety floor apply: when the assessment's `suicidality_flag` is true, the composite is raised to 85.00 if it fell below that figure, and left alone if it already reached or exceeded it. The result is written to the participant's `composite_need_index`. Exactly one assessment feeds this computation: the `NEEDS` row just recorded. Earlier `NEEDS` rows are not averaged in — the newest one wins outright — and `BASELINE`, `MIDPOINT` and `EXIT` rows never contribute to it at all.

`risk_tier` then follows from the composite on inclusive lower bounds. A composite of 70.00 or above is `HIGH`; a composite of 40.00 or above but below 70.00 is `MODERATE`; anything below 40.00 is `LOW`. A composite of exactly 70.00 is `HIGH`, and a composite of exactly 40.00 is `MODERATE`. The tier immediately drives the follow-up rhythm: `outreach_cadence_days` becomes 90 for a `LOW` tier, 60 for a `MODERATE` tier, and 30 for a `HIGH` tier. And when the recomputed tier lands on `HIGH`, every one of that participant's anniversary touchpoints still sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `DUE` is re-channelled — its `channel` becomes `PHONE` and its `escalated` flag becomes true. Touchpoints already `SENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `NO_RESPONSE` or `CANCELLED` are left exactly as they are.

The service dimension of the participant's status responds to assessments as well. A participant sitting in `INTAKE` moves `INTAKE → ASSESSED` on their first `NEEDS` assessment. A participant already in `ASSESSED`, `ACTIVE` or `MAINTENANCE` who receives another `NEEDS` assessment stays exactly where they are on the service dimension — the transition is a self-loop `ASSESSED → ASSESSED`, re-tiering the person without moving them backwards or forwards through the service pipeline. A `BASELINE` assessment writes its `normalized_score` to the participant's `baseline_score`. A `MIDPOINT` or `EXIT` assessment writes its `normalized_score` to the participant's `latest_score`, and an `EXIT` assessment additionally closes the clinical arc by moving `MAINTENANCE → DISCHARGED`. A participant's assessments can be listed in creation order and filtered by `kind`.

The outcome summary is a computed read that returns both outcome variants side by side, and it is the figure that leaves the building — one variant for families, one for funders. It selects a baseline and a latest observation from the participant's own assessments only. The baseline is the participant's earliest `BASELINE` assessment, ordered by `recorded_at` ascending and, where two share an instant, by `created_at` ascending, taking the first row. The latest is the participant's most recent assessment of kind `MIDPOINT` or `EXIT`, ordered by `recorded_at` descending and, where two share an instant, by `created_at` descending, taking the first row. `NEEDS` rows are excluded from both selections. If no `BASELINE` row exists, the read is refused with `ERR_NO_BASELINE_ASSESSMENT`. If a baseline exists but no `MIDPOINT` or `EXIT` row does, the summary is still returned, with `latest_score`, `raw_delta`, `rtm_correction` and `attendance_adjusted_delta` all null while `attendance_ratio_capped` is still computed and returned.

When both observations exist, three figures are derived in a **pinned order**, and applying them in any other order produces a different and wrong answer. First, `raw_delta` is the latest observation's normalised score minus the baseline observation's normalised score, rounded half up to two decimal places; this is the plain difference clinicians quote to families, with no correction and no weighting. Second, `rtm_correction` accounts for regression to the mean: take the baseline observation's normalised score, subtract the programme mean of 50.0, multiply the difference by 0.15, and round half up to two decimal places. Third, the attendance weight: `attendance_ratio_capped` is the participant's `sessions_attended_total` divided by the eight sessions a full course of the programme is expected to deliver, capped so that it never exceeds 1 — a participant who attended more than eight sessions is treated as having attended exactly the full course. `attendance_adjusted_delta` is then the raw delta minus the regression correction, multiplied by a weight of 0.6 plus 0.4 times the capped attendance ratio, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because the correction is subtracted *before* the attendance weighting is applied, a participant who attended half the series shows a materially smaller improvement than the raw figure claims — which is precisely the figure that goes into grant outcome reporting. The summary response carries `participant_id`, `baseline_score`, `latest_score`, `raw_delta`, `rtm_correction`, `attendance_ratio_capped`, `attendance_adjusted_delta` and `sessions_attended_total`.

### 3.3. Meeting Spaces, Cohort Formation and the Occupation Calendar

A meeting space is a physical room the programme can put people in. A `ProgramDirector` registers one with a human-readable `name` and a `capacity` of at least one seated occupant. Two further attributes are optional: `turnover_buffer_minutes`, the cleaning and turnover time that must separate two occupations of the room, which defaults to 30 minutes and may be zero but never negative; and `accessibility_features`, a free-form array of tags that defaults to an empty array. A space is registered in status `AVAILABLE`.

Taking a room out of service is a toggle between `AVAILABLE` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, driven by a `ProgramDirector` supplying the target `status`. The toggle is deliberately gentle on existing plans: cohorts and remembrance events already scheduled into the room keep their occupation of it and are not disturbed. What it blocks is new commitment — while a room is `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, no cohort meeting in it may be opened for enrolment and no remembrance event held in it may be confirmed, until the room is restored.

Forming a cohort is the `ProgramDirector`'s act of carving a recurring weekly block out of a room. The request names the cohort (`name`), fixes the single loss category it serves (`loss_type`), names the room (`meeting_space_id`), sizes the group (`seats_total`, at least one, and `min_viable_enrollment`, at least one and never larger than `seats_total`), and pins the schedule: `weekday` is one of `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU`, `FRI`, `SAT` or `SUN`; `start_minute_utc` is the session start expressed as minutes after midnight UTC, from 0 to 1439; `duration_minutes` is the session length, at least one minute, and constrained so that the start minute plus the duration does not exceed 1440 — a session may not run past midnight. `series_start_date` is the date of the first session and must itself fall on the weekday named by `weekday`; `sessions_total` is how many weekly sessions the series runs, at least one. Finally, `direct_cost_cents` records the planned direct programme cost of the whole series in whole cents, and may be zero but never negative. A cohort is created in status `FORMING` with `seats_open` set to `seats_total` and both `seats_held` and `seats_filled` at zero.

Two figures are derived at formation. `series_end_date` is the date of the last session: it is `series_start_date` advanced by seven days for every session after the first, so a six-session series starting on a Tuesday ends on the Tuesday thirty-five days later. And the room must be big enough — a `seats_total` exceeding the room's `capacity` is refused with `ERR_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

The other derived figure is money, and it is computed by a rule that must be read carefully because it looks circular and is not. The funder's handbook describes the indirect fee as a band on total programme cost, but total programme cost already contains the fee. This system resolves the circularity by pinning a single-pass evaluation order with no iteration. **Step one selects the tier from `direct_cost_cents` alone, never from the total.** A direct cost below 250,000 cents — that is, below twenty-five hundred dollars — selects a rate of 1200 basis points; a direct cost of 250,000 cents or more but below 750,000 cents selects 1000 basis points; a direct cost of 750,000 cents or more selects 800 basis points. Each band's lower boundary is inclusive and its upper boundary exclusive, so a direct cost of exactly 250,000 cents sits in the middle band at 1000 basis points. **Step two** computes `admin_fee_cents` by multiplying `direct_cost_cents` by the basis-point rate selected in step one, dividing by ten thousand, and rounding half up to a whole cent — this is the only rounding in the rule. **Step three** sets `program_cost_total_cents` to `direct_cost_cents` plus the `admin_fee_cents` computed in step two. A direct cost of 240,000 cents therefore yields an admin fee of 28,800 cents and a programme total of 268,800 cents; keying the tier off the total instead would drop the total into the second band and produce 26,880 and 266,880, which is the wrong answer. The award's own `indirect_rate_bps` plays no part here whatsoever — it is used only for grant reporting, as described in §3.9. The identical three-step rule computes `admin_fee_cents` and `program_cost_total_cents` for a remembrance event from its own `direct_cost_cents`.

Rooms are shared, and two structurally different occupation shapes contend for them: a cohort's recurring weekly block and a remembrance event's one-off block. Every cohort formation and every event planning request is therefore checked against the room's existing occupations, and a clash is refused with `ERR_SPACE_TIME_CONFLICT`. The check runs in two parts. First, do the two occupations ever fall on the same date? A cohort occupies its room on its `weekday`, every week, from `series_start_date` through `series_end_date` inclusive. An event occupies its room on exactly one date. So a cohort and an event share a date when the event's `event_date` falls on or after the cohort's `series_start_date` and on or before its `series_end_date`, and the event's date falls on the cohort's `weekday`. Two cohorts share dates when they run on the same `weekday` and their two date windows, each taken inclusively from series start to series end, overlap at all. Two events share a date only when their `event_date` values are identical.

Second, if the date sets intersect, do the minute intervals collide? Each occupation spans from its `start_minute_utc` to that start plus its `duration_minutes`. The turnover buffer of the room is applied to the incoming occupation on both sides: the occupations collide when the incoming start minus the buffer is less than or equal to the existing occupation's end minute, **and** the existing occupation's start minus the buffer is less than or equal to the incoming occupation's end minute. Both comparisons are non-strict, which has a consequence worth stating plainly: a gap of *exactly* the buffer still counts as a collision, and the gap must be strictly greater than the buffer to be legal. With a 30-minute buffer and an existing block running from minute 600 to minute 690, an incoming occupation starting at minute 720 has an adjusted start of 690, which is not greater than 690, and therefore collides; the same occupation starting at minute 721 has an adjusted start of 691, which clears 690, and is legal.

Only live occupations contend. Cohorts in `FORMING`, `OPEN_ENROLLMENT`, `UNDERSUBSCRIBED`, `RUNNING` or `SUSPENDED` and remembrance events in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` are checked. Cohorts that have reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, and events that have reached `HELD` or `CANCELLED`, release their claim on the room and are excluded from the check entirely. Cohorts can be listed and filtered by `meeting_space_id`, by `status` or by `loss_type`.

### 3.4. Seats: Enrolment, Confirmation and the Cohort Lifecycle

A cohort in `FORMING` accepts nobody. A `ProgramDirector` opens it with a transition into `OPEN_ENROLLMENT`, which is permitted from `FORMING` and, for a group that failed to fill the first time round, from `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` as well — the second path is what makes the cohort lifecycle a cycle rather than a line. Either way, the room must be `AVAILABLE`, and the cohort's `version` increases by one.

Placing a participant in a seat is an `IntakeCoordinator` act. The request names the `participant_id` and the `cohort_id`, and carries the `expected_cohort_version` described in §2.6. Before a seat is taken, a substantial ladder of checks fires — and their relative order is fixed by §2.5. The clinical safety gates come first: a participant with no `NEEDS` assessment on file cannot be placed in a group at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`; and when that participant's most recent `NEEDS` assessment carried `suicidality_flag` true, group placement additionally requires that they already have an open counselling referral, meaning one in status `WAITLISTED` or `ASSIGNED`, without which the placement is refused with `ERR_SUICIDALITY_REQUIRES_COUNSELING`. Group work is never a substitute for individual clinical cover. Next comes attribute agreement: the cohort's `loss_type` must equal the participant's `loss_type` exactly, otherwise `ERR_LOSS_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Then capacity and uniqueness: the cohort must have at least one seat in `seats_open`, otherwise `ERR_COHORT_FULL`; and the same participant must not already hold a seat in the same cohort in status `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `ATTENDING` or `COMPLETED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` — note that a previously `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` seat does not block a fresh placement. Enrolment is also only possible while the cohort is in `OPEN_ENROLLMENT`; against a cohort in any other status the attempt is refused with `ERR_COHORT_NOT_ENROLLING`. Any participant-scoped write attempted once a participant's `service_status` has become `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`.

A successful placement creates an enrollment in status `HELD` and answers with HTTP 201. Its `held_until` is stamped seventy-two hours after the record's `created_at` — a three-day window in which the family must confirm. One unit moves out of `seats_open` and into `seats_held`, the cohort's `version` increases by one, and if this is the participant's first live enrollment their `service_status` advances `ASSESSED → ACTIVE`. The new enrollment starts with `sessions_attended` at zero, `attendance_ratio` at zero and `withdraw_reason` null.

Confirming converts the hold into a real seat: `HELD → CONFIRMED`, allowed only while the cohort is still in `OPEN_ENROLLMENT` and only while the logical clock is at or before `held_until`. One unit moves out of `seats_held` and into `seats_filled` — `seats_open` does not change — and the cohort's `version` increases by one. Both an `IntakeCoordinator` and the `Participant` themselves may confirm. If the hold window lapses instead, the operational sweep expires the seat: `HELD → EXPIRED` once the logical clock passes `held_until`, moving one unit out of `seats_held` and back into `seats_open` and incrementing the cohort's `version`.

Withdrawing a single seat is available from `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` and records a `withdraw_reason` — one of `PARTICIPANT_REQUEST`, `NO_SHOW`, `TRANSFERRED` or `PROGRAM_CANCELLED`. Which compartment the seat comes out of depends on where it was: a `HELD` seat returns one unit from `seats_held` to `seats_open`, while a `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` seat returns one unit from `seats_filled` to `seats_open`. In every case the cohort's `version` increases by one. Withdrawing a `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` seat carries a further consequence: if the cohort is `RUNNING` and the resulting `seats_filled` has dropped below `min_viable_enrollment`, the cohort itself moves `RUNNING → SUSPENDED`, which in turn releases the cohort's committed grant money as described in §3.9.

Attendance is logged by an `IntakeCoordinator` supplying a `sessions_delta`, which is added to the enrollment's `sessions_attended`. The running total is clamped: it is never allowed above the cohort's `sessions_total` and never allowed below zero, so a negative delta can correct an over-count without driving the tally negative. Every attendance write recomputes `attendance_ratio` as `sessions_attended` divided by the cohort's `sessions_total`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Launch is the branch point of the whole cohort lifecycle, and the two branches carry genuinely different consequences. When the `ProgramDirector` launches an `OPEN_ENROLLMENT` cohort whose `seats_filled` has reached or exceeded `min_viable_enrollment`, the cohort moves `OPEN_ENROLLMENT → RUNNING`: every enrollment in `CONFIRMED` advances `CONFIRMED → ATTENDING` — no seat movement occurs here, since a confirmed seat and an attending seat both count toward `seats_filled` — and every enrollment still in `HELD` is expired, each returning one unit from `seats_held` to `seats_open`. When instead `seats_filled` is strictly below `min_viable_enrollment`, the cohort moves `OPEN_ENROLLMENT → UNDERSUBSCRIBED` and every enrollment is left exactly where it stands, untouched, so that a re-opened enrolment period can build on the seats already taken. Both branches increase the cohort's `version` by one.

A running cohort may be suspended and resumed, which is the second cycle in this lifecycle. Suspension takes a `RUNNING` cohort to `SUSPENDED`, releases every one of that cohort's funding allocations still in `COMMITTED`, and increments `version`. It also occurs automatically whenever a withdrawal drops `seats_filled` below `min_viable_enrollment` on a running cohort. Resuming takes the cohort `SUSPENDED → RUNNING`, but only if it still meets its viability floor: `seats_filled` must have reached or exceeded `min_viable_enrollment`, and a resume attempt below that floor is refused with `ERR_MIN_VIABLE_NOT_MET`. Resumption increments `version`.

Completion closes the series out and is the longest cascade in the group workflow. A `RUNNING` cohort moves to `COMPLETED`, and in the same write: every enrollment in `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` moves to `COMPLETED` with its `attendance_ratio` recomputed; each enrollment reaching `COMPLETED` adds its `sessions_attended` to that participant's `sessions_attended_total` and moves that participant's `service_status` `ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE`; every funding allocation on the cohort still in `COMMITTED` is settled on actual attendance as described in §3.9; and the cohort's `version` increases by one.

Cancellation is available from `FORMING`, `OPEN_ENROLLMENT`, `UNDERSUBSCRIBED` or `SUSPENDED` — never from `RUNNING`, which must be suspended first. It moves the cohort to `CANCELLED`, withdraws every enrollment in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` with `withdraw_reason` `PROGRAM_CANCELLED` and returns each of those seats to `seats_open`, releases every `COMMITTED` funding allocation on the cohort, and increments `version`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a cohort, and `COMPLETED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are terminal for an enrollment; any transition requested out of a terminal state is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Enrollments can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `cohort_id` or by `status`.

### 3.5. The Individual Counselling Waitlist

Individual counselling is the scarcest resource the programme offers, and it is rationed by a queue. A `ProgramDirector` registers a provider with their `full_name`, the `license_type` they hold — one of `LCSW`, `LPC`, `PSYCHOLOGIST` or `CHAPLAIN` — the `specialties` they are credentialed for, given as an array whose members are loss categories, a `caseload_cap` of at least one, and a `weekly_capacity_minutes` budget which may be zero but never negative. A provider is registered in status `ACTIVE` with `active_caseload` and `committed_minutes` both at zero.

Placing someone on the waitlist may be done by an `IntakeCoordinator` or by a `Counselor`. The request names the `participant_id`, the `requested_minutes_per_week` that referral will consume once assigned (at least one minute), the clinical `urgency` band — `ROUTINE`, `PRIORITY` or `URGENT` — and the `required_specialty`, the loss category whoever takes the case must be credentialed for. The referral is created in status `WAITLISTED` with HTTP 201, its `waitlisted_at` stamped from the logical clock, its `counselor_id`, `assigned_at` and `close_reason` all null, and its `queue_priority` computed immediately. Creating a counselling referral also advances the participant's `service_status` from `ASSESSED` to `ACTIVE`, exactly as a first group placement does.

`queue_priority` is a weighted score assembled from four capped components and rounded once, at the end. The urgency band contributes a flat weight: 10 points for `ROUTINE`, 25 points for `PRIORITY`, 45 points for `URGENT`. Clinical need contributes the participant's `composite_need_index` multiplied by 0.35, capped so that this component never exceeds 35 points — meaning a composite of one hundred and a composite of ninety-nine both contribute the full 35. Waiting contributes half a point for every whole day the referral has been waiting, where the wait is the number of whole days between `waitlisted_at` and the logical clock with any partial day discarded, capped at 20 points — which is to say the wait component tops out after forty days. Re-entry contributes 5 points for each time this participant's counselling has fallen back to the waitlist, taken from `waitlist_reentry_count`, capped at 10 points. The four components are added and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places; the rounding happens once, on the final sum, and never on the individual components. When the programme lists the queue, ordering is by `queue_priority` descending, then by `waitlisted_at` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending — never by identifier.

Assignment is an `IntakeCoordinator` act that moves a referral `WAITLISTED → ASSIGNED`. The request names the `counselor_id` and carries the `expected_counselor_version` described in §2.6. Four preconditions guard it. The named provider must be in status `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_COUNSELOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. The referral's `required_specialty` must appear in the provider's `specialties`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIALTY_MISMATCH`. Taking the case must not push `active_caseload` above `caseload_cap` — that is, the current caseload plus one must remain at or below the cap — otherwise `ERR_COUNSELOR_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED`. And the minute budget must hold: the provider's `committed_minutes` plus this referral's `requested_minutes_per_week` must not exceed `weekly_capacity_minutes`, otherwise `ERR_COUNSELOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. That last comparison is strictly greater-than, so landing exactly on the weekly budget is legal.

The minute budget is aggregate capacity, not a calendar. A provider's week is a rolling seven-day window and every referral in status `ASSIGNED` occupies `requested_minutes_per_week` of it for as long as it stays assigned, with no notion of *which* minutes — there is no geometric adjacency to reason about and no session-level scheduling. `committed_minutes` is therefore simply the sum of `requested_minutes_per_week` over that provider's referrals in status `ASSIGNED`; referrals in `WAITLISTED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. A successful assignment sets `counselor_id` and stamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock, increases the provider's `active_caseload` by one and their `committed_minutes` by the referral's `requested_minutes_per_week`, and increments the provider's `version` by one.

Closing a case releases the capacity again. Available from `ASSIGNED` to either an `IntakeCoordinator` or the assigned `Counselor` themselves, closure moves the referral to `CLOSED`, records a `close_reason` of `COMPLETED`, `PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`, `COUNSELOR_UNAVAILABLE` or `DECLINED`, decreases the provider's `active_caseload` by one and their `committed_minutes` by that referral's `requested_minutes_per_week`, and increments the provider's `version`.

Now consider a scenario the programme genuinely faces: a counsellor leaves the roster mid-quarter. A `ProgramDirector` deactivates them, moving the provider `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` and zeroing both `active_caseload` and `committed_minutes`. Every referral that provider held in `ASSIGNED` falls back `ASSIGNED → WAITLISTED`, with `counselor_id` and `assigned_at` cleared and `waitlisted_at` reset to the current logical clock so the referral takes its place in the queue afresh. Each such fall-back increments that participant's `waitlist_reentry_count` by one and recomputes the referral's `queue_priority` — which is where the re-entry bonus earned above begins to bite. And once a participant's `waitlist_reentry_count` has reached two or more, the programme treats repeated disruption as a clinical risk in its own right: that participant's `risk_tier` is escalated by exactly one level, `LOW` becoming `MODERATE` and `MODERATE` becoming `HIGH`, with a tier already at `HIGH` left unchanged. The escalated tier then recomputes `outreach_cadence_days` on the same 90 / 60 / 30 scale, and when the escalated tier is `HIGH`, every one of that participant's anniversary touchpoints in `SCHEDULED` or `DUE` has its `channel` switched to `PHONE` and its `escalated` flag set to true. The provider's `version` increments by one. This one deactivation therefore reaches five entities deep — provider, referral, participant tier, participant cadence, and outreach channel — and all of it lands in the same write.

The reverse cycle also exists: a referral that fell back to `WAITLISTED` can be assigned again, closing the loop `WAITLISTED → ASSIGNED → WAITLISTED`. Separately, when a participant withdraws from the programme entirely (§3.8), every counselling referral of theirs in `WAITLISTED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `close_reason` set to `PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`; where the referral was `ASSIGNED`, the assigned provider's `active_caseload` drops by one, their `committed_minutes` drops by that referral's `requested_minutes_per_week`, and their `version` increments. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a counselling referral, and `INACTIVE` is terminal for a provider. Counselling referrals can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `counselor_id` or by `status`.

### 3.6. Volunteer Companions: Training, Certification and Matching

A volunteer companion walks alongside a bereaved person outside clinical hours, and the programme will not put one in front of a family until they have been trained. A `ProgramDirector` registers an applicant with their `full_name` and the `endorsed_loss_types` they may accompany — an array of loss categories that defaults to empty — plus an optional `max_concurrent_matches` ceiling that defaults to 3 and must be at least one. The applicant starts in status `APPLIED` with `training_hours` at zero, an empty `modules_completed` list, `active_match_count` at zero, and both `certified_on` and `certification_expires_on` null.

Training is recorded one module at a time, by the volunteer themselves or by a `ProgramDirector`, supplying the `module_code` completed and the `hours` it took. Each record adds its `hours` to the volunteer's cumulative `training_hours`, carried to two decimal places, and appends the `module_code` to `modules_completed`, which preserves the order in which modules were recorded. The first record on an applicant moves them `APPLIED → IN_TRAINING`; a volunteer who has lapsed and returns to training moves `LAPSED → IN_TRAINING` the same way. Further records while the certification bar has not yet been cleared leave the volunteer where they are, in the self-loop `IN_TRAINING → IN_TRAINING` — that is, when the hours after adding this record still fall short of sixteen, or when `modules_completed` after appending this record still does not contain both `CORE_LISTENING` and `CORE_SAFETY`.

Certification fires on the record that clears both bars at once: after adding this record's `hours`, cumulative `training_hours` has reached sixteen or more, **and** after appending this record's `module_code`, `modules_completed` contains both `CORE_LISTENING` and `CORE_SAFETY`. The volunteer then moves to `CERTIFIED`, `certified_on` is stamped with the logical clock date, and `certification_expires_on` is set to three hundred and sixty-five days after `certified_on`. The same certification transition is available directly from `LAPSED`, so a lapsed volunteer who tops up their hours re-certifies without an intermediate step — the recertification loop `CERTIFIED → LAPSED → IN_TRAINING → CERTIFIED` is a designed path, not an accident.

Certification does not last forever. During the operational sweep, any volunteer in `CERTIFIED` whose `certification_expires_on` now lies strictly before the logical clock date moves `CERTIFIED → LAPSED`, and every companion match that volunteer holds in `ACTIVE` is ended with `end_reason` `COMPANION_LAPSED`. A `ProgramDirector` may also suspend a certified volunteer pending review, moving `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` and declining every match of theirs still in `PROPOSED`; reinstatement returns them `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`, but only while the logical clock date is on or before `certification_expires_on` — a volunteer whose certification expired while suspended cannot simply be reinstated and must train again. A volunteer record has no terminal status; every state it can reach has a way out.

Proposing a match is an `IntakeCoordinator` act naming the `companion_id` and the `participant_id`. The volunteer must be in status `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_COMPANION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The participant's `loss_type` must appear in the volunteer's `endorsed_loss_types`, otherwise `ERR_LOSS_TYPE_NOT_ENDORSED`. A safety gate applies to the most fragile participants: when the participant's `risk_tier` is `HIGH`, the volunteer must have accumulated at least twenty-four `training_hours` — half again the sixteen that certification requires — otherwise the proposal is refused with `ERR_COMPANION_TRAINING_INSUFFICIENT`. That gate is evaluated before the endorsement check and before any capacity rule, per §2.5. Finally, the same volunteer and participant must not already be paired in a match that is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_MATCH`. The match is created in `PROPOSED` with HTTP 201, `started_on`, `ended_on` and `end_reason` all null.

A `match_score` is computed at proposal time from three components and **frozen on the row forever** — it is never recomputed, however the volunteer's counters later move. Endorsement is worth 40 points, awarded in full when the participant's `loss_type` appears in the volunteer's `endorsed_loss_types` and worth nothing otherwise. Training is worth up to 30 points, scaled by the volunteer's `training_hours` divided by the sixteen hours certification requires, with that ratio capped at 1 so extra hours beyond sixteen add nothing. Headroom is worth up to 30 points, scaled by one minus the volunteer's `active_match_count` divided by their `max_concurrent_matches`, so a volunteer with a full book scores nothing on this component and an idle volunteer scores the full 30. The three components are added and the total rounded half up to two decimal places.

The volunteer answers the proposal themselves, supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`. Accepting moves the match `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, but only while the volunteer is still `CERTIFIED` and only when taking this match keeps their `active_match_count` at or below `max_concurrent_matches` — the current count plus one must not exceed the ceiling, otherwise `ERR_MATCH_LIMIT_REACHED`. Acceptance stamps `started_on` with the logical clock date, increases the volunteer's `active_match_count` by one, and sets the participant's `has_active_companion` to true. Declining moves the match `PROPOSED → DECLINED` and changes no counter anywhere.

Ending an active match is available to the volunteer or to an `IntakeCoordinator`, and records an `end_reason` of `COMPLETED`, `COMPANION_LAPSED`, `PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN` or `MISMATCH`. The match moves `ACTIVE → ENDED`, `ended_on` is stamped with the logical clock date, the volunteer's `active_match_count` drops by one, and the participant's `has_active_companion` is recomputed — it stays true only while at least one of that participant's matches remains `ACTIVE`. The same ending happens automatically when a volunteer lapses and when a participant withdraws. `DECLINED` and `ENDED` are terminal for a match. Matches can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `companion_id` or by `status`.

### 3.7. Remembrance Events and Family Invitations

Once or twice a year the programme gathers families for a remembrance service. A `ProgramDirector` plans one by giving it a `title`, naming the room in `meeting_space_id`, fixing the `event_date` as a calendar date, and pinning the block within that date with `start_minute_utc` (minutes after midnight UTC, from 0 to 1439) and `duration_minutes` (at least one minute, with the start plus the duration not exceeding 1440). `expected_attendance` records the planned headcount and must be at least one; `direct_cost_cents` records the planned direct cost of the event in whole cents, zero or more. The event is created in status `PLANNED`, with `admin_fee_cents` and `program_cost_total_cents` derived from `direct_cost_cents` by the same pinned three-step tier rule set out in §3.3, and with `invitations_sent`, `invitations_accepted` and `confirmed_headcount` all at zero.

Three guards sit on planning. An event may not be scheduled into the past: if `event_date` is earlier than the logical clock date, the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_PAST`. The room must hold the crowd: an `expected_attendance` exceeding the room's `capacity` is refused with `ERR_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. And the block must be free: the event's date and minute interval are tested against every live occupation of the same room under the closed-interval buffer arithmetic of §3.3, and a clash is refused with `ERR_SPACE_TIME_CONFLICT`.

Confirming moves the event `PLANNED → CONFIRMED` and requires the room to be `AVAILABLE`; the event's `version` increments by one. Confirmation matters because invitations cannot go out before it. Cancelling is available from `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` and moves the event to `CANCELLED`, voiding every invitation still in `DRAFT` or `SENT` and releasing every funding allocation on the event still in `COMMITTED`. Marking an event as held is available only from `CONFIRMED` and only once the logical clock date has reached or passed `event_date`; it moves the event to `HELD`, expires every invitation still in `DRAFT` or `SENT`, and settles every `COMMITTED` funding allocation on the event against confirmed headcount as described in §3.9. `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for an event. Events can be listed and filtered by `meeting_space_id` or by `status`.

An invitation is drafted by an `IntakeCoordinator` naming the `event_id` and the `participant_id`, plus an optional `guest_count` of additional family members accompanying the participant, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. The invitation is created in `DRAFT` with HTTP 201, `sent_at` and `responded_at` null, and its `rsvp_deadline` computed as the event's `event_date` less three days. That deadline is the hinge of the whole RSVP workflow.

Sending moves an invitation `DRAFT → SENT`. It requires the parent event to be in `CONFIRMED` — against an event in any other status the send is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONFIRMED` — and it requires the logical clock date to be on or before `rsvp_deadline`, otherwise `ERR_RSVP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A successful send stamps `sent_at` from the logical clock and increases the event's `invitations_sent` by one. Responding is the invited `Participant`'s own act, though an `IntakeCoordinator` may record it on their behalf; the request carries a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and it too requires the logical clock date to be on or before `rsvp_deadline` — a late RSVP is refused with `ERR_RSVP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Either response stamps `responded_at`. An acceptance moves the invitation `SENT → ACCEPTED`, increases the event's `invitations_accepted` by one, and adds one plus the invitation's `guest_count` to the event's `confirmed_headcount` — the participant themselves plus every guest they bring. A decline moves the invitation `SENT → DECLINED` and touches no headcount.

If nobody answers, the operational sweep expires the invitation: any invitation in `SENT` whose `rsvp_deadline` now lies strictly before the logical clock date moves `SENT → EXPIRED`, with no change to any headcount figure. Voiding, by contrast, happens only through event cancellation, and likewise changes no headcount. The event's `invitations_sent` counter reflects every invitation that ever went out — those in `SENT`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` — while `invitations_accepted` counts only those in `ACCEPTED` and `confirmed_headcount` sums one plus `guest_count` across those same accepted invitations. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOID` are all terminal for an invitation. Invitations can be listed and filtered by `event_id`, by `participant_id` or by `status`.

### 3.8. The Outreach Cadence, Holds, and Programme Withdrawal

A participant carries two independent status dimensions on the same record, and it is important not to conflate them. `service_status` is the clinical dimension — `INTAKE`, `ASSESSED`, `ACTIVE`, `MAINTENANCE`, `DISCHARGED`, `WITHDRAWN` — and it advances through assessment, enrolment, cohort completion and discharge as described in §3.2 and §3.4. `outreach_status` is the follow-up dimension — `SCHEDULED`, `ON_HOLD`, `LAPSED`, `CLOSED` — and it evolves under entirely different triggers. Neither dimension gates the other, with a single exception: when `service_status` reaches the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, `outreach_status` is forced to `CLOSED` in the same write.

The cadence plan is laid down once, at the moment a referral is accepted, and it always consists of exactly four touchpoints created in a fixed order: `ONE_MONTH`, `THREE_MONTH`, `SIX_MONTH`, then `FIRST_ANNIVERSARY`. Each row records the `participant_id` it belongs to, the `occasion` it marks, and a `due_on` date computed as the participant's `date_of_death` advanced by that occasion's offset — thirty days for `ONE_MONTH`, ninety for `THREE_MONTH`, one hundred and eighty for `SIX_MONTH`, and a full year of three hundred and sixty-five days for `FIRST_ANNIVERSARY`. All four are created in status `SCHEDULED` with `escalated` false, `sent_at` and `acknowledged_at` null, and a `channel` of `PHONE` when the participant's `risk_tier` is `HIGH` and `EMAIL` otherwise. Since a newly accepted participant is always tiered `LOW`, in practice all four rows begin as `EMAIL`; it is a later `NEEDS` assessment or a waitlist re-entry escalation landing the tier on `HIGH` that re-channels them, as described in §3.2 and §3.5. All four rows are created regardless of how far in the past the death lies — a participant referred two years after a bereavement still gets a full plan, with three or four of its dates already behind the logical clock.

Touchpoints ripen through the operational sweep. A touchpoint in `SCHEDULED` whose `due_on` has been reached — that is, the logical clock date is on or after `due_on` — moves `SCHEDULED → DUE`, provided the participant's `outreach_status` is itself `SCHEDULED`. This is where a hold takes effect. Either an `IntakeCoordinator` or the `Participant` themselves may place the outreach dimension on hold by supplying `hold` as true, moving `outreach_status` `SCHEDULED → ON_HOLD`; the participant's existing touchpoints are left exactly as they are, but the sweep skips over that participant entirely while the hold stands, so nothing ripens and nothing lapses. Supplying `hold` as false lifts it, moving `outreach_status` `ON_HOLD → SCHEDULED` and letting the sweep resume advancing that participant's rows. Placing a hold requires the participant's `service_status` not to be `WITHDRAWN`; the hold toggle never touches `service_status`.

Sending a touchpoint is done by an `IntakeCoordinator` or by a `VolunteerCompanion` accompanying that participant, and it is permitted only from `DUE` — a send against a touchpoint in any other status is refused with `ERR_OUTREACH_NOT_DUE`. It further requires that the participant's `service_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`. The touchpoint moves `DUE → SENT` and `sent_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

Silence has a deadline. When the logical clock reaches or passes twenty-one days after `sent_at`, the sweep moves the touchpoint `SENT → NO_RESPONSE` and, at the participant level, moves `outreach_status` `SCHEDULED → LAPSED` — the programme's signal that a family has gone quiet. Acknowledgement is the way back. An `IntakeCoordinator` or an accompanying `VolunteerCompanion` records that the family responded, moving the touchpoint to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamping `acknowledged_at`. Acknowledgement is available from `SENT` and also from `NO_RESPONSE` — this second path is explicitly declared, so a lapsed family that resurfaces can be re-engaged rather than written off. Where the participant's `outreach_status` was `LAPSED`, acknowledgement moves it back `LAPSED → SCHEDULED`, closing the outreach cycle. `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `NO_RESPONSE` and `CANCELLED` are the touchpoint's terminal states. A participant's touchpoints can be listed in occasion order and filtered by `status`.

Withdrawal is the largest single unwind in the system. It is available to an `IntakeCoordinator` or to the `Participant` themselves, from any `service_status` other than `WITHDRAWN` and `DISCHARGED`, and it fires the following in one atomic write. The participant's `service_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` and their `outreach_status` becomes `CLOSED`. Every enrollment of theirs in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` `PARTICIPANT_REQUEST`, returning each seat to its cohort's `seats_open` — and where that drops a `RUNNING` cohort below its `min_viable_enrollment`, that cohort moves to `SUSPENDED`, whose own `COMMITTED` allocations are then `RELEASED` and the award's money figures restored, which is the full four-step chain from a single withdrawal to a grant balance. Every counselling referral of theirs in `WAITLISTED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `close_reason` `PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`, releasing any assigned provider's `active_caseload` and `committed_minutes`. Every companion match of theirs moves out of flight: those in `PROPOSED` become `DECLINED` and those in `ACTIVE` become `ENDED` with `end_reason` `PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`. And every anniversary touchpoint of theirs in `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `SENT` moves to `CANCELLED`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal on the service dimension; any participant-scoped write attempted afterwards is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.9. Grant Money: Awards, Allocations and Funder Reporting

A grant award is the conserved money pool described in §2.7. A `ProgramDirector` records one with the `funder_name`, the funder's own `award_reference`, the `awarded_cents` granted (zero or more whole cents), the `allowed_categories` the money may be spent under — an array whose members are drawn from `GROUP_PROGRAM`, `REMEMBRANCE_EVENT`, `COUNSELING` and `VOLUNTEER_TRAINING` — and the award period as `period_start` and `period_end`, both inclusive calendar dates. An optional `indirect_rate_bps` records the funder's indirect-cost rate in basis points, from 0 to 10000, and defaults to 1200 when omitted. Award references are unique across the whole system: a second award carrying an `award_reference` that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AWARD_REFERENCE`. An award is created in status `ACTIVE` with `committed_cents` and `spent_cents` at zero and `available_cents` equal to `awarded_cents`.

An award may be closed only once nothing is still committed against it — `committed_cents` must be exactly zero — moving it `ACTIVE → CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal, and thereafter any attempt to allocate money from that award or to open a report against it is refused with `ERR_GRANT_CLOSED`.

Allocating money is the polymorphic write in this system: the same operation funds two structurally different subjects. A `ProgramDirector` names the `grant_award_id`, the `category`, and the subject — `cohort_id` for a group programme, `event_id` for a remembrance event — and carries the `expected_grant_award_version` described in §2.6. The checks proceed in the ladder order of §2.5. The `category` must be a member of the award's `allowed_categories`, otherwise `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_ALLOWED`; and the subject must match the category, meaning `cohort_id` is set for `GROUP_PROGRAM` and `event_id` is set for `REMEMBRANCE_EVENT`, with a mismatched or missing subject refused as `ERR_ALLOCATION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. A group-programme allocation requires its cohort to be in `OPEN_ENROLLMENT`, `RUNNING` or `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FUNDABLE`; an event allocation requires its event to be in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FUNDABLE`. The allocation's effective date — the logical clock date at the moment of creation — must fall inside the award's window from `period_start` to `period_end`, taken inclusively at both ends, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_GRANT_PERIOD`. And finally the award must be able to afford it: `available_cents` must be greater than or equal to the `provisional_cents` about to be committed, with equality passing, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT`.

The `provisional_cents` figure is **forward-looking** and is computed once at creation, then frozen on the row and never recomputed. For a `GROUP_PROGRAM` allocation, the programme projects how many participants the cohort will actually carry: take the number of that cohort's seats currently held or filled, which is `seats_filled` plus `seats_held`, and cap it at `seats_total`. Multiply the cohort's `program_cost_total_cents` by that projected count, divide by `seats_total`, and round half up to a whole cent. The allocation's `basis` is recorded as `PROJECTED_ENROLLMENT`. For a `REMEMBRANCE_EVENT` allocation there is nothing to project — `provisional_cents` is simply the event's `program_cost_total_cents`, and `basis` is recorded as `EVENT_PLAN`. Note precisely which seats count at this moment: enrollments in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` and `ATTENDING` all contribute, while `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` ones do not. Money is committed for every seat that is held or filled right now, whether or not anybody ever turns up.

A created allocation lands in status `COMMITTED` with HTTP 201, `settled_cents` and `settled_at` null. The award's `committed_cents` increases by the `provisional_cents`, `available_cents` is recomputed, and the award's `version` increases by one.

Settlement is **chronological** and reads a deliberately disjoint input set, which is exactly why identical cohort data yields a different number from the commitment. It fires automatically when the funded subject reaches its own conclusion: a cohort moving `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, or an event moving `CONFIRMED → HELD`. For a `GROUP_PROGRAM` allocation, sum `sessions_attended` across only those of the cohort's enrollments that have reached status `COMPLETED` or `WITHDRAWN` — enrollments still in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `ATTENDING`, and those that `EXPIRED`, contribute nothing. Multiply the cohort's `program_cost_total_cents` by that attended-session total, divide by the product of `seats_total` and `sessions_total`, and round half up to a whole cent; `basis` becomes `ACTUAL_ATTENDANCE`. A cohort that filled its seats but was attended sparsely therefore settles far below what it committed. For a `REMEMBRANCE_EVENT` allocation, take the ratio of the event's `confirmed_headcount` to its `expected_attendance`, cap that ratio at 1 so an over-subscribed event never settles above plan, multiply the event's `program_cost_total_cents` by the capped ratio, and round half up to a whole cent; `basis` becomes `CONFIRMED_HEADCOUNT`. Because `expected_attendance` is guaranteed to be at least one at planning time, this ratio is always well defined.

Settlement moves the allocation `COMMITTED → SETTLED`, writes `settled_cents` and stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, decreases the award's `committed_cents` by the allocation's `provisional_cents`, increases the award's `spent_cents` by the newly computed `settled_cents`, recomputes `available_cents`, and increments the award's `version`. Release is the other exit: a `ProgramDirector` may release a `COMMITTED` allocation directly, and the system releases them automatically when a cohort is suspended or cancelled and when an event is cancelled. Release moves the allocation `COMMITTED → RELEASED`, leaves `settled_cents` null, decreases the award's `committed_cents` by the `provisional_cents`, recomputes `available_cents`, and increments the award's `version`. `SETTLED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal. Allocations can be listed and filtered by `grant_award_id`, by `cohort_id`, by `event_id` or by `status`.

Reporting rolls allocations up into a funder-facing statement. A `ProgramDirector` opens a report over one award by naming the `grant_award_id` and the reporting window as `period_start` and `period_end`, both inclusive. Windows on the same award must not overlap while they are still in play: if a report already exists for that award in status `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` whose window overlaps the requested one, the request is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The report is created in status `DRAFT` with HTTP 201, its totals computed immediately, `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `return_reason` all null, and `revision_index` at zero.

Line selection is precise. A report includes those funding allocations of its own award whose status is `COMMITTED` or `SETTLED` and whose effective date falls inside the report's inclusive window. An allocation's effective date is the calendar date of its `settled_at` when it has reached `SETTLED`, and the calendar date of its `created_at` otherwise. Allocations in `RELEASED` are excluded outright — a released commitment leaves no trace in any report, ever. The selected lines are processed in a fixed order, by effective date ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, never by identifier, so the sequence of per-line remainders is deterministic.

For each line, the reportable amount is the allocation's `settled_cents` when it has settled and its `provisional_cents` otherwise. The line's indirect amount is that reportable amount multiplied by the award's `indirect_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and **rounded half up to a whole cent on that line**. The rounding order is pinned: round each line first, then sum the rounded per-line figures. The alternative reading — summing raw amounts and rounding the aggregate once — is explicitly not what this system does and produces a different answer whenever the per-line fractional remainders fail to cancel. Consider three lines each carrying a reportable amount of 11,446 cents against an award at 1200 basis points: each line's indirect amount is 1,373.52 cents, which rounds to 1,374, and the three rounded figures sum to 4,122. Summing first would give 34,338 cents at twelve percent, or 4,120.56, rounding to 4,121 — one cent adrift, and the wrong answer.

The report's four money figures follow directly. `line_count` is the number of selected lines. `direct_total_cents` is the sum of the per-line reportable amounts. `indirect_total_cents` is the sum of the per-line rounded indirect amounts. `reported_total_cents` is `direct_total_cents` plus `indirect_total_cents`. Alongside the money sits an outcome count: `participants_served` is the number of distinct participants holding an enrollment in status `CONFIRMED`, `ATTENDING` or `COMPLETED` on any cohort funded by one of the included lines.

The report lifecycle is a revision cycle. Submitting moves a `DRAFT` report to `SUBMITTED`, and requires the parent award to still be `ACTIVE` — submitting against a `CLOSED` award is refused with `ERR_GRANT_CLOSED`. Submission recomputes `line_count`, `direct_total_cents`, `indirect_total_cents`, `reported_total_cents` and `participants_served` from scratch, then stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID` and `submitted_at` from the logical clock. A `GrantOfficer` may then return the report for revision, moving `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, recording the free-text `return_reason` they supply and increasing `revision_index` by one. A `ProgramDirector` reopens a returned report by moving it `RETURNED → DRAFT`, which clears `submitted_by_user_id` and `submitted_at` and recomputes every total again against whatever the allocation ledger now says — so a report that sat in review while an allocation settled comes back with different numbers. The cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → DRAFT` may run as many rounds as the funder demands, and `revision_index` counts them. Acceptance by a `GrantOfficer` moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and is subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1. Reports can be listed and filtered by `grant_award_id` or by `status`.
