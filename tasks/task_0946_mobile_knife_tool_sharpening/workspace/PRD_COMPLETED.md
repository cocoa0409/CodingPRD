# Mobile Knife & Tool Sharpening API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mobile sharpening business is, underneath the marketing, a custody problem wearing a pricing problem as a coat. Chef's knives, salon shears, clipper blades, deli slicer blades, garden pruners and mandoline blades leave a customer's premises, ride in a van, sit on a workshop bench, come back, and at every instant somebody has to be able to say exactly where each blade is and who is answerable for it. Layered on that are two genuinely scarce pools that nobody can conjure more of on the day: the service minutes a van can commit on a single run, and the bench minutes the shop can grind in a single day. Three separate intake channels — the standing van route, the customer-operated drop-off locker, and warranty rework — all draw on those same pools, and the whole scheduling discipline of the business consists of refusing work that would overdraw them.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that business. It covers the configuration surface (plan tiers, edge-geometry class specs, technicians, vans, bench days, locker compartments), the customer surface (accounts, standing-route subscriptions, service requests, items, locker deposits and releases, warranty claims, payments), the operations surface (route planning, dispatch, spill and reschedule, bench work, loaner rotation) and the money surface (per-visit and monthly-rollup invoicing, subscription allowances, proration, warranty credits, late fees, payments and voids). All of it is mounted under the `/sharpening` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described below.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows and are worth stating up front, because they explain most of the rules in §2 and §3. First, **every rejection is ordered**. The system does not evaluate its checks as an unordered set and return whichever one happens to fire; there is a single 57-rung ladder, the same ladder on every endpoint, and the lowest-numbered rung that fails is the one the caller is told about. Second, **money and physical custody are conserved**. Every forward operation that books capacity, consumes an allowance or raises a receivable has a compensating operation that returns the affected quantity to exactly its prior value, and the system is expected to be auditable against that at all times.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they are acting. There are exactly five roles, spelled `PlatformAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `Technician`, `AccountOwner` and `BillingClerk`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string other than those five, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles §4 lists as authorized for that endpoint, the refusal is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the caller's `X-User-ID` is a real row's `id` in the database and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `AccountOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's account foreign key to `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). For a resource that does not name an account directly, ownership is resolved through its parents — an item through its service request, an invoice line through its invoice, a payment through its invoice, a loaner issue through its service request, a locker compartment through the request it is currently holding. Three limits on that rule are part of the contract rather than accidents of implementation. First, the account-ownership comparison is made on **single-resource reads and on writes only**: with one exception the collection listings are deliberately *not* account-scoped, so an `AccountOwner` calling `GET /sharpening/service-requests`, `/request-items`, `/subscriptions`, `/allowance-entries`, `/locker-compartments` (whose rows carry the compartment's `access_code`), `/loaner-issues`, `/warranty-claims`, `/invoices`, `/invoice-lines` or `/payments` receives every matching row in the system and not merely their own, and must apply the `account_id` filter themselves if they want their own. The exception is the per-invoice line listing `GET /sharpening/invoices/{id}/lines`, which resolves its parent invoice first and therefore does apply the ownership comparison, against that invoice's account. Second, when a resource's owning account cannot be resolved at all — a compartment holding no request, an orphaned child — there is nothing to compare and the rung passes. Third, the allowance-entry-through-its-subscription path never actually runs: the single-entry read at `GET /sharpening/allowance-entries/{id}` authorizes only `PlatformAdmin` and `BillingClerk`, so no `AccountOwner` ever reaches an ownership comparison on that entity.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Technician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`. For operations attached to a van run, the acting technician must be the one named on that run's `technician_id`; a different technician is refused with `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_OWNER`. Exactly two endpoints are "attached to a van run" in that sense: marking arrival at a stop, and picking the items up at that stop. On every other technician endpoint — assessment, start-work, complete-work, bench intake, delivery, loaner issue, loaner return, warranty approval and denial, and every technician-authorized read including the reads of runs and stops — the rung asks only that `X-User-ID` resolve to *some* existing `Technician` row, because bench work is not tied to a run assignment. A `X-User-ID` that resolves to no `Technician` row at all fails this rung.

The other three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any row's `id`. The same holds for `Dispatcher` and for `BillingClerk`. This has one consequence worth naming explicitly: payment application is shared between `AccountOwner` and `BillingClerk`, and when a `BillingClerk` applies a payment the ownership rung is skipped entirely, because there is no entity for the clerk's id to be compared against.

Ownership has a second carve-out on warranty-claim creation. There, the acting `AccountOwner` *is* the scope of the request — the body names only the item, never an account — so the ownership rung has nothing to compare and passes, and the question of whether the claimed item actually belongs to the caller is asked much later, as its own rung, with its own error code. §2.6 pins both positions exactly.

Role assignment per endpoint is given in §4. The `PlatformAdmin` owns configuration, physical infrastructure and the logical clock; the `Dispatcher` plans runs, places stops, dispatches, spills and reschedules; the `Technician` drives the run, takes custody, assesses damage, works the bench and adjudicates warranty claims on items they did not personally assess; the `AccountOwner` is the customer, who opens requests, runs the subscription, uses the locker, raises claims and pays; and the `BillingClerk` owns the money surface — invoice creation, issuing, voiding and payment application.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty random uppercase-alphanumeric characters, minted afresh on every response including error responses.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the whole API that is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, because it describes when the HTTP call was served rather than anything about the business state; it therefore bears no relation to any `created_at` in the same response.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. It is advisory prose only; nothing in the contract depends on its wording, and callers must branch on `error_id` alone.

Two shape conventions govern every request body. A body that is absent, empty, or not a JSON object at all is treated exactly as the empty object `{}`, so the failure it produces is whatever rung 4 says about the endpoint's missing required fields rather than a parse error of its own. And fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored silently — supplying an extra key, or an `id`, or a version field on an endpoint that is not version-guarded, neither fails nor has any effect. Requests to a path this API does not define answer `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 404, and requests using a method the path does not accept answer `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 405; both carry the standard error envelope.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on it. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, or either value supplied as something that is not an integer, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Both values are read from the raw query string and must be canonical decimal integers: surrounding whitespace is tolerated, but any form whose text differs from the canonical rendering of the integer it parses to — `+10`, `010`, `10.0`, `1e1` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` just as a non-numeric string is. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error: it returns an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false.

Results are sorted by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint says otherwise; the one endpoint that does say otherwise is the invoice-lines-of-an-invoice listing, which is ordered by `sequence_index` ascending. Because the logical clock moves only when an administrator moves it, many rows legitimately share an identical `created_at`, so the tie-break is part of the contract: rows with equal `created_at` are ordered by the server's own insertion sequence — a private, strictly increasing counter stamped on every row as it is created — and never by `id`, whose UUID ordering is meaningless. The same `created_at`-then-insertion-sequence rule is what breaks ties inside every internal ranking that names `created_at` as a tie-breaker: the receiving-run selector, the compartment selector, the loaner selector, the allowance ranking and the invoice-line sequence. Note also that the *global* invoice-line listing at `GET /sharpening/invoice-lines` is not the per-invoice listing and therefore takes the default `created_at`-then-insertion order, not `sequence_index` order. All four pagination fields — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — appear in `meta` on every list response. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the limit and offset are applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows returned is less than `total_count`.

Every list endpoint's declared filter parameters are optional and combine conjunctively: supplying two filters returns only rows matching both. A filter naming an id that does not exist is not an error; it simply matches nothing and returns an empty array with a `total_count` of zero. Foreign-key and free-text filters are exact string comparisons against the stored value, with no normalisation: `run_date` on the run listing compares against the run date rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD` and a malformed date simply matches nothing rather than raising a range error, and `billing_period` on the invoice listing compares against the literal `YYYY-MM` string the invoice was created with, so the side-effect invoices of §3.10 — whose `billing_period` is null — never match any value of it. The enum-typed filters are the exception: `status`, `channel`, `billing_status`, `custody_location`, `service_status`, `size`, `grade`, `zone_code`, `source` and `kind`, wherever a list endpoint declares them, are checked at rung 5 and a value outside the enumeration is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` rather than an empty result. The numeric filter `cycle_index` on the allowance-entry listing must parse as an integer or the answer is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` at rung 6. Query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored entirely.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a creation body is ignored.

Each resource is serialised to a fixed projection: the same field set on a creation response, a single read and a row of a list, with no expansion, embedding or field selection anywhere. Nothing internal leaks into it — the insertion counter that breaks `created_at` ties, an invoice's private record of which service requests it covers, and a late-fee line's private record of its loaner issue are all invisible. A field the server has not yet had cause to write is emitted as `null`, never omitted. Within each projection some fields are stored and some are **derived on every read from the current state of other rows**, and the distinction matters because the derived ones move without their own row being touched and without its `version` advancing. The complete list of derived fields is: `Account.outstanding_balance_cents`; `ServiceRequest.item_count` and `final_total_cents`; `RouteRun.scheduled_stop_count`, `booked_service_minutes` and `committed_minutes_provisional`; `RouteStop.stop_service_minutes`; `BenchDay.booked_minutes`; `LockerCompartment.stored_item_count` and `stored_value_cents`; and `Invoice.taxable_subtotal_cents`, `nontaxable_subtotal_cents`, `total_cents`, `amount_paid_cents` and `amount_due_cents`. Everything else, including `RouteRun.committed_minutes_final`, `Invoice.tax_cents`, `ServiceRequest.quoted_total_cents` and `Subscription.allowance_remaining`, is a stored value written by the operation that computes it.

Most resources also carry three common properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped at creation from the logical clock. `updated_at` is the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of that row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. Three entities are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` at all: `InvoiceLine`, `Payment` and `AllowanceLedgerEntry`. These three are append-only ledger records — once written they are never modified, so a mutation counter would be meaningless on them.

A caution that matters for the version-guarded endpoints in §2.7: a request that mutates several rows bumps the `version` of each row it actually changes, and a request that is rejected bumps nothing. Where a workflow narrative in §3 says a particular operation "advances the run's version" or similar, that is the observable the concurrency guard is pinned to.

"Actually changes" means *written to*, not *observably different*, and the granularity is per row per request rather than per field or per step. Five consequences of that are worth pinning because they are otherwise easy to guess the other way. A billing-configuration `PATCH` that supplies no changed value — or none at all — still succeeds and still bumps the account by one. A row rewritten twice inside one request still bumps only once: the loaner unit that runs `ISSUED → INSPECTION → IN_STOCK` inside a single return advances by exactly one, which is also why `INSPECTION` is never observable from outside. A row whose *derived* aggregates move but whose stored fields do not is not bumped at all, so a `BenchDay` never advances past 1 however much its `booked_minutes` swings, and a `RouteRun` is not bumped by an explicit spill, by a reschedule off it, or by a subscription skip that cancels one of its stops — only stop addition, stop deletion, reschedule *onto* it, first arrival, dispatch, completion and cancellation touch a run's `version`. A row created by a request is born at `version` 1 and is not additionally bumped by the same request, so a freshly created invoice answers with `version` 1 even though its lines, its `tax_cents` and its covered requests were all written during that call. And the same subscription is bumped once in total by an invoice creation that consumes several allowance units, but once *per restored unit* by the void that gives them back.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock** and never reads wall-clock time for any business decision. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative endpoint sets it to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. That answer is the caller's own string **echoed back verbatim**, never re-rendered into the three-fractional-digit form: a caller who sets `2026-06-01T09:00:00Z` reads back exactly `2026-06-01T09:00:00Z`, not `2026-06-01T09:00:00.000Z`, and before any client has set it `current_utc` reads the literal default `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. Every *other* instant in the API is server-generated or derived from a server-generated instant — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `completed_at`, `delivered_at`, `issued_at`, `due_at`, `returned_at`, `applied_at`, `paused_at`, `hold_expires_at`, `last_released_at` — and every one of those is rendered in the three-fractional-digit `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` form regardless of how the clock string that produced it was written. Calendar dates are rendered `YYYY-MM-DD` and day-window times `HH:MM`. On input, an instant is accepted in any ISO 8601 form the parser recognises — with or without fractional digits, with `Z` or with a numeric UTC offset, which is normalised to UTC — but only the clock endpoint ever echoes an input instant back. The clock may only move forward: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the clock's current value is refused with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422), and so is a value that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant. Setting the clock to exactly its current value is permitted.

Every expiry, every window, every date comparison and every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `completed_at`, `delivered_at`, `issued_at`, `due_at`, `returned_at`, `applied_at`, `paused_at`, `hold_expires_at` and `last_released_at` stamp is read from this clock. Where a rule compares a date rather than an instant — a run date, a requested date, a bench date, a subscription anchor — the comparison uses the clock's UTC calendar date.

### 2.6. The Ordered Validation Ladder

This is the single most important contract in the document. Every endpoint evaluates the same ordered ladder of 57 rungs. Rungs that do not apply to a given endpoint are simply skipped; among the rungs that do apply, evaluation proceeds strictly from rung 1 upward and **the first rung that fails decides the response**. No later rung is ever evaluated once an earlier one has failed, which means that when a request violates two rules at once, the caller is always told about the lower-numbered one. Nine of these positions are described elsewhere in this document as *gates*, and it is worth being precise about what that word means here: a gate is not a separate pass that runs before the ladder. Each gate simply occupies the numbered rung named below, and everything numbered above it is unreachable while that gate is failing.

The gates and their exact positions are:

- **Authentication** occupies rung 1, on every endpoint, and pre-empts rungs 2 through 57.
- **Role authorization** occupies rung 2, on every endpoint, and pre-empts rungs 3 through 57.
- **Path existence** occupies rung 3, on every endpoint that has path parameters, resolved left-to-right along the URL, and pre-empts rungs 4 through 57.
- **Request shape** occupies rungs 4 through 7, in the fixed internal order required-fields, then enum legality, then numeric and temporal range, then pagination; together they pre-empt rungs 8 through 57.
- **Ownership** occupies rung 8, on every endpoint whose authorized roles include an entity-backed role (`AccountOwner` or `Technician`), and pre-empts rungs 9 through 57.
- **Account status** occupies rung 9 — deliberately after ownership and *before* body-reference existence — on every mutating endpoint whose target resolves to an `Account`, and pre-empts rungs 10 through 57. The practical consequence: a suspended account is reported as suspended even when the same request body also carries an unknown foreign key.
- **Body-reference existence** occupies rung 10, on every endpoint with foreign keys in the request body, and pre-empts rungs 11 through 57.
- **Credit limit** occupies rung 11, on the new-work endpoints only, and pre-empts rungs 12 through 57.
- **Version guard** occupies rung 12 — deliberately *before* every state precondition — on the three version-guarded write endpoints, and pre-empts rungs 13 through 57. A stale version therefore wins over an illegal from-state on the same request.

The full ladder, rung by rung:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to all endpoints; pre-empts every later rung.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to all endpoints; pre-empts rungs 3–57.
3. **Path existence.** Every id embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing row. Segments are checked left to right and the leftmost unresolved segment decides which error is returned: `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOCKER_COMPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BENCH_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOANER_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOANER_ISSUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, each HTTP 404. Applies to all endpoints with path parameters; pre-empts rungs 4–57.
4. **Required fields and JSON types.** Every field the endpoint marks required is present, and every supplied field carries the declared JSON type. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to all endpoints with a request body; pre-empts rungs 5–57.
5. **Enum legality.** Every enum-typed field carries a declared member of that enum. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422). The closed list of fields that route here is exactly: `account_type`, `zone_code`, `billing_mode`, `cadence`, the members of `allowed_cadences`, `item_class`, `declared_damage_tier`, `assessed_damage_tier`, `channel`, `priority`, `required_certification`, `price_tier`, `min_locker_size`, `size`, `grade`, `requested_grade`, `method`, `source`, `status`, `billing_status`, `custody_location`, `service_status` and `kind`. No other field routes here — every other malformed or wrongly typed field routes to `ERR_VALIDATION`, and a numeric field that parses correctly but falls outside its range routes to `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Applies to all endpoints with an enum field in their input; pre-empts rungs 6–57.
6. **Numeric and temporal range.** Numeric fields satisfy their declared ranges — budgets and caps are at least 1, payment amounts are at least 1, a stop `sequence` is at least 1, and cents-valued inputs are at least 0 — and every date or date-time field parses as ISO 8601. Otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to all endpoints with numeric or temporal input; pre-empts rungs 7–57.
7. **Pagination.** `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to all list endpoints; pre-empts rungs 8–57.
8. **Ownership.** For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` owns the resource, as described in §2.1. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `AccountOwner` or `Technician` — with the qualification recorded in §2.1 that on a list endpoint the `AccountOwner` half of the rung is not applied at all, so a listing is never narrowed to the caller's own account, while the `Technician` half still demands that `X-User-ID` resolve to a `Technician` row. Two carve-outs, both already flagged in §2.1: on warranty-claim creation the acting `AccountOwner` is itself the scope, so the does-this-item-belong-to-me test is rung 28 and not this rung; and on payment application a `BillingClerk` is opaque and skips this rung entirely. Pre-empts rungs 9–57.
9. **Account status.** The `Account` the operation resolves to has `status` `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to every mutating endpoint whose target resolves to an account, with exactly three exceptions that must remain callable on a suspended account: account suspension, account reinstatement, and the account billing-configuration update. Pre-empts rungs 10–57.
10. **Body-reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a row, checked in the order the fields are listed in that endpoint's input. The error is the not-found code for the entity the missing key names — `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_CLASS_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BENCH_DAY_NOT_FOUND` — each HTTP 404. Applies to all endpoints with foreign keys in the body; pre-empts rungs 11–57.
11. **Credit limit.** The account's `outstanding_balance_cents` is not greater than its `credit_limit_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_BLOCKED` (402). Applies to exactly five new-work endpoints: service-request submission, item addition, stop addition, locker assignment and loaner issue. Pre-empts rungs 12–57.
12. **Version guard.** The supplied `expected_route_run_version`, `expected_invoice_version` or `expected_subscription_version` equals the parent's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to the three version-guarded write endpoints named in §2.7; pre-empts rungs 13–57.
13. **Terminal state.** The target entity is not already sitting in one of the terminal states of the axis the requested transition acts on. Otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to every state-transition endpoint; pre-empts rungs 14–57.
14. **From-state and transition preconditions.** The target entity's current state is a declared from-state of the requested transition, and every precondition declared on that transition in §3 that no more specific rung below already covers also holds. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Examples of preconditions that land on this rung rather than on a rung of their own: the clock-versus-`hold_expires_at` comparison on hold expiry, the abandonment-grace comparison on abandonment, the at-least-one-scheduled-stop requirement on dispatch, the no-stop-still-open requirement on run completion, and the every-item-finished requirement on delivery. Applies to every state-transition endpoint; pre-empts rungs 15–57.
15. **Run mutability.** The parent `RouteRun` is in a status that permits the stop operation: `PLANNED` for stop add, stop delete and reschedule; `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED` for spill; `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS` for arrival. Otherwise `ERR_RUN_NOT_MUTABLE` (409). On reschedule the "parent" is still the run the stop's `route_run_id` names, which a spill does not change — a spill only records the departure in `spilled_from_route_run_id`. This has a consequence that must not be implemented by symmetry: a stop shed by the *dispatch* overflow loop is left pointing at a run that the same call moved to `DISPATCHED`, so such a stop can never be rescheduled and always answers `ERR_RUN_NOT_MUTABLE`. Only a stop spilled deliberately off a run that is still `PLANNED` is reschedulable. Applies to stop add, stop delete, spill, reschedule and arrive; pre-empts rungs 16–57.
16. **Request mutability.** The parent `ServiceRequest` is in a status that permits the item operation: `DRAFT` for item addition, and `IN_SERVICE` for assessment, start-work, complete-work and decline. Otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_MUTABLE` (409). Applies to item addition and to every per-item action endpoint; pre-empts rungs 17–57.
17. **Subscription usable.** When the request body supplies a `subscription_id`, that subscription's `status` is not `CANCELLED`. Otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to service-request creation only. The skip, cadence-change and advance-cycle endpoints never reach this rung, because rung 14 has already rejected a non-`ACTIVE` subscription as an illegal from-state. Pre-empts rungs 18–57.
18. **Invoice payable.** The invoice's `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and its `amount_due_cents` is at least 1. Otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE` (409). Applies to payment application. The status half of this test is pre-empted by rung 14, since a `DRAFT` invoice is not a declared from-state of any payment transition, so the reachable trigger for this code is an `ISSUED` credit-memo invoice whose `amount_due_cents` is zero or negative. Pre-empts rungs 19–57.
19. **Custody.** Every `RequestItem` involved is in the `custody_location` the operation requires: `VAN` or `LOCKER` for bench intake, `BENCH` for assessment and start-work, `LOCKER` for locker release and locker abandonment. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_CUSTODY` (409). Applies to bench intake, assessment, start-work, locker release and locker abandonment; pre-empts rungs 20–57.
20. **Bench day is today.** The `BenchDay` named by `bench_day_id` has a `bench_date` equal to the logical clock's date. Otherwise `ERR_BENCH_DAY_DATE_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to bench intake. This rung sits deliberately low so that a wrong-day intake is reported as a wrong day rather than being masked by the bench capacity check at rung 54. Pre-empts rungs 21–57.
21. **Locker hold still live.** The logical clock is strictly before the compartment's `hold_expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_LOCKER_HOLD_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to locker deposit and locker release; pre-empts rungs 22–57.
22. **Certification.** The acting technician's `certifications` contain the `required_certification` of the item's `ItemClassSpec`, unless that requirement is `NONE`. Otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` (422). Applies to assessment and start-work; pre-empts rungs 23–57.
23. **Segregation of duties.** The acting technician's `X-User-ID` differs from the `assessed_by_technician_id` recorded on the claimed item. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to warranty approval and warranty denial; pre-empts rungs 24–57.
24. **Authorization present.** The item's `authorization_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` or `AUTHORIZED`. Otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to start-work; pre-empts rungs 25–57.
25. **Locker value authorization.** For a locker-channel item, the holding compartment's `stored_value_cents` after the re-pricing is at most 25000 cents, or the request's `locker_high_value_authorized` is true. Otherwise `ERR_LOCKER_VALUE_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to assessment; pre-empts rungs 26–57.
26. **Item class agreement.** The supplied `item_class` equals the `item_class` of the referenced `ItemClassSpec`. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to item addition; pre-empts rungs 27–57.
27. **Zone agreement.** The service request's account `zone_code` equals the run's `zone_code`. Otherwise `ERR_STOP_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to stop addition and reschedule; pre-empts rungs 28–57. On reschedule the rung is present but can never fail, because the receiving run is not named by the caller: the selector of §3.5 filters candidate runs on zone before ranking them, so any run it returns already agrees. `ERR_STOP_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` is therefore raisable only from stop addition.
28. **Claimed item belongs to the claimant.** The claimed item's service request `account_id` equals the acting `AccountOwner`'s id. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ITEM_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to warranty-claim creation — this is the rung the ownership carve-out at rung 8 defers to. Pre-empts rungs 29–57.
29. **Loaner eligibility.** The referenced `ItemClassSpec` has `loaner_eligible` true. Otherwise `ERR_LOANER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to loaner-unit creation and to loaner issue; pre-empts rungs 30–57.
30. **Invoice scope agreement.** A per-visit invoice supplies `service_request_id` and omits `billing_period`; a monthly-rollup invoice supplies `billing_period` and omits `service_request_id`. Otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to invoice creation only — invoices minted as a side effect of some other operation are not subject to it. Pre-empts rungs 31–57.
31. **Run date not in the past.** The `run_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's date. Otherwise `ERR_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). Applies to run creation and to dispatch; pre-empts rungs 32–57.
32. **Requested date not in the past.** The `requested_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's date. Otherwise `ERR_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). Applies to service-request creation; pre-empts rungs 33–57.
33. **Arrival window inside the day.** The `arrival_window_start` is at or after the run's `day_window_start`, and that start plus 60 minutes is at or before the run's `day_window_end`. Otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUT_OF_DAY` (422). Applies to stop addition and reschedule; pre-empts rungs 34–57. On stop addition the window is the caller's and the test is exactly as written. On reschedule the window is computed rather than supplied, so this rung reports the *absence of any* free 60-minute slot inside the receiving run's day, and it is necessarily evaluated after the receiving run has been chosen at rung 50 — the two conditions are disjoint (either a run qualifies or none does), so no observable ordering conflict with rung 50 arises.
34. **Arrival windows do not overlap.** The half-open interval running from `arrival_window_start` up to but not including `arrival_window_end` does not intersect the interval of any stop on the same run whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`. Touching endpoints — one window ending exactly where another begins — do not intersect and are accepted. Otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to stop addition and reschedule; pre-empts rungs 35–57.
35. **Locker turnaround.** Every size-fitting available compartment at the site satisfies the condition that the logical clock is at or after that compartment's `last_released_at` plus 30 minutes; the boundary is inclusive, so a clock reading exactly 30 minutes after the release passes. Otherwise `ERR_LOCKER_TURNAROUND_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to locker assignment; pre-empts rungs 36–57.
36. **Skip window open.** The logical clock is at least 48 hours before midnight UTC at the start of the subscription's `next_service_date`; exactly 48 hours before passes. Otherwise `ERR_SKIP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to the customer skip; pre-empts rungs 37–57.
37. **Cadence-change window open.** The same 48-hour test, evaluated against the same `next_service_date`. Otherwise `ERR_CADENCE_CHANGE_TOO_LATE` (409). Applies to the cadence change; pre-empts rungs 38–57.
38. **Warranty window open.** The logical clock is not later than the item's `delivered_at` plus 30 days; exactly on the boundary passes. Otherwise `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to warranty-claim creation and to resubmission; pre-empts rungs 39–57.
39. **Clock monotonicity.** The supplied `to_iso8601` is not earlier than the current logical clock. Otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to the clock-setting endpoint; pre-empts rungs 40–57.
40. **Item cap.** The request's `item_count` plus one is at most 8. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to item addition; pre-empts rungs 41–57.
41. **Resubmission cap.** The claim's `resubmit_count` is strictly below 2. Otherwise `ERR_RESUBMIT_CAP_REACHED` (409). Applies to warranty resubmission; pre-empts rungs 42–57.
42. **Spill cap.** The stop's `spill_hop_count` plus one is at most 2. Otherwise `ERR_SPILL_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to the explicit spill and to dispatch, whose overflow loop can trigger it; pre-empts rungs 43–57.
43. **Compartment fit.** At least one available compartment at the site satisfies both the size-rank test and the item-capacity test. Otherwise `ERR_COMPARTMENT_SIZE_UNFIT` (422). Applies to locker assignment; pre-empts rungs 44–57.
44. **One live subscription per account.** The account has no other subscription whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIPTION` (409). Applies to subscription creation; pre-empts rungs 45–57.
45. **Unique item class spec.** No other `ItemClassSpec` already declares this `item_class`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CLASS_SPEC` (409). Applies to class-spec creation; pre-empts rungs 46–57.
46. **Unique bench day.** No other `BenchDay` already declares this `bench_date`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_BENCH_DAY` (409). Applies to bench-day creation; pre-empts rungs 47–57.
47. **One draft invoice per account.** The account has no other invoice whose `status` is `DRAFT`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DRAFT_INVOICE` (409). Applies to invoice creation; pre-empts rungs 48–57.
48. **Nothing billed twice.** Every service request the invoice would cover has `billing_status` `UNBILLED`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVOICE` (409). Applies to invoice creation; pre-empts rungs 49–57. In practice only the per-visit branch can raise it: under monthly rollup the covered set is *selected* by `billing_status` `UNBILLED` among other filters, so a request that is already invoiced is silently outside the scope rather than a collision inside it. Under per-visit billing the caller names the single request explicitly, and naming one that is already `INVOICED` or `SETTLED` is what this rung catches.
49. **Compartment pool non-empty.** After the site, size, capacity and turnaround filters described in §3.6 have been applied, the compartment candidate pool is not empty. Otherwise `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_COMPARTMENT` (409). Applies to locker assignment; pre-empts rungs 50–57.
50. **Receiving-run pool non-empty.** The receiving-run candidate pool described in §3.5 is not empty. Otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RUN` (409). Applies to reschedule; pre-empts rungs 51–57.
51. **Loaner pool non-empty.** The loaner candidate pool described in §3.8 is not empty. Otherwise `ERR_LOANER_STOCK_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to loaner issue; pre-empts rungs 52–57.
52. **Stop cap.** The run's `scheduled_stop_count` plus one is at most its `stop_cap`. Otherwise `ERR_STOP_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to stop addition and reschedule; pre-empts rungs 53–57. As with rung 27, the reschedule instance is present but unreachable: the selector already applied the identical cap test when it built the candidate pool, so a run it returns always has room, and a pool emptied by that test reports `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RUN` at rung 50 instead.
53. **Run minute budget.** The run's `committed_minutes_provisional`, recomputed with the new stop included, is at most its `service_minute_budget`; equality passes. Otherwise `ERR_RUN_MINUTES_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to stop addition and reschedule; pre-empts rungs 54–57. The reschedule instance is unreachable for the same reason as rung 52 — the selector's minute filter is arithmetically the identical test — so on a reschedule an over-full receiving run manifests as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RUN`, never as this code.
54. **Bench minute budget.** The bench day's `booked_minutes` plus the incoming request's declared minutes is at most its `bench_minute_budget`; equality passes. Otherwise `ERR_BENCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to bench intake; pre-empts rungs 55–57.
55. **Invoice not empty.** The invoice has at least one `InvoiceLine`. Otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_INVOICE` (409). Applies to invoice creation and to invoice issuing; pre-empts rungs 56–57.
56. **No overpayment.** The payment's `amount_cents` is at most the invoice's `amount_due_cents` plus an overpayment tolerance of 0 cents — which is to say, at most `amount_due_cents` exactly. Otherwise `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` (422). Applies to payment application; pre-empts rung 57.
57. **Access code.** The `access_code` supplied equals the compartment's `access_code`. Otherwise `ERR_ACCESS_CODE_INVALID` (403). Applies to locker deposit and locker release. This is deliberately the last rung: on a compartment whose hold has already expired, the hold check at rung 21 wins even when the supplied code is also wrong.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write endpoints are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on a parent entity's `version`. In each case the guard exists because the operation is one that would otherwise legitimately succeed a second time — the parent happily accepts many of them — so the rejection cannot be expressed as a state precondition. It exists solely to stop a caller from committing against a view of the parent they have not actually observed.

- Adding a stop to a route run requires `expected_route_run_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RouteRun`. If it does not equal that run's current `version`, the add is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without this, a dispatcher could commit capacity against a stale reading of `booked_service_minutes`.
- Applying a payment to an invoice requires `expected_invoice_version`, carrying the payer's last observed `version` of the target `Invoice`. A mismatch with the invoice's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Partial payments legitimately stack, so a second sequential payment against the same `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice would otherwise simply succeed; the version check is what pins the payer's view of `amount_due_cents`.
- Changing a subscription's cadence requires `expected_subscription_version`, carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `Subscription`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A later cadence change legitimately supersedes an earlier one, so nothing about the state forbids it; the version check pins the allowance and fee the caller believed they were re-anchoring from.

All three fields are non-optional on their endpoints. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect. As stated at rung 12, this check is evaluated *before* any state precondition, so a request that is both stale and illegally timed reports the stale version.

### 2.8. Money, Minutes and Rounding

Every monetary field in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no decimal currency amounts anywhere in the API surface. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, it is resolved by **rounding half up** — to the nearest integer, with ties resolved away from zero, so a value of negative two and one half cents rounds to negative three cents.

Rounding *order* is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, and three places pin it explicitly. The subscription proration applies exactly one rounding to the whole product of fee and day fraction, never a per-day rounding followed by a multiplication. Invoice tax is computed once over the aggregate taxable subtotal and rounded once; rounding the tax of each taxable line and summing those roundings is explicitly *not* the contract and produces different cents on multi-line invoices. And item pricing has exactly two rounding points, named in §3.3, applied in the order given there.

Minutes are always whole minutes and are never rounded, because every input to a minute calculation is already an integer. Day counts are whole days. The one place a fractional quantity is converted to a whole count by rounding *up* rather than half up is the loaner late-day count in §3.8, and that is called out there.

### 2.9. Parallel Status Axes

Thirteen status axes live in this system, and two rows carry more than one of them. A `ServiceRequest` carries a **fulfilment axis** in `status`, moving through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_SERVICE`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, and, entirely independently on the very same row, a **billing axis** in `billing_status`, moving through `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED`, `SETTLED` and `CREDITED`. No transition on either axis moves the other; the two are read off the same row and reasoned about separately. A `RequestItem` carries a **work axis** in `service_status` — `INTAKE_PENDING`, `ASSESSED`, `IN_WORK`, `DONE`, `DELIVERED`, `REWORK_QUEUED`, `RETURNED_UNSERVICED`, `ABANDONED` — and a **custody axis** in `custody_location` — `CUSTOMER`, `VAN`, `BENCH`, `LOCKER`, `DELIVERED`, `ABANDONED` — plus a third, narrower **authorization axis** in `authorization_status` taking `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `DECLINED`. Note that `DELIVERED` and `ABANDONED` appear as values on both of the item's principal axes and mean different things on each: on the work axis they describe what has happened to the job, on the custody axis they describe where the metal physically is.

The remaining axes are one per entity: `Account.status` (`ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`), `Subscription.status` (`ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCELLED`), `RouteRun.status` (`PLANNED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`), `RouteStop.status` (`SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED`, `SERVICED`, `SKIPPED`, `SPILLED`, `CANCELLED`), `LockerCompartment.status` (`AVAILABLE`, `ASSIGNED`, `OCCUPIED`, `HOLD_EXPIRED`), `LoanerUnit.status` (`IN_STOCK`, `ISSUED`, `INSPECTION`, `RETIRED`), `LoanerIssue.status` (`OPEN`, `RETURNED`), `WarrantyClaim.status` (`SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`) and `Invoice.status` (`DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID`, `VOID`).

Terminal states matter because of rung 13. On the subscription axis, `CANCELLED` is terminal. On the request fulfilment axis, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; on its billing axis, `CREDITED` is terminal. On the item work axis, `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal; on its custody axis, `ABANDONED` is terminal. On the run axis, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; on the stop axis, `SERVICED`, `SKIPPED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. `RETIRED` is terminal for a loaner unit and `RETURNED` for a loaner issue; `APPROVED` is terminal for a warranty claim; `PAID` and `VOID` are terminal for an invoice. The account axis and the compartment axis have no terminal states at all — both cycle indefinitely.

Three of these axes are genuinely cyclic and the cycles are intentional. A stop can go `SCHEDULED → SPILLED → SCHEDULED` on the same row, bounded to two hops. A claim can go `SUBMITTED → DENIED → SUBMITTED`, bounded to two resubmissions. And an item's custody runs `CUSTOMER → VAN → BENCH → DELIVERED → CUSTOMER`, closed by the warranty cascade, which is what allows the same physical blade to be serviced twice under one original request.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Six named quantities must balance after every request completes, whether that request succeeded or failed. They are the audit contract of the system, and every workflow in §3 is written so that they hold.

**Custody conservation.** Every `RequestItem` holds exactly one `custody_location` from `CUSTOMER`, `VAN`, `BENCH`, `LOCKER`, `DELIVERED` and `ABANDONED` at all times — never none, never two — and every `ServiceRequest`'s `item_count` equals the number of `RequestItem` rows whose `service_request_id` names it. The compensating pair to check this against is pickup followed by a warranty approval: the approval returns custody from `DELIVERED` back to `CUSTOMER` without changing `item_count` at all.

**Route minute conservation.** A run's `booked_service_minutes` equals the sum of `stop_service_minutes` over that run's stops whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`, and its `scheduled_stop_count` equals the number of those same stops. Adding a stop and then deleting it must return both fields to exactly their pre-add values.

**Bench minute conservation.** A bench day's `booked_minutes` equals the sum, over every `RequestItem` whose `bench_day_id` names that day and whose `custody_location` is `BENCH`, of that item's `service_minutes_assessed` when it is set and its `service_minutes_declared` otherwise. Bench intake followed by delivery — or by a decline — must return `booked_minutes` to its pre-intake value.

**Loaner unit conservation.** For every class spec and grade, the count of `LoanerUnit` rows in `IN_STOCK` plus those in `ISSUED` plus those in `INSPECTION` plus those in `RETIRED` equals the total number of units ever created for that spec and grade; and the number of units in `ISSUED` equals the number of `LoanerIssue` rows in `OPEN`. Issuing a loaner and then returning it undamaged, with the unit still short of its cycle ceiling, must return the in-stock count to its pre-issue value.

**Allowance ledger conservation.** For each subscription and each `cycle_index`, the sum of `quantity` over `GRANTED` entries, minus the sum over `CONSUMED` entries, minus the sum over `EXPIRED` entries, plus the sum over `RESTORED` entries, equals that subscription's `allowance_remaining`, evaluated over the current cycle. Creating an invoice and then voiding it writes restoring entries that return `allowance_remaining` to its pre-invoice value.

**Money conservation.** An invoice's `amount_due_cents` equals its `total_cents` minus its `amount_paid_cents`; its `amount_paid_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over the `Payment` rows against it; and an account's `outstanding_balance_cents` equals the sum of `amount_due_cents` over that account's invoices whose status is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. Invoices in `DRAFT`, `PAID` or `VOID` never contribute. Issuing an invoice and then voiding it while unpaid must return `outstanding_balance_cents` to its pre-issue value. The sum is allowed to go negative, which represents a customer credit balance created by a credit memo.

### 2.11. Nested Retrieval and Path Resolution

Path parameters are always literally named by the resource of the segment immediately preceding them, and are resolved strictly left to right. Where a path names a parent and then a child — the lines of one invoice, the stops added to one run — the parent must resolve first, and a request naming a non-existent parent is answered with the parent's not-found code even if the child id is also unknown. A child that exists but belongs to a different parent than the one in the path is treated as not found for that path, reported with the child's own not-found code — a rule stated for completeness that never actually has an occasion to fire, because no path in this API carries two ids: the only nested paths are `/sharpening/invoices/{id}/lines` and `/sharpening/route-runs/{id}/stops`, and both name a parent followed by a collection rather than a parent followed by a child id. Every child resource is instead addressed by its own top-level path — `/sharpening/route-stops/{id}`, `/sharpening/request-items/{id}`, `/sharpening/invoice-lines/{id}` — where only rung 3 on that single id applies.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuration, Accounts and the Money Gate

Before any customer work can happen a `PlatformAdmin` has to lay down the reference data the rest of the system prices and schedules against. **Plan tiers** describe what a standing-route subscription buys: each carries a `code` such as a short tier label — conventionally unique across plan tiers, though nothing enforces it: there is no duplicate-plan-tier error in the catalogue and a second tier reusing an existing `code` is accepted without complaint — a `monthly_fee_cents` base fee, an `included_items_per_cycle` base allowance, an `overage_discount_pct` expressed as a fraction of at least zero and strictly below one, and an `allowed_cadences` array naming which of `WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY` and `MONTHLY` a subscription on this tier may take. The array must be non-empty and its members distinct. Both the fee and the allowance are integers of at least zero, and both are *base* figures — the cadence multiplies them, as §3.2 explains.

**Item class specs** are the heart of the pricing and scheduling model. Exactly one spec exists per edge-geometry class, and the seven classes are `CHEF_KNIFE`, `SERRATED_BREAD`, `SALON_SHEAR`, `CLIPPER_BLADE`, `DELI_SLICER_BLADE`, `GARDEN_PRUNER` and `MANDOLINE_BLADE`. A second spec declaring a class that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CLASS_SPEC`. Each spec records a `bevel_angle_degrees` grind angle between 10 and 60 inclusive, which is printed on the work ticket and drives nothing; a `base_service_minutes` figure of at least one minute, being the shop time a routine hone of this class takes; a `list_price_cents` non-subscriber price for that same routine hone; a `required_certification` drawn from `NONE`, `SERRATED`, `CLIPPER`, `SHEAR` and `SLICER`; a `price_tier` reporting band drawn from `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` and `TIER_3`, which is likewise reported only; a `min_locker_size` naming the smallest compartment that can physically hold the class, drawn from `SMALL`, `MEDIUM` and `LARGE`; and a `loaner_eligible` flag defaulting to false that decides whether a substitute tool may be lent out while this class is in the shop.

**Technicians** carry a `full_name` and a `certifications` array whose members are drawn from `SERRATED`, `CLIPPER`, `SHEAR` and `SLICER`; the array may be empty and its members must be distinct. **Vans** carry a fleet `code` and two defaults that runs inherit: `default_service_minute_budget`, which is 420 minutes when omitted, and `default_stop_cap`, which is 8 stops when omitted; both are integers of at least one. **Bench days** carry a unique `bench_date` and a `bench_minute_budget` of at least one minute, defaulting to 480 minutes, alongside a live `booked_minutes` figure that the shop reads to see how full the day already is. A second bench day for a date that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BENCH_DAY`. **Locker compartments** carry a `site_code` naming the physical location and a `size` from `SMALL`, `MEDIUM` and `LARGE`.

Accounts are created by the `PlatformAdmin` with a `name`, an `account_type` from `RESIDENTIAL`, `RESTAURANT`, `SALON` and `COMMERCIAL`, a `zone_code` from `ZONE_N`, `ZONE_S`, `ZONE_E` and `ZONE_W`, and a `contact_email` that is stored for notification purposes and otherwise unused. Three further inputs are optional: `billing_mode`, defaulting to `PER_VISIT` and otherwise `MONTHLY_ROLLUP`; `tax_exempt`, defaulting to false; and `credit_limit_cents`, an integer of at least zero defaulting to 50000 cents. The account type is reported on the statement and drives nothing by itself. The zone code, by contrast, is load-bearing: a stop may only be placed on a run whose `zone_code` equals the account's. A new account opens with `status` `ACTIVE` and `outstanding_balance_cents` of 0. The billing configuration — `billing_mode`, `tax_exempt`, `credit_limit_cents` and `contact_email` — can be revised later by an administrator, and such a revision advances the account's `version`. The update is a partial one over exactly those four fields: a field omitted from the body is left alone, a field supplied as an explicit `null` is likewise left alone rather than cleared, no other account field can be written this way (`name`, `account_type`, `zone_code` and `status` are immutable after creation), and the call still succeeds and still advances `version` even when it carries nothing that changes.

Suspension is the coarsest lever in the system. Moving an account `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` requires it to currently be `ACTIVE`, and it increments the account's `version`. While suspended, every account-scoped mutating endpoint refuses with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` at rung 9 — and because that rung sits before body-reference existence, a suspended account is reported as such even when the same body also names a foreign key that does not exist. Exactly three operations remain callable on a suspended account: suspension itself, reinstatement, and the billing-configuration update. Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, requires the account to currently be `SUSPENDED`, and likewise increments `version`.

The credit gate is finer. An account's `outstanding_balance_cents` is not a stored balance that drifts; it is always exactly the sum of `amount_due_cents` over that account's invoices whose status is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, recomputed whenever an invoice is issued, paid or voided. Draft, paid and voided invoices contribute nothing, and the figure is allowed to go negative when a credit memo outweighs the debits, which is simply a customer credit balance. Whenever that balance strictly exceeds `credit_limit_cents`, five new-work operations close: submitting a request, adding an item to a request, adding a stop to a run, assigning a locker compartment and issuing a loaner. All five refuse with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_BLOCKED`, and the refusal carries HTTP 402 rather than 403, because it is a payment problem and not a permission problem. A balance exactly equal to the limit passes; only a strict excess blocks.

A read-only statement pulls the account's money and allowance position together in one place: it reports the `account_id`, the live `outstanding_balance_cents`, a `draft_invoice_count` of that account's invoices currently in `DRAFT`, an `unbilled_request_count` of that account's service requests whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`, and the `allowance_remaining` of the account's subscription — zero when the account has none.

### 3.2. The Standing-Route Subscription

Restaurants and salons do not want to think about sharpening; they want a van on the doorstep on a rhythm. A subscription encodes that rhythm. Opening one names an `account_id`, a `plan_tier_id`, a `cadence` and an `anchor_date` in ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` form. It succeeds only if the account is `ACTIVE`, the account has no other subscription in `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` — a second live one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIPTION` — and the requested cadence is a member of the plan tier's `allowed_cadences`. A cadence that is a legal enum member but not on the tier's list is not an enum error; it is rejected by rung 14 as an illegal transition precondition, with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Two figures are derived from the cadence at once, and they are recomputed together whenever the cadence changes. The **effective monthly fee** is the plan tier's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by the cadence's fee multiplier and then rounded half up to a whole number of cents; the multiplier is 1.75 for `WEEKLY`, 1.25 for `BIWEEKLY` and 1.00 for `MONTHLY`. The result is stored as `effective_monthly_fee_cents`. The **cycle allowance** is the plan tier's `included_items_per_cycle` multiplied by the cadence's allowance multiplier, which is 4 for `WEEKLY`, 2 for `BIWEEKLY` and 1 for `MONTHLY`; the product is an exact integer and needs no rounding. It is stored as `included_items_this_cycle`. A weekly customer therefore pays one and three-quarter times the monthly headline fee and gets four times the headline allowance.

A new subscription opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `cycle_index` 1 and `cycle_start_date` equal to the supplied `anchor_date`. The derived `cycle_end_date` is always the `cycle_start_date` plus 30 days and is the *exclusive* upper bound of the cycle — a clock sitting exactly on `cycle_end_date` is already outside the cycle. The opening also grants the first cycle's allowance: a ledger entry of kind `GRANTED` is appended with `quantity` equal to `included_items_this_cycle` and a `reason` naming the cycle opening, and `allowance_remaining` is set to that same number. When `included_items_this_cycle` computes to zero, no ledger entry is written at all — the ledger never carries a zero-quantity row, because `quantity` is always a positive count and the `kind` carries the sign.

**The cadence series.** The next standing visit is derived from the anchor and the interval, where the interval is 7 days for `WEEKLY`, 14 for `BIWEEKLY` and 28 for `MONTHLY`. Candidate dates are the anchor date plus whole multiples of the interval. Two variants of the derivation exist and which one applies depends entirely on the operation that triggered it. Under the **anchored** variant — used at subscription creation, at a customer skip and at cycle advance — `next_service_date` is the candidate for the smallest whole multiple of at least zero whose date is on or after the logical clock's date; that is, the anchor itself qualifies when it has not yet passed. Under the **re-anchored** variant — used on resume from pause and on a cadence change, both of which first reset `anchor_date` to the logical clock's date — `next_service_date` is the candidate for the smallest whole multiple of at least *one*, so the series always lands a full interval out even though the freshly reset anchor would itself have qualified under the anchored variant. That difference of one is the entire distinction between the two variants and it is deliberate: a customer who resumes today does not get a visit today.

**Pausing.** A pause moves the subscription `ACTIVE → PAUSED`, is available only from `ACTIVE`, and drives four consequences at once. The `paused_at` stamp is set to the logical clock. Any allowance still standing is destroyed: a ledger entry of kind `EXPIRED` is appended with `quantity` equal to the current `allowance_remaining` and a `reason` naming the pause — skipped entirely when the remainder is already zero — and `allowance_remaining` is set to 0. The `next_service_date` is cleared to null. And the customer is credited for the unused part of the cycle: a proration line is appended to the account's draft invoice, creating that draft invoice when none exists, after which that invoice's totals are recomputed. The pause also increments the subscription's `version`.

The **proration** arithmetic is shared by pause and cadence change and has one input worth stating carefully: the number of unused days, which is the whole number of days from the logical clock's date up to the `cycle_end_date`, floored at zero so it is never negative. Under the **pause** variant, the line amount is the negative of the effective monthly fee multiplied by the unused days divided by a 30-day proration basis, with a single half-up rounding applied to that whole product; it is therefore always a credit, negative or zero. Under the **cadence-change** variant, the line amount is the *difference* between the new effective monthly fee and the old one, multiplied by the same unused-days fraction over the same 30-day basis, again with a single half-up rounding of the whole product; that amount is positive when the customer moves up to a more frequent cadence and negative when they move down, and it prices only the fee difference rather than the whole fee. Under neither variant is a per-day amount rounded and then multiplied — the rounding happens exactly once, at the end.

**Resuming.** A resume moves the subscription `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, is available only from `PAUSED`, and additionally requires the owning account to be `ACTIVE`. It re-anchors `anchor_date` to the logical clock's date, increments `cycle_index` by one and sets `cycle_start_date` to that same date, appends a `GRANTED` ledger entry for `included_items_this_cycle` with a `reason` naming the resume — again skipped when that number is zero — sets `allowance_remaining` to `included_items_this_cycle`, resets `skip_count_this_cycle` to 0, computes `next_service_date` under the re-anchored variant, and clears `paused_at` back to null.

**Changing cadence.** This operation leaves the subscription in `ACTIVE` — it is the self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`, and the observable change is the cadence, the allowance and the anchor, not the state name. It requires the subscription to be `ACTIVE`, requires `expected_subscription_version` to match, requires the new `cadence` to differ from the current one and to be a member of the tier's `allowed_cadences`, and requires the logical clock to be at least 48 hours before midnight UTC at the start of the current `next_service_date` — a late change is `ERR_CADENCE_CHANGE_TOO_LATE`. On success the cadence is written, the effective fee and cycle allowance are recomputed from the new multipliers, and the allowance difference is booked to the ledger: when the new allowance is larger, a `GRANTED` entry for the absolute difference; when it is smaller, an `EXPIRED` entry for the absolute difference; in both cases with a `reason` naming the cadence change. `allowance_remaining` becomes the greater of zero and its old value plus the new allowance minus the old allowance. The anchor is reset to the logical clock's date and `next_service_date` recomputed under the re-anchored variant. A proration line under the cadence-change variant is appended to the account's draft invoice, creating that invoice when none exists, and the invoice totals are recomputed. Finally the subscription's `version` increments.

**Skipping.** A customer may skip the next standing visit while the subscription is `ACTIVE`, provided `next_service_date` is not null and the logical clock is at least 48 hours before midnight UTC at the start of that date; exactly 48 hours before passes, and anything later is `ERR_SKIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The skip is also a self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`. It advances `next_service_date` by exactly one cadence interval, increments `skip_count_this_cycle` by one — a purely reporting counter that no rule anywhere reads, capped by nothing and reset only at resume and at cycle advance — and reaches out into the route: every `SCHEDULED` stop belonging to this account whose run date equals the skipped service date moves `SCHEDULED → SKIPPED`, and each such stop's service request returns `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, releasing its minutes back to the run. The stop and the request each advance their own `version`; the run does not, since its aggregates are derived. The reach-out is indifferent to the run's own status, so a stop still `SCHEDULED` on a `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS` run for that date is skipped just the same. Note that the skip window and the cadence-change window use the same 48-hour rule against the same date but report different codes, because they are different rungs.

**Advancing the cycle.** An administrator closes the current cycle and opens the next; this too is a self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`. The subscription must be `ACTIVE` and the logical clock's date must have reached or passed `cycle_end_date`. Because allowance rollover is disabled in this system, the closing act is destructive: an `EXPIRED` ledger entry is appended for whatever `allowance_remaining` still stands, with a `reason` naming the cycle end, skipped when that figure is zero. Then `cycle_index` increments by one and `cycle_start_date` is set to the previous `cycle_end_date`, `next_service_date` is recomputed under the anchored variant, a `GRANTED` entry for `included_items_this_cycle` is appended with a `reason` naming the cycle opening — skipped when that figure is zero — `allowance_remaining` is set to `included_items_this_cycle`, and `skip_count_this_cycle` resets to 0.

**Cancelling.** Cancellation is terminal and reachable from both live states — `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` and `PAUSED → CANCELLED` — and the two paths differ in exactly one respect. From `ACTIVE`, an `EXPIRED` ledger entry is appended for the standing `allowance_remaining` with a `reason` naming the cancellation — skipped when it is already zero — then `allowance_remaining` is set to 0 and `next_service_date` to null. From `PAUSED`, only `next_service_date` is cleared: no further expiry entry is written, because the allowance was already expired at the moment of pause and writing a second one would double-count against the ledger conservation rule.

Every movement of allowance, in either direction, leaves a row in the allowance ledger. Each row records the `subscription_id` it belongs to, the `cycle_index` it was booked against, a `kind` of `GRANTED`, `CONSUMED`, `EXPIRED` or `RESTORED`, a positive `quantity`, a short `reason` label naming the operation that produced it, and — on consumption and restoration entries only — the `request_item_id` whose invoicing moved the credit. Grant and expiry entries carry no item and leave `request_item_id` null. The ledger is queryable and is the audit trail the cycle balance in §2.10 is reconciled against.

### 3.3. Service Requests, Items and the Quoted Price

A **service request** is one visit's worth of work. The customer opens it by naming the `account_id`, the `channel` and a `requested_date`, and may additionally supply a `subscription_id`, a `priority`, a `locker_site_code` and a `locker_high_value_authorized` flag. The request is born in `status` `DRAFT` and `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, with `item_count` and both money totals at 0.

Three intake channels exist. `VAN_ROUTE` means the items are collected at a route stop. `LOCKER` means the customer deposits them into a compartment themselves, and for such a request `locker_site_code` is required — omitting it on a locker request is a missing required field and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — while on the other two channels it is ignored in the sense that nothing reads it, though a value supplied there is still stored on the row and echoed back on every read of the request. The same holds for `locker_high_value_authorized`, which is recorded on any channel and consulted only by the locker high-value rule of §3.6. `REWORK` is reserved: it is never accepted from a client. Although `REWORK` is a declared member of the channel enumeration, a client that supplies it is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` at the request-shape gate rather than with an enum error, because the value is well formed and simply not client-writable. Rework requests are created only by the system, as a link in the warranty cascade, and such a request is the only kind that carries a non-null `origin_warranty_claim_id`. The `priority` is `STANDARD` unless the customer asks for `RUSH`, and the `requested_date` must not precede the logical clock's date — an earlier date is `ERR_DATE_IN_PAST`. When a `subscription_id` is supplied it must not name a `CANCELLED` subscription; that is `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. A request with no subscription named is simply a non-subscriber visit and never touches an allowance.

Items are then added to the draft, one call per physical blade, each naming an `item_class_spec_id`, the `item_class` the customer believes it to be, and a `declared_damage_tier`. The declared class must equal the `item_class` of the referenced spec, or the addition is `ERR_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH`. A request may hold at most 8 items; a ninth is `ERR_ITEM_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Items may only be added while the request is `DRAFT` — afterwards the addition is `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_MUTABLE`. Every item opens with `service_status` `INTAKE_PENDING`, `custody_location` `CUSTOMER`, `authorization_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, `allowance_applied` false, `requires_reauthorization` false, and null values for `bench_day_id`, `rework_of_request_item_id`, `assessed_by_technician_id`, `assessed_damage_tier`, `service_minutes_assessed` and `delivered_at`.

**The damage tier fork** runs through the entire pricing and scheduling model. Four tiers exist: `ROUTINE_HONE`, `CHIP_REPAIR`, `RE_TIP` and `HANDLE_WORK`, in ascending order of severity. Each tier carries three constants. Its **price multiplier** is 1.00, 1.60, 2.25 and 3.00 respectively. Its **minutes supplement** is 0, 6, 12 and 20 minutes respectively. And its **parts fee** is 0, 0, 400 and 900 cents respectively — only re-tipping and handle work consume parts, and the parts fee is a separate, non-taxable amount that is never folded into the service price.

From those, two derivations follow immediately at item creation. The item's **declared minutes**, stored in `service_minutes_declared`, are the spec's `base_service_minutes` plus the minutes supplement of the `declared_damage_tier`. This is the figure the route and the bench plan against until a technician has actually seen the blade. The item's **quoted gross**, stored in `quoted_gross_cents`, is the spec's `list_price_cents` multiplied by the price multiplier of the `declared_damage_tier`, rounded half up to whole cents. That figure is what the customer was promised. `final_gross_cents` is set to the same value at creation and stays equal to `quoted_gross_cents` until an assessment replaces it. Adding an item also refreshes the parent request's `item_count` — always the count of item rows naming it — and its `quoted_total_cents`, which is the sum of `quoted_gross_cents` over the request's items.

The same two derivations exist in an **assessed** flavour that a technician's inspection produces later. The assessed minutes, stored in `service_minutes_assessed`, are the spec's `base_service_minutes` plus the minutes supplement of the `assessed_damage_tier`; the assessed gross, stored in `final_gross_cents`, is `list_price_cents` multiplied by that tier's price multiplier, rounded half up. Because an assessment may find the blade in better or worse shape than the customer declared, the assessed figures can be lower or higher than the declared ones in either direction — there is no assumption that assessment only ever raises the price.

Submitting the draft moves the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. It requires the request to currently be `DRAFT`, to hold at least one item, to belong to an `ACTIVE` account, and to pass the credit gate. Submission freezes `quoted_total_cents` from the items as they stand at that moment. The request's `final_total_cents` is a live figure rather than a frozen one: it is the sum, over the request's items whose `service_status` is neither `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` nor `ABANDONED`, of `charged_service_cents` plus `part_fee_cents`. Both of those inputs open at 0 and are written at different moments, which makes the figure read low for most of a request's life: `part_fee_cents` is first written by assessment, but `charged_service_cents` is written only by invoice creation, in step five of §3.10. A request that has been assessed, worked, delivered and completed but not yet invoiced therefore reports a `final_total_cents` consisting of parts fees alone, and only after the invoice exists does the figure carry the service money. Nothing subsequently resets either input: voiding the invoice leaves both at the values it wrote, so `final_total_cents` does not fall back to zero on a void.

Cancellation is available from three fulfilment states — `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, `SUBMITTED → CANCELLED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` — and does a different amount of cleanup from each. From `DRAFT`, nothing is released, because nothing was ever booked. From `SUBMITTED`, any compartment currently reserved for this request returns `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`, its `access_code` and `current_service_request_id` are cleared, and its `last_released_at` is stamped with the logical clock. From `SCHEDULED`, the request's scheduled stop moves `SCHEDULED → SKIPPED` and the run's `booked_service_minutes`, `scheduled_stop_count` and `committed_minutes_provisional` are all recomputed. From `IN_SERVICE` or later, cancellation is not available at all — the request is by then in the shop's hands, and rung 13 or 14 refuses it.

`RUSH` priority carries a surcharge, but it is applied late, at invoicing rather than at quoting, and it is described with the rest of the charged-amount arithmetic in §3.10. What matters here is only that the flag is set on the request and applies uniformly to every item on it.

### 3.4. Planning the Van Route

A `Dispatcher` plans a **route run** by naming the `van_id`, the `technician_id`, the `run_date` and the `zone_code` the run will cover. Four inputs are optional and fall back on defaults: `service_minute_budget` and `stop_cap` are copied from the chosen van's `default_service_minute_budget` and `default_stop_cap` when omitted, and `day_window_start` and `day_window_end` default to 08:00 and 17:00 local time respectively. The run is born `PLANNED` with `scheduled_stop_count`, `booked_service_minutes` and `committed_minutes_provisional` all at 0 and `committed_minutes_final` null. A `run_date` earlier than the logical clock's date is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_PAST`.

Placing a **stop** on a run is the operation where all the scheduling constraints bite at once, and it is also one of the three version-guarded writes. The body names the `service_request_id` being scheduled, the `sequence` the stop takes in the visit order, the `arrival_window_start` as an `HH:MM` local time, and the `expected_route_run_version`. The `sequence` is a 1-based visit order that the dispatcher is expected to keep unique within a run among its non-terminal stops; the server validates only that it is an integer of at least 1 and does not police uniqueness, so a duplicate `sequence` on the same run is accepted. The one rule that reads it is the dispatch overflow loop, which sheds the greatest `sequence` first and, on a tie, sheds whichever of the tied stops the scan reaches first. The stop's `arrival_window_end` is always derived, never supplied: it is the start plus 60 minutes, and it is an *exclusive* upper bound. The stop also copies the `account_id` off the service request at creation, and opens with `spill_hop_count` 0, `spilled_from_route_run_id` null and `status` `SCHEDULED`.

The checks a stop addition must clear, in the order they are evaluated: the run must be `PLANNED`, or the answer is `ERR_RUN_NOT_MUTABLE`; the account's `zone_code` must equal the run's `zone_code`, or `ERR_STOP_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; the arrival window must lie wholly inside the run's day window — the start at or after `day_window_start`, and the start plus 60 minutes at or before `day_window_end` — or `ERR_WINDOW_OUT_OF_DAY`; the window must not intersect the window of any stop on the same run whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`, where intersection is judged on half-open intervals so that two windows merely touching at a boundary are accepted, or `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; adding the stop must not push `scheduled_stop_count` past `stop_cap`, or `ERR_STOP_CAP_EXCEEDED`; and the recomputed provisional minutes must not exceed `service_minute_budget`, equality being acceptable, or `ERR_RUN_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`. The request itself must be `SUBMITTED` and must be on the `VAN_ROUTE` or `REWORK` channel — a locker request never takes a stop. On success the request moves `SUBMITTED → SCHEDULED`, the run's aggregates are recomputed, and the run's `version` increments.

**Committed minutes** are what the two capacity aggregates on a run actually measure, and they come in two variants that differ in both the per-item figure used and the buffers charged. In both variants, the active stops of a run are those whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`; a stop's billable items are the items of its service request whose `service_status` is neither `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` nor `ABANDONED`; and `booked_service_minutes` is the sum of `stop_service_minutes` over the active stops.

Under the **provisional** variant — recomputed on every stop add, stop delete, spill and reschedule while the run is `PLANNED`, and the variant the feasibility check at rung 53 tests against — each stop's `stop_service_minutes` is the sum of `service_minutes_declared` over its billable items, and `committed_minutes_provisional` is `booked_service_minutes` plus 14 minutes of drive buffer for each active stop. No return-leg buffer is charged under this variant. The provisional variant is also the *only* one the reported fields ever use: `booked_service_minutes` and `committed_minutes_provisional` on a run, and `stop_service_minutes` on a stop, are recomputed from declared minutes at every read for the whole life of the row, including after dispatch and after completion. The dispatch-final variant is never reported through them; its result is written once, to `committed_minutes_final`, and nowhere else. A dispatched run whose items were assessed will therefore show a `committed_minutes_final` that does not agree with its own `booked_service_minutes` plus buffers, and that disagreement is expected rather than a fault.

Under the **dispatch-final** variant — computed once, at dispatch — each stop's `stop_service_minutes` is the sum, over its billable items, of `service_minutes_assessed` when that is set and `service_minutes_declared` otherwise, and `committed_minutes_final` is `booked_service_minutes` plus 14 minutes of drive buffer for each active stop plus a single 25-minute return-leg buffer for the run as a whole. The assessed branch is genuinely reachable at dispatch time, and the reason is worth spelling out because it is not obvious: a warranty rework item is created already carrying an `assessed_damage_tier` of `ROUTINE_HONE` while its `declared_damage_tier` is copied from the original item's assessed tier, so a rework stop can carry assessed minutes before its run has ever been dispatched.

Deleting a stop is available only while its run is `PLANNED` and the stop is `SCHEDULED`. It removes the stop's row, returns the service request `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, releases the stop's minutes from `booked_service_minutes` and recomputes `scheduled_stop_count` and `committed_minutes_provisional`, and increments the run's `version`. It answers with no body at all. Deletion and addition are exact inverses on the run's two aggregates, which is what the route-minute conservation rule in §2.10 checks.

### 3.5. Dispatch, Spill, Reschedule and Running the Day

**Dispatch** is the moment a plan becomes a commitment, and it is the only place where the system will silently shed work. A run may be dispatched only from `PLANNED`, only when it carries at least one stop in `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`, and only when its `run_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's date. On dispatch, `committed_minutes_final` is computed under the dispatch-final variant. If that figure exceeds `service_minute_budget`, the run sheds its tail: the `SCHEDULED` stop with the greatest `sequence` is moved to `SPILLED`, its service request returns `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, and `committed_minutes_final` is recomputed. That loop repeats until the committed figure fits inside the budget, or until no `SCHEDULED` stop is left to shed — whichever comes first. Each such spill increments the affected stop's `spill_hop_count`; if any of those increments would take a stop past two hops, the entire dispatch fails with `ERR_SPILL_LIMIT_REACHED` and nothing is changed, which is why the whole shedding plan is decided before any of it is written. Two details follow from the loop's shape and must not be softened. The 25-minute return-leg buffer is charged unconditionally, including in the final recomputation after every stop has been shed, so a run whose budget is smaller than 25 minutes cannot be made to fit; and when the loop runs out of stops while still over budget it does not fail — the run moves `PLANNED → DISPATCHED` anyway and `committed_minutes_final` is written at the over-budget figure the last recomputation produced. The run then moves `PLANNED → DISPATCHED`.

Spilling can also be done deliberately, one stop at a time, while its run is `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED` and the stop is `SCHEDULED`. The stop keeps its row rather than being deleted — that is the whole point of the `SPILLED` status — and waits there for a reschedule. Spilling increments `spill_hop_count` by one, records the run it left in `spilled_from_route_run_id`, releases `stop_service_minutes` from the source run's `booked_service_minutes`, decrements that run's `scheduled_stop_count`, recomputes `committed_minutes_provisional`, and returns the service request `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`. A spill that would take the stop past two hops is refused with `ERR_SPILL_LIMIT_REACHED`. The stop's `route_run_id` is *not* cleared by the spill — it still names the run the stop just left, which is both what rung 15 reads when a reschedule is attempted and what makes `spilled_from_route_run_id` equal to it until a reschedule moves the stop elsewhere. Because the source run's three aggregates are all derived, the release costs the run nothing stored and its `version` does not advance; only the stop and its service request advance theirs.

**Rescheduling** takes a `SPILLED` stop and finds it a new home. The receiving run is chosen by the system, not named by the caller. Eligible runs are those that are still `PLANNED`, whose `zone_code` equals the `zone_code` of the account on the stop, whose `run_date` is strictly later than the `run_date` of the run the stop was spilled off, that can take one more stop without breaching their `stop_cap`, and whose `committed_minutes_provisional` plus this stop's provisional service minutes plus one further 14-minute drive buffer is at most their `service_minute_budget`. Candidates are ranked by `run_date` ascending, with ties broken by the run's `created_at` ascending, and the first is chosen. When no run qualifies, the reschedule fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RUN`.

Placement on the receiving run is likewise computed. The stop's new `sequence` is one greater than the largest `sequence` among that run's stops in `SCHEDULED`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`, or 1 when the run has none. The new `arrival_window_start` is the earliest time at or after the receiving run's `day_window_start`, on a grid of 60-minute steps, whose resulting 60-minute window neither overlaps an existing non-terminal stop's window on that run nor ends after `day_window_end`; when no such slot exists, the reschedule fails with `ERR_WINDOW_OUT_OF_DAY`. On success the stop moves `SPILLED → SCHEDULED`, its `route_run_id` is re-pointed at the chosen run, its minutes are re-booked there, and the service request moves `SUBMITTED → SCHEDULED`. What the stop does **not** do is forget where it came from: `spilled_from_route_run_id` is left pointing at the run it was spilled off and is never cleared, so a rescheduled stop reads as `SCHEDULED` on its new run while still naming its old one, and `spill_hop_count` likewise stays at the count it reached. That surviving pointer is the audit trail of the hop and is also the field the `spilled_from_route_run_id` list filter searches on. The receiving run's `version` advances, as does the stop's and the request's; the run the stop left does not advance, having no stored field to change. The same zone, cap, window and minute checks that guard a fresh stop addition are nominally in force here too, but only the window check can actually fire: because the receiving run is chosen by the selector rather than named by the caller, and the selector applies the zone, stop-cap and minute tests itself while building the pool, a failure of any of those three surfaces as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RUN` at rung 50 and never as its own code. See rungs 27, 33, 52 and 53.

The `SCHEDULED → SPILLED → SCHEDULED` path is a genuine re-entry loop on a single stop row, and two hops is where it stops. Note also that a run may only be spilled *from*, never *onto*, by the dispatch loop — dispatch only ever removes stops. One asymmetry between the two ways a stop can be spilled is decisive and is easy to miss: only a stop spilled *deliberately*, off a run that remains `PLANNED`, can ever be rescheduled. A stop shed by the dispatch overflow loop still points at the run that shed it, and that run is `DISPATCHED` by the time the call returns, so rung 15 refuses every reschedule of it with `ERR_RUN_NOT_MUTABLE`. A dispatch-shed stop is therefore a dead end: its request is back at `SUBMITTED` and must be given a fresh stop on another run rather than rescheduled.

**On the day.** A `Technician` marks arrival at a stop, which moves it `SCHEDULED → ARRIVED` and requires its run to be `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. The first arrival on a run also moves the run itself `DISPATCHED → IN_PROGRESS` and increments the run's `version`; later arrivals leave the run's status alone. Pickup then collects the physical items: it requires the request to be `SCHEDULED`, on the `VAN_ROUTE` or `REWORK` channel, with its stop in `ARRIVED`. Pickup moves every item's `custody_location` from `CUSTOMER` to `VAN`, moves the stop `ARRIVED → SERVICED`, and moves the request `SCHEDULED → IN_SERVICE`. Van custody is not metered against any pool — the minutes were already committed when the stop was booked.

Completing a run requires it to be `IN_PROGRESS` and requires that no stop on it is still `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED`; the run moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and its `committed_minutes_final` is frozen at its current value.

### 3.6. The Locker Channel

A locker request bypasses the van entirely: the customer drives to a site, drops the blades in a compartment, and collects them from the same compartment when they are done. It still consumes exactly the same bench minutes as a van-route request, which is the whole reason the two channels compete.

**Assignment** picks the compartment. The customer asks for one while the request is `SUBMITTED` and on the `LOCKER` channel, and the system chooses. The required size rank is the largest, across the request's items, of the rank of that item's spec `min_locker_size`, where `SMALL` ranks 1, `MEDIUM` ranks 2 and `LARGE` ranks 3. A compartment is eligible when it is `AVAILABLE`, its `site_code` equals the request's `locker_site_code`, its own size rank is at least the required rank, its size's item capacity is at least the request's `item_count`, and either its `last_released_at` is null or the logical clock is at or after `last_released_at` plus 30 minutes. Item capacity by size is 4 for `SMALL`, 10 for `MEDIUM` and 20 for `LARGE`. Eligible compartments are ranked by size rank *ascending* — the smallest compartment that fits wins, so large compartments stay free for large jobs — with ties broken by `created_at` ascending.

When nothing is eligible, the reason is adjudicated in a fixed order and each reason has its own code. First: if the site named by `locker_site_code` has no compartments at all, the answer is `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_COMPARTMENT`. Second: if every available compartment at the site fails the size-rank or item-capacity test, the answer is `ERR_COMPARTMENT_SIZE_UNFIT`. Third: if at least one available compartment passes size and capacity but every such compartment fails the 30-minute turnaround test, the answer is `ERR_LOCKER_TURNAROUND_CONFLICT`. Otherwise — for instance when the site has compartments but none of them is `AVAILABLE` — the answer is `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_COMPARTMENT`. That order matters: a site whose only fitting compartment was released 10 minutes ago reports a turnaround conflict, not an absence.

On success the compartment moves `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED`, a 6-digit `access_code` is minted, `current_service_request_id` is set to the request, and `hold_expires_at` is set to the logical clock plus 72 hours. Assignment is one of the five credit-gated operations, and — unusually — it answers with the assigned compartment rather than with the service request that asked for it. The request itself is not written by the assignment at all: its `status` stays `SUBMITTED` and its `version` does not advance, and it holds no field naming the compartment — the link runs only one way, from the compartment's `current_service_request_id`. A consequence is that assignment is not idempotent and carries no guard of its own: calling it a second time while the request is still `SUBMITTED` selects and reserves a *second* compartment while leaving the first one `ASSIGNED` and still naming the request. Callers must not do that, and the operations that later look up "the compartment holding this request" resolve whichever of the two the server encounters first.

**Deposit** is the customer physically loading the compartment, and it requires the supplied `access_code` to equal the compartment's. The compartment must be `ASSIGNED` and holding this very request; the logical clock must be strictly before `hold_expires_at`, or the deposit is `ERR_LOCKER_HOLD_EXPIRED`. On success every item moves `custody_location` `CUSTOMER → LOCKER`, the compartment moves `ASSIGNED → OCCUPIED`, the request moves `SUBMITTED → IN_SERVICE`, and the compartment's two derived counters are recomputed. `stored_item_count` is the number of items of `current_service_request_id` whose `custody_location` is `LOCKER`. `stored_value_cents` is the sum of `final_gross_cents` over the items of `current_service_request_id` whose `custody_location` is `LOCKER` *or* `BENCH` — the value follows the metal into the shop, because the shop's liability does not end when the compartment empties.

That stored value is what the high-value rule guards. Whenever a technician re-prices a locker-channel item, the compartment's `stored_value_cents` after the re-pricing must not exceed 25000 cents, unless the request's `locker_high_value_authorized` is true; a breach is `ERR_LOCKER_VALUE_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED`. The flag is a customer pre-authorization supplied at request creation, and it is the only way an unattended compartment is permitted to hold that much value.

When the items leave for the bench, the compartment moves `OCCUPIED → ASSIGNED` rather than back to `AVAILABLE`: it is emptied, `stored_item_count` falls to 0, but the reservation is held for the return leg, so no other customer can be given it. When the finished items come back, the compartment moves `ASSIGNED → OCCUPIED` again and `hold_expires_at` is reset to the logical clock plus 72 hours, restarting the customer's collection window from the moment the work was actually finished.

**Release** is collection. It requires the compartment to be `OCCUPIED`, the logical clock to be strictly before `hold_expires_at`, every item of the holding request to have `service_status` `DELIVERED`, and the supplied `access_code` to match. On success the compartment moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE`, its `access_code` and `current_service_request_id` are cleared, `last_released_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and every item moves `custody_location` `LOCKER → DELIVERED` with `delivered_at` stamped — which, as §3.8 describes, closes any open loaner on that request and runs the inspection chain.

**Expiry and abandonment** handle the customer who never comes back. An administrator may move a compartment `OCCUPIED → HOLD_EXPIRED` once the logical clock has reached or passed `hold_expires_at`; that clears the `access_code` so the customer can no longer self-serve. Later, once the clock has reached `hold_expires_at` plus a further 168 hours of grace, the administrator may abandon the contents: every item of `current_service_request_id` moves `LOCKER → ABANDONED` on the custody axis and, on the work axis, moves to `ABANDONED` from whatever value it was sitting at when the grace ran out. `INTAKE_PENDING → ABANDONED` and `ASSESSED → ABANDONED` are the usual two, but they are not the only ones: the commonest case in practice is a finished job the customer never collected, whose items are back in the compartment at `DELIVERED` on the work axis, and `DELIVERED → ABANDONED` is taken exactly the same way. The work axis is overwritten unconditionally; no from-state is required of it. The compartment moves `HOLD_EXPIRED → AVAILABLE`, `current_service_request_id` is cleared, and `last_released_at` is stamped. Abandoned items are excluded from `final_total_cents` and from every capacity aggregate, so abandonment is also a release of any minutes they were still holding.

Note the deliberate ordering trap in the ladder: on a compartment whose hold has already expired, a deposit or release carrying the *wrong* access code reports `ERR_LOCKER_HOLD_EXPIRED`, not `ERR_ACCESS_CODE_INVALID`, because the hold check sits at rung 21 and the code check is the last rung of all.

### 3.7. The Bench: Intake, Assessment and Work

The shop's daily grinding capacity is a `BenchDay`, and both intake channels draw on it. **Bench intake** takes a whole request at once — the body names only the `bench_day_id` — and requires every one of the request's items to be in the same custody: all `VAN` for a van-route or rework request, all `LOCKER` for a locker request. A mixed or wrong custody is `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`, and so is a request carrying no items at all, since the requirement is read as "at least one item, every one of them in the expected custody". Custody is the *only* precondition intake imposes on the request: it declares no from-state on the fulfilment axis, so rungs 13 and 14 are simply not on this endpoint's ladder. In practice the custody test does the work a status test would have done — items reach `VAN` only through pickup and `LOCKER` only through deposit, both of which leave the request `IN_SERVICE` — but the check is on the items, not on the status, and re-intaking an already-benched request fails on custody (`BENCH` is neither `VAN` nor `LOCKER`) rather than on state. The named bench day's `bench_date` must equal the logical clock's date, or the intake is `ERR_BENCH_DAY_DATE_MISMATCH`; that check sits above the capacity check so that a technician booking onto the wrong day is told so rather than being told the day is full.

Capacity is then tested: the bench day's `booked_minutes` plus the sum of the incoming request's declared minutes must be at most its `bench_minute_budget`, with equality passing. A breach is `ERR_BENCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success each item's `bench_day_id` is set, each item's `custody_location` moves `VAN → BENCH` or `LOCKER → BENCH` according to where it came from, and each item's `service_minutes_declared` is added to `booked_minutes`. A locker request's compartment additionally moves `OCCUPIED → ASSIGNED`, keeping its reservation as described above.

`booked_minutes` is always, at any instant, the sum over every item whose `bench_day_id` names that day *and* whose `custody_location` is currently `BENCH` of that item's `service_minutes_assessed` when it is set and `service_minutes_declared` otherwise. Items that have left bench custody stop counting immediately. Every operation that changes an item's assessed minutes or moves it off the bench therefore adjusts the figure, and the bench-minute conservation rule in §2.10 is the statement that those adjustments always net out.

**Assessment** is the technician's verdict. The body carries the `assessed_damage_tier` the technician actually found. The item must be in `BENCH` custody, its parent request must be `IN_SERVICE`, and the acting technician's `certifications` must contain the spec's `required_certification` unless that requirement is `NONE` — a gap there is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. For a locker-channel item the high-value rule of §3.6 is checked against the compartment's re-priced `stored_value_cents`. On success the item records `assessed_damage_tier` and `assessed_by_technician_id`, recomputes `service_minutes_assessed`, recomputes `final_gross_cents` under the assessed flavour and `part_fee_cents` as the parts fee of the assessed tier, moves `service_status` `INTAKE_PENDING → ASSESSED`, and adjusts the bench day's `booked_minutes` by the difference between the assessed minutes and the declared minutes — which can be negative when the blade turned out to be in better shape than the customer thought.

**Re-authorization** protects the customer from a surprise. The system computes the delta between `final_gross_cents` and `quoted_gross_cents` and sets `requires_reauthorization` true only when the increase clears *both* of two thresholds: the delta must be strictly greater than 1000 cents in absolute terms, and the delta must also be strictly greater than 25 percent of the quoted gross. The percentage comparison is performed in basis points against integers — the delta multiplied by 10000 compared against the quoted gross multiplied by 2500 — so no floating-point comparison is involved anywhere. Both comparisons are strict: a delta that merely ties either threshold does not trip the rule. When `requires_reauthorization` comes out true the item's `authorization_status` is set to `PENDING`; otherwise it stays `NOT_REQUIRED`.

A `PENDING` item is frozen. Starting work requires `authorization_status` to be `NOT_REQUIRED` or `AUTHORIZED`, and a `PENDING` or `DECLINED` item refuses with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED`. The customer resolves it either way. **Authorizing** moves `authorization_status` `PENDING → AUTHORIZED` and nothing else. **Declining** is heavier: it moves `authorization_status` `PENDING → DECLINED` and `service_status` `ASSESSED → RETURNED_UNSERVICED`, zeroes both `charged_service_cents` and `part_fee_cents`, releases the item's minutes from the bench day's `booked_minutes`, and — when that item had already consumed a subscription allowance unit — appends an allowance ledger entry of kind `RESTORED` with `quantity` 1 and a `reason` naming the decline, raising `allowance_remaining` by exactly one. That last leg is written for completeness and can never actually fire, and the reason is worth stating so nobody chases the missing ledger row: `allowance_applied` is set on an item only by invoice creation, which requires its request to be `COMPLETED`, while a decline requires the same request to be `IN_SERVICE` and the item to be `ASSESSED` — and an item sitting at `ASSESSED` is precisely what blocks delivery, so the request can never have reached `COMPLETED`. The only path that clears `allowance_applied` back to false in practice is the invoice void of §3.10. The release of the bench minutes is effected by clearing the item's `bench_day_id` to null rather than by moving it off the bench: a declined item keeps `custody_location` `BENCH` and keeps it permanently, because delivery moves only the items that finished at `DONE` and leaves the returned-unserviced ones where they stand. A returned-unserviced item is terminal on the work axis and is excluded from the request's `final_total_cents` and from every capacity aggregate thereafter.

**Working the item** is two calls. Starting work moves `service_status` `ASSESSED → IN_WORK` and requires the item to be in `BENCH` custody, to be authorized, and the acting technician to hold the required certification — the same certification test as assessment, checked again because a different technician may pick the blade up. It increments the item's `version`. Completing work moves `IN_WORK → DONE` and likewise increments `version`. Both require the parent request to be `IN_SERVICE`.

### 3.8. Delivery, Loaner Rotation and Late Fees

**Delivery closes a request**, and its side-effect set forks on the intake channel. Delivery requires the request to be `IN_SERVICE` and requires every one of its items to have `service_status` `DONE` or `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` — one item still `IN_WORK` blocks the whole request. On success the request moves `IN_SERVICE → COMPLETED` and `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and every item still at `DONE` moves `DONE → DELIVERED` on the work axis. In every case the items' minutes are released from the bench day's `booked_minutes`.

For a `VAN_ROUTE` or `REWORK` request, each such item's `custody_location` moves `BENCH → DELIVERED` and its `delivered_at` is stamped. That stamp is what starts the warranty clock. Stamping it also closes the request's open loaner, if it has one, which in turn drives the inspection chain described below. For a `LOCKER` request, the fork runs the other way: each item's `custody_location` moves `BENCH → LOCKER`, the compartment moves `ASSIGNED → OCCUPIED`, and `hold_expires_at` is reset to the logical clock plus 72 hours. A locker item is therefore *not* delivered in the custody sense at this point and its `delivered_at` is not yet stamped — that happens only when the customer actually releases the compartment. This asymmetry is deliberate: the warranty window for a locker customer starts when they take the blade home, not when the shop finishes it.

It follows that the loaner legs of the delivery cascade do **not** fire on a locker request. Delivery of a van-route or rework request runs four linked steps: the finished items move to `DELIVERED` on both axes and `delivered_at` is stamped; that stamping closes the request's open loaner issue, moving it `OPEN → RETURNED`; that closure moves the loaner unit `ISSUED → INSPECTION` and computes the late fee; and the inspection resolves the unit to `RETIRED` or back to `IN_STOCK`. On a locker request none of the last three fire at delivery, because nothing was delivered in the custody sense; they fire later, when the customer releases the compartment. A request that never had a loaner issued against it likewise runs only the first step, whatever its channel.

**Loaners** keep a restaurant cutting while its knives are away. A loaner unit is added to the rotation pool for a specific `item_class_spec_id` at a `grade` of `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, with a `cycle_ceiling` of at least one defaulting to 12; the referenced spec must have `loaner_eligible` true, or the creation is `ERR_LOANER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A new unit is `IN_STOCK` with `cycle_count` 0 and `damage_reported` false.

Issuing a loaner names the `service_request_id` it covers, the `item_class_spec_id` it substitutes for, and the `requested_grade`. The unit is chosen by the system. Eligible units are those that are `IN_STOCK`, belong to the requested spec, and whose grade rank is at least the requested grade's rank, where `STANDARD` ranks 1 and `PREMIUM` ranks 2 — so a premium unit may stand in for a standard request, but never the other way round. Candidates are ranked first by whether their grade exactly equals `requested_grade`, exact matches ahead of substitutes; then by grade rank ascending, so the cheapest acceptable substitute is used before a dearer one; then by `cycle_count` ascending, which is the rotation rule proper and puts the least-worn unit into service first; and finally by `created_at` ascending. An empty candidate pool is `ERR_LOANER_STOCK_EXHAUSTED`. The chosen unit moves `IN_STOCK → ISSUED`; the issue record is born `OPEN`, records `loaner_unit_id`, stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, derives `due_at` as `issued_at` plus 14 days, and sets `substituted` true exactly when the issued unit's grade differs from `requested_grade`. `substituted` is a reporting flag that no rule reads. A grade substitution is free — the customer is never charged for the upgrade. Issuing is credit-gated, and the loaner-eligibility check applies here as well as at unit creation. Beyond those, issuing imposes nothing on the covered request: it declares no from-state on the fulfilment axis, does not require the request to hold an item of the substituted class, and does not check whether a loaner is already out against that request — so a second issue against the same request is accepted and leaves two `OPEN` issues, of which the delivery cascade will close whichever the server encounters first. Callers are expected to issue at most one.

**Return** happens either explicitly, with an optional `damage_reported` flag in the body, or implicitly as a link of the delivery cascade. Either way the issue moves `OPEN → RETURNED`, `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `delay_attribution` and `late_fee_cents` are computed, and the unit moves `ISSUED → INSPECTION`, recording `damage_reported` from the return.

The **late fee** turns on who is responsible for the delay, and the attribution is computed rather than asserted. Attribution is `SERVICE` when the issue's service request carries a non-null `origin_warranty_claim_id`, or when any item of that request has `service_status` `REWORK_QUEUED` at the moment of return; otherwise it is `CUSTOMER`. Under the **service-delay** variant the `late_fee_cents` is 0 no matter how far past the deadline the return is, because the shop owns a delay it caused by having the customer's blade back in for rework. Under the **customer-late** variant, the grace end is `due_at` plus 24 hours; the overdue seconds are the return instant minus the grace end, floored at zero; the late days are those overdue seconds divided by 86400 and rounded *up* to a whole number — this is the one ceiling in the system, and any part of a day counts as a whole day; and `late_fee_cents` is the lesser of a 2500-cent cap and the late days multiplied by 250 cents per day. A return at or before the grace end yields zero late days and therefore no fee. When the computed fee is greater than zero, a late-fee line is appended to the account's draft invoice, creating that draft invoice when none exists.

The **inspection chain** runs immediately after `ISSUED → INSPECTION` and always resolves in the same request; a unit never sits in `INSPECTION` between calls. The unit's `cycle_count` increments by one, and then exactly one of two branches is taken. If the incremented `cycle_count` has reached or passed `cycle_ceiling`, or `damage_reported` is true, the unit moves `INSPECTION → RETIRED`, which is terminal and removes it from the loanable pool — an effect that can push a later issue onto the substitute-grade branch of the selector, or all the way to `ERR_LOANER_STOCK_EXHAUSTED`. Otherwise the unit moves `INSPECTION → IN_STOCK` and rejoins the rotation with its higher `cycle_count`, which under the selector's ranking sends it to the back of the queue. Damage forces retirement regardless of how many cycles the unit had left.

### 3.9. Quality, Warranty and the Six-Link Cascade

A workmanship complaint is raised against one delivered item. The claim body names the `request_item_id` and a free-text `reason`. Two tests guard it beyond the ordinary ladder. The claimed item's service request must belong to the acting `AccountOwner`, or the claim is `ERR_CLAIM_ITEM_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` — this is the rung that the ownership carve-out in §2.1 defers to, and it deliberately reports a referential mismatch rather than a permission failure, because the caller is a legitimate account owner who simply named somebody else's blade. And the logical clock must not be later than the item's `delivered_at` plus 30 days, with exactly the boundary passing; beyond that it is `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Note that "delivered" is enforced at creation only through that second test — what the window rung actually requires is a non-null `delivered_at`, and an item that has never been delivered fails it, since a null stamp cannot satisfy the comparison and reports `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The item's current `service_status` is not examined at creation at all; the `DELIVERED` requirement is imposed later, by approval. So a claim may legitimately be raised against an item that has since moved on — one already `REWORK_QUEUED` from an earlier approved claim, for instance — and it is approval that will then refuse it with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A claim opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `resubmit_count` 0, `abuse_finding` false, `decided_by_technician_id` and `rework_service_request_id` null, `account_id` copied from the claimed item's request, and `credit_basis_cents` computed as described below.

The **credit basis** is the service amount the customer was actually charged for that blade, and nothing else. It is the `amount_cents` of the service-kind invoice line whose `request_item_id` names the claimed item, looked up across all of that account's non-voided invoices — and, should more than one such line somehow exist, the first one written, ties on `created_at` broken by insertion sequence as everywhere else; when no such line exists — for instance because the subscription allowance absorbed the item entirely and its line was written at zero, or because the item was never invoiced — the basis is 0. Parts are never credited: a re-tip whose parts fee was 400 cents gets those 400 cents back only if the shop chooses to, and this system does not, because a part fee line is a separate, non-taxable line that the warranty credit does not reach.

**Segregation of duties** governs both adjudication paths. The technician deciding a claim may not be the technician who assessed the item the claim is raised against: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the item's `assessed_by_technician_id`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. The rule applies identically to approval and to denial — there is no asymmetry where a self-denial would be permitted.

**Denial** is the short path. It requires the claim to be `SUBMITTED`, records `decided_by_technician_id` and the `abuse_finding` supplied in the body, and moves the claim `SUBMITTED → DENIED`. `abuse_finding` is a required boolean and is recorded verbatim; it is a reporting field only, read by no rule anywhere — it does not bar resubmission, does not change the credit and does not touch the item. Note also the one place denial is *less* demanding than approval: approval requires the claimed item to be at `service_status` `DELIVERED`, and denial imposes no condition on the item's state at all, so a claim can be denied whatever has since become of the blade. No rework order is created and no credit is emitted. A denied claim may be resubmitted by the customer, which moves it `DENIED → SUBMITTED`, increments `resubmit_count` by one, and clears `decided_by_technician_id` and `abuse_finding` back to their initial values. Resubmission requires `resubmit_count` to be strictly below 2 — the third resubmission is refused with `ERR_RESUBMIT_CAP_REACHED` — and it re-checks the 30-day warranty window against the item's `delivered_at`, so a claim that was denied late in the window can time out before the customer gets a second answer.

**Approval** is the long path, and it is a chain of six links in which each link is caused by the one before it rather than by the approval call directly. It requires the claim to be `SUBMITTED` and the claimed item to be at `service_status` `DELIVERED`.

*Link one.* The claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `decided_by_technician_id` is set to the acting technician, and `credit_basis_cents` is recomputed from the item's service line as above.

*Link two.* Because the claim was granted, the blade must come back. The claimed item moves `service_status` `DELIVERED → REWORK_QUEUED` and, on the very same row, `custody_location` `DELIVERED → CUSTOMER`. This is the edge that makes the custody axis cyclic — the item re-enters the custody cycle for a second visit under a new order.

*Link three.* Because the item is now `REWORK_QUEUED`, it must have an open rework order to be queued *for*. A new service request is created with `channel` `REWORK`, `status` `SUBMITTED`, `priority` `STANDARD`, `subscription_id` null and `origin_warranty_claim_id` naming the claim; the claim's `rework_service_request_id` is set to point back at it. One item is created on that request, whose `rework_of_request_item_id` points at the claimed item, whose `declared_damage_tier` is copied from the claimed item's `assessed_damage_tier`, whose `assessed_damage_tier` is set directly to `ROUTINE_HONE`, whose `assessed_by_technician_id` is the approving technician, and whose `service_minutes_assessed` are therefore the routine-hone minutes of its class. Because it arrives pre-assessed, its `service_status` is `ASSESSED` rather than `INTAKE_PENDING`, and its `authorization_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED` — a rework line is never re-priced, so re-authorization can never arise on it. This pre-assessment is precisely what makes the assessed branch of the dispatch-final minute calculation reachable, as §3.4 noted.

*Link four.* Because a rework order has been opened, the workmanship credit is owed. A warranty-credit line is appended to the account's draft invoice, creating that draft invoice when none exists, carrying an `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `credit_basis_cents` and a `source_warranty_claim_id` naming the claim. The line is taxable.

*Link five.* Because a line was appended, that invoice's `taxable_subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents`, `total_cents` and `amount_due_cents` are all recomputed under the rules in §3.10.

*Link six.* Because the credit has landed, the *original* item's service request moves on its billing axis to `CREDITED` — `INVOICED → CREDITED` when it had only been invoiced, and `SETTLED → CREDITED` when it had already been paid. Those are the only two from-states that move: an original request still `UNBILLED` at the moment of approval is left `UNBILLED`, and the link simply does not fire for it, which is the ordinary case when a customer complains before the visit has been billed. Nor does anything re-run the link later, so such a request stays billable and is invoiced normally afterwards even though a credit line for it already sits on the account's draft. `CREDITED` is terminal on that axis. Note that the fulfilment axis of that original request is untouched by any of this: it stays `COMPLETED`, exactly as §2.9 promised.

Two legs of this cascade do **not** fire, and their conditions are worth stating so that nobody implements them by symmetry. A denial fires none of links two through six — no rework order, no credit, no billing movement. And within an approval, link four still fires when `credit_basis_cents` is 0 — the line is written at zero rather than skipped — because the line is the audit record that the claim was honoured; the invoice-recompute of link five then simply produces no change in the money.

When the rework request is eventually delivered, the *original* item moves `service_status` `REWORK_QUEUED → DELIVERED`, its `custody_location` moves `CUSTOMER → DELIVERED`, and its `delivered_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock — which restarts the 30-day warranty window from the redelivery rather than the original delivery.

### 3.10. Billing: Invoices, Lines, Allowances and Payments

A `BillingClerk` opens an invoice by naming the `account_id` and exactly one scope selector. Which selector is required depends on the account's `billing_mode`, and getting it wrong is `ERR_INVOICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Under **per-visit** billing the body must supply `service_request_id` and must omit `billing_period`; the invoice covers exactly one `COMPLETED` service request and carries no subscription-fee line. Under **monthly-rollup** billing the body must supply `billing_period` as a `YYYY-MM` string and must omit `service_request_id`; the invoice covers every service request of the account whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED` and whose `completed_at` falls inside that calendar month, and it carries exactly one subscription-fee line equal to the subscription's `effective_monthly_fee_cents` when the account has a subscription in `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`. The invoice's own `billing_mode` is copied from the account at creation and frozen thereafter, so a later change to the account's billing configuration never rewrites history.

An account may hold only one invoice in `DRAFT` at a time — a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_DRAFT_INVOICE` — and every request the new invoice would cover must be `UNBILLED`, or the creation is `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVOICE`. An invoice that would be created with no lines at all is `ERR_EMPTY_INVOICE`. On success each covered request moves on its billing axis `UNBILLED → INVOICED`.

Note that invoices minted as a *side effect* of some other operation — a warranty credit, a proration credit, a loaner late fee — are not subject to the scope rule at all. Such a draft invoice is created with `service_request_id` null and `billing_period` null, with `billing_mode` copied from the account, and it covers no service request. The scope rule constrains only an explicit invoice creation.

**The charged amount** of each item is evaluated at invoice creation, in a pinned order. The order is part of the contract because a different order changes which item absorbs the allowance and therefore changes the invoice total.

*Step zero.* If the item's service request is on the `REWORK` channel, its `charged_service_cents` and `part_fee_cents` are both 0 and no further step is taken. Rework is free; that is what a warranty is.

*Step one.* The invoice's candidate items are ranked by `final_gross_cents` descending — before any discount and before any rush surcharge — with ties broken by the item's `created_at` ascending.

*Step two.* The first K ranked items are marked `allowance_applied` true, where K comes from the allowance plan below.

*Step three.* Each item's base amount is then determined by exactly one of three mutually exclusive branches. If `allowance_applied` is true, the base is 0. Otherwise, if the item's service request names a `subscription_id` whose subscription is in `ACTIVE`, the base is `final_gross_cents` multiplied by one minus the plan tier's `overage_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Otherwise the base is `final_gross_cents` unchanged. Note what the middle branch means in practice: the overage discount is what a subscriber gets on items *beyond* their allowance, and it is unavailable to a subscriber whose subscription is `PAUSED` or `CANCELLED`.

*Step four.* If the service request's `priority` is `RUSH`, the surcharge is added to the base. The surcharge is the greater of 500 cents and 20 percent of the base rounded half up — so a rush on a cheap blade costs a flat 500 cents and a rush on an expensive one costs a fifth of its price. The surcharge is computed on the *post-allowance, post-discount* base, which means an item the allowance absorbed still costs 500 cents to rush, since 20 percent of zero is zero and the floor wins.

*Step five.* The resulting figure is written to `charged_service_cents`, and `part_fee_cents` is set to the parts fee of the item's `assessed_damage_tier` — 0 when that tier is still null, which is why an item invoiced without ever having been assessed carries no parts fee. Note that this rewrites `part_fee_cents` from the tier rather than trusting what assessment left there, so the two agree by construction; and note that for a `REWORK` item step zero has already fixed both figures at 0, so neither the discount, the surcharge nor the parts fee ever reaches it. There are exactly two rounding points in this whole sequence — the discount in step three and the percentage half of the surcharge in step four — and each is a half-up rounding to whole cents.

**The allowance plan** decides K. Candidate items for a given scope are the items whose service request names a `subscription_id`, whose subscription is `ACTIVE`, whose service request is *not* on the `REWORK` channel, and whose `service_status` is neither `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` nor `ABANDONED`. They are ranked by `final_gross_cents` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, so the dearest item absorbs the allowance and the customer gets the most value out of it. K is the lesser of the number of candidate items and the subscription's `allowance_remaining`. Each of the first K items gets `allowance_applied` true and one allowance ledger entry of kind `CONSUMED` with `quantity` 1, a `reason` naming the invoicing, and its `request_item_id`; `allowance_remaining` decreases by exactly K.

The **scope** of that ranking forks on the account's `billing_mode`, and the fork changes outcomes materially. Under **per-visit** billing the scope is the single service request named by the invoice's `service_request_id`, so the ranking never sees items from the account's other visits and the allowance is spent visit by visit, in the order the invoices happen to be created. Under **monthly-rollup** billing the scope is every `COMPLETED`, `UNBILLED` service request of the account whose `completed_at` falls inside the invoice's `billing_period`, so the ranking is global across the whole cycle and the allowance lands on the dearest items of the period regardless of which visit produced them. The same items and the same allowance can therefore produce different totals under the two modes, and that is intended.

**Lines and totals.** Every line carries an `invoice_id`, a `source`, a human-readable `description`, a signed `amount_cents`, a `taxable` flag and a 1-based `sequence_index` recording its append order within the invoice. Service and part lines additionally carry a `request_item_id`; warranty-credit lines carry a `source_warranty_claim_id`; the other kinds carry neither, both fields reading null on them. That is true of the late-fee line in particular: although its `sequence_index` is ordered by the `created_at` of the loaner issue that produced it, the line exposes no field naming that issue, and the link from a late fee back to its loaner is not retrievable through the API. A line also carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, being one of the three append-only ledger entities of §2.4 — yet its `sequence_index` is nevertheless rewritten whenever a later line is appended to the same invoice, since the ordering is by kind and not by arrival. Six line kinds exist and `sequence_index` is assigned in exactly this order: first the `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE` line, which only a monthly-rollup invoice has; then the `SERVICE` lines ordered by their item's `created_at` ascending; then the `PART` lines ordered the same way; then the `LATE_FEE` lines ordered by their loaner issue's `created_at` ascending; then the `PRORATION_CREDIT` lines in the order they were inserted; and last the `WARRANTY_CREDIT` lines ordered by their claim's `created_at` ascending.

Invoice creation emits exactly one service line per covered item whose `service_status` is neither `RETURNED_UNSERVICED` nor `ABANDONED`, with `amount_cents` equal to that item's `charged_service_cents` — which is 0 when the allowance absorbed the item, and the line is still written — and exactly one part line per such item whose `part_fee_cents` is greater than 0. An item with no parts fee gets no part line at all.

Taxability is fixed per kind and is not negotiable per line: `SERVICE`, `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE`, `PRORATION_CREDIT` and `WARRANTY_CREDIT` lines are taxable; `PART` and `LATE_FEE` lines are not. Credit lines — warranty and proration — carry negative or zero amounts. From there, `taxable_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the taxable lines and may itself be negative; `nontaxable_subtotal_cents` is the sum over the non-taxable lines; `tax_cents` is 0 when the account is `tax_exempt`, and otherwise the greater of zero and `taxable_subtotal_cents`, multiplied by a service tax rate of 725 basis points and divided by 10000, rounded half up once; `total_cents` is `taxable_subtotal_cents` plus `nontaxable_subtotal_cents` plus `tax_cents`; and `amount_due_cents` is `total_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents`. The single-rounding discipline of §2.8 applies here in its strictest form: the tax is computed once over the aggregate taxable subtotal and rounded once, never per line and summed. A negative taxable subtotal produces no tax at all rather than a negative tax, because of the floor at zero — while `taxable_subtotal_cents` itself is reported as the negative figure it is. These totals are recomputed on every operation that appends a line to a draft invoice, not merely at creation.

**Issuing** an invoice moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED`, requires at least one line, and brings the invoice's `amount_due_cents` into the account's `outstanding_balance_cents`. Neither of those last two is a stored figure that issuing writes. "Freezes all computed totals" describes the effect, not the mechanism: `taxable_subtotal_cents`, `nontaxable_subtotal_cents`, `total_cents`, `amount_paid_cents` and `amount_due_cents` are recomputed from the invoice's current lines and payments on every single read, for the whole life of the invoice and in every status including `VOID`, and `tax_cents` is the one stored figure, rewritten whenever a line is appended. What issuing actually changes is only the status — and because the side-effect line appenders of §3.2, §3.8 and §3.9 all target the account's `DRAFT` invoice and mint a new draft when there is none, an issued invoice never gains another line, which is what makes the totals stable in practice. Likewise `outstanding_balance_cents` is derived, not incremented: it is recomputed from scratch on every read as the sum of `amount_due_cents` over the account's `ISSUED` and `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoices, so the invoice's arrival in `ISSUED` is the whole of the effect.

**Payments** are applied against an `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice, by either the account owner or a billing clerk, and are the third version-guarded write. The body carries `amount_cents`, a `method` from `CARD`, `ACH`, `CHECK` and `CASH`, and `expected_invoice_version`. The amount must be at least 1 and must not exceed the invoice's `amount_due_cents`, since the overpayment tolerance is 0 cents; an excess is `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`. An invoice whose `amount_due_cents` has fallen below 1 — which is reachable on an issued credit memo whose credits outweigh its charges — is `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`. Each payment records `applied_at` from the logical clock and the `resulting_invoice_status` the invoice landed in.

A payment strictly smaller than the amount due moves the invoice `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_PAID`, or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` when it was already there — that self-loop is the normal case for a customer paying in instalments, and each such payment raises `amount_paid_cents`, lowers `amount_due_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents`, and increments the invoice's `version`, which is exactly why the next instalment needs a fresh `expected_invoice_version`. A payment exactly equal to the amount due moves the invoice `ISSUED → PAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`, drives `amount_due_cents` to 0, decrements `outstanding_balance_cents` accordingly, increments `version`, and moves every service request covered by that invoice on its billing axis `INVOICED → SETTLED`.

**Voiding** unwinds an invoice that should never have been raised. `DRAFT → VOID` is available unconditionally; `ISSUED → VOID` is available only when `amount_paid_cents` is exactly 0 — a partly paid invoice cannot be voided. Voiding returns every covered service request `INVOICED → UNBILLED` and writes an allowance ledger entry of kind `RESTORED` for each unit the invoice had consumed, raising the subscription's `allowance_remaining` back by that count. The restoration is driven off the items themselves rather than off the ledger: for every item of a covered request whose `allowance_applied` is true, one `RESTORED` entry naming that `request_item_id` is written, `allowance_remaining` rises by one, and — the step that makes the unwind idempotent and that no other operation performs — **the item's `allowance_applied` is set back to false**, so a subsequent re-invoicing of the same request sees the item as a fresh candidate for the allowance. The undo stops there: the items' `charged_service_cents` and `part_fee_cents` keep the values the voided invoice wrote, and so does `final_total_cents`. Two limits on the billing-axis rollback are equally load-bearing. Only a request sitting at `INVOICED` is returned to `UNBILLED`; one that the warranty cascade has already moved to `CREDITED` is left at `CREDITED`, that state being terminal, while its allowance is still restored and its link to the invoice still cut. And the covered set is the invoice's own record of which requests it billed, so a request that was never marked as covered by this invoice is untouched. When the invoice being voided was `ISSUED`, `outstanding_balance_cents` is additionally decremented by its `amount_due_cents`; when it was still `DRAFT`, no balance movement is needed because a draft never contributed. Creation followed by void is the compensating pair the allowance-ledger and money conservation rules of §2.10 are checked against.

Cancelling a whole run is available only from `PLANNED` and moves it `PLANNED → CANCELLED`. Every `SCHEDULED` stop on it moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, each affected service request returns `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, and `booked_service_minutes`, `scheduled_stop_count` and `committed_minutes_provisional` are all zeroed.
