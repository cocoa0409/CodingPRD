# Precision Spring & Wire Form Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A precision spring house lives or dies on traceability. A coil spring destined for an airbag inflator, a torsion leg inside a surgical stapler and a bulk industrial extension spring may all be coiled on the same computer-controlled machine from the same rack of music wire, yet each carries a completely different burden of proof: a different fatigue criterion, a different corrosion and biocompatibility requirement, a different depth of production-part approval documentation, and a different answer to the question "may this box leave the dock today?". Today that burden is discharged across a scatter of spreadsheets, tool-room notebooks, oven logs and email approvals from the customer's quality engineer. Nothing links the shear stress the design engineer calculated to the heat number the technician actually loaded, to the Cpk the inspector measured on the third subgroup, to the cents of tooling cost the last shipment recovered.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record covering that whole chain. It opens with the commercial frame — a customer program carrying a volume commitment, a minimum order quantity, acceptance floors for fatigue margin and long-run capability, and a tooling amortization method — and closes with a packaged, priced, shipped lot whose every gate is auditable. In between it carries the engineering stack (spring rate, Wahl-corrected shear stress, Goodman and Soderberg fatigue margins, predicted life tier, wire mass per piece and tangle-risk classification), the shop-floor stack (coiler setup windows, wire reservations, first-article inspection, statistical process control, stress relief and shot peening), and the qualification stack (production-part approval rounds decided by the customer itself).

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **segregation of duties is enforced by the server, not by convention**: the engineer who authors a design can never be the manager who releases it, and the inspector who measured a first article can never be the inspector who dispositions it. Second, **every quantity that must balance is modelled as a conserved ledger**: kilograms of wire, cents of capitalised tooling cost, and pieces of customer commitment each move between named buckets and are restored exactly when an operation is compensated. Implementers should read the workflow narratives in section 3 as the authoritative statement of behaviour; section 4 supplies only the HTTP surface those workflows are reached through.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. Both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before they are examined, and a header that is present but empty or whitespace-only counts as absent, so a blank `X-User-ID` is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` too. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint in section 4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `DesignEngineer`, `EngineeringManager`, `ProcessTechnician`, `QualityInspector`, `ProgramManager`, `CustomerRepresentative` and `PlantAdmin`.

`DesignEngineer` owns the engineering record — material specifications, spring and wire-form designs, the statistical-process-control plan of critical characteristics, and revisions. It may submit a design for review but may never decide one. `EngineeringManager` is the independent engineering authority: it decides designs against the program's fatigue-margin floor, retires designs, and capitalises tooling; it may neither author nor revise a design. `ProcessTechnician` runs the floor: booking and activating coiling setups, opening, completing and cancelling production runs, scheduling, charging, discharging and cancelling stress-relief batches, and packing finished lots; it holds no quality authority. `QualityInspector` owns incoming wire certification, first-article and in-process measurement, both dispositions, and lot quarantine and release; it may neither open nor complete a run. `ProgramManager` owns the commercial record: opening programs, pricing and freezing quotes, filing production-part approval packages, allocating, shipping and taking back lots. `CustomerRepresentative` is the buying organisation acting as itself and is the sole holder of the production-part approval verdict. `PlantAdmin` is the asset and infrastructure custodian: it commissions coilers and stress-relief ovens and drives the logical clock. `PlantAdmin` is deliberately **not** a superset of the other roles — it can neither engineer, run, inspect nor sell, and an attempt to use it as an override answers `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Identity semantics differ between the plant-internal roles and the customer role, and getting this wrong invalidates every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `DesignEngineer`, `EngineeringManager`, `ProcessTechnician`, `QualityInspector`, `ProgramManager` or `PlantAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on the resources those roles touch — as `created_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a spring design, `set_by_user_id` on a machine setup, `certified_by_user_id` on a wire lot, `inspected_by_user_id` on an inspection record, and `submitted_by_user_id` on a qualification submission — and it compares such recorded values against the incoming `X-User-ID` for the two segregation-of-duties gates, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CustomerProgram`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `CustomerProgram` compare the resource's program foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: a customer representative may read only its own program, only the designs whose `program_id` is its own program, only the qualification submissions and shipment lots whose `program_id` is its own program, and may decide only qualification submissions filed against its own program. Any customer-representative request that targets a program, design, submission or lot belonging to a different program is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). When a customer representative decides a submission round, the server stamps `reviewed_by_program_id` with the incoming `X-User-ID`, so that field always equals the submission's `program_id`. On the list endpoints a `CustomerRepresentative` may call, the `program_id` filter is forced rather than merely checked: omitting it scopes the page down to the caller's own program instead of returning every row, while supplying a `program_id` other than the caller's own answers `ERR_PROGRAM_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. On `GET /customer-programs/{id}` the ownership comparison is made against the path segment itself, before the program row is looked up, so a customer representative naming an id that does not exist answers `ERR_PROGRAM_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; on every other program-referencing resource the row must resolve first, so an unknown `/spring-designs/{id}` answers `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND` and the ownership comparison is then made against the resolved row's `program_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's wall clock, not from the logical shop clock of section 2.5, so it moves on its own and is unaffected by clock-setting calls.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent from `meta` entirely on every single-resource read, computed read, creation and state-changing operation.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three identifiers sit outside the published catalog of section 5 and are reachable only through the framework rather than through any workflow rule: a request to a path matching no endpoint of section 4 answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a request using a method that path does not declare answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). Because no dedicated not-found code is minted for an inspection record, `GET /inspection-records/{id}` against an unknown id also answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Separately, an absent request body is read as the empty object on every `POST`, so the operations that declare no payload at all may be called with nothing attached, while a body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or that parses to something other than a JSON object, answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) on any endpoint including those.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Both parameters must be written as plain integer literals, optionally carrying a leading sign; a value that is empty, fractional or otherwise non-numeric is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` on the same footing as an out-of-range one.

Unless a subsection states otherwise, list endpoints return rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row appears first and paging forward walks the collection chronologically. Rows written inside the same millisecond keep their insertion order, so the sort is stable and equal `created_at` values never reshuffle a page. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values in force, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` set true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters are validated before pagination, so an enum-valued filter carrying a value outside its declared set answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) even when `limit` or `offset` is also out of range; an id-valued filter is never resolved, so an id matching no row is not an error and simply yields an empty page; and a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Revision Counter

Every resource in the system is addressed by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply an `id` on creation. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was first written. Like `updated_at`, it is read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical shop clock of section 2.5; the logical clock is stamped only into the fields section 2.5 names.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by 1 each time the row is mutated. `CustomerProgram`, `SpringDesign`, `ToolingAsset`, `WireLot`, `CoilingMachine`, `MachineSetup`, `ProductionRun`, `StressReliefOven`, `HeatTreatBatch`, `QualificationSubmission` and `ShipmentLot` all carry the full set. Three resources are deliberately write-once and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with **both** `updated_at` and `version` absent from their representations entirely: a `MaterialSpec`, a `CriticalCharacteristic` and an `InspectionRecord` are never edited after creation, so there is nothing for those two fields to describe.

Two version counters have hand-written rules that override the general "increments on mutation" reading, because both are load-bearing for optimistic concurrency:

- `CustomerProgram.version` is incremented **only** by the program quoting operation, `POST /customer-programs/{id}/quote`, and by nothing else. The piece counters and the delivery posture on a program move constantly as lots are allocated, shipped, quarantined, released and returned and as submissions are decided, and none of those cascaded writes touch `version`. A client that read a program, then saw someone else allocate a lot against it, still holds a valid version for quoting.
- `CoilingMachine.version` is incremented by every operation that changes the coiler's occupancy: scheduling a machine setup, activating a setup, aborting a setup that was `ACTIVE`, opening a production run, completing a run, cancelling a run, and rejecting a first article (which cancels the run). Each of those increments the counter by exactly 1.

`SpringDesign.version` follows the general rule, and the revision operation increments it by 1 like any other mutation of the design row.

### 2.5. The Logical Shop Clock

Time in this system is a logical value the server holds, never the host's wall clock. Before any request is made the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlantAdmin` advances it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting endpoint; the clock is monotonic, so a requested instant earlier than the current reading is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422) and the clock is left untouched. An instant exactly equal to the current reading is accepted and leaves the clock where it stands. The instant travels in a required `to_iso8601` body field, and a value that is absent, non-string or unparseable as an ISO 8601 instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Advancing the clock mutates no entity, and because there is no clock resource to hand back the endpoint answers HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object.

Every temporal gate in the specification reads this clock and nothing else: whether a setup window lies in the past, whether a setup may be activated now, whether an oven batch may be charged, and what instant is stamped into `decided_at` on a qualification verdict and `shipped_at` on a shipment. Because the clock only ever moves forward, a test scenario can drive the whole manufacturing calendar deterministically.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision Across the Engineering and Money Stacks

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every quantity is rounded to the decimal count its own rule names, never to a house default. The counts differ by discipline and are not interchangeable:

- Spring rate is carried to **3 decimal places**.
- Corrected shear stresses, the stress amplitude, the mean stress and the exposed endurance limit are carried to **2 decimal places**. The uncorrected stress values feeding the amplitude and mean split are held at full machine precision and rounded exactly once, at the end.
- Fatigue margin ratio and the tangle-risk index are carried to **3 decimal places**.
- Predicted fatigue life is a whole number of cycles selected from a tier table; there is no rounding.
- Wire mass per piece is carried to **exactly 6 decimal places**, and this rounded value is the only per-piece mass any downstream calculation may read.
- Every kilogram quantity on a wire lot, a production run, an oven and a heat-treat batch is carried to **3 decimal places**, with the rounding applied once per posting.
- Inspection statistics use three different counts on the same record: the subgroup mean to **4 decimal places**, the Bessel-corrected standard deviation to **6 decimal places**, and both capability indices to **3 decimal places**. Capability floors are compared against the **rounded** index.
- Money is integral cents everywhere it is stored. Inside the quoting calculation, per-piece amounts are carried to **4 decimal places** (tenths of a mill) while the intermediate terms accumulate, and are collapsed to whole cents only at the four steps that the quoting narrative names.

Where a rule calls for rounding toward positive infinity or toward negative infinity rather than half-up — the per-piece tooling amortization figures and the amortization cap — the narrative for that rule says so explicitly.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Designs, Coilers and Quotes

Three write operations can silently destroy work that another writer has already done, so all three are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`. In each case the client must send the version it last observed as a **required, non-optional** field in the request body; omitting it is a malformed request. If the value does not equal the parent row's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is mutated. Each of the three fields must be a JSON integer — a boolean, a float or a numeric string is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and no range bound is applied to it beyond that. No endpoint other than these three *reads* a version field; a version field sent to any other endpoint is silently ignored, exactly as every other unrecognised body property is ignored throughout the API — an extra field is never on its own a reason to reject a request.

- **`POST /spring-designs/{id}/revisions` requires `expected_design_version`**, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SpringDesign`. A revision legitimately supersedes the prior revision and rewrites every computed engineering field, so a writer holding a stale view would base new geometry on superseded stress and fatigue numbers. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **`POST /machine-setups` requires `expected_machine_version`**, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CoilingMachine` named by `machine_id`. A new setup for the same machine and design legitimately supersedes the prior scheduled setup, so a writer holding a stale machine view would supersede a setup it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MACHINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **`POST /customer-programs/{id}/quote` requires `expected_program_version`**, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CustomerProgram`. A new quote legitimately supersedes the previous frozen price that later shipment lots snapshot, so a writer holding a stale program view would overwrite a price computed against tooling and commitment figures it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost unresolvable segment decides the answer. A request against a sub-resource of a design whose design `id` does not exist answers `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND` without ever looking at the sub-resource; a request against a characteristic under a design that does exist answers `ERR_CHARACTERISTIC_NOT_FOUND` only after the design has resolved. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after the path but before any business rule reads them.

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one with the **lowest priority number** in the following ladder. Two consequences of this ladder deserve highlighting because they are easy to get backwards. On the revision operation, rank 20 outranks rank 21: an `OBSOLETE` design answers `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`, while a design still in a revisable state that carries an in-flight or granted approval package answers `ERR_DESIGN_LOCKED`. And on lot allocation, rank 44 is structurally shadowed by rank 41 — a program whose posture is `BLOCKED` has no granted approval on file, so rank 41 answers first — which means `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED` surfaces in practice on the shipping operation, where an already packed lot is presented after a resubmission dropped the program's posture back to `BLOCKED`. A third consequence concerns the two measurement endpoints, `POST /production-runs/{id}/fai` and `POST /production-runs/{id}/inspections`, and it is the one place where the "path before body" rule this section opens with outranks even rank 4: on those two endpoints the path `run_id` is resolved first, so an unknown run answers `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND` (rank 14) in preference both to a missing or malformed `characteristic_id` or `measurements` payload (rank 4) and to an unresolvable `characteristic_id` (rank 9). `POST /qualification-submissions/{id}/review` behaves the same way for the same reason: the submission is resolved and its customer-ownership comparison made before the `decision` and `interim_piece_cap` fields are examined at all, so an unknown submission answers `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` (rank 17) even when the body carries no `decision` whatsoever. On every other endpoint in the system the body's field-level typing and range validation genuinely does run ahead of every path and foreign-key resolution, exactly as rank 4 states.

### 2.9. Conserved Ledgers

Three quantities in this system are conserved: they may move between buckets but the total may never change, and any operation that undoes an earlier posting must restore the buckets exactly.

**Wire mass.** A wire lot's `received_kg` is fixed at receipt and never changes. It is partitioned at all times across three buckets — `on_hand_kg` (unreserved kilograms still in the rack), `reserved_kg` (kilograms held by non-terminal production runs) and `consumed_kg` (kilograms burned by completed runs, good pieces and scrap alike). Every reservation, release and consumption posting moves mass between these three and never creates or destroys any. When a reservation is released, the release uses the `wire_reserved_kg` value stored on the run verbatim; it is never recomputed from the design, because the design may have been revised in the meantime.

**Tooling cost.** A tooling asset's `original_cost_cents` is fixed at capitalisation. `recovered_cents` and `unamortized_cents` always sum to it. Shipping a lot posts a recovery into the first and out of the second; taking that lot back reverses the posting using the `tooling_recovery_cents` stored on the lot verbatim.

**Piece commitment.** A program's `committed_pieces` is fixed when the program is opened. `open_commitment_pieces`, `allocated_pieces` and `shipped_pieces` move as lots are allocated, shipped, quarantined, released and returned, and every one of those moves is a transfer, never a mint.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Fields

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. When they do, all effects are applied within the same request — consistency is immediate, never eventual — and each mutated row has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` advanced under the rules of section 2.4. A cascade that fires several hops deep applies every hop: for example, a customer's full approval of a package promotes the design, which arms the design's tooling, which freezes the program's quote and re-derives the program's posture, all in one request.

Several fields are **derived** rather than supplied, and are recomputed rather than incrementally patched whenever their inputs move. `CoilingMachine.active_setup_id` is a denormalised pointer resolved as the single machine setup row on that machine whose `setup_status` is `ACTIVE`, and is null when no such row exists; it is never accepted as an input. `MachineSetup.superseded_by_setup_id` is likewise a denormalised pointer to the setup whose creation superseded this one, and is null unless the row's `setup_status` is `SUPERSEDED`. `StressReliefOven.scheduled_load_kg` is the sum of `load_kg` over that oven's batches whose `batch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`. `ProductionRun.heat_treat_state` is derived from the run's heat-treat batches. `CustomerProgram.delivery_posture` is re-derived from scratch on every event that changes the program's submissions, its quarantine count, or the release status of its designs. Implementations that patch these fields incrementally instead of re-deriving them will diverge as soon as a compensating operation runs.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Program and Registering Wire Materials

Everything downstream hangs off a **customer program**, which a `ProgramManager` opens by declaring who is buying and on what terms. The payload names a `program_code` — a customer-facing identifier that must be unique across all programs and between 3 and 40 characters long — together with the `customer_name` of the buying organisation. The length bound is measured on the raw string as supplied, and `customer_name` must be a non-empty string but carries no length bound of its own. The single most consequential field is `application_class`, which must be one of `AUTOMOTIVE_SAFETY`, `AUTOMOTIVE_NON_SAFETY`, `MEDICAL_IMPLANT`, `MEDICAL_DEVICE` or `INDUSTRIAL`; it selects which fatigue criterion the engineering stack applies, which packaging methods the shipping dock may use, and how deep the approval documentation runs. Alongside it the manager states `ppap_level`, the production-part approval submission level the customer demands, as a whole number from 1 through 5 inclusive.

Volume terms come next. `committed_pieces` is the total number of pieces the customer has committed to over the life of the program and must be greater than zero; `minimum_order_quantity` is the smallest shippable lot size the program accepts and must likewise be greater than zero. Four acceptance floors are optional and each has a default when omitted: `required_salt_spray_hours` is the minimum coating salt-spray endurance the chosen material must provide and defaults to 0 with a floor of 0; `biocompatibility_required` defaults to false; `min_fatigue_margin`, the fatigue margin a design must reach before it can be released for this program, defaults to 1.000; and `required_ppk`, the long-run capability floor every safety-critical characteristic must reach before pieces may be allocated, also defaults to 1.000. Typing is pinned as follows: `ppap_level`, `committed_pieces`, `minimum_order_quantity` and `required_salt_spray_hours` must each be a JSON integer, so a fractional or boolean value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas `min_fatigue_margin` and `required_ppk` are plain numbers carrying no bound of their own — a zero or even negative floor is accepted and simply makes the corresponding gate vacuous. An optional field sent as an explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omission and takes its default; this holds for every optional field on every endpoint in the system. Finally `amortization_method` must be either `FORECAST_BASIS` or `ACTUAL_BASIS`, and it decides which of two very different tooling-recovery formulas runs every time a lot ships.

A freshly opened program starts its commitment ledger at rest: `open_commitment_pieces` is initialised to the same number as `committed_pieces`, while `allocated_pieces`, `shipped_pieces` and `quarantined_lot_count` all start at 0. `quote_frozen` starts false and every quoted figure — `quoted_qty`, `quoted_pre_discount_subtotal_cents`, `quoted_volume_discount_pct`, `quoted_tooling_amort_per_piece_cents`, `quoted_unit_price_cents` and `quoted_total_cents` — starts null. `delivery_posture` starts at `BLOCKED`, which is simply what the posture rule below returns for a program with no granted approval on file.

**Delivery posture** is a derived gate over the entire program and is re-derived from scratch, never patched, on every event that changes the program's submissions, its quarantine count, or the release status of one of its designs. The rule reads three inputs and returns one of `BLOCKED`, `AT_RISK` or `ON_TRACK`, considering only rows belonging to this program: whether at least one qualification submission of this program is in `APPROVED` or `INTERIM_APPROVED` status; whether at least one production run whose design belongs to this program currently has a quality status of `SPC_HOLD`; and the program's own `quarantined_lot_count`. If no submission of the program is in either granted status, the posture is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if `quarantined_lot_count` is at least 1 **or** such a held run exists, the posture is `AT_RISK`. Otherwise the posture is `ON_TRACK`. The re-derivation writes to the program only when the value it computes differs from the value already stored: a re-derivation that lands on the posture the program was already holding leaves the program row entirely alone, its `updated_at` included. Programs may be listed with `application_class` and `delivery_posture` as optional filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

Wire materials are registered separately, by a `DesignEngineer`, and are shared across programs. A material specification carries a unique `spec_code`, a `wire_material` drawn from `MUSIC_WIRE_ASTM_A228`, `CHROME_SILICON_A401`, `STAINLESS_302_A313`, `ELGILOY_AMS5876` and `TITANIUM_GR5`, and the mechanical constants the engineering stack consumes: `tensile_grade_mpa` (the minimum ultimate tensile strength of the grade, in megapascals), `shear_modulus_gpa` (the shear modulus used by the spring-rate rule, in gigapascals), `elastic_modulus_gpa` (Young's modulus, in gigapascals, recorded for torsion forms) and `density_kg_per_m3` (used by the wire-mass rule). All four must be greater than zero. The corrosion story is carried by `coating`, one of `NONE`, `ZINC_PHOSPHATE`, `PASSIVATED`, `ELECTROPOLISHED` or `PTFE`, and `salt_spray_hours`, the coating's endurance in hours, which must be at least 0. `biocompatible` records whether the grade plus coating is qualified for implant or device contact and defaults to false. `cost_per_kg_cents` is the purchased wire cost in cents per kilogram and must be greater than zero. `salt_spray_hours` and `cost_per_kg_cents` must both be whole numbers — hours and cents respectively — while the four mechanical constants are plain numbers; `spec_code` must be a non-empty string but, unlike `program_code`, carries no length bound. Material specifications may be listed filtered by `wire_material`.

Uniqueness is enforced across six collections and always answers the same code. If a supplied `program_code`, `spec_code`, `heat_number`, `tool_code`, `machine_code` or `oven_code` already exists in its own collection, the creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409). The three shop-floor codes among them — `heat_number`, `machine_code` and `oven_code` — must each be a non-empty string but carry no length bound of their own, and the uniqueness sweep compares them literally against every existing row of their own collection, with no case folding and no whitespace normalisation.

### 3.2. Engineering the Wire Form: Geometry, Stress, Fatigue and Mass

A `DesignEngineer` creates a spring or wire-form design by naming the program it is for through a payload carrying the `program_id`, the material it will be coiled from through `material_spec_id`, and the shop's own `part_number`. The form family is declared as `form_type`, one of `COMPRESSION`, `EXTENSION` or `TORSION`, and the end configuration as `end_type`, one of `CLOSED_GROUND`, `CLOSED_UNGROUND`, `OPEN`, `HOOK_EXTENSION` or `TORSION_LEG`. Geometry follows: `wire_diameter_mm` must be greater than zero; `outer_diameter_mm`, the coil outer diameter, must be strictly greater than `wire_diameter_mm`; `free_length_mm` must be greater than zero; and `active_coils`, the active coil count, must be at least 1 — it is a plain number rather than an integer, so a fractional coil count such as 8.5 is accepted so long as it reaches 1. `part_number` must be a non-empty string and is deliberately **not** unique: two designs, even of the same program, may carry the same part number. The duty cycle is declared by two loads in newtons — `max_working_load_n`, which must be greater than zero, and `min_working_load_n`, which must be at least 0 and strictly less than `max_working_load_n` — plus `cycle_seconds_per_piece`, the coiling cycle time per piece, which must be greater than zero and which later drives the labour term of the quote. The optional `shot_peening_required` flag defaults to false; when true it both raises the endurance limit the fatigue rule uses and obliges every heat-treat batch of the part to actually peen.

Two metallurgical gates are checked against the owning program before any of this is persisted. The chosen material's `salt_spray_hours` must be at least the program's `required_salt_spray_hours`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_CORROSION_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 422). And if the program's `biocompatibility_required` is true, the material's `biocompatible` must also be true; otherwise the answer is `ERR_BIOCOMPATIBILITY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Both gates apply identically when a design is later revised.

On acceptance the server stamps `created_by_user_id` with the incoming `X-User-ID`, leaves `reviewed_by_user_id` null, sets `current_revision_no` to 1 with `superseded_revision_no` null, sets `release_status` to `DRAFT`, and then runs the complete engineering stack.

**Spring rate.** Let the mean coil diameter be the outer diameter less the wire diameter. Convert the material's shear modulus from gigapascals to megapascals by multiplying it by one thousand. The value written to `spring_rate_n_per_mm` is that megapascal shear modulus multiplied by the wire diameter raised to the fourth power, divided by the product of eight, the mean coil diameter cubed, and the active coil count, with the result rounded half up to three decimal places. Nothing outside the design's own geometry and the linked material's shear modulus contributes.

**Corrected shear stress.** The spring index is the mean coil diameter divided by the wire diameter. The Wahl correction factor is four times the spring index plus two, all divided by four times the spring index minus three. A geometry whose spring index is exactly 0.75 — an `outer_diameter_mm` of exactly 1.75 times the `wire_diameter_mm`, which the "strictly greater" bound on the outer diameter happily admits — drives that divisor to zero, and such a request is rejected as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); the check lives in the engineering stack itself, so it is reached only after the corrosion and biocompatibility gates below have already passed. For a given load, the corrected shear stress is the Wahl factor multiplied by eight, multiplied by that load, multiplied by the mean coil diameter, then divided by the product of pi and the wire diameter cubed; pi is taken as 3.141592653589793. Evaluating that expression at `max_working_load_n` and rounding half up to two decimal places gives `shear_stress_max_mpa`; evaluating it at `min_working_load_n` and rounding the same way gives `shear_stress_min_mpa`. The amplitude and mean split is taken from the **unrounded** stress values, not from the two rounded fields: `stress_amplitude_mpa` is the unrounded maximum-load stress minus the unrounded minimum-load stress, divided by two, rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end; `mean_stress_mpa` is the sum of those same two unrounded stresses divided by two, rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end.

**Fatigue margin.** Three strength allowables are derived from the material's `tensile_grade_mpa`. The endurance limit in shear is the tensile grade multiplied by 0.30 when `shot_peening_required` is true and by 0.20 when it is false — shot peening buys half again as much fatigue strength, which is the whole reason the flag exists. The ultimate shear strength is the tensile grade multiplied by 0.67, and the shear yield strength is the tensile grade multiplied by 0.45. Two candidate denominators are then formed from the **unrounded** stress amplitude and unrounded mean stress: the Goodman denominator is the unrounded amplitude divided by the endurance limit, plus the unrounded mean divided by the ultimate shear strength; the Soderberg denominator is the unrounded amplitude divided by the endurance limit, plus the unrounded mean divided by the shear yield strength. Which one binds is decided by the owning program's `application_class`: the design's `fatigue_criterion` is set to `SODERBERG` when the class is `AUTOMOTIVE_SAFETY` or `MEDICAL_IMPLANT`, and to `GOODMAN` for `AUTOMOTIVE_NON_SAFETY`, `MEDICAL_DEVICE` or `INDUSTRIAL`. `fatigue_margin_ratio` is then one divided by the denominator belonging to the criterion in force, rounded half up to three decimal places. A denominator that is not strictly positive — which a spring index below 0.75 produces, because the Wahl factor then turns negative and drags the amplitude and mean stresses negative with it — yields a **null** `fatigue_margin_ratio` rather than an error or a zero, and each of the two variants is judged for positivity on its own, so the fatigue read described below can expose one null variant beside one numeric one. Because the shear yield strength is the smaller allowable, the `SODERBERG` variant always yields a strictly smaller margin than the `GOODMAN` variant for identical geometry, loads and material — the safety-critical classes are held to the harsher line, and the less conservative `GOODMAN` line divides the mean stress by the larger ultimate shear strength.

**Predicted life.** `predicted_fatigue_life_cycles` is a whole number of cycles chosen by comparing the already-rounded three-decimal `fatigue_margin_ratio` against four descending breakpoints, each comparison inclusive at its lower bound. A ratio of at least 1.300 yields ten million cycles. Otherwise a ratio of at least 1.150 yields five million. Otherwise a ratio of at least 1.050 yields one million. Otherwise a ratio of at least 1.000 yields two hundred and fifty thousand. A ratio below 1.000 yields zero cycles, and a null ratio clears no breakpoint at all and likewise yields zero cycles. There is no interpolation and no rounding.

**Wire mass per piece.** The developed wire length starts from the coil: pi multiplied by the mean coil diameter, multiplied by the active coil count plus an end-coil allowance of two full coils. To that is added twice the tail allowance for the declared `end_type`, where a `HOOK_EXTENSION` end contributes 15.0 millimetres of tail per end, a `TORSION_LEG` end contributes 25.0 millimetres per end, and `CLOSED_GROUND`, `CLOSED_UNGROUND` and `OPEN` ends contribute 0.0. The cross-sectional area in square metres is pi multiplied by the square of the wire diameter divided by two thousand — that divisor converts a diameter in millimetres to a radius in metres in one step. `wire_mass_per_piece_kg` is then the developed length converted from millimetres to metres by dividing by one thousand, multiplied by that area, multiplied by the material's `density_kg_per_m3`, rounded half up to exactly six decimal places. This stored six-decimal value is the **only** per-piece mass any downstream rule may read; recomputing an unrounded mass for a reservation or a consumption posting produces different kilograms and is wrong.

**Tangle risk.** `tangle_risk_index` is the free length divided by the outer diameter, rounded half up to three decimal places. `tangle_risk_class` is `HIGH` when that index is at least 4.000 **or** the `end_type` is `HOOK_EXTENSION`, and `LOW` in every other case. A long slender coil and a hooked end both catch on their neighbours in a bulk box, and the packaging rules in section 3.11 read this classification directly.

A dedicated computed read exposes the fatigue picture without persisting anything. It returns `criterion_in_force` — the `GOODMAN` or `SODERBERG` variant whose ratio the design actually persisted — alongside `fatigue_margin_goodman` and `fatigue_margin_soderberg`, each the three-decimal result of its own variant so the two lines can be compared side by side, the stress state they are built from as `stress_amplitude_mpa` and `mean_stress_mpa` at two decimal places, `endurance_limit_mpa` as the shear endurance limit after the shot-peening factor has been applied, rounded to two decimal places, and `predicted_fatigue_life_cycles` as the integer tier. Those seven fields are the whole of the read's `data` object — it carries no `id`, no `design_id` and no timestamps — and they are the only place `fatigue_margin_goodman`, `fatigue_margin_soderberg` and `endurance_limit_mpa` are visible at all: the persisted `SpringDesign` representation exposes `fatigue_criterion` and `fatigue_margin_ratio` but never the losing variant nor the endurance limit. Designs may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `material_spec_id` and `release_status`.

### 3.3. The Critical-Characteristic Plan, Engineering Review and Revision

A design's `release_status` walks a branching lifecycle with a revert edge, and the six values it may take are `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED`, `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`, `REJECTED` and `OBSOLETE`. Only `OBSOLETE` is terminal.

While a design is still `DRAFT`, the authoring `DesignEngineer` builds its statistical-process-control plan by adding **critical characteristics**, each of which is an immutable sub-resource of the design. A characteristic declares `char_code`, one of `FREE_LENGTH`, `SPRING_RATE`, `OUTER_DIAMETER`, `COIL_COUNT`, `HOOK_POSITION` or `LOAD_AT_HEIGHT`, and that code must be unique within the design — a second characteristic carrying a code the design already has is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHARACTERISTIC` (HTTP 409). It also declares the print `nominal_value`, a `lsl` lower specification limit that must be strictly less than the nominal, and a `usl` upper specification limit that must be strictly greater than the nominal. All three are plain numbers bounded by nothing but that ordering, so a negative nominal with negative limits is perfectly acceptable, and the two cross-field comparisons are checked before the design in the path is resolved. Two optional fields carry the quality weight: `is_safety_critical` defaults to false, and when it is true an out-of-control subgroup on this characteristic escalates the entire production run onto a quality hold; `cpk_floor`, the short-run capability floor for this characteristic, defaults to 1.333, carries no bound of its own — any number, zero or negative included, is accepted — and is compared with the measured index using an at-least test. Adding a characteristic to a design that has left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The plan of one design can be read back as a sub-resource list.

Submitting for review advances the design `DRAFT → ENG_REVIEW` and is the author's own act. It requires the design to be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`, and it requires the design to have at least one critical characteristic on file: a design with an empty characteristic set cannot be reviewed, because there would be nothing to inspect, and the attempt answers `ERR_CHARACTERISTIC_SET_EMPTY` (HTTP 422).

The engineering verdict belongs to an `EngineeringManager` and is delivered as a `decision` of either `APPROVE_PROTOTYPE` or `REJECT`. Both branches require the design to be in `ENG_REVIEW` — anything else answers `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE` — and both branches enforce segregation of duties: if the reviewer's `X-User-ID` equals the design's recorded `created_by_user_id`, the verdict is refused with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409), whichever way the decision falls. Both branches stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewer's `X-User-ID`. The branches then diverge on exactly one further gate. `APPROVE_PROTOTYPE` additionally requires the design's binding `fatigue_margin_ratio` to be at least the owning program's `min_fatigue_margin`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_FATIGUE_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422), and a design whose `fatigue_margin_ratio` came out null because its fatigue denominator was not positive is treated as a shortfall and answers the same error. When it passes, the design moves `ENG_REVIEW → PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` and the program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived. `REJECT` has no fatigue gate at all — a manager may reject a design whose margin comfortably clears the program floor, for any reason the manager likes — and it moves the design `ENG_REVIEW → REJECTED`. The owning program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived on **both** branches, not only on the approving one; since a design's own `release_status` is not one of the three inputs the posture rule reads, the re-derivation on the `REJECT` branch normally lands on the value the program already held.

Consider a design that has been rejected, or a prototype that has been released and now needs another engineering loop. Either can be pulled back to the drawing board by cutting a **revision**, an act reserved to a `DesignEngineer`. The design's `release_status` must be one of `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `REJECTED` or `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED`; an `OBSOLETE` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED` design cannot be revised and answers `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. Independently of state, the engineering record is frozen once the customer is involved: if any qualification submission of this design is in `SUBMITTED`, `INTERIM_APPROVED` or `APPROVED` status, the revision is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). A revision against a `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` design is further blocked while a granted package exists for it, which is the same condition seen from the other side. The request must carry `expected_design_version` as described in section 2.7, and may optionally carry replacements for `material_spec_id`, `wire_diameter_mm`, `outer_diameter_mm`, `free_length_mm`, `active_coils`, `max_working_load_n`, `min_working_load_n`, `cycle_seconds_per_piece` and `shot_peening_required`; fields not supplied keep their current values. The corrosion and biocompatibility gates of section 3.2 are re-checked against whatever material ends up selected. `expected_design_version` must be a JSON integer, and any optional replacement sent as an explicit `null` counts as not supplied and leaves the current value in place. Ordering on this endpoint departs from the plain reading of the section 2.8 ladder in one place, and the departure is pinned deliberately. The per-field typing and range checks on the supplied replacements — each geometry figure greater than zero, `active_coils` at least 1, `min_working_load_n` at least 0 — are made up front, as rank 4 requires. The **cross-field** geometry checks, however, are applied to the *merged* design only after the OCC, state and lock gates have passed: `outer_diameter_mm` must still exceed `wire_diameter_mm` and `min_working_load_n` must still be strictly below `max_working_load_n`, but a stale `expected_design_version`, an `OBSOLETE` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED` status, or a locking approval package is reported in preference to invalid merged geometry. That merged-geometry check in turn runs **before** the corrosion and biocompatibility gates, which are the last two things checked, corrosion first. A `material_spec_id` supplied in the body is resolved with the path design, ahead of the OCC check, so an unknown one answers `ERR_MATERIAL_SPEC_NOT_FOUND` even when the version is also stale.

An accepted revision does the following bookkeeping: `superseded_revision_no` is set to the value `current_revision_no` held before the revision, `current_revision_no` is increased by 1, `release_status` returns to `DRAFT`, `reviewed_by_user_id` is cleared to null, and `created_by_user_id` is re-stamped with the revising engineer's `X-User-ID` — so the segregation-of-duties gate on the next review is measured against whoever cut the latest revision. Every computed engineering field is then recalculated from the post-revision geometry: spring rate, all four stress fields, the fatigue criterion and margin, the life tier, the per-piece wire mass, and the tangle-risk index and class. One cascade reaches outside the design: every machine setup for this design whose `setup_status` is still `SCHEDULED` transitions to `ABORTED`, and each such abort returns that setup's coiler to `machine_state` `IDLE` with its `active_setup_id` cleared. The coiler's `version` is **not** advanced by this sweep — section 2.4 counts only the abort of an `ACTIVE` setup among the occupancy events — and a coiler that is already `IDLE` with a null `active_setup_id` is left entirely untouched, its `updated_at` included. The owning program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived at the end of the revision as well, for the same reason it is re-derived after an engineering verdict. Pulling a prototype back to `DRAFT` therefore also clears the shop schedule that was built around it.

Retirement is the `EngineeringManager`'s act and is the third and last way a design leaves the live population. A design may be retired from `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED`, `REJECTED` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`; any other status answers `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. Retirement additionally requires that no production run of this design is still in `FAI_PENDING` or `RUNNING` process status, and an attempt made while such a run is open is likewise refused as a conflict with `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the design moves to `OBSOLETE`; every tooling asset of the design whose `amortization_status` is `PENDING` or `AMORTIZING` transitions to `WRITTEN_OFF`, with `unamortized_cents` and `recovered_cents` left exactly as they stood — a write-off records the loss, it does not zero the ledger; every machine setup of the design still in `SCHEDULED` transitions to `ABORTED`, and unlike the revision cascade this sweep leaves the coilers themselves entirely untouched, because a merely `SCHEDULED` setup was never occupying one; and the owning program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived, which lands on `BLOCKED`. A prototype abandoned before its approval package was ever granted writes its tooling off directly from `PENDING` rather than from `AMORTIZING`.

### 3.4. Tooling Capitalisation and the Amortization Ledger

Coiling tools are capitalised by an `EngineeringManager` against a design, through a payload naming the `design_id`, a tool-room `tool_code` that must be unique across all tooling, and `original_cost_cents`, the capitalised cost in cents, which must be greater than zero and which is the conserved quantity of the amortization ledger. `original_cost_cents` must be a whole number of cents and `tool_code` a non-empty string with no length bound. The design's `release_status` is not consulted at all here, so a tool may be capitalised against a design in any status, `OBSOLETE` included. A newly capitalised tool opens in `amortization_status` `PENDING` with `recovered_cents` at 0 and `unamortized_cents` equal to `original_cost_cents`. Both `amortization_basis_pieces` and `amort_per_piece_cents` stay null: until the customer has granted full approval, nobody knows over how many pieces the tool will be recovered.

The ledger state walks four values — `PENDING`, `AMORTIZING`, `FULLY_AMORTIZED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` — of which the last two are terminal. The `PENDING → AMORTIZING` promotion fires as a system event when the tool's design reaches `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`, and it is the moment the amortization basis is frozen: `amortization_basis_pieces` takes the owning program's `committed_pieces`, and `amort_per_piece_cents` becomes `original_cost_cents` divided by that basis, rounded **up** to the next whole cent. Once armed, each shipment posts a recovery into `recovered_cents` and out of `unamortized_cents`; when a posting drives `unamortized_cents` to exactly 0, the tool transitions `AMORTIZING → FULLY_AMORTIZED`. From either `PENDING` or `AMORTIZING`, the design's retirement drives the tool to `WRITTEN_OFF` and freezes both ledger figures where they stood.

Recovery amount depends entirely on the program's `amortization_method`, and the two variants are genuinely different financial policies rather than a rounding difference. In both, the tool consulted is the newest tooling asset by `created_at` for the design the run was coiled from, and the program's `open_commitment_pieces` is read as it stood **before** the shipment changes any counter.

- Under `FORECAST_BASIS` — a straight-line forecast recovery — the amount is the lot's `piece_count` multiplied by the tool's frozen `amort_per_piece_cents`, capped at the tool's live `unamortized_cents`; whichever of the two is smaller is posted. Recovery is linear in piece count and completely independent of how much of the tool remains outstanding.
- Under `ACTUAL_BASIS` — an accelerated declining recovery — the amount starts from the tool's live `unamortized_cents` multiplied by an acceleration factor of 1.5, multiplied by the lot's `piece_count`, divided by the program's `open_commitment_pieces` (or by 1 if that figure is zero or lower, so the division is always defined), rounded **up** toward positive infinity to a whole cent, and then capped at the tool's live `unamortized_cents`; again the smaller of the two is posted. This variant recovers one and a half times the pro-rata share of the *remaining* balance against the *remaining* commitment, front-loading recovery heavily in the early lots.

Both variants are integral cents; there is no half-up rounding anywhere in this rule. A computed read on the program exposes them side by side for a hypothetical lot of a stated `piece_count`, returning the program's own `amortization_method`, the `tooling_asset_id` of the newest tooling asset by `created_at` for the program's most recently quoted design, that tool's `original_cost_cents`, `recovered_cents` and `unamortized_cents`, the program's live `open_commitment_pieces`, and then both hypothetical figures: `forecast_basis_recovery_cents` and `actual_basis_recovery_cents`. When the tool's `amort_per_piece_cents` has not yet been frozen, the forecast figure is computed on the fly as `original_cost_cents` divided by the program's `committed_pieces`, rounded up to a whole cent, multiplied through as the forecast variant prescribes. Both hypothetical figures are returned whatever the program's own `amortization_method` says, and both are capped at the tool's live `unamortized_cents`. The `piece_count` is read from the query string when it is present there and from the request body otherwise; either way it must be an integer greater than zero, and anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. "The program's most recently quoted design" means precisely the design named on the program's last successful quote, which the program remembers internally and never exposes as a field of its own representation. When the program has never been quoted, or when its last quoted design carries no tooling asset at all, the read still answers HTTP 200: `tooling_asset_id`, `original_cost_cents`, `recovered_cents` and `unamortized_cents` all come back null, `open_commitment_pieces` still carries the live figure, and both `forecast_basis_recovery_cents` and `actual_basis_recovery_cents` come back 0. Tooling assets may also be listed filtered by `design_id` and `amortization_status`.

### 3.5. Incoming Wire: Heats, Certification and the Three-Bucket Mass Ledger

Wire arrives from the mill in heats, and a `QualityInspector` books each one in against the grade it was purchased on. The receiving payload names the `material_spec_id`, the mill's `heat_number` — unique across all wire lots — and `received_kg`, the kilograms received, which must be greater than zero and is carried to three decimal places. That figure is the conserved quantity of the wire ledger and never changes afterwards. At receipt the three buckets are opened with all mass free: `on_hand_kg` equals `received_kg`, while `reserved_kg` and `consumed_kg` both start at 0.000. `certification_status` starts at `UNCERTIFIED`, and both `tensile_test_mpa` and `certified_by_user_id` are null.

Certification is a one-shot branching verdict recorded by a `QualityInspector` who supplies the measured `tensile_test_mpa`, a required plain number that must be greater than zero and that is recorded on the lot exactly as supplied rather than re-rounded to any decimal count. The lot's `certification_status` must still be `UNCERTIFIED`; presenting a lot that has already been decided answers `ERR_WIRE_LOT_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409), and because both outcomes are terminal a heat can never be re-tested. Both outcomes record the supplied `tensile_test_mpa` on the lot and stamp `certified_by_user_id` with the inspector's `X-User-ID`. The outcomes differ only in the comparison against the grade: when the measured value is at least the linked material specification's `tensile_grade_mpa`, the lot moves `UNCERTIFIED → CERTIFIED`; when the measured value falls below that grade, the lot moves `UNCERTIFIED → REJECTED` and no run may ever draw from it. Wire lots may be listed filtered by `material_spec_id` and `certification_status`, and each read exposes the full three-bucket ledger.

### 3.6. Coilers, Setup Windows and Machine Occupation

A `PlantAdmin` commissions each computer-controlled coiler with a `machine_code` unique across all machines, a `max_wire_diameter_mm` greater than zero giving the largest wire the coiler can form, and `changeover_buffer_minutes`, the whole number of minutes of tooling changeover that must separate two setup windows on this machine, which must be at least 0. A new coiler opens in `machine_state` `IDLE` with `active_setup_id` null. The machine cycles `IDLE → SETUP → PRODUCTION → IDLE` with an early exit back to `IDLE` when a setup is aborted, and the three values `IDLE`, `SETUP` and `PRODUCTION` are the only ones it takes. Machines may be listed filtered by `machine_state`.

Booking a coiling setup is the `ProcessTechnician`'s act and is the densest temporal gate in the system. The payload names the `machine_id` of the coiler, the `design_id` being set up, the `tooling_asset_id` of the tool set being mounted, and the occupation window as `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive), both ISO 8601 UTC instants. Each instant is parsed into an absolute UTC value for the comparisons below, but the setup row stores and echoes back the **string exactly as the client supplied it**, so a window sent as `2026-08-05T08:00:00+00:00` reads back in that form rather than normalised to the three-fractional-digit shape `created_at` uses; the heat-treat batch windows of section 3.9 are stored and echoed the same way. Any form a standard ISO 8601 parser accepts is accepted, a value carrying no zone offset is read as UTC, and a non-string or unparseable value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). It must also carry `expected_machine_version` per section 2.7. The design must have reached a released state — its `release_status` must be `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`, and a `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `REJECTED` or `OBSOLETE` design answers `ERR_PPAP_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422). The named tool must belong to that same design, or the booking answers `ERR_TOOLING_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The design's `wire_diameter_mm` must not exceed the coiler's `max_wire_diameter_mm`, or the booking answers `ERR_WIRE_DIAMETER_EXCEEDS_MACHINE` (HTTP 422) — and this ceiling is checked **before** the window arithmetic, so a too-heavy wire on a busy machine reports the diameter problem rather than a clash. Then `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422); and `window_start` must not be earlier than the current reading of the logical clock, or `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422).

**The buffered window conflict.** Setup windows are treated as half-open intervals that include their start and exclude their end, and each is widened at the far end by the machine's own changeover buffer expressed as a duration in minutes. Two windows conflict when the new window's start is strictly earlier than the existing window's end plus the buffer **and** the existing window's start is strictly earlier than the new window's end plus the buffer. Only setups on the same machine whose `setup_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` are considered; `RELEASED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `ABORTED` setups never conflict, however their windows fall. If any such conflicting setup exists, the booking is rejected with `ERR_SETUP_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A gap exactly equal to `changeover_buffer_minutes` between two windows is permitted — the comparison is strict on both sides, so touching the buffer boundary is legal.

A successful booking stamps `set_by_user_id` from the technician's `X-User-ID`, opens the setup in `setup_status` `SCHEDULED`, and increases the coiler's `version` by 1. It also performs a supersession sweep: every **other** setup on the same machine for the same design whose `setup_status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED`, and each superseded row's `superseded_by_setup_id` is set to the id of the setup that has just been created. This is precisely why the booking is guarded by `expected_machine_version` — a client working from a stale machine view would silently supersede a schedule it never saw. Setups may be listed filtered by `machine_id`, `design_id`, `tooling_asset_id` and `setup_status`.

Activating a setup takes it `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE`. The setup must be `SCHEDULED` — a setup that is already `ACTIVE`, or that is `RELEASED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `ABORTED`, answers `ERR_SETUP_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The coiler must be at rest: if its `machine_state` is anything but `IDLE`, the activation answers `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_IDLE` (HTTP 409). And the logical clock must be inside the booked window, at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`; outside that span the answer is `ERR_OUTSIDE_SETUP_WINDOW` (HTTP 409). On success the coiler transitions `IDLE → SETUP`, its `active_setup_id` is set to this setup's id, and its `version` increases by 1.

Aborting is the escape hatch and accepts a setup in either `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; anything else answers `ERR_SETUP_INVALID_STATE`. The setup transitions to `ABORTED`. If it had been `ACTIVE`, the coiler returns to `machine_state` `IDLE` with `active_setup_id` cleared to null and its `version` increased by 1; a setup aborted straight out of `SCHEDULED` was never occupying the machine and leaves it alone. In addition, every production run of the aborted setup whose `process_status` is `FAI_PENDING` or `RUNNING` is cancelled, in the collection's chronological order, and each such cancellation is the *full* cancellation of section 3.7: it releases that run's wire reservation, sweeps the run's `ALLOCATED` and `PACKED` lots to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE` with the owning programs' counters moved accordingly, and re-derives every affected `delivery_posture`. The order inside this operation is pinned: the setup is driven to `ABORTED` **before** the runs are cancelled, so the setup-abort hop inside each cancellation finds a setup that is no longer `ACTIVE` and does nothing. The coiler is therefore freed and its `version` advanced exactly once for the whole operation however many runs the abort sweeps up, which is what section 2.4's "exactly 1" per occupancy event means here. Two further edges into terminal states are driven by the system rather than by a technician: a setup goes `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED` when a newer setup for the same machine and design is booked, and a setup goes `ACTIVE → RELEASED` when its production run reaches `COMPLETE`, which also returns the coiler `PRODUCTION → IDLE`.

### 3.7. Production Runs, the Wire Reservation and the First Article

Opening a production run is the `ProcessTechnician`'s act and joins three things: the mounted setup named by `setup_id`, the certified heat named by `wire_lot_id`, and the design named by `design_id`, together with `planned_pieces`, the number of pieces the run is scheduled to coil, which must be greater than zero. The gates run in this order. The setup must currently be `ACTIVE`; a `SCHEDULED`, `RELEASED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `ABORTED` setup answers `ERR_SETUP_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The heat's `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED`; an `UNCERTIFIED` or `REJECTED` heat answers `ERR_WIRE_LOT_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422). The design's `release_status` must be `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_PPAP_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422) — note that a merely `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` design may be run for first articles and prototype quantities long before any approval package exists, which is exactly how the shop generates the evidence the package needs. The `design_id` in the body must equal the design the setup was tooled for, or the answer is `ERR_SETUP_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The heat's `material_spec_id` must equal the design's `material_spec_id`, or the answer is `ERR_MATERIAL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Finally the heat must physically hold enough wire, as computed below; a shortfall answers `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WIRE` (HTTP 409).

**The wire reservation.** The kilograms a run reserves are computed in a pinned order that must not be rearranged. Start from the design's **stored** `wire_mass_per_piece_kg`, already rounded to six decimal places. Multiply it by `planned_pieces`. Inflate that product by a scrap allowance of three percent — that is, multiply by one plus 0.03. Round the result half up to three decimal places, once, at the very end. That figure is written to the run's `wire_reserved_kg` and is the value compared against the heat's `on_hand_kg` for the sufficiency gate; the heat's ledger is never fed into the computation, only compared against its result. That comparison is strict in the shortfall direction: only a required mass **greater than** the heat's live `on_hand_kg` is rejected, so a run that needs exactly what the rack still holds is opened and drives `on_hand_kg` to 0.000. Recomputing an unrounded per-piece mass and scaling that instead changes the third decimal on large runs and is wrong. On success the reservation moves mass without creating any: `wire_reserved_kg` is subtracted from the heat's `on_hand_kg` and added to its `reserved_kg`, leaving `received_kg` untouched.

A newly opened run starts on both axes at rest — `process_status` `FAI_PENDING`, `quality_status` `OPEN`, `fai_disposition` `PENDING`, `heat_treat_state` `NOT_STARTED`, `peening_verified` false, `produced_pieces` and `scrapped_pieces` at 0, `wire_consumed_kg` at 0.000, and both `min_cpk_observed` and `min_ppk_observed` null. Its coiler transitions `SETUP → PRODUCTION` and the machine's `version` increases by 1, while `active_setup_id` keeps pointing at the setup the run was opened on. Nothing limits an `ACTIVE` setup to one run at a time: a second run may be opened on the same setup while an earlier one is still `FAI_PENDING` or `RUNNING`, and it takes its own reservation and advances the coiler's `version` again even though the coiler already reads `PRODUCTION`. The two status axes are genuinely independent: `process_status` walks `FAI_PENDING → RUNNING → COMPLETE` with `CANCELLED` as an early exit, `quality_status` moves between `OPEN` and `SPC_HOLD` and then to either `DISPOSITIONED_ACCEPT` or `SCRAPPED`, and neither axis gates the other's transitions except where a rule below says so explicitly. A run may be `COMPLETE` while sitting on `SPC_HOLD`, and may be on `SPC_HOLD` while still `FAI_PENDING`. Runs may be listed filtered by `setup_id`, `wire_lot_id`, `design_id`, `process_status` and `quality_status`.

**First-article measurement.** A `QualityInspector` records a first-article subgroup against one characteristic of the run's design, supplying the `characteristic_id` and `measurements`, an array of numbers in measurement order. The run must be `FAI_PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The characteristic must belong to the run's design, otherwise `ERR_CHARACTERISTIC_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). And the subgroup must contain at least three measurements — below that there is no Bessel-corrected standard deviation to compute — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SUBGROUP_SIZE` (HTTP 422). The two failure modes around `measurements` sit at different ranks and must not be conflated: a `measurements` that is absent, is not a JSON array, is an **empty** array, or contains any non-numeric element — a boolean counts as non-numeric — is a rank 4 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), while only a well-formed all-numeric array of exactly one or two values ever reaches `ERR_INVALID_SUBGROUP_SIZE`. The server stamps `record_kind` as `FAI` from the endpoint itself; `record_kind` is never accepted from a request body. It also stamps `inspected_by_user_id` with the inspector's `X-User-ID`, which is what makes the segregation gate on the verdict possible.

**Capability statistics.** Every inspection record, first-article or in-process alike, is computed the same way. `subgroup_size` is the number of values in `measurements`. `mean_value` is their arithmetic mean, rounded half up to four decimal places. `sigma_value` is the Bessel-corrected sample standard deviation: take each measurement's deviation from the **unrounded** mean, square it, sum the squares, divide by one less than the subgroup size, take the square root, and round half up to six decimal places. `cpk_value` is the short-run capability of this subgroup: take the characteristic's `usl` minus the unrounded mean, and the unrounded mean minus the characteristic's `lsl`, keep whichever of those two distances is **smaller**, divide it by three times the unrounded standard deviation, and round half up to three decimal places. When that unrounded standard deviation comes out zero — every value in the subgroup identical, which a three-measurement subgroup reaches easily — there is nothing to divide by, and the index is neither an error nor null but the fixed sentinel `999.999`, written verbatim with no further rounding and with the two distances not consulted at all; the same sentinel stands in for `ppk_value` whenever the pooled standard deviation is zero. Because the smaller of the two distances binds, the metric is non-linear — which side of the specification band is limiting flips as the mean drifts across the nominal. Each record also keeps `measurements` itself, echoed back as the array of numbers that was supplied in the order it was supplied, alongside `run_id`, `characteristic_id`, `record_kind` and `inspected_by_user_id`; being write-once it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version` at all, per section 2.4.

`ppk_value` is the pooled long-run capability and is **null on every first-article record**. On an in-process record it is computed by pooling every measurement of every inspection record whose `record_kind` is `SPC` and which shares both this record's `run_id` and its `characteristic_id`, including the record being created, into one single sample; records belonging to other runs never pool in, and the order in which records are pooled does not change the result. From that pooled sample take the pooled mean, form the same two distances against `usl` and `lsl`, keep the smaller, divide by three times the pooled standard deviation, and round half up to three decimal places. Each record keeps the pooled figure it was stamped with at its own creation — earlier rows are never restamped when a later subgroup arrives.

`control_state` is `OUT_OF_CONTROL` when the **rounded** `cpk_value` is below the characteristic's `cpk_floor`, or when any single measurement in the subgroup lies above `usl` or below `lsl`; it is `IN_CONTROL` otherwise. The comparison against the floor is made after rounding, and a `cpk_value` exactly equal to the floor passes. At the run level, `min_cpk_observed` is refreshed to the lowest `cpk_value` across all of the run's inspection records, while `min_ppk_observed` is refreshed to the lowest `ppk_value` across only those of the run's records whose `record_kind` is `SPC` and whose characteristic has `is_safety_critical` true. First-article records therefore move `min_cpk_observed` but leave `min_ppk_observed` untouched. A first-article record with an `OUT_OF_CONTROL` control state records that fact and nothing more: the quality axis stays `OPEN`, because only in-process subgroups can drive a run onto a hold. Inspection records may be read individually and listed filtered by `run_id`, `characteristic_id`, `record_kind` and `control_state`.

**The first-article verdict.** A second `QualityInspector` closes out the first article with a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. Both branches enforce exactly the same five gates, in this order: the run's `process_status` must be `FAI_PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; `fai_disposition` must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_FAI_ALREADY_DISPOSITIONED` (HTTP 409); the acting `X-User-ID` must not match the `inspected_by_user_id` of any first-article record on this run, otherwise `ERR_SELF_DISPOSITION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409) — whoever measured may not judge; the run's design must have at least one critical characteristic, otherwise `ERR_CHARACTERISTIC_SET_EMPTY` (HTTP 422); and every critical characteristic of that design must have at least one first-article record on this run, otherwise `ERR_INCOMPLETE_FAI_COVERAGE` (HTTP 422). Coverage is about characteristics, not counts: one subgroup per characteristic suffices, and a sixth subgroup on an already-covered characteristic does nothing for the missing one.

`ACCEPT` sets `fai_disposition` to `ACCEPTED` and advances the run `FAI_PENDING → RUNNING`, and nothing else moves. `REJECT` sets `fai_disposition` to `REJECTED` and drives a four-hop cascade: the run's `process_status` becomes `CANCELLED`; the wire reservation is released using the run's stored `wire_reserved_kg` verbatim, subtracting it from the heat's `reserved_kg` and adding it back to the heat's `on_hand_kg`; the run's machine setup transitions `ACTIVE → ABORTED`; and that abort in turn returns the coiler to `machine_state` `IDLE` with `active_setup_id` null and its `version` increased by 1. That cancellation is the very same one the abandonment paragraph below describes, so it also runs the lot-quarantine sweep and re-derives the owning program's `delivery_posture` — both vacuous on a run that never reached `COMPLETE` and therefore never carried a lot, but both fire all the same.

**Closing out and abandoning a run.** A `ProcessTechnician` completes a `RUNNING` run by supplying `produced_pieces` and `scrapped_pieces`, each a required JSON integer of at least 0 — zero of both is a legitimate close-out that consumes no wire and hands the whole reservation back. The run must not be on `SPC_HOLD` — a held run cannot be closed and answers `ERR_RUN_ON_SPC_HOLD` (HTTP 409) — and its `process_status` must be `RUNNING`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. The two counts together must not exceed the run's `planned_pieces`; a request that overruns the plan is rejected as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On success both counts are recorded and `process_status` becomes `COMPLETE`. Consumption is then computed as the design's stored six-decimal `wire_mass_per_piece_kg` multiplied by the sum of `produced_pieces` and `scrapped_pieces` — scrap burns wire exactly like good product does — rounded half up to three decimal places, and written to `wire_consumed_kg`. Three ledger postings follow in this exact order, each rounded half up to three decimal places: the run's stored `wire_reserved_kg` is subtracted from the heat's `reserved_kg`; `wire_consumed_kg` is added to the heat's `consumed_kg`; and the difference between the stored `wire_reserved_kg` and `wire_consumed_kg` is added back to the heat's `on_hand_kg`, returning whatever the scrap allowance over-reserved. `received_kg` is conserved throughout. The run's setup transitions `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, and the coiler returns `PRODUCTION → IDLE` with `active_setup_id` cleared and `version` increased by 1. That last hop is conditional on the setup still being `ACTIVE`: a run completed after its setup had already been released by a sibling run settles its own ledger normally and leaves both the setup and the coiler entirely untouched.

Abandoning a run is available from `FAI_PENDING` or `RUNNING`; any other `process_status` answers `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. The run moves to `CANCELLED`, the whole reservation comes back using the stored `wire_reserved_kg` verbatim — subtracted from `reserved_kg`, added to `on_hand_kg`, with `consumed_kg` untouched — the setup transitions `ACTIVE → ABORTED`, and the coiler returns to `IDLE` with `active_setup_id` null and `version` increased by 1. Because pieces from a cancelled run must never reach a customer, every shipment lot of this run whose `lot_status` is `ALLOCATED` or `PACKED` transitions to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE`, and for each such lot the owning program's `quarantined_lot_count` increases by 1, its `allocated_pieces` decreases by the lot's `piece_count` and its `open_commitment_pieces` increases by the same `piece_count`, after which that program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived.

### 3.8. In-Process Capability, SPC Holds and Quality Dispositions

Once a run is coiling, a `QualityInspector` records in-process subgroups against the same characteristics the first article covered, again supplying a `characteristic_id` and a `measurements` array. These records may be taken while the run's `process_status` is `RUNNING` **or** after it has become `COMPLETE`: dimensional and load-test subgroups drawn after the last piece has been coiled are still in-process statistical-process-control records, not first-article records. Any other process status answers `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The characteristic must belong to the run's design, otherwise `ERR_CHARACTERISTIC_DESIGN_MISMATCH`, and the subgroup must again carry at least three measurements, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SUBGROUP_SIZE`. The server stamps `record_kind` as `SPC` — again from the endpoint, never from the body — stamps `inspected_by_user_id`, and computes the full statistic set of section 3.7 including the pooled `ppk_value`.

An in-process record whose `control_state` comes out `OUT_OF_CONTROL` on a characteristic whose `is_safety_critical` is true escalates the whole run, and the escalation is a five-hop cascade that a naive implementation will only get half right. Hop one is the record itself. Hop two: the run's `quality_status` transitions `OPEN → SPC_HOLD`. Hop three: every shipment lot of this run whose `lot_status` is `ALLOCATED` or `PACKED` transitions to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE`. Hop four: for each lot so quarantined, the lot's owning program has the lot's `piece_count` added back to `open_commitment_pieces`, has `quarantined_lot_count` increased by 1, and has `allocated_pieces` decreased by that same `piece_count`. Hop five: because `quarantined_lot_count` has risen above zero, each affected program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived, which drops an `ON_TRACK` program to `AT_RISK`. Hop five also reaches the program owning the run's **own** design, whether or not any lot of the run was quarantined and even when the run carries no lot at all: the held run is itself one of the three inputs of the posture rule of section 3.1, so a lotless program still falls from `ON_TRACK` to `AT_RISK` the moment one of its runs goes onto a hold. An out-of-control record on a characteristic whose `is_safety_critical` is false records its control state and nothing more — the quality axis is untouched, no lot is quarantined, and no counter moves. The hold only fires while the run's `quality_status` is `OPEN`; a run already on `SPC_HOLD` simply accumulates further records.

Lifting a hold is a `QualityInspector`'s judgement, delivered as a `decision` of `RELEASE`, `ACCEPT_AS_IS` or `SCRAP`. All three require the run's `quality_status` to be `SPC_HOLD`; a run that is `OPEN`, `DISPOSITIONED_ACCEPT` or `SCRAPPED` answers `ERR_RUN_NOT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 409).

`RELEASE` returns the run `SPC_HOLD → OPEN` and is the cyclic edge on the quality axis — a run can be held and released repeatedly over its life. It requires evidence that the process has come back: for **every** safety-critical characteristic of the run's design, the newest inspection record by `created_at` must have a `control_state` of `IN_CONTROL`. If any safety-critical characteristic's newest record is still `OUT_OF_CONTROL`, the release is refused with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The newest record is chosen across both `record_kind` values, an `FAI` row included, and a safety-critical characteristic that has **no** record on this run at all is simply skipped rather than treated as missing evidence, so it never blocks either verdict. `ACCEPT_AS_IS` records a concession instead and moves the run `SPC_HOLD → DISPOSITIONED_ACCEPT`, which is terminal on the quality axis; it carries the mirror-image requirement, namely that no safety-critical characteristic of the design has an `OUT_OF_CONTROL` newest record by `created_at`, and the same refusal when one does. Neither verdict un-quarantines anything: lots quarantined by the cascade stay `QUARANTINED`, no program counter moves, and this operation only lifts the run-level hold. Each lot returns to `ALLOCATED` solely through the quarantine-release act described in section 3.11. `delivery_posture` is nevertheless re-derived for every program holding a lot on this run and — when it is not already one of those — for the program owning the run's own design as well, which is how a program carrying no lot at all climbs back from `AT_RISK` to `ON_TRACK` once its last held run is released; a program whose `quarantined_lot_count` is still positive stays `AT_RISK`.

`SCRAP` is the write-off verdict and carries no evidence requirement at all — a run can always be scrapped. It moves the run's `quality_status` to `SCRAPPED`. If the run's `process_status` is still `FAI_PENDING` or `RUNNING`, that axis is driven to `CANCELLED` as well, which releases the wire reservation using the stored `wire_reserved_kg` verbatim and aborts the machine setup exactly as an ordinary run cancellation does, freeing the coiler back to `IDLE`. Every shipment lot of the run still in `ALLOCATED` or `PACKED` transitions to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE`, and for each of those lots the owning program's `quarantined_lot_count` increases by 1, `allocated_pieces` decreases by the lot's `piece_count`, and `open_commitment_pieces` increases by that same `piece_count`, after which `delivery_posture` is re-derived.

### 3.9. Stress Relief, Shot Peening and Oven Capacity

A `PlantAdmin` commissions each stress-relief oven with a unique `oven_code`, a `capacity_kg` greater than zero giving the maximum aggregate mass that may be co-resident in the oven at any instant, and a `max_temp_c` greater than zero giving the temperature ceiling it holds. A new oven opens with `scheduled_load_kg` at 0.000, and that field is always the sum of `load_kg` over the oven's batches whose `batch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` — every such batch of the oven, irrespective of window, not merely the ones overlapping some interval of interest. Unlike the kilogram figures that move along a ledger, `capacity_kg` and `max_temp_c` are plain numbers recorded exactly as supplied and never re-rounded to three decimal places. `scheduled_load_kg` is recomputed from scratch, never patched by a delta, every time a batch of this oven is scheduled, discharged or cancelled, and each recomputation refreshes the oven row, advancing its `updated_at` and its `version` by 1 even when the recomputed total turns out unchanged. Ovens are read one at a time by id; there is no oven list endpoint.

Scheduling a batch is a `ProcessTechnician`'s act. The payload names the `oven_id`, the `run_id` whose pieces are being relieved, the occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end` — both **inclusive** for this entity — the weighed basket `load_kg` in kilograms carried to three decimal places and greater than zero, the requested soak `target_temp_c` greater than zero, and the optional `peening_requested` flag recording whether shot peening is part of this batch's recipe, defaulting to false. The gates run in the following order, which follows the ladder of section 2.8 rather than the narrative sequence one might otherwise expect. The run must not be on a hold, and this is checked **first**: a `quality_status` of `SPC_HOLD` answers `ERR_RUN_ON_SPC_HOLD` (HTTP 409), so a run that is simultaneously held and in a wrong process status — a cancelled run that was held while it was still coiling, for instance — reports the hold rather than the process status. Only then must the run's `process_status` be `RUNNING` or `COMPLETE`; `FAI_PENDING` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The requested `target_temp_c` must not exceed the oven's `max_temp_c`, or `ERR_TEMP_EXCEEDS_OVEN` (HTTP 422). `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422), and `window_start` must not be earlier than the logical clock, or `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). The capacity gate comes last.

**The closed-interval capacity gate.** Oven windows are **closed** intervals, so two windows collide even when they merely touch at a single endpoint: they collide when the first window's start is at or before the second window's end **and** the second window's start is at or before the first window's end. Sum `load_kg` over every batch on the same oven whose `batch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` and whose window collides with the new one; batches that are `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETE` are excluded even when their windows collide, because their mass has left the oven. Add the new batch's `load_kg` to that occupied total, round the sum half up to three decimal places, and compare it against the oven's `capacity_kg`: the comparison is strict, so a total that exceeds capacity is rejected with `ERR_OVEN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) while a total exactly equal to capacity is accepted. A successful booking opens the batch in `batch_status` `SCHEDULED` and adds its `load_kg` to the oven's `scheduled_load_kg`. The run's `heat_treat_state` stays `NOT_STARTED` — scheduling a batch is not charging one. Batches may be listed filtered by `oven_id`, `run_id` and `batch_status`.

Charging a batch takes it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING`. The batch must be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and the logical clock must have reached at least `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). There is no upper temporal bound at all here: a clock that has run past `window_end` does not prevent a charge. The cascade is a single hop, and that hop is a **re-derivation** rather than an assignment: the run's `heat_treat_state` is recomputed from every batch of that run, taking `COMPLETE` if any of them is `COMPLETE`, otherwise `IN_PROCESS` if any is `RUNNING`, otherwise `NOT_STARTED`. In the ordinary case that lands on `IN_PROCESS`, but on a run that already has a discharged batch on file it stays `COMPLETE`, because charging a second basket cannot un-finish the first. Charging always refreshes the run row, advancing its `updated_at` and `version` even when the derived value does not move.

Discharging takes a `RUNNING` batch to `COMPLETE` — any other `batch_status` answers `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` — and requires the technician to state `peening_performed`, whether shot peening actually happened. Note the asymmetry between the two peening fields: `peening_requested` is the recipe's intent stated at scheduling time, while `peening_performed` is the outcome recorded at discharge and stays null until the batch is `COMPLETE`. Discharge removes the batch's `load_kg` from the oven's `scheduled_load_kg` by the same re-derivation, re-derives the run's `heat_treat_state`, which now necessarily reads `COMPLETE`, and sets the run's `peening_verified` to true only when `peening_performed` is true — a `false` never clears a flag an earlier peened batch already raised. Then comes the trap: **if the design requires shot peening and the batch reports that peening was not performed**, that is, `shot_peening_required` on the design is true while `peening_performed` on the batch is false, the run's `quality_status` transitions `OPEN → SPC_HOLD` and the same cascade as an out-of-control safety-critical subgroup fires — the run's `ALLOCATED` and `PACKED` lots are quarantined with `quarantine_reason` `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE`, each affected program's `quarantined_lot_count`, `allocated_pieces` and `open_commitment_pieces` are adjusted, and each affected `delivery_posture` is re-derived. A part whose endurance limit was calculated with the peened factor cannot be shipped unpeened.

Cancelling a batch is available only from `SCHEDULED`; anything else answers `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. The batch moves to `CANCELLED` and its `load_kg` is removed from the oven's `scheduled_load_kg`, so the interval it occupied stops counting toward capacity for every later batch booking. The run's `heat_treat_state` is re-derived by exactly the rule stated for charging, so it returns to `NOT_STARTED` when no `RUNNING` or `COMPLETE` batch remains for that run. Cancellation is the one heat-treat transition that touches the run row **only** when that re-derivation actually changes the value — charging and discharging always refresh it — so cancelling a scheduled basket alongside an already-discharged one leaves the run's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they stood.

### 3.10. Production-Part Approval Rounds and the Customer Verdict

A qualification submission is the PPAP package a `ProgramManager` puts in front of the customer. It records the `program_id` it is filed under, the `design_id` the package covers, and the `ppap_level` claimed for it, an integer from 1 to 5. Five gates guard the filing, in this order. The design must exist and its `release_status` must be `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` — a `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `PRODUCTION_RELEASED`, `REJECTED` or `OBSOLETE` design cannot be submitted and answers `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The design's `program_id` must equal the body's `program_id`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The body's `ppap_level` must equal the program's `ppap_level`, otherwise `ERR_PPAP_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the customer's declared submission level and the package's claimed level are one number, not two. No other submission for the same `program_id` and `design_id` pair may be in flight or partially granted, that is, none may have a `submission_status` of `SUBMITTED`, `INTERIM_APPROVED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_SUBMISSION_EXISTS` (HTTP 409); a pair whose only prior submission is `APPROVED` or `REJECTED_FINAL` may be filed again. Finally there must be evidence the part can actually be made: at least one production run on that `design_id` must carry a `fai_disposition` of `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_NO_QUALIFIED_RUN` (HTTP 422). A successful filing opens at `round_no` 1 in `submission_status` `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` stamped from the header, `interim_piece_cap`, `reviewed_by_program_id` and `decided_at` all null. The filing also re-derives the program's `delivery_posture`, which lands on the value the program already held, because a merely `SUBMITTED` package is not one of the two granted statuses the posture rule looks for. Submissions may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `design_id` and `submission_status`.

The verdict belongs to the `CustomerRepresentative` alone, and only for its own program: because that role is entity-backed, its `X-User-ID` must equal the submission's `program_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. The submission must be `SUBMITTED`; any other status answers `ERR_SUBMISSION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Every verdict stamps `reviewed_by_program_id` from the header and `decided_at` from the logical clock, and every verdict re-derives the program's `delivery_posture`. Four decisions are available.

`APPROVE` is full release and drives the deepest cascade in the system, four hops long. Hop one sets `submission_status` to `APPROVED`, a terminal status. Hop two promotes the design `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED → PRODUCTION_RELEASED`. Hop three takes every `PENDING` tooling asset of the design to `AMORTIZING`, freezing `amortization_basis_pieces` from the program's `committed_pieces` and `amort_per_piece_cents` as the cost divided by that basis rounded up; that sweep hangs off the `APPROVE` verdict itself rather than off the promotion, so in the corner where hop two finds the design in some status other than `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` and therefore promotes nothing, the tooling sweep still runs. Hop four sets the program's `quote_frozen` to true — the commercial terms are now contractual and no further quote may be run — and re-derives `delivery_posture`, which reaches `ON_TRACK` on a program with no quarantined lots and no held run. An implementation that stops after hop two will pass a design-status assertion and then fail every shipment, because tooling that never left `PENDING` blocks the ship gate.

`INTERIM_APPROVE` is a capped, conditional release for a customer who wants first pieces before the full package is signed off. It requires an `interim_piece_cap` in the body that is present, a JSON integer and greater than zero; a missing, non-integer or non-positive cap is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and because that typing check is made before the submission's own status is read, it is reported even on a submission that is not `SUBMITTED`. The submission moves to `INTERIM_APPROVED` with the cap recorded, and `delivery_posture` is re-derived — an interim verdict counts as an approval for posture purposes, so a blocked program becomes `ON_TRACK` or `AT_RISK`. What it deliberately does **not** do is promote anything: the design stays `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` and every tooling asset stays `PENDING`, because only a full `APPROVE` arms amortization. The practical consequence is precise and worth stating plainly — an interim-approved program may allocate and pack lots up to its cap, but those lots cannot ship, because the ship gate requires tooling in `AMORTIZING` or `FULLY_AMORTIZED`.

`REQUEST_REVISION` is the soft rejection: the submission moves to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and nothing else changes. The design keeps its `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` status and its tooling stays `PENDING`; because no verdict of record is in force, the program's re-derived `delivery_posture` falls back to `BLOCKED` unless some other submission on the program is still `APPROVED` or `INTERIM_APPROVED`. `REJECT_FINAL` is the hard rejection and, like `APPROVE`, cascades: `submission_status` becomes `REJECTED_FINAL`, which is terminal, the design is driven `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED → REJECTED`, and the program's `delivery_posture` is re-derived to `BLOCKED`. A rejected design is not dead — the engineering team can cut a fresh revision from `REJECTED` and start the review chain again — but this particular submission can never be revived.

Resubmission is how the cycle closes. A `ProgramManager` may resubmit a package whose `submission_status` is `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `INTERIM_APPROVED`; `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED_FINAL` answer `ERR_SUBMISSION_INVALID_STATE`. The round budget is three: `round_no` must be strictly less than `MAX_SUBMISSION_ROUNDS`, which is 3, so a package already on round 3 answers `ERR_MAX_SUBMISSION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) and the customer relationship has to be repaired outside the API. The design must still be `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. These three gates are applied in exactly the order just written, which departs from the section 2.8 ladder deliberately: the submission-status gate is checked first and the round budget second, so a package already on round 3 whose design has since left `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` answers `ERR_MAX_SUBMISSION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` rather than the numerically lower-ranked `ERR_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`, and a `SUBMITTED` package whose design has left that status answers `ERR_SUBMISSION_INVALID_STATE`. A successful resubmit increments `round_no` by 1, returns `submission_status` to `SUBMITTED`, clears `interim_piece_cap` back to null, clears `reviewed_by_program_id` and `decided_at` back to null, and re-stamps `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting header. Resubmitting from `INTERIM_APPROVED` therefore withdraws the cap that was letting the program allocate pieces, and the re-derived `delivery_posture` returns to `BLOCKED` while no verdict is in force — lots already allocated stay allocated, but no new one can be created until the next verdict lands.

### 3.11. Quoting, Allocation, Packaging, Shipment and Quarantine

**Pricing a program.** A `ProgramManager` prices a program against one design for a stated `quoted_qty`, guarded by `expected_program_version`. `quoted_qty` must be a JSON integer greater than zero. The program's `quote_frozen` must be false, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) — once the customer has fully approved, the numbers are fixed. The design must belong to the program, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_DESIGN_MISMATCH`. The design's own `release_status` is not gated here at all — a `DRAFT` design may be priced — but it must resolve, so an unknown `design_id` answers `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND` ahead of both the version check and the freeze check. The eleven steps below run **in this exact order**, and the order is pinned because the dependency looks circular: the discount tier is keyed on a subtotal computed before any discount or amortization, while the amortization cap is keyed on the unit price after the discount.

1. The material content per piece is the design's `wire_mass_per_piece_kg` multiplied by the material spec's `cost_per_kg_cents`, rounded to 4 decimal places.
2. The labour content per piece is the design's `cycle_seconds_per_piece` divided by 3600 and multiplied by the labour rate of 6500 cents per hour, rounded to 4 decimal places.
3. The base unit cost is the sum of those two, rounded to 4 decimal places.
4. `quoted_pre_discount_subtotal_cents` is the base unit cost multiplied by `quoted_qty`, rounded to whole cents.
5. `quoted_volume_discount_pct` is read off that subtotal against the breakpoints 50000, 250000 and 1000000 cents: below 50000 it is 0.00, below 250000 it is 0.03, below 1000000 it is 0.06, and at or above 1000000 it is 0.09. The comparisons are strict below and inclusive at the top tier.
6. The discounted unit cost is the base unit cost multiplied by one minus the discount fraction, rounded to 4 decimal places.
7. The raw amortization ask is the newest tooling asset's `unamortized_cents` divided by `quoted_qty`, rounded **up** to the next whole cent. When the design carries no tooling asset at all the ask is 0, which makes step 9 zero as well and leaves step 10's unit price equal to the discounted unit cost rounded to whole cents.
8. The amortization cap is 0.25 of the discounted unit cost, rounded **down** to a whole cent.
9. `quoted_tooling_amort_per_piece_cents` is the smaller of the raw ask and the cap. The cap exists so that tooling recovery can never dominate the piece price: on an expensive tool quoted in small quantity the raw ask is the binding value only until it crosses a quarter of the discounted unit cost.
10. `quoted_unit_price_cents` is the discounted unit cost plus that per-piece amortization, rounded to whole cents.
11. `quoted_total_cents` is `quoted_unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quoted_qty` plus the flat setup fee of 125000 cents.

Only the named design, its material spec, the newest tooling asset by `created_at` for that design and the request's `quoted_qty` feed this calculation; rows of other designs never contribute. Per-piece money is carried at 4 decimal places — mills — all the way through step 9, and only steps 4, 8, 10 and 11 land on whole cents. The endpoint also increments the program's `version` by 1.

**Allocating a lot.** Creating a shipment lot is the densest gate in the API: a `ProgramManager` names a `run_id`, a `program_id` and a `piece_count` that must be a JSON integer greater than zero, and the substantive conditions below apply in the priority order of section 2.8. The run's `process_status` must be `COMPLETE`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_NOT_COMPLETE` (HTTP 409); its `quality_status` must be `OPEN` or `DISPOSITIONED_ACCEPT`, and a held or scrapped run answers `ERR_RUN_ON_SPC_HOLD` (HTTP 409). The PPAP standing gate requires both that the design's `release_status` is `PROTOTYPE_RELEASED` or `PRODUCTION_RELEASED` **and** that the newest qualification submission by `created_at` for the program and the run's design has a `submission_status` of `APPROVED` or `INTERIM_APPROVED`; a failure answers `ERR_PPAP_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422). Heat treatment must be finished and peening honoured: the run's `heat_treat_state` must be `COMPLETE`, and either the design does not require shot peening or the run's `peening_verified` is true, otherwise `ERR_HEAT_TREAT_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). The run's `min_ppk_observed` must be non-null and at least the program's `required_ppk`, otherwise `ERR_PPK_BELOW_PROGRAM_FLOOR` (HTTP 422) — a run that has never taken a safety-critical SPC subgroup has a null `min_ppk_observed` and fails here. The program's `delivery_posture` must not be `BLOCKED`, which answers `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409); as section 2.8 explains, on this endpoint that row is structurally shadowed by the PPAP gate. The design behind the run must belong to the named program, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Then the quantity gates: `piece_count` must be at least the program's `minimum_order_quantity`, inclusive at the boundary, otherwise `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_ORDER_QUANTITY` (HTTP 422); `piece_count` must not exceed the run's `produced_pieces` minus the pieces already carried by lots of this run whose `lot_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `PACKED` or `SHIPPED` — quarantined and returned lots do not consume run availability — otherwise `ERR_RUN_PIECES_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 422). A quote must be on file, that is `quoted_unit_price_cents` must be non-null, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_QUOTED` (HTTP 409). `piece_count` must not exceed the program's `open_commitment_pieces`, otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). And if the newest submission is `INTERIM_APPROVED`, the program's `allocated_pieces` plus `shipped_pieces` plus this `piece_count` must not exceed that submission's `interim_piece_cap`, otherwise `ERR_INTERIM_PIECE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

A lot that survives all of that opens in `lot_status` `ALLOCATED` with `packaging_method` null, and it snapshots the commercial terms at creation: `unit_price_cents` from the program's `quoted_unit_price_cents`, `tooling_amort_per_piece_cents` from the program's `quoted_tooling_amort_per_piece_cents`, and `extended_price_cents` as `piece_count` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`. `tooling_recovery_cents` starts at 0 and `shipped_at` and `quarantine_reason` start null. Snapshotting matters: a later re-quote cannot move the price of a lot already allocated. On the program, `open_commitment_pieces` decreases by `piece_count` and `allocated_pieces` increases by it, conserving `committed_pieces`, and `delivery_posture` is re-derived. Lots may be listed filtered by `run_id`, `program_id` and `lot_status`.

**Packing.** A `ProcessTechnician` packs an `ALLOCATED` lot with a stated `packaging_method`, and two independent rules apply in order. First, the method must be in the permitted set for the program's `application_class`: `MEDICAL_IMPLANT` and `MEDICAL_DEVICE` permit only `CLEANROOM_DOUBLE_BAG` and `INTERLEAVED_TUBE`; `AUTOMOTIVE_SAFETY` permits only `TANGLE_FREE_TRAY` and `INTERLEAVED_TUBE`; `AUTOMOTIVE_NON_SAFETY` permits those two plus `BAG_AND_TAG`; `INDUSTRIAL` permits all five. A method outside the set answers `ERR_PACKAGING_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). Second, independently of class, a design whose `tangle_risk_class` is `HIGH` may not be packed in `BULK_BOX` or `BAG_AND_TAG`, which answers `ERR_TANGLE_RISK_PACKAGING` (HTTP 422). The two rules can both bite — an `AUTOMOTIVE_NON_SAFETY` program carrying a high-tangle extension spring cannot use `BAG_AND_TAG` even though its class permits it, and there the class rule passes while the tangle rule rejects. Success records the method and moves the lot `ALLOCATED → PACKED`.

**Shipping.** A `ProgramManager` ships a `PACKED` lot; any other `lot_status` answers `ERR_LOT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The run must not be on `SPC_HOLD`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_ON_SPC_HOLD`. The program's `delivery_posture` must not be `BLOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED` — and this is the endpoint where that error is genuinely reachable, because a lot packed while the program was healthy can be presented after a resubmit dropped the posture back to `BLOCKED`. The design's newest tooling asset must have an `amortization_status` of `AMORTIZING` or `FULLY_AMORTIZED`, otherwise `ERR_TOOLING_NOT_AMORTIZING` (HTTP 409) — this is the gate that holds interim-approved lots in the warehouse. Shipping then posts the tooling recovery computed under the program's `amortization_method` as described in section 3.4, writes it to the lot's `tooling_recovery_cents`, adds it to the tool's `recovered_cents` and subtracts it from `unamortized_cents`, and takes the tool `AMORTIZING → FULLY_AMORTIZED` if that leaves `unamortized_cents` at zero. On the program, `allocated_pieces` decreases by `piece_count` and `shipped_pieces` increases by it — `open_commitment_pieces` is untouched, because the commitment was consumed at allocation. The lot becomes `SHIPPED` with `shipped_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `delivery_posture` is re-derived.

**Returns.** A returned lot reverses the shipment exactly. Only a `SHIPPED` lot may be returned. The stored `tooling_recovery_cents` is subtracted from the tool's `recovered_cents` and added back to `unamortized_cents` **verbatim**, using the historical posting amount rather than a recomputation, which is what keeps `original_cost_cents` conserved even when the tool's balance has moved since. The tool that receives the reversal is resolved afresh at return time as the design's newest tooling asset by `created_at`, not remembered from the shipment, and a lot whose recorded `tooling_recovery_cents` is 0 leaves the tooling row untouched altogether — neither its ledger figures nor its `updated_at` and `version` move. If the tool had reached `FULLY_AMORTIZED` and the reversal leaves `unamortized_cents` positive, its `amortization_status` returns to `AMORTIZING`. On the program, `shipped_pieces` decreases by `piece_count` and `open_commitment_pieces` increases by it, so returned pieces go back on the commitment rather than back into allocation. The lot becomes `RETURNED`, which is terminal, `shipped_at` is cleared to null, `tooling_recovery_cents` is left as the historical posting amount, and `delivery_posture` is re-derived.

**Quarantine and release.** A `QualityInspector` may quarantine any `ALLOCATED` or `PACKED` lot manually, supplying a `quarantine_reason`; the manual reason is `INSPECTOR_ACTION`, while `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE` is what the automatic cascades of sections 3.7, 3.8 and 3.9 stamp. The field is a required enum over both of those values and the endpoint records whichever was supplied without preferring one, so a manual hold stamped `SPC_HOLD_CASCADE` is accepted rather than refused. The lot becomes `QUARANTINED` and retains its `packaging_method` — and so does a lot swept up by an automatic cascade, which likewise leaves a `PACKED` lot holding the method it was packed with. The program's `quarantined_lot_count` increases by 1, `allocated_pieces` decreases by `piece_count` and `open_commitment_pieces` increases by the same amount — the pieces go back on the commitment — and `delivery_posture` is re-derived, which is what drops a healthy program to `AT_RISK`. Releasing runs the mirror image: the lot must be `QUARANTINED`, the run's `quality_status` must be `OPEN` or `DISPOSITIONED_ACCEPT` so a lot cannot be released while its run is still held — a run sitting on `SPC_HOLD` or already `SCRAPPED` answers `ERR_RUN_ON_SPC_HOLD` (HTTP 409), which section 2.8 ranks ahead of the commitment check — and `piece_count` must not exceed the program's live `open_commitment_pieces`, otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED`. That last check is a real constraint, not a formality: while the lot sat in quarantine its pieces were back on the commitment and may have been allocated to another lot, in which case the release fails. Success returns the lot to `ALLOCATED`, clears `quarantine_reason` back to null and clears `packaging_method` to null — **the lot must be packed again**, and the packaging rules are re-applied from scratch. The program's `quarantined_lot_count` decreases by 1, `allocated_pieces` increases by `piece_count`, `open_commitment_pieces` decreases by it, and `delivery_posture` is re-derived, returning to `ON_TRACK` only when the last quarantined lot has been released and no run of the program is on hold.

## 4. API Endpoints

Every endpoint below requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers and answers inside the envelope of section 2.2. Creation endpoints return HTTP 201 with the new resource; reads, lists and state-changing operations on an existing resource return HTTP 200 with the affected resource.
