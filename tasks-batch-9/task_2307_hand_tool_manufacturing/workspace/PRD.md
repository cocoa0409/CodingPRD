# Hand Tool Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hand tool manufacturer lives or dies by two promises: that a wrench will take its rated torque without yielding, and that if it ever does fail, the company will replace it. Both promises are expensive to keep and easy to break quietly. Forging, heat treatment, machining, plating and assembly each leave a metallurgical fingerprint that only destructive testing can read, and the specimens available for that testing are consumed the moment they are used. Meanwhile the lifetime warranty that differentiates the branded range from private-label work is a funded liability that can be drained by duplicate claims, by professional accounts gaming replacement allowances, and above all by counterfeit product circulating in secondary channels and arriving at the warranty desk as if it were genuine.

This document specifies the Hand Tool Manufacturing API, the system of record that binds the shop floor, the qualification laboratory, the commercial catalogue, the warranty desk and the brand-protection office into one auditable chain. The API is mounted under `/toolworks`. It governs the catalogue of finished SKUs across branded, private-label and professional channels; the production lots forged against those SKUs and the heat-treat campaigns and salt-spray exposures that qualify them; the industry-standard test batteries whose results certify or reject a lot; retail packaging configurations for display cartons, security packs and multipacks; and the lifetime warranty programme with its reserve ledger, claim intake, fraud controls and replacement fulfilment.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **nothing is asserted twice from different arithmetic** — each derived quantity has exactly one pinned evaluation order, and where two plausible orders would give different answers, this document names the one that is correct. Second, **every physical or monetary quantity is conserved**: destructive-test specimens, finished replacement units and reserve money each obey a stated identity that must hold after every mutation, including after a cascade has torn down half a lot's schedule. Engineers implementing this API should treat those identities as the acceptance criteria they will be measured against.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity, precision, concurrency and the order in which requests are refused. Everything here applies to every endpoint in §4 unless a specific rule says otherwise.

### 2.1. Users, Roles and the Segregation of Duties

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's operational capacity. Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ChannelManager`, `ProductionPlanner`, `QualityEngineer`, `WarrantyAdministrator`, `BrandProtectionAnalyst`, `Claimant` and `PlatformAdmin`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, or whose value is not one of those seven strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is well-formed but not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint it targets is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The single exception to the authentication requirement is the logical-clock capability described in §2.8, which may be called without headers; if it is called with a role header, that role must be `PlatformAdmin`.

The role model is one of **segregation of duties** rather than seniority. `ChannelManager` owns the commercial surface — SKU registration and amendment, list pricing, warranty tier assignment, supersession lineage, outside stock receipts and retail pack configuration. `ProductionPlanner` owns the shop floor — lot opening, stage advancement, furnace registration and availability, heat-treat campaign scheduling and completion, rework and release of certified lots into finished stock. `QualityEngineer` owns qualification — salt-spray chamber registration, test scheduling and specimen commitment, results adjudication, run voiding and lot certification. `WarrantyAdministrator` owns warranty administration — programme funding, claimant registration and blocking, claim adjudication, reserve charging, fulfilment and revocation. `BrandProtectionAnalyst` owns counterfeit detection and the fraud-side interventions a confirmed counterfeit implies. `PlatformAdmin` appears on every endpoint so that a single caller can compose an end-to-end chain, and it is the only role permitted to move the logical clock.

`PlatformAdmin` is emphatically **not** an override for the two duty separations that matter. The first is that certification is exclusively a `QualityEngineer` act while release into finished stock is exclusively a `ProductionPlanner` act, so no single operational role can both pass a lot and bank its units. The second is that the user who places a warranty claim on fraud hold may neither clear that hold nor approve the claim afterwards; that check compares `X-User-ID` against the identifier recorded on the claim and applies to `PlatformAdmin` identically to everyone else, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when violated. A `BrandProtectionAnalyst` may place a hold but may never clear one — clearing is reserved to `WarrantyAdministrator`.

Identity semantics differ by role. When `X-User-Role` is `Claimant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Claimant` row; ownership checks compare a claim's `claimant_id` against `X-User-ID`, so a claimant may file only for themselves, may add replacement lines only to their own claims, and may read only their own claims. A violation is `ERR_CLAIMANT_MISMATCH`, and this check is evaluated before the system looks up whether the referenced rows exist, so quoting another party's identifier returns the permission error even when that identifier resolves to nothing. When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelManager`, `ProductionPlanner`, `QualityEngineer`, `WarrantyAdministrator`, `BrandProtectionAnalyst` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a rule requires it (specifically as `held_by_user_id` on a warranty claim placed on fraud hold) but never compares it against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and List Filters

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Every list response echoes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. This is not merely a display convention: several cascades and every ledger recomputation described in §3 process their input rows in `created_at` ascending order, so creation order is the system's canonical sequencing.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept optional equality filters drawn from the attributes of the resource being listed. The SKU catalogue can be narrowed by `channel`, `tool_family`, `warranty_posture` or `supersedes_sku_id` — the last of these returns the successors of a given SKU rather than the SKU itself. Production lots can be filtered by `process_status`, `cert_status` or `disposition`, independently of one another. Test runs can be filtered by `chamber_id`, `test_type`, `status` or the boolean `mandatory`; heat-treat campaigns by `lot_id`, `furnace_id` or `status`; furnaces and chambers by `status` where they carry one. Packaging configurations accept `primary_sku_id`, `pack_type` and `status`. Claimants accept `status` and `claimant_type`. Warranty claims accept `claimant_id`, `sku_id`, `program_id` and `status`, which is what lets a claim be reached from any of its three parents. Counterfeit reports accept `status` and `evidence_grade`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and Nested Paths

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a recomputed hardness index, a refreshed reserve balance or a re-derived warranty posture. Two resources are exceptions because they are immutable once written: a pack member line and a claim replacement line carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them at all.

Child rows record their parent's identifier: a pack member line carries `pack_id`, a claim replacement line carries `claim_id`, a production lot carries `sku_id`, a heat-treat campaign carries both `lot_id` and `furnace_id`, a test run carries `lot_id` and optionally `chamber_id`, a counterfeit report carries `sku_id`, and a warranty claim carries `claimant_id`, `sku_id` and `program_id` at once.

For any path containing more than one resource identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first unresolvable one determines the error. A request for the lines of a non-existent claim returns `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; the members of a non-existent packaging configuration return `ERR_PACK_CONFIG_NOT_FOUND`; the test runs of a non-existent lot return `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`; and the production lots, counterfeit reports or pack memberships of a non-existent SKU return `ERR_TOOL_SKU_NOT_FOUND`. The same family of codes applies to single-resource paths: `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIMANT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` are each returned when the addressed row of that entity does not exist. Identifiers supplied in a request body are checked with the same code as their entity: an unresolvable `sku_id`, `primary_sku_id` or `supersedes_sku_id` gives `ERR_TOOL_SKU_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `lot_id` gives `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `furnace_id` gives `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `chamber_id` gives `ERR_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `claimant_id` gives `ERR_CLAIMANT_NOT_FOUND` and `program_id` gives `ERR_WARRANTY_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.5. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Four precisions are in use and they are not interchangeable.

Torque requirements are carried to **one decimal place** in newton-metres. Per-run test means are carried to **one decimal place** in the natural unit of the test (Rockwell C points, newton-metres, hours of salt-spray exposure, mechanism cycles, or millimetres of deviation). Lot hardness indices and signed result margins are carried to **two decimal places**. Money is held as **integer cents** throughout: there are no fractional cents anywhere in the API, and every monetary product is rounded to a whole cent at the point the specification says to round it, never earlier and never later. Brand-risk points, specimen counts, coupon counts, unit counts and fraud scores are plain integers.

Where a formula both rounds and sums, the order is pinned by the rule that owns the formula, and the two orders are not equivalent. Reserve charges and pack member extensions round **each line first and then sum the rounded lines exactly**; taking one product across all lines and rounding the sum is wrong and can differ by a cent. Where a formula both clamps and rounds, the clamp is applied to the unrounded value and the rounding is applied last. Time is handled at whole-minute UTC resolution: campaign and test windows must be whole-minute instants, and buffer arithmetic adds whole minutes.

### 2.6. Stale-Read Protection on Shared Parents

Two operations draw down a shared parent resource whose balance the caller must have observed before acting, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on that parent's `version`.

Releasing a production lot into finished stock requires the non-optional field `expected_sku_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the parent `ToolSku` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the SKU's current `version`, the release is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SKU_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Releasing a second, different lot against the same SKU is perfectly legitimate — finished-goods inventory simply accumulates — so no state precondition excludes it; the version check is what forces the writer to confirm it observed the unit totals it is adding to.

Approving a warranty claim requires the non-optional field `expected_program_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the claim's `WarrantyProgram` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the programme's current `version`, the approval is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Approving a second, different claim against the same programme is likewise legitimate product behaviour, so nothing else blocks it; the version check forces the administrator to have observed the reserve balance the approval draws down. Because a hold, a clear-hold, a fraud denial, another approval and a revocation all increment the programme's `version`, an administrator who read the programme before any of those events must re-read it before approving.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a stale-version check. Booking outside stock onto a SKU accepts an optional `expected_sku_version` for symmetry with release, but it is not gated on that value and never returns a stale-version error.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Many requests violate several rules at once, and exactly one error must come back. Checks are therefore arranged on one global ladder and the **first** violated rung determines the response. In ascending order the rungs are: authentication of the role header; role authorisation for the endpoint; claimant ownership; existence of every identifier named in the path and then in the body; request shape, meaning required fields present and correctly typed, enum values drawn from the declared members, integers within their declared ranges, pagination bounds, window sanity and the reading-array length — a missing required field, a wrongly typed value, a number outside its declared range or an undeclared enum member is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while the pagination, window and reading-length faults have their own identifiers; state preconditions on the entity the request addresses; the stale-version check of §2.6; cross-entity state dependencies such as a furnace being available or a member SKU being certified; metallurgical and qualification safety gates; temporal window and buffer rules; capacity rules over pools, coupons and stock; fiscal rules over reserve money; and finally integrity rules covering ledgers, uniqueness, lineage and segregation of duties.

Three consequences of that ordering are worth stating outright, because a natural reading of the workflow would get them backwards. A malformed reading array is a request-shape fault and is therefore reported **before** the test run's own state is examined, so submitting the wrong number of readings to a run that has already been resolved returns `ERR_READING_COUNT_MISMATCH` rather than a state error. The stale-version check sits **after** the addressed entity's state precondition but **before** every business rule, so a claim in the wrong status reports its state error even when the supplied programme version is also stale, while a claim in the right status with a stale version reports the version error even when its reserve charge also exceeds the balance. And segregation of duties sits at the very bottom of the ladder, so an approval that violates duty separation and also lacks replacement lines reports the missing lines first. Where two rules in the same band both apply, the more specific orderings are stated with the workflows in §3.

### 2.8. The Logical Shop Clock

The system does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical UTC clock which reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until it is moved, and which never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may advance or rewind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the capability returns an empty data object rather than a resource. Two behaviours depend on it: the rolling window of the intake fraud score, which looks back a fixed number of days from the clock's current reading, and the certification timestamp stamped onto a lot when its dossier closes. Windows submitted on campaigns and test runs are ordinary data and are not compared against the clock.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: Specimens, Units and Reserve Money

Three quantities in this system are physically or financially conserved, and each has an identity that must hold after **every** mutation, including after an automatic cascade.

The first is destructive-test material. A lot is opened with a fixed `specimen_pool_total` that is immutable thereafter, and at every instant `specimen_pool_total` equals `specimens_available` plus `specimens_committed` plus `specimens_consumed`. Scheduling a test moves specimens from available to committed; recording a result moves them from committed to consumed; voiding a scheduled run moves them from committed back to available, which makes scheduling and voiding an exact forward-and-compensating pair. Voided runs belong to none of the three buckets.

The second is physical finished units. A SKU's `units_received_total` only ever increases — by a released lot's `unit_count`, or by the `units_received` of an outside stock receipt — and at every instant it equals `units_on_hand` plus `units_reserved` plus `units_shipped`. Approving a claim moves units from on-hand to reserved, fulfilling it moves them from reserved to shipped, and revoking returns them to on-hand from whichever bucket they were in.

The third is reserve money. A warranty programme is funded once with `reserve_funded_cents`, immutable thereafter, and at every instant `reserve_funded_cents` equals `reserve_balance_cents` plus the sum of the `reserve_charge_cents` of the programme's claims that are currently in `APPROVED` or `FULFILLED` status. Because a revoked claim keeps its frozen charge on the row for audit while leaving the charged set, an approve-then-revoke pair returns the balance to exactly its pre-approval value with zero drift.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Some transitions in this system are not requested by a client at all: the server performs them because a rule it enforces has become true. Two large cascades exist — a mandatory test failure tearing down a lot's open schedule, and a confirmed counterfeit degrading a SKU's warranty posture and holding every open claim on it — and both are specified link by link in §3. Three properties are global. First, cascades run to completion within the request that triggered them, so the response to that request already reflects every consequence. Second, whenever a cascade touches a set of rows, it processes them in `created_at` ascending order, which makes the outcome deterministic. Third, every row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly as if the change had been requested directly, including parent rows whose derived aggregates were merely recomputed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Product Catalogue and Its Supersession Lineage

Everything downstream hangs off a **ToolSku**, the catalogue entry for one finished tool in one commercial channel. A `ChannelManager` registers one by supplying a `sku_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across the catalogue, a `tool_family` of `WRENCH`, `SOCKET` or `PLIER`, a `channel` of `BRANDED`, `PRIVATE_LABEL` or `PROFESSIONAL`, a `nominal_size_mm` across-flats or jaw size expressed as a whole number of millimetres between 1 and 80 inclusive, an `alloy_grade` of `CR_V`, `CR_MO` or `S2`, a `list_price_cents` of at least one cent, and a `warranty_tier` of `LIFETIME`, `LIMITED_5Y` or `NONE`. Reusing a `sku_code` — or any other globally unique business code, namely `lot_code`, `furnace_code`, `chamber_code`, `pack_code`, `program_code` and a claimant's `email` — is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Registration derives the SKU's **proof-torque requirement**, the industry acceptance bar that every torque test on the product will be judged against, and stores it as `proof_torque_required_nm`. It is computed in a pinned order of five steps. Step one takes a raw figure: a base torque per millimetre of nominal size — 4.2 newton-metres per millimetre for a `WRENCH`, 6.5 for a `SOCKET`, 2.8 for a `PLIER` — multiplied by `nominal_size_mm`, multiplied by an alloy factor of 1.00 for `CR_V`, 1.15 for `CR_MO` or 1.30 for `S2`. Step two scales that raw figure by the channel factor: 1.10 for `BRANDED`, 1.00 for `PRIVATE_LABEL`, 1.25 for `PROFESSIONAL`. Step three adds a size-tier term, computed as a per-millimetre adder multiplied by `nominal_size_mm`, where the adder is zero for a size of 10 millimetres or less, 1.5 for a size greater than 10 and up to and including 19, and 3.0 for a size greater than 19. Step four clamps the result into the industry band, raising anything below 12.0 newton-metres up to 12.0 and cutting anything above 900.0 newton-metres down to 900.0. Step five rounds half up to one decimal place. The order matters: applying the clamp before the additive size-tier term, or rounding before the clamp, produces a different number. Only the SKU's own `tool_family`, `nominal_size_mm`, `alloy_grade` and `channel` participate — no lot and no test data enters this figure.

Private-label and professional variants are normally respins of an existing catalogue entry, and the optional `supersedes_sku_id` records that ancestry; it defaults to null. When it is supplied and non-null it must resolve to an existing SKU, that ancestor must share the new SKU's `tool_family` — otherwise `ERR_LINEAGE_FAMILY_MISMATCH` — and the resulting chain is walked to derive `lineage_depth`, the number of hops from this SKU to an ancestor whose own `supersedes_sku_id` is null. The walk starts at the candidate ancestor and follows each row's `supersedes_sku_id`. If the walk ever revisits a SKU it has already seen, including the SKU being registered or amended itself, the request is refused with `ERR_SKU_LINEAGE_CYCLE`; the cycle test runs first, so a SKU that names itself as its own predecessor is reported as a cycle and never as a depth problem. If the hop count would exceed three, the request is refused with `ERR_LINEAGE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. A SKU with no ancestor has a `lineage_depth` of 0. Both lineage checks precede the duplicate-code check, so a request that is both a cycle and a code reuse reports the cycle.

A `ChannelManager` may later amend a SKU's commercial attributes, supplying any of `list_price_cents`, `warranty_tier`, `nominal_size_mm`, `alloy_grade` or `supersedes_sku_id`. Amendment re-runs both derivations: `proof_torque_required_nm` is recomputed from the amended attributes and `lineage_depth` is re-walked with the same cycle-before-depth ordering, and the SKU's `version` increments. Amending a SKU does **not** retro-change any lot already opened against it, because each lot pins its own copy of the acceptance bar at the moment it is opened.

Four unit counters and four brand-protection fields round out the catalogue row, and all eight are server-derived. `units_received_total`, `units_on_hand`, `units_reserved` and `units_shipped` all start at zero and obey the conservation identity of §2.9; the last three are recomputed from claim status whenever a claim moves, while the first only grows. Finished units can enter stock from outside the shop — customer returns, an inter-plant transfer, a contract packer — and a `ChannelManager` books them by supplying a `units_received` count, which is added to `units_received_total` before `units_on_hand` is recomputed and the SKU's `version` increments. `brand_risk_score` and `confirmed_counterfeit_count` both start at zero, `warranty_posture` starts at `OPEN`, and all three are maintained by the brand-protection machinery of §3.9.

### 3.2. Opening Lots and Booking the Heat-Treat Furnaces

A **ProductionLot** is a traveller through the shop. A `ProductionPlanner` opens one against a SKU by supplying that `sku_id`, a `lot_code` unique across all lots, a `unit_count` of at least one finished unit, and a `specimen_pool_total` of at least one — the destructive-test specimens drawn from the lot, a figure that is immutable once the lot exists. The new lot begins with `process_status` `FORGED`, `cert_status` `DRAFT` and `disposition` `IN_PROCESS`; `specimens_available` equals `specimen_pool_total` while `specimens_committed` and `specimens_consumed` are zero; `rework_cycle_count` is zero; `hardness_index_sample` and `hardness_index_pooled` are both null; `certified_at` is null; and `proof_torque_required_nm` is copied from the parent SKU so that later catalogue amendments cannot move this lot's acceptance bar.

The lot carries **three independent status dimensions** and confusing them is the single most common way to get this domain wrong. `process_status` is the routing dimension and runs `FORGED` → `HEAT_TREATED` → `MACHINED` → `PLATED` → `ASSEMBLED`. `cert_status` is the certification-dossier dimension and runs `DRAFT` → `IN_TEST` → `CERTIFIED`, with a rejection-and-revision cycle through `REJECTED` back to `DRAFT`. `disposition` is the material dimension and holds `IN_PROCESS`, `RELEASED` or `QUARANTINED`. The dimensions evolve independently: a lot may sit at `ASSEMBLED` while its dossier is still `DRAFT`, or be `IN_TEST` while the metal has only reached `PLATED`. The one coupling in the forward direction is that a rejection quarantines the lot, and the one coupling in the reverse direction is that rework regresses the routing dimension, both described in §3.5.

Advancing the routing dimension is a `ProductionPlanner` act that names the destination stage as `target_process_status`. The requested stage must be the immediate successor of the lot's current stage — `HEAT_TREATED` only from `FORGED`, `MACHINED` only from `HEAT_TREATED`, `PLATED` only from `MACHINED`, `ASSEMBLED` only from `PLATED` — and anything else is `ERR_LOT_PROCESS_STATE_INVALID`. Before that, the dossier dimension must be `DRAFT` or `IN_TEST`, since a lot that is already `CERTIFIED` or currently `REJECTED` cannot be advanced at all: that failure is `ERR_LOT_CERT_STATE_INVALID` and it is evaluated **first**, ahead of the stage check. After the stage check comes the quarantine check: a lot whose `disposition` is `QUARANTINED` accepts no advancement, and that is `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. Advancement itself only moves `process_status` and bumps `updated_at` and `version`; it never touches the dossier dimension.

The step into `HEAT_TREATED` carries one extra gate, evaluated after the quarantine check: at least one heat-treat campaign on this lot must have reached `COMPLETED` status. A campaign that is still `SCHEDULED`, or one that was `RELEASED` by a rejection cascade, does not satisfy the gate, and the refusal is `ERR_HEAT_TREAT_NOT_COMPLETED`.

Austenitising happens in a **HeatTreatFurnace**, registered by a `ProductionPlanner` with a unique `furnace_code`, a `max_charge_kg` basket limit of at least one kilogram, and a non-empty `alloy_grades_supported` array whose members are drawn from `CR_V`, `CR_MO` and `S2`. A furnace starts `AVAILABLE` and may be moved between `AVAILABLE` and `MAINTENANCE` by supplying the desired `status`. The requested status must differ from the current one, otherwise `ERR_FURNACE_STATUS_UNCHANGED`. Taking a furnace into `MAINTENANCE` prevents new campaigns from being booked on it but leaves campaigns already scheduled entirely untouched; each status move increments the furnace's `version`.

A **FurnaceCampaign** occupies one furnace for one lot over one interval. Scheduling it requires the `lot_id` of the lot being charged, the `furnace_id` of the furnace, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the occupation, and a `charge_kg` basket weight of at least one kilogram. The interval is half-open: `window_start` is inclusive, `window_end` is exclusive, `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, and both must be whole-minute UTC instants — a violation of any of those is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, reported before any state or business rule. The lot's dossier must be `DRAFT` or `IN_TEST` and the lot must not be quarantined, giving `ERR_LOT_CERT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` respectively in that order. The furnace must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_FURNACE_UNAVAILABLE`.

Then come three physical checks whose order is pinned and deliberately not the order an engineer would guess. **Qualification comes first**: the furnace must list the alloy grade of the lot's SKU among its `alloy_grades_supported`, and if it does not, the answer is `ERR_ALLOY_NOT_SUPPORTED` even when the requested window also collides and the basket is also overweight. **The window comes second.** A furnace has exclusive occupancy — capacity one — and each campaign's interval is extended by a trailing ramp-and-cool-down buffer of 45 minutes, recorded on the row as `buffered_end`, which is simply `window_end` plus those 45 minutes and is the instant the furnace becomes bookable again. Only campaigns on the same furnace whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` block; campaigns that a cascade has moved to `RELEASED` never block. A collision exists when the requested `window_start` falls strictly before an existing campaign's buffered end **and** that existing campaign's `window_start` falls strictly before the requested `window_end` plus 45 minutes; both comparisons are strict, so a campaign starting exactly at a prior campaign's `buffered_end` is accepted while one starting a single minute earlier is refused. A collision is `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **The charge comes third**: `charge_kg` may not exceed the furnace's `max_charge_kg`, and exceeding it is `ERR_FURNACE_CHARGE_EXCEEDED`. A campaign is created `SCHEDULED` with `soak_minutes` null.

Completing a campaign is the planner's record that the charge actually soaked, and it supplies the achieved `soak_minutes`. The campaign must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`, and the reported soak must be at least a full hour — 60 minutes — otherwise `ERR_SOAK_TIME_TOO_SHORT`. Completion stores `soak_minutes`, moves the campaign `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and increments its `version`. Note that completion does **not** free the furnace: a `COMPLETED` campaign still occupies its buffered window and still blocks overlapping bookings, because the basket and fixtures remain in place until the lot moves on.

### 3.3. Scheduling Qualification Tests Against a Finite Specimen Pool

A **TestRun** is one industry test executed on specimens drawn from a lot, scheduled by a `QualityEngineer`. It names the `lot_id` under test, a `test_type` of `HARDNESS`, `PROOF_TORQUE`, `DIMENSIONAL`, `SALT_SPRAY` or `MECHANISM_CYCLE`, a `specimen_count` of at least one, and a half-open `window_start` and `window_end` obeying exactly the same whole-minute, strictly-increasing rules as a campaign window and failing with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Corrosion testing additionally names a `chamber_id`: for a `SALT_SPRAY` run the field is required and its absence is `ERR_CHAMBER_REQUIRED`, while for every other test type it must be absent or null and supplying it is `ERR_CHAMBER_NOT_APPLICABLE`. When present it must resolve to a registered cabinet, and that existence check precedes both applicability rules.

Corrosion cabinets are **SaltSprayChamber** rows registered by a `QualityEngineer` with a unique `chamber_code` and a `coupon_capacity` of at least one — the number of corrosion coupons the cabinet can expose at a single instant.

Scheduling a run is gated in a fixed sequence. The lot's dossier must be `DRAFT` or `IN_TEST` (`ERR_LOT_CERT_STATE_INVALID`) and the lot must not be quarantined (`ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`). Then comes the **process-stage gate**, because a test performed on metal that has not reached the right stage measures nothing: `DIMENSIONAL` and `PROOF_TORQUE` runs require the lot to have reached at least `MACHINED`, `SALT_SPRAY` runs require at least `PLATED`, and `MECHANISM_CYCLE` runs require `ASSEMBLED`. A `HARDNESS` run carries no stage requirement. Scheduling too early is `ERR_TEST_STAGE_TOO_EARLY`. Then the **temporal gate**, which applies to exactly those test types that require heat treatment to have happened — `DIMENSIONAL`, `PROOF_TORQUE`, `SALT_SPRAY` and `MECHANISM_CYCLE`, but not `HARDNESS`: the run's `window_start` may not fall before the earliest `window_end` among the lot's `COMPLETED` campaigns, and a window that starts earlier is `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_BEFORE_HEAT_TREAT`.

Then the two capacity gates. First the specimen pool: `specimen_count` may not exceed the lot's current `specimens_available`, and exact equality is accepted and drives `specimens_available` to zero. Asking for more is `ERR_SPECIMENS_EXHAUSTED`. Second, for `SALT_SPRAY` runs only, the cabinet's coupon capacity. Unlike a furnace, a chamber is **additive**: many runs may share it as long as the coupons inside it never exceed `coupon_capacity` at any instant, and there is no buffer of any kind. The runs that occupy a chamber are those on that `chamber_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED` and whose half-open interval genuinely intersects the requested one, meaning the existing run starts strictly before the requested `window_end` and the requested run starts strictly before the existing `window_end`; a run beginning exactly when another ends never conflicts, and `VOID` runs never occupy anything. Occupancy is probed at the requested `window_start` and at every occupying run's start instant that is later than the requested `window_start`; at each probe instant the load is the requested `specimen_count` plus the `specimen_count` of every occupying run whose own interval covers that instant, treating each interval as including its start and excluding its end. If the greatest of those loads is strictly greater than `coupon_capacity`, the request is refused with `ERR_CHAMBER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a peak load exactly equal to `coupon_capacity` is accepted.

A successfully scheduled run is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, and its `specimen_count` moves from the lot's `specimens_available` into `specimens_committed`, which increments the lot's `version` as well as creating the run. Two values are pinned on the run at this moment. `mandatory` is set true when the run's `test_type` belongs to the mandatory battery of its SKU's `tool_family`: for a `WRENCH` and for a `SOCKET` the battery is `HARDNESS`, `PROOF_TORQUE`, `DIMENSIONAL` and `SALT_SPRAY`; for a `PLIER` it is `HARDNESS`, `DIMENSIONAL`, `SALT_SPRAY` and `MECHANISM_CYCLE`. Note the asymmetry: proof torque is mandatory for wrenches and sockets but not for pliers, while mechanism cycling is mandatory for pliers alone. `requirement_value` is pinned from the run's `test_type`, the lot's own `proof_torque_required_nm` and the SKU's `alloy_grade` and `channel`, so that a later catalogue amendment cannot move the bar an already-scheduled run will be judged against. `readings`, `mean_reading`, `governing_statistic`, `result_margin_pct` and `void_reason` are all null until the run resolves.

Scheduling the first test run on a lot whose dossier is still `DRAFT` **opens the dossier**: the system moves `cert_status` `DRAFT → IN_TEST` in the same request and increments the lot's `version`. Nothing else opens the dossier — in particular, advancing the routing dimension does not, which is precisely why a lot can reach `ASSEMBLED` while still `DRAFT`.

A scheduled run that will not be executed is voided by a `QualityEngineer`, who supplies a `void_reason` in free text. The run must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_TEST_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. Voiding sets `status` to `VOID`, stores the supplied `void_reason`, returns the run's `specimen_count` from `specimens_committed` back to `specimens_available` — the exact compensating action of scheduling — releases whatever chamber coupons the run was holding so that later corrosion runs see the capacity again, and increments both the run's and the lot's `version`.

### 3.4. Adjudicating Readings and the Consequences of a Failure

Recording a result is a `QualityEngineer` act that supplies `readings`, a non-empty array of per-specimen measurements. Its length must equal the run's `specimen_count`, and a mismatch is `ERR_READING_COUNT_MISMATCH` — evaluated as a request-shape fault, and therefore reported ahead of the run's own state. The run must be `SCHEDULED`, and results on a run that has already resolved are `ERR_TEST_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.

Adjudication first computes `mean_reading`, the arithmetic mean of the supplied readings rounded half up to one decimal place. It then selects a single `governing_statistic` and compares it against the run's `requirement_value`, and the choice of statistic and the direction of the comparison both depend on `test_type`. For `HARDNESS` the governing statistic is `mean_reading` itself and the requirement is the alloy minimum in Rockwell C — 47.0 for `CR_V`, 50.0 for `CR_MO`, 55.0 for `S2` — and the run passes when the statistic is at least the requirement. For `PROOF_TORQUE` the governing statistic is the **smallest** reading, because the weakest specimen defines the product, and the requirement is the lot's pinned `proof_torque_required_nm`, again pass-when-at-least. For `SALT_SPRAY` the governing statistic is the smallest reading in hours of exposure and the requirement is the channel's corrosion standard — 96 hours for `BRANDED`, 48 hours for `PRIVATE_LABEL`, 168 hours for `PROFESSIONAL` — pass-when-at-least. For `MECHANISM_CYCLE` the governing statistic is the smallest reading in cycles and the requirement is five thousand cycles, pass-when-at-least. For `DIMENSIONAL` the direction inverts: the governing statistic is the largest absolute value among the readings, treating a deviation of minus 0.12 millimetres exactly as severe as plus 0.12, the requirement is a tolerance of 0.15 millimetres, and the run passes when the statistic is at most the requirement. Every comparison is inclusive, so a hardness mean landing exactly on the alloy minimum passes, and a worst deviation landing exactly on the tolerance passes.

The run also records `result_margin_pct`, a signed percentage of headroom rounded half up to two decimal places. For the four pass-when-at-least types it is the governing statistic minus the requirement, divided by the requirement, multiplied by one hundred. For `DIMENSIONAL` the subtraction reverses — the requirement minus the governing statistic, divided by the requirement, multiplied by one hundred — so that in every case a positive margin means comfort and a negative margin means the run failed. A result exactly on the boundary yields a margin of 0.00.

On a **pass**, the run moves `SCHEDULED → PASSED`, and `readings`, `mean_reading`, `governing_statistic`, `requirement_value` and `result_margin_pct` are all stored. The run's `specimen_count` moves out of the lot's `specimens_committed` and into `specimens_consumed` — destroyed material does not come back. If, and only if, the resolved run was a `HARDNESS` run, both of the lot's hardness indices are recomputed. Both the run's and the lot's `version` increment.

The lot's two hardness indices are computed from the same input set — every `HARDNESS` run on the lot whose status is `PASSED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, with `SCHEDULED`, `FAILED` and `VOID` runs excluded from both — but they aggregate it differently, and the difference is not cosmetic. `hardness_index_sample` is the **provisional in-process reading**: each qualifying run's mean is rounded half up to one decimal place first, and then those per-run means are averaged with equal weight and the average rounded half up to two decimal places, so a single-specimen run counts exactly as much as a nine-specimen run. `hardness_index_pooled` is the **certification reading**: every raw reading from every qualifying run is pooled in `created_at` order, one mean is taken over the whole pool with no intermediate rounding, and that mean is rounded half up to two decimal places, so each run's influence is proportional to its `specimen_count`. Both are stored on every recompute and both are visible on the lot, but only the pooled figure is allowed to certify.

On a **fail**, the run moves `SCHEDULED → FAILED`, and `readings` and every derived statistic are stored exactly as on the passing branch. The run's `specimen_count` likewise moves from `specimens_committed` to `specimens_consumed`. Two things then differ from the passing branch. The hardness indices are **not** recomputed, even for a `HARDNESS` run, because a failed run never enters either index's input set. And if the failed run was `mandatory` and the lot's dossier is `IN_TEST`, the failure **rejects the whole lot**, firing a three-link cascade in the same request.

Link one moves the lot's dossier `IN_TEST → REJECTED`. Link two sets the lot's `disposition` to `QUARANTINED`, which immediately closes the lot to further test scheduling, campaign booking, advancement, certification and release. Link three tears down the lot's open schedule in two parts, each processing its rows in `created_at` ascending order: every heat-treat campaign on the lot still in `SCHEDULED` status becomes `RELEASED`, freeing that furnace's buffered window for other work, and every **other** test run on the lot still in `SCHEDULED` status becomes `VOID` with `void_reason` set to the literal string `CASCADE_LOT_REJECTED`, returning each run's specimens from `specimens_committed` to `specimens_available`. The specimen identity of §2.9 holds throughout: the failed run's own specimens are consumed, the cascaded runs' specimens are returned, and the pool total is unchanged. The lot's `version` increments for the rejection, as does the `version` of every campaign and run the cascade touched. A failed run that is **not** mandatory, or a mandatory failure on a lot whose dossier is not `IN_TEST`, resolves the run alone and fires no cascade.

### 3.5. Certification, Rework and Release into Finished Stock

Certification closes the dossier and is exclusively a `QualityEngineer` act, taking no payload at all. Five gates apply, in this order. The dossier must be `IN_TEST` — a lot still `DRAFT`, already `CERTIFIED` or currently `REJECTED` gives `ERR_LOT_CERT_STATE_INVALID`. The routing dimension must have reached `ASSEMBLED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_PROCESS_STATE_INVALID`. The lot must not be quarantined, otherwise `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`; note that because the dossier check comes first, a rejected-and-quarantined lot reports the dossier error rather than the quarantine error. Next, every test type in the mandatory battery for the SKU's `tool_family` must have at least one `PASSED` run on this lot, and a gap is `ERR_MANDATORY_TEST_MISSING`. Next, no run on the lot may be in `FAILED` status at all, and the presence of one is `ERR_FAILED_TEST_PRESENT`. Finally the metallurgical margin: `hardness_index_pooled` must be at least the alloy minimum for the SKU's `alloy_grade` — 47.0, 50.0 or 55.0 Rockwell C points for `CR_V`, `CR_MO` and `S2` respectively — plus a channel margin of 1.0 point for `BRANDED`, 0.0 points for `PRIVATE_LABEL` and 2.5 points for `PROFESSIONAL`. The comparison is inclusive, so a pooled index landing exactly on the adjusted minimum certifies; falling short is `ERR_HARDNESS_MARGIN_NOT_MET`. A professional-channel `S2` lot therefore needs a pooled index of at least 57.5, while the same metal sold private-label needs only 55.0.

Certification recomputes and freezes both hardness indices, moves `cert_status` to `CERTIFIED`, stamps `certified_at` with the current reading of the logical clock, and increments the lot's `version`. `CERTIFIED` is terminal for the dossier dimension: there is no transition out of it.

**Rework** is the revision path for a rejected lot and is a `ProductionPlanner` act carrying a `rework_note`. The dossier must be `REJECTED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_CERT_STATE_INVALID`, and `rework_cycle_count` must still be below the rework limit of two completed cycles, otherwise `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — a lot that has already been reworked twice is scrap. Rework touches all three dimensions at once. The dossier returns `REJECTED → DRAFT`. The `disposition` returns `QUARANTINED → IN_PROCESS`, lifting the quarantine. The routing dimension regresses to `MACHINED`: a lot sitting at `PLATED` when it was rejected goes `PLATED → MACHINED` to be re-machined and re-plated, and a lot that had reached `ASSEMBLED` goes `ASSEMBLED → MACHINED`, meaning the assembly is torn down and the parts re-machined and re-plated. Both hardness indices are reset to null, discarding the dossier's metallurgical history, and `rework_cycle_count` increments. The lot's `version` increments once for the whole operation. Because the dossier is back at `DRAFT`, scheduling the next test run re-opens it into `IN_TEST`, and the revision cycle `DRAFT → IN_TEST → REJECTED → DRAFT` may be traversed until the rework limit bites.

**Release** banks the lot's units and is exclusively a `ProductionPlanner` act, deliberately separated from certification. It carries `expected_sku_version` and nothing else. Its gates run in this order: the lot must not be `QUARANTINED` (`ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`); its `disposition` must be `IN_PROCESS`, so a lot already `RELEASED` cannot be released twice (`ERR_LOT_ALREADY_DISPOSITIONED`); the supplied `expected_sku_version` must equal the parent SKU's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_SKU_VERSION`, HTTP 412); and only then, the dossier must be `CERTIFIED` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_CERTIFIED`). That ordering is worth internalising: an uncertified lot released with a stale version reports the stale version, not the missing certification. Release sets `disposition` to `RELEASED`, adds the lot's `unit_count` to the SKU's `units_received_total`, recomputes `units_on_hand` from the unit ledger, and increments both the SKU's `version` and the lot's `version`.

### 3.6. Retail Packaging and Channel Differentiation

A **PackConfiguration** is the artwork and bill of materials for one retail presentation, opened by a `ChannelManager` with a `pack_code` unique across all packs, a `pack_type` of `RETAIL_DISPLAY`, `SECURITY` or `MULTIPACK`, and a `primary_sku_id` naming the hero SKU the pack is built around. It starts in `DRAFT` with `member_subtotal_cents`, `security_surcharge_cents`, `pack_total_cents` and `distinct_member_count` all zero.

Lines are added to a `DRAFT` pack one at a time, each naming a `sku_id` and a `quantity` of at least one unit of that SKU per pack. The pack must be `DRAFT` to accept a line, otherwise `ERR_PACK_STATE_INVALID`. A SKU may appear at most once among a pack's members, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PACK_MEMBER`. All members must share a single commercial channel — a `PROFESSIONAL` tool may not be packed alongside a `BRANDED` or `PRIVATE_LABEL` one — and a member whose `channel` differs from the channel already established for the pack is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_MIX_FORBIDDEN`. Each accepted line stores `line_extended_cents`, the SKU's `list_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity` multiplied by the pack-type discount, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The discount is 1.00 for `RETAIL_DISPLAY` and for `SECURITY` — those formats are sold at list — and 0.88 for `MULTIPACK`, the twelve-percent volume concession that makes a multipack worth buying. Adding a line refreshes `member_subtotal_cents` and `distinct_member_count` on the pack and increments the pack's `version`.

Approving a pack for retail release is where the pack is priced and where the commercial gates bite, and it takes no payload. Four checks run in order. The pack must be `DRAFT` (`ERR_PACK_STATE_INVALID`). Every member SKU must have at least one production lot whose `cert_status` is `CERTIFIED`, since untested product cannot go on a shelf; a member without one is `ERR_MEMBER_SKU_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and the check is vacuously satisfied by a pack with no members at all. No member SKU may have a `warranty_posture` of `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_SKU_RESTRICTED`; a merely `RESTRICTED` posture does not block packaging. Finally the shape rules for the pack's own `pack_type`: a `RETAIL_DISPLAY` pack must have between 1 and 12 members inclusive; a `SECURITY` pack must have between 1 and 3 members inclusive **and** must include its own `primary_sku_id` among those members; a `MULTIPACK` must have at least 2 members and a total `quantity` across all members of at least 3. A violation is `ERR_PACK_CONFIG_INVALID`.

Approval then prices the pack in three pinned steps, and the order exists to break an apparent circularity between the surcharge and the total. Step one sums the members' `line_extended_cents`, taken in `created_at` ascending order and each priced from its SKU's current `list_price_cents`, into `member_subtotal_cents`, and counts the members into `distinct_member_count`. Step two derives `security_surcharge_cents`, the anti-theft packaging charge, and it is keyed on the **pre-surcharge** subtotal: for any pack whose `pack_type` is not `SECURITY` the surcharge is zero; for a `SECURITY` pack it is a flat 350 cents when `member_subtotal_cents` is 5,000 cents or less, a flat 700 cents when the subtotal is 20,000 cents or less, and otherwise four percent of `member_subtotal_cents` rounded half up to a whole cent. Step three sets `pack_total_cents` to `member_subtotal_cents` plus `security_surcharge_cents`. Keying the tier on the finished total instead of the subtotal is the classic error and gives a different answer for any pack whose subtotal sits just below a break. Approval moves the pack `DRAFT → APPROVED` and increments its `version`.

An approved pack is withdrawn from retail by a `ChannelManager` or a `BrandProtectionAnalyst` supplying a `withdrawal_reason`; the pack must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PACK_STATE_INVALID`, and withdrawal moves it `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN` and increments its `version`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. The same `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN` move can also be made by the system, without any client request, when a member SKU's brand posture degrades to `SUSPENDED`; that path is described in §3.9.

### 3.7. Warranty Intake: Programmes, Claimants and Replacement Lines

A **WarrantyProgram** is a funded pot of replacement money, stood up by a `WarrantyAdministrator` with a `program_code` unique across programmes, a `reserve_funded_cents` amount of zero or more that is immutable once the programme exists, and a `fraud_score_threshold` between 1 and 100 inclusive — the adjudication score at or above which a claim cannot be approved and a new claim is held on arrival. `reserve_balance_cents` starts equal to `reserve_funded_cents` and `open_hold_count` starts at zero.

A **Claimant** is the party who owns the failed tool, registered with an `email` unique across claimants and a `claimant_type` of `CONSUMER` or `PRO_ACCOUNT`. A claimant starts `ACTIVE` with `fraud_risk_score_intake`, `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` and `lifetime_claim_count` all zero. A `WarrantyAdministrator` may block a claimant after confirmed abuse by supplying a `block_reason`; the claimant must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIMANT_STATE_INVALID`, and blocking moves the claimant `ACTIVE → BLOCKED`, leaves every existing claim in place, and increments the claimant's `version`. `BLOCKED` is terminal, and a blocked claimant may not be named on a new claim: that refusal is `ERR_CLAIMANT_BLOCKED`.

Filing a **WarrantyClaim** requires a `claimant_id`, the `sku_id` of the failed tool, the `program_id` of the reserve that will fund the replacement, the `serial_code` stamped on the returned tool, and a `claim_reason` of `BREAKAGE`, `MECHANISM_FAILURE` or `CORROSION`. Either a `WarrantyAdministrator` or the claimant themselves may file; when the caller acts as a `Claimant`, `claimant_id` must equal their own `X-User-ID` or the request is `ERR_CLAIMANT_MISMATCH`. A SKU whose `warranty_tier` is `NONE` carries no coverage and cannot be claimed against: `ERR_WARRANTY_TIER_NOT_COVERED`. And a claimant may not hold two live claims on the same tool: if they already have a claim in `SUBMITTED`, `FRAUD_HOLD` or `APPROVED` status on the same `sku_id` and `serial_code` pair, the new filing is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_CLAIM`, while claims that ended in `DENIED`, `DENIED_FRAUD`, `FULFILLED` or `REVOKED` no longer block a refiling.

Intake scores the claimant. The **fraud score** is a weighted count computed over a set of the claimant's claims, and it exists in two variants which select different sets. The **intake variant** takes the claimant's claims created within the trailing 365 days measured back from the logical clock's current reading, in any status other than `REVOKED` — that is, `SUBMITTED`, `FRAUD_HOLD`, `APPROVED`, `FULFILLED`, `DENIED` or `DENIED_FRAUD` — together with the claim now being created. Over that set: start from the number of claims in it, subtract an allowance of 2 for a `PRO_ACCOUNT` claimant or 0 for a `CONSUMER`, and multiply by 9 points per claim. Then add 18 points for every duplicate serial, counted by grouping the set by `serial_code` and summing one less than the size of each group, so three claims quoting one serial contribute two duplicates and therefore 36 points. The intake variant applies **no** counterfeit term at all. Clamp the result to at least 0 and at most 100. That figure is written to the claimant's `fraud_risk_score_intake` and frozen onto the new claim as `fraud_score_at_intake`, and `lifetime_claim_count` — the count of the claimant's claims in any status — increments.

The claim is then persisted in one of two states. If `fraud_score_at_intake` is below the programme's `fraud_score_threshold`, the claim is `SUBMITTED`. If it is at or above the threshold, the claim is persisted **directly** in `FRAUD_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to the literal string `INTAKE_FRAUD_SCORE` and `held_by_user_id` left null, and the programme's `open_hold_count` and `version` both increment. Either way the claim's `line_count`, `replacement_unit_count` and `reserve_charge_cents` all start at zero, and `fraud_cleared` starts false with `denial_code`, `shipment_ref` and `revocation_reason` all null.

Replacement lines are added one at a time by a `WarrantyAdministrator` or by the owning claimant, each naming a `component_code` for the replacement component or tool, a `component_list_price_cents` of at least one cent, and a `quantity` of at least one unit. Lines may be added while the claim is `SUBMITTED` or `FRAUD_HOLD` — in any other status the addition is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Each line stores `line_reserve_cents`: the line's `component_list_price_cents` multiplied by its `quantity`, multiplied by the channel reserve percentage of the claim's SKU — 62 percent for `BRANDED`, 45 percent for `PRIVATE_LABEL`, 78 percent for `PROFESSIONAL` — multiplied by the warranty tier factor of that SKU, which is 1.00 for `LIFETIME`, 0.70 for `LIMITED_5Y` and 0.00 for `NONE`, and then rounded half up to a whole cent. The claim's three roll-ups are refreshed from its lines in `created_at` ascending order: `reserve_charge_cents` is the **sum of the already-rounded per-line amounts**, `replacement_unit_count` is the sum of the lines' `quantity` values, and `line_count` is the number of lines. Rounding each line before summing is mandatory; taking one product across all lines and rounding once at the end can differ by a cent and is wrong. Adding a line increments the claim's `version`.

### 3.8. Claim Adjudication: Holds, Approval, Fulfilment and Revocation

Adjudication is where the reserve is spent, and its state graph branches. A claim enters at `SUBMITTED`, may cycle through `FRAUD_HOLD` and back any number of times, and ends in one of four terminal states: `FULFILLED`, `DENIED`, `DENIED_FRAUD` or `REVOKED`.

A `WarrantyAdministrator` or a `BrandProtectionAnalyst` places a `SUBMITTED` claim on hold for manual review by supplying a `hold_reason`; any other starting status is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The hold moves the claim `SUBMITTED → FRAUD_HOLD`, stores the supplied `hold_reason`, and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `held_by_user_id`. The programme's `open_hold_count` is recomputed upward and its `version` increments. The claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` is recomputed, and the claim's `version` increments.

The **adjudication variant** of the fraud score differs from the intake variant in three ways and is recomputed on every hold, clear-hold, denial, fraud denial, approval, revocation and cascade hold. It has **no time window**: every claim the claimant has ever filed is in scope. It **excludes `REVOKED` claims entirely**, so revoking a claim removes it from the set rather than merely re-labelling it. And it **adds** a counterfeit term of 25 points for every claim in the set whose referenced SKU has a `warranty_posture` other than `OPEN`. The rest of the scoring core is identical to intake: the count of the set less the `PRO_ACCOUNT` allowance of 2 (or 0 for a `CONSUMER`), multiplied by 9 points per claim, plus 18 points per duplicate serial counted by `serial_code` groups, with the whole result clamped to the range 0 through 100. The result is written to `fraud_risk_score_adjudication`. Because the two variants differ in window, in membership and in the counterfeit term, the same claimant will routinely show different values in `fraud_risk_score_intake` and `fraud_risk_score_adjudication`, and both are visible side by side on the claimant record.

Clearing a hold returns the claim to the queue and is reserved to a `WarrantyAdministrator` — a `BrandProtectionAnalyst` may place a hold but may never clear one. The claim must be `FRAUD_HOLD`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the claim's `held_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; that check applies only when `held_by_user_id` is non-null, so a hold placed automatically by intake scoring or by the counterfeit cascade constrains nobody. The caller supplies a `clearance_note`. Clearing moves the claim `FRAUD_HOLD → SUBMITTED`, sets `fraud_cleared` to true, and clears `hold_reason` to null — but **retains** `held_by_user_id`, because the duty separation must still bind at approval time. The programme's `open_hold_count` is recomputed downward, its `version` increments, the claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` is recomputed, and the claim's `version` increments. The cycle `SUBMITTED → FRAUD_HOLD → SUBMITTED` is repeatable.

The two denial verbs are **not** interchangeable, and each is valid from exactly one status. Ordinary denial on the merits — out of scope, misuse, not our product — applies to a `SUBMITTED` claim, records the supplied `denial_code`, moves the claim `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, recomputes the claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication`, increments the claim's `version`, and moves no money and no units. Fraud denial applies to a `FRAUD_HOLD` claim, records the supplied `denial_code`, moves the claim `FRAUD_HOLD → DENIED_FRAUD` so that fraud outcomes stay separately auditable, recomputes the claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication`, increments the claim's `version`, and additionally recomputes the programme's `open_hold_count` downward with a `version` increment because a hold has left the queue. Attempting an ordinary denial on a held claim, or a fraud denial on a submitted one, returns `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` in both directions.

**Approval** is the heaviest operation in the system. It is a `WarrantyAdministrator` act carrying `expected_program_version`, and it is gated by eight checks in this exact order. The claim must be `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`). The supplied `expected_program_version` must match the programme's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION`, HTTP 412). The SKU's `warranty_posture` must be `OPEN`, with both `RESTRICTED` and `SUSPENDED` blocking (`ERR_SKU_WARRANTY_RESTRICTED`). The SKU's `units_on_hand` must be at least the claim's `replacement_unit_count` (`ERR_REPLACEMENT_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`). The programme's `reserve_balance_cents` must be at least the claim's `reserve_charge_cents` (`ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`, HTTP 402). The claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` must be strictly below the programme's `fraud_score_threshold`, **unless** `fraud_cleared` is true, in which case a manual clearance waives this gate — but note that clearance waives only the score gate and never the posture gate (`ERR_FRAUD_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`). The claim's `line_count` must be at least 1, since there is nothing to ship otherwise (`ERR_CLAIM_HAS_NO_LINES`). And last of all, the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the claim's retained `held_by_user_id` when that field is non-null (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), so the administrator who once held a claim can never be the one who approves it even after somebody else cleared the hold.

Approval then freezes `reserve_charge_cents` and `replacement_unit_count` from the claim's lines, moves the claim `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and settles both ledgers. On the SKU, `replacement_unit_count` units move out of `units_on_hand` and into `units_reserved`, and the SKU's `version` increments. On the programme, `reserve_balance_cents` is recomputed as the funded amount less the charges of all claims now in `APPROVED` or `FULFILLED` status, and the programme's `version` increments. The claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` is recomputed and the claim's `version` increments.

**Fulfilment** ships the replacement. The claim must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and the caller supplies a carrier `shipment_ref`. The claim moves `APPROVED → FULFILLED`, `replacement_unit_count` units move out of `units_reserved` and into `units_shipped` with a SKU `version` increment, and the claim's `version` increments. The reserve balance does **not** change: a fulfilled claim remains in the charged set, because the money really was spent.

**Revocation** is the compensating action for a settled claim discovered to be fraudulent, available to a `WarrantyAdministrator` or a `BrandProtectionAnalyst` with a `revocation_reason`. It is valid from `APPROVED` and from `FULFILLED`, and any other status is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Both branches move the claim to `REVOKED`, store the `revocation_reason`, recompute the programme's `reserve_balance_cents` so the charge leaves the counted set and the balance returns to exactly its pre-approval value, increment the programme's `version`, recompute the claimant's `fraud_risk_score_adjudication` — which now drops the claim from the set entirely — and increment the claim's `version`. The branches differ in exactly one respect: which unit bucket is debited. Revoking an `APPROVED` claim moves `replacement_unit_count` units out of `units_reserved` and back into `units_on_hand`, because the goods never shipped. Revoking a `FULFILLED` claim moves them out of `units_shipped` and back into `units_on_hand`, because the goods shipped and are being written back. Both branches increment the SKU's `version`. In neither branch are `reserve_charge_cents` or `replacement_unit_count` zeroed on the claim: they stay on the row as the audit trail of what was once promised, which is precisely what makes the money identity of §2.9 close with zero drift.

### 3.9. Counterfeit Sightings and the Brand-Protection Cascade

Counterfeit hand tools reach consumers through secondary marketplaces and reach the manufacturer through the warranty desk. A **CounterfeitReport** records one sighting, filed by a `BrandProtectionAnalyst` or a `ChannelManager` against the genuine `sku_id` being copied, with the `channel_name` of the marketplace where the listing was found, the `seller_handle` behind it, an `evidence_grade` of `WEAK`, `MODERATE` or `STRONG`, and a `unit_estimate` of zero or more counterfeit units believed to be in circulation from that listing.

Filing freezes the report's `risk_points` from the evidence in hand and the SKU's channel exposure at that moment, so that later catalogue changes cannot rewrite history. Evidence contributes 5 points for `WEAK`, 15 for `MODERATE` and 30 for `STRONG`. Scale contributes a further 0 points when `unit_estimate` is 50 or less, 8 points when it is greater than 50 and up to and including 500, and 20 points when it is greater than 500. Those two contributions are added, then multiplied by the channel exposure factor of the SKU — 1.0 for `BRANDED`, since a counterfeit of the flagship brand does the most damage, 0.6 for `PRIVATE_LABEL` and 0.8 for `PROFESSIONAL` — and the product is rounded half up to a whole number of points. A new report is `OPEN` with `confirmation_note` and `dismissal_note` null, and an `OPEN` report contributes **nothing** to the SKU: `brand_risk_score` is untouched until an analyst confirms the sighting.

Dismissal is the quiet branch. A `BrandProtectionAnalyst` supplies a `dismissal_note`, the report must be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`), and the report moves `OPEN → DISMISSED` with the note stored and its `version` incremented. Nothing else happens at all: no roll-up, no posture change, no claim cascade, and the SKU's `brand_risk_score` is exactly what it was before, because dismissed reports never enter the roll-up.

Confirmation is the loud branch, and it is a five-link cascade plus a packaging consequence, all completed within the confirming request. The report must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, and the analyst supplies a `confirmation_note`.

Link one moves the report `OPEN → CONFIRMED`, stores the `confirmation_note` and increments the report's `version`. Link two re-rolls the SKU's brand-protection figures over the reports on that SKU whose status is `CONFIRMED` — `OPEN` and `DISMISSED` reports contribute nothing: `confirmed_counterfeit_count` becomes the number of such reports and `brand_risk_score` becomes the sum of their frozen `risk_points`, capped at 100. Link three re-derives `warranty_posture` from that score against two inclusive thresholds: a score of 85 or more makes the posture `SUSPENDED`, a score of 40 or more but below 85 makes it `RESTRICTED`, and anything below 40 leaves it `OPEN`. A professional-channel SKU whose confirmed reports total exactly 40 points is `RESTRICTED`; at 39 it is still `OPEN`. The SKU's `version` increments.

Link four fires when the posture has just left `OPEN` in either direction, to `RESTRICTED` or to `SUSPENDED`. Every warranty claim on that SKU currently in `SUBMITTED` status is moved `SUBMITTED → FRAUD_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to the literal string `COUNTERFEIT_POSTURE` and `held_by_user_id` left null — the hold was placed by the system, not by a person, so it constrains no future clearer or approver. The affected claims are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each affected programme's `open_hold_count` and `version` are updated. Link five recomputes the adjudication fraud score of every claimant touched by link four, and those scores now carry the 25-point counterfeit term for each of that claimant's claims sitting on a SKU whose posture is not `OPEN` — which is how one confirmed sighting can push an otherwise ordinary claimant above a programme's `fraud_score_threshold`. Since approval requires an `OPEN` posture outright, every held claim on that SKU is now doubly blocked: by `ERR_SKU_WARRANTY_RESTRICTED` on the posture and, once released from hold, potentially by `ERR_FRAUD_SCORE_TOO_HIGH` on the score.

One further consequence applies **only** when the posture becomes `SUSPENDED`, not merely `RESTRICTED`: every packaging configuration in `APPROVED` status that holds this SKU as a member is moved `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with each pack's `version` incremented. A suspended SKU cannot remain on a shelf.
