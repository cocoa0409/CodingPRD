# Consulting Bench Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A technology consulting firm sells hours it does not yet own. Sales commits to a client date before anyone knows who will deliver, the delivery organisation carries the payroll of consultants whether or not they are billing, and the gap between those two facts — the bench — is simultaneously the firm's largest controllable cost and its only source of surge capacity. The Consulting Bench Management API exists to make that gap measurable and governable: it holds the skill taxonomy the firm sells against, the demand pipeline sales is building, the assignments that couple the two, the bench spells that accumulate when the coupling fails, and the utilization measurements finance closes the books on.

The product surface deliberately keeps four decisions separate, because in practice four different people own them. A `SalesLead` decides what the firm is trying to sell and at what price. A `ResourceManager` decides who delivers it, and — when nobody internal can — whether a partner firm is asked instead. A `PracticePartner` owns the shape of the practice, the partner channel, and the authority to end a consultant's tenure. A `FinanceController` owns the measurement period and the fiscal veto. The system's job is not to make those decisions but to refuse the ones that are inconsistent: a discount nobody is senior enough to approve, an assignment that would take a consultant past full capacity, a partner placement that would push a practice past its own sourcing ceiling, a separation approved by the same person who proposed it.

Two properties run through every requirement below. First, **every derived number is recomputed inside the request that changed its inputs** — there is no batch reconciliation, no read-time lag, and no eventual consistency anywhere in the product. When an assignment is released, the consultant's allocation, their availability, their bench spell, their practice group's bench ratio, and that group's entire ancestor chain of postures have all settled before the response is written. Second, **time is a server-controlled input, not an ambient fact**. Bench ages, certification expiries, delivered hours and cohort windows are all evaluated against a logical clock that tests set explicitly, and no rule in this document may consult real wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, common properties, the clock and interval conventions, precision, existence resolution, concurrency control, the total ordering of validations, and the two cross-cutting governance mechanisms (practice posture and segregation of duties).

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity they are acting in. A request missing either header, carrying an empty value for either, or naming a role that is not one of the five recognised names is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The five roles are spelled exactly `SalesLead`, `ResourceManager`, `PracticePartner`, `FinanceController`, and `Consultant`. When the headers are well formed but the role is not among those authorised for the operation being attempted, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Four of the five roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `SalesLead`, `ResourceManager`, `PracticePartner`, or `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server never compares it to any row's `id`, and no such role has an "own rows" restriction. The server does, however, *record* it wherever an accountability trail is required — as `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a discount approval, and as `separation_proposed_by_user_id` and `separation_decided_by_user_id` on a bench spell — and those recorded values are later compared against each other to enforce segregation of duties (§2.11).

The fifth role is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Consultant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant` row; a `Consultant`-role request whose `X-User-ID` matches no existing consultant is itself an `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` failure, because the identity cannot be resolved. Ownership checks on any resource that references a consultant compare that resource's `consultant_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Consultant` caller may read only their own consultant row, may list and read only their own utilization periods, and may create or revise skill assertions only where `consultant_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. Any of those addressed at another consultant's data is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A utilization period identifier that exists but hangs off a different consultant than the one named in the path is a different failure — see §2.7.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list-returning endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Nearly every resource in this system carries four server-managed properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. The `created_at` property is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written. The `updated_at` property is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations that only touch derived fields, so a practice group whose `rollup_bench_ratio` moved because a distant descendant's consultant went on the bench has a new `updated_at`. The `version` property is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the row; it is the value optimistic concurrency control is quoted against (§2.8).

Practice groups, skills, consultants, skill assertions, demand opportunities, assignments, bench spells, reskilling programmes, subcontractor firms, external placements, discount approvals and utilization periods all carry the full set of four. The hiring forecast is the sole exception: it is an immutable snapshot, so a forecast row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` property and no `version` property at all, and there is no operation anywhere in the API that modifies one after creation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Date Formats and Interval Conventions

Every age, expiry, delivery-hour, cohort and window rule in this document reads a server-held **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. The clock is initialised to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts, and an authenticated caller in any of the four operator roles may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant. The clock must not fall back to real time before or after any such call. Moving the clock re-derives every computed field that depends on it on the next read — a consultant's `allocated_pct` and `availability_status`, a skill assertion's `assertion_status`, a bench spell's `age_days`, a demand's `earned_realization_pct`, and a utilization period's hour buckets — but moving the clock on its own never advances a state machine. Only the operations checkpoint described in §3.9 does that.

Wherever a field name ends in `_date`, `_on`, or names a date (`start_date`, `end_date`, `started_on`, `cohort_start_on`, `period_end_on`, `horizon_start_on`, and so on) the value is an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Wherever a field names an instant (`generated_at`, `separated_on`, `current_utc`) the value is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. A malformed date, a malformed instant, or a pair whose end precedes its permitted relation to its start is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The interval conventions differ deliberately between entities, and the difference is load-bearing:

- An **assignment** interval is **half-open**: `start_date` is inclusive and `end_date` is exclusive. An assignment whose `end_date` is the day D and another whose `start_date` is that same day D therefore never overlap, and an assignment contributes nothing to allocation on its own `end_date`. `end_date` must be strictly later than `start_date`.
- An **external placement** interval is **closed** on both ends: `start_date` and `end_date` are both inclusive. A placement ending on day D and another starting on day D therefore *do* collide on D — the exact opposite of the assignment convention. `end_date` must be on or after `start_date`.
- A **demand opportunity**'s delivery window is closed on both ends: `start_date` is the first delivery day and `end_date` the last, and `end_date` must be on or after `start_date`.
- A **reskilling cohort** window is closed on both ends: `cohort_start_on` is the first cohort day and `cohort_end_on` the last, and `cohort_end_on` must be on or after `cohort_start_on`.
- A **utilization period** is closed on both ends: `period_start_on` through `period_end_on` inclusive, with `period_end_on` on or after `period_start_on`.
- A **forecast horizon** is closed on both ends: `horizon_start_on` through `horizon_end_on` inclusive, with `horizon_end_on` on or after `horizon_start_on`.
- A **bench spell's investment window** runs from `investment_started_on` inclusive to `investment_ended_on` exclusive; while `investment_ended_on` is still absent the window is treated as open-ended and is truncated at the right edge of whatever period is being measured.

Hours are counted in **workdays**, meaning dates that fall Monday through Friday. Saturdays and Sundays contribute nothing to any capacity, delivery or overlap count, and every workday is worth eight hours. There is no partial-day arithmetic anywhere in the system: workday counts are integers and day counts are whole calendar days.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, applied at the decimal width the field declares. Monetary rates (`cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, `standard_bill_rate_usd_per_hour`, `standard_rate_usd_per_hour`, `requested_rate_usd_per_hour`, `approved_rate_usd_per_hour`, `effective_rate_usd_per_hour`, `bill_rate_usd_per_hour`, `pass_through_rate_usd_per_hour`, `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour`), percentages (`allocation_pct`, `win_probability_pct`, `markup_pct`, `external_sourcing_cap_pct`, `discount_pct`, `provisional_realization_pct`, `projected_margin_pct`, `booked_realization_pct`, `earned_realization_pct`, `blended_margin_pct`, `target_utilization_pct`, `utilization_pct_target_basis`, `utilization_pct_available_basis`, `utilization_pct_of_record`, `max_investment_pct`), percentage-point differences (`variance_pp`), hour quantities (`holiday_hours`, `capacity_hours`, `net_available_hours`, `billable_hours`, `investment_hours`, `bench_hours`) and FTE quantities (`required_fte`, `fte`, `covered_internal_fte`, `covered_external_fte`, `open_fte`, `weighted_fte`, `weighted_demand_fte`, `internal_supply_fte`, `external_supply_fte`, `gap_fte`, `allocated_pct`) all carry exactly **two decimal places**.

Three families carry four decimals instead: `scarcity_index`, `own_bench_ratio`, `rollup_bench_ratio`, and `match_score` together with every component that feeds it. Integer fields (`min_proficiency_level`, `proficiency_level`, `target_proficiency_level`, `onsite_nights_per_week`, `travel_max_nights_per_week`, `seat_capacity`, `seats_taken`, `cooldown_days`, `max_concurrent_placements`, `period_workdays`, `age_days`, `escalation_count`, `revision_count`, `total_headcount`, `bench_headcount`, `escalated_l2_count`, `recommended_hires`, `version`) hold whole numbers only.

Two rounding disciplines matter enough to state separately because they produce different answers from the same inputs. Realization and margin ratios are rounded **once at the end**, never per contributing assignment. Probability-weighted demand, by contrast, is rounded **per opportunity first and only then summed**, and the sum is rounded again — summing raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong and reports a different number. Threshold comparisons always use the *rounded* value: a raw 5.004 percent discount rounds to 5.00 and is compared as 5.00, and a bench ratio is compared against its breakpoints after being rounded to four decimals. Numbers are returned in JSON as numbers, not strings.

### 2.7. Existence Resolution and Left-to-Right Path Validation

Path identifiers are resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that names no existing row decides the error. The only nested paths in this API are a practice group's child-group listing and a consultant's utilization periods; in the latter, the `{id}` segment naming the consultant is resolved before the `{periodId}` segment naming the period, so a request quoting a nonexistent consultant *and* a nonexistent period is rejected with `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`. A period identifier that resolves to a real row belonging to some *other* consultant than the one in the path is also treated as not found: `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`.

Every collection has its own 404 code, and each is used both for path segments and for foreign keys carried in a request body: `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_NOT_FOUND` for a group path segment or a `practice_group_id` or `parent_group_id` value; `ERR_SKILL_NOT_FOUND` for a skill path segment or a `required_skill_id`, `skill_id` or `development_goal_skill_id` value; `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND` for a consultant path segment or a `consultant_id` value; `ERR_SKILL_ASSERTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BENCH_SPELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND` for their own path segments; `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_FOUND` for a demand path segment or a `demand_opportunity_id` value; `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` for a firm path segment or a `subcontractor_firm_id` value; and `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` for a period segment. All carry HTTP 404.

Body-borne foreign keys are resolved **in the order the owning entity declares its fields**, which is the order §3 introduces them. Proposing an assignment therefore resolves `consultant_id` before `demand_opportunity_id`, so a payload naming a missing consultant and a missing demand reports the consultant. Requesting an external placement resolves `subcontractor_firm_id` before `demand_opportunity_id`. Any path-borne identifier is always resolved before any body-borne one.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Versions

Two creation operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only those two. Both are creations rather than state transitions, and that is precisely why they need the guard: a second row of either kind is legitimate product behaviour, so no state precondition can refuse a writer who is working from an outdated picture of the parent.

**Creating a discount approval** requires a non-optional `expected_opportunity_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `DemandOpportunity` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the demand's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A demand may legitimately carry several discount rounds — each one re-derives its tier and its projected margin from the demand's current approved rate and current assignment mix — so nothing about the demand's state makes a second request refusable. The stale-version check is what makes the refusal deterministic: a writer quoting an outdated version is required to re-read the demand, observe the rate that has since been approved, and re-price before asking again.

**Proposing an assignment** requires a non-optional `expected_consultant_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `Consultant` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the consultant's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONSULTANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A consultant may legitimately hold several assignments, and a newly proposed one reserves no capacity at all, so again no state precondition applies. The guard exists because two fields are snapshot onto the assignment row at creation and never recomputed afterwards — the consultant's `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` and the computed `match_score` — so a writer whose view of `allocated_pct`, `availability_status` or `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` predates another write must re-read the consultant before proposing.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_opportunity_version`, no `expected_consultant_version`, and no version input of any kind; supplying one is an unknown body key and is rejected as a malformed request.

### 2.9. The Validation Precedence Ladder

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned and **no mutation of any kind occurs**. Which error wins is fixed by a total ordering — it is not a matter of implementation convenience, and it is not the textual order of the paragraphs in §3. The ordering reflects how a consulting firm actually refuses work: it refuses on compliance grounds (who is allowed to approve what, is the certification still valid) before it refuses on scheduling grounds, and on scheduling grounds before it argues about price and capacity. A badly priced but legal assignment is a negotiation; an illegally approved one is an audit finding.

The full ladder, from highest precedence (evaluated first, wins over everything below it) to lowest:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-Role` present and equal to one of the five role names, `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, and for the entity-backed `Consultant` role the supplied id must resolve to an existing consultant row. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorisation** — the caller's role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Request shape** — unknown body keys rejected; required fields present; types, enum members, numeric ranges and decimal widths exactly as this document declares them; ISO 8601 dates and instants well formed; intra-payload ordering satisfied (an assignment's `end_date` strictly after its `start_date`; a demand's `end_date` on or after its `start_date`; `cohort_end_on` on or after `cohort_start_on`; `period_end_on` on or after `period_start_on`; `horizon_end_on` on or after `horizon_start_on`); and pagination `limit` within 1 through 100 with `offset` at least 0. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path existence** — every templated identifier in the path resolves, checked left to right as described in §2.7. Applies to every endpoint with a templated segment. Failure: the relevant 404 code.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key supplied in the body resolves, checked in field-declaration order as described in §2.7. Applies to consultant creation, skill-assertion creation, demand creation, assignment proposal, reskilling-programme creation, cohort enrolment, external-placement request, discount-approval creation, consultant update and practice-group update. Failure: the relevant 404 code.
6. **Ownership** — a `Consultant`-role caller may only read or write rows belonging to the consultant whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`. Applies to reading one consultant, listing a consultant's utilization periods, reading one utilization period, creating a skill assertion and updating a skill assertion. Failure: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied parent-version field equals the parent's current `version`, per §2.8. Applies to discount-approval creation and assignment proposal. Deliberately evaluated *after* existence, so a request quoting both a missing foreign key and a stale version returns the 404, not the 412. Failure: `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONSULTANT_VERSION` (412).
8. **Demand pipeline state** — qualification requires `QUALIFYING`; staffing-branch selection requires `QUALIFYING` or `QUALIFIED` with the branch still unset; closure requires `QUALIFIED`; auto-staffing, assignment proposal and external-placement request all require `QUALIFIED`; discount-approval creation is likewise gated here. Failure: `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID` (409).
9. **Assignment state** — confirmation requires `PROPOSED`; release requires `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE`; cancellation requires `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`. A second confirmation, or a release of an already-`RELEASED` row, is rejected here; idempotency is **not** granted. Failure: `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
10. **Bench spell state** — cohort enrolment requires `ESCALATED_L1` or `ESCALATED_L2`; internal-project placement requires `ESCALATED_L1` or `ESCALATED_L2`; a separation proposal requires `ESCALATED_L2`; a separation decision requires `SEPARATION_PROPOSED`. A `CLOSED` or `SEPARATED` spell accepts nothing at all. Failure: `ERR_BENCH_SPELL_STATE_INVALID` (409).
11. **Programme state** — enrolment requires `PLANNED` or `RUNNING`; graduation requires `RUNNING` and requires `cohort_end_on` to be on or before the logical clock date. Failure: `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` (409).
12. **Placement state** — approval requires `REQUESTED`; termination requires `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`. Failure: `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
13. **Discount approval state** — deciding requires `PENDING`; revising requires `REJECTED`. An `APPROVED` row is terminal and accepts neither. Failure: `ERR_APPROVAL_STATE_INVALID` (409).
14. **Utilization period state** — closing requires the period to be `OPEN` and requires `period_end_on` to be strictly earlier than the logical clock date. A `CLOSED` period accepts no further operation. Failure: `ERR_PERIOD_STATE_INVALID` (409).
15. **Skill retirement** — a skill whose `retired` flag is true may not be referenced by a new demand, by a new reskilling programme, or by a qualification call. Failure: `ERR_SKILL_RETIRED` (409).
16. **Staffing branch selected** — auto-staffing, assignment proposal and external-placement request all require the demand's `staffing_mode` to be set. Failure: `ERR_STAFFING_MODE_NOT_SET` (409).
17. **Consultant not separated** — assignment proposal, assignment confirmation, skill-assertion creation, cohort enrolment and utilization-period creation all require the named consultant's `availability_status` to be anything other than `SEPARATED`. Failure: `ERR_CONSULTANT_SEPARATED` (409).
18. **Consultant not mid-programme** — assignment confirmation additionally requires the consultant's `availability_status` to be anything other than `RESKILLING`. A proposal may legitimately have been created before the enrolment; the confirmation is where it is caught. Applies to assignment confirmation only. Failure: `ERR_CONSULTANT_IN_RESKILLING` (409).
19. **Segregation of duties** — the user deciding a discount approval must differ from its `requested_by_user_id`, and the user deciding a separation must differ from the spell's `separation_proposed_by_user_id`. Applies to the discount decision and the separation decision. Failure: `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (403).
20. **Approval tier authority** — the caller's role must satisfy the approval's `required_tier`. Applies to the discount decision only. Failure: `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (403).
21. **Certification currency** — the skill assertion backing a confirmation must have `assertion_status` equal to `ACTIVE`. Applies to assignment confirmation only. Failure: `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (409).
22. **Separation eligibility** — a separation proposal requires the spell's `escalation_count` to be at least 2, and an `APPROVED` separation decision requires the consultant's `allocated_pct` to be exactly 0.00. Applies to the separation proposal and the separation decision. Failure: `ERR_SEPARATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
23. **Uniqueness** — `name` unique across practice groups, `code` unique across skills, `email` unique across consultants, `name` unique across subcontractor firms, and at most one skill assertion per consultant-and-skill pair. Applies to practice-group creation, skill creation, consultant creation, firm creation and skill-assertion creation. Failure: `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT` (409).
24. **Hierarchy acyclicity** — `parent_group_id` must not equal the group's own `id` and must not close a cycle through the transitive parent chain. Applies to practice-group creation and practice-group update. Failure: `ERR_GROUP_CYCLE` (409).
25. **Proficiency sufficiency** — the consultant must hold an `ACTIVE` assertion for the demand's `required_skill_id` at a `proficiency_level` at or above the demand's `min_proficiency_level`. Applies to assignment confirmation only. Failure: `ERR_PROFICIENCY_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
26. **Travel fit** — the demand's `onsite_nights_per_week` must be at most the consultant's `travel_max_nights_per_week`, and when the demand's `is_international` is true the consultant's `travel_willing_international` must also be true. Applies to assignment confirmation only. Failure: `ERR_TRAVEL_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATED` (409).
27. **Period non-overlap** — utilization periods of one consultant may not intersect on their inclusive ranges, and a period starting on the very day a previous one ends *is* an overlap. Applies to utilization-period creation. Failure: `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
28. **Cohort clash with committed work** — the cohort's inclusive window must not intersect any `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE` assignment of the enrolling consultant on that assignment's half-open interval. Applies to cohort enrolment. Failure: `ERR_COHORT_CONFLICTS_WITH_ASSIGNMENT` (409).
29. **Reskilling cooldown** — `cohort_start_on` must be strictly later than the `cohort_end_on` of the consultant's most recent `COMPLETED` programme advanced by the *new* programme's `cooldown_days`. Applies to cohort enrolment. Failure: `ERR_RESKILLING_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE` (409).
30. **Cohort capacity** — `seats_taken` must be strictly below `seat_capacity` at the moment of enrolment. Applies to cohort enrolment. Failure: `ERR_COHORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
31. **Firm concurrency ceiling** — on every date of the placement's closed interval, the count of the firm's other `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` placements covering that date plus this one must be at most `max_concurrent_placements`. Applies to placement approval. Failure: `ERR_FIRM_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED` (409).
32. **Allocation capacity** — on every date of the assignment's half-open interval, the consultant's committed allocation from `CONFIRMED` and `ACTIVE` assignments plus this assignment's `allocation_pct` must be at most 100.00; a total of exactly 100.00 is allowed. Applies to assignment confirmation. Failure: `ERR_ALLOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
33. **Hour conservation** — a period whose `hour_overallocated` flag is true may not be closed. Applies to period closure. Failure: `ERR_HOUR_OVERALLOCATION` (409).
34. **Non-empty candidate pool** — the auto-staffing eligibility filter must yield at least one candidate. Applies to auto-staffing. Failure: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONSULTANT` (409).
35. **Geography coherence** — a consultant's `home_geo_code` must equal the `geography_code` of their practice group, and a forecast's `skill_code` must match the `code` of an existing skill. Applies to consultant creation, consultant update and forecast generation. Failure: `ERR_GEOGRAPHY_MISMATCH` (409).
36. **Branch coherence** — assignment proposal and auto-staffing require `staffing_mode` to be `INTERNAL`; external-placement request and placement approval require `staffing_mode` to be `EXTERNAL`. Failure: `ERR_STAFFING_MODE_MISMATCH` (409).
37. **Firm vetting** — the firm's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED` (a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` firm may not be used) and the firm's `geo_code` must equal the demand's practice group's `geography_code`. Applies to external-placement request and placement approval. Failure: `ERR_FIRM_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
38. **No premium disguised as a discount** — a discount approval's `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` must be at most the demand's `standard_rate_usd_per_hour`, and a demand's own `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` must be at most its `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` at creation. Applies to demand creation, discount-approval creation and discount revision. Failure: `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_STANDARD` (422).
39. **Discount hard cap** — the computed `discount_pct` must not exceed 35.00 percent. No role may approve past the cap, so the request is refused outright rather than routed to a tier. Applies to discount-approval creation, the discount decision and discount revision. Failure: `ERR_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
40. **Margin floor on confirmation** — confirming must not drive the demand's `blended_margin_pct` below 20.00 percent; the margin is projected *with* the candidate assignment included before the write is accepted. Applies to assignment confirmation. Failure: `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH` (422).
41. **Revisions must concede** — a revised `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` must be strictly greater than the value currently stored on the approval. Applies to discount revision. Failure: `ERR_REVISION_NOT_HIGHER` (422).
42. **Frozen posture** — the demand's practice group `posture` must not be `FROZEN` for any external-sourcing write; a `FROZEN` posture admits no carve-out of any kind. Applies to external-placement request and placement approval. Failure: `ERR_PRACTICE_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409).
43. **Constrained posture with scarcity carve-out** — when the demand's practice group `posture` is `CONSTRAINED`, external sourcing is refused unless the demand's required skill has a `scarcity_index` of at least 0.7000. Applies to external-placement request and placement approval. Failure: `ERR_PRACTICE_POSTURE_CONSTRAINED` (409).
44. **External sourcing cap** — approving the placement must keep the group's external coverage share at or below its `external_sourcing_cap_pct`. Applies to placement approval. Failure: `ERR_EXTERNAL_SOURCING_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).

Read the ladder as bands: rungs 1 through 3 are authentication and shape, 4 and 5 are existence, 6 is ownership, 7 is concurrency, 8 through 18 are state, 19 through 22 are safety and compliance, 23 through 34 are constraints, 35 through 37 are referential coherence, 38 through 41 are fiscal, and 42 through 44 are capacity and posture. Two consequences are worth stating outright. A `PracticePartner` who tries to decide a discount routed to `L2_FINANCE` is rejected at rung 20 with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` even though rung 2 admitted their role, because the endpoint's role list and the row's tier are two separate gates. And a request that is simultaneously self-approving *and* under-tiered reports the self-approval, because rung 19 sits above rung 20.

### 2.10. Practice Posture and the Bench-Ratio Rollup

Each `PracticeGroup` carries a computed gating `posture` — one of `OPEN`, `CONSTRAINED`, or `FROZEN` — that governs whether the practice is allowed to spend money outside its own payroll. Posture is derived, never set by a client, and it is re-derived inside any request that moves a consultant into or out of the bench population: a consultant hire, a bench spell state change, a separation, or an assignment confirmation, release or cancellation.

Three counts feed the derivation. A group's `total_headcount` is the number of consultants whose `practice_group_id` is that group and whose `availability_status` is anything other than `SEPARATED`. Its `bench_headcount` is the number of those consultants whose `availability_status` is either `BENCH` or `RESKILLING` — note carefully that a consultant parked on an internal project is *not* counted, while one mid-reskilling *is*. Its `escalated_l2_count` is the number of bench spells in state `ESCALATED_L2` or `SEPARATION_PROPOSED` whose consultant belongs to that group **or to any transitive descendant group**.

From those, `own_bench_ratio` is the group's own `bench_headcount` divided by its own `total_headcount`, rounded half-up to four decimals, and is exactly 0.0000 when the group has no non-separated headcount at all. The `rollup_bench_ratio` is the sum of `bench_headcount` over the group and all of its transitive descendants divided by the sum of `total_headcount` over the same set, rounded half-up to four decimals, and is exactly 0.0000 when that denominator is zero.

Posture then follows three arms, evaluated in this order. The group is `FROZEN` when its `rollup_bench_ratio` is at least 0.5000 **or** when its `escalated_l2_count` is at least 3. Otherwise it is `CONSTRAINED` when its `rollup_bench_ratio` is at least 0.3000. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. Both ratio comparisons are made against the already-rounded four-decimal value. The escalated-count arm is entirely independent of headcount, which is the point: three spells sitting at the second escalation rung anywhere in the subtree freeze the whole group even at a bench ratio of 0.1000, and a group can therefore be `FROZEN` while its own `own_bench_ratio` sits below the `CONSTRAINED` breakpoint.

Propagation is **child-to-parent**. When a consultant's situation changes, the system recomputes the touched consultant's group and then every transitive ancestor of that group, walking upwards so that a parent always reads settled child values. Sibling subtrees are re-read to supply the rollup numerator and denominator but their own posture is not recomputed. The walk always terminates because the parent chain is acyclic — rung 24 of the ladder refuses any `parent_group_id` that would close a cycle with `ERR_GROUP_CYCLE` — and each group is visited exactly once.

Alongside posture, each group carries `level_targets`: a per-career-level policy object whose keys are level names and whose values are objects with four properties, `target_utilization_pct`, `bench_escalation_l1_days`, `bench_escalation_l2_days` and `max_investment_pct`. The object is optional when the group is registered and defaults to empty. Resolution is **per key, not per object**: for any given consultant the system looks up the entry under that consultant's `level`, and if — and only if — that specific level key is absent from the group's object it falls back to the firm-wide default for that level. A group that supplies an entry for `ANALYST` alone therefore still uses firm-wide defaults for all four other levels. The firm-wide defaults are: `ANALYST` targets 70.00 percent utilization with escalation rungs at 21 and 45 days and a 20.00 percent investment ceiling; `CONSULTANT` targets 75.00 percent with rungs at 14 and 30 days and a 15.00 percent ceiling; `SENIOR_CONSULTANT` targets 80.00 percent with rungs at 14 and 28 days and a 12.00 percent ceiling; `MANAGER` targets 70.00 percent with rungs at 30 and 60 days and a 25.00 percent ceiling; and `PRINCIPAL` targets 55.00 percent with rungs at 45 and 90 days and a 35.00 percent ceiling.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Tiered Fiscal Authority

Two decisions in this system are structurally two-person decisions, and the separation is enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than merely on `X-User-Role`. Holding the correct role is necessary but never sufficient.

The first is a discount. Whoever files a discount request has their `X-User-ID` recorded as `requested_by_user_id`, and a decision whose caller's `X-User-ID` equals that value is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN`. The second is a separation. Whoever files a separation proposal has their `X-User-ID` recorded as `separation_proposed_by_user_id`, and a decision whose caller's `X-User-ID` equals that value is rejected with the same code. Note that a revision of a rejected discount **re-stamps** `requested_by_user_id` to the reviser's `X-User-ID`, so the identity that is forbidden from deciding moves with each negotiation round.

Layered on top of the two-person rule is a tier of fiscal authority carried on the discount approval itself as `required_tier`, one of `NONE`, `L1_PRACTICE_PARTNER`, or `L2_FINANCE`. A tier of `NONE` means no human decision is needed at all: the approval is created already `APPROVED` with `decided_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM_AUTO`. A tier of `L1_PRACTICE_PARTNER` is satisfied by either a `PracticePartner` or a `FinanceController`. A tier of `L2_FINANCE` is satisfied by a `FinanceController` and by nobody else — a `PracticePartner` attempting it is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. Both the approval and the rejection of a discount are subject to the tier rule, not just the approval: a `PracticePartner` may not reject an `L2_FINANCE` row either.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Practice Hierarchy and the Skill Taxonomy

Nothing else in the system can be created until a `PracticePartner` has registered at least one **practice group** — the organisational unit that recruits, bills, carries a bench, and is gated by a posture. A group is registered with a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters that must be unique across all groups, and a `geography_code` naming the staffing geography it recruits and bills from, one of `NA`, `EMEA`, `APAC`, or `LATAM`. Optionally the registration may supply a `parent_group_id` — the `id` of another group that becomes this one's parent in the practice hierarchy — which defaults to absent, an `external_sourcing_cap_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 that defaults to 20.00, and the `level_targets` policy object described in §2.10, which defaults to empty. A duplicate `name` is rejected with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`; a `parent_group_id` naming the group itself, or one that would close a loop through the transitive parent chain, is rejected with `ERR_GROUP_CYCLE`.

A freshly registered group starts empty in every derived respect: `total_headcount`, `bench_headcount` and `escalated_l2_count` are all 0, `own_bench_ratio` and `rollup_bench_ratio` are both 0.0000, and `posture` is `OPEN`. When a `parent_group_id` was supplied, registering the child immediately recomputes `rollup_bench_ratio` and `posture` up the new parent chain, because the parent's rollup denominators now include a new subtree even when that subtree is empty.

A `PracticePartner` may later adjust a group's `external_sourcing_cap_pct`, its `level_targets`, or its `parent_group_id`. Any such update re-derives `own_bench_ratio`, `rollup_bench_ratio` and `posture` for the group and every transitive ancestor — and when `parent_group_id` changes, for the *previous* ancestor chain as well, since that chain has lost a subtree. One thing an update explicitly does **not** do is reach backwards into measurement: a changed `level_targets` never retro-changes the `target_utilization_pct` recorded on any existing utilization period, because that value is frozen onto the period row when the period is opened. Groups can be listed filtered by `geography_code`, by `parent_group_id` — which doubles as the reverse-reachability filter for the self-referential parent link — and by `posture`, and a group's direct children can be listed as a nested read under that group.

**Skills** form the taxonomy everything is matched against, and they too are registered by a `PracticePartner`. A skill carries a `code` that is the stable natural key: a string of 3 to 30 characters beginning with an uppercase letter and continuing with uppercase letters, digits, or underscores, unique across all skills. It carries a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `family` naming its taxonomy branch — one of `CLOUD`, `DATA`, `SECURITY`, `ERP`, or `AI` — an optional `scarcity_index` between 0.0000 and 1.0000 at four decimals which defaults to 0.5000, and an optional `retired` boolean which defaults to false. A duplicate `code` is an `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`.

Two levers on a skill matter far beyond the taxonomy, and both may be adjusted after registration. Raising `scarcity_index` to 0.7000 or higher opens the carve-out that lets external sourcing proceed against a `CONSTRAINED` practice — the market judgement being that a genuinely scarce skill is worth buying even from a practice already carrying too much bench. Setting `retired` to true blocks the skill from being named by any *new* demand opportunity, any *new* reskilling programme, or a qualification call, each rejected with `ERR_SKILL_RETIRED`; existing rows that already reference the skill are left entirely untouched. Skills can be listed filtered by `family` and by `retired`, and every role including `Consultant` may read them.

### 3.2. Hiring Consultants and Recording What They Can Do

A `ResourceManager` hires a **consultant** into a practice group. The payload carries a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all consultants, a `level` naming the career level — one of `ANALYST`, `CONSULTANT`, `SENIOR_CONSULTANT`, `MANAGER`, or `PRINCIPAL`, which is what selects the row of the group's `level_targets` that governs this person — the `practice_group_id` of their group, a `home_city` of 1 to 80 characters, a `home_geo_code` from the same four-value geography enumeration, a `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` between 0.01 and 2000.00 representing the fully loaded internal cost of the person's time, and a `standard_bill_rate_usd_per_hour` between 0.01 and 5000.00 which is the person's list rate and is held for reference only, since the commercial rate that actually governs a deal lives on the demand. Four further inputs are optional: `travel_max_nights_per_week`, an integer from 0 to 5 defaulting to 3; `travel_willing_international`, a boolean defaulting to false; `development_goal_skill_id`, the `id` of the skill this person is deliberately developing, defaulting to absent; and `industry_experience`, an array of objects each carrying an `industry_code` string and a `years` number between 0.0 and 40.0, defaulting to an empty array. Two entries sharing the same `industry_code` in one array are rejected as a malformed payload. A duplicate `email` is an `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`, and a `home_geo_code` that differs from the `geography_code` of the named practice group is an `ERR_GEOGRAPHY_MISMATCH`.

Hiring has consequences beyond the consultant row. The new consultant's `availability_status` is `BENCH` and their `allocated_pct` is 0.00, their `separated_on` is absent, and — critically — the hire **creates the consultant's first bench spell** in state `MONITORING`, with `started_on` stamped from the logical clock date, `age_reset_on` absent, and `escalation_count` at 0. That spell row is not returned by the hire; it is discoverable only by listing bench spells. The hire also increments the group's `total_headcount` and its `bench_headcount`, and then re-derives `own_bench_ratio`, `rollup_bench_ratio` and `posture` for the group and every transitive ancestor per §2.10 — so hiring into a small, heavily benched practice can by itself tip that practice and its parents from `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED`.

A consultant's `availability_status` is always derived, never supplied, and the branch order is normative. The person is `SEPARATED` whenever `separated_on` has been stamped. Otherwise they are `RESKILLING` when their open bench spell is in state `RESKILLING`, and `INTERNAL_PROJECT` when that spell is in state `INTERNAL_PROJECT`. Otherwise they are `ASSIGNED` when their `allocated_pct` is at least 50.00. Otherwise they are `BENCH`. Because the reskilling and internal-project branches sit above the allocation branch, a consultant enrolled in a cohort is never reported `ASSIGNED` even if some stray allocation exists on their record. The `allocated_pct` itself is the sum of `allocation_pct` over that consultant's assignments whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE` and whose effective interval contains the logical clock date, where the effective interval begins at the assignment's `start_date` inclusive and ends — exclusively — at `released_on` when the assignment is `RELEASED` and at `end_date` otherwise. Because both of those right-hand bounds are exclusive, an assignment whose `end_date` is exactly today contributes 0.00 today. The sum is rounded half-up to two decimals and is 0.00 when nothing qualifies.

A `ResourceManager` may later update a consultant's `cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, `standard_bill_rate_usd_per_hour`, `travel_max_nights_per_week`, `travel_willing_international`, `development_goal_skill_id`, `industry_experience`, `home_city` or `home_geo_code`, subject to the same geography-coherence rule. Every such update increments the consultant's `version` by one, which invalidates any `expected_consultant_version` a client captured beforehand. What the update does *not* do is rewrite history: a new `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` does not re-price any existing assignment, because each assignment snapshots the cost rate onto itself at creation, and it does not re-score any stored `match_score`. Consultants can be listed filtered by `practice_group_id`, by `development_goal_skill_id` — those two being the reverse-reachability filters for the consultant's two skill and group links — by `availability_status`, and by `level`.

**Skill assertions** are the many-to-many junction between a consultant and the taxonomy, and they are the only evidence the staffing gates accept. An assertion is created by a `ResourceManager`, or by a `Consultant` acting on their own record, and names a `consultant_id`, a `skill_id`, and a `proficiency_level` integer from 1 (aware) to 5 (authority). It may optionally carry a `certification_code` of 1 to 40 characters naming the vendor certification that backs it, a `certification_expires_on` date on which that certification lapses, and a `last_used_on` date recording when the skill was last exercised on a client engagement, which is purely informational. Both the code and the expiry default to absent, and `certification_expires_on` must be absent whenever `certification_code` is absent — an expiry with nothing to expire is a malformed payload. An absent `certification_expires_on` on an assertion that *does* carry a code means the certification never expires. At most one assertion may exist for any consultant-and-skill pair; a second is an `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. Asserting against a consultant whose `availability_status` is `SEPARATED` is an `ERR_CONSULTANT_SEPARATED`, and a `Consultant`-role caller naming somebody else's `consultant_id` gets `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Each assertion carries a derived `assertion_status` of either `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`. It is `ACTIVE` when there is no `certification_code` at all, or when `certification_expires_on` falls on or after the logical clock date; it is `EXPIRED` otherwise. Because the comparison is against the clock, an assertion can flip from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` with nothing more than a clock advance, and a pending confirmation that depended on it then fails with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Holding an `ACTIVE` assertion makes a consultant eligible for demands whose `min_proficiency_level` is at or below the asserted `proficiency_level`, and — when the assertion is certification-backed and still `ACTIVE` — earns them a certification bonus in the match scoring of §3.5. A `ResourceManager` or the owning `Consultant` may revise the `proficiency_level`, attach or renew the `certification_code` and `certification_expires_on`, or record a new `last_used_on`; the revision re-derives `assertion_status` but never re-scores any stored `match_score` on any assignment. Assertions can be listed filtered by `consultant_id`, `skill_id` and `assertion_status`, and that listing is the only way to observe the assertions that a graduating reskilling cohort creates on the system's own initiative.

### 3.3. The Demand Pipeline: Registration, Qualification and Closure

A `SalesLead` registers a **demand opportunity** for each piece of client work the firm is pursuing. The commercial and delivery shape of the deal is fixed at registration: a `client_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `practice_group_id` of the group that will own delivery, the `required_skill_id` of the single skill the staffing decision will be matched on, a `min_proficiency_level` from 1 to 5, a `required_fte` between 0.25 and 20.00 expressing how many full-time-equivalents the client needs, a `win_probability_pct` integer from 0 to 100 supplied by sales, a `start_date` and `end_date` bounding the inclusive delivery window, an `industry_code` following the same uppercase-code pattern as a skill code, an `onsite_nights_per_week` integer from 0 to 5, an optional `is_international` boolean defaulting to false, a `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` between 1.00 and 5000.00 which is the firm's list rate and the denominator of every realization figure computed anywhere in the system, and a `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` between 0.01 and 5000.00 which is the rate sales actually wants to quote. Naming a skill whose `retired` flag is true is `ERR_SKILL_RETIRED`, and a `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` above the `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` is `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_STANDARD` — a premium is not a discount and the system will not pretend otherwise.

A demand carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**, and keeping them straight is the single most important structural fact about this entity. The sales dimension is `pipeline_status`, which begins at `QUALIFYING` and runs to one of the terminal values `CLOSED_WON` or `CLOSED_LOST`. The coverage dimension is `staffing_status`, which begins at `UNSTAFFED` and is described in §3.4. A newly registered demand therefore has `pipeline_status` at `QUALIFYING`, `staffing_status` at `UNSTAFFED`, and `staffing_mode` unset. At registration `weighted_fte`, `open_fte` and `booked_realization_pct` are computed, while `earned_realization_pct` and `blended_margin_pct` are both absent because nothing has been staffed or delivered, `approved_rate_usd_per_hour` is absent because no discount has been approved, `margin_breach` is false, and `closed_outcome` is absent while the demand is open.

**Qualification** by a `SalesLead` advances `pipeline_status` from `QUALIFYING` to `QUALIFIED`. It requires the demand to currently be `QUALIFYING`, requires `win_probability_pct` to be at least 25, and requires the demand's required skill to still have `retired` false. Qualification recomputes `weighted_fte` as `required_fte` multiplied by `win_probability_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals — the staffing-priority weight that sales visibility exposes to everyone downstream — and increments the demand's `version` by one. Two scoping consequences follow, and both are invisible in the response: the demand now enters the hiring-forecast scope, because only `QUALIFIED` demands are counted there, and it enters the denominator of its practice group's external-sourcing cap. Unqualified demand is deliberately invisible to both staffing and forecasting; qualification is the input-set filter for the whole forward-looking half of this system. Attempting to qualify a demand that is not `QUALIFYING` is `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`.

**Closure** by a `SalesLead` supplies an `outcome` of either `WON` or `LOST` and an optional free-text `note`, and it is where the two dimensions rejoin. Closing `WON` requires `pipeline_status` to be `QUALIFIED`, requires `staffing_status` to have reached `COVERED` — which is exactly the condition that `open_fte` has fallen to 0.00 — and requires `margin_breach` to be false. It sets `pipeline_status` to `CLOSED_WON` and `closed_outcome` to `WON`, and then it activates whichever branch the demand chose: on the internal branch every assignment on the demand whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` becomes `ACTIVE` with `activated_on` stamped from the logical clock date, and on the external branch every placement on the demand whose `status` is `APPROVED` becomes `ACTIVE`. Neither branch ever touches the other's rows. The demand's `version` increments by one.

Closing `LOST` is permitted from either `QUALIFIED` or `QUALIFYING`. From `QUALIFYING` it is a plain abandonment: `pipeline_status` becomes `CLOSED_LOST`, `closed_outcome` becomes `LOST`, and `version` increments — there is nothing staffed to unwind. From `QUALIFIED` the same two status writes happen and then the branch is unwound: on the internal branch every assignment on the demand whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED`, and every assignment whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `RELEASED` with `released_on` stamped from the logical clock date; on the external branch every placement whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `TERMINATED` with `terminated_on` stamped. Every newly released or cancelled assignment runs the full release cascade documented in §3.5 — allocation, then availability, then bench spell, then the group's bench ratio, then the ancestor chain of postures — and every terminated placement frees the firm's concurrency slot for its interval. Coverage is then recomputed: `covered_internal_fte`, `covered_external_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status` all settle to their new values, and `version` increments by one. Either outcome removes the demand from the hiring-forecast scope, because both closed pipeline states are out of scope there.

Note what closure `WON` cannot do. A demand still at `QUALIFYING` can never be closed `WON`, because the join condition requires `COVERED`, reaching `COVERED` requires coverage, and coverage requires a staffing branch to have been selected — and branch selection itself requires `QUALIFIED`. Demands can be listed filtered by `practice_group_id`, `required_skill_id`, `pipeline_status`, `staffing_status` and `staffing_mode`, and reading one demand is how a client obtains the `version` that an optimistic-concurrency write must quote.

### 3.4. Choosing a Staffing Branch and Tracking Coverage

Once a demand is `QUALIFIED`, a `ResourceManager` selects the **staffing branch** by supplying a `staffing_mode` of either `INTERNAL` or `EXTERNAL`. The selection is made once and is never revisited: it requires `pipeline_status` to be `QUALIFIED` and requires `staffing_mode` to still be unset, and a demand that is not `QUALIFIED` — whether still `QUALIFYING` or already closed — or one whose branch is already chosen is rejected with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`. Selecting `INTERNAL` moves `staffing_status` from `UNSTAFFED` to `INTERNAL_STAFFING`; selecting `EXTERNAL` moves it from `UNSTAFFED` to `EXTERNAL_SOURCING`. Either way the demand's `version` increments by one.

The branch is a hard fork in what the demand will accept afterwards. On an `INTERNAL` demand, proposing a named consultant and running the automatic selector both become permitted, while requesting a partner placement against it is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_MODE_MISMATCH`. On an `EXTERNAL` demand the mirror holds: partner placements become permitted, while proposing a consultant or running the selector is refused with the same code. Before the branch is selected at all, every one of those three staffing actions is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_MODE_NOT_SET` — a distinct failure from the mismatch, because an unset branch is a sequencing mistake while a mismatched branch is a category mistake.

**Coverage** is recomputed on every assignment status change, every placement status change, and every write that affects the demand's `required_fte`. Three quantities are derived. `covered_internal_fte` is the sum of `allocation_pct` over the demand's assignments whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE`, divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimals. `covered_external_fte` is the sum of `fte` over the demand's placements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, rounded half-up to two decimals. `open_fte` is `required_fte` less `covered_internal_fte` less `covered_external_fte`, floored at 0.00 so it never goes negative, rounded half-up to two decimals. Assignments that are merely `PROPOSED` and placements that are merely `REQUESTED` contribute nothing to any of the three — which is exactly why proposing never moves `staffing_status`, and why releasing a commitment restores the previous value precisely.

The `staffing_status` value then follows from those three quantities together with the branch. It is `COVERED` when `open_fte` is exactly 0.00 and the sum of `covered_internal_fte` and `covered_external_fte` is greater than 0.00. It is `PARTIALLY_COVERED` when `open_fte` is greater than 0.00 and that same sum is greater than 0.00. When no coverage exists at all, it falls back to the branch: `INTERNAL_STAFFING` when `staffing_mode` is `INTERNAL`, `EXTERNAL_SOURCING` when `staffing_mode` is `EXTERNAL`, and `UNSTAFFED` when no branch has been selected.

Because the value is derived rather than latched, the coverage dimension is **cyclic** — coverage can be lost and regained without `pipeline_status` moving at all — and every edge it can traverse is worth naming explicitly. Confirming an assignment on an internally staffed demand moves it from `INTERNAL_STAFFING` to `PARTIALLY_COVERED` when some coverage now exists but `open_fte` remains above 0.00, and straight from `INTERNAL_STAFFING` to `COVERED` when that single confirmation drives `open_fte` to 0.00 in one step. Approving a placement on an externally sourced demand does the same from `EXTERNAL_SOURCING`, reaching `PARTIALLY_COVERED` when `covered_external_fte` is above 0.00 with `open_fte` still positive, and `COVERED` when the approval covers the whole demand at once. Either channel's second commitment moves `PARTIALLY_COVERED` to `COVERED` when `open_fte` reaches 0.00, and that is the join gate a `WON` closure requires. In the other direction, releasing or cancelling an assignment, or terminating a placement, moves `COVERED` back to `PARTIALLY_COVERED` when `open_fte` rises above 0.00 while some coverage survives. When the *last* covering commitment goes away, the demand falls all the way back to its branch value: to `INTERNAL_STAFFING` on an internal demand and to `EXTERNAL_SOURCING` on an external one. That fall-back happens from `PARTIALLY_COVERED` when several commitments existed, and directly from `COVERED` when a single assignment or a single placement had covered the whole demand on its own — such a demand skips `PARTIALLY_COVERED` entirely on the way back.

### 3.5. Internal Staffing: Proposing, Scoring, Confirming and Unwinding Assignments

An **assignment** couples one consultant to one demand for a bounded interval, and a `ResourceManager` creates it by naming a `consultant_id`, a `demand_opportunity_id`, an `allocation_pct` between 1.00 and 100.00, a `start_date`, an `end_date` that must be strictly later than the start and is **exclusive** per §2.5, a `category` of either `BILLABLE` or `INVESTMENT`, and the `expected_consultant_version` demanded by §2.8. The row is created with `status` at `PROPOSED`, `confirmed_on`, `activated_on` and `released_on` all absent. Three values are computed once and fixed: `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` is snapshot from the consultant's current cost rate and never re-read afterwards; `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` is pinned to the demand's `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` when `category` is `BILLABLE` and is 0.00 when `category` is `INVESTMENT`; and `match_score` is computed and stored and never recomputed for the life of the row. Creating the proposal increments the consultant's `version` by one but changes no coverage and no allocation whatsoever — a `PROPOSED` assignment reserves nothing, which is precisely why this creation needs the stale-version guard instead of a state precondition.

The **match score** quantifies how good a fit a consultant is for a demand, on a 0.0000-to-1.0000 scale at four decimals. It is built from five weighted components plus a bonus, all evaluated against a single reference assertion: among that consultant's `ACTIVE` assertions for the demand's `required_skill_id`, the one with the highest `proficiency_level`, with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. The skill component is that assertion's `proficiency_level` divided by 5. The industry component is the `years` value from the entry of the consultant's `industry_experience` whose `industry_code` equals the demand's `industry_code` — or zero when the consultant has no entry for that industry — divided by three years of full credit and capped at 1.0000, so three years of industry exposure earns full marks and six years earns no more than three. The development-goal component is 1.0000 when the consultant's `development_goal_skill_id` is exactly the demand's `required_skill_id` and 0.0000 otherwise. The travel component is 0.0000 outright when the demand's `onsite_nights_per_week` exceeds the consultant's `travel_max_nights_per_week`, or when the demand's `is_international` is true while the consultant's `travel_willing_international` is false; otherwise it is one minus the demand's `onsite_nights_per_week` divided by five, so a fully remote engagement scores 1.0000 and a five-night-a-week engagement scores 0.0000. The cost component is the demand's `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` less the consultant's `cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, divided by that same standard rate, clamped below at 0.0000 and above at 1.0000 — the lower clamp meaning every consultant costing at or above the demand's standard rate scores identically here. Each of the five components is rounded half-up to four decimals.

The components are then combined at fixed weights: the skill component at 0.35, the industry component at 0.15, the development-goal component at 0.15, the travel component at 0.20 and the cost component at 0.15 — five weights summing to exactly 1.00. To that sum a certification bonus of 0.10 is added whenever the reference assertion carries a `certification_code` and its `assertion_status` is `ACTIVE`, and nothing is added otherwise. The result is rounded half-up to four decimals and then capped at 1.0000, since the weighted sum plus a full bonus can reach 1.10 before capping. One override sits above all of that: the `match_score` is 0.0000 whenever the consultant's `availability_status` is `RESKILLING` or `SEPARATED`, regardless of every component. An `EXPIRED` assertion is never the reference assertion, so it contributes neither a skill component nor a certification bonus, and it also makes the consultant unconfirmable.

Rather than name a consultant, a `ResourceManager` may run the **automatic selector** over the demand's eligible bench. Eligibility is a conjunction of five filters, all of which must hold. First, the consultant's `practice_group_id` is either the demand's own `practice_group_id` or a transitive descendant of it. Second, the consultant's `availability_status` is `BENCH` or `INTERNAL_PROJECT` — a consultant parked on an internal project remains a candidate, while one mid-reskilling does not. Third, the consultant holds an assertion for the demand's `required_skill_id` whose `assertion_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `proficiency_level` is at least the demand's `min_proficiency_level`. Fourth, that consultant's travel component for this demand is strictly greater than 0.0000. Fifth, they have headroom: taking the maximum, over every date in the demand's inclusive delivery window, of the summed `allocation_pct` of that consultant's `CONFIRMED` and `ACTIVE` assignments whose half-open interval contains the date, one hundred less that peak must be at least 100.00 — which is to say the selector only takes a consultant who is entirely free for the whole window, since the assignment it creates commits the full 100.00.

Candidates are then ranked in this exact order: highest `match_score` first; among ties, lowest `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` first — a tie-break that is genuinely reachable because the cost component clamps at 0.0000, so two expensive consultants can score identically and still differ in cost; among still-tied candidates, the longest-benched first, meaning the greatest `age_days` on their open bench spell; and finally, as the sole last resort, the earliest `created_at`. All comparisons use the stored four-decimal score and the stored two-decimal cost rate exactly as stored, with no tolerance of any kind. The winner receives one new assignment with `status` at `PROPOSED`, `allocation_pct` of 100.00, `category` of `BILLABLE`, `start_date` equal to the demand's `start_date`, `end_date` equal to the day *after* the demand's `end_date` — because the demand's window is inclusive on the right while the assignment's is exclusive — and the winner's `match_score` stored on the row. If the candidate set is empty the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONSULTANT` and **nothing at all is mutated**: no assignment row, no `version` bump on the demand or on any consultant, and no change to coverage or `staffing_status`.

**Confirmation** by a `ResourceManager` is where every staffing gate fires, and it is the single most heavily guarded operation in the system. The assignment must be `PROPOSED`. Its demand must be `QUALIFIED` with `staffing_mode` of `INTERNAL`. The consultant's `availability_status` must be one of `ASSIGNED`, `BENCH` or `INTERNAL_PROJECT`; `SEPARATED` is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_SEPARATED` and `RESKILLING` with `ERR_CONSULTANT_IN_RESKILLING`. The consultant must hold an assertion for the demand's `required_skill_id` whose `assertion_status` is `ACTIVE` — where an assertion exists but has lapsed the refusal is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, and where no assertion exists at all it is `ERR_PROFICIENCY_INSUFFICIENT` — and that assertion's `proficiency_level` must be at least the demand's `min_proficiency_level`. Travel must fit, on the same two conditions the travel component uses, else `ERR_TRAVEL_CONSTRAINT_VIOLATED`. When `category` is `INVESTMENT`, the demand's practice group `posture` must not be `FROZEN`. Allocation must fit: for every date in the assignment's half-open interval, the summed `allocation_pct` of the consultant's *other* `CONFIRMED` and `ACTIVE` assignments covering that date plus this assignment's own `allocation_pct` must be at most 100.00, with exactly 100.00 permitted, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. And when `category` is `BILLABLE`, the margin must hold: one hundred multiplied by the demand's `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` less this row's `cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, divided by that effective rate, rounded half-up to two decimals, must be at least 20.00 percent, else `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`. §2.9 fixes which of those refusals wins when several apply at once.

A successful confirmation sets `status` to `CONFIRMED` and stamps `confirmed_on` with the logical clock date, and re-pins `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` from the demand's current `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` — or to 0.00 when the category is `INVESTMENT`. It then recomputes the consultant's `allocated_pct`, and when that value reaches 50.00 or more it sets `availability_status` to `ASSIGNED` and **closes the consultant's open bench spell**: any spell not already `CLOSED` or `SEPARATED` moves to `CLOSED` with `ended_on` stamped from the clock date, its `age_days` frozen at that point, and — when the spell had been in `RESKILLING` or `INTERNAL_PROJECT` — its `investment_ended_on` stamped too. Closing the spell decrements the group's `bench_headcount`, and decrements `escalated_l2_count` as well when the spell had been `ESCALATED_L2` or `SEPARATION_PROPOSED`, which recomputes `own_bench_ratio` and `rollup_bench_ratio` for the group and every ancestor and re-derives each of their postures. Confirmation additionally recomputes the demand's `covered_internal_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status`, and its `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, `earned_realization_pct`, `blended_margin_pct` and `margin_breach`. Every `OPEN` utilization period of the consultant that intersects the assignment interval has its hour buckets recomputed. Both the consultant's `version` and the demand's `version` increment by one. One consequence deserves naming: a spell sitting in `RESKILLING` can never be closed this way, because confirmation refuses a reskilling consultant outright.

The operations checkpoint of §3.9 later moves a `CONFIRMED` assignment whose `start_date` has arrived to `ACTIVE`, stamping `activated_on` with the clock date, and a `WON` closure does the same for every `CONFIRMED` assignment on the demand. Activation recomputes the demand's `earned_realization_pct`, since `ACTIVE` assignments enter the delivered-hours denominator, and recomputes every intersecting `OPEN` utilization period.

**Release** by a `ResourceManager`, optionally carrying a free-text `reason`, ends a commitment early, and is permitted from `ACTIVE` or from `CONFIRMED`. From either state it requires the logical clock date to be on or after the assignment's `start_date`; releasing something that has not started yet is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, because an unstarted commitment is cancelled rather than released. The effects are identical from both states, and they form the six-hop cascade this system is built around. The row's `status` becomes `RELEASED` and `released_on` is stamped from the clock date, becoming the exclusive effective end for every hour computation from then on, and `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` freezes at its current value so later rate changes on the demand no longer touch this row. Hop one to two: the consultant's `allocated_pct` is recomputed. Hop two to three: because that value has fallen below 50.00, the consultant's `availability_status` becomes `BENCH`. Hop three to four: because the consultant is now on the bench and holds no open spell, a new bench spell row is created in state `MONITORING` with `started_on` stamped from the clock date and `escalation_count` at 0. Hop four to five: because that spell now exists, the group's `bench_headcount` increments and `own_bench_ratio` and `rollup_bench_ratio` are recomputed for the group and every ancestor. Hop five to six: because `rollup_bench_ratio` may have crossed a breakpoint, `posture` is re-derived for the group and every ancestor — moving from `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED` at a ratio of 0.3000 or more and to `FROZEN` at 0.5000 or more. Beyond the cascade, the demand's `covered_internal_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status` are recomputed along with `earned_realization_pct` and `blended_margin_pct`, and every `OPEN` utilization period of the consultant intersecting the interval is recomputed — where `billable_hours` falls, `bench_hours` rises by exactly the same amount, conserving `capacity_hours` penny for penny. The assignment's `version` increments, and so do the consultant's and the demand's.

**Cancellation** by a `ResourceManager`, also accepting an optional `reason`, is permitted from `PROPOSED` and from `CONFIRMED`, and the two cases differ sharply. Cancelling a `PROPOSED` row simply sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and increments the assignment's `version` and the consultant's `version`; the consultant's `allocated_pct` and `availability_status` are untouched because a proposal never reserved capacity, and the demand's coverage is untouched because a proposal never counted toward `covered_internal_fte`, so the demand's `version` does not move either. Cancelling a `CONFIRMED` row additionally requires the logical clock date to be strictly earlier than the assignment's `start_date` — a commitment that has already started must be released, not cancelled — and it is the compensating action that restores the FTE-coverage identity exactly: `allocated_pct` is recomputed and, if it falls below 50.00, the identical availability-to-bench-spell-to-bench-ratio-to-posture cascade described above runs unchanged; the demand's `covered_internal_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status` are recomputed along with realization and margin; and the demand's `version` increments as well. In both cases `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` freezes and the row contributes zero hours to every utilization bucket thereafter. A second confirmation, a release of an already-released row, or a cancellation of a released or cancelled row are all refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`; idempotency is not granted anywhere in this lifecycle. Assignments can be listed filtered by `consultant_id`, `demand_opportunity_id`, `status` and `category`, and reading one exposes its frozen `match_score` alongside its pinned bill and cost rates.

### 3.6. Partner Sourcing When the Bench Cannot Cover the Work

When internal capacity is short, the firm buys it. A `PracticePartner` registers a **subcontractor firm** with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters unique across all firms, a `geo_code` from the four-value geography enumeration naming the geography the firm supplies from, a `max_concurrent_placements` integer from 1 to 50 capping how many committed placements the firm may hold on any single calendar day, and a `pass_through_rate_usd_per_hour` between 0.01 and 5000.00 which is the cost rate the firm charges the consultancy. Two inputs are optional: `markup_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 defaulting to 15.00, which records the contractual markup the firm charges over its pass-through rate, and `approval_status` — the firm's master-data vetting state, one of `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, or `SUSPENDED` — which defaults to `APPROVED`. Only an `APPROVED` firm may take placements. A newly registered firm has an `active_placements_count` of 0; that count is always the number of the firm's placements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`. A duplicate `name` is an `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`.

A `PracticePartner` may later suspend or re-approve a firm by changing `approval_status`, or adjust `max_concurrent_placements`, `markup_pct` or `pass_through_rate_usd_per_hour`. Suspension blocks new placement requests and new approvals but leaves already-`APPROVED` and already-`ACTIVE` placements standing, and a new `pass_through_rate_usd_per_hour` never re-prices an existing placement, because that rate is snapshot onto the placement row. Firms can be listed filtered by `geo_code` and `approval_status`.

An **external placement** is requested by a `ResourceManager` against an externally sourced demand, naming a `subcontractor_firm_id`, a `demand_opportunity_id`, an `fte` between 0.25 and 10.00, and a `start_date` and `end_date` bounding a **closed** interval per §2.5 where the end must fall on or after the start. The request requires the demand to be `QUALIFIED` with `staffing_mode` of `EXTERNAL`, requires the firm's `approval_status` to be `APPROVED` and its `geo_code` to equal the demand's practice group's `geography_code` — a mismatch on either count being `ERR_FIRM_NOT_APPROVED` — and is refused when the demand's group `posture` is `FROZEN` with `ERR_PRACTICE_POSTURE_FROZEN`, or when that posture is `CONSTRAINED` and the demand's required skill has a `scarcity_index` below 0.7000 with `ERR_PRACTICE_POSTURE_CONSTRAINED`. The row is created with `status` at `REQUESTED`, `terminated_on` absent, `pass_through_rate_usd_per_hour` snapshot from the firm's current rate, and `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` at 0.00 — a merely requested placement has no billing rate yet. No coverage moves, because `REQUESTED` placements do not count toward `covered_external_fte`.

**Approval** is a `PracticePartner` decision and it is where the sourcing economics are actually policed. The placement must be `REQUESTED`. The firm's `approval_status` must still be `APPROVED`. The demand must still be `QUALIFIED` with `staffing_mode` of `EXTERNAL`. The group's `posture` must not be `FROZEN` — a frozen practice admits no carve-out of any kind — and where the posture is `CONSTRAINED` the approval proceeds only under the scarcity carve-out, meaning the demand's required skill must carry a `scarcity_index` of at least 0.7000. Then two ceilings are tested. The **firm concurrency ceiling**: for every single date in this placement's closed interval, the count of the firm's other placements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` and whose own closed interval contains that date, plus one for this placement, must be at most the firm's `max_concurrent_placements`, else `ERR_FIRM_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`. Because placement intervals are closed on both ends, a placement ending on a day D and one starting on that same day D *do* collide on D — the exact opposite of the assignment convention, and a distinction the concurrency count depends on. The **external sourcing ceiling**: take the sum of `fte` over all `APPROVED` and `ACTIVE` placements sitting on `QUALIFIED` demands of the demand's practice group, add this placement's `fte`, divide by the sum of `required_fte` over that group's `QUALIFIED` demands, round half-up to four decimals, and require the result to be at most the group's `external_sourcing_cap_pct` divided by one hundred, else `ERR_EXTERNAL_SOURCING_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

A successful approval sets `status` to `APPROVED` and sets `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` to the demand's current `effective_rate_usd_per_hour`. It recomputes the firm's `active_placements_count`, recomputes the demand's `covered_external_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status` — this is how a demand reaches `PARTIALLY_COVERED` and then `COVERED` on the external branch — and increments the demand's `version` by one. It also makes the placement's `fte` visible to the supply side of any hiring forecast generated afterwards for the demand's skill and geography. The operations checkpoint of §3.9 later moves an `APPROVED` placement whose `start_date` has arrived to `ACTIVE`, and a `WON` closure does the same for every `APPROVED` placement on the demand.

**Termination** by a `PracticePartner`, optionally carrying a `reason`, is permitted from `APPROVED` and from `ACTIVE`, with identical effects from both: `status` becomes `TERMINATED`, `terminated_on` is stamped from the logical clock date, the firm's `active_placements_count` is recomputed so the concurrency slot for that interval is freed, and the demand's `covered_external_fte`, `open_fte` and `staffing_status` are restored exactly to their pre-approval values — the compensating action that keeps the FTE-coverage identity exact. The demand's `version` increments by one. Approving anything other than a `REQUESTED` row, or terminating a `REQUESTED` or already-`TERMINATED` row, is `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Placements can be listed filtered by `subcontractor_firm_id`, `demand_opportunity_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Rate Realization and the Discount Approval Ladder

Every demand reports how much of its list price the firm is actually capturing, and it does so twice, from two different angles that are persisted side by side and are meant to be read as a reconciliation pair. Both are recomputed on every write that changes the demand's effective rate, its assignment mix, or any assignment's status.

The rate that governs a demand is its `effective_rate_usd_per_hour`: the `approved_rate_usd_per_hour` when a discount has been approved, and the `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` otherwise. The **booked** figure, `booked_realization_pct`, is one hundred multiplied by `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` divided by `standard_rate_usd_per_hour`, rounded half-up to two decimals. It is a pure price ratio: it is available from the moment the demand exists, it is never absent, and it is entirely unaffected by assignment category or by hours delivered.

The **earned** figure is hours-weighted and therefore needs delivery history. For each assignment, the delivered workdays are the Monday-to-Friday dates from its `start_date` inclusive up to — but excluding — the earlier of its effective end and the day after the logical clock date, where the effective end is `released_on` when the assignment is `RELEASED` and `end_date` otherwise. Future-dated days are not delivered. That workday count multiplied by `allocation_pct` divided by one hundred and again by eight hours gives that assignment's delivered hours, and the demand's total delivered hours is the sum of those over its assignments whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ACTIVE` or `RELEASED`; `PROPOSED` and `CANCELLED` rows are excluded entirely. When the total is zero, `earned_realization_pct` is absent. Otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by the sum over those assignments of `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` times that assignment's delivered hours, divided by the product of `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` and the total delivered hours, rounded half-up to two decimals — and the ratio is rounded exactly once at the end, never per assignment. Because `INVESTMENT` assignments carry a `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` of 0.00 while still contributing their hours to the denominator, every investment hour drags the earned figure down while leaving the booked figure untouched. Delivery consisting only of `BILLABLE` work makes the earned figure equal the booked figure; any investment hours push it strictly below.

Two further derived fields on the demand follow from the same delivered-hours weighting. The `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour` is 0.00 when no committed hours exist, and otherwise the sum over those assignments of `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` times delivered hours divided by the total delivered hours, rounded half-up to two decimals. The `blended_margin_pct` is absent whenever `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour` is 0.00 — nothing is staffed, so there is no margin to report — and is otherwise one hundred multiplied by `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` less `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, divided by that effective rate, rounded half-up to two decimals. The `margin_breach` flag is true exactly when `blended_margin_pct` is present and below the firm's 20.00 percent margin floor, and false otherwise — including when the margin is absent. A demand carrying `margin_breach` true cannot be closed `WON`.

When sales wants to quote below list, a `SalesLead` files a **discount approval** naming the `demand_opportunity_id`, the `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` they want blessed, the `expected_opportunity_version` demanded by §2.8, and an optional `decision_note` of 1 to 280 characters. A requested rate above the demand's `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` is `ERR_RATE_ABOVE_STANDARD`. Creation stamps `requested_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, sets `revision_count` to 0, leaves `decided_by_user_id` absent, and increments the demand's `version` by one. It then runs a **single ordered pass of seven steps**. No step re-reads a value that a later step writes, and the pass is never iterated to a fixed point.

The first step snapshots the demand's `standard_rate_usd_per_hour` onto the approval row and computes `discount_pct` as one hundred multiplied by the snapshot standard rate less the requested rate, divided by that snapshot standard rate, rounded half-up to two decimals. The second step computes `provisional_realization_pct` as one hundred multiplied by the requested rate divided by the snapshot standard rate, rounded half-up to two decimals — the mirror image of the discount — and that value is never recomputed by any later step even when a later step rewrites the tier. The third step computes `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour` under one of two variants, chosen purely by whether the demand has any assignment whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE` at the moment this step runs. Where at least one such assignment exists, the **mix-weighted** variant applies: the sum over those assignments of `cost_rate_usd_per_hour` times delivered hours, divided by the sum of those delivered hours, rounded half-up to two decimals — falling back to weighting each assignment by its `allocation_pct` when no workday has yet been delivered. This is the real staffed cost, and it can sit far above any policy floor. Where no such assignment exists, the **level-floor** variant applies: a policy proxy indexed by the demand's `min_proficiency_level`, being 45.00 at level 1, 60.00 at level 2, 80.00 at level 3, 105.00 at level 4 and 130.00 at level 5. Because the proxy is a policy number rather than an observed cost, the very same requested rate can clear the margin floor before anyone is staffed and fail it afterwards.

The fourth step computes `projected_margin_pct` as one hundred multiplied by the requested rate less `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, divided by that requested rate, rounded half-up to two decimals. The fifth step routes the approval to a tier by `discount_pct` alone: `NONE` when the discount is at most 5.00 percent, `L1_PRACTICE_PARTNER` when it is above 5.00 and at most 15.00 percent, and `L2_FINANCE` when it is above 15.00 percent. Because the comparison uses the already-rounded `discount_pct`, a raw discount of 5.004 percent rounds to 5.00 and stays in tier `NONE`. The sixth step is a fiscal override: when `projected_margin_pct` is below the 20.00 percent margin floor and `required_tier` is not already `L2_FINANCE`, the tier is rewritten to `L2_FINANCE` and `tier_escalated` is set to true; otherwise `tier_escalated` stays false. The seventh step decides the row's `status`: when `required_tier` came out of the sixth step as `NONE`, the approval is created already `APPROVED` with `decided_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM_AUTO` and the demand's full rate cascade is applied immediately; in every other case the row is created `PENDING`.

The ordering of those last two steps is the whole point of the design. A four-percent discount that projects a 16.67 percent margin is escalated to `L2_FINANCE` by the sixth step and therefore does **not** auto-approve at the seventh, even though its discount alone would have needed no approval whatsoever. Independently of tiering, a computed `discount_pct` above 35.00 percent is refused outright with `ERR_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED` — no role in this firm may approve past the hard cap, so the request is turned away rather than routed anywhere.

**Deciding** a pending approval is done by a `PracticePartner` or a `FinanceController`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `decision_note`. The row must be `PENDING`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`; the caller's role must satisfy `required_tier` per §2.11; and the stored `discount_pct` must still be within the 35.00 percent hard cap. A rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED` and stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and pointedly leaves the demand's `approved_rate_usd_per_hour` alone — the demand carries on quoting its `requested_rate_usd_per_hour`. An approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, which is terminal, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and then runs a four-hop rate cascade. Hop one to two: the demand's `approved_rate_usd_per_hour` is set to the approval's `requested_rate_usd_per_hour`, so the demand's `effective_rate_usd_per_hour` and `booked_realization_pct` are recomputed. Hop two to three: because the effective rate changed, every assignment on the demand whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE` and whose `category` is `BILLABLE` has its `bill_rate_usd_per_hour` re-pinned to the new effective rate, while `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` rows keep their frozen values. Hop three to four: because those bill rates changed, the demand's `earned_realization_pct`, `blended_margin_pct` and `margin_breach` are recomputed from the re-priced rows. The demand's `version` increments by one on approval. Any *other* discount approval on the same demand that is still `PENDING` is deliberately left untouched — each must be decided or revised on its own terms, and each will find a changed demand when it is.

**Revision** returns a rejected approval to the negotiation table and is a `SalesLead` action, supplying a new `requested_rate_usd_per_hour` and an optional `decision_note`. The row must be `REJECTED`, and the new rate must be strictly greater than the rate currently stored on the row — a revision must give ground on price — else `ERR_REVISION_NOT_HIGHER`. It must also still sit at or below the demand's `standard_rate_usd_per_hour`. A successful revision sets `status` back to `PENDING`, increments `revision_count` by one, clears `decided_by_user_id`, and re-stamps `requested_by_user_id` with the reviser's `X-User-ID` — so the identity barred from deciding follows the negotiation. The first six steps of the pass are then re-run from scratch against the new rate and the **current** assignment mix, so `discount_pct`, `provisional_realization_pct`, `blended_cost_rate_usd_per_hour`, `projected_margin_pct`, `required_tier` and `tier_escalated` may all change. In particular the third step may switch from the level-floor variant to the mix-weighted variant if an assignment was confirmed in the meantime, which can move `required_tier` even though the requested rate went up. This loop between `PENDING` and `REJECTED` may repeat indefinitely, and `revision_count` records how many rounds it took. Deciding a row that is not `PENDING`, or revising one that is `PENDING` or already `APPROVED`, is `ERR_APPROVAL_STATE_INVALID`. Approvals can be listed filtered by `demand_opportunity_id`, `status` and `required_tier`.

### 3.8. The Bench Spell: Aging, Reskilling, Internal Projects and Separation

A **bench spell** is the row that tracks one continuous stretch of a consultant's unsold time, and it is the spine of the whole system. It is never created or deleted directly: it appears as a cascade of hiring a consultant and of releasing the last assignment that kept them busy, and it is closed as a cascade of a confirmation that takes committed allocation to at least 50.00 percent. It records the `consultant_id` it belongs to, the `started_on` date stamped from the logical clock when it opened, an `ended_on` date that stays absent while the spell is open, a `state`, an `escalation_count` beginning at 0, an `age_days` count, an `age_reset_on` date, a `reskilling_program_id`, an `internal_project_code` of 1 to 40 characters when present, an `investment_started_on` and `investment_ended_on` pair bounding the investment-hours window, and the three separation fields `separation_proposed_by_user_id`, `separation_decided_by_user_id` and `separation_decision`. A consultant has at most one spell whose `state` is not `CLOSED` and not `SEPARATED`; that is the open spell every other rule means when it says "the consultant's open spell". Spells are read as a list filtered by `consultant_id`, `state` and `reskilling_program_id`, or one at a time; there is no way to create one by hand.

The spell's age is the whole number of calendar days from the later of `started_on` and `age_reset_on` — that is, from `age_reset_on` when one has been stamped and from `started_on` otherwise — up to the logical clock date while the spell is open, and it freezes at the same difference measured to `ended_on` once the spell is closed. There is no partial-day arithmetic anywhere in the ladder.

The **aging ladder** is driven only by the operations checkpoint, never by moving the clock. At each checkpoint every open spell is evaluated in ascending order of `started_on` and, for ties, of `created_at`. A spell in `MONITORING` whose `age_days` has reached the level-one escalation threshold for its consultant's level moves to `ESCALATED_L1` and its `escalation_count` increases by one; a spell in `ESCALATED_L1` whose `age_days` has reached the level-two threshold moves to `ESCALATED_L2` and its `escalation_count` increases by one again. Both thresholds are inclusive, so the threshold day itself escalates. The thresholds come from `bench_escalation_l1_days` and `bench_escalation_l2_days` resolved through the per-key `level_targets` fallback of §2.10 against the consultant's `level`. Crucially, **a single checkpoint advances a spell by at most one rung**: a spell sitting in `MONITORING` with an age already past the level-two threshold still lands only in `ESCALATED_L1`, so reaching `ESCALATED_L2` always takes two separate checkpoints no matter how far the clock has been moved. Spells in `RESKILLING`, `INTERNAL_PROJECT`, `SEPARATION_PROPOSED`, `CLOSED` and `SEPARATED` are skipped by the ladder entirely — they do not age. The second rung has a posture consequence: each move into `ESCALATED_L2` increments `escalated_l2_count` for the consultant's group and every ancestor, which can flip a group to `FROZEN` on the count arm alone with no bench ratio anywhere near the frozen threshold. `MONITORING` is the only state from which nothing but the ladder and a confirmation can move a spell: enrolment and internal-project placement both refuse a `MONITORING` spell with `ERR_BENCH_SPELL_STATE_INVALID`, because the firm does not spend money on a consultant who has only just come off a project.

**Reskilling** starts with a `ResourceManager` opening a cohort for a non-retired `skill_id` — a retired skill is `ERR_SKILL_RETIRED` — at a `target_proficiency_level` from 1 to 5 that every graduate will be asserted at, over an inclusive window from `cohort_start_on` to `cohort_end_on` where the end is on or after the start, with a `seat_capacity` from 1 to 50 and an optional `cooldown_days` from 0 to 30 defaulting to 3. The cohort is created `PLANNED` with `seats_taken` at 0, where `seats_taken` is always the count of spells currently enrolled in it. The operations checkpoint moves a `PLANNED` cohort whose `cohort_start_on` has arrived to `RUNNING`. Programmes are listed filtered by `skill_id` and `status`.

**Enrolment** is a `ResourceManager` action naming a `consultant_id`, and it returns the consultant's bench spell rather than the programme, because the spell is the row that changes state. Four gates apply beyond the spell's own state being `ESCALATED_L1` or `ESCALATED_L2`: the programme must be `PLANNED` or `RUNNING`, so a `COMPLETED` cohort takes nobody, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`; `seats_taken` must be strictly below `seat_capacity`, else `ERR_COHORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the cohort's **closed** window must intersect no `CONFIRMED` or `ACTIVE` assignment of this consultant on that assignment's **half-open** interval, else `ERR_COHORT_CONFLICTS_WITH_ASSIGNMENT` — the two interval conventions meeting head-on means an assignment ending on the cohort's first day does *not* clash, while one starting on the cohort's last day does; and where this consultant has a previous `COMPLETED` programme, this cohort's `cohort_start_on` must be strictly later than that programme's `cohort_end_on` advanced by the **new** programme's `cooldown_days`, else `ERR_RESKILLING_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. Note which cooldown governs: the waiting period is a property of the cohort being joined, not of the one just finished. A separated consultant may not be enrolled at all, which is `ERR_CONSULTANT_SEPARATED`.

A successful enrolment sets the spell's `state` to `RESKILLING`, stamps `reskilling_program_id` and stamps `investment_started_on` from the logical clock date, recomputes the programme's `seats_taken` upward by one, and sets the consultant's `availability_status` to `RESKILLING` — which removes them from every auto-staff candidate pool and forces their match score to 0.0000. Because `bench_headcount` counts `BENCH` and `RESKILLING` alike, the consultant stays in the bench population and neither bench ratio nor posture moves; but where the spell was in `ESCALATED_L2`, `escalated_l2_count` decrements for the group and every ancestor, which can *relax* a posture from `FROZEN`. Every `OPEN` utilization period intersecting the newly opened investment window is recomputed, moving hours out of `bench_hours` and into `investment_hours` by exactly the same amount.

**Graduation** is a `ResourceManager` completing the programme, which requires `status` to be `RUNNING` and `cohort_end_on` to have fallen on or before the logical clock date — completing a `PLANNED` cohort or completing one early is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The programme's `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, which is terminal, and `seats_taken` returns to 0. Then, for every spell enrolled in it, the spell's `state` returns to `MONITORING`, `age_reset_on` is stamped from the logical clock date so `age_days` falls back to 0 and the whole ladder can be climbed again, `investment_ended_on` is stamped as the exclusive end of the investment window, and the consultant's `availability_status` returns to `BENCH`. The `escalation_count` is deliberately **preserved** across the cycle: it is a lifetime counter of how many times this spell has escalated, not a measure of current age, and preserving it is what allows a consultant who has been reskilled once and drifts back onto the bench to become separation-eligible on a shorter path than a first-time bencher. Each graduate also gets a `SkillAssertion` for the programme's skill: a new row at `proficiency_level` equal to `target_proficiency_level` when none existed, or, where one already existed for that consultant and skill, its `proficiency_level` raised to whichever is greater of the existing level and the target — never lowered. That cascade-created or cascade-upgraded assertion is discoverable only by listing skill assertions, which is the intended reverse read.

An **internal project** is the other investment route: a `ResourceManager` parks an `ESCALATED_L1` or `ESCALATED_L2` spell on a non-client project by supplying an `internal_project_code`, which requires the consultant's `allocated_pct` to be strictly below 50.00. The spell's `state` becomes `INTERNAL_PROJECT`, the code is stored, `investment_started_on` is stamped, and the consultant's `availability_status` becomes `INTERNAL_PROJECT`. The two investment routes diverge sharply here. A reskilling consultant leaves the candidate pool but stays in `bench_headcount`; an internal-project consultant **stays** in the candidate pool — eligibility admits both `BENCH` and `INTERNAL_PROJECT` — but **leaves** `bench_headcount`, so the bench ratio drops and posture is re-derived for the group and every ancestor. Parking consultants on internal work therefore makes a practice look healthier, while reskilling them does not. As with enrolment, `escalated_l2_count` decrements when the spell was in `ESCALATED_L2`, and intersecting `OPEN` periods shift hours from `bench_hours` into `investment_hours`. The return is automatic: at the first operations checkpoint at which the whole number of days from `investment_started_on` to the logical clock date has reached 30, the spell returns to `MONITORING` with `age_reset_on` and `investment_ended_on` both stamped from the clock date, and the consultant's `availability_status` returns to `BENCH`, which puts them back into `bench_headcount` and re-derives the ratios and posture up the ancestor chain.

**Separation** closes the loop for a consultant the firm cannot re-employ. A `ResourceManager` files a proposal, optionally with a `rationale`, against a spell whose `state` is `ESCALATED_L2` and whose `escalation_count` is at least 2 — a spell that reached the second rung on a single pass through the ladder qualifies on that count, but one whose count was reset would not, and a shortfall is `ERR_SEPARATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The `state` becomes `SEPARATION_PROPOSED` and `separation_proposed_by_user_id` is stamped with the caller's `X-User-ID`. Because `escalated_l2_count` counts `ESCALATED_L2` and `SEPARATION_PROPOSED` together, posture does not move on proposal — the pressure the firm is under is unchanged by having filed paperwork.

The **decision** belongs to a `PracticePartner`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `note`. The spell must be `SEPARATION_PROPOSED`, and the decider's `X-User-ID` must differ from `separation_proposed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN`. An `APPROVED` decision additionally requires the consultant's `allocated_pct` to be exactly 0.00 — the firm will not separate someone still committed to client work — and a non-zero allocation is `ERR_SEPARATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. On approval the spell's `state` becomes `SEPARATED`, which is terminal, `separation_decision` is set to `APPROVED`, `separation_decided_by_user_id` is stamped, and `ended_on` is stamped from the clock date, freezing `age_days`. The consultant's `availability_status` becomes `SEPARATED` and `separated_on` is stamped with the logical clock instant. The consultant then leaves `total_headcount`, `bench_headcount` and `escalated_l2_count` all at once, so `own_bench_ratio`, `rollup_bench_ratio` and `posture` are re-derived for the group and every ancestor — and because the departure removes one from both the numerator and the denominator of the bench ratio, separating a benched consultant can leave a small group's ratio *higher* than before. They are also removed from every auto-staff candidate pool and from the internal supply of any forecast generated afterwards. A `REJECTED` decision instead returns the `state` to `ESCALATED_L2` with `escalation_count` untouched and `escalated_l2_count` unchanged, sets `separation_decision` to `REJECTED`, and stamps `separation_decided_by_user_id`. The spell is then back in the escalation cycle: it may be proposed again, enrolled in a cohort, parked on an internal project, or closed by a confirmation. Any write to a `CLOSED` or `SEPARATED` spell is `ERR_BENCH_SPELL_STATE_INVALID`; those two states accept nothing.

One asymmetry deserves stating plainly. A spell can be closed by a confirmation from `MONITORING`, `ESCALATED_L1`, `ESCALATED_L2` or `INTERNAL_PROJECT`, and when it was in `INTERNAL_PROJECT` the closure also stamps `investment_ended_on`. A spell in `RESKILLING` can never be closed that way at all, because a confirmation naming a consultant whose `availability_status` is `RESKILLING` is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_IN_RESKILLING`. A mid-cohort consultant is unstaffable, full stop.

### 3.9. Measurement: The Operations Checkpoint, Utilization Periods and Hiring Forecasts

The **operations checkpoint** is a `ResourceManager` action taking no input and returning no data, and it is the only time-driven mover in the system. Setting the logical clock re-derives every computed field that reads the clock — a consultant's `allocated_pct` and `availability_status`, an assertion's `assertion_status`, a spell's `age_days`, a demand's `earned_realization_pct`, a period's hour buckets — but it never advances a state machine. Only the checkpoint does that, and it does four things in one sweep. It runs the aging ladder over every open bench spell in ascending order of `started_on` and then `created_at`, advancing each by at most one rung and incrementing `escalated_l2_count` up the ancestor chain for each spell that reaches the second rung. It returns every `INTERNAL_PROJECT` spell whose investment window is 30 or more days old to `MONITORING` with the aging clock reset and the consultant back on the bench. It activates due commitments: every `CONFIRMED` assignment whose `start_date` has arrived becomes `ACTIVE` with `activated_on` stamped, and every `APPROVED` external placement whose `start_date` has arrived becomes `ACTIVE`. And it starts due cohorts: every `PLANNED` programme whose `cohort_start_on` has arrived becomes `RUNNING`. Nothing about the checkpoint is reported in its own response — its effects are observed by reading bench spells, assignments, programmes and practice groups afterwards.

A **utilization period** is how finance measures one consultant over one stretch of calendar. A `FinanceController` opens a period against a consultant with an inclusive `period_start_on` and `period_end_on` where the end is on or after the start, plus an optional `holiday_hours` between 0.00 and 2000.00 defaulting to 0.00 covering holiday and paid leave falling inside the period. Periods of a single consultant may not overlap on their **inclusive** ranges, and because both ends are inclusive a period starting on the very day a previous one ends *is* an overlap — that shared boundary day is `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A period may not be opened for a separated consultant, which is `ERR_CONSULTANT_SEPARATED`. On creation the row is `OPEN`, and `target_utilization_pct` is resolved once from the consultant's group `level_targets` for the consultant's `level` and **frozen** on the row, so a later change to the group's targets never moves it retroactively.

The period's arithmetic starts from `period_workdays`, the count of Monday-to-Friday dates in the inclusive range, from which `capacity_hours` is that count multiplied by eight, and `net_available_hours` is `capacity_hours` less `holiday_hours`. Four buckets then divide the calendar. The `billable_hours` bucket sums, over the consultant's assignments whose `category` is `BILLABLE` and whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ACTIVE` or `RELEASED`, the assignment's `allocation_pct` divided by one hundred, multiplied by its overlapping workdays, multiplied by eight — where the overlapping workdays are the Monday-to-Friday dates from the later of the assignment's `start_date` and `period_start_on`, up to but excluding the earlier of the assignment's effective end and the day after `period_end_on`, with the effective end being `released_on` for a `RELEASED` assignment and `end_date` otherwise. The `investment_hours` bucket sums the same expression over `INVESTMENT` assignments in those same three statuses, and adds the bench-spell contribution: for every spell of the consultant with an `investment_started_on`, the Monday-to-Friday dates from the later of `investment_started_on` and `period_start_on` up to but excluding the earlier of `investment_ended_on` — or the day after `period_end_on` when the window is still open — and the day after `period_end_on`, multiplied by eight. Reskilling and internal-project time is therefore investment even though no assignment exists. The `bench_hours` bucket is whichever is greater of 0.00 and `net_available_hours` less `billable_hours` less `investment_hours`. `PROPOSED` and `CANCELLED` assignments contribute zero hours to any bucket.

Those definitions exist to make one identity hold: whenever `hour_overallocated` is false, `billable_hours` plus `investment_hours` plus `bench_hours` plus `holiday_hours` equals `capacity_hours` exactly. Releasing an assignment never destroys hours — whatever `billable_hours` loses, `bench_hours` gains, to the penny. The `hour_overallocated` flag is true precisely when `billable_hours` plus `investment_hours` exceeds `net_available_hours`, which is possible because overlapping investment windows and assignment hours are not jointly capped, and it blocks the close. All of this is recomputed when the period is created, on every assignment confirmation, activation, release or cancellation that intersects the period while it is `OPEN`, on every change to a bench-spell investment window, and once more at close.

The period reports its utilization twice, under two denominators. The **available-basis** figure, `utilization_pct_available_basis`, is one hundred multiplied by `billable_hours` divided by `net_available_hours`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and is absent when `net_available_hours` is 0.00; it removes holiday from the denominator, which is right for managing a period in flight. The **target-basis** figure, `utilization_pct_target_basis`, is one hundred multiplied by `billable_hours` divided by `capacity_hours`, rounded half-up to two decimals; holiday and paid leave stay in the denominator and dilute the number, which is right for reporting against a firm-wide target. Both are persisted side by side, and `utilization_pct_of_record` is the available-basis figure while the period is `OPEN` and the target-basis figure once it is `CLOSED`. The `variance_pp` field is `utilization_pct_of_record` less the frozen `target_utilization_pct`, in percentage points, rounded half-up to two decimals.

Closing is a `FinanceController` action and it is where that switch bites. The period must be `OPEN` and its `period_end_on` must be strictly before the logical clock date — closing a period that is already `CLOSED`, or one that has not finished, is `ERR_PERIOD_STATE_INVALID` — and a period whose `hour_overallocated` is true cannot be closed at all, which is `ERR_HOUR_OVERALLOCATION`. On close the `status` becomes `CLOSED`, `utilization_pct_of_record` switches to the target-basis figure and `variance_pp` is recomputed, so **the reported percentage and the variance move without a single hour changing** — and whenever `holiday_hours` is above 0.00 the closed figure is strictly lower than the open one. The row then stops being recomputed: later confirmations and releases no longer touch its buckets. Periods are listed per consultant filtered by `status`, and a `Consultant`-role caller may list and read only their own.

A **hiring forecast** is an immutable snapshot cut by one skill and one geography. A `FinanceController` or `PracticePartner` generates one by supplying a `skill_code` that must match the `code` of an existing skill — a `skill_code` matching nothing is `ERR_GEOGRAPHY_MISMATCH` — a `geography_code` matched against practice groups' `geography_code`, an inclusive `horizon_start_on` and `horizon_end_on` where the end is on or after the start, and an optional `basis` of `WEIGHTED` or `COMMITTED` defaulting to `WEIGHTED`. The forecast is cut by code rather than by identifier so that a snapshot survives a skill being re-registered. The row is the sole exception to §2.4: it carries `id` and `created_at` plus a `generated_at` instant stamped from the logical clock, and it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because there is no endpoint that modifies it.

The forecast's scope is every demand whose `pipeline_status` is `QUALIFIED`, whose required skill's `code` equals the forecast's `skill_code`, whose practice group's `geography_code` equals the forecast's `geography_code`, whose `start_date` is on or before `horizon_end_on` and whose `end_date` is on or after `horizon_start_on`. `QUALIFYING` demand is invisible because sales has not committed to it, and both `CLOSED_WON` and `CLOSED_LOST` are out of scope — won demand is already covered, lost demand is gone.

The `weighted_demand_fte` figure is the sum over in-scope demands of each demand's contribution, and the contribution is **rounded to two decimals per demand before the sum**, the total then being rounded again. That per-demand rounding is normative and observable: three demands of 0.25 FTE each at a 33 percent win probability contribute 0.08 apiece for a total of 0.24, not the 0.25 that summing the raw products and rounding once would report. Under the `WEIGHTED` variant every in-scope demand contributes its `required_fte` multiplied by its `win_probability_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded to two decimals — exactly the value already stored in that demand's `weighted_fte` — so even long-tail demand moves the forecast a little. Under the `COMMITTED` variant, in-scope demands with a `win_probability_pct` below 70 are dropped entirely and contribute 0.00, while the survivors contribute their **full** `required_fte` rounded to two decimals rather than a probability-weighted share. `COMMITTED` can therefore report both less than `WEIGHTED`, because the tail is discarded, and more, because the survivors are counted whole — on identical data.

Supply is counted from two sources. The `internal_supply_fte` figure is the sum, over consultants whose practice group's `geography_code` equals the forecast's geography, whose `availability_status` is `BENCH` or `INTERNAL_PROJECT`, and who hold a skill assertion for the forecast's skill with `assertion_status` of `ACTIVE` at a `proficiency_level` of at least 3, of one hundred less that consultant's `allocated_pct`, divided by one hundred — that is, their unallocated fraction — rounded to two decimals. Consultants whose `availability_status` is `RESKILLING` are pointedly **not** supply: they are mid-programme and the assertion their cohort will produce does not exist yet. Separated consultants are not supply either, and neither are `ASSIGNED` consultants. The `external_supply_fte` figure is the sum of `fte` over external placements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` and whose demand is in scope, rounded to two decimals.

The `gap_fte` figure is `weighted_demand_fte` less `internal_supply_fte` less `external_supply_fte`, rounded to two decimals; a negative value means surplus. The `recommended_hires` figure is 0 whenever `gap_fte` is 0.00 or negative, and otherwise `gap_fte` divided by the 0.85 expected yield per hire, **rounded up to the next whole number** — half a recruit cannot be hired. A gap of 0.24 FTE therefore still recommends one hire, and a gap of 1.30 FTE recommends two, because 1.30 divided by 0.85 is 1.5294 and the ceiling of that is 2. Every figure is computed once at creation and frozen, and the stored `basis` tells any later reader which variant produced them. Forecasts are listed filtered by `skill_code`, `geography_code` and `basis`, and read one at a time; `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND` covers an unknown identifier.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are rooted at the server origin with no version or product prefix. Endpoints that create a resource return HTTP 201; every other successful call returns HTTP 200, including the state-transition endpoints that modify a resource, the two endpoints that return no data at all (the operations checkpoint and the clock setter, whose `data` is an empty object), and the auto-staff endpoint's sibling reads. Two endpoints are exceptions to the "creation returns 201" reading in one direction or the other: the demand auto-staff endpoint returns 201 because it creates an assignment, and the cohort-enrolment endpoint returns 200 because it changes an existing bench spell rather than creating anything. List endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` query parameters of §2.3 in addition to the filters named below.
