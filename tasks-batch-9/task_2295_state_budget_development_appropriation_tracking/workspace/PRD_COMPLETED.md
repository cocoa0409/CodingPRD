# State Budget Appropriation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state budget office sits at the junction of four institutions that do not share a ledger: the agencies that ask for money, the governor's office that recommends a spending plan, the legislature that appropriates it, and the controller that actually releases cash. Today that junction is a folder of spreadsheets. A revenue estimate is revised in one workbook while a reduction scenario is being priced from the previous one; a conference committee strikes a line item that three allotment schedules already assumed; a federal grant loses its state match because an unrelated transfer quietly drained the appropriation that had been pledged as match. Every one of these failures is a reconciliation failure, and every one of them is discovered months later, during audit.

This document specifies the State Budget Appropriation API, mounted under `/fiscal`, which replaces that folder with a single authoritative fiscal record. The API carries a budget cycle from the moment it is opened through agency request intake, executive recommendation and balance certification, legislative committee action and enactment, quarterly cash release under cash-flow constraints, mid-year amendment by transfer and emergency appropriation, expenditure posting, federal match and maintenance-of-effort tracking, and finally the reversion and carryforward sweep that closes the year. Because the system is a ledger, its central obligation is arithmetic honesty: every derived money figure is recomputed from its constituent rows on every mutation, never incremented in place, and every compensating action — a recall, a reversal, a void — must return the ledger exactly to the state it held before the action it compensates.

Two constraints shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that spending authority and cash are different things: a line item can be legally enacted and hold no cash, and the same expenditure request can pass the authority test while failing the cash test. The second is separation of duties: the official who prepares an allotment, proposes a transfer, or drafts an executive recommendation may never be the official who approves it, even when one person legitimately holds both roles. Engineers reading this document should treat both constraints as invariants rather than features.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level behaviour that holds across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, arithmetic discipline, the logical clock, phase gating, version pinning, separation of duties, cascade semantics, conservation invariants, and the fixed order in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting official, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `SystemAdmin`, `AgencyBudgetOfficer`, `BudgetAnalyst`, `BudgetDirector`, `LegislativeFiscalOfficer`, and `StateController`.

`SystemAdmin` is the statewide financial-system operator: it registers agencies and budget cycles and owns the logical clock. `AgencyBudgetOfficer` is the budget officer of exactly one agency. `BudgetAnalyst` is budget-office staff who review requests, record revenue forecasts and federal grants, build recommendations, reduction scenarios and allotments. `BudgetDirector` adopts forecasts, drives cycle phases, certifies balance, approves recommendations, allotments and transfers, and grants emergency appropriations; a director may also prepare work, in which case separation of duties blocks that same director from approving it. `LegislativeFiscalOfficer` introduces appropriation line items, records committee action, and enacts the bill. `StateController` releases and recalls cash, executes and reverses transfers, voids charges, freezes and unfreezes cash, and closes the fiscal year. Every role, including `AgencyBudgetOfficer`, may call every read endpoint.

Identity binding differs between roles and matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyBudgetOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency` — the value returned when that agency was registered — and ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's agency reference to `X-User-ID`. That comparison is transitive: an officer acting on an `AgencyRequest`, an `Appropriation`, a `DecisionPackage`, a `PerformanceMeasure`, an `Expenditure`, or a `MatchCommitment` is checked against the `agency_id` reached by following that row's chain to its owning agency, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `BudgetAnalyst`, `BudgetDirector`, `LegislativeFiscalOfficer`, or `StateController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `prepared_by_user_id` on an allotment or an executive recommendation, as `proposed_by_user_id` on a transfer, and as `approved_by_user_id` on all three — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.6 rather than from wall-clock time, so it repeats verbatim across every request taken at the same logical instant.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range from 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as is a value that is not an integer literal. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list endpoints return rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, ties on `created_at` being broken by server insertion order — because the logical clock stands still between operator moves, rows created within one clock position share a `created_at` exactly, so this tie-break decides most pages. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window. Filters behave uniformly across every listing: an enum-valued filter whose value is not a member of that enum, and a `quarter` filter outside 1 to 4, are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent; and an identifier-valued filter is matched literally without an existence check, so an id that names nothing yields an empty page rather than a 404.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Immutable Records

Every resource in the system is addressed by an `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; foreign-key inputs always name an `id` that the server previously minted.

Most resources — `BudgetCycle`, `Agency`, `RevenueForecast`, `AgencyRequest`, `DecisionPackage`, `ExecutiveRecommendation`, `Appropriation`, `Allotment`, `BudgetTransfer`, `Expenditure`, `FederalGrant`, and `MatchCommitment` — carry the full audit set:

- `id` (string): a unique, server-generated UUID.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only rewrite derived money fields.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. This is the value clients pin for optimistic concurrency control.

Three record types are deliberately thinner because they are either immutable or append-only, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations. `PerformanceMeasure` is immutable once written. `ReductionScenario` is a computed snapshot that is never revised; recomputing a scenario means creating a new one. `CommitteeAction` is append-only from the client's point of view: the server may rewrite its `running_balance_cents` during a replay, but the row exposes no `updated_at` and no `version`.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and the Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in the system is an integer number of cents. Field names ending in `_cents` are integers, are transported as JSON numbers, and are never fractional. Because they are integers, additions and subtractions of money are exact and require no rounding.

Rounding enters only where a percentage or an average is applied to money. Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero, so 50.5 cents becomes 51 cents. Two ratio fields are not money and are reported as decimal fractions rather than cents: `cash_utilization_pct` is rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and `moe_compliance_ratio` is rounded half up to 6 decimal places; both are transported as plain JSON numbers and both open at 0. Configuration percentages supplied by operators — `constitutional_reserve_pct`, `target_reduction_pct`, `required_match_ratio` — preserve the precision in which they were supplied.

The **order** of rounding is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, in three places. First, the constitutional reserve is rounded to the cent before it is subtracted from adopted revenue, never after. Second, in the allotment ceiling, the quarter cap and the projected cash figure are each rounded to the cent before they enter the minimum, never after. Third, at cycle close, the carryforward cap is rounded to the cent separately for each appropriation, and the cycle-level totals are the sums of those already-rounded per-appropriation values; summing authority across appropriations and rounding the percentage once at the end is wrong and can differ by a cent per row.

### 2.6. The Fiscal Calendar and the Logical Clock

All time-dependent behaviour reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until an operator moves it, that is the current instant, and the server never falls back to the host's real time. A `SystemAdmin` moves the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the new instant as `current_utc` in a `data` object carrying that single field. The instant may lie earlier than the current one; the clock is simply set to it. Moving the clock recomputes the current quarter of every budget cycle and, with it, each cycle's `projected_cumulative_cash_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture` — in practice it runs the full recomputation of §2.10 over every cycle in `created_at` order, so every derived appropriation figure and every `release_hold` settles again too.

A cycle's quarters are derived from `fiscal_year_start_date`, the first calendar day of quarter 1, supplied as an ISO 8601 date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Quarter *q* spans the half-open interval that begins three times (*q* minus one) months after the start date and ends three times *q* months after it: the start instant belongs to the quarter, the end instant belongs to the next one. Adding months to the start date clamps the day of month down to the last day of the shorter target month, so a year beginning on the 31st has quarter boundaries on the 30th where April, June, September or November is reached. The **current cycle quarter** is the quarter whose interval contains the logical clock instant. Two out-of-band values complete the derivation: while the clock stands before the start date the derived quarter is 0, and once the clock reaches or passes twelve months after the start date it is 5. Operations that name a quarter compare against that derived value: naming a quarter earlier than the current one is `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), and naming one later than the current one, where the operation forbids it, is `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — so before the fiscal year opens every quarter is not yet open, and after it has ended every quarter is closed. Where a quarter instead indexes one of the pinned constant tables — the allotment quarter cap and the cash-flow factor — the derived value is first clamped into the range 1 to 4, so a cycle whose year has not started prices cash flow at the quarter-1 factor and one whose year has ended prices it at the quarter-4 factor.

### 2.7. Cycle Phases, Flat Collections and Reference Resolution

Each `BudgetCycle` moves through six phases in a fixed order: `DEVELOPMENT`, `EXECUTIVE_PREP`, `LEGISLATIVE`, `ENACTED`, `EXECUTION`, `CLOSED`. A cycle opens in `DEVELOPMENT` and `CLOSED` is terminal. Phase advancement is strictly stepwise: the only legal `to_phase` is the immediate successor of the current phase, so skipping a phase, naming a predecessor, or naming the current phase again is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`; a `to_phase` that is not a member of the six-phase enumeration at all is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `CLOSED` is never a legal `to_phase` — a cycle reaches it only through the close endpoint — so advancing a cycle that already sits in `EXECUTION` is likewise `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`. The phase gates which operations are legal at all, and any write whose owning cycle is in a phase that does not permit it — including every write against a `CLOSED` cycle — is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The gate is narrower than `CLOSED` only where §3 names the permitted phases explicitly; every other write endpoint requires nothing of the phase beyond its not being `CLOSED`. In particular creating a revenue forecast, an agency request, a decision package, a performance measure, an executive recommendation, a reduction scenario, an appropriation line, a committee action, an allotment, a budget transfer, a federal grant or a match commitment, and beginning review, returning a request for revision, approving or releasing or recalling or cancelling an allotment, freezing or unfreezing cash, approving or rejecting or executing a transfer, posting an expenditure and assessing maintenance of effort, are all legal in any phase other than `CLOSED`.

The resource model is flat. Every entity is a peer collection under `/fiscal`, and parenthood is expressed through reference fields in request bodies rather than through nested ownership in URLs. Where a path does carry an identifier, the server validates identifiers left to right and rejects on the **first** identifier that resolves to nothing, returning that resource's own not-found code — for example a request naming a non-existent appropriation fails with `ERR_APPROPRIATION_NOT_FOUND` before any body field is examined. Once the path resolves, the references carried in the request body are resolved in the order in which the endpoint declares its inputs, and the first unresolvable reference determines the error: `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVENUE_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENCY_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROPRIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPENDITURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEDERAL_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_MATCH_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. `ERR_REVENUE_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND` also covers a `supersedes_forecast_id` that names nothing, and `ERR_DECISION_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` also covers a `depends_on_package_id` or any member of `recommended_package_ids` that names nothing.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Cycle, Request and Source Appropriation

Three creation endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a **parent** row's `version`. In each case the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and it must carry the `version` the client last observed on the named parent. If the value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

- `POST /fiscal/revenue-forecasts` requires `expected_budget_cycle_version` matching the current `version` of the `BudgetCycle` named by `budget_cycle_id`. Adopting a forecast rewrites the cycle's `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents` and `required_reserve_cents`, so a writer holding a stale cycle version would be estimating against a revenue base that no longer exists.
- `POST /fiscal/executive-recommendations` requires `expected_agency_request_version` matching the current `version` of the `AgencyRequest` named by `agency_request_id`. A new recommendation legitimately supersedes the prior one for that request, so a writer whose view of the request is stale would silently displace a recommendation it never saw.
- `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers` requires `expected_source_appropriation_version` matching the current `version` of the `Appropriation` named by `source_appropriation_id`. Executing another transfer, releasing an allotment, or posting an expenditure all move the source row's `available_authority_cents`, so a proposal drafted against the earlier figure would be approved on arithmetic that no longer holds.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Note that each of the three guarded creations also *advances* the pinned parent's `version` by 1 as a side effect, so a client creating two forecasts against the same cycle must re-read the cycle between them.

### 2.9. Separation of Duties

Three approval actions compare the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier recorded when the row was prepared or proposed, and reject the approval when they are equal, with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Approving an allotment compares against its `prepared_by_user_id`; approving an executive recommendation compares against its `prepared_by_user_id`; approving a budget transfer compares against its `proposed_by_user_id`. The check is on the identifier alone: holding a role that is authorised to approve does not exempt an official from it, which is precisely the case that arises when a `BudgetDirector` both prepares an allotment and later tries to approve it.

### 2.10. Cascading Recomputation of Derived Figures

No derived money field is ever incremented in place. Whenever a contributing row changes, the derived field is recomputed from the full set of contributing rows, and the recomputation propagates upward through the ownership chain until no further figure depends on the change. Each row touched by a cascade is a mutation of that row: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, even when the operation the client requested targeted a different resource. "Touched" means *actually moved*: a row whose recomputation lands on exactly the values it already held is not a mutation at all, and its `updated_at` and `version` are left alone — a cascade that reaches a row and changes nothing on it is invisible. The rows a transition names are the exception, and they are bumped by exactly 1 even when every recomputed value came out identical: the row the endpoint acts on, both appropriations of a transfer execution or reversal, the appropriation behind an enactment, a committee action, an expenditure posting or void, an allotment release or recall or cancellation, a grant under assessment, and the cycle row on forecast adoption, phase advancement, balance certification, recommendation approval, the supersession of an already-`APPROVED` recommendation, allotment release, allotment recall, an emergency grant and close. Regardless of how many derived figures a single request rewrites on one row, that row is bumped once, never once per figure.

Four cascade paths recur throughout §3 and are stated here once. Releasing or recalling cash rewrites the allotment, then the appropriation's `released_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and `cash_status`, then the cycle's `total_released_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`, and finally the `release_hold` flag on every `GENERAL`-fund appropriation of that cycle whose `cash_status` is `UNRELEASED`. Executing or reversing a transfer rewrites both appropriations' transfer figures and `available_authority_cents`, then may move `MatchCommitment` rows on the source between `COMMITTED` and `AT_RISK`, then recomputes the affected `FederalGrant` rows' `committed_match_cents` and `match_status`, then recomputes each owning `Agency`'s `federal_compliance_flag`. Posting or voiding an expenditure rewrites the appropriation's `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and `available_authority_cents`, and refreshes `moe_current_spend_cents` on every federal grant of the same agency and cycle. Closing a cycle sweeps every appropriation, releases its match commitments, and recomputes grant match status and agency compliance flags behind them.

Two properties of the recomputation machinery cut across all four paths. First, `release_hold` is not confined to the release and recall cascade: *every* recomputation of a cycle's derived figures rewrites `release_hold` on every appropriation of that cycle from the freshly computed posture, so enacting a line, recording a committee action, posting a charge, executing a transfer, adopting a forecast or moving the clock can each set or clear the flag as a side effect. Second, the cascades that stop at the appropriation genuinely leave the cycle row alone: enactment, committee replay, expenditure posting and voiding, and transfer execution and reversal write no field on the cycle and do not bump its `version`, so cycle-level rollups such as `total_expended_cents` stand at their previous value — 0 for a fresh cycle — until the next operation that does recompute the cycle row.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Four invariants are named because the compensating actions in this system exist to preserve them, and any implementation that satisfies the individual transitions but violates one of these has a defect.

**Released-authority conservation.** A cycle's `total_released_cents` equals the sum of `released_cents` over its appropriations, which equals the sum of `released_cents` over all allotments in status `RELEASED` whose appropriation belongs to that cycle. A release followed by the recall of that same allotment returns every one of those three figures to its pre-release value.

**Transfer-authority conservation.** Summed across the appropriations of a cycle, `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents` equals the plain sum of `enacted_amount_cents`: a transfer moves authority between rows and never creates or destroys it. This must hold after every transfer execution and every transfer reversal.

**Expenditure-ledger conservation.** An appropriation's `expended_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over its expenditures in status `POSTED`. Posting a charge and then voiding it returns `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and `available_authority_cents` to their pre-posting values.

**Reversion–carryforward conservation.** For every closed appropriation, `carryforward_cents` plus `reverted_cents` equals the unexpended authority of the row, namely the greater of zero and (`enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents` minus `expended_cents`). This must hold after a cycle close.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule could reject a request, the outcome is determined by a single global ladder, evaluated top to bottom; the first rule that fails produces the response and no later rule is consulted. The ladder matters because several checks in this system can fail together on the same request — a stale version *and* a wrong phase, a frozen appropriation *and* an active release hold — and the observable error must be deterministic.

1. Headers present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every resource named in the path exists, validated left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
4. Every reference in the request body resolves, validated in the endpoint's input declaration order — otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every creation endpoint carrying reference fields.
5. For `AgencyBudgetOfficer`, `X-User-ID` equals the agency reached from the target row, directly or through the referenced `AgencyRequest`, `Appropriation`, or `FederalGrant` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to exactly six operations: `POST /fiscal/agency-requests`, `POST /fiscal/agency-requests/{id}/submit`, `POST /fiscal/decision-packages`, `POST /fiscal/performance-measures`, `POST /fiscal/expenditures`, and `POST /fiscal/match-commitments`. `POST /fiscal/match-commitments` carries two chains and checks both, the grant's owning agency first and the pledged appropriation's second, so a pledge that crosses agencies is rejected whichever side the officer belongs to. The check is skipped for any reference that did not resolve, since the unresolved reference will surface its own 404 or 400 instead.
6. The body is well formed — required fields present, enum members legal, integer fields integral, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint. This rung also carries three families of check that are easy to mistake for rung 7. A body that is not parseable JSON, or is JSON that is not an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an absent or empty body is read as the empty object, so its consequence is whichever required field is then reported missing. Every free-text `reason` and `justification` field is required and must be a string of 1 to 500 characters. And every numeric **range** rule on an endpoint that rung 7 does not list is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`: `fiscal_year` outside 2000 to 2100, `reserve_balance_cents` below zero, `constitutional_reserve_pct` outside 0 to 0.5, a forecast `amount_cents` at or below zero, `effective_to` not strictly after `effective_from`, `introduced_amount_cents` below zero, `recommended_base_cents` below zero, `target_reduction_pct` at or below 0 or above 0.5, a measure's `baseline_value` below zero, `award_cents` or `moe_baseline_cents` at or below zero, `required_match_ratio` outside 0 to 3, either prior-year MOE spend below zero, and `period_end` not strictly after `period_start`.
7. Monetary and count fields respect their sign rules: an `amount_cents` that its entity requires to be positive is greater than zero, a `CommitteeAction` carries `amount_cents` equal to zero for `RESTORE` and `STRIKE` and greater than zero for `SET`, `INCREASE`, and `DECREASE`, a `sequence_number` and a `priority_rank` are at least 1, and `quarter` lies between 1 and 4 inclusive — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/agency-requests`, `POST /fiscal/decision-packages`, `POST /fiscal/committee-actions`, `POST /fiscal/allotments`, `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`, `POST /fiscal/expenditures`, `POST /fiscal/match-commitments`, and `POST /fiscal/appropriations/emergency`. Within one endpoint the sign rules are evaluated in the order the fields are listed here: on a committee action the action-type amount rule before the `sequence_number` rule, on a decision package the `priority_rank` rule before the `amount_cents` rule, and on an allotment and an expenditure the `quarter` rule before the `amount_cents` rule.
8. A performance measure's `target_value` is strictly greater than its `baseline_value` — otherwise `ERR_PERFORMANCE_TARGET_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/performance-measures` only. This ordering is why a measure whose target is both below baseline *and* attached to a body that also breaks a sign rule surfaces the sign rule first.
9. The supplied expected parent version equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /fiscal/revenue-forecasts`, `POST /fiscal/executive-recommendations`, and `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`.
10. The owning `BudgetCycle` is in a phase that permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID` (409). Applies to every write endpoint that touches a cycle-scoped row.
11. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
12. The referenced appropriation's `authority_status` is `ENACTED` — otherwise `ERR_APPROPRIATION_NOT_ENACTED` (409). Applies to six operations: `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/prepare`, `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/approve`, `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/execute`, `POST /fiscal/expenditures`, `POST /fiscal/match-commitments`, and `POST /fiscal/appropriations/{id}/freeze-cash`.
13. The referenced appropriation's `cash_status` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_CASH_FROZEN` (409). Applies to four operations: `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/prepare`, `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/approve`, `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/release`, and `POST /fiscal/expenditures`.
14. The referenced appropriation's `release_hold` is false — otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_HOLD_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/prepare` only. Because this sits below the frozen check, a frozen appropriation that also carries a release hold reports `ERR_CASH_FROZEN`.
15. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the identifier that prepared or proposed the row — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to three operations: `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/approve`, `POST /fiscal/executive-recommendations/{id}/approve`, and `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/approve`.
16. A supplied `depends_on_package_id` points at a package of the same `agency_request_id` whose status is not `DEFERRED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_DEFERRED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/decision-packages`.
17. On recommend, the prerequisite named by `depends_on_package_id` has status `RECOMMENDED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_RECOMMENDED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/decision-packages/{id}/recommend`.
18. Every member of `recommended_package_ids` has status `RECOMMENDED` — otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_RECOMMENDED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/executive-recommendations`.
19. Every member of `recommended_package_ids` belongs to the same `agency_request_id` as the recommendation — otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_REQUEST_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/executive-recommendations`. Note that this sits *below* the recommended-status check, so a foreign package that is also not recommended reports `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_RECOMMENDED`.
20. Every `EXPANSION` package in scope carries at least one performance measure whose `target_value` exceeds its `baseline_value` — otherwise `ERR_PERFORMANCE_MEASURE_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to two operations: `POST /fiscal/agency-requests/{id}/submit` and `POST /fiscal/decision-packages/{id}/recommend`.
21. The target quarter is not already past — otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED` (409). Applies to three operations: `POST /fiscal/allotments`, `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/release`, and `POST /fiscal/expenditures`. An expenditure's `quarter` must equal the current cycle quarter; an allotment's `quarter` must be at least the current cycle quarter at creation and exactly equal to it at release.
22. The target quarter is not still in the future — otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to two operations: `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/release` and `POST /fiscal/expenditures`. Release demands exact equality with the current cycle quarter; an expenditure's `quarter` may not exceed it.
23. The logical clock has reached twelve months after `fiscal_year_start_date` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_ENDED` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles/{id}/close`.
24. The adopted window keeps a 30-day quiet buffer from every other adopted window of the same cycle and `source_code`, excluding the row named in `supersedes_forecast_id` — otherwise `ERR_FORECAST_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/revenue-forecasts/{id}/adopt`.
25. No other allotment on the same `appropriation_id` and `quarter` is in status `DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `APPROVED`, or `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_ALLOTMENT_QUARTER_OCCUPIED` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/allotments`.
26. `sequence_number` is not already used by another committee action of the same appropriation — otherwise `ERR_ACTION_SEQUENCE_DUPLICATE` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/committee-actions`.
27. `priority_rank` is not already used by another decision package of the same `agency_request_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIORITY_RANK` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/decision-packages`.
28. The agency has no other request in the same `budget_cycle_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGENCY_REQUEST` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/agency-requests`.
29. Code uniqueness holds: `agency_code` is unique across agencies, `fiscal_year` is unique across cycles, and `program_code` is unique within a `budget_cycle_id` and `agency_id` pair — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to `POST /fiscal/agencies`, `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles`, and `POST /fiscal/appropriations`.
30. `source_appropriation_id` differs from `target_appropriation_id` — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_SAME_APPROPRIATION` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`.
31. Source and target appropriations share the same `fund_type` — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_FUND_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`.
32. `transfer_type` matches the agency relationship: `INTRA_AGENCY` requires equal `agency_id` on both rows, `INTER_AGENCY` requires different ones — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`.
33. The replayed committee balance is never negative at any sequence position — otherwise `ERR_NEGATIVE_APPROPRIATION_BALANCE` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/committee-actions`.
34. The transfer amount is within the statutory authority cap — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_AUTHORITY_CAP` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`.
35. The transfer amount fits within available authority on the authority basis — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` (422). Applies to four operations: `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers`, `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/approve`, `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/execute`, and `POST /fiscal/budget-transfers/{id}/reverse`.
36. The charge fits within the appropriation's `available_authority_cents` — otherwise `ERR_APPROPRIATION_OVERSPEND` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/expenditures`.
37. The charge fits within the appropriation's `available_cash_cents` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH_RELEASED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/expenditures` and to `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/recall`, where it guards a recall from pushing released cash below what has already been spent. Because the authority test sits one rung above the cash test, a charge that breaches both reports `ERR_APPROPRIATION_OVERSPEND`.
38. The allotment release ceiling is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_NO_RELEASE_CAPACITY` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/prepare`.
39. When `allow_partial` is false, the ceiling is at least `requested_cents` — otherwise `ERR_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDS_CEILING` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/allotments/{id}/prepare`. A zero ceiling therefore reports `ERR_NO_RELEASE_CAPACITY`, never `ERR_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDS_CEILING`, regardless of `allow_partial`.
40. At least one adopted `GENERAL`-fund revenue forecast exists for the cycle — otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_FORECAST` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles/{id}/advance-phase` and `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles/{id}/certify-balance`.
41. Approved recommendations do not exceed adopted revenue net of the constitutional reserve, and `balance_certified` is true when `LEGISLATIVE` is requested — otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_BALANCED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles/{id}/certify-balance` and `POST /fiscal/budget-cycles/{id}/advance-phase`.
42. The emergency request fits inside the reserve capacity — otherwise `ERR_EMERGENCY_EXCEEDS_RESERVE_CAPACITY` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/appropriations/emergency`.
43. Overlapping committed match pledges plus the new pledge fit inside the appropriation's available authority — otherwise `ERR_MATCH_AGGREGATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/match-commitments`.
44. At least one eligible reduction candidate package exists for the cycle — otherwise `ERR_NO_REDUCTION_CANDIDATES` (422). Applies to `POST /fiscal/reduction-scenarios`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Cycle and Settling the Revenue Estimate

A `SystemAdmin` begins by registering the institutions that will spend money. Registering an agency records its `agency_code` — a short statewide code such as `DOH`, between 1 and 12 characters and unique across all agencies — together with its legal `name`, between 1 and 200 characters, and its `agency_type`, one of `EXECUTIVE`, `JUDICIAL`, `LEGISLATIVE`, or `HIGHER_ED`. Branch classification is reported but drives no fiscal arithmetic. Each agency also carries a derived `federal_compliance_flag`, which starts at `CLEAR`; the rules that move it to `AT_RISK` or `DEFICIENT` are described in §3.8. The identifier returned by registration is the value an officer of that agency presents as `X-User-ID`.

Opening a budget cycle records the `fiscal_year` label — an integer of at least 2000 and at most 2100, unique across cycles — the `fiscal_year_start_date`, and `reserve_balance_cents`, the cash sitting in the budget stabilization reserve, which must be zero or positive and which is the sole funding source for emergency appropriations. The `constitutional_reserve_pct` may be supplied and otherwise defaults to five percent expressed as the fraction 0.05; it must be at least 0 and at most 0.5. A newly opened cycle sits in `DEVELOPMENT`, its `version` is 1, `balance_certified` is false, `cash_posture` is `NORMAL`, and every computed money figure — `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents`, `total_recommended_cents`, `required_reserve_cents`, `emergency_granted_cents`, `total_released_cents`, `total_expended_cents`, `total_reverted_cents`, `total_carryforward_cents`, `projected_cumulative_cash_cents` — starts at zero, as does `cash_utilization_pct`.

Revenue estimates arrive from the consensus forecasting process. A `BudgetAnalyst` records an estimate against a cycle by supplying `budget_cycle_id`, a `source_code` of 1 to 32 characters such as `SALES_TAX`, the `fund_type` the estimate credits (`GENERAL`, `FEDERAL`, or `DEDICATED`), the `forecast_round` that produced it (`CONSENSUS_SPRING`, `CONSENSUS_FALL`, or `REVISED`), an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and the window the estimate governs as `effective_from` and `effective_to`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, where `effective_from` is inclusive, `effective_to` is exclusive, and `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`; both are stored and echoed back byte for byte as the client supplied them, while the comparisons above and the quiet buffer below run on the parsed UTC instants. The estimate is recorded in `DRAFT`. Only `GENERAL`-fund rows ever feed the cycle's adopted revenue total.

Adoption is a `BudgetDirector` action that moves a forecast `DRAFT → ADOPTED`. It requires the row to be in `DRAFT`, and the owning cycle to be in either `DEVELOPMENT` or `EXECUTIVE_PREP`; a cycle further along rejects the adoption with `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`. The adopting request may name a `supersedes_forecast_id`, in which case the named row must belong to the same cycle, carry the same `source_code`, and itself be `ADOPTED`; a row failing any of those three conditions is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Adoption also enforces a **30-day quiet buffer**: no other `ADOPTED` forecast of the same cycle and the same `source_code` — excluding the row explicitly being superseded — may sit within thirty days of the window being adopted, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The buffer is a half-open overlap test on windows each widened by thirty days at its exclusive end: the two collide when the adopting row's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than thirty days after the other's `effective_to` **and** the other's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than thirty days after the adopting row's `effective_to`. A gap of exactly thirty days therefore clears the buffer, and windows that overlap outright always collide. `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` rows are never candidates for a collision. On success the adopted row becomes `ADOPTED`; the row named by `supersedes_forecast_id`, if any, moves `ADOPTED → SUPERSEDED` and its amount stops counting toward the cycle total; and the cycle recomputes `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents`, `required_reserve_cents`, `projected_cumulative_cash_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`, incrementing its `version` by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a forecast.

The cycle's `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every forecast of that cycle whose status is `ADOPTED` and whose `fund_type` is `GENERAL`; `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` rows contribute nothing, and `FEDERAL` and `DEDICATED` rows contribute nothing regardless of status. The forecasts of a cycle can be listed with optional `status` and `fund_type` filters.

### 3.2. Agency Requests, Decision Packages and Performance Measures

An `AgencyBudgetOfficer` opens the agency's ask by creating a request carrying `budget_cycle_id`, `agency_id`, and `base_budget_cents` — the continuation budget needed to keep current services, which must be strictly greater than zero. The officer's `X-User-ID` must equal the `agency_id` supplied. A request is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, `last_return_reason` null, and `total_request_cents` equal to `base_budget_cents`. An agency may hold at most one request per cycle; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGENCY_REQUEST`. Requests can be listed with optional `budget_cycle_id`, `agency_id`, and `status` filters.

Within a `DRAFT` request the officer attaches **decision packages**, each a discrete funding proposal. A package carries `agency_request_id`, a `package_type` of `EXPANSION`, `REDUCTION`, `TECHNICAL`, or `FEDERAL_MATCH`, a `priority_rank` of at least 1 that is unique within the request (lower rank is funded first), and an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero which is always a positive magnitude — for a `REDUCTION` package it is the magnitude of savings offered, not a negative number. An optional `fte_delta` records the full-time-equivalent change the package requests, may be fractional or negative, and defaults to 0. An optional `depends_on_package_id` names a prerequisite package of the same request that must be recommended before this one can be; it defaults to null for an independent package, and if it names a package that is currently `DEFERRED` **or** a package belonging to a different `agency_request_id` the creation is rejected with `ERR_DEPENDENCY_DEFERRED` — the one error identifier covers both failures of that reference. Packages are created in `PROPOSED`. Listing packages accepts optional `agency_request_id`, `depends_on_package_id`, `status`, and `package_type` filters, the second of which is how a caller finds everything blocked behind a given prerequisite.

Every `EXPANSION` package must be justified by at least one **performance measure**. A measure carries `decision_package_id`, a `measure_code` of 1 to 32 characters, a `unit` of `COUNT`, `PERCENT`, `DAYS`, or `DOLLARS`, a `baseline_value` of at least zero describing current measured performance, and a `target_value` describing the performance promised if the package is funded. Both accept fractional values. A negative `baseline_value` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that check runs *before* the target comparison, so a body with a negative baseline and a target below it reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The target must be strictly greater than the baseline; equal or lower values are rejected with `ERR_PERFORMANCE_TARGET_INVALID`. Measures are immutable once written and can be listed per package.

Submission moves the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. It requires the row to be in `DRAFT`, the owning cycle to be in `DEVELOPMENT`, the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the request's `agency_id`, and every `EXPANSION` package of the request to carry at least one performance measure whose target exceeds its baseline; a bare expansion package is rejected with `ERR_PERFORMANCE_MEASURE_REQUIRED`. "Every" here means every expansion package of the request whatever its own status — a `DEFERRED` expansion package with no qualifying measure still blocks submission — and a request holding no expansion packages at all clears the check trivially. Submission recomputes `total_request_cents` and increments the request's `version` by 1.

A `BudgetAnalyst` then drives the review loop. Beginning review moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and requires the row to be in `SUBMITTED`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the analyst has two exits of equal standing. Returning the request for revision moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, requires the row to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, takes a `reason` which is stored in `last_return_reason`, increments `revision_count` by 1, and increments `version` by 1. Accepting the request moves `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED`, requires the row to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` and the owning cycle to be in either `DEVELOPMENT` or `EXECUTIVE_PREP`, recomputes `total_request_cents`, and increments `version` by 1. `ACCEPTED` is terminal.

The loop closes because a returned request can be submitted again: submission also applies from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED`, under exactly the same four preconditions as the first submission — the row in `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` rather than `DRAFT`, the cycle in `DEVELOPMENT`, the acting identifier equal to `agency_id`, and every expansion package still carrying a qualifying measure — with the same recomputation of `total_request_cents` and the same version increment. A request can therefore cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED` any number of times, and `revision_count` records how many times it entered `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`.

**How the request total is computed.** `total_request_cents` is the base budget adjusted by the packages that are still live. Take every package of the request whose status is either `PROPOSED` or `RECOMMENDED`; packages in `DEFERRED` are excluded entirely. Of those, sum the `amount_cents` of every package whose `package_type` is `EXPANSION`, `TECHNICAL`, or `FEDERAL_MATCH` to obtain the additive total, and sum the `amount_cents` of every package whose `package_type` is `REDUCTION` to obtain the subtractive total. The request total is `base_budget_cents` plus the additive total minus the subtractive total. All three terms are integer cents, so no rounding arises, and because the result is a pure sum the order of accumulation is irrelevant. This figure is recomputed whenever a package is attached to the request, on submission, on acceptance, and on every package disposition change.

Package disposition is the analyst's second lever. Recommending a package moves it `PROPOSED → RECOMMENDED`, and requires that the package be in `PROPOSED`, that the owning request already be `ACCEPTED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), that a non-null `depends_on_package_id` name a package already in `RECOMMENDED` (otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_RECOMMENDED`), and that an `EXPANSION` package have at least one measure whose target exceeds its baseline (otherwise `ERR_PERFORMANCE_MEASURE_REQUIRED`). Deferring a package moves it `PROPOSED → DEFERRED` and requires only that it be in `PROPOSED`. A deferred package is not dead: recommending applies again from `DEFERRED → RECOMMENDED` under the same four preconditions as the recommendation from `PROPOSED` — the row in `DEFERRED` this time, the owning request `ACCEPTED`, any prerequisite already `RECOMMENDED`, and any expansion justified by a measure. Each of these three transitions recomputes the owning request's `total_request_cents` and increments the package's `version` by 1, so deferring a package removes its contribution from the total and reviving it restores that contribution.

### 3.3. Building the Executive Budget: Recommendations, Balance and Reduction Scenarios

Once a request is `ACCEPTED`, either a `BudgetAnalyst` or a `BudgetDirector` prepares the executive recommendation for it. The payload names the `agency_request_id`, the `recommended_base_cents` the executive is willing to fund (which may differ from what the agency asked and must be zero or positive), an array of `recommended_package_ids` naming the packages to fund, the `policy_priority` bucket the recommendation is charged to — one of `WORKFORCE`, `INFRASTRUCTURE`, `PUBLIC_SAFETY`, `EDUCATION`, or `HEALTH` — and the pinned `expected_agency_request_version` described in §2.8. Every package named must currently be in `RECOMMENDED`, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_RECOMMENDED`, and must belong to the same request as the recommendation, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_REQUEST_MISMATCH`. The array itself is a required input: omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is a member that is not a non-empty string. An empty array is entirely legal and simply funds the recommended base with no packages. Members are read positionally and never de-duplicated, so a package named twice is resolved twice and contributes its `amount_cents` twice to the total below.

The recommended total is computed at preparation time from exactly the packages named — not from the request's live package set. Sum the `amount_cents` of the named packages whose `package_type` is `EXPANSION`, `TECHNICAL`, or `FEDERAL_MATCH`; sum the `amount_cents` of the named packages whose `package_type` is `REDUCTION`; then `recommended_amount_cents` is `recommended_base_cents` plus the first sum minus the second. With an empty array the result is simply `recommended_base_cents`. All values are integer cents and no rounding occurs.

The new row is created in `PREPARED` with `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` and `approved_by_user_id` null. Creating it also **supersedes** whatever recommendation already existed for that request: any prior row in `PREPARED` moves `PREPARED → SUPERSEDED`, and any prior row in `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`. The two supersessions are not symmetric in their consequences. Superseding a `PREPARED` row changes nothing beyond its own status, because a prepared row never counted toward the cycle. Superseding an `APPROVED` row removes its amount from the cycle's `total_recommended_cents`, which is therefore recomputed, and additionally forces the cycle's `balance_certified` back to false, because a certification made against a recommendation set that no longer exists is void. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. The creation also increments the owning request's `version` by 1. Recommendations for a request can be listed newest last, with an optional `status` filter.

Approval is reserved to a `BudgetDirector` and moves the row `PREPARED → APPROVED`. It requires the row to be in `PREPARED`, the owning request to be `ACCEPTED`, the owning cycle to be in `EXECUTIVE_PREP`, and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `prepared_by_user_id`. On success `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting identifier, the cycle's `total_recommended_cents` is recomputed, the cycle's `balance_certified` is set to false — a newly approved amount invalidates any prior certification, even one taken moments earlier — and both the recommendation's `version` and the cycle's `version` increment by 1.

**Certifying that the budget balances.** The cycle's `total_recommended_cents` is the sum of `recommended_amount_cents` over every recommendation in status `APPROVED` whose request belongs to that cycle; `PREPARED` and `SUPERSEDED` rows are excluded. Against that stands the appropriable revenue. First the cycle's adopted general-fund revenue is totalled from its `ADOPTED` `GENERAL` forecasts. Then `required_reserve_cents` is `constitutional_reserve_pct` multiplied by that adopted revenue, rounded half up to the cent. The appropriable amount is the adopted revenue minus that already-rounded reserve. The budget balances when `total_recommended_cents` is less than or equal to the appropriable amount; exact equality balances.

A `BudgetDirector` certifies balance while the cycle is in `EXECUTIVE_PREP`. Certification is a self-transition on the phase dimension, `EXECUTIVE_PREP → EXECUTIVE_PREP` — the cycle remains in `EXECUTIVE_PREP` and only `required_reserve_cents` and `balance_certified` change. It requires the cycle to be in `EXECUTIVE_PREP`; it requires adopted general-fund revenue to be greater than zero, meaning at least one `ADOPTED` `GENERAL` forecast exists, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_FORECAST`; and it requires the balance test above to pass, otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_BALANCED`. On success `required_reserve_cents` is rewritten, `balance_certified` becomes true, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Phase advancement is the director's other cycle-level lever, and each step has its own gate. Advancing `DEVELOPMENT → EXECUTIVE_PREP` requires the cycle to be in `DEVELOPMENT` and at least one forecast of the cycle to have reached `ADOPTED`, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_FORECAST`. Advancing `EXECUTIVE_PREP → LEGISLATIVE` requires the cycle to be in `EXECUTIVE_PREP` and `balance_certified` to be true, otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_BALANCED`. Advancing `LEGISLATIVE → ENACTED` requires the cycle to be in `LEGISLATIVE` and every appropriation of the cycle to have an `authority_status` of either `ENACTED` or `LAPSED`; a single line item still `PENDING` blocks the step with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Advancing `ENACTED → EXECUTION` requires only that the cycle be in `ENACTED`, and on success recomputes `projected_cumulative_cash_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`. Every advancement sets `phase` to the requested `to_phase` and increments the cycle's `version` by 1, and every advancement — not only the step into `EXECUTION` — runs the full cycle recomputation of §2.10 in the same mutation, so `required_reserve_cents`, `total_recommended_cents` and the cash figures all settle again on any step. Where a step gates on more than the current phase, the gate that fires first is the one listed for that step: on the step into `ENACTED` the appropriation sweep (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) is the only additional gate, and a cycle with no appropriations at all passes it.

**Reduction scenarios.** During executive preparation an analyst prices a hypothetical cut by computing a reduction scenario against a cycle, supplying `budget_cycle_id` and a `target_reduction_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 0.5. The scenario is a snapshot: it never mutates a decision package, and it is never revised after computation.

The scenario is computed as a waterfall. First the accepted base is totalled: the sum of `base_budget_cents` over every request of the cycle whose status is `ACCEPTED`. Then `target_reduction_cents` is `target_reduction_pct` multiplied by that accepted base, rounded half up to the cent exactly once, before the walk begins. The candidate pool is every package whose `package_type` is `REDUCTION`, whose own status is `PROPOSED`, and whose owning request is `ACCEPTED` in this cycle; reduction packages already `RECOMMENDED` or `DEFERRED` are excluded, as are packages hanging off requests that have not yet been accepted. If the pool is empty, the computation is rejected with `ERR_NO_REDUCTION_CANDIDATES`.

Candidates are ordered by `priority_rank` ascending, then by `amount_cents` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by insertion order for rows that tie on all three, and walked in that order. Before each candidate is taken, the walk stops if the amount accumulated so far is already greater than or equal to the target — the stop test is inclusive, so hitting the target exactly ends the walk without taking another package. Otherwise the candidate's identifier is appended to `selected_package_ids` and its full `amount_cents` is added to the running total; packages are never split. When the walk ends, `achieved_reduction_cents` is that running total, `overshoot_cents` is the greater of zero and `achieved_reduction_cents` minus `target_reduction_cents`, and `feasibility` is `FEASIBLE` when `achieved_reduction_cents` is greater than or equal to `target_reduction_cents` and `INFEASIBLE` otherwise. `selected_package_ids` preserves selection order. Scenarios computed for a cycle can be listed.

### 3.4. The Legislative Bill: Line Items, Committee Action and Enactment

A `LegislativeFiscalOfficer` introduces each **appropriation** line item into the bill by supplying `budget_cycle_id`, `agency_id`, the `fund_type` the authority is drawn on (`GENERAL`, `FEDERAL`, or `DEDICATED`), a `program_code` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique within that cycle and agency, and `introduced_amount_cents`, the amount as introduced before any committee touches it, which must be zero or positive.

An appropriation carries **two independent status dimensions**, and neither constrains the other's vocabulary. The legal-authority dimension, `authority_status`, is one of `PENDING`, `ENACTED`, `LAPSED`, or `CLOSED` and starts at `PENDING`. The cash-release dimension, `cash_status`, is one of `UNRELEASED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, `FULLY_RELEASED`, or `FROZEN` and starts at `UNRELEASED`. A newly introduced line therefore sits at `PENDING` authority and `UNRELEASED` cash, with `committee_balance_cents` equal to `introduced_amount_cents`, `enacted_amount_cents` at 0, `is_emergency` false, `pre_freeze_cash_status` and `freeze_reason` null, `release_hold` false in the ordinary case but true when the owning cycle's `cash_posture` already reads `CONSTRAINED` and the new line is `GENERAL` fund — the flag is derived at creation from the same rule that drives it thereafter, not hardwired to false — and every ledger figure — `transfers_in_cents`, `transfers_out_cents`, `released_cents`, `expended_cents`, `reverted_cents`, `carryforward_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, `available_authority_cents` — at 0. Appropriations can be listed with optional `budget_cycle_id`, `agency_id`, `fund_type`, and `authority_status` filters.

Committee action is recorded as an append-only sequence. Each action names the `appropriation_id` it acts on, the `chamber` that acted (`HOUSE`, `SENATE`, or `CONFERENCE`), a `committee_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `action_type`, an `amount_cents`, and a `sequence_number` of at least 1 that must be unique within the appropriation — gaps in the sequence are perfectly legal, duplicates are rejected with `ERR_ACTION_SEQUENCE_DUPLICATE`. The five action types behave as follows: `SET` replaces the balance with the action's amount, `INCREASE` adds the amount to the balance, `DECREASE` subtracts the amount from the balance, `RESTORE` resets the balance to the line's `introduced_amount_cents`, and `STRIKE` zeroes the line. `RESTORE` and `STRIKE` ignore any amount and therefore require `amount_cents` to be exactly 0; `SET`, `INCREASE`, and `DECREASE` require it to be strictly greater than 0. Violations of that rule are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`.

**The running balance is a full replay, not an append.** Recording an action does not simply adjust a stored total. The server discards the previous result and replays the appropriation's entire action set from scratch, with no status filter, ordered by `sequence_number` ascending and, for equal sequence numbers, by `created_at` ascending. The replay starts from `introduced_amount_cents` and applies each action in turn, writing the post-action balance onto that action as its `running_balance_cents`. If the balance would fall below zero at any position in the replay, the entire insert is rejected with `ERR_NEGATIVE_APPROPRIATION_BALANCE` and no row is written and no balance is changed. When the replay completes, the appropriation's `committee_balance_cents` is the final balance; with no actions at all it equals `introduced_amount_cents`. The consequence to design for is that inserting an action with a *lower* `sequence_number` than actions that already exist rewrites the `running_balance_cents` of every later action and can change `committee_balance_cents` for an unchanged set of actions — applying actions in arrival order instead of sequence order would leave earlier running balances untouched and produce a different final balance from the identical inputs. Recording an action increments the appropriation's `version` by 1. Recording is not gated on the line's `authority_status`: an action may be appended to a line that has already been enacted or lapsed, and when that happens the replay rewrites `committee_balance_cents` and every `running_balance_cents` but never touches `enacted_amount_cents`, so an enacted line's authority does not move behind a late committee amendment. The actions of an appropriation can be listed in `sequence_number` ascending order — with `created_at` and then insertion order breaking ties on equal sequence numbers, matching the replay order — and that listing accepts no filters beyond pagination.

Enactment is the officer's final legislative act and requires the owning cycle to be in `LEGISLATIVE`. It has two destinations, chosen by the replayed balance rather than by the caller. When `committee_balance_cents` is greater than zero, the line moves `PENDING → ENACTED`: `enacted_amount_cents` is frozen at `committee_balance_cents`, `available_authority_cents` and `available_cash_cents` are recomputed, and the row's `version` increments by 1. When `committee_balance_cents` is exactly zero — because the line was struck or decreased to nothing — the same action instead moves the line `PENDING → LAPSED`: `enacted_amount_cents` is set to 0 and the row's `version` increments by 1. Both branches require `authority_status` to be `PENDING`; attempting enactment on an already-enacted or lapsed line is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A lapsed line holds no authority and never receives cash, but it does participate in cycle close.

### 3.5. Releasing Cash: Allotments, Quarter Caps and Cash Posture

Spending authority is not spendable cash. During `EXECUTION`, cash reaches an agency one quarter at a time through **allotments**, each covering one quarter of one appropriation. A `BudgetAnalyst` opens an allotment by supplying `appropriation_id`, the `quarter` it covers (an integer from 1 to 4), the `requested_cents` the agency asks to have released (strictly greater than zero), and optionally `allow_partial`, which defaults to false. The requested quarter may be the current cycle quarter or a later one; naming an already-past quarter is `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`. At most one live allotment may occupy a given appropriation-and-quarter slot: if another allotment on the same `appropriation_id` and `quarter` sits in `DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `APPROVED`, or `RELEASED`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_QUARTER_OCCUPIED`. A new allotment starts in `DRAFT` with `computed_release_cents` at 0, `binding_constraint` null, `released_cents` at 0, and `prepared_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, and `recall_reason` all null; the row also echoes the `allow_partial` it was created with. Opening the request is gated only on the cycle not being `CLOSED` — the `EXECUTION` requirement bites at preparation, so a `DRAFT` allotment may legitimately be opened while the cycle is still `ENACTED` or earlier, provided its quarter is not already past. Allotments of an appropriation can be listed with optional `status` and `quarter` filters.

Preparation, open to a `BudgetAnalyst` or a `BudgetDirector`, is where the arithmetic happens and where the release amount is locked. It moves the allotment `DRAFT → PREPARED` and requires: the row to be in `DRAFT`; the owning appropriation's `authority_status` to be `ENACTED`, otherwise `ERR_APPROPRIATION_NOT_ENACTED`; that appropriation's `cash_status` not to be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CASH_FROZEN`; its `release_hold` to be false, otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_HOLD_ACTIVE`; and the owning cycle to be in `EXECUTION`.

**The release ceiling.** Four independent limits are computed and the smallest wins.

The first limit is simply `requested_cents`. The second is the **quarter cap**: the appropriation's current authority multiplied by the share of that authority the fund type permits in that quarter, rounded half up to the cent. Current authority here means `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents`. The permitted shares differ by fund and quarter. A `GENERAL`-fund line may release 22 percent of its authority in quarter 1 and 26 percent in each of quarters 2, 3, and 4. A `FEDERAL` line is level: 25 percent in each of the four quarters. A `DEDICATED` line is front-loaded: 30 percent in each of quarters 1 and 2 and 20 percent in each of quarters 3 and 4. The third limit is **unreleased authority**: current authority minus the appropriation's `released_cents`. The fourth is **statewide cash flow**: the cycle's projected cumulative cash for the allotment's quarter, minus everything the cycle has already released. Projected cumulative cash for a quarter is the cycle's `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents` multiplied by that quarter's cumulative cash-flow factor — 20 percent through quarter 1, 45 percent through quarter 2, 70 percent through quarter 3, and 100 percent through quarter 4 — rounded half up to the cent. From that, subtract the cycle's `total_released_cents`, which counts releases across *all* of the cycle's appropriations including `FEDERAL` and `DEDICATED` ones; if the subtraction is negative, the cash-flow limit is zero rather than negative.

Each of the quarter cap and the projected cash figure is rounded to the cent before entering the comparison, never after. The ceiling is the smallest of the four limits. `binding_constraint` records which limit bound, and ties are broken by a fixed precedence rather than by discovery order: scan the labels `REQUEST`, `QUARTER_CAP`, `UNRELEASED_AUTHORITY`, `CASH_FLOW` in that order and record the first whose limit equals the ceiling. Allotments in `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing to the appropriation's `released_cents` and therefore nothing to any of these limits.

If the ceiling comes out at zero or below — the unreleased-authority limit can go negative once transfers out have shrunk a line whose cash was already released — preparation is rejected with `ERR_NO_RELEASE_CAPACITY`. If the ceiling is below `requested_cents` while `allow_partial` is false, preparation is rejected with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDS_CEILING`; with `allow_partial` true the smaller amount is accepted and recorded. On success the ceiling is written to `computed_release_cents`, the winning label to `binding_constraint`, the acting identifier to `prepared_by_user_id`, the status becomes `PREPARED`, and `version` increments by 1.

A `BudgetDirector` then approves, moving the allotment `PREPARED → APPROVED`. Approval requires the row to be in `PREPARED`, the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, and the owning appropriation's `cash_status` not to be `FROZEN`. It writes `approved_by_user_id` and increments `version` by 1. Approval does not re-run the ceiling: the amount computed at preparation stands.

Release is the `StateController`'s act and moves the allotment `APPROVED → RELEASED`. It requires the row to be in `APPROVED`, the appropriation's `cash_status` not to be `FROZEN`, and the allotment's `quarter` to equal the current cycle quarter exactly — a later quarter is `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_OPEN` and an earlier one is `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`. Those three are evaluated in that order, so a frozen appropriation whose allotment also names the wrong quarter reports `ERR_CASH_FROZEN`. Release does **not** re-test the appropriation's `authority_status`: it is absent from the rung-12 list of §2.12, and an approved allotment therefore still releases even if its line is no longer `ENACTED`. On success `released_cents` on the allotment becomes `computed_release_cents`, and the cascade of §2.10 runs: the appropriation recomputes `released_cents` and `available_cash_cents` and moves its cash dimension; the cycle recomputes `total_released_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`; and a posture that has become `CONSTRAINED` sets `release_hold` to true on every appropriation of that cycle whose `fund_type` is `GENERAL` and whose `cash_status` is `UNRELEASED`. The allotment's, the appropriation's, and the cycle's `version` each increment by 1.

The cash dimension of the appropriation follows the released total against current authority. Where current authority again means `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents`, a release that leaves `released_cents` greater than zero but strictly below current authority moves the row `UNRELEASED → PARTIALLY_RELEASED`; a release that brings `released_cents` to current authority or above moves it `UNRELEASED → FULLY_RELEASED` in one step; and a release from a partly released row that reaches or passes current authority moves it `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → FULLY_RELEASED`. The derivation is written once and reused everywhere it is needed: zero released is `UNRELEASED`, released at or above current authority is `FULLY_RELEASED`, anything between is `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, and a `FROZEN` row is never moved by it. None of the three re-tests the appropriation's `authority_status`; all three recompute `released_cents` and `available_cash_cents`, cascade to the cycle's `total_released_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`, and increment the appropriation's `version` by 1.

**Cash posture and release holds.** A cycle's `projected_cumulative_cash_cents` is its `adopted_general_fund_revenue_cents` multiplied by the cash-flow factor of the *current* quarter as derived from the logical clock, rounded half up to the cent. Its `cash_utilization_pct` is `total_released_cents` divided by that projected figure — using 1 as the divisor whenever the projected figure is zero or less, so the ratio is always defined — rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The `cash_posture` is `CONSTRAINED` when `cash_utilization_pct` is greater than or equal to 0.90, and `NORMAL` otherwise. While the posture is `CONSTRAINED`, `release_hold` is true on exactly those appropriations of the cycle that are `GENERAL` fund and whose `cash_status` is `UNRELEASED`, and that flag blocks allotment preparation with `ERR_RELEASE_HOLD_ACTIVE`. When the posture returns to `NORMAL`, the hold clears across the cycle.

Recall is the compensating action of release and is likewise the controller's. It moves the allotment `RELEASED → RECALLED`, requires the row to be in `RELEASED`, takes a `reason` stored in `recall_reason`, and requires that the recall not push the appropriation's released cash below what it has already spent — formally, that the appropriation's `available_cash_cents` minus the allotment's `released_cents` be at least zero — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH_RELEASED`. On success the allotment's `released_cents` returns to 0 and the same cascade runs in reverse: the appropriation recomputes `released_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and its cash dimension; the cycle recomputes `total_released_cents`, `cash_utilization_pct`, and `cash_posture`; and a posture returning to `NORMAL` clears `release_hold` on every `GENERAL` `UNRELEASED` appropriation of the cycle. Released-authority conservation must hold afterwards. All three rows' `version` values increment by 1. On the appropriation, a recall that brings `released_cents` back to exactly zero moves the cash dimension `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → UNRELEASED`, while a recall that leaves `released_cents` still above zero moves it `FULLY_RELEASED → PARTIALLY_RELEASED`; the cash dimension is genuinely cyclic, and a release followed by its recall returns the row to `UNRELEASED`. `RECALLED` is terminal for the allotment.

Cancellation, available to a `BudgetAnalyst`, retires an allotment that was never released and frees its quarter slot for a replacement. It is permitted while the row is in `DRAFT` or `PREPARED` and rejected from anywhere else with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both origins run the identical write: the status becomes `CANCELLED`, `computed_release_cents` is set back to 0 and `binding_constraint` back to null so a locked ceiling does not survive the cancellation — from `DRAFT` those two writes are simply no-ops on values already 0 and null — the appropriation-and-quarter slot is freed, and `version` increments by 1 in either case. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

**Freezing cash.** A `StateController` may freeze the cash dimension of an enacted appropriation, supplying a `reason`. Freezing requires `authority_status` to be `ENACTED`, otherwise `ERR_APPROPRIATION_NOT_ENACTED`, and `cash_status` to be one of `UNRELEASED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, or `FULLY_RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — freezing an already frozen row is not legal. The two tests are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.12 rather than the order named here: the cash-status test is rung 11 and runs first, the enactment test is rung 12 and runs second, so a row that is both already `FROZEN` and not `ENACTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The transition captures the outgoing value in `pre_freeze_cash_status`, stores the reason in `freeze_reason`, sets `cash_status` to `FROZEN`, and increments `version` by 1. The shape is identical from all three origins — `UNRELEASED → FROZEN`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → FROZEN`, and `FULLY_RELEASED → FROZEN` behave the same way; `pre_freeze_cash_status` is what records which one it was.

Unfreezing does **not** restore `pre_freeze_cash_status`. It requires `cash_status` to be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and then recomputes the cash dimension from scratch: comparing `released_cents` against current authority — `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents` — a released total of zero takes the row `FROZEN → UNRELEASED`, a released total at or above current authority takes it `FROZEN → FULLY_RELEASED`, and anything strictly in between takes it `FROZEN → PARTIALLY_RELEASED`. The zero test is applied first, so a row whose released total is zero and whose current authority is also zero lands in `UNRELEASED` rather than `FULLY_RELEASED`. `freeze_reason` is cleared and `version` increments by 1. The recomputed value can legitimately differ from `pre_freeze_cash_status`, which is exactly what happens when a transfer changed the appropriation's authority while cash was frozen; `pre_freeze_cash_status` exists so that divergence is observable rather than silent.

### 3.6. Amending the Budget: Transfers of Authority

Mid-year amendment moves authority between two line items without changing the total. A `BudgetAnalyst` or `BudgetDirector` proposes a **budget transfer** naming `source_appropriation_id`, `target_appropriation_id`, the `amount_cents` to move (strictly greater than zero), a `transfer_type` of `INTRA_AGENCY` or `INTER_AGENCY`, a `justification` of 1 to 500 characters, and the pinned `expected_source_appropriation_version` from §2.8.

Four structural rules are checked at proposal. The two appropriations must differ, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_SAME_APPROPRIATION`. They must share the same `fund_type`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_FUND_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The `transfer_type` must match the agency relationship — `INTRA_AGENCY` demands that both rows carry the same `agency_id`, `INTER_AGENCY` demands that they differ — otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_TYPE_MISMATCH`. And the amount must respect the statutory transfer cap: it may not exceed ten percent of the source line's `enacted_amount_cents`, that cap being rounded half up to the cent, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_AUTHORITY_CAP`. The amount must also fit inside the source's `available_authority_cents`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`; that same available-authority test is re-applied at approval, at execution, and — against the *target* row — at reversal, because the figure moves underneath a proposal that sits in the queue. A proposed transfer is created in `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` set to the acting identifier, `approved_by_user_id` and `decision_reason` null, and it increments the source appropriation's `version` by 1. Transfers touching an appropriation on either side can be listed, with an optional `side` filter accepting `SOURCE` or `TARGET` and an optional `status` filter.

From `PROPOSED` a `BudgetDirector` has two mutually exclusive exits, and both are specified here in full. Approval moves the transfer `PROPOSED → APPROVED`; it requires the row to be in `PROPOSED`, the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, the source appropriation's `authority_status` to be `ENACTED`, and `amount_cents` to be within the source's `available_authority_cents`; it writes `approved_by_user_id` and increments the transfer's `version` by 1, and it touches no ledger figure whatsoever. Rejection moves the transfer `PROPOSED → REJECTED`; it requires only that the row be in `PROPOSED`, takes a `reason` stored in `decision_reason`, and increments the transfer's `version` by 1; it likewise touches no ledger figure, and `REJECTED` is terminal.

Execution is the `StateController`'s act and is the only point at which authority actually moves. It advances the transfer `APPROVED → EXECUTED` and requires the row to be in `APPROVED`, `amount_cents` to still fit inside the source's `available_authority_cents`, and the target appropriation's `authority_status` to be `ENACTED`. On success the transfer records `executed_at` from the logical clock, the source's `transfers_out_cents` and the target's `transfers_in_cents` are recomputed from the set of executed transfers, and both rows' `available_authority_cents` are recomputed. Because the source's available authority has fallen, the match-commitment cascade of §3.8 runs: any `COMMITTED` pledge on the source whose overlapping committed total no longer fits inside that available authority moves to `AT_RISK`, each affected grant recomputes `committed_match_cents` and `match_status`, and each owning agency recomputes `federal_compliance_flag`. Transfer-authority conservation must hold afterwards. The transfer's, the source's, and the target's `version` each increment by 1.

Reversal is the compensating action of execution and belongs to the controller as well. It moves the transfer `EXECUTED → REVERSED`, requires the row to be in `EXECUTED`, requires the *target* appropriation's `available_authority_cents` to be at least `amount_cents` (otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`, since the target must still hold the authority being taken back), requires the owning cycle to be in `EXECUTION`, and takes a `reason` stored in `decision_reason`. The reversed row stops counting, so both `transfers_out_cents` and `transfers_in_cents` are recomputed; because the source's available authority has recovered, every `AT_RISK` pledge on the source whose overlapping committed total once again fits returns to `COMMITTED`, each affected grant recomputes `committed_match_cents` and `match_status`, and each owning agency recomputes `federal_compliance_flag`. Transfer-authority conservation must hold afterwards, and all three rows' `version` values increment by 1. `REVERSED` is terminal.

### 3.7. Spending: Expenditures, the Two Bases, and Emergency Appropriations

An `AgencyBudgetOfficer` charges an appropriation by posting an **expenditure** naming `appropriation_id`, the `quarter` the charge is posted into, the `amount_cents` charged (strictly greater than zero), an `object_code` of 1 to 16 characters, and a `vendor_reference` of 1 to 64 characters holding the vendor invoice reference. The charge's `quarter` must equal the current cycle quarter derived from the logical clock: an earlier quarter is `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED` and a later one is `ERR_QUARTER_NOT_OPEN`. The appropriation must be `ENACTED` and must not be `FROZEN`. A charge is created in `POSTED` with `posted_at` taken from the logical clock and `void_reason` null. Posting recomputes the appropriation's `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and `available_authority_cents`, refreshes `moe_current_spend_cents` on every federal grant of the same agency and cycle, and increments the appropriation's `version` by 1. The charges of an appropriation can be listed with optional `status` and `quarter` filters.

**Two bases, two verdicts.** An appropriation reports available spending room twice, and the two figures answer different questions from the same underlying rows. Both start from the same inputs: `expended_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the appropriation's expenditures in status `POSTED`, with `VOIDED` rows excluded; `released_cents` is the sum of `released_cents` over the appropriation's allotments in status `RELEASED`, with `DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `APPROVED`, `RECALLED`, and `CANCELLED` rows excluded; and current authority is `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents`.

The **cash basis**, `available_cash_cents`, is `released_cents` minus `expended_cents`. It counts only cash that has actually been released through allotments, so unreleased enacted authority is invisible to it: for an `ENACTED` appropriation with nothing yet released, the cash basis is 0 even though its authority is positive. The **authority basis**, `available_authority_cents`, is current authority minus `expended_cents`. It counts the full enacted authority as adjusted by executed transfers and is indifferent to whether cash was released, so for any given row it is always greater than or equal to the cash basis. Both are integer cents and neither requires rounding.

Which basis applies is fixed per rule. Posting an expenditure is checked against **both**, authority first: a charge exceeding `available_authority_cents` is `ERR_APPROPRIATION_OVERSPEND`, and a charge that clears authority but exceeds `available_cash_cents` is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH_RELEASED`. Transfer proposal, approval, execution, and reversal are checked on the authority basis only, as is the match-commitment capacity gate. Allotment recall is checked on the cash basis. A read-only balance summary exposes both side by side, reporting `appropriation_id`, `authority_cents` (the current authority figure), `released_cents`, `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, `available_authority_cents`, and `basis_delta_cents`, the last being `available_authority_cents` minus `available_cash_cents`.

Voiding is the compensating action of posting and belongs to the `StateController`. It moves the charge `POSTED → VOIDED`, requires the row to be in `POSTED` and the owning cycle to be in `EXECUTION`, and takes a `reason` stored in `void_reason`. The owning appropriation then recomputes `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and `available_authority_cents`, and every federal grant of the same agency and cycle refreshes `moe_current_spend_cents`. Expenditure-ledger conservation must hold afterwards, and both the expenditure's and the appropriation's `version` increment by 1. `VOIDED` is terminal, and a voided charge counts toward nothing.

**Emergency appropriations.** When an unbudgeted obligation lands mid-year, a `BudgetDirector` may grant an emergency appropriation out of the stabilization reserve. The request names `budget_cycle_id`, `agency_id`, `program_code`, the `requested_amount_cents`, and a `justification`. The owning cycle must be in `EXECUTION`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`. Emergency rows skip the legislative dimension entirely: the created row is `GENERAL` fund with `is_emergency` set to true, `introduced_amount_cents`, `committee_balance_cents`, and `enacted_amount_cents` all equal to `requested_amount_cents`, `authority_status` already `ENACTED`, and `cash_status` `UNRELEASED`, all in the same creation. `available_authority_cents` opens at `requested_amount_cents` while `released_cents`, `expended_cents`, `available_cash_cents`, and both transfer totals open at 0, `pre_freeze_cash_status` and `freeze_reason` are null, and `release_hold` is derived from the cycle's posture exactly as it is for an ordinary appropriation, so an emergency row granted under a `CONSTRAINED` posture is born held. A `program_code` of 1 to 32 characters is required, but it is **not** checked for uniqueness on this route: an emergency row may reuse a `program_code` that an ordinary appropriation of the same cycle and agency already carries, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` is never raised here. The cycle's `emergency_granted_cents` — the sum of `enacted_amount_cents` over its appropriations where `is_emergency` is true and `authority_status` is `ENACTED` — is recomputed and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Capacity is evaluated in a **pinned four-step order**, and the row being requested is deliberately excluded from step 1 even though granting it will raise the figure for the *next* request; there is no fixed-point iteration. Step 1: total the current authority — `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents` — over every appropriation of the cycle whose `fund_type` is `GENERAL` and whose `authority_status` is `ENACTED`, which includes previously granted emergency rows because they are `ENACTED`, and excludes `PENDING`, `LAPSED`, and `CLOSED` rows as well as the request under evaluation. Step 2: multiply the cycle's `constitutional_reserve_pct` by that total and round half up to the cent to obtain the required minimum reserve. Step 3: the emergency capacity is the greater of zero and the cycle's `reserve_balance_cents` minus its `emergency_granted_cents` minus that already-rounded required minimum reserve. Step 4: if `requested_amount_cents` exceeds the capacity, reject with `ERR_EMERGENCY_EXCEEDS_RESERVE_CAPACITY`; otherwise grant the appropriation and add `requested_amount_cents` to the cycle's `emergency_granted_cents`.

### 3.8. Federal Awards: Match Commitments and Maintenance of Effort

A `BudgetAnalyst` records a **federal grant** by supplying `agency_id`, `budget_cycle_id`, a `cfda_number` of 1 to 16 characters, an `award_cents` strictly greater than zero, the performance period as `period_start` (inclusive) and `period_end` (exclusive, and strictly later than `period_start`), and a `moe_baseline_cents` strictly greater than zero recording the maintenance-of-effort baseline the state must at least match. Optional inputs are `required_match_ratio`, the state dollars required per federal dollar, which defaults to 0.25 and must be at least 0 and at most 3, and the two operator-supplied prior-year figures `moe_prior_year1_spend_cents` and `moe_prior_year2_spend_cents`, each defaulting to 0 and each at least 0, recording maintenance-of-effort-eligible state spend in the year before the cycle and two years before it. At creation the grant's `required_state_match_cents` is fixed once as `award_cents` multiplied by `required_match_ratio`, rounded half up to the cent; `committed_match_cents` and `moe_current_spend_cents` are 0, `moe_compliance_ratio` is 0, `moe_assessed_basis` is null, `match_status` is `UNMET`, and `moe_compliance_status` is `NOT_ASSESSED`. `moe_current_spend_cents` opens at 0 unconditionally: creation performs no retroactive sweep, so `POSTED` charges that already exist against qualifying appropriations of the same agency and cycle are invisible on the freshly created grant and only reach the figure when an assessment is run or a later posting or voiding cascade refreshes it. The owning cycle need only be in a phase other than `CLOSED`; grants may be recorded during development, legislative deliberation, or execution alike. Grants can be listed with optional `agency_id`, `budget_cycle_id`, and `match_status` filters.

State match is pledged through **match commitments**, each naming a `federal_grant_id`, the `appropriation_id` whose authority is being pledged, and the `committed_match_cents` pledged from it, which must be strictly greater than zero. An `AgencyBudgetOfficer` creates them, and the pledged appropriation must be `ENACTED`. A commitment is created in `COMMITTED`. Commitments can be read individually and listed with optional `federal_grant_id`, `appropriation_id`, and `status` filters.

**The overlapping-period capacity gate.** A single appropriation may back several grants, but it may not be over-pledged across grants whose performance periods run at the same time. When a new commitment is created, gather the existing commitments on the same `appropriation_id` whose status is `COMMITTED` and whose grant's performance period overlaps the incoming grant's period. Overlap is half-open in both directions: two periods overlap when the existing grant's `period_start` is strictly earlier than the incoming grant's `period_end` and the incoming grant's `period_start` is strictly earlier than the existing grant's `period_end`, so periods that merely touch end-to-start do **not** overlap. Sum `committed_match_cents` over that gathered set, add the incoming pledge, and if the result exceeds the appropriation's `available_authority_cents`, reject with `ERR_MATCH_AGGREGATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Match coverage.** A grant's `committed_match_cents` is the sum of `committed_match_cents` over its commitments in status `COMMITTED`; commitments in `AT_RISK` contribute nothing to that sum, and commitments in `RELEASED_FROM_COMMITMENT` contribute to nothing at all. Separately, note whether the grant has at least one commitment in `AT_RISK`. The grant's `match_status` then resolves four ways, in this order. If the committed total is below `required_state_match_cents` and the committed total is exactly zero, the status is `SHORTFALL` when at least one commitment is `AT_RISK` and `UNMET` when none is. Otherwise, if the committed total is below `required_state_match_cents`, the status is `SHORTFALL`. Otherwise, if at least one commitment is `AT_RISK`, the status is `AT_RISK` — a fully covered grant still carries risk while any pledge behind it is impaired. Otherwise the status is `MET`. Coverage is recomputed when a commitment is created, when a transfer touching a pledged appropriation is executed or reversed, and during cycle close.

Behind the grants, each agency's `federal_compliance_flag` resolves from the grants it owns: `DEFICIENT` when any grant of the agency is in `SHORTFALL`, otherwise `AT_RISK` when any grant is in `AT_RISK`, otherwise `CLEAR`.

Commitments move between states only as a consequence of other operations; there is no direct endpoint. A pledge moves `COMMITTED → AT_RISK` when a transfer executes on the pledged appropriation and the summed `committed_match_cents` of the `COMMITTED` pledges on that appropriation whose grant periods overlap this one now exceeds the appropriation's `available_authority_cents`; the pledged amount then stops counting toward its grant's `committed_match_cents`. A pledge moves `AT_RISK → COMMITTED` when a transfer on that appropriation is reversed and the overlapping committed total once again fits inside `available_authority_cents`, at which point the amount counts again. Both directions recompute the owning grant's coverage and the owning agency's flag.

Both sweeps are **iterative rather than simultaneous**, and the order is pinned. Each sweep walks the appropriation's commitments in creation order — ascending `created_at`, ties broken by insertion order — and re-reads the overlapping committed total after every move it makes, so a decision made on an earlier pledge is visible to every later one. On impairment the overlapping total counts only pledges still in `COMMITTED`, which means the oldest over-pledged commitment is impaired first and each impairment shrinks the total the remaining pledges are measured against; the sweep therefore stops impairing as soon as the total fits, leaving the youngest pledges `COMMITTED`. The comparison is strict: a total exactly equal to `available_authority_cents` fits and impairs nothing. On restoration the candidate pledge is excluded from the overlapping total and its own `committed_match_cents` added back before the comparison, and the test is inclusive — a restored total exactly equal to `available_authority_cents` is allowed — so the oldest `AT_RISK` pledge that fits is restored first and consumes the recovered headroom, which can leave a younger and smaller pledge impaired even though it would have fit on its own. Neither sweep looks past the one appropriation named by the transfer, and both compare against `available_authority_cents` as it stands after the transfer figures have already been rewritten. `RELEASED_FROM_COMMITMENT` rows are inert in both directions.

**Maintenance of effort.** A `BudgetAnalyst` assesses maintenance of effort on a grant by supplying the `basis` to assess on, either `SINGLE_YEAR` or `THREE_YEAR_AVERAGE`. The assessment first refreshes `moe_current_spend_cents`: the sum of `amount_cents` over every expenditure in status `POSTED` charged to an appropriation whose `fund_type` is `GENERAL` and whose `agency_id` and `budget_cycle_id` match the grant's; `FEDERAL` and `DEDICATED` charges and `VOIDED` rows are excluded.

The two bases produce genuinely different verdicts from identical data. Under `SINGLE_YEAR` the assessed spend is `moe_current_spend_cents` alone, so a state that front-loads the current year passes. Under `THREE_YEAR_AVERAGE` the assessed spend is the sum of `moe_current_spend_cents`, `moe_prior_year1_spend_cents`, and `moe_prior_year2_spend_cents` divided by three, rounded half up to the cent before any comparison; identical current-year data therefore yields a lower assessed figure whenever the prior years were leaner, and the same grant can be `COMPLIANT` under one basis and `DEFICIENT` under the other.

The verdict compares integers, never the reported ratio: `moe_compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when the assessed spend is greater than or equal to `moe_baseline_cents` — exact equality is compliant — and `DEFICIENT` otherwise. The assessment also writes `moe_compliance_ratio`, the assessed spend divided by `moe_baseline_cents` rounded half up to 6 decimal places, and records the basis used in `moe_assessed_basis`. The grant's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Closing the Fiscal Year: Reversion and Carryforward

Closing is the `StateController`'s final act on a cycle and moves it `EXECUTION → CLOSED`. It requires the cycle to be in `EXECUTION` and the logical clock to have reached at least twelve months after `fiscal_year_start_date`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_ENDED`.

The close runs a sweep over every appropriation of the cycle whose `authority_status` is `ENACTED` or `LAPSED`; rows already `CLOSED` are skipped, and so are rows still in `PENDING`. A `PENDING` row survives the close untouched — it keeps `authority_status` `PENDING`, keeps `carryforward_cents` and `reverted_cents` at 0, does not bump its `version`, and its match commitments are not discharged in the cascade below — even though the owning cycle itself becomes `CLOSED` and refuses every subsequent write. For an `ENACTED` row the split is computed per appropriation and rounded per appropriation. Current authority is `enacted_amount_cents` plus `transfers_in_cents` minus `transfers_out_cents`. Unexpended authority is the greater of zero and that authority minus `expended_cents`. The carryforward cap is the fund type's permitted share of current authority, rounded half up to the cent: a `GENERAL`-fund line may carry nothing forward at all — its share is zero percent — a `FEDERAL` line may carry its entire authority forward at one hundred percent, and a `DEDICATED` line may carry 15 percent. Then `carryforward_cents` is the smaller of the unexpended authority and that already-rounded cap, and `reverted_cents` is the unexpended authority minus `carryforward_cents`. A `LAPSED` row is handled differently and explicitly: it receives `carryforward_cents` of 0 and `reverted_cents` of 0, because a lapsed line carries no authority to sweep. Both kinds of row then move on the authority dimension — an enacted line `ENACTED → CLOSED` and a lapsed line `LAPSED → CLOSED` — each incrementing its own `version` by 1.

The cycle's rollups are the sums of those already-rounded per-appropriation figures: `total_carryforward_cents` is the sum of `carryforward_cents` across the cycle's appropriations and `total_reverted_cents` is the sum of `reverted_cents`. Totalling authority across appropriations and applying the percentage once at the end is wrong and can differ by a cent per row. `total_reverted_cents` is written only by this sweep, and `total_expended_cents` — the sum of `expended_cents` across the cycle's appropriations — settles at its final value here. Reversion–carryforward conservation must hold for every closed row.

Two cascades follow the sweep. Because every appropriation the sweep touched has moved to `CLOSED`, every match commitment pointing at one of those swept appropriations — and only those — is discharged: a pledge still in `COMMITTED` moves `COMMITTED → RELEASED_FROM_COMMITMENT` and a pledge in `AT_RISK` moves `AT_RISK → RELEASED_FROM_COMMITMENT`, and `RELEASED_FROM_COMMITMENT` is terminal. Because those commitments have left `COMMITTED`, each affected grant recomputes `committed_match_cents` and re-resolves `match_status` under the four-way rule of §3.8, and because those grant statuses have changed, each owning agency recomputes `federal_compliance_flag`. Finally the cycle's `phase` becomes `CLOSED` and its `version` increments by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal for a cycle, and every subsequent write against it is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`.
