# Cloud Cost Allocation & Chargeback API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise cloud spend arrives from the provider as an undifferentiated stream of billing lines. Finance needs it as an auditable ledger: every cent attributed to a business unit, every shared cost redistributed by a defensible methodology, every invoice defensible against the business unit that receives it. The gap between those two views is where cloud financial management lives, and it is the gap this API closes. The system is mounted under `/finops` and governs the full arc of that reconciliation — tagging policy enforced at the moment a resource is provisioned, cost records absorbed into shared pools or struck directly against their owning unit, commitment purchases whose discounts must be recognised in exactly one place, showback and chargeback runs that publish into invoices, and a dispute channel through which a charged business unit can contest what it was billed.

Three pressures shape the design. The first is **conservation**: allocation must never create or destroy money. A shared pool distributed across four business units must sum back to the pooled amount to the cent, and a run computed on the final basis must reconcile exactly against the effective cost of the records it consumed. The second is **segregation of duties**: the analyst who builds a cost model may not be the controller who publishes it, the person who drafts a tagging policy may not be the one who activates it, and the business unit that files a dispute may not be represented by the user who resolves it. The third is **reversibility under control**: published runs can be reopened, invoices voided, budgets un-breached and resized resources returned to service, but each of those reversals is capped, gated and fully cascaded so that no derived total is left describing a world that no longer exists.

Alongside allocation, the API carries the operational disciplines that keep spend honest: idle and rightsizing scans that propose remediation and give resource owners a bounded window to react, spend-spike anomaly detection with severity-based alert routing and root-cause attribution, budget setting with forecast-accuracy scoring and mandatory variance explanation after a breach, and unit economics reporting that divides allocated cost by a business volume driver to produce a cost per unit. Every temporal rule in the system reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that windows, terms and deadlines are deterministic and testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning of resources, temporal conventions, rounding discipline, cascade semantics, concurrency control, and the order in which a request is admitted or rejected.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the declared role is not a recognised role name, or is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognises exactly five roles: `FinOpsAnalyst`, `FinOpsController`, `BusinessUnitOwner`, `ResourceOwner`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `FinOpsAnalyst` prepares the cost model. Analysts draft tagging policies, drive provisioning requests through retag, approval and provisioning, open allocation runs, attach and seal shared cost pools, absorb untagged spend, compute runs, run the idle-detection scan, the remediation-window sweep and the anomaly scan, notify resource owners, and acknowledge and attribute anomaly alerts. A `FinOpsController` owns the money: activating and retiring tagging policies, publishing, reopening and closing allocation runs, appending manual adjustment lines, issuing and writing off invoices, starting review on and resolving disputes, purchasing, activating and evaluating commitments, closing anomaly alerts, and activating and closing budgets. Both roles share the ability to draft a tagging policy, open an allocation run, create, revise and evaluate budgets, register and compute unit economics drivers, and read every resource in the system. Ingesting cost records and creating provisioning requests are shared between the `FinOpsAnalyst` and the `PlatformAdmin`.

A `BusinessUnitOwner` acts on behalf of exactly one business unit: it settles that unit's invoices, files, answers and withdraws disputes on them, records variance explanations on that unit's budgets, and reads its own unit, runs, lines, invoices, disputes, budgets, alerts and unit economics. A `ResourceOwner` is the engineering owner recorded against a cloud resource; it snoozes, approves and cancels remediation tasks on resources it owns and reads cloud resources and remediation tasks. A `PlatformAdmin` maintains the business unit tree, pushes resource telemetry, ingests billing data and drives the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ per role, and ownership enforcement depends on them:

- When `X-User-Role` is `BusinessUnitOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BusinessUnit`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `BusinessUnit` compare the resource's business-unit reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, settling an invoice, filing a dispute, responding to an information request, withdrawing a dispute and recording a variance explanation all require `X-User-ID` to equal the `business_unit_id` of the invoice or budget being acted on, or the `filed_by_business_unit_id` of the dispute being acted on. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is enforced on those write operations only: read endpoints apply no owner scoping at all, so a `BusinessUnitOwner` listing or fetching business units, runs, lines, invoices, disputes, budgets, alerts or drivers sees every row of those collections and not merely its own.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ResourceOwner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does not compare it to any entity's `id`; instead it compares it to the `owner_user_id` string recorded on the `CloudResource` behind the remediation task being acted on. Snoozing, approving and cancelling a remediation task as a `ResourceOwner` all require that string equality, and a mismatch is likewise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; reads of cloud resources and remediation tasks are again unscoped, so a `ResourceOwner` may fetch and list resources and tasks it does not own.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FinOpsAnalyst`, `FinOpsController`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for provenance — as `created_by_user_id` and `activated_by_user_id` on `TaggingPolicy`, as `created_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on `AllocationRun`, as `resolved_by_user_id` on `Dispute`, and as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on `AnomalyAlert` — and it compares those recorded strings against each other for the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.9, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`. The `filed_by_user_id` recorded on a `Dispute` is the `X-User-ID` of the filing `BusinessUnitOwner`, and the `owner_user_id` carried on a provisioning request and copied onto the resulting resource is the opaque identifier of the `ResourceOwner` who will be notified for remediation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by 20 random uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and for those endpoints only. The scan and sweep operations — idle detection, the remediation-window sweep and the anomaly scan — also answer with `response_type` `array`, but their `data` holds only the resources that particular pass created or moved and their `meta` carries no pagination fields.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than clamped; an empty string for either parameter is treated as absent. `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items actually returned is still below `total_count`. Both appear in the response envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count` and `has_more`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, and because `created_at` is a millisecond timestamp that two rows can share, ties are broken by a monotonically increasing server-side creation sequence — that is, by insertion order — so the ordering is always total and deterministic. Every list endpoint additionally accepts the domain filters named in its §3 narrative — for example business units filter on `parent_business_unit_id` and `status`, cost records on `cloud_resource_id`, `shared_cost_pool_id`, `business_unit_id`, `applied_commitment_id`, `allocated_by_run_id` and `allocation_state`, cloud resources on `provisioning_request_id`, `business_unit_id`, `tag_state` and `lifecycle_state`, allocation runs on `mode`, `scope_business_unit_id` and `status`, allocation lines on `allocation_run_id`, `business_unit_id`, `shared_cost_pool_id` and `line_kind`, invoices on `allocation_run_id`, `business_unit_id`, `settlement_status` and `dispute_status`, disputes on `chargeback_invoice_id`, `filed_by_business_unit_id` and `status`, commitments on `commitment_type`, `region` and `status`, remediation tasks on `cloud_resource_id`, `kind` and `status`, anomaly alerts on `business_unit_id`, `root_cause_cloud_resource_id`, `severity` and `status`, budgets on `business_unit_id` and `status`, provisioning requests on `tagging_policy_id`, `business_unit_id` and `status`, shared cost pools on `status`, and unit economics drivers on `business_unit_id`, `allocation_run_id` and `driver_name`. Filters are conjunctive and never change the ordering rule. A filter is applied only when it is supplied with a non-empty value; matching is by exact string comparison against the field's serialised value, with the literal `null` matching rows whose field is null and `true`/`false` matching boolean fields. An unrecognised query parameter is ignored, and a filter value that matches nothing yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0 rather than an error.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Versioning

Every resource the API persists carries the following system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by a client. Wherever a URL path contains an identifier segment such as `{id}`, that segment is one of these UUIDs.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation. Like `updated_at`, it is read from real wall-clock time, not from the logical clock of §2.5 — only the domain-temporal fields named in §2.5 (periods, terms, windows, `issued_at`, `detected_at`) read the logical clock.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations that only touch computed fields.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource.

`AllocationLine` is the single exception. Allocation lines are write-once artifacts of a compute or adjustment operation, never edited in place, so a line carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. Every other entity in the system — `BusinessUnit`, `TaggingPolicy`, `ProvisioningRequest`, `CloudResource`, `CostRecord`, `SharedCostPool`, `AllocationRun`, `ChargebackInvoice`, `Dispute`, `Commitment`, `RemediationTask`, `AnomalyAlert`, `Budget` and `UnitEconomicsDriver` — carries all four.

A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation of the resource that owns the field. When publishing a run recomputes a business unit's chargeback balance, that business unit's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if a client had edited it directly. A recomputation that lands on the value the field already held is not a mutation and leaves `updated_at` and `version` alone, and the refresh that a read endpoint performs before serialising a business unit, tagging policy, shared cost pool or invoice never bumps `version` or `updated_at` at all — reads are always side-effect free from the client's point of view.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

Every temporal decision in this system — commitment terms, allocation periods, dispute deadlines, remediation windows, anomaly detection timestamps — is evaluated against a server-controlled **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. The clock starts at the fixed default instant 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, and the server must not fall back to wall-clock time even before that capability has ever been called. `current_utc` is a required ISO 8601 string — absent or unparseable, it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and a successful set answers `200 OK` with an empty object in `data` rather than echoing the clock.

Interval inclusivity is deliberately **not** uniform across the domain, and the differences are load-bearing:

- **Allocation run periods are half-open**: a run covers `period_start` inclusive through `period_end` exclusive. Two runs of the same `mode` and the same `scope_business_unit_id` may not have overlapping periods unless the earlier one is already `CLOSED`. Because the interval is half-open, abutting periods — where one run's `period_end` is exactly the next run's `period_start` — are permitted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
- **Commitment terms are closed on both ends**, spanning `term_start` through `term_end` inclusive, and they are compared with a 24-hour cool-off pad applied to both ends before the comparison. A new commitment in the same `region` and of the same `commitment_type` may not sit within 24 hours of the term of any existing commitment in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_UTILIZED` status. Because the buffer is applied first, abutting terms are rejected here — the opposite of the allocation-run rule. The rejection is `ERR_COMMITMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
- **Remediation windows are closed on both ends**, spanning `remediation_window_starts_at` through `remediation_window_ends_at` inclusive. Two windows on the same cloud resource may not share even a single instant, so touching endpoints collide and are rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
- **Deadline comparisons are inclusive.** A dispute may be filed while the logical clock is at or before the invoice's `dispute_window_ends_at`; later filings are rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A remediation task may be snoozed or approved while the clock is at or before `remediation_window_ends_at`; later attempts are rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
- **Cost record containment is by full enclosure.** A cost record is inside a run period, a budget period or a commitment term when its `billing_period_start` is at or after the container's start and its `billing_period_end` is at or before the container's end.

Wherever a resource declares an interval, the end instant must be strictly after the start instant: `period_end` strictly after `period_start` for allocation runs and budgets, `term_end` strictly after `term_start` for commitments, and `billing_period_end` strictly after `billing_period_start` for cost records. A violation is a shape error and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every instant a client submits is parsed as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC; a value carrying no timezone designator is read as already being UTC, and one that cannot be parsed is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Monetary Units, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in **whole cents as integers**. Field names ending in `_cents` are integers unless the field is explicitly a per-unit figure. The two per-unit figures on a unit economics driver, `provisional_cost_per_unit_cents` and `final_cost_per_unit_cents`, are decimal numbers carried to 2 decimal places; the driver's `base_allocated_cents` and `platform_overhead_cents` remain whole-cent integers.

Percentages are decimal numbers carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up. This covers `compliance_score`, `coverage_pct`, `utilization_pct` on both commitments and cloud resources, `coverage_target_pct`, `utilization_target_pct`, `observed_utilization_pct`, `waste_score`, `variance_pct`, `forecast_accuracy_pct` and `severity_score`. The single exception is an anomaly alert's `spike_ratio`, which is carried to 4 decimal places, also rounded half up.

Rounding is **round half up** everywhere a rounding step is named, and the *position* of the rounding step inside a calculation is part of the specification, not an implementation detail. Two cases matter especially. When a commitment grants discounts, the discount is rounded to whole cents **per cost record** and the rounded per-record values are then summed; summing list costs first and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. When a shared pool is distributed, each participant's exact share is **floored**, never rounded, and the leftover cents are then handed out by the remainder rule described in §3.5 — this guarantees the distributed total can never overshoot the pooled amount.

One derived value is truncated rather than rounded: an anomaly alert's `baseline_cents` is the mean of three prior effective costs **floored** to whole cents.

### 2.7. Derived-Field Conservation and Cascade Propagation

A large fraction of this system's state is derived. Business units carry `resource_count`, `tracked_run_rate_cents`, `direct_spend_cents`, `showback_total_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents`; commitments carry `covered_spend_cents`, `total_discount_cents`, `waste_cents`, `coverage_pct` and `utilization_pct`; runs carry `scoped_effective_cost_cents`, `total_allocated_cents` and `line_count`; invoices carry `credited_amount_cents`, `paid_amount_cents` and `net_due_cents`. None of these are client-writable, and none may be left stale after an operation that changes their inputs.

Cascades therefore run to completion in a single request, immediately and synchronously. The deepest chain in the system starts at a remediation sweep: auto-executing an idle-termination task drives the cloud resource to `TERMINATED`, which zeroes that resource's `monthly_run_rate_cents`, which reverses every cost record attached to the resource, which drops each reversed record out of the `covered_spend_cents` of the commitment it had been applied to, which recomputes that commitment's `waste_cents` and `utilization_pct` and may move it from `ACTIVE` to `UNDER_UTILIZED`, and which in parallel bubbles the owning provisioning request up to `TERMINATED` and recomputes the owning business unit's `resource_count`, `tracked_run_rate_cents` and `direct_spend_cents`. Every entity touched along that chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

The system also maintains a **compensating invariant** on allocation. After any compute on the final variant, a run's `total_allocated_cents` must equal its `scoped_effective_cost_cents`. On the provisional variant the two figures deliberately differ, and the difference is exactly the commitment discount recognised on the run's records. Manual adjustment lines sit outside this identity: they increment `line_count` but never contribute to `total_allocated_cents`, which by definition sums only `DIRECT` and `SHARED` lines.

Reversals restore derived state rather than patching it. Reopening a published run releases its cost records, voids its invoices, deletes its lines, resets its totals and recomputes both the showback and the chargeback aggregates on every affected business unit. Resolving a dispute with a credit lowers the invoice's `net_due_cents`, recomputes the business unit's `chargeback_balance_cents`, and then re-evaluates that unit's `ACTIVE` or `BREACHED` budget — which is how a breached budget can return to `ACTIVE` without anyone touching the budget directly.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Amendments, Revisions and Adjustments

Exactly three write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control, and each names its own version field. In all three cases the field is a **required** input, not an optional one, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

1. **Appending a manual adjustment line to an allocation run** requires `expected_allocation_run_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AllocationRun`. If the run's version has advanced since the client read it — most commonly because a recompute has already replaced the run's entire line set — the adjustment the client derived from the older line set no longer describes the run it would be appended to, and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.
2. **Revising a budget's forecast** requires `expected_budget_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Budget`. A prior revision or evaluation will have already appended to `forecast_history`, so the accuracy basis the client computed its new forecast against is stale; the revision is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.
3. **Amending a tagging policy's mandatory dimensions or enforcement mode** requires `expected_tagging_policy_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TaggingPolicy`. If the policy's version has advanced, the amendment was authored against a dimension set that has since been replaced, and it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside this list of three must not carry one and are never subject to a version check.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three duties in this system are deliberately split between two people, and the split is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against a provenance field recorded earlier in the workflow. The publishing user of an allocation run must differ from the run's `created_by_user_id`. The activating user of a tagging policy must differ from the policy's `created_by_user_id`. The resolving user of a dispute must differ from the dispute's `filed_by_user_id`. Any of these three collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and the check applies to exactly those three operations — publish, policy activation, and dispute resolution — and to no others.

### 2.10. Request Admission Order and Path Resolution

When more than one rule could reject a request, the outcome is determined by a fixed evaluation ladder. The first failing rung determines the response, so an earlier rung can mask a later one, and tests depend on that masking behaviour. The ladder is:

1. **Header presence.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` both present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** The declared role is a known role and is listed for the target endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Business-unit ownership.** For a `BusinessUnitOwner` acting on an invoice, dispute or budget, `X-User-ID` matches the owning business unit, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The owning entity must obviously be resolved before its owner can be compared, so when the entity is named in the request body rather than the path — dispute filing, which names `chargeback_invoice_id` — an unresolvable reference surfaces as that entity's 404 and masks this rung.
4. **Resource ownership.** For a `ResourceOwner` acting on a remediation task, `X-User-ID` matches the `owner_user_id` of the resource behind the task, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rung is checked immediately after the task id resolves and before the task's own state is examined, and it is skipped entirely when the task's cloud resource can no longer be found.
5. **Path identifier existence**, validated strictly left to right. The first identifier segment in the URL that does not resolve determines the 404, and the server does not attempt to resolve any later segment. Each entity has its own 404: `ERR_BUSINESS_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAGGING_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVISIONING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLOUD_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHARED_COST_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANOMALY_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Request-body reference existence.** Every foreign reference supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row, and the failure carries the matching 404 for the referenced entity. This rung applies to business unit creation, provisioning request creation, cost record ingestion, allocation run creation, shared cost pool attachment, manual adjustments, dispute filing, budget creation, unit economics driver registration and anomaly alert attribution.
7. **Request-body shape.** All write endpoints require the declared fields to be present, enum values to be within their declared sets, integers to respect their non-negativity or strict-positivity constraints, interval ends to be strictly after their starts, `mandatory_dimensions` to be a non-empty subset of `team`, `environment` and `application`, and `covered_resource_kind` to be present exactly when `commitment_type` is `RESERVED_INSTANCE` and absent otherwise. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
8. **Optimistic concurrency**, per §2.8, yielding `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) on the three protected operations.
9. **State admissibility.** The target must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Each entity has its own conflict code: `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RESOURCE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID`, all at HTTP 409.
10. **Dispute filing admissibility**, immediately after generic state admissibility: an invoice is disputable only while its `settlement_status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` and its `dispute_status` is `NONE`, else `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE` (409).
11. **Business unit liveness.** The business unit involved must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_BU_SUSPENDED` (409). This rung guards provisioning approval, provisioning execution, invoice issuance and budget activation.
12. **Segregation of duties**, per §2.9, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
13. **Mandatory tag completeness** on provisioning approval, yielding `ERR_MANDATORY_TAG_MISSING` (422).
14. **Tagging policy liveness.** The referenced policy must be `ACTIVE` when a provisioning request is created, retagged or approved, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
15. **Variance explanation gate** on provisioning approval, yielding `ERR_VARIANCE_EXPLANATION_REQUIRED` (422).
16. **Pool participant scope** on pool attachment, yielding `ERR_PARTICIPANT_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (422).
17. **Chargeback enablement**, guarding both run publication and invoice issuance, yielding `ERR_BU_NOT_CHARGEBACK_ENABLED` (422).
18. **Pool sealing** on run publication, yielding `ERR_POOL_NOT_SEALED` (409).
19. **Resource remediability**, guarding manual remediation task creation, owner notification and owner approval, yielding `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_REMEDIABLE` (409).
20. **Full settlement** on run closure, yielding `ERR_RUN_NOT_FULLY_SETTLED` (409).
21. **Reopen admissibility**, yielding `ERR_RUN_REOPEN_BLOCKED` (409).
22. **Temporal conflict and expiry rules**, in the order `ERR_ALLOCATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, as described in §2.5.
23. **Counter caps**, in the order `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` (409) then `ERR_SNOOZE_LIMIT` (409).
24. **Remaining domain constraints**, in this order: `ERR_DRIVER_VOLUME_INVALID` (422), `ERR_UNTAGGED_ABSORB_EMPTY` (422), `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` (422), `ERR_PROPORTIONAL_BASIS_ZERO` (422), `ERR_COST_RECORD_ALREADY_ALLOCATED` (409), `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE` (422), `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` (422), `ERR_BUDGET_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` (409) and `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_WOULD_GO_NEGATIVE` (422).

Note the consequence of rung 9 preceding rungs 11 through 24: an operation attempted from an inadmissible state surfaces as that entity's state conflict even when a business rule further down the ladder would also have rejected it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Business Unit Rollup Tree and Its Derived Ledgers

The organisational spine of the system is the **business unit**. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying its display `name` and the finance `cost_center_code` that will appear on its chargeback invoices, and may optionally place it beneath an existing unit by supplying `parent_business_unit_id` — a self-reference into the same collection that builds the rollup tree. Left unset, `parent_business_unit_id` is null and the unit is a root. Three further optional inputs seed the unit's role in allocation: `chargeback_enabled`, which defaults to `false` and which, while false, restricts the unit to showback only and forbids any chargeback invoice ever reaching it; `headcount`, defaulting to 0; and `revenue_cents`, defaulting to 0, both of which serve as proportional bases for shared cost distribution and both of which are plain integers carrying no non-negativity constraint of their own. The unit's `status` may also be supplied and defaults to `ACTIVE`, the other admissible values being `SUSPENDED` and `ARCHIVED`. Only an `ACTIVE` unit may have provisioning approved, be provisioned into, receive an issued invoice, have a budget activated, or participate as an eligible shared-pool participant.

Five derived figures hang off each unit and are all initialised to 0 at creation. `resource_count` is the number of that unit's cloud resources whose `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `TERMINATED`. `tracked_run_rate_cents` is the sum of `monthly_run_rate_cents` across those same non-terminated resources. `direct_spend_cents` sums `effective_cost_cents` over the unit's cost records whose `allocation_state` is anything other than `REVERSED`. `showback_total_cents` sums the `allocated_cents` of allocation lines belonging to the unit across every showback-mode run whose status is `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED`. `chargeback_balance_cents` sums the `net_due_cents` of the unit's chargeback invoices, counting every invoice whose `settlement_status` is neither `VOIDED` nor `WRITTEN_OFF`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may later adjust a unit's allocation bases and posture, changing any of `chargeback_enabled`, `headcount`, `revenue_cents` and `status`; such an update increments the unit's `version` by 1. The update is a partial one: only the four fields just named are amendable, each is applied only when supplied, any field omitted keeps its current value, `name`, `cost_center_code` and `parent_business_unit_id` are not amendable and are ignored if submitted, and the `version` increments by exactly 1 even when the submitted body changes nothing. Because `headcount` and `revenue_cents` are live inputs to proportional distribution and `direct_spend_cents` is another, changing them between two computes of the same run legitimately changes the distribution. Lists of business units may be narrowed to the direct children of one parent, and every role in the system may read the tree.

### 3.2. Tagging Governance: Drafting, Amending, Activating and Retiring Policies

A **tagging policy** declares which tag dimensions the organisation insists on and how hard it insists. Either a `FinOpsAnalyst` or a `FinOpsController` drafts one by supplying a `name` and the ordered `mandatory_dimensions` list, which must be an array of non-empty strings, must itself be non-empty, must draw only from the three recognised dimensions `team`, `environment` and `application`, and must not repeat a dimension — a duplicate entry is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` just as an unrecognised one is. The optional `enforcement_mode` defaults to `ADVISORY`, the alternative being `BLOCKING`: under `ADVISORY` a non-compliant provisioning request records its tagging gap and proceeds, while under `BLOCKING` a non-compliant request is turned away. A freshly drafted policy is in `DRAFT` status, its `created_by_user_id` set to the drafter's `X-User-ID`, its `activated_by_user_id` null, and its `rejection_count` at 0.

While the policy is still `DRAFT`, its dimension set and enforcement mode may be amended wholesale, replacing both `mandatory_dimensions` and `enforcement_mode` with the submitted values; because the amendment is wholesale rather than partial, both fields are required inputs on it and `enforcement_mode` does not fall back to a default the way it does on the draft. That amendment is the tagging-policy leg of optimistic concurrency: it requires `expected_tagging_policy_version` matching the policy's current `version`, and a stale value is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. A successful amendment increments the policy's `version` by 1.

Moving `DRAFT → ACTIVE` is a `FinOpsController` action, and it is one of the three separated duties: the activating user's `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Activation stores the activating user as `activated_by_user_id`, sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, and increments `version`. Only an `ACTIVE` policy may be referenced by a provisioning request, and only an `ACTIVE` policy may be consulted during retag or approval — referencing a policy still in `DRAFT` or already in `RETIRED` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE`. A `FinOpsController` may later move `ACTIVE → RETIRED`, which likewise increments `version` and permanently removes the policy from the provisioning path. Attempting activation, retirement or amendment from a status that does not declare that transition — amending a policy that is no longer `DRAFT`, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_STATE_INVALID`.

The policy's `rejection_count` is an audit signal maintained by the provisioning workflow rather than by any policy operation: it counts the provisioning requests referencing this policy that currently sit in `TAG_REJECTED`, and §3.3 describes exactly when it moves up and down.

### 3.3. From Provisioning Request to Live Cloud Resource

Provisioning begins with a **request**, submitted by a `FinOpsAnalyst` or a `PlatformAdmin`. The payload names the `tagging_policy_id` to be evaluated against, the `business_unit_id` that will own the resource, the `resource_kind` — one of `COMPUTE`, `DATABASE` or `STORAGE` — the `requested_tags` map whose keys are dimension names and whose values are strings — the map must be a JSON object of string keys to string values, but an empty or whitespace-only value passes shape validation and is instead counted as a missing dimension by the compliance evaluation below — the projected `monthly_run_rate_cents` as a non-negative integer, and the `owner_user_id` of the engineering owner who will be notified when the resource later needs remediation.

**Tag compliance is evaluated the instant the request is created**, and again on every retag. The evaluation walks the referenced policy's `mandatory_dimensions` in policy order and collects into `missing_dimensions`, in that same order, every dimension whose key is absent from `requested_tags` or whose value trims to the empty string. Each dimension carries a fixed weight: `team` is worth 40, `environment` 35 and `application` 25. The satisfied weight is the sum of the weights of the mandatory dimensions **not** in `missing_dimensions`; the mandatory weight is the sum of the weights of all the mandatory dimensions. Dividing the satisfied weight by the mandatory weight, multiplying by 100 and rounding half up to two decimal places gives `compliance_score`. From that score the system pins `projected_tag_state`: `TAGGED` when the score is exactly 100.00, `UNTAGGED` when it is exactly 0.00, and `PARTIAL` at every value in between. Note that the weights are normalised against the *declared* mandatory set, so a policy that mandates only `team` and `application` yields 100.00 when both are present, even though those two weights sum to 65 out of the full 100.

A newly created request lands in `SUBMITTED` with `retag_count` at 0 — unless the referenced policy is in `BLOCKING` mode and `missing_dimensions` came back non-empty, in which case the request takes the `SUBMITTED → TAG_REJECTED` path immediately as a system evaluation at creation. This is deliberately **not** an error: the request is persisted in `TAG_REJECTED` so that the tagging gap is auditable, the compliance fields are stored, and the referenced policy's `rejection_count` is incremented by 1. The mandatory-tag error is raised only later, at approval.

A `TAG_REJECTED` request can be repaired. Retagging is a `FinOpsAnalyst` action that replaces `requested_tags` with the submitted map, re-runs the compliance evaluation to refresh `missing_dimensions`, `compliance_score` and `projected_tag_state`, and increments `retag_count` by 1. It requires the referenced policy still to be `ACTIVE`. Where it lands depends on the re-evaluation, and the two branches are symmetric in every respect but their destination. If the re-evaluated `missing_dimensions` is empty, or the policy is in `ADVISORY` mode, the request follows `TAG_REJECTED → SUBMITTED` and the policy's `rejection_count` is decremented by 1 as the request leaves the rejected population. If the policy is in `BLOCKING` mode and the re-evaluated `missing_dimensions` is still non-empty, the request re-enters `TAG_REJECTED`: `retag_count` still increments by 1, the policy's `rejection_count` is decremented for the departure and incremented again for the fresh rejection, leaving it net unchanged. This cycle may repeat any number of times.

Approval is where enforcement bites. A `FinOpsAnalyst` moves a request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` only when four conditions hold together: the referenced policy is `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE`; the owning business unit is `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_BU_SUSPENDED`; under `BLOCKING` enforcement `missing_dimensions` is empty, or the request fails with `ERR_MANDATORY_TAG_MISSING` at HTTP 422; and, if the owning business unit has a budget in `BREACHED` status whose half-open period covers the current logical clock, that budget's `variance_explanation_text` is non-null, or the request fails with `ERR_VARIANCE_EXPLANATION_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422 — and where more than one breached budget covers the clock, the earliest-created one is the one consulted. Approval itself has no side effects beyond the status change, and the response body is the updated provisioning request.

Provisioning an `APPROVED` request is the moment a **cloud resource** comes into existence, and it too requires the owning business unit to be `ACTIVE`. The `APPROVED → PROVISIONED` transition creates a resource in `lifecycle_state` `ACTIVE` that freezes the request's tag data: `applied_tags` is a snapshot of `requested_tags` at provision time, `tag_state` is copied from `projected_tag_state`, and `environment` takes the value of the request's `environment` tag or, when that tag is absent, the literal string `untagged`. The resource also copies `resource_kind`, `owner_user_id` and `monthly_run_rate_cents` from the request, records the originating `provisioning_request_id` and the owning `business_unit_id`, and starts with `utilization_pct` at 100.00. Creating the resource then recomputes the owning unit's `resource_count` and `tracked_run_rate_cents`. Because resources are born as a cascade rather than by direct creation, they are discovered through the resource listing, filterable by `provisioning_request_id`, `business_unit_id`, `tag_state` and `lifecycle_state`; the provisioning call itself answers with the updated provisioning request, now in `PROVISIONED`, and not with the resource it minted, so the new resource's `id` is learned by listing resources filtered on `provisioning_request_id`. Note also that `environment` falls back to `untagged` whenever the request's `environment` tag is absent *or* blank, while `applied_tags` still snapshots the submitted map verbatim.

Telemetry keeps a resource's picture current. A `PlatformAdmin` pushes an observed `utilization_pct`, required and constrained to the range from 0.00 through 100.00 inclusive, stored rounded half up to two decimal places, and may optionally push a fresh `monthly_run_rate_cents`, which when supplied must be a non-negative integer and when omitted leaves the current run rate untouched. This recomputes the owning unit's `tracked_run_rate_cents` and increments the resource's `version` by 1. Telemetry is admissible from every lifecycle state except `TERMINATED`, so a resource sitting in `FLAGGED_IDLE`, `RIGHTSIZE_PENDING` or `RESIZED` still accepts a push. Telemetry also carries one state transition of its own, described in §3.8. Attempting any action on a resource whose `lifecycle_state` does not declare that transition — including anything at all on a `TERMINATED` resource — is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_STATE_INVALID`.

Finally, a provisioning request mirrors the fate of the resource it created. When that resource reaches `TERMINATED`, the request bubbles up `PROVISIONED → TERMINATED` as a system cascade, so no further provisioning action is possible against it. `TERMINATED` is the request's terminal state. Retag, approval or provisioning attempted from a status that does not declare that transition is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Billing Ingestion and Commitment Coverage

A **cost record** is one billing line for one period. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `FinOpsAnalyst` ingests it with a `billing_period_start` (inclusive), a `billing_period_end` (exclusive), the `resource_kind` of spend it represents, its undiscounted `list_cost_cents` as a non-negative integer, and optionally the `cloud_resource_id` it belongs to. When a resource is named, the record's `business_unit_id` is derived from that resource and the owning unit's `direct_spend_cents` is recomputed — ingestion imposes no constraint on the named resource's `lifecycle_state`, so billing may be attached even to a resource already `TERMINATED`; when no resource is named, `business_unit_id` stays null and the record is *untagged spend* awaiting absorption into a shared pool. On ingestion `effective_cost_cents` is set equal to `list_cost_cents`, `allocation_state` is `UNALLOCATED`, and `shared_cost_pool_id`, `applied_commitment_id` and `allocated_by_run_id` are all null. Across its life a record's `allocation_state` moves among `UNALLOCATED`, `ALLOCATED`, `DISPUTED_HOLD` and `REVERSED`. A record that cannot be located is `ERR_COST_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.

**Commitments** are how the organisation buys down list cost. A `FinOpsController` purchases one by declaring its `commitment_type` — `RESERVED_INSTANCE`, which covers a single resource kind, or `SAVINGS_PLAN`, which covers compute and database spend — the `region` it is bought in, the closed term bounded by `term_start` and `term_end`, and the `hourly_commit_cents` as a strictly positive integer. A `RESERVED_INSTANCE` must name its `covered_resource_kind`; a `SAVINGS_PLAN` must leave it unset, and supplying it anyway is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two reporting targets may be supplied: `coverage_target_pct`, defaulting to 80.00, which is informational, and `utilization_target_pct`, defaulting to 85.00, which is the threshold below which the commitment is considered under-utilised. Both are numbers that must be at least 0 — no upper bound is imposed, so a target above 100 is accepted — and each is stored rounded half up to two decimal places. A new commitment lands in `PENDING` with `committed_capacity_cents` already set to `hourly_commit_cents` multiplied by the 720 hours in a standard 30-day commitment period.

Two purchase-time guards apply beyond the 24-hour term buffer of §2.5. Regional capacity is an aggregate: summing `hourly_commit_cents` across every commitment in the same `region` whose status is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_UTILIZED`, and including the new purchase in that sum, the total may not exceed 500 cents per hour. Breaching it is `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422.

Activation is where the money moves. Moving `PENDING → ACTIVE` requires the logical clock to be at or before `term_end` — a clock strictly past `term_end` is rejected with `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`, the same code the status check itself uses — and it runs the **coverage waterfall** over eligible cost records. First, `committed_capacity_cents` is recomputed as `hourly_commit_cents` multiplied by 720 — this is the conserved capacity envelope for the pass. The coverable kinds follow from the type: a `RESERVED_INSTANCE` covers only its declared `covered_resource_kind`, while a `SAVINGS_PLAN` covers `COMPUTE` and `DATABASE` but never `STORAGE`. Candidate records are those whose `allocation_state` is `UNALLOCATED`, whose `applied_commitment_id` is still null, whose `resource_kind` is among the coverable kinds, whose `billing_period_start` is at or after `term_start`, and whose `billing_period_end` is at or before `term_end`.

The processing order is **pinned** and no UUID may participate in it: candidates are sorted by resource kind rank first, with `COMPUTE` ranking ahead of `DATABASE`, which ranks ahead of `STORAGE`, then by `billing_period_start` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, with any residual tie broken by ingestion sequence as in §2.3. A running remaining balance begins at `committed_capacity_cents` and `total_discount_cents` begins at 0. Each candidate is considered in turn, and coverage is **all-or-nothing per record** — there is no partial coverage. If the record's `list_cost_cents` is less than or equal to the remaining balance, the record is covered: its discount is the `list_cost_cents` multiplied by the type's discount rate — 0.40 for a `RESERVED_INSTANCE` and 0.275 for a `SAVINGS_PLAN` — rounded half up to whole cents at this point, per record; its `effective_cost_cents` becomes `list_cost_cents` less that discount; its `applied_commitment_id` is set to this commitment; the rounded discount is added to `total_discount_cents`; and the record's full `list_cost_cents` is subtracted from the remaining balance. If the record's `list_cost_cents` exceeds the remaining balance, that record is simply skipped and the walk continues to the next candidate, which may still be small enough to fit. The per-record rounding position matters: summing the list costs first and rounding a single time at the end produces a different `total_discount_cents` and is wrong.

After the walk, four figures are set. `covered_spend_cents` is the sum of `list_cost_cents` over every record whose `applied_commitment_id` is this commitment and whose `allocation_state` is not `REVERSED`. `waste_cents` is `committed_capacity_cents` less `covered_spend_cents`, floored at 0 so it never goes negative. `utilization_pct` is `covered_spend_cents` divided by `committed_capacity_cents`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. `coverage_pct` is `covered_spend_cents` divided by the total `list_cost_cents` of all candidates evaluated in this pass — covered and skipped alike — multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; that denominator is frozen at activation and re-used unchanged by every later recomputation of `coverage_pct`, and when it is zero — no candidates at all — `coverage_pct` is 0.00 rather than undefined. Activation then recomputes `direct_spend_cents` for every business unit owning a covered record, and increments the commitment's `version` by 1. Activation always lands the commitment in `ACTIVE`, even when the resulting `utilization_pct` is already strictly below `utilization_target_pct`: the slide into `UNDER_UTILIZED` only ever comes from a later evaluation or from a record-reversal cascade, never from the activation itself.

Re-evaluation keeps a live commitment honest, and the same evaluation drives three different outcomes. If the logical clock has passed `term_end`, the commitment expires along either `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `UNDER_UTILIZED → EXPIRED`, freezing coverage so no further cost record may ever be covered by it; `EXPIRED` is terminal. Otherwise, coverage is recomputed and the commitment oscillates: `ACTIVE → UNDER_UTILIZED` when the recomputed `utilization_pct` is strictly below `utilization_target_pct`, and `UNDER_UTILIZED → ACTIVE` when it is at or above `utilization_target_pct`. Both directions recompute `covered_spend_cents`, `waste_cents`, `coverage_pct` and `utilization_pct`, and both leave `total_discount_cents` frozen at the figure the activation waterfall booked — no later evaluation, expiry or reversal ever restates it. An expiry recomputes those same four figures on its way to `EXPIRED`, and every evaluation increments the commitment's `version` by 1 whether or not the status moved. The `ACTIVE → UNDER_UTILIZED` move can also arrive as a system cascade rather than an explicit evaluation: when a covered cost record is reversed it drops out of `covered_spend_cents`, and if that pushes `utilization_pct` below the target the commitment slides into `UNDER_UTILIZED` on its own. Activating or evaluating from a status that does not declare the transition — activating an `EXPIRED` commitment, for instance — is `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.5. Allocation Runs: Scope, Shared Pools and the Two Compute Variants

An **allocation run** is the unit of reconciliation. A `FinOpsAnalyst` or a `FinOpsController` opens one by declaring its `mode` — `SHOWBACK`, which produces informational lines only, or `CHARGEBACK`, which produces invoices at publication — the half-open period bounded by `period_start` and `period_end`, and optionally a `scope_business_unit_id` restricting the run to one business unit subtree. The run is created in `DRAFT` with `reopen_count` at 0, `variant_applied` null, `scoped_effective_cost_cents`, `total_allocated_cents` and `line_count` all at 0, `published_by_user_id` null, and `created_by_user_id` set to the opening user's `X-User-ID`. That last field is what makes publication a separated duty.

Before computing, an analyst may attach one or more **shared cost pools** to a `DRAFT` or `REOPENED` run. Each pool declares a `name` such as `shared-networking` or `untagged-absorption`, a `methodology` of either `PROPORTIONAL` or `EVEN_SPLIT`, the ordered `participant_business_unit_ids` that will share it — a required, non-empty array of non-empty strings, an empty array being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and — when the methodology is `PROPORTIONAL` — a `proportional_basis` of `SPEND`, `HEADCOUNT` or `REVENUE`, defaulting to `SPEND` and ignored entirely under `EVEN_SPLIT`. Being ignored is not being cleared: an `EVEN_SPLIT` pool still persists and returns a `proportional_basis`, which is whatever value was submitted or `SPEND` when none was, and the distribution simply never reads it. Every participant id must resolve to an existing business unit, and when the run carries a `scope_business_unit_id` every participant must sit inside that unit's subtree; a participant outside the scope subtree is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_OUT_OF_SCOPE` at HTTP 422. A new pool is created in `OPEN` status with `pooled_cost_cents` at 0, `absorbed_record_count` at 0, and its `allocation_run_id` set to the run it hangs from.

Absorbing spend into an `OPEN` pool is how untagged and shared costs enter the model. The analyst submits `cost_record_ids`, and each named record that actually qualifies — resolvable, `UNALLOCATED`, with a null `business_unit_id`, and fully enclosed by the run's period — has its `shared_cost_pool_id` set to the pool, after which the pool's `pooled_cost_cents` — the sum of `effective_cost_cents` over its absorbed records — and its `absorbed_record_count` are recomputed and its `version` incremented; the pool itself is the response body. A named id that does not resolve, or that resolves to a record failing any of those qualifications, is silently skipped rather than raising a 404 or a validation error, so — save for the `ALLOCATED` and `REVERSED` cases called out next — a partially qualifying set absorbs the qualifying subset and succeeds. Absorption is refused in two ways. If the submitted set is empty, or if it matches no `UNALLOCATED` cost record with a null `business_unit_id` inside the run's period, the request fails with `ERR_UNTAGGED_ABSORB_EMPTY` at HTTP 422. If a named record's `allocation_state` is already `ALLOCATED` or `REVERSED`, it can neither be absorbed nor re-allocated and the request fails with `ERR_COST_RECORD_ALREADY_ALLOCATED` at HTTP 409. Sealing moves a pool `OPEN → SEALED`, after which no further record may be absorbed, and increments the pool's `version`; absorbing into or re-sealing an already-`SEALED` pool is `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`.

**Computing** a run is an analyst action that takes a single `variant` input, either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, and it is destructive by design: every existing allocation line of the run is deleted and the line set is built again from scratch. Four transitions are admissible, all triggered by the same operation and distinguished only by the current status and the submitted `variant`: `DRAFT → COMPUTED_PROVISIONAL` when the variant is `PROVISIONAL`, `DRAFT → COMPUTED_FINAL` when it is `FINAL`, `COMPUTED_PROVISIONAL → COMPUTED_FINAL` when a provisional model is re-struck on the final basis, and `REOPENED → COMPUTED_FINAL` when a reopened run is rebuilt. A reopened run may only ever be recomputed on the `FINAL` variant. Every compute requires that every shared cost pool attached to the run have at least one eligible participant, and a pool whose participant set contains no `ACTIVE` business unit at compute time fails the run with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` at HTTP 422. Compute sets `variant_applied`, `scoped_effective_cost_cents`, `total_allocated_cents` and `line_count`, and increments the run's `version` by 1.

The **in-scope record set** is fixed for both variants. A cost record is in scope when its `billing_period_start` is at or after the run's `period_start`, its `billing_period_end` is at or before the run's `period_end`, its `allocation_state` is either `UNALLOCATED` or `DISPUTED_HOLD`, and — when the run declares a `scope_business_unit_id` — its `business_unit_id` is that unit or one of that unit's descendants in the `parent_business_unit_id` tree. Records already `ALLOCATED` by another run, and records that have been `REVERSED`, are excluded. From that set, the **direct records** are those whose `shared_cost_pool_id` is null and whose `business_unit_id` is not null.

Direct records produce exactly one `DIRECT` line per business unit. Business units are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and within each unit its direct records are aggregated in ascending `created_at` order; the line's `allocated_cents` is the sum of each record's basis amount. Shared pools are then processed in ascending pool `created_at` order, each emitting the `SHARED` lines described below. Finally, `scoped_effective_cost_cents` is set to the sum of `effective_cost_cents` across the whole in-scope set, `total_allocated_cents` to the sum of `allocated_cents` across this run's `DIRECT` and `SHARED` lines only, and `line_count` to the count of *all* the run's lines, `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` lines included.

The two variants differ in exactly one operand, and that difference is the reconciliation story of the whole system. Under `PROVISIONAL`, a direct record's basis amount is its `list_cost_cents`, so direct lines are struck at undiscounted list cost and carry `method_applied` of `LIST_BASIS`; commitment discounts are deliberately not recognised, and `total_allocated_cents` therefore exceeds `scoped_effective_cost_cents` by exactly the commitment discount resting on the run's records. Under `FINAL`, a direct record's basis amount is its `effective_cost_cents`, so direct lines are struck net of commitment discount and carry `method_applied` of `EFFECTIVE_BASIS`; `total_allocated_cents` then satisfies the compensating invariant and equals `scoped_effective_cost_cents` exactly. `FINAL` is the only publishable variant, which is why a `COMPUTED_PROVISIONAL` run must be recomputed on `FINAL` before it can go out; that recompute deletes the provisional lines, rebuilds them on the final basis, sets `variant_applied` to `FINAL` and replaces `total_allocated_cents` with the final total.

**Shared pool distribution** runs once per pool, and it is engineered so that the distributed cents sum back to the pooled amount exactly. The participant list is first filtered to business units whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; an empty result is the `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` case above. Under `PROPORTIONAL`, each eligible participant's basis is its `direct_spend_cents` when `proportional_basis` is `SPEND`, its `headcount` when the basis is `HEADCOUNT`, and its `revenue_cents` when the basis is `REVENUE`. The basis total is the sum of those bases across eligible participants, and if that total is zero the run fails with `ERR_PROPORTIONAL_BASIS_ZERO` at HTTP 422 — division would otherwise be undefined. Each participant's exact share is the pooled amount multiplied by that participant's basis and divided by the basis total, and `method_applied` on the resulting line is `PROPORTIONAL`. Under `EVEN_SPLIT`, each participant's exact share is the pooled amount divided by the number of eligible participants, and `method_applied` is `EVEN_SPLIT`.

Each participant's allocation is then the **floor** of its exact share — floored, never rounded, so the running total can never overshoot. The leftover is the pooled amount less the sum of the floored allocations, and it is handed out one cent at a time under a pinned remainder rule: participants are ordered by the fractional part of their exact share descending, with ties broken by the participant business unit's `created_at` ascending, and UUID values must not participate in that ordering. Each participant reached while cents remain receives exactly one additional cent and has its line's `rounding_remainder_cents` set to 1; every other line keeps `rounding_remainder_cents` at 0. Each pool then emits one line per eligible participant with `line_kind` of `SHARED`, the pool recorded in `shared_cost_pool_id`, `allocated_cents` equal to the participant's final allocation, and `basis_value` equal to that participant's basis rounded half up to two decimal places. On `DIRECT` lines, on `EVEN_SPLIT` shared lines — where no basis is consulted — and on manual adjustments, `basis_value` stays at 0.00. `shared_cost_pool_id` is null on every line that is not `SHARED`, and `memo` is present on every line but null on every line that is not a `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`. Because the ordering of eligible participants is already `created_at` ascending, the remainder rule's tie-break on `created_at` reduces to that same list order.

**Manual adjustments** are the controller's escape hatch and the third leg of optimistic concurrency. Appending one takes `expected_allocation_run_version` matching the run's current `version`, the `business_unit_id` the adjustment lands on, the signed `allocated_cents`, and a `memo` explaining it. A stale version yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The only run statuses that declare the append are `COMPUTED_PROVISIONAL` and `COMPUTED_FINAL`; a `DRAFT`, `REOPENED`, `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED` run refuses it with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. An adjustment creates a line with `line_kind` of `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` and `method_applied` of `MANUAL`, carrying the explanation in `memo`; `allocated_cents` may be negative on this line kind and only on this line kind. The adjustment increments the run's `line_count` by 1 and the run's `version` by 1, but leaves `total_allocated_cents` untouched, since that total covers only `DIRECT` and `SHARED` lines. An adjustment that would drive the affected business unit's line total on the run below zero is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_WOULD_GO_NEGATIVE` at HTTP 422. Lines are read-only artifacts, discovered through the line listing or fetched individually; a line id that does not resolve is `ERR_ALLOCATION_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.

Compute, publication, reopening, closure, pool attachment or manual adjustment attempted from a run status that does not declare that transition is rejected with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` — and that includes attempting to publish a run whose `variant_applied` is still `PROVISIONAL`.

### 3.6. Publication, Chargeback Invoicing and Settlement

Publication is a `FinOpsController` action and the point at which a cost model becomes a financial fact. Moving `COMPUTED_FINAL → PUBLISHED` requires four things at once: `variant_applied` must be `FINAL`; the publishing user's `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `created_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; every shared cost pool attached to the run must be `SEALED`, or the request fails with `ERR_POOL_NOT_SEALED` at HTTP 409; and, when the run's `mode` is `CHARGEBACK`, every business unit appearing on a line must have `chargeback_enabled` set to true, or the request fails with `ERR_BU_NOT_CHARGEBACK_ENABLED` at HTTP 422.

Publication then does four things. It records `published_by_user_id`. It sets every cost record referenced by the run's `DIRECT` and `SHARED` lines to `allocation_state` `ALLOCATED` and stamps each with `allocated_by_run_id` pointing at this run. For a `CHARGEBACK` run it creates one **chargeback invoice** per business unit appearing on any of the run's lines — invoices are minted in ascending business-unit `created_at` order — each in `settlement_status` `UNBILLED` and `dispute_status` `NONE`, with `gross_amount_cents` equal to that unit's line total on the run at publish time summed across *every* `line_kind`, `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` lines included, so a unit that appears only on an adjustment still receives an invoice; `credited_amount_cents` and `paid_amount_cents` at 0; `net_due_cents` accordingly opening at `gross_amount_cents`; and `issued_at` and `dispute_window_ends_at` both null. And it recomputes the affected business units' derived aggregates — `showback_total_cents` for `SHOWBACK` runs and `chargeback_balance_cents` for `CHARGEBACK` runs. The run's `version` increments. Invoices are never created directly by a client; they are discovered through the invoice listing.

An invoice carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and they evolve without reference to each other. `settlement_status` tracks money movement through `UNBILLED`, `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`, `SETTLED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `VOIDED`, of which the last three are terminal. `dispute_status` tracks contestation through `NONE`, `DISPUTED` and `DISPUTE_RESOLVED`. Filing a dispute moves the dispute dimension while leaving the settlement dimension exactly where it was; issuing an invoice moves the settlement dimension and leaves the dispute dimension untouched.

Issuing an invoice moves it `UNBILLED → ISSUED` and is a controller action. It requires the issuing run to be in `PUBLISHED` status — a run that has since been reopened or closed refuses the issuance with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`, checked immediately after the invoice's own settlement status — the billed business unit to have `chargeback_enabled` true — otherwise `ERR_BU_NOT_CHARGEBACK_ENABLED` — and that unit's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_BU_SUSPENDED`. Issuance stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock and sets `dispute_window_ends_at` to exactly 168 hours — seven days — after `issued_at`, then recomputes `net_due_cents` and the unit's `chargeback_balance_cents`, and increments the invoice's `version`.

The **settlement ledger** governs the three money figures on an invoice. `credited_amount_cents` is the sum of `granted_credit_cents` across the invoice's disputes that reached `RESOLVED_ADJUSTED`. `paid_amount_cents` is the sum of the `payment_cents` recorded by settlement calls on the invoice. `net_due_cents` is zero whenever `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `VOIDED`, and otherwise is `gross_amount_cents` less `credited_amount_cents` less `paid_amount_cents`. A business unit's `chargeback_balance_cents` is then the sum of `net_due_cents` across that unit's invoices excluding any whose `settlement_status` is `VOIDED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` — which is precisely why a written-off invoice drops out of the balance identity entirely rather than contributing zero to it.

Settling is the one invoice action reserved to the billed `BusinessUnitOwner`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the invoice's `business_unit_id`. The owner submits `payment_cents`, a required non-negative integer, and the payment must **exactly clear** the invoice: `paid_amount_cents` plus the submitted `payment_cents` must equal `gross_amount_cents` less `credited_amount_cents`. Anything else — short or over — is rejected with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` at HTTP 422. Settlement is also refused while `dispute_status` is `DISPUTED`, and that refusal carries `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409, checked after the settlement-status admissibility check and before the exact-clearance arithmetic. A successful settlement is admissible from either `ISSUED → SETTLED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED → SETTLED`; in both cases it increases `paid_amount_cents`, drives `net_due_cents` to 0, recomputes the unit's `chargeback_balance_cents` and increments the invoice's `version`. Writing off is the controller's symmetric option, admissible from either `ISSUED → WRITTEN_OFF` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED → WRITTEN_OFF`, likewise refused while `dispute_status` is `DISPUTED` and likewise with `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`. It requires and records the supplied `memo` as the write-off explanation — recorded internally for audit rather than surfaced as a field on the invoice representation — drives `net_due_cents` to 0, recomputes `chargeback_balance_cents` and increments `version`. Issue, settle or write-off attempted from a settlement status that does not declare that transition is `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID`.

Reopening reverses a publication. A `FinOpsController` moves a run `PUBLISHED → REOPENED` with a `reason`, and the move is blocked in two circumstances: if any invoice of the run has reached `settlement_status` `SETTLED`, or if `reopen_count` has already reached its cap of 2. Either blocks the request with `ERR_RUN_REOPEN_BLOCKED` at HTTP 409. A permitted reopen takes a required `reason` string, which is consumed as the justification for the reversal but is not persisted onto the run or surfaced in the response. It increments `reopen_count` by 1, returns every cost record carrying this run's `allocated_by_run_id` to `allocation_state` `UNALLOCATED` and clears that reference, drives every invoice of the run to `VOIDED` as a system cascade — from `UNBILLED`, `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` alike, permitted only while the invoice is neither `SETTLED` nor already terminal, and recomputing `net_due_cents` to 0 as it goes — deletes every allocation line of the run and resets both `total_allocated_cents` and `line_count` to 0 while leaving `scoped_effective_cost_cents` and `variant_applied` standing at the values the last compute left, and recomputes both `chargeback_balance_cents` and `showback_total_cents` on the affected business units. A `REOPENED` run re-enters compute on the `FINAL` variant and can be published again, and the compensating invariant must hold after every recompute.

Closure is the run's terminal state and a compound gate: closability is derived from the aggregate state of the run's invoices and their disputes. A `FinOpsController` moves `PUBLISHED → CLOSED` only when either the run's `mode` is `SHOWBACK`, or every chargeback invoice of the run has `settlement_status` of `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; and, additionally, no dispute on any invoice of the run remains in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. A failure on either half is `ERR_RUN_NOT_FULLY_SETTLED` at HTTP 409. Closure freezes the run: no further compute, publication, reopening or manual adjustment is permitted, and the run's `version` increments one last time.

### 3.7. Disputes and Credit Adjudication

When a business unit believes it has been billed wrongly, its `BusinessUnitOwner` files a **dispute**. The payload names the `chargeback_invoice_id`, a `reason_code` drawn from `WRONG_OWNER`, `SHARED_METHODOLOGY`, `UNTAGGED_ABSORPTION` and `DOUBLE_CHARGE`, the strictly positive `claimed_credit_cents` being asked for, and a free-text `narrative` justifying the claim. The system copies `filed_by_business_unit_id` from the disputed invoice and stores the filer's `X-User-ID` as `filed_by_user_id`. Filing is admissible only while the invoice's `settlement_status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` and its `dispute_status` is `NONE`; otherwise the request is `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTABLE` at HTTP 409. It must also arrive while the logical clock is at or before the invoice's `dispute_window_ends_at`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` at HTTP 422, and the `claimed_credit_cents` may not exceed the invoice's outstanding amount after existing credits and payments — `gross_amount_cents` less `credited_amount_cents` less `paid_amount_cents` — else `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE` at HTTP 422.

A successful filing has a distinctive side effect on the cost ledger: the invoice's dispute dimension moves `NONE → DISPUTED`, its `version` increments, and **every cost record referenced by that invoice's lines is put on `DISPUTED_HOLD`** — the hold applies to exactly those records currently in `ALLOCATED`, so a record the publication never reached, or one already `REVERSED` or already on hold, is left where it is. Records on hold remain in scope for a later run's compute — recall that the in-scope set admits both `UNALLOCATED` and `DISPUTED_HOLD` — but they are no longer treated as cleanly allocated, and they will be returned to `ALLOCATED` the moment the dispute leaves its open population.

The dispute itself starts in `OPEN` and runs a bounded review conversation, capped at 3 rounds. A `FinOpsController` moves it `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires `review_round` to be strictly below 3 and increments `review_round` by 1. The controller may pause the review by asking for more information with a `question`, moving the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and recording the question — the `question` is required and is retained internally for audit rather than surfaced as a field on the dispute representation; that step requires `review_round` to be at or below 3. The filing owner answers with a required `response_text`, moving `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, appending the response to `narrative` separated from the existing text by a single newline, and incrementing `review_round` by 1; that step requires the acting owner's `X-User-ID` to equal `filed_by_business_unit_id` and `review_round` to be strictly below 3. Review and information requests may therefore alternate until `review_round` reaches 3, after which starting another round or accepting another response is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` at HTTP 409. Every one of these steps increments the dispute's `version`.

Resolution is a controller action from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and it is the third separated duty: the resolving user's `X-User-ID` must differ from `filed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Resolution takes an `outcome` of either `ADJUSTED` or `UPHELD`, and — for an adjusted outcome — the `granted_credit_cents` actually being awarded, which is required in that branch and must be a non-negative integer, zero included; under `UPHELD` the field is optional and, if supplied, validated as a non-negative integer and then discarded. Both branches require the invoice's `settlement_status` to be `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`, and an invoice outside those two states refuses the resolution with `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409, checked after the dispute's own state and before the segregation-of-duties comparison. The two branches are specified in full and in parallel:

- **`UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_ADJUSTED`** additionally requires the submitted `granted_credit_cents` to be no greater than the invoice's `gross_amount_cents` less its `credited_amount_cents` less its `paid_amount_cents`, else `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE` at HTTP 422. It sets `outcome` to `ADJUSTED`, records `granted_credit_cents` and `resolved_by_user_id`, and increases the invoice's `credited_amount_cents` by the granted credit, which lowers `net_due_cents`. When the granted credit is strictly positive and the sum of the granted credit, the existing `credited_amount_cents` and the existing `paid_amount_cents` is strictly less than `gross_amount_cents`, the invoice's settlement dimension moves `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_CREDITED` as a system cascade. The invoice change recomputes the business unit's `chargeback_balance_cents`, which in turn re-evaluates that unit's `ACTIVE` or `BREACHED` budget and may return a `BREACHED` budget to `ACTIVE`. Finally the invoice's dispute dimension moves `DISPUTED → DISPUTE_RESOLVED` and every cost record held on the invoice's lines returns from `DISPUTED_HOLD` to `ALLOCATED`.
- **`UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_UPHELD`** sets `outcome` to `UPHELD`, fixes `granted_credit_cents` at 0, and records `resolved_by_user_id`. No credit is applied, so the invoice's `credited_amount_cents` and settlement dimension are unchanged. The invoice's dispute dimension still moves `DISPUTED → DISPUTE_RESOLVED` and every cost record held on the invoice's lines still returns from `DISPUTED_HOLD` to `ALLOCATED`.

Both resolutions increment the invoice's `version` and the dispute's `version` by 1.

Withdrawal is the filing owner's exit, admissible from either `OPEN → WITHDRAWN` or `INFO_REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN`, requiring the acting owner's `X-User-ID` to equal `filed_by_business_unit_id`, and taking no request body at all. Both source states behave identically: the dispute lands in `WITHDRAWN`, the invoice's dispute dimension returns `DISPUTED → NONE` — permitted only when no other dispute on that invoice is still in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — every held cost record returns from `DISPUTED_HOLD` to `ALLOCATED`, and both the invoice's and the dispute's `version` increment. Because the invoice returns to `NONE` rather than to `DISPUTE_RESOLVED`, a fresh dispute may be filed against it while the dispute window is still open. `RESOLVED_ADJUSTED`, `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and `WITHDRAWN` are the dispute's terminal states, and starting review, requesting information, responding, resolving or withdrawing from a status that does not declare that transition is `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Waste Control: Idle Detection, Rightsizing and Remediation Windows

The **idle-detection scan** is a `FinOpsAnalyst` capability, optionally narrowed to one `business_unit_id` — accepted either as a field of the request body or as a `business_unit_id` query parameter, the body taking precedence when both are supplied, and an absent or blank value meaning "every unit" — that walks every cloud resource currently in `lifecycle_state` `ACTIVE` in a pinned order — `monthly_run_rate_cents` descending, then `created_at` ascending — and skips any resource that already carries a remediation task in a non-terminal status. A resource whose `monthly_run_rate_cents` is below 20,000 cents is skipped as too small to bother with. Above that floor, utilization decides the verdict: a resource whose `utilization_pct` is at or below 5.00 is idle and draws an `IDLE_TERMINATE` proposal whose `projected_savings_cents` is the full `monthly_run_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; a resource whose `utilization_pct` is above 5.00 but at or below 30.00 is oversized and draws a `RIGHTSIZE` proposal whose `projected_savings_cents` is half of `monthly_run_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; a resource above 30.00 is left alone.

Each proposal carries a composite `waste_score`. Take 100 less the resource's `utilization_pct` and multiply by 0.6; separately take `monthly_run_rate_cents` divided by 25,000 and cap that second term at 40; add the two; cap the sum at 100; and round half up to two decimal places. The task also records `observed_utilization_pct`, the `utilization_pct` read from the resource at scan time, and sets `auto_remediation_eligible` to true only when both conditions hold: the `waste_score` is at or above 60.00 **and** the resource's `environment` is not the literal string `prod`. Anything in production is therefore never a candidate for unattended execution, no matter how wasteful. Each proposal is emitted as a `RemediationTask` in `PROPOSED` status with `realized_savings_cents` at 0, `snooze_count` at 0, and both `remediation_window_starts_at` and `remediation_window_ends_at` null, and the resource is moved `ACTIVE → FLAGGED_IDLE` for an `IDLE_TERMINATE` proposal or `ACTIVE → RIGHTSIZE_PENDING` for a `RIGHTSIZE` proposal. Tasks born of a scan are discovered through the task listing.

An analyst may also raise a task by hand on a resource a scan has already flagged, for the case where the automated proposal picked the wrong `kind`. The payload names the `cloud_resource_id` and the desired `kind`, and the task is created in `PROPOSED` with `waste_score`, `observed_utilization_pct` and `auto_remediation_eligible` derived from the resource's current telemetry by the same evaluation, while `projected_savings_cents` follows the **submitted** `kind` — the whole `monthly_run_rate_cents` for `IDLE_TERMINATE`, half of it for `RIGHTSIZE` — rather than the verdict the scan itself would have reached. Neither the 20,000-cent floor nor the utilization bands are re-tested on this path, so a hand-raised task is accepted on any remediable resource whatever its telemetry, and nothing forbids a second task on a resource that already carries an open one. Manual creation, owner notification and owner approval all require the underlying resource to be in `lifecycle_state` `FLAGGED_IDLE` or `RIGHTSIZE_PENDING`; a resource in any other lifecycle state is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_REMEDIABLE` at HTTP 409.

Notifying the owner moves a task `PROPOSED → OWNER_NOTIFIED` and opens the clock on it: `remediation_window_starts_at` is set to the current logical clock and `remediation_window_ends_at` to 72 hours — three days — later. No two windows on the same cloud resource may share an instant, so a new notification whose window would touch or overlap an existing open window on that resource is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409. That comparison looks only at the resource's other tasks whose status is `OWNER_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_SNOOZED` or `APPROVED` and whose two window endpoints are both set, and it is made only on this `PROPOSED → OWNER_NOTIFIED` opening — a re-notification of a snoozed task never runs it.

Inside the window the resource owner has three moves, and each requires the acting `ResourceOwner`'s `X-User-ID` to equal the resource's `owner_user_id`. **Snoozing** moves `OWNER_NOTIFIED → OWNER_SNOOZED`, extends `remediation_window_ends_at` by a further 72 hours and increments `snooze_count` by 1; it requires the clock to be at or before the current `remediation_window_ends_at`, else `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, and requires `snooze_count` to be strictly below its cap of 2, else `ERR_SNOOZE_LIMIT` at HTTP 409. An analyst may re-notify a snoozed task, moving `OWNER_SNOOZED → OWNER_NOTIFIED` and recording a fresh notification without moving `remediation_window_ends_at`; that too requires the clock to be at or before the window end. Notification and snoozing therefore alternate while the window is open, up to the two-snooze cap. **Approving** moves the task along either `OWNER_NOTIFIED → APPROVED` or `OWNER_SNOOZED → APPROVED`, requires the clock to be at or before `remediation_window_ends_at`, and has no side effects beyond the status change — the work itself happens on the next sweep. **Cancelling** with a `reason` moves the task along either `OWNER_NOTIFIED → CANCELLED` or `OWNER_SNOOZED → CANCELLED`; an analyst may also take a task `PROPOSED → CANCELLED`, with no precondition at all. Cancellation is open to a `FinOpsAnalyst` from all three of those source states, not merely from `PROPOSED`; `reason` is required on every path, the `PROPOSED` one included; the window's expiry is never consulted; and the resource's remediability is not re-tested, so a cancellation still succeeds after the resource has moved on. Cancellation in all three cases cascades the cloud resource back to `ACTIVE` — `FLAGGED_IDLE → ACTIVE` or `RIGHTSIZE_PENDING → ACTIVE` — but only when no other remediation task on that resource remains in a non-terminal status. Every one of these owner and analyst actions increments the task's `version` by 1.

The **remediation-window sweep** is the analyst-run capability that actually executes work, and it forks three ways on a single pass, visiting every task in ascending `created_at` order and taking no scoping argument. A task in `APPROVED` executes: it moves `APPROVED → EXECUTED`, sets `realized_savings_cents` equal to `projected_savings_cents`, and cascades according to `kind`. A task in `OWNER_NOTIFIED` whose `remediation_window_ends_at` has passed forks on `auto_remediation_eligible`: when the flag is true the task moves `OWNER_NOTIFIED → AUTO_EXECUTED` with the same savings booking and the same cascades; when the flag is false the task instead moves `OWNER_NOTIFIED → CANCELLED` and the resource is returned to `ACTIVE` when no other non-terminal task remains on it. The two branches of that fork carry completely different side-effect sets, and the flag alone decides which one applies. "Has passed" is strict: a window ending exactly at the current clock has not yet passed and the task is left where it is. The sweep considers only those two statuses — a `PROPOSED` or `OWNER_SNOOZED` task is passed over untouched however long its window has been expired — and the pass reports back exactly the tasks it moved, in that same order. `EXECUTED`, `AUTO_EXECUTED` and `CANCELLED` are the task's terminal states, and notify, snooze, approve or cancel attempted from a status that does not declare that transition is `ERR_REMEDIATION_STATE_INVALID`.

The cascades on execution differ by `kind`, and both fire identically whether the task reached `EXECUTED` or `AUTO_EXECUTED`. A `RIGHTSIZE` task moves the resource `RIGHTSIZE_PENDING → RESIZED` — only from `RIGHTSIZE_PENDING`, a resource sitting in any other lifecycle state keeping it — and reduces its `monthly_run_rate_cents` by the task's `realized_savings_cents`, floored at 0 so the rate never goes negative, then recomputes the owning unit's `tracked_run_rate_cents`. An `IDLE_TERMINATE` task moves the resource `FLAGGED_IDLE → TERMINATED`, which is the deepest cascade in the system: `monthly_run_rate_cents` is set to 0; every cost record whose `cloud_resource_id` is this resource and whose `allocation_state` is `UNALLOCATED` or `ALLOCATED` is set to `REVERSED`; each reversed record that carries an `applied_commitment_id` drops out of that commitment's `covered_spend_cents`, which recomputes that commitment's `utilization_pct` and `waste_cents` and may move it to `UNDER_UTILIZED`; the owning provisioning request bubbles `PROVISIONED → TERMINATED`; and the owning business unit's `resource_count`, `tracked_run_rate_cents` and `direct_spend_cents` are all recomputed. `TERMINATED` is the resource's terminal state.

A resized resource is not stranded. When telemetry later reports a submitted `utilization_pct` strictly above 30.00, the resource moves `RESIZED → ACTIVE` and the new utilization is stored — at which point a later scan may flag it all over again.

### 3.9. Spend Anomalies, Budget Discipline and Unit Economics

**Anomaly detection** runs as an analyst-triggered scan, optionally narrowed to one `business_unit_id` — again accepted in the request body or as a `business_unit_id` query parameter, the body winning when both appear — and it evaluates each `ACTIVE` business unit independently, in ascending unit `created_at` order. The unit's cost records whose `allocation_state` is not `REVERSED` are ordered by `billing_period_start` descending and then `created_at` descending. A unit with fewer than four such records is skipped outright. Otherwise `observed_cents` is the `effective_cost_cents` of the newest record, and `baseline_cents` is the mean of the `effective_cost_cents` of the next three records, floored to whole cents; a unit whose baseline floors to 0 is also skipped. From there, `delta_cents` is `observed_cents` less `baseline_cents`, and `spike_ratio` is `observed_cents` divided by `baseline_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

An alert is emitted only when **both** gates clear, each inclusive at its boundary: `spike_ratio` must be at least 1.20 **and** `delta_cents` must be at least 50,000 cents. Anything else is skipped silently.

For an observation that clears both gates, `severity_score` is the sum of three components, rounded half up to two decimal places. The ratio component is `spike_ratio` less 1.20, multiplied by 25, floored at 0 and capped at 50. The magnitude component is `delta_cents` divided by 100,000, capped at 30. The budget component contributes 20 points when the business unit has a budget in `BREACHED` status whose period covers the observed cost record; when such a covering budget exists but is not breached, it contributes 12 points if that budget's `actual_spend_cents` divided by its `amount_cents`, multiplied by 100, is at least 80.00; and in every other case, including the absence of a covering budget, it contributes 0. "Covers the observed cost record" means the budget's half-open period contains that record's `billing_period_start`; the `BREACHED` search runs first and, only if it comes up empty, the pressure test is applied to a covering budget in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status — a covering budget that has been `CLOSED` is invisible to both searches and so contributes 0 — and where several of the unit's budgets cover the instant the earliest-created one is the one consulted. A covering budget whose `amount_cents` is zero contributes 0 rather than dividing by it.

The score bands into `severity`: `CRITICAL` at 70 or above, `HIGH` at 45 or above, `MEDIUM` at 25 or above, and `LOW` below that. The alert is created with `detected_at` set to the current logical clock, in `DETECTED` status, and immediately moves `DETECTED → ROUTED` in the routing step of the same scan once `severity` is set — `routed_queue` becoming `CONTROLLER_PAGER` for `HIGH` and `CRITICAL`, `BU_OWNER_INBOX` for `MEDIUM`, and `WEEKLY_DIGEST` for `LOW`. Routing is part of the same indivisible synthesis, so `DETECTED` is never externally observable: the alert first becomes readable already in `ROUTED` with `routed_queue` populated and `version` at 1, and no client can catch it in between. Before attribution, `root_cause_cloud_resource_id`, `root_cause_kind` and `acknowledged_by_user_id` are all null. Alerts synthesised by a scan are discovered through the alert listing.

Triage is an analyst's job. Acknowledging moves an alert `ROUTED → ACKNOWLEDGED` with no precondition and stores the acting user's `X-User-ID` as `acknowledged_by_user_id`. Attributing moves it `ACKNOWLEDGED → ATTRIBUTED` and takes both a `root_cause_cloud_resource_id` and a `root_cause_kind` drawn from `NEW_RESOURCE`, `UTILIZATION_SPIKE`, `COMMITMENT_EXPIRY` and `UNTAGGED_ABSORPTION`; the submitted resource must belong to the alert's own `business_unit_id` and must not be in `lifecycle_state` `TERMINATED`. Those two guards are checked after the status gate and in that order, and they answer differently: a `TERMINATED` root cause is `ERR_RESOURCE_STATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409, while a resource belonging to some other business unit is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400. A `FinOpsController` closes the alert by supplying a `resolution`, and the two closures start from different states: `resolution` of `EXPLAINED` moves `ATTRIBUTED → CLOSED_EXPLAINED` and requires `root_cause_kind` to be non-null, while `resolution` of `FALSE_POSITIVE` moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED_FALSE_POSITIVE` directly and clears both `root_cause_cloud_resource_id` and `root_cause_kind`. Both closures are terminal, and each triage step increments the alert's `version`. Acknowledging, attributing or closing from a status that does not declare that transition — closing as explained before attribution, for instance — is `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID`.

**Budgets** give a business unit a spending envelope. A `FinOpsAnalyst` or `FinOpsController` drafts one against a `business_unit_id` for the half-open period bounded by `period_start` and `period_end`, with a strictly positive `amount_cents` and a non-negative initial `forecast_cents`, plus an optional `accuracy_basis` that defaults to `MAPE_BASIS` and may otherwise be `PERIOD_END_BASIS`. The budget starts in `DRAFT` with an empty `forecast_history`, `revision_count` at 0, `variance_explanation_text` null, `accuracy_basis_applied` null, `warning_flag` false, and `actual_spend_cents`, `variance_cents`, `variance_pct` and `forecast_accuracy_pct` all at zero. A business unit may not hold two non-`CLOSED` budgets whose periods overlap; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_BUDGET_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` at HTTP 409. A `FinOpsController` moves the budget `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which requires the governed unit to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_BU_SUSPENDED`.

Revising the standing forecast is the budget leg of optimistic concurrency: it requires `expected_budget_version` matching the budget's current `version`, and a stale value is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. A revision appends the *previous* `forecast_cents` onto `forecast_history`, replaces `forecast_cents` with the submitted value, and increments both `revision_count` and `version` by 1. The version comparison is made before any state test. A revision is admissible from `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `BREACHED` alike — the forecast can be re-cut before activation and while in breach — and only a `CLOSED` budget refuses it, with `ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID`. The submitted `forecast_cents` must be a non-negative integer.

Evaluating a budget recomputes its whole picture. It is admissible only from `ACTIVE` or `BREACHED` — a `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` budget is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID` — and it takes no request body and increments `version` by 1 on every call, even the idempotent one where every recomputed figure comes back identical to what was already stored. `actual_spend_cents` is the sum of two components. The **invoiced component** sums, across the unit's chargeback invoices whose `settlement_status` is not `VOIDED` and whose issuing run's `period_start` is at or after the budget's `period_start` and whose issuing run's `period_end` is at or before the budget's `period_end`, each invoice's `gross_amount_cents` less its `credited_amount_cents`. The **uninvoiced component** sums the `effective_cost_cents` of the unit's cost records whose `allocation_state` is still `UNALLOCATED` and whose billing period sits inside the budget period — containment here being inclusive at both ends, the record's `billing_period_start` at or after the budget's `period_start` and its `billing_period_end` at or before the budget's `period_end`, exactly as for the run periods above. From that total, `variance_cents` is `actual_spend_cents` less `amount_cents`, and `variance_pct` is `variance_cents` divided by `amount_cents`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places. `warning_flag` is true when `variance_pct` is at or above 5.00 but strictly below 10.00, and false otherwise.

`forecast_accuracy_pct` is produced by whichever variant the budget's `accuracy_basis` names, and `accuracy_basis_applied` records which one was actually used on the most recent evaluation. Under `MAPE_BASIS` — the default, which scores the whole revision trail — take each historical forecast in `forecast_history`, compute the absolute difference between it and `actual_spend_cents` divided by `actual_spend_cents`, average those values across every entry in the trail, multiply the mean by 100, subtract the result from 100, and round half up to two decimal places; the current standing `forecast_cents` is deliberately excluded from this trail. Under `PERIOD_END_BASIS` — which scores only the latest standing forecast — take the absolute difference between `forecast_cents` and `actual_spend_cents`, divide by `actual_spend_cents`, multiply by 100, subtract from 100, and round half up to two decimal places; `forecast_history` is deliberately ignored. Two degenerate cases are pinned ahead of either variant: when the recomputed `actual_spend_cents` is 0 there is nothing to score against and `forecast_accuracy_pct` is 0.00 under both bases; and when `MAPE_BASIS` is in force over an as-yet-empty `forecast_history` — a budget that has never been revised — the trail carries no error at all and `forecast_accuracy_pct` is 100.00. `accuracy_basis_applied` is stamped with the budget's `accuracy_basis` on every evaluation regardless.

The evaluation then drives the budget's status. `ACTIVE → BREACHED` occurs when the recomputed `variance_pct` is at or above 10.00, and it does two things: it stores `actual_spend_cents`, `variance_cents`, `variance_pct`, `forecast_accuracy_pct`, `accuracy_basis_applied` and `warning_flag`, and it gates approval of any further provisioning request for that business unit until `variance_explanation_text` has been recorded. `BREACHED → ACTIVE` occurs when the recomputed `variance_pct` falls strictly below 10.00, recomputing `actual_spend_cents`, `variance_cents`, `variance_pct` and `warning_flag` and releasing the provisioning gate. That un-breach can arrive either from an explicit evaluation or as a system cascade when a dispute credit lowers the unit's invoiced spend, so a budget legitimately oscillates between `ACTIVE` and `BREACHED` as spend and credits move. Recording the mandatory narrative in `variance_explanation_text` is available to the billed `BusinessUnitOwner` or a `FinOpsController` and unblocks provisioning without changing the budget's status. A controller may close a budget from either `ACTIVE → CLOSED` or `BREACHED → CLOSED`, with no precondition on either path and no side effects beyond the status change; `CLOSED` is terminal. Every budget mutation increments its `version` by 1, and activation, revision, evaluation, explanation or closure attempted from a status that does not declare that transition is `ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID`.

**Unit economics** close the loop back to the business. An analyst or controller registers a driver against a `business_unit_id` and an `allocation_run_id`, naming the `driver_name` — one of `ORDERS`, `ACTIVE_USERS`, `API_CALLS` or `SHIPMENTS` — and the `driver_volume` observed for the run period. The volume must be a strictly positive integer so that cost per unit is always defined; zero or negative is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_VOLUME_INVALID` at HTTP 422. The driver is stored with every computed figure still unset — `base_allocated_cents` and `platform_overhead_cents` at 0, `provisional_cost_per_unit_cents` and `final_cost_per_unit_cents` at 0.00, and `efficiency_tier` null — and the referenced run must be in `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED` status for the computation to have meaning. That run-status requirement is enforced at computation time and not at registration, so a driver may be registered against a `DRAFT` or merely computed run and it is the compute call that then refuses with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409 until the run reaches `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED`.

The computation has a **pinned evaluation order** that resolves an apparent circularity: the overhead rate depends on the cost-per-unit tier, and the final cost per unit depends on the overhead. The circle is broken by pinning the tier to the provisional figure and never re-deriving it. First, `base_allocated_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` across every allocation line of the referenced run whose `business_unit_id` is the driver's business unit, taken across all values of `line_kind` including `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`. Second, `provisional_cost_per_unit_cents` is `base_allocated_cents` divided by `driver_volume`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Third, `efficiency_tier` is pinned from that provisional figure alone: `EFFICIENT` when it is at or below 50.00, `STANDARD` when it is above 50.00 but at or below 250.00, and `INEFFICIENT` above 250.00. Fourth, `platform_overhead_cents` is `base_allocated_cents` multiplied by the pinned tier's overhead rate — 0.03 for `EFFICIENT`, 0.07 for `STANDARD` and 0.12 for `INEFFICIENT` — rounded half up to whole cents. Fifth, `final_cost_per_unit_cents` is `base_allocated_cents` plus `platform_overhead_cents`, divided by `driver_volume`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The tier is **not** recomputed from that final figure, even when the final figure lands in a different tier band. Computing increments the driver's `version` by 1, and a driver id that does not resolve is `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every other successful operation, including state transitions, telemetry pushes, scans, sweeps, computations and all reads, returns `200 OK`.
