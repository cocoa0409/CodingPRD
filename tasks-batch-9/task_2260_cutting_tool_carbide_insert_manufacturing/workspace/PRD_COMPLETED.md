# Cutting Tool & Carbide Insert Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A metal cutting tool manufacturer does not sell a shape; it sells a promise about how long an edge will survive in a named workpiece material. That promise is assembled from four largely independent engineering disciplines — carbide powder metallurgy, insert and chip-breaker geometry, coating deposition chemistry, and precision grinding — and it is only credible once machining trials on real workpiece material have measured flank wear and surface finish under controlled conditions. Today those four disciplines keep their own records, and the commercial organisation quotes lead times and prices from whichever spreadsheet was most recently refreshed. When a metallurgist revises a blend recipe, nobody downstream learns that the released products built on the superseded recipe are no longer covered by their qualification evidence, and confirmed customer orders continue to move toward shipment on an obsolete substrate.

This document specifies the Cutting Tool & Carbide Insert Manufacturing API, a single system of record mounted under `/tooling` that governs the whole chain: grade development and the blend-press-sinter route, geometry and chip-breaker release, coating recipes and deposition chamber loading, grinding wheel life and cell scheduling, product definition as a join of grade, geometry and coating, qualification through machining trials, catalog and engineered-special order fulfilment, application-engineering speeds and feeds advice, and the reconditioning service that extends tool life through successive regrind passes. Every collection sits directly beneath the API prefix and links to its neighbours by identifier rather than by URL nesting, because the same grade feeds many products and the same product is ground, coated, ordered and reconditioned by different departments on different schedules.

Two structural commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **evidence is pinned, not referenced**: when a product enters qualification it captures the revision numbers of the grade, geometry and coating recipe it was tested against, and when any of those upstream definitions advances, the product is automatically restricted, its supply posture is downgraded, and confirmed orders are pushed back into engineering review. Second, **the party that produces evidence may not be the party that accepts it**: the quality authority that approves a qualification dossier may be neither the engineer who submitted it nor any engineer who operated one of its trials. The rules below are written so that both commitments hold without any client having to remember them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts, conventions and cross-cutting principles that apply across every endpoint in the API. Where a rule below conflicts with a convenience, the rule wins.

### 2.1. Caller Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. There is exactly one exception to the identity requirement: the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.5 may be called without `X-User-ID`, because test harnesses reposition the clock before any user context exists; when headers are supplied on that endpoint the role must still be one of the six staff roles.

Seven roles exist, and their spellings are exact:

- `MetallurgyEngineer` owns carbide grade development and the powder blend, press and sinter route, including the sintering QC verdict.
- `GeometryEngineer` owns insert and solid-carbide geometry, chip breaker development, and the product definition that binds grade, geometry and coating together.
- `CoatingEngineer` owns coating recipes, deposition chamber scheduling, and adhesion and thickness measurement.
- `ApplicationEngineer` owns qualification dossiers, machining trials, speeds-and-feeds advice, and the commercial side of engineered-special orders.
- `QualityApprover` is the independent quality authority: it decides dossiers, releases and withdraws products, and quarantines blend batches.
- `ProductionPlanner` owns grinding wheels and cells, product supply posture, customer accounts and credit, order fulfilment, and the reconditioning shop floor.
- `Customer` is the buying account.

Six of these seven roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `MetallurgyEngineer`, `GeometryEngineer`, `CoatingEngineer`, `ApplicationEngineer`, `QualityApprover` or `ProductionPlanner`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the specification says so — as `authored_by_user_id` on a carbide grade, as `operator_user_id` on a machining trial, and as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a qualification dossier — and those recorded values are later compared against each other to enforce segregation of duties.

The `Customer` role is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare that resource's `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Customer` caller that reads or mutates a tool order or a reconditioning job whose `customer_id` is not its own value, or that supplies a `customer_id` other than its own in a request body, is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This scope check governs seven operations: order creation, order submission, order cancellation, single-order reads, reconditioning job creation, single-job reads, and single-customer reads — a `Customer` reading `/tooling/customers/{customerId}` for an account that is not its own is rejected with the same code. List reads are not rejected but silently narrowed instead: when a `Customer` lists tool orders or reconditioning jobs, the server forces the `customer_id` filter to the caller's own `X-User-ID`, overriding any `customer_id` query parameter the caller supplied, so the page can only ever contain the caller's own rows. The staff roles are not scope-restricted: an `ApplicationEngineer` may raise an order on behalf of any customer.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Requests that never reach an endpoint at all are wrapped in the same envelope with an `error_id` outside the catalogue of §5: a path matching no route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a path that exists but does not accept the method used answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 405, an unparseable request line answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400, and any unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets the number of rows to skip before the returned page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not parseable as an integer, a `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the same two parameters are accepted and validated identically on the single-resource reads and on the computed cutting-envelope read, where they simply have no effect on the result. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and because `created_at` is written at millisecond resolution and two rows can therefore share it, ties are broken by the order in which the server first wrote the rows, so a stable page walk with a fixed `limit` and an advancing `offset` visits every row exactly once.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page — computed as `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned being strictly less than `total_count`, so an `offset` past the end of the collection yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this system — carbide grades, powder blend batches, insert geometries, coating recipes, coating runs, grinding wheels, grinding runs, tool products, qualification dossiers, machining trials, customers, tool orders and reconditioning jobs — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix, no sequence number and no domain-flavoured decoration.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was first written. These two bookkeeping stamps, and the `api_request_timestamp` of the envelope, are the one place the server reads its real wall clock rather than the logical shop clock of §2.5; the logical clock governs only the temporal rules and the one field that quotes it explicitly, an order's `shipped_at`.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, equal to `created_at` at the moment of creation and refreshed on every subsequent mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade from another request.
- **`version`** (integer): starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when approving a dossier rewrites a product's `tool_life_index`, the product's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though the client never addressed the product.

No resource in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`; the four properties above are universal.

Several fields the specification describes as belonging to a row are **recomputed on every read from the rows they summarise** rather than stored: a grade's `qualified_product_count`, a coating recipe's `adhesion_class` and `is_validated`, a grinding wheel's `remaining_life_units`, a tool product's `open_order_units`, a dossier's `completed_trial_count`, `passed_trial_count` and `composite_score`, and a customer's `reserved_credit_cents`. They are always current in the response body, and a change in one of them is not by itself a mutation of the summarising row: the row's `version` and `updated_at` advance only where a rule below says that row is mutated. Every other field named in this document is stored on its own row.

Business codes are separate from `id` and are supplied by the caller: `grade_code`, `batch_code`, `geometry_code`, `recipe_code`, `wheel_code`, `sku`, `dossier_code`, `trial_code`, `customer_code`, `order_code` and `job_code` must each be unique across their own collection, compared as exact case-sensitive strings, and a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Apart from `grade_code`, whose length band is stated in §3.1, no length or character constraint applies to any of them beyond being a JSON string.

### 2.5. The Logical Shop Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — whether a scheduling window lies in the past, and whether a run's window has elapsed before completion is permitted — reads a **logical UTC clock** held by the server rather than the machine's wall clock. On startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a client repositions it, that is the value every temporal rule sees; the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A test-driving capability lets any staff role reposition the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes the resulting `current_utc` back. `current_utc` is a mandatory ISO 8601 datetime string — a body missing it, or carrying any other field, or carrying a value that does not parse, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a value without an explicit offset is read as UTC; and the echo in `data` is the caller's own string reproduced verbatim rather than a normalised rendering, with `data` holding nothing but that single `current_utc` key. Every datetime this API accepts is parsed the same way — `current_utc` and the `window_start` and `window_end` of a coating or grinding run alike: a trailing `Z` in either case, an explicit numeric offset, or no offset at all are all accepted, surrounding whitespace is ignored, and the instant is converted to UTC before any comparison, so two spellings of the same instant behave identically. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and fires no scheduled work retroactively: a coating run whose window has now elapsed does not complete itself, it merely becomes completable.

### 2.6. Parallel Status Dimensions

Two entities in this system carry **two independent status dimensions each**, and neither dimension implies the other.

A tool product carries `release_status`, which is the commercial release dimension and takes the values `DEVELOPMENT`, `QUALIFICATION`, `RELEASED`, `RESTRICTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, alongside `supply_status`, which is the fulfilment dimension and takes the values `UNAVAILABLE`, `MAKE_TO_ORDER` and `STOCKED`. A product may be `RELEASED` and `UNAVAILABLE` at once; it may equally be `RESTRICTED` and `MAKE_TO_ORDER`. Where a rule needs both, it says both.

A powder blend batch carries `stage`, the physical process dimension, taking the values `BLENDED`, `PRESSED`, `SINTERED` and `CONSUMED`, alongside `qc_status`, the metallurgical acceptance dimension, taking the values `PENDING`, `PASSED`, `FAILED` and `QUARANTINED`. Quarantining a batch moves `qc_status` from `PASSED` to `QUARANTINED` and deliberately leaves `stage` untouched — a `SINTERED` batch remains `SINTERED`.

### 2.7. Revision Pinning and the Restriction Cascade

Grades, geometries and coating recipes each carry a `revision` counter that starts at 1 and advances by 1 whenever the definition is revised. Products do not follow those definitions live; they **pin** them, recording `pinned_grade_revision`, `pinned_geometry_revision` and `pinned_coating_revision` (the last remaining null for an uncoated product). A coating run likewise pins `pinned_recipe_revision` at scheduling time, and a blend batch pins `pinned_grade_revision` at blend time.

When a grade, a geometry or a coating recipe is revised, a four-hop cascade runs inside the same request, and each hop exists **because** the previous hop happened:

1. The upstream definition's `revision` advances by 1.
2. Every tool product referencing that definition whose corresponding pinned revision is now lower than the new revision, and whose `release_status` is `RELEASED`, moves `RELEASED` → `RESTRICTED`.
3. Because those products became `RESTRICTED`, each one whose `supply_status` is `STOCKED` moves `STOCKED` → `MAKE_TO_ORDER`. Products already at `MAKE_TO_ORDER` or `UNAVAILABLE` are left alone.
4. Because that `supply_status` became `MAKE_TO_ORDER`, every tool order on such a product whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED` → `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` and has `requires_reconfirmation` set to true.

Independently of the cascade, every product referencing the revised definition has `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible` recomputed, because both derive from the *current* status of the referenced grade, geometry and recipe. Each row the cascade touches — the definition itself, every restricted product, every reopened order — has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed. A product's `version` advances only when the request actually changed something the product stores: either the cascade restricted it, or the recomputation landed a different `engineering_lead_time_days` or `expedite_eligible` than the product already held. A product that the cascade neither restricted nor re-valued — a product still in `DEVELOPMENT`, say, or one whose lead-time contributions did not move — keeps its `version` and `updated_at` untouched even though it was inspected. Products are walked in `created_at` ascending order, and within a single request hop 3 and hop 4 run to completion for one product before the next product is considered.

A restricted product is not repaired by revising anything further. It is repaired by **repinning**, which captures the current revisions again and returns the product to `QUALIFICATION`, from which the quality authority may release it once more.

### 2.8. Reference Resolution Order

No endpoint in this API nests two resource identifiers: every collection sits directly under `/tooling`, and a path carries at most one identifier — `{gradeId}`, `{batchId}`, `{geometryId}`, `{recipeId}`, `{runId}`, `{wheelId}`, `{productId}`, `{dossierId}`, `{trialId}`, `{customerId}`, `{orderId}` or `{jobId}`. Identifiers are nevertheless resolved in a fixed order: the path identifier first, then each foreign key supplied in the request body in the order the body's fields are documented. The first identifier that matches no row terminates the request with that entity's own not-found code, and no later reference is examined. A grinding run creation that names both an unknown wheel and an unknown product therefore reports `ERR_WHEEL_NOT_FOUND`, because `wheel_id` is documented before `product_id`. Being well-formed is a prerequisite for being looked up: an identifier field that is present but is not a non-empty string — the empty string, a number, an object — is a type violation caught at rung 3 or 4 of §2.11 and reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, never as a not-found. An optional identifier supplied as an explicit `null` — `coating_recipe_id`, `base_catalog_product_id`, `predecessor_job_id` — is read as omitted and resolves to nothing.

The not-found codes are entity-specific: `ERR_GRADE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEOMETRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COATING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WHEEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRINDING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOSSIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`.

Because nothing is nested in the URL, reaching a child collection from its parent is done by filtering: blend batches by `grade_id`, `stage` and `qc_status`; coating runs by `recipe_id`, `chamber_id` and `status`; grinding runs by `wheel_id`, `product_id`, `cell_id` and `status`; tool products by `grade_id`, `geometry_id`, `coating_recipe_id`, `base_catalog_product_id`, `release_status` and `order_class`; dossiers by `product_id` and `status`; machining trials by `dossier_id`, `batch_id`, `status` and `verdict`; tool orders by `customer_id`, `product_id` and `status`; reconditioning jobs by `customer_id`, `product_id`, `predecessor_job_id`, `wheel_id` and `status`; grades by `status` and `target_workpiece_group`; geometries by `application_class` and `status`; coating recipes by `deposition_type` and `is_validated`; grinding wheels by `status`; and customers by `tier` and `account_status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and an unsupplied filter constrains nothing. A filter whose domain is an enum — every one above except the identifier filters — rejects a value outside that enum with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than returning an empty page; an identifier filter is matched as an opaque exact string, so an identifier that names no row simply yields an empty page. The one boolean filter, `is_validated` on coating recipes, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the enum filters, by contrast, are matched case-sensitively against their exact spellings. Query parameters other than these filters and the two pagination parameters are ignored rather than rejected: the unknown-field rule of §2.11 governs request bodies only.

### 2.9. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every rounded quantity in this system uses **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every rounding step names its own scale:

- Sintering yield (`yield_pct`) and dossier scores (`composite_score`, and each per-trial subscore that feeds it) are rounded to 2 decimal places.
- Tool life figures (`rated_life_min`, `projected_life_min`, `tool_life_index`) and reconditioning percentages (`life_extension_pct`, `warranted_life_extension_pct`) are rounded to 1 decimal place.
- Coating thickness (`total_thickness_um`, `measured_thickness_um`) and loaded substrate area (`loaded_area_cm2`) are carried at 2 decimal places; measured diameter deviation (`measured_diameter_dev_um`) and measured lengths (`measured_length_mm`, `measured_corner_wear_um`) at 1 decimal place.
- Money is held as whole integer cents throughout — `list_price_cents`, `per_unit_net_cents`, `total_price_cents`, `regrind_credit_cents`, `reserved_credit_cents` and `rebate_cents` — and any rounding to cents is round half up at the point the specification names, never later.
- Lead times (`engineering_lead_time_days`, `promised_lead_time_days`), abrasive life (`initial_life_units`, `consumed_life_units`, `remaining_life_units`) and every unit count are whole integers computed with integer arithmetic; no rounding step applies to them.

The scales above bind the **server's own** rounding steps. Where a scale is quoted alongside a field the *caller* supplies — `planned_mass_kg` and `measured_density_gcc` at two places, `co_binder_pct` and `wc_grain_um` at one, `loaded_area_cm2` and `measured_thickness_um` at two, `measured_diameter_dev_um`, `measured_length_mm` and `measured_corner_wear_um` at one, `measured_surface_ra_um` at two, and the `nominal_length_mm` and `regrind_step_mm` of a product — it states the precision the shop is expected to report and is **not** a validation rule and **not** a rounding instruction: the server stores and echoes such a value exactly as the caller sent it, and a figure carrying more decimal places than the stated scale is neither rejected nor truncated. It is used at full supplied precision by every formula that consumes it, and only the server's own derived outputs are rounded, at the scale named for each.

Where a formula pins an evaluation order — round each per-trial subscore before averaging, round per-unit price before multiplying by quantity — that order is part of the contract and not an implementation detail. Averaging unrounded subscores, or rounding a multiplied total once at the end, produces different results and is wrong.

### 2.10. Quoting Against an Observed Row: Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two creation endpoints derive their entire result from a parent row the client has already read, and both therefore require the client to state which version of that parent it is quoting against. This is a stale-version guard, not a lock.

Creating a tool order requires `expected_product_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `ToolProduct`. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that the quoted lead time, the price basis and the open-order load all derive from the product row the client observed; if the product's `version` has advanced — because its grade was revised, because it was restricted, because its supply posture changed, or because another order pinned it — the quote the client is about to commit was built on a superseded product. Note that raising a second order against the same product is entirely legitimate in its own right, which is exactly why this conflict is settled by a version comparison rather than by a state precondition.

Creating a reconditioning job requires `expected_customer_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `Customer`. If that value does not equal the customer's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Intake reserves prepaid credit out of the balance the client observed, so a superseded customer row means the reservation would be computed against a ledger that has already moved.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection perform no version comparison, and supplying an unexpected version field to them is an unknown-field violation.

### 2.11. The Order of Request Evaluation

Many requests violate more than one rule at once. The system evaluates checks in a fixed ascending order and reports the **first** violation it finds; later checks are not evaluated and their codes are never returned for that request. Implementations must respect the ordering below, because it determines which of two applicable codes a client sees.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` absent or empty → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
2. **Authorisation.** The caller's role is not authorised for the endpoint → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Body schema.** A required input field is missing, a field has the wrong JSON type, or an unknown field is supplied → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every endpoint with a request body. An absent body, an empty body and a literal JSON `null` body are all read as the empty object, so an endpoint whose every field is optional accepts a request with no body at all; conversely, the nine action endpoints that declare no input field whatsoever — grade start-pilot, geometry release, product enter-qualification, product release, product repin, dossier submission, order submission, order confirmation and order shipping — accept only an absent or empty body, and any field supplied to them is an unknown-field violation. A body that is valid JSON but not an object, or that is not valid JSON at all, is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Within one body the unknown-field check runs before the missing-field check, so a request that is both unknown-bearing and incomplete is reported for the unknown field; the remaining per-field type and domain checks then run in the order the fields are documented in §3.
4. **Enum and numeric domain.** An enum field carries a value outside its declared set, or a numeric field falls outside its declared domain → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The per-field domains are stated with each field in §3; among them, `quantity` must be at least 1, `planned_mass_kg` must be greater than 0, every layer's `thickness_um` must be greater than 0, `adhesion_hf_class` must lie between 1 and 6 inclusive, every unit count must be non-negative, `nominal_length_mm`, `min_regrind_length_mm` and `regrind_step_mm` must be greater than 0 — and `min_regrind_length_mm` must in addition be strictly less than the `nominal_length_mm` supplied in the same product-creation body, a relation whose violation is reported here as a domain violation rather than by any later check — and `co_binder_pct` and `wc_grain_um` must be greater than 0. This check applies to every endpoint whose request body declares an enum or numeric field — among them grade creation, geometry creation, coating-recipe creation, product creation, order creation, blend-batch creation, machining-trial creation, machining-trial completion, coating-run completion, grinding-run completion, reconditioning-job intake, reconditioning inspection and reconditioning return — so, for example, a machining trial whose `units_consumed` lies outside the 1-to-50 band declared in §3.6 is rejected here, before the batch-availability check of item 34 is reached. Three further domains are worth stating because they are otherwise easy to guess wrong. Every measured magnitude the shop reports must be at or above zero without being required to exceed it — `measured_density_gcc`, `measured_hardness_hv`, `measured_thickness_um`, `measured_length_mm`, `measured_corner_wear_um`, and each wear-curve entry's `minutes` and `vb_um` — whereas `measured_surface_ra_um` must be strictly greater than 0, because it divides into the finish ratio. `expected_product_version` and `expected_customer_version` must be integers of at least 1. And the two money inputs that may legitimately be negative, a customer's opening `regrind_credit_cents` and a credit adjustment's `delta_cents`, carry no sign constraint at all: they need only be integers. Every field this specification describes as a count, a version, a hardness, a price in cents, a lead time in days, an abrasive-life figure or a trial-count target must be a JSON integer, and a fractional number in such a field is a type violation reported here; the fields described with a decimal scale accept either an integer or a fractional number.
5. **Wear-curve well-formedness.** Applies to trial completion → `ERR_INVALID_WEAR_CURVE`.
6. **Layer-stack well-formedness.** Applies to coating-recipe creation and coating-recipe revision → `ERR_INVALID_LAYER_STACK`.
7. **Path identifier existence.** The identifier in the URL matches no row → that entity's not-found code from §2.8. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
8. **Body reference existence.** A foreign key in the body matches no row → that entity's not-found code. Applies to blend-batch creation, coating-run creation, grinding-run creation, product creation, trial creation, order creation, reconditioning-job intake and reconditioning refurbish.
9. **Customer scoping.** → `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, on the seven operations listed in §2.1.
10. **Parent version currency.** → `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` on order creation and `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` on reconditioning-job intake, per §2.10.
11. **Business code uniqueness.** → `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, on every entity-creating request.
12. **Subject state permits the transition.** The addressed entity is not in a state from which the requested transition is declared → the entity's own invalid-state code: `ERR_INVALID_GRADE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_GEOMETRY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COATING_RUN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WHEEL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_GRINDING_RUN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DOSSIER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TRIAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_CUSTOMER_STATE`. Applies to every action endpoint.
13. **Dependent creation requires an open parent.** A child is created under a parent that has left the state in which children may be added — a trial under a dossier that is not `OPEN` (`ERR_INVALID_DOSSIER_STATE`), a blend batch under a `RETIRED` grade (`ERR_INVALID_GRADE_STATE`), or an order against a `WITHDRAWN` product (`ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`). This check is scoped to trial creation, blend-batch creation, coating-run creation and order creation; a coating recipe never closes to new runs, so coating-run creation has no parent-state rejection of this kind.
14. **Wheel not expired.** → `ERR_WHEEL_EXPIRED`, on grinding-run creation and reconditioning refurbish.
15. **Wheel not awaiting dressing.** → `ERR_WHEEL_REQUIRES_DRESSING`, on grinding-run creation and reconditioning refurbish.
16. **Trial material QC passed.** → `ERR_BATCH_QC_NOT_PASSED`, on trial creation.
17. **Product orderable.** → `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`, on order creation, order submission, order confirmation and order shipping.
18. **Grade qualified at confirmation.** → `ERR_GRADE_NOT_QUALIFIED`, on order confirmation.
19. **Geometry released at confirmation.** → `ERR_GEOMETRY_NOT_RELEASED`, on order confirmation.
20. **Coating validated at confirmation.** → `ERR_COATING_NOT_VALIDATED`, on order confirmation, and — at this same rung, before the reachability rules of items 21 onward — on entering qualification and on repinning a product. On reconditioning refurbishment the recoat variant of this code sits lower instead, after the wheel checks of items 14 and 15 and after the remaining-regrinds check of item 40, so a recoat asked for on an uncoated product whose tools have no regrinds left reports `ERR_NO_REGRINDS_REMAINING`.
21. **Pinned revisions current.** → `ERR_PINNED_REVISION_STALE`, on order confirmation and product release.
22. **Special product derivation.** → `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_DERIVATION`, on product creation.
23. **Product regrindable.** → `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_REGRINDABLE`, on reconditioning-job intake.
24. **Predecessor chain valid.** → `ERR_INVALID_PREDECESSOR_CHAIN`, on reconditioning-job intake.
25. **Window bounds ordered.** → `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, on coating-run creation and grinding-run creation.
26. **Window not in the past.** → `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`, on coating-run creation and grinding-run creation.
27. **Chamber area capacity.** → `ERR_CHAMBER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on coating-run creation.
28. **Cell exclusive occupancy.** → `ERR_CELL_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, on grinding-run creation.
29. **Cell setup buffer.** → `ERR_CELL_BUFFER_CONFLICT`, on grinding-run creation.
30. **Run window elapsed before completion.** → `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`, on coating-run completion and grinding-run completion.
31. **Minimum order quantity.** → `ERR_BELOW_MIN_ORDER_QTY`, on order creation.
32. **Expedite eligibility.** → `ERR_EXPEDITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, on order creation.
33. **Unit conservation.** → `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`, on batch sintering, grinding-run completion, reconditioning inspection and reconditioning return.
34. **Batch units available.** → `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BATCH_UNITS`, on trial creation.
35. **Dossier trial count met.** → `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRIALS`, on dossier submission.
36. **Dossier composite score met.** → `ERR_COMPOSITE_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, on dossier approval.
37. **Workpiece coverage met.** → `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WORKPIECE_COVERAGE`, on dossier approval.
38. **Segregation of duties.** → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on dossier approval and dossier rejection.
39. **Release requires an approved dossier.** → `ERR_NO_APPROVED_DOSSIER`, on product release.
40. **Refurbish requires remaining regrinds.** → `ERR_NO_REGRINDS_REMAINING`, on reconditioning refurbish.
41. **Open demand blocks retirement.** → `ERR_OPEN_DEMAND_EXISTS`, on grade retirement, and by the same principle on product withdrawal and customer closure.
42. **Customer account orderable.** → `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402) when the account is on credit hold and `ERR_CUSTOMER_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) when it is closed, on order submission, order confirmation and reconditioning-job intake. On all three the account is examined at this rung — last, after every product and engineering gate — even where the prose of §3.8 introduces it first.
43. **Regrind credit sufficient.** → `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGRIND_CREDIT` (HTTP 402), on reconditioning-job intake.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out, because they are counter-intuitive. On dossier approval, the composite-score check (36) and the workpiece-coverage check (37) are both evaluated **before** the segregation-of-duties check (38): an approver who is also the submitter of a dossier whose score is 0.80 sees `ERR_COMPOSITE_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, not `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And on order creation, the orderability check (17) precedes the minimum-quantity check (31), which in turn precedes the expedite check (32), so an under-quantity expedited order on an `UNAVAILABLE` product reports `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties in Quality Decisions

The quality decision on a qualification dossier must come from someone who neither prepared nor produced the evidence. Concretely, the `X-User-ID` deciding a dossier must differ from the dossier's recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, and must differ from the `operator_user_id` of **every** machining trial attached to that dossier — including trials that were later invalidated. A violation of either condition is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403, and the rule is enforced identically on approval and on rejection: a `QualityApprover` cannot escape the constraint by rejecting instead of approving.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Carbide Grade Development and the Powder Route

A grade is where every tool begins. A `MetallurgyEngineer` opens a development record by supplying a `grade_code` (a human designation such as GC4325, unique across all grades and between 3 and 24 characters long), the `target_workpiece_group` the grade is being developed for — one of `P`, `M`, `K`, `N`, `S` or `H` in the ISO material classification — and the four metallurgical targets: `co_binder_pct`, the cobalt binder mass percent of the blend, at least 3.0 and at most 15.0 with one decimal place; `wc_grain_um`, the mean tungsten-carbide grain size in micrometres, at least 0.2 and at most 6.0 with one decimal place; `hardness_hv`, the target Vickers hardness of the sintered body, at least 1200 and at most 2000; and `trs_mpa`, the target transverse rupture strength in megapascals, at least 1000 and at most 4500. `hardness_hv` and `trs_mpa` are integers; `co_binder_pct` and `wc_grain_um` are fractional numbers whose stated one-decimal scale is descriptive only, per §2.9. A value outside any of those bands is a domain violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The new record opens with `status` at `DRAFT`, `revision` at 1, `qualified_product_count` at 0 — that figure being the derived count of §2.4, so it reads 0 because no product yet references the grade — and `authored_by_user_id` recording the creating `X-User-ID`.

Powder is then weighed out against that grade. Blending is a shared shop-floor operation open to both a `MetallurgyEngineer` and a `ProductionPlanner`: the request names the `grade_id` whose recipe the batch realises, a `batch_code` unique across all batches, and the `planned_mass_kg` of blended powder, which must be greater than 0 and is carried to two decimal places. The new batch opens at `stage` `BLENDED` with `qc_status` `PENDING`, captures `pinned_grade_revision` from the grade's current `revision`, sets `pressed_units`, `sintered_units`, `scrap_units` and `remaining_units` all to 0, and leaves `measured_density_gcc`, `measured_hardness_hv` and `yield_pct` null until the sinter reports them. Alongside the revision it also captures, invisibly, the `hardness_hv` target the grade carried at that instant, which is the figure the sintering QC comparison later reads. A batch cannot be opened against a grade that has already reached `RETIRED`; that request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_GRADE_STATE`, and because that is a parent-state rejection it is reported only after the batch's own `batch_code` has been found unique.

Pressing advances the `stage` dimension from `BLENDED` to `PRESSED` and records `pressed_units`, the number of green compacts the press produced. It requires the batch to be at `stage` `BLENDED` with `qc_status` still `PENDING`, and requires `pressed_units` to be at least 1. The `qc_status` dimension is untouched by pressing — a pressed batch is still `PENDING`, because nothing has yet been measured.

Sintering is reserved to the `MetallurgyEngineer` and is the single most consequential step in the powder route, because it both advances `stage` from `PRESSED` to `SINTERED` and drives the independent `qc_status` dimension out of `PENDING` in the same request. The request carries `sintered_units`, the count of good units out of the furnace; `scrap_units`, the count lost during sintering; `measured_density_gcc`, the measured sintered density in grams per cubic centimetre at two decimal places; and `measured_hardness_hv`, the measured Vickers hardness as a non-negative integer. It is `stage` alone that gates the request — the batch must be at `PRESSED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, and the `qc_status` dimension is not examined as a precondition because sintering is what drives it. Units must be conserved: `sintered_units` plus `scrap_units` must equal `pressed_units` exactly, and any other total is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`.

The sintering QC evaluation then runs. First, `yield_pct` is computed as 100 multiplied by `sintered_units` divided by `pressed_units`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The batch's `qc_status` then moves from `PENDING` to `PASSED` when all three of the following hold: `measured_density_gcc` is at least 14.35; the absolute difference between `measured_hardness_hv` and the `hardness_hv` target of the batch's grade, read at the revision the batch pinned, is at most 75; and `yield_pct` is at least 85.00. All three comparisons are inclusive at the boundary, so a density of exactly 14.35, a hardness deviation of exactly 75 and a yield of exactly 85.00 all pass. If any one of the three fails, `qc_status` moves from `PENDING` to `FAILED` instead, and the batch is permanently ineligible as a source of trial tools — there is no re-test path out of `FAILED`. In both outcomes `remaining_units` is set to `sintered_units`.

Only once the current recipe revision has produced physical evidence may the grade advance. Moving a grade from `DRAFT` to `PILOT` requires the grade to be at `DRAFT` and requires at least one blend batch on that grade to be simultaneously at `stage` `SINTERED`, at `qc_status` `PASSED`, and pinned to the grade's *current* `revision`. When no such batch exists, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_PASSED_BATCH` — which is precisely what happens if the engineer revises the recipe after a successful sinter, because the revision advance orphans the evidence. A grade never reaches `QUALIFIED` by direct request: that transition happens only as a consequence of a dossier approval, as described in §3.7.

Revising a blend recipe is permitted from either `PILOT` or `QUALIFIED`, and in both cases the grade lands back at `DRAFT`; the two declared moves are `QUALIFIED` → `DRAFT` and `PILOT` → `DRAFT`. The request restates `co_binder_pct`, `wc_grain_um`, `hardness_hv` and `trs_mpa` and requires a `revision_note`, a mandatory string that is validated for the record but neither persisted on the grade nor echoed in any response — the same is true of the `revision_note` of a geometry or coating-recipe revision, the `retire_note` of a retirement, the `dress_note` of a dressing, the `quarantine_reason` of a quarantine, the `void_reason` of a void, the `withdraw_reason` of a withdrawal, the `cancel_reason` of a cancellation, the `close_reason` of a closure, the `reason` of a credit adjustment and the `scrap_reason` of a scrap, none of which appears on the resource it was supplied to; only a dossier's `decision_note` is kept. Every one of those note and reason fields, and every free-text field in this specification other than `grade_code` and a dossier's `decision_note`, is constrained only to being a JSON string: the empty string satisfies a mandatory one, and no minimum length, maximum length or character rule applies. `revision` advances by 1, and every product built on the grade has `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible` recomputed because the grade is no longer `QUALIFIED`. The difference between the two starting states is the size of the blast radius. Revising from `QUALIFIED` runs the full four-hop restriction cascade of §2.7: released products on the grade become `RESTRICTED`, any of those that were `STOCKED` fall to `MAKE_TO_ORDER`, and their `CONFIRMED` orders return to `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `requires_reconfirmation` set to true, with a `version` increment on the grade and on every product and order the cascade touched. Revising from `PILOT` runs no such cascade, because a grade that has only ever been in pilot has no `RELEASED` product to restrict; the only mutations are the revision advance, the lead-time recomputation and the grade's own `version` increment — and on this branch too a product's `version` advances only where the recomputation actually landed a different `engineering_lead_time_days` or `expedite_eligible`, per §2.7.

Retirement is terminal. A grade may be retired only from `QUALIFIED`, may carry an optional `retire_note`, and is blocked while open demand exists: if any tool order in `SUBMITTED`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CONFIRMED` or `ON_HOLD` references a product built on the grade, the request is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_DEMAND_EXISTS`. When it succeeds, the grade moves `QUALIFIED` → `RETIRED`, every product on the grade whose `release_status` is `QUALIFICATION`, `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and because those products became `WITHDRAWN` their `supply_status` moves to `UNAVAILABLE`. That is the whole of the retirement cascade: it is not the withdrawal endpoint of §3.5 run repeatedly, so no `DRAFT` order is cancelled and no lead time is recomputed, and a product already at `DEVELOPMENT` or `WITHDRAWN` is left alone. The grade's `version` and the `version` of every mutated product each advance by 1.

Quarantine is the metallurgical undo, and it belongs to the `QualityApprover` rather than to the engineer who produced the batch. A batch whose `qc_status` is `PASSED` may be quarantined with a `quarantine_reason` after a downstream finding; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. The `qc_status` dimension moves `PASSED` → `QUARANTINED` while `stage` stays exactly where it was, and a four-hop cascade follows. Every machining trial fed by the batch whose `status` is `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` moves to `INVALIDATED`, each returning its `units_consumed` to the batch; because the batch is quarantined, `remaining_units` is then driven to 0, so the value observable after the request is 0 regardless of how many trials were reversed. Because those trials left the countable population, every dossier that held one of them recomputes `completed_trial_count`, `passed_trial_count` and `composite_score` — which, those three being the derived figures of §2.4, needs no separate write. And because the recomputed `passed_trial_count` may now sit below `required_trial_count`, any affected dossier whose `status` was `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED` → `OPEN` and has `submitted_by_user_id` set back to null, so a fresh submitter must own the resubmission. That reversion is conditional on the shortfall actually existing: it is the recomputed `passed_trial_count` — not the `completed_trial_count` that the submission gate of §3.7 counted — that is compared against `required_trial_count`, and a `SUBMITTED` dossier whose surviving PASS trials still reach the required number stays `SUBMITTED` with its submitter intact. The batch's `version` advances by 1, each invalidated trial's `version` advances by 1, and each affected dossier's `version` advances by 1 whether or not it reverted, because the loss of a trial is a mutation of the dossier either way. A dossier is "affected" exactly when one of the trials this request invalidated named it, and each such dossier's `version` advances once however many of its trials were reversed.

### 3.2. Geometry and Chip Breaker Development

Geometry development is the `GeometryEngineer`'s exclusive territory. A geometry record is opened with a `geometry_code` unique across all geometries (for example WM-MM4), an `iso_designation` carrying the full ISO insert or tool designation string, and an `application_class` that is one of `ROUGHING`, `MEDIUM` or `FINISHING`. That class is not cosmetic: it sets the tool-life and surface-finish limits a machining trial must beat, and it caps the speeds and feeds application engineering will recommend. The chip breaker itself is identified by `chipbreaker_code`, and the cutting geometry is described by `rake_angle_deg`, the effective rake angle in degrees, at least -10.0 and at most 25.0; `nose_radius_mm`, at least 0.05 and at most 3.2; `max_depth_of_cut_mm`, which must be greater than 0; and `edge_prep`, the cutting-edge preparation, one of `SHARP`, `HONED`, `CHAMFERED` or `T_LAND`. A new geometry opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `revision` 1.

Releasing a geometry moves it `DRAFT` → `RELEASED` and is what makes it usable for production tooling. The release has a side effect that reaches beyond the geometry itself: because an unreleased geometry contributes eight days of engineering lead time and a released one contributes none, every tool product referencing this `geometry_id` has `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible` recomputed, and each such product has its own `version` incremented alongside the geometry's — but only where the recomputation actually landed a different value, per §2.7, so a product whose lead time was already correct is inspected and left untouched. Release runs the lead-time refresh alone: it is not a revision, so none of the four restriction hops of §2.7 fires, and no product's `release_status` or `supply_status` moves.

Revising a released geometry — changing `chipbreaker_code`, `rake_angle_deg`, `nose_radius_mm`, `max_depth_of_cut_mm` or `edge_prep`, with a `revision_note` for the record — is permitted only from `RELEASED` and returns the geometry to `DRAFT` with `revision` advanced by 1. The same four-hop cascade described in §2.7 then runs, this time keyed on products whose `pinned_geometry_revision` is now lower than the new revision: those that were `RELEASED` become `RESTRICTED`, those among them that were `STOCKED` fall to `MAKE_TO_ORDER`, and their `CONFIRMED` orders return to `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `requires_reconfirmation` set to true. Lead times are recomputed for every product on the geometry, and the geometry's own `version` advances.

### 3.3. Coating Recipes and Deposition Chamber Loading

A coating recipe is owned end to end by the `CoatingEngineer`. It is defined by a `recipe_code` unique across all recipes, a `deposition_type` of either `PVD` or `CVD`, and `layers`, an ordered stack in which each entry names a `material` and a `thickness_um`. The stack must hold between 1 and 5 entries, each `material` must be one of `TiN`, `TiAlN`, `AlCrN`, `Al2O3` or `TiCN`, each `thickness_um` must be at least 0.10 and at most 8.00, and no two **adjacent** entries may name the same material — a TiN/TiN pair is a process impossibility. An empty stack, a stack longer than five entries, or an adjacent-material repetition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LAYER_STACK`. The three `ERR_INVALID_LAYER_STACK` conditions are judged only once every entry has passed its own field checks, so per-entry faults win: an entry that is not an object, that omits `material` or `thickness_um`, that carries any field beyond those two, or whose `material` or `thickness_um` falls outside its domain is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the stack is also the wrong length, and `layers` that is not an array at all is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On acceptance, `total_thickness_um` is computed as the sum of the entries' `thickness_um` values carried to two decimal places, `revision` is 1, `adhesion_class` is null and `is_validated` is false. A recipe carries no lifecycle status of its own; `is_validated` is the whole of its state. A recipe therefore starts life unproven: nothing in the recipe definition asserts that the stack will adhere.

Proof comes from the chamber. Booking a deposition run names the `recipe_id` to be deposited, the `chamber_id` occupied — one of `CH1`, `CH2` or `CH3` — the occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and `loaded_area_cm2`, the total substrate surface area going into the chamber, which must be greater than 0 and is carried to two decimal places. The run opens at `status` `SCHEDULED` and captures `pinned_recipe_revision` from the recipe's current `revision`, which is what later ties the run's measurements to a specific layer stack rather than to whatever the recipe has since become. `adhesion_hf_class` and `measured_thickness_um` open null. The two window fields are echoed back exactly as the caller wrote them — the same string, offset and precision — rather than being re-rendered into a canonical form; the parsed instants are used only for the comparisons below.

Chamber windows are **half-open**: the interval runs from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive. `window_start` must be strictly earlier than `window_end`, or the booking is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; and `window_start` must not be strictly earlier than the current logical UTC time, or the booking is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`. Unlike a grinding cell, a chamber is shared: several runs may occupy it at once, subject to an area budget of 4000.00 square centimetres. For the requested chamber, at every instant covered by the new half-open interval, the sum of `loaded_area_cm2` over all `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` runs whose own half-open windows cover that instant, plus the requested `loaded_area_cm2`, must not exceed 4000.00; otherwise the booking is rejected with `ERR_CHAMBER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the intervals are half-open, two touching windows — where the earlier run's `window_end` equals the new run's `window_start` — share no instant at all, and both may legitimately be loaded to the full 4000.00.

Closing out a deposition run requires the run to be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COATING_RUN_STATE`, and requires the current logical UTC time to have reached `window_end`; completing early is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. The engineer supplies `adhesion_hf_class`, the Rockwell-C indentation adhesion class measured on the load, an integer from 1 (best) to 6 (worst), and `measured_thickness_um`, the measured total coating thickness. The run moves `SCHEDULED` → `COMPLETED`, and the recipe is then re-evaluated from its completed evidence pool.

That pool consists of the recipe's completed runs whose `pinned_recipe_revision` equals the recipe's current `revision` — no other run counts. The recipe's `adhesion_class` becomes the lowest, and therefore best, `adhesion_hf_class` across the pool, and is null whenever the pool is empty. The recipe's `is_validated` becomes true if and only if at least one run in the pool satisfies both conditions at once: its `adhesion_hf_class` is at most 2, and the absolute difference between its `measured_thickness_um` and the recipe's `total_thickness_um` is at most 15 percent of `total_thickness_um`. Both `adhesion_class` and `is_validated` are among the derived figures of §2.4: they are not stored on the recipe but recomputed from the live pool whenever the recipe is serialized, so a later run, a later void or a revision changes them without any further write. Both the run's `version` and the recipe's `version` advance by 1 — the recipe's unconditionally, because the completion enlarged its evidence pool, whether or not the recomputation moved either figure.

Revising a recipe replaces `layers` and records a `revision_note`; both fields are mandatory, the new stack is validated exactly as at creation, and — the recipe having no lifecycle status — there is no state precondition whatever, so any recipe may be revised at any time, however many times, validated or not. Because `revision` advances, the completed-run pool is emptied at a stroke: `total_thickness_um` is recomputed from the new stack, `adhesion_class` returns to null and `is_validated` returns to false, and the recipe must be proven again in the chamber. The revision also runs the four-hop cascade of §2.7 against products whose `pinned_coating_revision` is now behind, restricting released products, downgrading `STOCKED` supply to `MAKE_TO_ORDER`, and returning their `CONFIRMED` orders to `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `requires_reconfirmation` set to true.

### 3.4. Grinding Wheels, Cells and Tolerance Control

Solid-carbide endmills and drills are finished by grinding, and grinding consumes abrasive. A `ProductionPlanner` registers a wheel with a `wheel_code` unique across all wheels, a `spec_code` naming the abrasive specification (for example D46-C75-B), and `initial_life_units`, the abrasive life available between dressings, at least 100 and at most 100000. A new wheel opens at `status` `AVAILABLE`, with `consumed_life_units` at 0, `remaining_life_units` equal to `initial_life_units`, and `dress_count` at 0. Throughout the wheel's life, `remaining_life_units` is `initial_life_units` minus `consumed_life_units`, and `consumed_life_units` is the total charged to the wheel since its last dressing by completed grinding runs and by reconditioning refurbishments.

Booking a grinding run names the `wheel_id` to be mounted, the `product_id` being ground, the `cell_id` occupied — either `CELL_A` or `CELL_B` — the occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and `planned_units`, the number of tools planned, which must be at least 1. The run opens at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `consumed_life_units` at 0 and `tolerance_class` null. A wheel already at `EXPIRED` cannot be mounted at all and the booking is rejected with `ERR_WHEEL_EXPIRED`; a wheel at `DRESSING` is rejected with `ERR_WHEEL_REQUIRES_DRESSING`, because it has fallen below its dressing threshold and has not yet been restored.

Cell windows, unlike chamber windows, are **closed** intervals running from `window_start` to `window_end` inclusive, because a single run occupies the whole cell. `window_start` must be strictly before `window_end` (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`) and must not precede the current logical UTC time (`ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`). If the requested closed window intersects the closed window of any `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` run already in that cell, the booking is rejected with `ERR_CELL_DOUBLE_BOOKED` — and because both intervals are closed, touching endpoints *do* collide: a new run starting at the exact instant an existing run ends is a conflict, not a handover. Even where two runs do not overlap, the cell needs time for setup: if the gap on either side to the nearest neighbouring run in the same cell is smaller than 45 minutes, the booking is rejected with `ERR_CELL_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. A gap of exactly 45 minutes is accepted.

Closing out a grinding run requires the run to be `SCHEDULED`, requires the logical clock to have reached `window_end` (otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`), and requires `produced_units` plus `scrap_units` to equal `planned_units` exactly (otherwise `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`). The planner supplies `produced_units` and `scrap_units`, each a non-negative integer, and `measured_diameter_dev_um`, the measured mean diameter deviation from nominal in micrometres at one decimal place, which is signed and unbounded because a tool may finish under or over nominal; and the run moves `SCHEDULED` → `COMPLETED`. All three are mandatory, and the unit-count equality is checked only after the state and window preconditions above.

Two derivations follow. The achieved `tolerance_class` is graded from the absolute value of `measured_diameter_dev_um`: at most 5.0 micrometres is `IT6`; otherwise at most 12.0 is `IT7`; otherwise at most 25.0 is `IT8`; anything larger is `OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`. Every band is inclusive at its upper bound, so a deviation of exactly 5.0 grades as `IT6`. Then the wheel is charged: the run's own `consumed_life_units` is set to `produced_units` plus `scrap_units`, multiplied by the ground product's `grind_units_per_tool`, and that figure is added to the wheel's `consumed_life_units`. Because the charge may push the wheel's `remaining_life_units` below the 150-unit dressing threshold, a wheel at `AVAILABLE` whose remaining life is now under 150 moves `AVAILABLE` → `DRESSING` in the same request. Both the run's `version` and the wheel's `version` advance by 1. Note that the per-run charge is *retained* on the run rather than merely accumulated on the wheel, and the reason is the void path below.

Voiding a grinding run — for a metrology dispute or a cancelled setup — is open to both a `ProductionPlanner` and a `QualityApprover` and records a `void_reason`. Its effects differ sharply depending on where the run was. Voiding a `COMPLETED` run — the move `COMPLETED` → `VOIDED` — is a compensating action: the run's stored `consumed_life_units` is subtracted back from the wheel's `consumed_life_units` and the run's own figure is then set to 0, `produced_units` and `scrap_units` are reset to 0, and `measured_diameter_dev_um` and `tolerance_class` are set to null, leaving the wheel's remaining life exactly as it stood before the completion. If the wheel had dropped to `DRESSING` and the reversal lifts `remaining_life_units` back to 150 or above, the wheel moves `DRESSING` → `AVAILABLE`, and — this is the important asymmetry — `dress_count` is *not* changed, because no abrasive was actually dressed. Voiding a `SCHEDULED` run does none of that: no wheel life is reversed, because none was ever charged, and the only effect beyond the terminal `SCHEDULED` → `VOIDED` move is that the cell's occupation window is released for another booking. Releasing the window is not special to that branch — a `VOIDED` run is invisible to the double-booking and buffer tests whichever state it was voided from, so a voided completion frees its cell too, while its `window_start`, `window_end` and `cell_id` remain on the record. Both paths advance the run's `version`; the completed path also advances the wheel's.

Dressing restores abrasive, and it is bounded. A dressing request is valid only while the wheel is at `DRESSING`, may carry an optional `dress_note`, and branches on how many dressings the wheel has already had. When `dress_count` plus one is at most 3, the wheel moves `DRESSING` → `AVAILABLE`, `consumed_life_units` is reset to 0 so that `remaining_life_units` returns to the full `initial_life_units`, and `dress_count` advances by 1 — the cycle `AVAILABLE` → `DRESSING` → `AVAILABLE` that a wheel walks up to three times. When `dress_count` plus one would exceed 3, the same request instead moves the wheel `DRESSING` → `EXPIRED`: `dress_count` still advances by 1, but `consumed_life_units` is deliberately *not* reset, so the expired wheel's remaining life stands as a record of how it ended. Both branches advance the wheel's `version` by 1, and dressing a wheel that is not at `DRESSING` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WHEEL_STATE`.

### 3.5. Tool Products, Lead Time and the Speeds-and-Feeds Envelope

A tool product is the join of the three engineering disciplines into something a customer can buy, and it may be created by a `GeometryEngineer` or an `ApplicationEngineer`. The definition carries a `sku` unique across all products; an `order_class` of `CATALOG` or `SPECIAL`; the `grade_id` of the substrate grade, the `geometry_id` of the geometry and chip breaker, and optionally a `coating_recipe_id` — omitted or null for an uncoated product; a `tool_type` of `INSERT`, `SOLID_CARBIDE_ENDMILL` or `SOLID_CARBIDE_DRILL`; a `list_price_cents` per-unit list price of at least 1; a `min_order_qty` of at least 1; and four grinding and regrind geometry figures: `nominal_length_mm`, the usable flute or edge length when new, greater than 0 at one decimal place; `min_regrind_length_mm`, the shortest usable length, greater than 0 and strictly less than `nominal_length_mm`; `regrind_step_mm`, the length removed by one regrind pass, greater than 0 at one decimal place; and `grind_units_per_tool`, the wheel life units one ground tool consumes, at least 1.

An engineered special must descend from a catalog parent, and the derivation is exactly one hop deep. A `SPECIAL` product must supply `base_catalog_product_id`, that identifier must not be the product's own, and the product it names must itself be `CATALOG` — a special derived from another special is refused. A `CATALOG` product must not supply `base_catalog_product_id` at all. Every one of those failures is reported as `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_DERIVATION`. Because `base_catalog_product_id` is documented after the three engineering references, it is the last body reference resolved under §2.8: a creation naming both an unknown grade and an unknown base reports `ERR_GRADE_NOT_FOUND`, and a base that does not exist at all is `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND` rather than a derivation failure. A `SPECIAL` product may not name itself as its own base — but since the row does not yet exist, that can only ever surface as `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`. A new product opens at `release_status` `DEVELOPMENT` and `supply_status` `UNAVAILABLE`, captures the three pinned revisions from its referenced grade, geometry and recipe, computes `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible`, and sets `tool_life_index` to null, `qualified_workpiece_groups` to the empty list and `open_order_units` to 0.

Engineering lead time is recomputed whenever the product is created, whenever it enters qualification or is repinned, and whenever any grade, geometry or coating change touches it. It reads the **current** status of the referenced rows, not the pinned revisions. The base contribution is 5 days for a `CATALOG` product and 15 days for a `SPECIAL` one. The geometry contributes nothing when the geometry's `status` is `RELEASED` and 8 days otherwise. The coating contributes nothing when `coating_recipe_id` is null, 4 days when the recipe's `deposition_type` is `PVD`, and 7 days when it is `CVD`. The grade contributes nothing when the grade's `status` is `QUALIFIED` and 6 days otherwise. Those four contributions are summed into a raw lead time, and here the evaluation order is pinned and matters: `expedite_eligible` is decided on the **raw** total, being true when the raw total is at most 21 days, and is never revisited afterwards. Only if the product is expedite eligible is 3 days shaved off the raw total. The result is then floored at 2 days, and that final figure is `engineering_lead_time_days`. The consequence is deliberate — a raw total of 22 days yields an `engineering_lead_time_days` of 22 with `expedite_eligible` false, even though 22 minus 3 would have been 19, because the shave never re-opens eligibility.

Moving a product from `DEVELOPMENT` to `QUALIFICATION` is open to an `ApplicationEngineer` or a `GeometryEngineer` and is the act that captures the qualification evidence baseline. It requires the grade's `status` to be `PILOT` or `QUALIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_GRADE_STATE`), the geometry's `status` to be `RELEASED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_GEOMETRY_STATE`), and — when the product is coated — the recipe's `is_validated` to be true (otherwise `ERR_COATING_NOT_VALIDATED`). On success `pinned_grade_revision`, `pinned_geometry_revision` and `pinned_coating_revision` are refreshed to the referenced entities' current `revision` values, `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible` are recomputed, and `version` advances by 1.

Release is the `QualityApprover`'s decision and is the only path from `QUALIFICATION` to `RELEASED`. It requires at least one qualification dossier on the product to be `APPROVED` and `tool_life_index` to be non-null — a failure of either is `ERR_NO_APPROVED_DOSSIER` — and it requires all three pins to still match the current revisions of the referenced rows, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_REVISION_STALE`. On release the `supply_status` dimension moves in the same request, and it moves differently by class: a `CATALOG` product goes `UNAVAILABLE` → `STOCKED`, skipping `MAKE_TO_ORDER` entirely, while a `SPECIAL` product goes `UNAVAILABLE` → `MAKE_TO_ORDER` and stops there. Lead time and expedite eligibility are recomputed, the grade's `qualified_product_count` advances by 1 — which needs no write, that count being one of the figures §2.4 derives on read, here from the grade's products whose `release_status` is `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED` — and both the product's `version` and the grade's `version` advance by 1, the grade's unconditionally because its qualified population changed. Note also that the two release gates are evaluated in ladder order, not in the order stated here: a product whose pins have gone stale *and* which has no approved dossier reports `ERR_PINNED_REVISION_STALE`, per item 21 preceding item 39 in §2.11.

Repinning is how a `RESTRICTED` product is rescued. It is open to an `ApplicationEngineer` or a `GeometryEngineer`, requires the same three referenced-entity conditions as entering qualification, and returns the product `RESTRICTED` → `QUALIFICATION` with all three pins refreshed and the lead time recomputed. Crucially, `tool_life_index` and `qualified_workpiece_groups` are left exactly as they were — the historical evidence is not discarded — but the product is nevertheless back in qualification and must be released again by the quality authority before it can be ordered. A `RESTRICTED` product can never be released directly.

The supply dimension is otherwise the `ProductionPlanner`'s to set, by naming the desired `supply_status`, and each target has its own admissibility rule. Moving to `MAKE_TO_ORDER` from `UNAVAILABLE` requires `release_status` to be `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`. Moving to `MAKE_TO_ORDER` from `STOCKED` is always admissible and imposes no `release_status` condition at all, because it is a withdrawal of stocked availability rather than a grant of it. Moving to `STOCKED` from `UNAVAILABLE` requires `release_status` to be `RELEASED` *and* `order_class` to be `CATALOG` — an engineered special is never stocked. Moving to `STOCKED` from `MAKE_TO_ORDER` requires `release_status` to be `RELEASED`. Moving to `UNAVAILABLE` is permitted from either `MAKE_TO_ORDER` or `STOCKED`, again with no `release_status` condition. Naming the status the product already holds is a no-op that no rule above admits, so it is refused like any other unreachable target. A request whose target is not reachable under those rules is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`, the product's own invalid-state code from item 12 of §2.11. One of these moves carries a cascade: `STOCKED` → `MAKE_TO_ORDER`, whether requested directly or triggered as hop 3 of an upstream revision, moves every `CONFIRMED` order on the product to `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `requires_reconfirmation` set to true. Every supply move advances the product's `version` by 1, and none of them touches `release_status`.

Withdrawal is the `QualityApprover`'s terminal act, recorded with a `withdraw_reason`. It is admissible from `DEVELOPMENT`, `QUALIFICATION`, `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`, and is blocked while any order on the product is in `SUBMITTED`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CONFIRMED` or `ON_HOLD`, which is reported as `ERR_OPEN_DEMAND_EXISTS`. On success `release_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, `supply_status` becomes `UNAVAILABLE`, every `DRAFT` order on the product is cancelled — walked in `created_at` ascending order, each such order's `version` advancing by 1 — and the product's `version` advances by 1. Withdrawing an already-`WITHDRAWN` product is `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`, checked before the open-demand test.

Application engineering also publishes a cutting envelope, and this is a pure computed read: nothing is persisted, and the envelope is evaluated fresh on every call. The caller supplies three inputs — `workpiece_group`, the ISO group of the material to be cut, one of `P`, `M`, `K`, `N`, `S` or `H`; `machine_rigidity`, one of `RIGID`, `MEDIUM` or `WEAK`; and `coolant`, one of `FLOOD`, `THROUGH_TOOL` or `DRY` — and receives four values back: `recommended_vc`, `recommended_fz`, `recommended_ap` and `confidence_class`, and nothing else; `data` holds exactly those four keys and no `id`, `version` or timestamps, this being a computation rather than a resource. All three inputs arrive as query parameters and all three are mandatory: an absent one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, exactly as a value outside its enum is. The envelope is published for a product in **any** `release_status`, including `DEVELOPMENT`, `RESTRICTED` and `WITHDRAWN` — the posture is reported through `confidence_class` rather than by refusing the request — and no state, dossier or validation precondition applies.

The envelope is built from four multipliers against a material baseline. The grade factor is 1.00 plus 0.02 for each percentage point by which `co_binder_pct` falls below 10.0 — equivalently, 1.00 plus 0.02 multiplied by the quantity 10.0 minus `co_binder_pct`, which is negative for a high-binder grade — and the result is then clamped to lie between a floor of 0.85 and a ceiling of 1.20. The coating factor is 1.00 for an uncoated product; for a coated one it is 1.10 plus 0.02 multiplied by the recipe's `total_thickness_um`, capped at a ceiling of 1.35. The rigidity factor is 1.00 for `RIGID`, 0.85 for `MEDIUM` and 0.70 for `WEAK`. The coolant factor is 1.00 for `FLOOD` and 1.10 for `THROUGH_TOOL`; for `DRY` it depends on the tool, being 0.60 for an uncoated product and 0.75 for a coated one.

The material baseline cutting speed in metres per minute is 220 for group `P`, 170 for `M`, 150 for `K`, 400 for `N`, 60 for `S` and 45 for `H`. `recommended_vc` is that baseline multiplied by all four factors, then capped by the geometry's `application_class` ceiling — 260 for `ROUGHING`, 340 for `MEDIUM`, 420 for `FINISHING` — and rounded half up to a whole number of metres per minute. `recommended_fz` is the geometry's `nose_radius_mm` multiplied by 0.35, capped by the class feed ceiling of 0.55 millimetres for `ROUGHING`, 0.30 for `MEDIUM` or 0.14 for `FINISHING`, and rounded half up to three decimal places. `recommended_ap` is the geometry's `max_depth_of_cut_mm`, capped by the class depth ceiling of 6.0 millimetres for `ROUGHING`, 3.0 for `MEDIUM` or 1.0 for `FINISHING`, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Finally `confidence_class` is `HIGH` when the requested `workpiece_group` appears in the product's `qualified_workpiece_groups`; otherwise `MEDIUM` when the product's `release_status` is `RELEASED`; otherwise `LOW`. A released product asked about an unqualified material therefore returns advice, but flags it as `MEDIUM` confidence.

### 3.6. Machining Trials and the Wear Curve

A qualification dossier is the folder in which the evidence for one product is assembled. An `ApplicationEngineer` opens it against a product that is in `QUALIFICATION` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE` — supplying the `product_id`, a `dossier_code` unique across all dossiers, and optionally a `required_trial_count`, the number of PASS trials the dossier must carry before approval, which must be at least 2 and at most 8 and defaults to 2 when omitted. The dossier opens at `status` `OPEN` with `completed_trial_count` and `passed_trial_count` at 0, `composite_score` null, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` all null. The `dossier_code` uniqueness check runs before the product-state check, so a duplicate code against a `DEVELOPMENT` product reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

A machining trial is one cutting test on one workpiece material. Creating it names the `dossier_id` it belongs to and the `batch_id` the test tools were drawn from, plus a `trial_code` unique across all trials, the `workpiece_material_code` of the material actually cut (for example 42CrMo4), the `workpiece_group` that material belongs to, `units_consumed`, the number of test tools drawn, at least 1 and at most 50, and the cutting conditions used: `cutting_speed_vc` in metres per minute, `feed_fz` in millimetres and `depth_ap` in millimetres, each greater than 0, together with the `coolant` strategy of `FLOOD`, `THROUGH_TOOL` or `DRY`. Trials may be opened only while the dossier is `OPEN`; once it has been submitted, no further trial may be added, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DOSSIER_STATE`. The supplying batch must have `qc_status` `PASSED` and `stage` `SINTERED` or `CONSUMED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_QC_NOT_PASSED`, and `units_consumed` must not exceed the batch's `remaining_units`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BATCH_UNITS`. On creation the trial opens at `status` `RUNNING` with `wear_measurements` an empty array and `measured_surface_ra_um`, `rated_life_min`, `projected_life_min`, `censored` and `verdict` all null, records `operator_user_id` from the calling `X-User-ID`, reduces the batch's `remaining_units` by `units_consumed`, and — when that reduction brings `remaining_units` to 0 — advances the batch's `stage` to `CONSUMED`. The batch's `version` advances by 1 whether or not the stage moved, since its remaining units changed either way, and the `trial_code` uniqueness check precedes the dossier-state check, so a duplicate code on a `SUBMITTED` dossier reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Closing out a trial is where the tool life is actually established. The engineer supplies `wear_measurements`, the flank-wear curve as an ordered array in which each entry pairs `minutes`, the cutting time elapsed, with `vb_um`, the flank wear land width measured at that time; and `measured_surface_ra_um`, the workpiece surface roughness at two decimal places. The curve must hold at least 2 entries, its `minutes` values must be strictly increasing, its `vb_um` values must be non-decreasing, and its last two `vb_um` values must not be equal; a curve that fails any of those is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WEAR_CURVE`. The trial must be `RUNNING`, and the supplying batch's `qc_status` must still be `PASSED`.

Two tool-life figures are then computed from that single curve, and both are persisted, because the two disciplines that consume them ask different questions. The **measured, interpolated** figure is the qualification number of record and is written to `rated_life_min`. Walking consecutive pairs in ascending `minutes` order, the server takes the first pair whose earlier `vb_um` is strictly below the 300-micrometre flank-wear limit and whose later `vb_um` is at or above it; within that pair the life is interpolated linearly, as the earlier entry's `minutes` plus the shortfall from the earlier `vb_um` to 300 divided by the wear increase across the pair and multiplied by the time increase across the pair, rounded half up to one decimal place, with `censored` set to false. If no pair brackets the limit — the curve never reaches 300 micrometres — `rated_life_min` is instead the `minutes` value of the **last** entry, rounded half up to one decimal place, and `censored` is set to true. Every point after the crossing is ignored by this variant.

The **projected** figure is the catalog projection quoted by application engineering and is written to `projected_life_min`. It uses only the last two entries of the curve, regardless of where the crossing occurred: the wear rate in micrometres per minute is the increase in `vb_um` across the final pair divided by the increase in `minutes` across it, and `projected_life_min` is the last entry's `minutes` plus the remaining wear allowance from the last `vb_um` to 300 divided by that rate, rounded half up to one decimal place. When the last `vb_um` is already above 300 the allowance is negative and the projection walks backwards from the final point, which is legitimate. The rate is always strictly positive because a curve whose last two `vb_um` values are equal was already refused. The two figures agree only when the crossing pair happens to be the final pair; wear that decelerates or accelerates after the crossing makes them diverge, and the projected figure produces an uncensored number even for a curve that never reached the limit.

The verdict follows immediately, and it depends on the `application_class` of the geometry behind the trial's dossier product. The required tool life is 12.0 minutes for `ROUGHING`, 18.0 for `MEDIUM` and 25.0 for `FINISHING`; the maximum permitted surface roughness is 3.2 micrometres for `ROUGHING`, 1.6 for `MEDIUM` and 0.8 for `FINISHING`. The `verdict` is `PASS` when all three of the following hold: `censored` is false, `rated_life_min` is at least the required life for the class, and `measured_surface_ra_um` is at most the maximum for the class. Both numeric comparisons are inclusive at the boundary. Otherwise the `verdict` is `FAIL` — and note that a censored curve is always `FAIL`, however long the tool actually ran, because the measurement never demonstrated the wear criterion. Only `rated_life_min` enters the verdict; `projected_life_min` never does.

The dossier's aggregates are then rebuilt. `completed_trial_count` becomes the number of trials on the dossier whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, of any verdict, and `passed_trial_count` the number of those whose `verdict` is `PASS`. The `composite_score` is computed over the dossier's `COMPLETED` trials only — `RUNNING` and `INVALIDATED` trials are excluded — and is null when that set is empty. For each such trial, taken in ascending `created_at` order, a life ratio is formed as `rated_life_min` divided by the class's required life, capped at 2.0; a finish ratio is formed as the class's maximum roughness divided by `measured_surface_ra_um`, also capped at 2.0; and a per-trial subscore is 0.6 multiplied by the life ratio plus 0.4 multiplied by the finish ratio, **rounded half up to two decimal places at that point**. The `composite_score` is then the sum of those rounded subscores divided by the number of considered trials, rounded half up to two decimal places. The rounding order is pinned: each subscore is rounded before the average is taken, and averaging unrounded subscores with a single rounding at the end is a different and incorrect answer. Trials with a `FAIL` verdict are included in the average — a failure drags the score down rather than being ignored. Both the trial's `version` and the dossier's `version` advance by 1, the dossier's unconditionally because its evidence pool grew. And where the dossier has already been `APPROVED`, the closing trial reaches further: the product's `tool_life_index` and `qualified_workpiece_groups` are recomputed on the spot from the enlarged PASS population of §3.7 and the product's `version` advances by 1 as well. On any other dossier status no product figure moves.

### 3.7. Dossier Decisions and Product Qualification

Submitting a dossier is an `ApplicationEngineer` act. It requires the dossier to be `OPEN`, the product's `release_status` to still be `QUALIFICATION`, and `completed_trial_count` to be at least `required_trial_count` — a shortfall is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRIALS`, and note that this gate counts *completed* trials of any verdict, not passed ones. The dossier moves `OPEN` → `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_by_user_id` records the calling `X-User-ID`, and `version` advances by 1. From that moment the dossier is closed to new trials.

Deciding it belongs to the `QualityApprover`, and the three gates on approval are checked in a fixed order. First the score: if `composite_score` is null, or is strictly less than 1.00, the request is rejected with `ERR_COMPOSITE_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Second the coverage: if the number of distinct `workpiece_group` values across the dossier's `COMPLETED` trials with a `PASS` verdict is fewer than 2, the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WORKPIECE_COVERAGE` — one material, however well it performed, does not qualify a grade. Only third is the independence of the approver checked, as described in §2.12. The ordering is observable: an approver who is also the submitter, presenting a dossier with a score of 0.90, is told about the score, not about the conflict of interest.

An approval moves the dossier `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED`, which is terminal. It records `decided_by_user_id` and the optional `decision_note`, of at most 400 characters, and then recomputes the product's qualification figures from all PASS trials inside all `APPROVED` dossiers of that product. `tool_life_index` becomes the unweighted mean of those trials' `rated_life_min` values — the sum divided by the trial count, not weighted by `units_consumed` — rounded half up to one decimal place, and is null when no such trial exists. `qualified_workpiece_groups` becomes the distinct `workpiece_group` values of those trials, sorted ascending by the alphabetical spelling of the enum value. Dossiers in `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to either figure. Approval does **not** release the product: it stays in `QUALIFICATION` and still requires the explicit release described in §3.5. It does, however, complete the grade's own qualification: if the product's grade is at `PILOT`, the grade moves `PILOT` → `QUALIFIED` in the same request. The dossier's `version`, the product's `version` and the grade's `version` each advance by 1.

A rejection is equally terminal but has a deliberately different side-effect set. It requires a `decision_note` rather than accepting an optional one — capped at the same 400 characters, a longer note being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` on either branch — records `decided_by_user_id`, and sends the product back: `release_status` moves `QUALIFICATION` → `DEVELOPMENT` and all three of `pinned_grade_revision`, `pinned_geometry_revision` and `pinned_coating_revision` are set to null, so the product must re-enter qualification under a fresh dossier before it can ever be released. There is no release, no grade qualification and no index recomputation on this branch — `tool_life_index` and `qualified_workpiece_groups` are left standing at whatever they held — though `engineering_lead_time_days` and `expedite_eligible` are refreshed, the product having changed `release_status`. Neither the score gate nor the coverage gate applies to a rejection: the only checks are the dossier's `SUBMITTED` state and then the segregation of §2.12. The dossier's `version` and the product's `version` each advance by 1.

### 3.8. Customers, Credit and Tool Orders

A customer account is registered by a `ProductionPlanner` or an `ApplicationEngineer` with a `customer_code` unique across all customers, a `name`, a commercial `tier` of `STANDARD`, `PREFERRED` or `STRATEGIC`, and optionally an opening `regrind_credit_cents` balance, which defaults to 0 and, per item 4 of §2.11, may be any integer including a negative one. The account opens at `account_status` `ACTIVE` with `reserved_credit_cents` at 0 — and it opens `ACTIVE` even when the opening balance is negative, because the credit-hold cascade below belongs to the adjustment endpoint and is not run at registration. Throughout its life `regrind_credit_cents` is the unreserved prepaid reconditioning balance and `reserved_credit_cents` is the sum of `reserved_credit_cents` over the customer's jobs in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REFURBISHED`.

A credit adjustment is a `ProductionPlanner` act carrying a signed `delta_cents` and a `reason`, and it is the only way the balance may be driven negative after registration. It is admissible only while the account is `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD`: an adjustment against a `CLOSED` account is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTOMER_STATE`, that ledger being sealed. The adjustment applies `delta_cents` to `regrind_credit_cents`, and then a three-hop cascade may follow in either direction. If the resulting balance is below 0 while the account is `ACTIVE`, `account_status` moves `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD`, and because the account went on hold, every order of that customer in `SUBMITTED` or `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` moves to `ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `CREDIT_HOLD`. If instead the resulting balance is 0 or above while the account is at `CREDIT_HOLD`, the account moves `CREDIT_HOLD` → `ACTIVE`, and every order of that customer at `ON_HOLD` returns to `SUBMITTED` with `hold_reason` set back to null. That resumption is uniform: an order that was in `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` when the hold landed resumes as `SUBMITTED`, not as `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`. The customer's `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every order the cascade held or resumed.

Closing an account is permitted from `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD`, as `ACTIVE` → `CLOSED` or `CREDIT_HOLD` → `CLOSED`, carries a `close_reason`, and is refused while work is outstanding: no order of the customer may be in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CONFIRMED` or `ON_HOLD`, and no reconditioning job of the customer may be in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REFURBISHED`. A close attempt with outstanding orders or jobs is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_DEMAND_EXISTS`; the caller must cancel or settle them first. `CLOSED` is terminal and advances the customer's `version` by 1; closing an account that is already `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTOMER_STATE`, and that state check precedes the open-demand test.

Raising an order may be done by the buying `Customer` itself or by an `ApplicationEngineer` acting for it. The request carries the `customer_id`, the `product_id`, an `order_code` unique across all orders, the `quantity` of at least 1, optionally `expedite_requested`, and the mandatory `expected_product_version` described in §2.10. The order's `order_class` is copied from the product and is never supplied by the caller. The product must be orderable — an order against a product whose `release_status` is `RESTRICTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, or whose `supply_status` is `UNAVAILABLE`, is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`; a `WITHDRAWN` product specifically also trips the parent-state guard as `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`, which is the earlier of the two checks. The `quantity` must be at least the product's `min_order_qty`, with equality accepted, otherwise `ERR_BELOW_MIN_ORDER_QTY`; and `expedite_requested` may be true only when the product's `expedite_eligible` is true, otherwise `ERR_EXPEDITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The order opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `requires_reconfirmation` false and with `per_unit_net_cents`, `total_price_cents`, `promised_lead_time_days`, `hold_reason` and `shipped_at` all null — a draft is not yet priced — and the product's `version` advances by 1 because the quoted product row is now pinned by an order.

Submission prices the order and routes it, and the two order classes take visibly different branches. Both require the order to be `DRAFT` and the customer's `account_status` to be `ACTIVE` — a `CREDIT_HOLD` account is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` and a `CLOSED` one with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CLOSED` — but the account is examined **last**, at rung 42 of §2.11, after the order-state and every product gate below, so a `CREDIT_HOLD` customer submitting against an `UNAVAILABLE` product is told `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`, not about the hold. A `CATALOG` order additionally requires the product's `release_status` to be `RELEASED`, moves `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`, and sets `promised_lead_time_days` to the product's `engineering_lead_time_days` unchanged. A `SPECIAL` order is admissible while the product is in `QUALIFICATION` *or* `RELEASED`, moves `DRAFT` → `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` instead, and sets `promised_lead_time_days` to the product's `engineering_lead_time_days` plus 5 review days. Both class conditions are reported as `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`, and both are judged after the general orderability test of §3.8 above — the one that refuses a `RESTRICTED` or `WITHDRAWN` release posture and an `UNAVAILABLE` supply posture — which runs on submission exactly as it does on creation. A `Customer` caller may submit only its own order. Both branches price the order, recompute the product's `open_order_units`, and advance both the order's `version` and the product's `version` by 1.

Pricing works from the product's `list_price_cents` and applies three surcharges and one discount. The grade surcharge is 0.08 when the grade's `target_workpiece_group` is `N`, `S` or `H`, and nothing for `P`, `M` or `K`. The coating surcharge is nothing for an uncoated product, 0.06 for a `PVD` recipe and 0.10 for a `CVD` one. The special surcharge is 0.18 for a `SPECIAL` order and nothing for a `CATALOG` one. The volume discount is 0.12 when `quantity` is at least 500, otherwise 0.07 when it is at least 100, otherwise 0.03 when it is at least 25, otherwise nothing. The gross per-unit price is `list_price_cents` multiplied by the quantity one plus the sum of the three surcharge fractions; `per_unit_net_cents` is that gross figure multiplied by the quantity one minus the volume discount fraction, **rounded half up to whole cents at that point**; and `total_price_cents` is `per_unit_net_cents` multiplied by `quantity`. The rounding order is pinned to the unit: rounding once on the multiplied-out total instead differs by tens of cents on a large order and is incorrect.

Confirmation is the join point of the two branches, and both an `ApplicationEngineer` and a `ProductionPlanner` may perform it, from either `SUBMITTED` or `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`. It re-checks the whole engineering chain at the moment of commitment, in this order: the product's `release_status` must be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`; the grade's `status` must be `QUALIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_NOT_QUALIFIED`; the geometry's `status` must be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_GEOMETRY_NOT_RELEASED`; a referenced coating recipe must have `is_validated` true, otherwise `ERR_COATING_NOT_VALIDATED`; and all three revision pins must match the current revisions of the referenced rows, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_REVISION_STALE`; and only then, at rung 42 of §2.11, the customer's `account_status` must be `ACTIVE`, a `CREDIT_HOLD` account giving `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` and a `CLOSED` one `ERR_CUSTOMER_CLOSED`. On success the order moves to `CONFIRMED`, `requires_reconfirmation` is set to false, and the order's `version` advances by 1 — the order's alone: confirmation touches neither the product nor the customer, since it changes no figure either of them holds. An order pushed back to `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` by an upstream revision therefore cannot be re-confirmed until the product has been repinned and released again — the cycle `CONFIRMED` → `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` → `CONFIRMED` closes only when the engineering chain is whole.

Shipping is the `ProductionPlanner`'s act and moves the order `CONFIRMED` → `SHIPPED`. It requires the order to be `CONFIRMED` and `requires_reconfirmation` to be false — both conditions carrying the single code `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, so an order awaiting reconfirmation is refused as a state violation rather than as an unorderable product — and then requires the product's `supply_status` to be `MAKE_TO_ORDER` or `STOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`; the `release_status` is not re-examined at this point. It sets `shipped_at` to the current logical clock instant, rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits as the bookkeeping stamps of §2.4, recomputes the product's `open_order_units`, and advances both the order's `version` and the product's `version` by 1. `SHIPPED` is terminal.

Cancellation, recorded with a `cancel_reason`, is admissible from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CONFIRMED` or `ON_HOLD` — the declared moves are `DRAFT` → `CANCELLED`, `SUBMITTED` → `CANCELLED`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` → `CANCELLED`, `CONFIRMED` → `CANCELLED` and `ON_HOLD` → `CANCELLED` — and may be requested by the owning `Customer`, an `ApplicationEngineer` or a `ProductionPlanner`. `CANCELLED` is terminal. The effect differs by origin: a `DRAFT` order was never counted in the product's open load, so cancelling it advances only the order's `version`; cancelling from any of the other four states recomputes the product's `open_order_units` and advances the product's `version` as well as the order's. An attempt to cancel an order that has already shipped, or one already `CANCELLED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, the order's own invalid-state code from item 12 of §2.11.

### 3.9. Reconditioning and the Tool Life Extension Programme

Reconditioning regrinds worn solid-carbide tools and pays the customer back for the life it recovers. Intake may be requested by the owning `Customer` or by a `ProductionPlanner` and carries the `customer_id`, the `product_id` of the tools, a `job_code` unique across all jobs, `units_received`, the count of tools received, at least 1 and at most 500, optionally the `predecessor_job_id` of the prior regrind pass on the same tools, and the mandatory `expected_customer_version` described in §2.10.

Three intake gates apply beyond the account checks. The product must be regrindable: a job on a product whose `tool_type` is `INSERT`, or whose `min_regrind_length_mm` is not strictly below its `nominal_length_mm`, is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_REGRINDABLE`. A supplied `predecessor_job_id` must name a job that is in `RETURNED`, belongs to the same `customer_id` and the same `product_id`, and is not already the predecessor of some other job — one pass may have only one successor — and any of those failures is `ERR_INVALID_PREDECESSOR_CHAIN`. And the intake must be prepaid: the reservation of `units_received` multiplied by 1850 cents must not exceed the customer's `regrind_credit_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGRIND_CREDIT`. On success the job opens at `status` `RECEIVED`, `regrind_pass_no` is set to the length of the `predecessor_job_id` chain plus one, `reserved_credit_cents` is set to `units_received` multiplied by 1850, the customer's `regrind_credit_cents` is reduced by that reservation while `reserved_credit_cents` rises by it, and the customer's `version` advances by 1. Everything the later steps will fill in opens empty: `wheel_id`, `units_inspected`, `measured_length_mm`, `measured_corner_wear_um`, `remaining_regrinds`, `life_extension_pct`, `warranted_life_extension_pct`, `units_returned` and `rebate_cents` are all null, and `scrap_recommended` and `recoat_applied` are both false. Note also that the reservation is deducted from the balance without the credit-hold cascade of §3.8 being run, so an intake that drives `regrind_credit_cents` to exactly 0 leaves the account `ACTIVE` and holds no orders.

Inspection may be performed by a `ProductionPlanner` or a `QualityApprover`. It requires the job to be `RECEIVED` and carries `units_inspected`, which must equal `units_received` — any other figure is `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH` — together with `measured_length_mm`, the mean remaining usable length at one decimal place, and `measured_corner_wear_um`, the mean corner wear at one decimal place. The job moves `RECEIVED` → `INSPECTED`.

Two life-extension figures are then computed, and they answer different questions from the same job. The **residual-geometry** figure is derived from the measurement. `remaining_regrinds` is the whole number of passes left, computed as `measured_length_mm` minus the product's `min_regrind_length_mm`, divided by the product's `regrind_step_mm`, rounded **down** to a whole number, and then floored at 0 so it is never negative. `life_extension_pct` is 100 multiplied by `remaining_regrinds`, multiplied by the product's `regrind_step_mm`, divided by the product's `nominal_length_mm`, rounded half up to one decimal place. And `scrap_recommended` is true exactly when `measured_length_mm` is strictly below the product's `min_regrind_length_mm`. The **cumulative-pass-history** figure is the contractual number quoted in the tool life extension programme and ignores every measurement: `warranted_life_extension_pct` is 35.0 multiplied by 0.7 raised to the power of `regrind_pass_no` minus one, rounded half up to one decimal place — 35.0 on the first pass, 24.5 on the second, 17.2 on the third, and so on. Because one figure reads the length and the other reads only the pass number, they disagree on identical inputs, and only the residual-geometry figure enters the money path or gates the refurbishment.

Refurbishment is the `ProductionPlanner`'s shop-floor act and names the `wheel_id` to grind on, with an optional `recoat_applied` flag. The job must be `INSPECTED`, `scrap_recommended` must be false and `remaining_regrinds` must be at least 1 — a failure of either is `ERR_NO_REGRINDS_REMAINING`. The wheel must be at `AVAILABLE`: an `EXPIRED` wheel is refused with `ERR_WHEEL_EXPIRED` and a wheel at `DRESSING` with `ERR_WHEEL_REQUIRES_DRESSING`. Those two paragraphs read in narrative order, not evaluation order: after the job's own state check, the three wheel conditions are evaluated **before** the regrinds condition, per items 14, 15 and 40 of §2.11, so a scrapped-out job booked onto an `EXPIRED` wheel reports `ERR_WHEEL_EXPIRED`. The wheel must also have enough abrasive left for the whole job: its `remaining_life_units` must be at least `units_inspected` multiplied by the product's `grind_units_per_tool`, and a wheel with less is likewise refused with `ERR_WHEEL_REQUIRES_DRESSING`, because it must be dressed before it can take the work. And a recoat may be asked for only when the product actually has a coating: if `recoat_applied` is true while the product's `coating_recipe_id` is null or the referenced recipe's `is_validated` is false, the request is rejected with `ERR_COATING_NOT_VALIDATED` — the last of all these checks, as item 20 of §2.11 records, so a recoat requested on a job with no regrinds left reports `ERR_NO_REGRINDS_REMAINING` instead. `recoat_applied` is optional and defaults to false, in which case no coating condition is examined at all. On success the job moves `INSPECTED` → `REFURBISHED`, `wheel_id` and `recoat_applied` are recorded, `units_inspected` multiplied by `grind_units_per_tool` is added to the wheel's `consumed_life_units`, and if that charge takes the wheel's `remaining_life_units` below 150 the wheel moves `AVAILABLE` → `DRESSING`. Both the job's `version` and the wheel's `version` advance by 1.

Returning the tools settles the money and moves the job `REFURBISHED` → `RETURNED`. The job must be `REFURBISHED`, and the request carries `units_returned`, which must not exceed `units_inspected`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`; the persisted `units_returned` is set to `units_inspected`. The `rebate_cents` is `life_extension_pct` divided by 100, multiplied by `units_returned`, multiplied by a rebate basis of 900 cents, rounded half up to whole cents — and it uses the residual-geometry figure only, never `warranted_life_extension_pct`. That rebate is added to the customer's `regrind_credit_cents`, and the intake reservation is **consumed rather than refunded**: the job's `reserved_credit_cents` is set to 0 and the customer's `reserved_credit_cents` is reduced by it, with no corresponding credit back to the balance. If the customer was at `CREDIT_HOLD` and the rebate brings `regrind_credit_cents` back to 0 or above, the account moves `CREDIT_HOLD` → `ACTIVE` and its `ON_HOLD` orders resume as `SUBMITTED` with `hold_reason` set to null. The job's `version` and the customer's `version` each advance by 1, and `RETURNED` is terminal.

Scrapping is the alternative ending, open to a `ProductionPlanner` or a `QualityApprover`, admissible from either `RECEIVED` or `INSPECTED` as `RECEIVED` → `SCRAPPED` or `INSPECTED` → `SCRAPPED`, and recorded with a `scrap_reason`. Its side-effect set is deliberately the mirror image of the return path: the intake reservation is **refunded in full** — the job's `reserved_credit_cents` is added back to the customer's `regrind_credit_cents`, the customer's `reserved_credit_cents` is reduced by it, and the job's `reserved_credit_cents` is set to 0 — while `units_returned` and `rebate_cents` are both set to 0 and no rebate is paid. Because a scrap refund merely returns money the customer had already paid in, it cannot by itself lift a credit hold that an adjustment created; only a credit adjustment or a job return restores an account to `ACTIVE`. The job's `version` and the customer's `version` each advance by 1, and `SCRAPPED` is terminal.
