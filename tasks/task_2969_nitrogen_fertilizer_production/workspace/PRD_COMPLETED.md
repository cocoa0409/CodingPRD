# Nitrogen Fertilizer Production API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A nitrogen fertilizer complex is one of the few industrial businesses where the cost of the product is dominated by a single commodity input whose price moves daily, and where the physical asset that converts that input degrades measurably with every hour it runs. Natural gas becomes ammonia across a steam-methane reformer; ammonia becomes urea, ammonium nitrate and UAN solution across a slate of downstream lines; the finished tons sit in domes, tanks and terminals waiting for a spring application season that is short, seasonal and unforgiving. Between the gas meter and the customer's field sit a hedge book, a catalyst whose activity is falling, an air permit with a rolling mass ceiling, a process-safety-management program, a regulated-substance security plan, and a rail-barge-truck network whose terminals each have their own capacity and their own operator signature.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that complex. The service is not a reporting layer over someone else's ERP: it is the system of record for the decisions themselves. It decides whether a campaign may be approved given the state of the catalyst, what a settled ton of ammonia actually cost given which hedge lots were drawn in which order, whether a commercial reservation may be booked against ammonia the plant has not yet made, whether a regulated product may be routed through a particular terminal, and whether the site may trade at all given its emissions position. Every one of those decisions is deterministic, auditable, and reproducible from the stored state — the same request against the same state must always produce the same answer, including the same rejection.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **time is explicit**: every window, expiry, seasonal month boundary and rolling-emissions cut-off is evaluated against a server-side logical UTC clock that only ever moves when a regulatory administrator moves it. Nothing in this service reads wall-clock time. Second, **degradation propagates**: a plant that breaches its permit, a catalyst that falls below its minimum activity, or a train that goes down for an outage does not merely change one row — it unwinds the commercial commitments that were built on top of it, in a defined order, in a single request. Those cascades are the heart of the specification and are described in full in Section 3.

Every path in this specification is mounted under `/nitrogen-ops`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or present but empty or whitespace-only, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six role names below — the comparison is exact, so any difference of spelling or letter case is a miss — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401. If the role is recognised but is not one of the roles authorised for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These three checks run before anything else, in exactly that order.

The six roles are `RegulatoryAdmin`, `ProcessEngineer`, `SafetyOfficer`, `CommercialManager`, `LogisticsCoordinator` and `TerminalOperator`. Their responsibilities are deliberately disjoint:

- A `RegulatoryAdmin` is the environmental and permitting authority for the site. It registers plants, publishes the spot natural-gas index, records air-permit standing changes, ingests continuous-emissions-monitoring readings, and drives the logical clock. It may not touch production, commercial or logistics state.
- A `ProcessEngineer` owns the reformer trains, the catalyst charges, the ammonia campaigns, the authoring and execution of outages, the outage task graph, and the act of booking finished tons into storage. An engineer authors outages but can never approve one, and can never approve a campaign they themselves created.
- A `SafetyOfficer` is the process-safety and regulated-substance authority. It maintains the plant's process-safety-management program standing, signs the pre-startup safety review on an outage, approves or rejects submitted outages, and records the regulated-substance clearance decision on an order. It never authors an outage or a campaign, so an outage approval is always a second signature.
- A `CommercialManager` owns the natural-gas hedge book, the product slate, customer quoting, and the order book including ammonia reservations and their release. It cannot produce tons, cannot move product, and cannot clear a security check.
- A `LogisticsCoordinator` owns the storage and terminal network and the rail, barge and truck movements up to the moment of delivery. It books, departs and cancels movements but never acknowledges receipt.
- A `TerminalOperator` is the receiving end of that network, and acknowledging receipt is the only write it may perform.

Five of the six roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `RegulatoryAdmin`, `ProcessEngineer`, `SafetyOfficer`, `CommercialManager` or `LogisticsCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `created_by_user_id` on a campaign or an order, as `security_checked_by_user_id` on an order, and as `authored_by_user_id`, `psm_reviewed_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on an outage — and it compares recorded identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

`TerminalOperator` is the one exception and is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `TerminalOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StorageAsset`. Exactly one ownership check consumes that fact: a terminal operator may acknowledge receipt of a movement only when the movement's `dest_asset_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; any other movement is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The check is a plain string comparison against the stored foreign key — the identifier is never itself resolved to a `StorageAsset` row, so an `X-User-ID` naming no asset at all earns `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_OWNER` rather than `ERR_STORAGE_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, and the read endpoints a terminal operator may call accept any non-empty value.

Segregation of duties is enforced with a single code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and is described in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase A–Z and the digits 0–9.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and so is a value that is not a whole number. A parameter supplied with an explicitly empty value is treated as absent and takes its default. `offset` carries no upper bound: an offset at or beyond `total_count` is accepted and answers with an empty `data` array and `has_more` false.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific workflow in Section 3 declares a different processing order for a sweep. Because the logical clock only moves when a client moves it, many rows legitimately share one `created_at`; ties are broken by the order in which the rows were written, so the effective ordering is always insertion order. The same tie-break applies wherever Section 2 or 3 names `created_at` ascending as a sweep order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses carry neither of the four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Version Semantics and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never parse them. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment the row is written. Every instant this service emits — whether a resource field or the `api_request_timestamp` of the envelope — is rendered in that one canonical form, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, always in UTC and always with exactly three fractional digits, never more and never fewer. Instants **accepted on input** are deliberately more permissive than that single output form: the date and time may be separated by `T` or a space, seconds and fractional digits are optional, a bare date with no time at all reads as midnight UTC, and a trailing `Z` or a `±HH:MM` offset are both accepted, an offset being converted to UTC before the value is stored. Anything that does not parse that way is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a value carrying more than three fractional digits is accepted on input, its sub-millisecond remainder being dropped when the instant is emitted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that arrive as a cascade from an operation aimed at some other resource. The increment is counted **once per row per request**: when a single request writes the same row more than once — because two hops of one cascade both reach it, or because the endpoint writes a row that its own cascade then revisits — that row's `version` still advances by exactly 1, and its `updated_at` carries the one clock value the whole request was served at. Where Section 3 says an operation "bumps" or "increments the version" of a secondary resource, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed at the same time. Two entities are deliberately exempt: a `ProductLine` and an `EmissionsReading` are both immutable once written, so each carries only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on those two resources at all, and no endpoint accepts a version expectation against them.

Time in this service is a **logical UTC clock**. Before any client moves it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `RegulatoryAdmin` may set it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, which answers with a `data` object carrying the single field `current_utc` holding the new value. The supplied instant may move the clock backwards as well as forwards; the only requirement on it is that it parses, so there is no monotonicity check to fail. Moving the clock is not a no-op: it re-derives every storage asset's seasonal set-aside and available position, and it re-derives every plant's rolling 30-day emissions aggregate, exceeded-pollutant list and operating posture — plants being swept in `created_at` ascending order — because readings that have aged out of the window stop counting. It expires nothing on its own — quote expiry is only ever realised by the commercial expiry checkpoint described in §3.8.

### 2.5. Flat Topology, Path Resolution and List Filters

The thirteen resource families in this service are peers. No resource is nested inside another in a URL; every path is either a collection, a single resource under that collection, or an action beneath a single resource. Relationships are expressed by foreign keys carried in request bodies, and reverse navigation is always a filtered list on the child collection rather than a nested path.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right and the first unresolvable identifier decides the error. Because there is exactly one path parameter per addressable resource, that resolution yields a family-specific 404: `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALYST_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEDGE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORAGE_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURNAROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_EMISSIONS_READING_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys in a request body are resolved after the path, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and produce the same family-specific 404 codes.

Filtered lists are the only navigation mechanism, so the filter vocabulary is part of the contract. Trains may be listed by `plant_id` and by `status`. Catalyst charges may be listed by `train_id` and `status`. Hedge lots may be listed by `plant_id`, `status` and `delivery_month`. Product lines may be listed by `plant_id`. Campaigns may be listed by `train_id`, `catalyst_charge_id` and `status`. Orders may be listed by `run_id`, `product_line_id`, `destination_asset_id`, `allocation_status` and `security_status`. Storage assets may be listed by `plant_id`, `asset_kind` and `product_code`. Movements may be listed by `origin_asset_id`, `dest_asset_id`, `production_order_id`, `status` and `mode`. Quotes may be listed by `plant_id`, `product_line_id` and `status`. Outages may be listed by `plant_id`, `train_id`, `status` and `scope`. Outage tasks may be listed by `turnaround_id`, `predecessor_task_id` and `status`. Emissions readings may be listed by `plant_id` and `pollutant`. Every one of those filters is optional, and all of them compose with `limit` and `offset`. A filter drawn from a closed vocabulary that is handed a value outside that vocabulary is **not** a schema violation — it simply matches no rows — and neither is an identifier filter naming a row that does not exist: both answer 200 with an empty `data` array rather than a 422 or a 404. A filter supplied with an explicitly empty value is treated as absent, and query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

### 2.6. Rounding, Precision and Tonnage Discipline

All rounding in this service is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. The number of decimal places is fixed per quantity class and is never negotiable:

- Currency per finished or ammonia ton, and currency totals: 2 decimal places. This covers `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, `quoted_price_usd_per_ton`, `landed_cost_usd`, `base_freight_usd`, `fuel_surcharge_usd` and `freight_total_usd`. Two currency-per-ton figures sit deliberately outside this class and are named where they are defined: `freight_per_ton_usd` at 4 places, below, and a product line's cash `conversion_cost_usd_per_ton` at **3** places, which §3.5 states alongside the feed intensity `ammonia_tons_per_product_ton` — also 3 places — that it is quoted next to.
- Gas unit prices: 4 decimal places. This covers `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu`, `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu`, `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu` and `gas_basis_usd_per_mmbtu`. Freight per ton, `freight_per_ton_usd`, also carries 4 decimal places.
- Tonnages: 3 decimal places. This covers `target_ammonia_tons`, `actual_ammonia_tons`, `requested_tons`, the reservation input `reserve_tons` alongside the stored `reserved_tons`, `allocated_ammonia_tons`, `produced_tons`, `unallocated_ammonia_tons`, `opening_balance_tons`, `on_hand_tons`, `seasonal_reserve_tons`, `available_tons`, `capacity_tons`, `tons`, `received_tons`, and the per-reading and rolling emissions figures `rolling_30d_tons`. Two registry inputs are tonnage-shaped but sit outside this class and carry **2** decimal places each: a plant's `permit_limit_tons_30d` and a train's nameplate `rated_ammonia_tpd`.
- Gas volumes in MMBtu (`mmbtu_volume`, `consumed_mmbtu`, `remaining_mmbtu`, `consumed_gas_mmbtu`), operating hours (`design_life_hours` and the `new_charge_design_life_hours` supplied when an outage installs a replacement charge, `baseline_operating_hours`, `cumulative_operating_hours`, `actual_operating_hours`), averaging-period lengths (`interval_hours`), and tariff distance (`distance_miles`): 1 decimal place. Emissions mass rate `rate_lb_per_hour` carries 3 decimal places.
- Activity and percentage-like fractions: `activity_pct` and `calendar_activity_pct` carry 2 decimal places; `poisoning_index` and `seasonal_reserve_pct` carry 2 decimal places and must each lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive.
- Whole-day quantities — `window_days`, `critical_path_days`, `earliest_start_day`, `duration_days` — are exact integers with no rounding, as are the counters `regeneration_count`, `tasks_total_count`, `tasks_done_count` and `psm_findings_count`.

**Where the rounding happens is as much a part of the contract as how much.** Two computations in this domain round early and deliberately, and two round exactly once at the end; §3 states which is which for each. Getting this backwards produces answers that are wrong by a cent or two on realistic tonnages, which is a defect, not a tolerance.

Precision is enforced on the way in as well. A client literal carrying more decimals than its field's declared places is **not** rejected: it is rounded half up to those places as the body is validated, and it is that rounded figure that is stored, echoed back and fed into every later calculation. An integer-valued field accepts a JSON number whose fractional part is exactly zero and rejects any genuinely fractional value. Numeric fields are always emitted as JSON numbers rather than strings, and a field's declared places bound its precision rather than forcing trailing zeros onto the wire, so a tonnage of exactly twelve and a half tons is emitted as `12.5`.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Allocation and Booking

Two writes in this service sit on top of a parent whose state can legitimately support several different correct answers, so state alone cannot tell a correct writer from one working off a stale read. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency against the parent's `version`.

The first is **reserving finished tons against a campaign**. That request must carry `expected_run_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent `ProductionRun`. If the supplied value does not equal the run's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A reservation legitimately succeeds whenever the run still has enough unallocated ammonia, so without this guard a writer reasoning from an out-of-date view of the run's ammonia pool would be indistinguishable from a correct one. Note that a successful reservation itself advances the run's `version`, so a client that reserves twice against the same campaign must re-read the run between the two calls.

The second is **booking a movement out of a storage asset**. That request must carry `expected_origin_asset_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the origin `StorageAsset`. A mismatch against the asset's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because the aggregate-commitment rule of §3.7 deliberately permits several bookings to occupy the same window, a second booking is legitimate on its own terms; the version check pins which view of the asset the booking was priced and sized against. A successful booking advances the origin asset's `version`.

Both fields are integers bounded at **at least 0** by the body schema, since `version` itself starts at 1 and never decreases. That bound matters for the order of rejection: a negative expectation never reaches the version comparison at all but is refused earlier, at rung 4 of §2.11, as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than as the 412 a merely-stale value earns. A value that is non-negative but simply wrong — zero, or any figure other than the parent's current `version` — is the 412.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version-expectation field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no `expected_run_version` and no `expected_origin_asset_version`, and supplying one is not a way to guard them.

### 2.8. Operating Posture: The Site-Level Gate

Every plant carries `operating_posture`, one of `NORMAL`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never written directly by a client; it is derived, and it is re-derived on every read of the plant and after every event that could change its inputs. Those inputs are the plant's `permit_status`, its `psm_program_status`, and the set of pollutants currently breaching the air permit.

The derivation is a strict ladder and **the order of evaluation is part of the contract, with `FROZEN` tested first and winning outright**. If `permit_status` is `SUSPENDED`, or if two or more distinct pollutants are currently in exceedance, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if exactly one pollutant is in exceedance, or `psm_program_status` is `OVERDUE`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `NORMAL`. Because the first branch is tested first, a plant whose permit is suspended is `FROZEN` regardless of how clean its stack is, and a plant with two exceedances is `FROZEN` even if its safety program is `CURRENT`.

A `FROZEN` plant is closed for business but not closed for compliance. It accepts regulatory writes — air-permit standing, process-safety-program standing and emissions readings — plus outage authoring and outage progression, plus all read traffic. It refuses eight commercial and production writes: creating a campaign, approving a campaign, starting a campaign, reopening a campaign, reserving tons against a campaign, producing tons into storage, booking a movement, and issuing a quote. All eight refusals carry `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Note what is deliberately absent from that list: settling a campaign and aborting one are both permitted while the plant is `FROZEN`, so the restatement loop of §3.4 is closed on its way back in but not on its way out. A `RESTRICTED` plant blocks none of those writes; instead it prices new quotes at a premium, as §3.8 describes.

The plant that governs an operation is not always the one named in the request body. For a campaign it is the plant of the campaign's reformer train; for an order it is the plant behind the order's campaign; for a movement it is the plant of the origin storage asset; for a quote it is the quote's own `plant_id`.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties Across the Signature Chain

Four points in this service require that the person performing a step is not the person who performed the step before it, and one requires the opposite. All five are enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

- Approving an outage requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the outage's `authored_by_user_id` **and** from its `psm_reviewed_by_user_id`. Two distinct people must already have touched the outage before a third can approve it.
- Rejecting an outage requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `authored_by_user_id`. The reviewer identity is not consulted on this arm.
- Revising a rejected outage inverts the rule: the acting `X-User-ID` must **equal** `authored_by_user_id`. Only the original author may take their own outage back to draft.
- Approving a campaign requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the campaign's `created_by_user_id`.
- Recording a regulated-substance clearance decision on an order requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the order's `created_by_user_id`.

### 2.10. Business Code Uniqueness Scopes

Every resource family carries an operator-facing business code alongside its UUID, and each code has its own uniqueness scope. A collision inside the declared scope is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409); the same string outside that scope is perfectly legal. Codes are compared as exact case-sensitive strings, and a scope covers **every** row of the family in it whatever that row's status: a code already spent on a `RETIRED` catalyst charge, a `CLOSED` hedge lot, an `ABORTED` campaign, a `CANCELLED` movement or a `WITHDRAWN` quote is still taken and cannot be re-used.

`site_code` is unique across all plants and must be 3 to 12 characters of uppercase A–Z and digits only. `train_code` is unique per `plant_id`; `charge_code` is unique per `train_id`; `lot_code` is unique per `plant_id`; `asset_code` is unique per `plant_id`; `run_code` is unique per plant; `turnaround_code` is unique per `plant_id`; `task_code` is unique per `turnaround_id`. Four codes are unique **globally**, with no parent scoping at all: `product_code`, `order_code`, `shipment_code` and `quote_code`. The global uniqueness of `product_code` matters beyond duplicate detection: the product slate is a single register keyed by product code, so looking up "the product line for this product code" is always single-valued, which is what makes the regulated-substance lookups of §3.5 and §3.7 well defined. The `new_charge_code` supplied when an outage installs a replacement catalyst charge is checked against the same per-train scope as `charge_code`.

Business codes other than `site_code` are 2 to 16 characters, except `train_code`, which is 2 to 12. A plant's `name` is 1 to 120 characters and must not be blank. Length bounds are measured on the value exactly as supplied, and that is also how it is stored — nothing is trimmed — while the not-blank test is applied to the trimmed value.

### 2.11. The Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is deterministic: the rules are evaluated in the ascending order listed below and **the first violated rule wins**. Implementations must not short-circuit on a later rule because it is cheaper to evaluate. The ladder is:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared role names — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. The acting role is authorised for the target endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
4. The request body is schema-valid: required fields present, types correct, enum values within their declared sets, and numeric fields inside their declared bounds — tonnages strictly positive **except where a field is explicitly bounded at zero below**, fractions between 0 and 1 inclusive, `poisoning_index` between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive, `distance_miles` strictly positive, `duration_days` at least 1, `priority_rank` between 1 and 99, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, `expires_at` after the quote's `created_at`, and `delivery_month` matching exactly the four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between 01 and 12. Six numeric input fields are bounded at **at least zero** rather than strictly positive, and each therefore accepts exactly 0 as a legitimate statement: the physically counted `opening_balance_tons` of §3.6, the measured `rate_lb_per_hour` of §3.10, a catalyst charge's optional `baseline_operating_hours`, the safety review's `findings_count`, and the two version-expectation fields `expected_run_version` and `expected_origin_asset_version` of §2.7 — which, being bounded here at zero, make a *negative* expectation this rung's `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than rung 11's stale-version 412. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint. One member of this rung is not a body-only test: at delivery acknowledgement `received_tons` must also be no greater than the movement's tendered `tons`, and although that is a comparison against stored state it is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) all the same — it simply cannot be evaluated before the movement is resolved, so it is reached only once rungs 7 and 9 have passed, exactly as §3.7 describes. An absent or empty request body reads as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint that requires no fields accepts a request carrying no body at all; a body that is not parseable JSON, or is JSON but not an object, is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and a field present with an explicit null counts as absent, so a required field is never satisfied by null while an optional one falls back to its declared default.
5. Every identifier in the request path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — otherwise the family-specific 404 of §2.5. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
6. Every foreign key in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the field order the endpoint declares — otherwise the family-specific 404. Applies to every creation endpoint and to any action endpoint carrying a foreign key in its body.
7. When the acting role is `TerminalOperator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `dest_asset_id` of the movement being acknowledged — otherwise `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_OWNER` (403). Applies to delivery acknowledgement only.
8. Scoped business codes are unique inside the scopes of §2.10 — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to every creation endpoint and to outage completion, which mints a `new_charge_code`.
9. The target resource's current state permits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, or one of the three more specific state codes `ERR_RUN_NOT_COMPLETED`, `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_PRODUCED` and `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` where the workflows in §3 nominate them, all at 409. Applies to every action endpoint. One scope note: for outage-task completion this rung covers only the task's own status being `DONE` or `BLOCKED`, and the parent outage not being `IN_PROGRESS`. A `PENDING` task inside an `IN_PROGRESS` outage is **not** reported here; it is reported at rung 24 as `ERR_TASK_PREDECESSOR_NOT_DONE`, which is the only way that situation ever surfaces.
10. The governing plant's `operating_posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` (409). Applies to exactly eight operations: campaign creation, campaign approval, campaign start, campaign reopen, order reservation, order production, movement booking, and quote issue. Campaign settlement and campaign abort are **not** on that list and proceed on a `FROZEN` plant.
11. The supplied `expected_run_version` or `expected_origin_asset_version` equals the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_STORAGE_VERSION` (412). Applies to order reservation and movement booking respectively.
12. Segregation of duties as specified in §2.9 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to outage approval, outage rejection, outage revision, campaign approval and order security check.
13. The reformer train's `ACTIVE` catalyst charge has `activity_pct` of at least 55.00, evaluated on the severity-adjusted variant only — otherwise `ERR_CATALYST_ACTIVITY_TOO_LOW` (409). Applies to campaign approval and campaign start.
14. The reformer train is available for the requested campaign operation — otherwise `ERR_TRAIN_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to campaign creation, campaign approval, campaign start and campaign reopen, but the test is not the same width on all four: for creation, approval and start a train that is `DERATED` or `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND` is unavailable, while **reopen refuses only `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`**, so a campaign may legitimately be reopened onto a `DERATED` train — which is exactly the train state a settlement that spent the charge leaves behind. §3.4 states both widths.
15. The outage carries `psm_review_decision` equal to `ENDORSED` — otherwise `ERR_PSM_REVIEW_MISSING` (409). Applies to outage approval, outage start and outage completion.
16. Regulated-substance routing: when the product line is regulated, the order's destination storage asset must be `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`; and when the product line whose `product_code` equals a movement's origin-asset `product_code` is regulated, both origin and destination assets must be `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`. Otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT` (403). Applies to order creation, order production and movement booking — the destination-asset test runs again when tons are actually produced, so an asset downgraded between creation and production refuses the production even though the order was accepted. Because `product_code` is globally unique the lookup is single-valued, and when no product line carries that code the movement is treated as unregulated.
17. An order on a regulated product line has `security_status` of `CLEARED` or `EXEMPT` before tons may be produced into storage — otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_CHECK_NOT_CLEARED` (409). Applies to order production only. Reserving ammonia is deliberately **not** gated on clearance.
18. Product identity agrees along the chain — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to order creation, movement drafting, movement booking and quote drafting.
19. No two campaigns on the same reformer train hold overlapping half-open windows while both are live — otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to campaign creation and campaign approval, and the two consult different live sets: creation tests against campaigns in `PLANNED`, `APPROVED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`, while approval tests against the narrower set `APPROVED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` — a `PLANNED` neighbour therefore blocks a new campaign but not the approval of an existing one — and approval always excludes the campaign being approved from its own comparison. §3.4 states both sets.
20. No other outage on the same plant sitting in `APPROVED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` holds a closed window intersecting the proposed one, and every such neighbouring outage is separated from the proposed window by at least 14 whole days — otherwise `ERR_TURNAROUND_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409). Only those three statuses populate the outage calendar: a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` neighbour is invisible to this rung however close its window sits. Applies to outage approval.
21. An emissions reading's `reading_at` is not after the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_EMISSIONS_READING_IN_FUTURE` (422). Applies to emissions ingestion.
22. A task's `predecessor_task_id` does not close a cycle in the outage's dependency graph, and the predecessor belongs to the same outage — otherwise `ERR_TASK_CYCLE_DETECTED` (409). Applies to outage-task creation.
23. `critical_path_days` does not exceed `window_days` — otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT` (422). Applies to outage submission and outage approval.
24. A task may be completed only when its `predecessor_task_id` is null or that predecessor is `DONE` — otherwise `ERR_TASK_PREDECESSOR_NOT_DONE` (409). Applies to outage-task completion.
25. Ammonia conservation: a reservation's ammonia requirement does not exceed the campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons`, and a settlement's `actual_ammonia_tons` is not below the tonnage already promised to that campaign's `RESERVED` and `CONSUMED` orders — otherwise `ERR_AMMONIA_BALANCE_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to order reservation and campaign completion.
26. Tonnage entering a storage asset, or promised into one, does not push it past `capacity_tons` — otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to order reservation, order production, movement booking, delivery acknowledgement and opening-balance entry. The five sites sum slightly different things, and §3.5, §3.6 and §3.7 give each in full: reservation adds the destination asset's `on_hand_tons`, the summed `reserved_tons` of its other reserved orders and this `reserve_tons`; production and delivery add `on_hand_tons` to the tonnage landing now; booking adds the destination asset's inbound `BOOKED` and `IN_TRANSIT` tons; and opening-balance entry simply compares the counted figure against `capacity_tons`.
27. Aggregate outbound commitment on the origin asset stays inside its uncommitted position at every instant of the proposed window — otherwise `ERR_STORAGE_AVAILABILITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to movement booking.
28. `produced_tons` does not exceed the order's `reserved_tons` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCED_EXCEEDS_RESERVED` (409). Applies to order production.

The practical consequence worth internalising is that authentication precedes authorisation, authorisation precedes schema, schema precedes existence — with the single carve-out noted at rung 4, the `received_tons` ceiling, which wears rung 4's error code but cannot be evaluated until the movement has been resolved and its status checked — existence precedes uniqueness, uniqueness precedes state, state precedes the posture gate, the posture gate precedes the stale-version guards, and every business, safety, temporal and fiscal rule sits below all of those. A frozen plant therefore surfaces as `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` rather than as the stale-version rejection it would also have earned, and a stale-version rejection surfaces in preference to a capacity or ammonia-balance breach.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Sweep Order

Several operations in this service mutate many rows. Consistency is **immediate**: by the time the response is written, every downstream row the cascade touches has already been moved, its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. There is no deferred queue and no eventual reconciliation.

Because cascades sweep sets of candidate rows, the traversal order is specified so that outcomes are reproducible:

- Campaign sweeps driven by a train or plant event process candidates in `window_start` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Order-release sweeps process candidates in the product line's `priority_rank` **descending**, then the order's `created_at` ascending. A lower `priority_rank` denotes a more important product, so ordering descending means the least important product's reservation is unwound first and the most important product's reservation is released last. The movement-cancellation sweep each individual release then drives processes that order's `DRAFT` and `BOOKED` movements in `created_at` ascending.
- Quote-withdrawal sweeps driven by a posture change process candidates in `created_at` ascending. The quote-expiry checkpoint processes candidates in `expires_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Successor-task release processes candidates in `created_at` ascending.

Two invariants hold across every cascade and are worth checking any implementation against. **Ammonia conservation**: the sum of a campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons` and the `allocated_ammonia_tons` of its `RESERVED` and `CONSUMED` orders always equals the campaign's current ammonia pool basis, so releasing a reservation restores exactly the tonnage it withdrew. **Gas-volume conservation**: for every hedge lot, `consumed_mmbtu` plus `remaining_mmbtu` always equals `mmbtu_volume`, so reversing a settlement returns the hedge book to precisely the state it held beforehand.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning the Site: Plants, Trains and Catalyst Charges

A `RegulatoryAdmin` brings a site into the system by registering it with a human-readable `name`, an operator `site_code`, the air-permit ceiling `permit_limit_tons_30d`, and the opening natural-gas index `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu`. The permit ceiling is expressed in tons and must be strictly positive; it is applied **independently to each pollutant's** rolling 30-day mass rather than to their combined total, which is why a plant with one small ceiling can find itself in exceedance on one species while comfortably compliant on the other three. The spot index is strictly positive and carries four decimal places. A newly registered plant opens at `permit_status` `ACTIVE`, `psm_program_status` `CURRENT`, `operating_posture` derived to `NORMAL` and `exceeded_pollutants` as an empty list; its `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu` is null because no hedge lots exist yet, its `gas_index_updated_at` is stamped from the logical clock at registration and so opens equal to `created_at`, and its `last_vessel_inspection_at` is null because no outage has yet covered vessel inspection.

Publishing a fresh gas index is a separate regulatory act. It overwrites `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu` and stamps `gas_index_updated_at` from the logical clock, and it advances the plant's `version` by 1. It deliberately does **not** reprice anything already settled: an `ISSUED` quote keeps the price it was issued at, and a `COMPLETED` campaign keeps the unit cost it settled at. Gas moves change the future, never the past.

Reformer trains are commissioned by a `ProcessEngineer` with a payload carrying the owning `plant_id`, the operator designation `train_code`, and the nameplate ammonia output `rated_ammonia_tpd` in tons per day. A new train opens at `status` `IDLE` with `active_charge_id` null. A train's `status` moves among four values — `IDLE`, `RUNNING`, `DERATED` and `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND` — and every one of those moves is a consequence of something happening to a campaign, a catalyst charge or an outage; there is no endpoint that sets a train's status directly. The `active_charge_id` field is a **mirror only**: it holds the identifier of the single catalyst charge on that train whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or null when the train has none, and it is re-derived whenever the train is read. Nothing in this specification ever makes a decision from `active_charge_id` alone — gates always read the charge itself.

Loading a catalyst charge is likewise an engineer's act, taking the `train_id`, the designation `charge_code`, the vendor `design_life_hours`, and two optional seeding fields: `baseline_operating_hours`, the carry-over meter reading already on the charge, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative, and `poisoning_index`, the latest laboratory sulphur and chloride figure, which defaults to 0.00 and must lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. Both `design_life_hours` and `baseline_operating_hours` carry one decimal place. The seeding fields exist so that a charge can be positioned anywhere on its decay curve at registration without having to run campaigns to age it. A new charge opens with `regeneration_count` at 0, `installed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `status` `ACTIVE`; its `cumulative_operating_hours`, `calendar_activity_pct` and `activity_pct` are computed immediately. Installing a charge is also how a charge is swapped outside an outage: **any other charge on the same train whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions to `RETIRED`** as a side effect, and that retired charge's own `version` and the train's `version` each advance by 1. A charge's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SPENT` or `RETIRED`, and `RETIRED` is terminal — a retired charge never returns to service.

### 3.2. Catalyst Activity, Regeneration and the Derate Cascade

Everything the plant is allowed to do on a train ultimately answers to one number: the severity-adjusted activity of that train's `ACTIVE` charge. Two activity figures are maintained on every charge and they are **not** interchangeable.

Both start from the same accumulator. A charge's `cumulative_operating_hours` is its `baseline_operating_hours` plus the summed `actual_operating_hours` of every campaign whose pinned charge is this one and whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. Campaigns in any other status contribute nothing — an `ABORTED` campaign and a `RUNNING` campaign both count as zero hours — so aborting a campaign hands its operating hours back to the catalyst, and so does reopening a settled one.

The first figure, `calendar_activity_pct`, is the straight-line life fraction and ignores both poisoning and regeneration entirely. It is 100 minus the quantity 100 multiplied by the ratio of `cumulative_operating_hours` to `design_life_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because it only ever falls, no clamp is needed. It is reported for asset-life planning and outage scheduling and is never compared against any gate.

The second figure, `activity_pct`, is the severity-adjusted variant, and it is the **only** value any gate reads. Its derivation inflates the hour meter for poisoning and then credits completed regenerations. First, severity-adjusted hours are `cumulative_operating_hours` multiplied by the quantity one plus 0.45 times the charge's `poisoning_index` — so a charge at a poisoning index of 1.00 is treated as though it had run 45 percent longer than it actually did. Then `activity_pct` is 100, minus the quantity 100 multiplied by the ratio of those severity-adjusted hours to `design_life_hours`, plus 12.00 points for each completed regeneration recorded in `regeneration_count`. That whole expression is rounded half up to two decimal places, and **the rounded result is then clamped into the range 0.00 to 100.00 inclusive** — the clamp is applied after the rounding, not before.

Because the two variants weight the same data differently, they diverge in both directions on identical inputs: heavy poisoning pushes the severity-adjusted figure below the calendar figure, while a regeneration pushes it above. A charge can therefore be perfectly eligible to gate a campaign, with `activity_pct` at or above 55.00, while its `calendar_activity_pct` sits below 55.00 — and the reverse is equally possible. Only the severity-adjusted figure decides.

The minimum activity for service is **55.00**. Two events can recompute activity and discover the charge has fallen through it: settling a campaign, which accrues fresh operating hours onto the pinned charge, and recording a revised laboratory poisoning figure. The latter is an engineer's write carrying a new `poisoning_index`; it recomputes `activity_pct` and leaves `calendar_activity_pct` untouched, since the calendar variant is blind to poisoning. That write is not gated on the charge's `status` at all — a `SPENT` or `RETIRED` charge accepts the new laboratory figure and advances its `version` by 1 — but only a charge still sitting `ACTIVE` can fall through the minimum and set the cascade below in motion. A revision that *lowers* the poisoning index never restores a `SPENT` charge either: the only two ways back are the regeneration arm and a campaign reopen.

When a recomputed `activity_pct` lands strictly below 55.00, a four-hop **derate cascade** runs in a single request:

1. The hour meter or poisoning index moves and `activity_pct` is recomputed.
2. Because the recomputed figure is under 55.00, the charge transitions `ACTIVE → SPENT`, its own `version` advances by 1, and the train's `active_charge_id` is cleared to null.
3. Because the charge became `SPENT`, its reformer train transitions to `DERATED` — from `RUNNING` when a settlement drove the cascade, or from `IDLE` when a poisoning revision did — and the train's `version` advances by 1. A train already sitting `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND` is the one exception, because an outage owns that train's status outright: hops 3 and 4 are both skipped for it, so the charge still transitions `ACTIVE → SPENT` and still advances its own `version`, while the train's `status` and `version` are left exactly as they stand and no `APPROVED` campaign on that train is reverted.
4. Because the train became `DERATED`, every campaign on that train whose `status` is `APPROVED` reverts `APPROVED → PLANNED` with `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` cleared to null, processed in `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, each reverted campaign's `version` advancing by 1.

Hop 4 is the one implementers most often miss. Losing a catalyst does not merely stop the campaign that was running; it withdraws the approval — and therefore the provisional cost — of every other campaign already booked on that train.

Regeneration is the way back. An in-situ regeneration is an engineer's action with no request body, and it behaves differently depending on where the charge stands. On a charge that is already `ACTIVE`, it is a self-transition `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` permitted only while `regeneration_count` is strictly below 3; it increments `regeneration_count` by 1, recomputes `activity_pct` and `calendar_activity_pct`, and advances the charge's `version` by 1. A fourth regeneration attempt on an `ACTIVE` charge is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On a charge that has gone `SPENT`, regeneration is the recovery arm `SPENT → ACTIVE`, permitted when the recomputed `activity_pct` reaches 55.00 or above once `regeneration_count` has been incremented, and only when no other charge on the same `train_id` is already `ACTIVE`. The three-regeneration ceiling constrains the `ACTIVE` self-transition only; the recovery arm carries no such cap, so a charge that has already been regenerated three times may still be bought back into service by a fourth. When either recovery condition fails the speculative increment is discarded — `regeneration_count` is left exactly as it stood — and the request answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Recovery restores the train's `active_charge_id` to this charge, moves the train `DERATED → IDLE` when the train has no campaign running, and advances both the charge's and the train's `version` by 1. Reopening a settled campaign can also drive `SPENT → ACTIVE`, by subtracting that campaign's `actual_operating_hours` back out of `cumulative_operating_hours`; when the reopened campaign resumes, the train moves `DERATED → RUNNING` instead of `DERATED → IDLE`. That arm carries the same no-other-`ACTIVE`-charge condition the regeneration arm does, so a `SPENT` charge whose train has meanwhile been loaded with a fresh `ACTIVE` charge stays `SPENT` however healthy the recomputed figure is, and the train's status is then left alone as well.

Finally, a train sitting `DERATED` also returns to `IDLE` when an outage completes and leaves it with an `ACTIVE` charge whose `activity_pct` is at least 55.00.

### 3.3. The Gas Hedge Book and the Feedstock Blend

A `CommercialManager` books hedged gas as a lot against a specific plant, supplying the `plant_id`, the trade ticket `lot_code`, the transacted `mmbtu_volume`, the fixed `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu`, and a `delivery_month` written as exactly a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month from 01 to 12. Volume and strike are both strictly positive. A new lot opens with `consumed_mmbtu` at 0, `remaining_mmbtu` equal to its `mmbtu_volume`, and `status` `OPEN`. Booking a lot immediately moves the plant's `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu`, which is derived on every read rather than stored, so the new blend is visible on the very next fetch of the plant; booking is the one blend-moving event that does **not** advance the plant's own `version`.

That blend is the **volume-weighted average strike of the plant's live hedge position**, and its eligibility rule matters. The pool consists of the lots on that plant whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `remaining_mmbtu` is strictly greater than zero. If the pool is empty, `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu` is null and every caller that wants a hedged basis falls back to the plant's `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu` instead. Otherwise the blend is the sum, across the pool, of each lot's `remaining_mmbtu` multiplied by its `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu`, divided by the sum of those same `remaining_mmbtu` figures, rounded half up to four decimal places. Note the deliberate consequence of testing `remaining_mmbtu` as well as `status`: a lot drained to zero by a settlement stops influencing the blend within the very same request that drained it, before any status flip is even considered. `CLOSED` lots are likewise never in the pool. The blend is recomputed on every read of the plant, on every lot creation, and after any settlement that draws hedged gas.

A lot's `status` is derived from what is left in it. It is `OPEN` while `remaining_mmbtu` is strictly positive and `CLOSED` once `remaining_mmbtu` reaches exactly zero, where `remaining_mmbtu` is always `mmbtu_volume` less `consumed_mmbtu`. The `OPEN → CLOSED` transition happens when a campaign settlement's waterfall draws the lot to nothing: `consumed_mmbtu` and `remaining_mmbtu` are written, the plant's blend is recomputed, and both the lot's and the plant's `version` advance by 1. The reverse transition `CLOSED → OPEN` is the audit-reversal arm: reopening a settled campaign decrements each drawn lot's `consumed_mmbtu` by exactly the volume that campaign had drawn from it, and any lot whose `remaining_mmbtu` becomes strictly positive again returns to `OPEN`, with the plant's blend recomputed and the same two `version` counters advanced. A lot's identity never changes across this cycle, so a fetch of a lot that cannot be resolved answers `ERR_HEDGE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.4. Ammonia Campaigns: Schedule, Approve, Run, Settle, Restate

A campaign is the unit of ammonia production. A `ProcessEngineer` schedules one against a `train_id` with a campaign `run_code`, a `window_start`, a `window_end`, and a planned output `target_ammonia_tons`. Both window endpoints are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`. **The campaign window is half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive, `window_end` is exclusive. Two campaigns on the same train may not hold overlapping half-open windows while both sit in `PLANNED`, `APPROVED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`; windows that merely touch, where one ends at exactly the instant the next begins, do not overlap and are accepted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Scheduling also refuses a train that is `DERATED` or `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`, with `ERR_TRAIN_UNAVAILABLE`, and refuses outright while the governing plant is `FROZEN`. A new campaign opens at `status` `PLANNED` with `consumed_gas_mmbtu` at 0, `unallocated_ammonia_tons` at 0, `catalyst_charge_id` null, `actual_ammonia_tons`, `actual_operating_hours`, `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, `abort_reason` and `reopen_reason` all null, and `created_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`.

Approval moves the campaign `PLANNED → APPROVED` and is where the **provisional** feedstock estimate is struck. Five conditions must hold: the governing plant's posture is not `FROZEN`; the train's `status` is `IDLE` or `RUNNING`; the train has a charge whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; that charge's `activity_pct` is at least 55.00 — those last two are one and the same rung, so a train carrying no `ACTIVE` charge at all is refused with `ERR_CATALYST_ACTIVITY_TOO_LOW` exactly as an under-strength charge is, at approval and at start alike; and no other campaign on the same train in `APPROVED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` has a half-open window intersecting this one. Approval also requires a second signature — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the campaign's `created_by_user_id`. On success, `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` is written and the campaign's `version` advances by 1.

Starting the campaign moves it `APPROVED → RUNNING` and requires that the logical clock has reached or passed `window_start` — a clock still short of that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — that the plant is not `FROZEN`, that the train is `IDLE` or `RUNNING`, and that the train has an `ACTIVE` charge at `activity_pct` of at least 55.00. Starting **pins** `catalyst_charge_id` to that `ACTIVE` charge and never rewrites it afterwards, not even across a reopen — the charge that wore during the campaign is a matter of historical record. The train moves `IDLE → RUNNING`, and both the campaign's and the train's `version` advance by 1 — a train already sitting `RUNNING` keeps that status while its `version` still advances.

#### Feedstock unit cost: one waterfall, two variants

Both the provisional and the final ammonia unit cost run the same shape of calculation, and both consume gas at an intensity of **33.0 MMBtu per ton of ammonia** and add a fixed conversion charge of **42.50 US dollars per ton of ammonia**. The requirement in MMBtu is the ammonia tonnage multiplied by 33.0. The waterfall then walks the variant's eligible hedge lots in the variant's declared order: at each lot it draws the lesser of that lot's `remaining_mmbtu` and the volume still needed, accrues the drawn volume multiplied by that lot's `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu` into a running gas cost, reduces the outstanding need by the draw, and stops as soon as the need reaches zero. Any need still outstanding after the eligible lots are exhausted is bought at the plant's `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu`. When no lot is eligible at all, the entire requirement is bought at the spot index. The unit cost is then the running gas cost divided by the ammonia tonnage, plus 42.50, and **that sum is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once**. The running gas cost is never rounded, no per-lot subtotal is rounded, and the gas-cost-per-ton quotient is carried at full precision until the single final rounding.

The two variants disagree on identical data in three independent ways, so the final figure is not merely the provisional one recomputed with a different tonnage.

- The **provisional** variant, struck at approval and written to `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, uses `target_ammonia_tons` as the ammonia tonnage. Its eligible lots are the plant's `OPEN` lots with `remaining_mmbtu` strictly positive **and** `delivery_month` equal to the UTC year-and-month of the campaign's `window_start` — an exact single-month match, so only gas contracted for the month the campaign starts in counts. It walks them **cheapest first**: `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. It mutates **nothing**: no lot's `remaining_mmbtu` or `consumed_mmbtu` moves, which is precisely why re-approving a campaign after a revert yields the same number it did the first time.
- The **final** variant, struck at settlement and written to `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, uses the `actual_ammonia_tons` supplied in the settlement request. Its eligible lots are the plant's `OPEN` lots with `remaining_mmbtu` strictly positive **and** `delivery_month` at or before the UTC year-and-month of the campaign's `window_end`. There is no lower bound on the month: gas contracted for any earlier month is still physically in the tank and settles first. It walks them **oldest contracted first**: `delivery_month` ascending, then `strike_price_usd_per_mmbtu` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — not cheapest first. And the draw is destructive: every lot drawn has its `consumed_mmbtu` increased by its draw so `remaining_mmbtu` falls, any lot reaching zero remaining flips to `CLOSED`, and the campaign's `consumed_gas_mmbtu` records the total hedged draw.

#### Settlement

Settling a campaign moves it `RUNNING → COMPLETED` and carries two required inputs: `actual_ammonia_tons`, the ammonia actually made, and `actual_operating_hours`, the reformer hours actually logged. Both must be strictly positive; tonnage carries three decimal places and hours one. The clock must have reached or passed `window_start`, a shortfall answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Settlement is not one of the writes the posture gate closes, so a campaign settles even while its governing plant sits at `FROZEN`. And critically, `actual_ammonia_tons` may not fall below the summed `allocated_ammonia_tons` of that campaign's orders sitting in `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED` — settling below what has already been promised is refused with `ERR_AMMONIA_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`.

A successful settlement does a great deal in one request. It writes `actual_ammonia_tons` and `actual_operating_hours`. It runs the final-variant waterfall, writing `consumed_gas_mmbtu`, incrementing each drawn lot's `consumed_mmbtu`, closing any lot drawn to nothing, and recomputing the plant's `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu`. It writes `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`. It switches the campaign's ammonia pool basis from `target_ammonia_tons` to `actual_ammonia_tons` and recomputes `unallocated_ammonia_tons` against the orders already reserved and consumed. It accrues `actual_operating_hours` onto the pinned charge's `cumulative_operating_hours` and recomputes both activity figures, which may launch the whole derate cascade of §3.2. And it returns the train `RUNNING → IDLE` when the charge is still `ACTIVE`, or `RUNNING → DERATED` when the charge has just gone `SPENT`. The campaign's `version` advances by 1, along with the `version` of every drawn lot, the charge, the train, the plant and every campaign the derate cascade reverted.

#### The ammonia pool and its shifting basis

`unallocated_ammonia_tons` is the campaign's ammonia pool basis less the summed `allocated_ammonia_tons` of that campaign's orders whose `allocation_status` is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`. **The basis switches at settlement**: it is `actual_ammonia_tons` once the campaign's `status` is `COMPLETED`, and `target_ammonia_tons` in every other status but one. The one exception is `PLANNED`: a campaign that has not been approved yet has no pool at all, so its `unallocated_ammonia_tons` reads exactly 0.000 whatever its `target_ammonia_tons` and whatever tonnage is already promised against it. The pool comes into existence at approval, and a campaign a cascade reverts `APPROVED → PLANNED` collapses back to 0.000 while the reservations already booked against it stay in place — so the ammonia-conservation identity of §2.12 holds from `APPROVED` onward and not in `PLANNED`. Reservations may therefore be booked forward against a campaign that has not run yet, and settlement can reveal that the campaign under-produced — which is exactly the situation the settlement floor above exists to prevent from silently over-committing the plant.

#### Aborting and reopening

Aborting is terminal. A campaign in `APPROVED` aborts with no preconditions at all: `abort_reason` is written from the request body, `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` is cleared to null, and the `version` advances by 1. A campaign in `RUNNING` may abort only while it has drawn no hedged gas, which a `RUNNING` campaign never has; that arm leaves `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` exactly as it stands, since only the `APPROVED` arm withdraws the provisional price, and on abort it writes `abort_reason`, releases every order against it that sits in `RESERVED` to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `RUN_ABORTED` — processed in the product line's `priority_rank` descending then the order's `created_at` ascending — cancels every `DRAFT` or `BOOKED` movement on those released orders, recomputes `reserved_tons` and `available_tons` on every affected origin asset, and returns the train `RUNNING → IDLE` unless an outage cascade has already set it to `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`. When the outage cascade rather than an operator drives the abort, `abort_reason` is written as `TRAIN_DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND` instead of a request-body value. `ABORTED` is a terminal status; nothing leaves it.

Reopening is the restatement loop and moves a campaign `COMPLETED → RUNNING`, carrying a `reopen_reason`. It is refused when any order on the campaign has `allocation_status` `CONSUMED`, because a produced order's landed cost is priced off the final unit cost and cannot be un-priced; that refusal is `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_PRODUCED`. It also requires that the governing plant is not `FROZEN` and that the train is not `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`. A reopen reverses the settlement exactly: every lot the campaign drew has its `consumed_mmbtu` decremented by precisely the volume drawn, `CLOSED` lots whose `remaining_mmbtu` becomes positive return to `OPEN`, `consumed_gas_mmbtu` returns to 0, `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` is cleared to null, and the plant's blend is recomputed. It subtracts `actual_operating_hours` from the pinned charge's `cumulative_operating_hours` and recomputes `activity_pct`, so a `SPENT` charge returns to `ACTIVE` when the recomputed figure reaches 55.00 or more. It releases every order on the campaign sitting in `RESERVED` to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `RUN_REOPENED`, in `priority_rank` descending then `created_at` ascending order. And it clears `actual_ammonia_tons` and `actual_operating_hours` to null, so the ammonia pool basis reverts to `target_ammonia_tons`; with every reservation released, `unallocated_ammonia_tons` returns to the full `target_ammonia_tons`. The train returns to `RUNNING`, and the campaign's `version` advances by 1 along with every restored lot, the charge and the plant; the train's own `version` advances only when the reopen actually moves its `status`, so a train already sitting `RUNNING` is left untouched.

### 3.5. The Product Slate and the Order Book

The product slate is a register of what the complex can make. A `CommercialManager` defines a line with the producing `plant_id`, the finished `product_code` — one of `ANHYDROUS_AMMONIA`, `AMMONIUM_NITRATE`, `UAN_SOLUTION` or `UREA` — the ammonia feed intensity `ammonia_tons_per_product_ton` in tons of ammonia consumed per finished ton, the cash `conversion_cost_usd_per_ton` excluding that ammonia feed, the boolean `regulated` flag marking the product as a regulated substance under the site security plan, and a `priority_rank` between 1 and 99. Both cost inputs are strictly positive and carry three decimal places. A product line is **immutable once created** and, as §2.4 notes, carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Its `priority_rank` is consulted only when reservations are unwound: a lower rank denotes a more important product, and because release sweeps order candidates by `priority_rank` descending then `created_at` ascending, the lowest-ranked line's reservation is released last.

An order is a commercial claim on a campaign's ammonia. A commercial manager creates one with the `run_id` of the campaign whose ammonia it draws, the `product_line_id` of the finished product, the `destination_asset_id` where the finished tons will land, an `order_code`, and `requested_tons`, the tonnage the commercial team wants. A new order opens with `allocation_status` `UNALLOCATED`, `reserved_tons` at 0, `allocated_ammonia_tons` at 0, and `produced_tons`, `landed_cost_usd`, `release_reason` and `security_checked_by_user_id` all null. Its `security_status` is decided at creation from the product line: **`EXEMPT` when the line is not regulated, `PENDING` when it is**. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `created_by_user_id`. Creation additionally requires that the product line's `product_code` matches the destination asset's `product_code`, that the campaign and the destination asset sit at the same plant — either mismatch answers `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH` — and, when the line is regulated, that the destination asset's `security_class` is `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SECURITY_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`. Those three tests are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11 rather than in the order narrated here, so the security-class refusal outranks either product mismatch: a regulated order pointed at a `BASIC` asset that also carries the wrong `product_code` answers `ERR_SECURITY_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`.

An order carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**, and this independence is deliberate. The allocation dimension, `allocation_status`, moves among `UNALLOCATED`, `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED`. The clearance dimension, `security_status`, moves among `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `DENIED` and `EXEMPT`. Neither dimension consults the other as it advances: an order may be `CLEARED` while still `UNALLOCATED`, or `RESERVED` while still `PENDING`. The two only converge at the moment tons are actually produced.

Reserving is how ammonia is claimed. The request carries `reserve_tons` and, as §2.7 requires, `expected_run_version`. It is permitted from `UNALLOCATED` and from `RELEASED` — re-reserving after a release is a normal commercial act and additionally clears `release_reason` back to null. `reserve_tons` is bounded by the campaign's ammonia pool and the destination asset's capacity and by nothing else: the order's own `requested_tons` is a purely advisory record of what the commercial team asked for, so a reservation above `requested_tons` is accepted, and each reservation overwrites `reserved_tons` rather than adding to it. Five conditions hold: the campaign's `status` is `APPROVED`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`; the governing plant is not `FROZEN`; `expected_run_version` equals the campaign's current `version`; the ammonia the reservation needs fits inside the campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons`; and the destination asset can hold the tons — its `on_hand_tons`, plus the summed `reserved_tons` of that asset's other reserved orders, plus `reserve_tons`, may not exceed its `capacity_tons`, which is refused with `ERR_STORAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The ammonia the reservation needs is `reserve_tons` multiplied by the product line's `ammonia_tons_per_product_ton`, rounded half up to three decimal places, and that figure is written to `allocated_ammonia_tons`; exceeding the pool is refused with `ERR_AMMONIA_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`. Clearance is explicitly **not** checked here. On success `reserved_tons` and `allocated_ammonia_tons` are written, the campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons` is reduced by `allocated_ammonia_tons`, and both the campaign's and the order's `version` advance by 1 — the campaign's advance being the same increment the stale-version guard is defending.

Releasing returns a reservation to the pool. It moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`, writes `release_reason`, zeroes both `reserved_tons` and `allocated_ammonia_tons`, and increases the campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons` by exactly the `allocated_ammonia_tons` being given back, which is what keeps the ammonia-conservation invariant of §2.12 exact. It then cancels downstream: every movement whose `production_order_id` is this order and whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `BOOKED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `UPSTREAM_ALLOCATION_RELEASED`, and each affected origin asset has its `reserved_tons` and `available_tons` recomputed — only the origin asset of a cancelled `BOOKED` movement counts as affected, since cancelling a `DRAFT` gives back no tonnage hold. The order's `version` advances by 1, as does the campaign's, and so does that of every cancelled movement and touched asset. An order already at `CONSUMED` cannot be released and answers `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_PRODUCED` rather than the generic state error. Releases also arrive as cascades — from a campaign abort, from a campaign reopen, and from a plant freezing — and in those cases `release_reason` carries the cascade's own value rather than a request-body one.

Clearance is the `SafetyOfficer`'s dimension and is recorded by supplying a `decision`. Supplying `CLEARED` moves `PENDING → CLEARED`; supplying `DENIED` moves `PENDING → DENIED`; supplying `PENDING` moves `DENIED → PENDING`, which is the re-submission loop used after a customer's security paperwork is corrected. All three arms require the order's product line to be regulated — a non-regulated line is `EXEMPT` from creation and never has a `PENDING` state to move out of, and a decision aimed at such an order is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` is written to `security_checked_by_user_id`, which stays null while the order is `PENDING` or `EXEMPT`, and that identifier must differ from the order's `created_by_user_id`. The clearance write leaves `allocation_status` entirely untouched and advances the order's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Storage Assets, Production into Storage, and the Seasonal Set-Aside

A `LogisticsCoordinator` registers each holding asset with its owning `plant_id`, an `asset_code`, an `asset_kind` of `DOME`, `TANK` or `TERMINAL`, the single `product_code` the asset may hold, the nameplate `capacity_tons`, a `security_class` of `BASIC`, `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`, and an optional `seasonal_reserve_pct` that defaults to 0.00 and must lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. A new asset opens with `opening_balance_tons` at 0 and every position figure derived from that.

Because this service does not fabricate production history, the operator states a starting position directly: the opening-balance write sets `opening_balance_tons` to a physically counted figure — exactly 0 is accepted here, a counted-empty asset being a legitimate statement, while a negative figure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — refusing any value above `capacity_tons` with `ERR_STORAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, recomputes the full position, and advances the asset's `version` by 1.

Four position figures are derived on every read of an asset and after every event that could move them. `on_hand_tons` is `opening_balance_tons`, plus the summed `produced_tons` of the orders destined for this asset whose `allocation_status` is `CONSUMED`, plus the summed `received_tons` of the movements into this asset whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, minus the summed `tons` of the movements out of this asset whose `status` is `DELIVERED`. `reserved_tons` is the summed `tons` of the movements out of this asset whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `IN_TRANSIT`. `seasonal_reserve_tons` is `capacity_tons` multiplied by `seasonal_reserve_pct`, rounded half up to three decimal places, **but only while the logical clock's UTC month is December, January or February** — the pre-spring build months — and exactly 0.000 in every other month. `available_tons` is `on_hand_tons` less `reserved_tons` less `seasonal_reserve_tons`, and it is an exact difference that is never re-rounded; it may legitimately go negative when a seasonal set-aside is imposed on an asset whose tonnage is already committed.

Two consequences follow from that definition and both matter. Booked and in-transit outbound tons are **not** deducted from `on_hand_tons`; they sit in `reserved_tons` until delivery. Booking therefore never moves inventory, and cancelling a booking needs no compensating tonnage entry. And transit loss — the difference between the `tons` tendered and the `received_tons` acknowledged — simply leaves the network: the receiving asset gains only what was acknowledged, and nothing anywhere absorbs the shortfall.

Producing is the moment the two order dimensions converge, and it is a `ProcessEngineer`'s act carrying `produced_tons`. It moves the order `RESERVED → CONSUMED`. Six conditions hold, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11 rather than in the order narrated here — so the regulated-routing and clearance refusals outrank the product-code, capacity and reserved-tonnage ones, and `ERR_PRODUCED_EXCEEDS_RESERVED` is the last of the six to be reached: the campaign's `status` is `COMPLETED`, since without a settled `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` the feedstock component of the cost has no input — an order on a running campaign is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_COMPLETED`; `produced_tons` does not exceed the order's `reserved_tons`, refused with `ERR_PRODUCED_EXCEEDS_RESERVED`; `security_status` is `CLEARED` or `EXEMPT`, refused with `ERR_SECURITY_CHECK_NOT_CLEARED`; the destination asset's `product_code` equals the product line's `product_code`; the destination asset has headroom, so `on_hand_tons` plus `produced_tons` does not exceed `capacity_tons`; and when the line is regulated, the destination asset's `security_class` is `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`. On success `produced_tons` and `landed_cost_usd` are written, the asset's `on_hand_tons` grows by `produced_tons` with `reserved_tons`, `seasonal_reserve_tons` and `available_tons` recomputed, and both the order's and the asset's `version` advance by 1.

The **landed cost** is a fully-loaded figure built from four components, and **the rounding order is part of the contract: each of the four components is rounded to two decimal places independently, and the four already-rounded figures are then summed.** Summing at full precision and rounding once at the end is wrong and will differ by a cent or two on realistic tonnages. First, the ammonia embedded in the tons produced is `produced_tons` multiplied by the line's `ammonia_tons_per_product_ton`, rounded half up to three decimal places. The **feedstock** component is that ammonia tonnage multiplied by the campaign's `final_unit_cost_usd_per_ton`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **conversion** component is `produced_tons` multiplied by the line's `conversion_cost_usd_per_ton`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **handling** component is `produced_tons` multiplied by a storage handling charge of 1.333 US dollars per ton, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **security** component is `produced_tons` multiplied by a regulated-substance fee of 2.755 US dollars per ton, rounded half up to two decimal places, when the line is regulated, and exactly 0.00 when it is not. `landed_cost_usd` is the exact sum of those four rounded values and is never re-rounded.

### 3.7. Moving Product by Rail, Barge and Truck

A movement is drafted by a `LogisticsCoordinator` between two storage assets. The payload carries the `origin_asset_id`, the `dest_asset_id`, an optional `production_order_id`, a `shipment_code`, a `mode` of `BARGE`, `RAIL` or `TRUCK`, the `tons` tendered, the tariff `distance_miles` — which carries one decimal place — and a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the asset occupation window. Tonnage and mileage are both strictly positive and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; as with campaigns, **the occupation window is half-open** — `window_start` inclusive, `window_end` exclusive. The `production_order_id` is null for an inter-terminal repositioning move that carries no order; when it is supplied, the `origin_asset_id` must equal that order's `destination_asset_id`. Drafting also requires the origin and destination assets to carry the same `product_code`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH`. A new movement opens at `status` `DRAFT`: no freight is priced and no tonnage is committed. Its `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `BOOKED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Booking is where the movement becomes real, and it is the most heavily guarded write in the logistics domain. It moves the movement `DRAFT → BOOKED` and carries only `expected_origin_asset_version`. Six conditions hold beyond the version guard: the plant behind the origin asset is not `FROZEN`; the origin and destination assets carry the same `product_code`; when `production_order_id` is non-null, `origin_asset_id` equals that order's `destination_asset_id` and that order's `allocation_status` is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`; when the product line whose `product_code` equals the origin asset's `product_code` is regulated, **both** the origin and the destination asset's `security_class` are `CONTROLLED` or `HIGH_SECURITY`; the origin asset's aggregate outbound commitment stays inside its uncommitted position; and the destination asset has inbound headroom. Those six are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11 rather than in the order narrated here, so the security-class refusal outranks the product-mismatch one and the destination-capacity refusal outranks the origin-availability one. A movement whose named order has since been released, or was never reserved at all, fails the allocation-status condition at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Those last two conditions are worth spelling out. The origin-side rule is an **aggregate overlap check**, not a pairwise one: for every instant covered by this movement's half-open window, the summed `tons` of the origin asset's movements in `BOOKED` or `IN_TRANSIT` whose own half-open windows cover that same instant, plus this movement's `tons`, must not exceed the origin asset's `on_hand_tons` less its `seasonal_reserve_tons`. Several bookings may therefore legitimately share one window as long as their combined tonnage fits, which is exactly why the version guard of §2.7 is needed to make the outcome deterministic. A breach answers `ERR_STORAGE_AVAILABILITY_EXCEEDED`. The destination-side rule is simpler: the destination asset's `on_hand_tons`, plus the summed `tons` of its inbound movements in `BOOKED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, plus this movement's `tons`, must not exceed its `capacity_tons`, and a breach answers `ERR_STORAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

On a successful booking the freight is priced, the origin asset's `reserved_tons` and `available_tons` are recomputed, the origin asset's `version` advances by 1 and the movement's `version` advances by 1.

**Freight pricing** depends on nothing but the movement's own `mode`, `tons` and `distance_miles` — no hedge, plant or storage state enters it. Each mode carries a ton-mile rate and a minimum charge: rail is priced at 0.0412 US dollars per ton-mile with a minimum charge of 2200.00 dollars; barge at 0.0189 per ton-mile with a minimum of 4800.00; truck at 0.1075 per ton-mile with a minimum of 450.00. The linehaul is `tons` multiplied by `distance_miles` multiplied by the mode's rate, and `base_freight_usd` is the greater of that linehaul and the mode's minimum charge, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a short, light move is priced off the minimum rather than off the ton-mile rate.

The fuel surcharge is then selected from a three-band tariff, and **the band is chosen from the pre-surcharge figure**. When `base_freight_usd` is below 25000.00 the surcharge rate is 8.5 percent; when it is at or above 25000.00 but below 100000.00 the rate is 6.2 percent; when it is at or above 100000.00 the rate is 4.1 percent. The band boundaries are half-open in the sense that a base freight of exactly 25000.00 falls in the middle band and exactly 100000.00 falls in the top band. `fuel_surcharge_usd` is `base_freight_usd` multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up to two decimal places. There is exactly **one** band lookup per booking and it never iterates: adding the surcharge can push the running total across a band boundary, and that does not re-select the band. `freight_total_usd` is the exact sum of the two already-rounded figures, `base_freight_usd` and `fuel_surcharge_usd`, and `freight_per_ton_usd` is `freight_total_usd` divided by `tons`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Departure moves the movement `BOOKED → IN_TRANSIT` and requires only that the logical clock has reached or passed `window_start`, a clock still short of that instant being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The tonnage stays in the origin asset's `reserved_tons`; no inventory moves yet, and the movement's `version` advances by 1.

Delivery is the `TerminalOperator`'s only write and moves the movement `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`. It carries `received_tons`, the tons the receiving terminal actually acknowledges, which must be strictly positive and no greater than the `tons` tendered. Because that ceiling is a comparison against stored state rather than a bound the body schema can carry, a `received_tons` above the tendered `tons` is still reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) but is only reached once the ownership check of rung 7 and the `IN_TRANSIT` status check of rung 9 have both passed. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal `dest_asset_id`, and the destination asset's `on_hand_tons` plus `received_tons` must not exceed its `capacity_tons`. On success `received_tons` is written, the origin asset's `on_hand_tons` falls by the full `tons` tendered while the destination asset's `on_hand_tons` rises by `received_tons`, both assets have `reserved_tons`, `seasonal_reserve_tons` and `available_tons` recomputed, and the movement's `version` plus both assets' `version` counters advance by 1.

Cancellation is available from `DRAFT` and from `BOOKED` with no preconditions, and carries a `cancel_reason`. From `DRAFT` it simply records the reason and advances the movement's `version` by 1. From `BOOKED` it additionally recomputes the origin asset's `reserved_tons` and `available_tons` and advances that asset's `version` by 1, since the tonnage hold is being given back. Cancellation also arrives as a cascade when an upstream order releases its reservation, in which case `cancel_reason` is written as `UPSTREAM_ALLOCATION_RELEASED` rather than taken from a request body.

### 3.8. Customer Quoting on a Hedged or Spot Basis

A quote is drafted by a `CommercialManager` against a supplying `plant_id` and a `product_line_id`, with a `quote_code`, the `tons` the customer is asking for, a `pass_through_basis` of either `HEDGED_BLEND` or `SPOT_INDEX`, and an `expires_at` instant after which the quote may no longer be accepted. The product line must belong to the quote's plant's slate, else `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH`, and `expires_at` must be after the quote's `created_at`. A new quote opens at `status` `DRAFT` with no price computed. Its `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED`; `EXPIRED` is terminal.

Issuing moves the quote `DRAFT → ISSUED` and requires that the plant's posture is not `FROZEN` and that the logical clock is strictly before `expires_at` — issuing at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Of those two the expiry test is the earlier rung — §2.11 places `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` at rung 9 and `ERR_PLANT_FROZEN` at rung 10 — so an expired quote on a frozen plant answers `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Issue snapshots `gas_basis_usd_per_mmbtu`, writes `quoted_price_usd_per_ton` and `issued_at` from the logical clock, and advances the quote's `version` by 1. Nothing is re-derived afterwards: a later gas move does not reprice an `ISSUED` quote.

The price is built in four steps. **Step one** snapshots the gas basis. When `pass_through_basis` is `HEDGED_BLEND` the basis is the plant's `hedged_blend_usd_per_mmbtu`, falling back to `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu` when the hedge pool is empty and the blend is therefore null; when it is `SPOT_INDEX` the basis is `spot_gas_index_usd_per_mmbtu` outright. That basis is written to `gas_basis_usd_per_mmbtu` and is used at its stored four-decimal precision. **Step two** builds the cash cost. The ammonia cash cost per ton is the gas basis multiplied by 33.0 MMBtu per ammonia ton, plus 42.50 dollars per ammonia ton — the same intensity and conversion charge the campaign waterfall uses. The feedstock cost per finished ton is that ammonia cash cost multiplied by the line's `ammonia_tons_per_product_ton`. The cash cost per finished ton is that feedstock cost plus the line's `conversion_cost_usd_per_ton`. **Step three** adds margin: the margin per ton is the **greater** of a floor of 25.00 dollars per ton and 22 percent of the cash cost per ton, and the list price per ton is the cash cost plus that margin.

**Step four** applies three independent multipliers, each drawn from a different dimension. The seasonal multiplier is 1.18 when the UTC month of `expires_at` is March, April or May, and 1.00 in every other month. The volume tier is 0.94 when the quote's `tons` is at least 25000, 0.97 when it is at least 5000 but below 25000, and 1.00 below 5000. The posture factor is 1.06 when the plant's `operating_posture` is `RESTRICTED`, and 1.00 otherwise — a restricted plant does not stop quoting, it quotes at a premium. `quoted_price_usd_per_ton` is the list price multiplied by all three factors, rounded half up to two decimal places **once**, at the very end; no intermediate step is rounded, and because full-precision multiplication is order-independent the three factors may be applied in any order.

Withdrawing an issued quote moves it `ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` with no preconditions, writes `withdraw_reason` and advances the `version` by 1. Withdrawal also arrives as a cascade from a plant posture degradation, as §3.10 describes. Revising moves a withdrawn quote `WITHDRAWN → DRAFT`, closing the negotiation loop. Revision optionally overwrites `tons`, `pass_through_basis` and `expires_at` from the request body — each of the three is optional and an omitted field is left as it stands, and a supplied `expires_at` is still tested against the quote's original `created_at` rather than against the current logical clock, so a revision may legitimately install an expiry the clock has already passed, leaving a `DRAFT` that cannot be issued and that the checkpoint below will sweep to `EXPIRED` — and clears `quoted_price_usd_per_ton`, `gas_basis_usd_per_mmbtu`, `issued_at` and `withdraw_reason` all to null. A revised quote is therefore re-priced at the gas basis prevailing when it is re-issued, not the one it originally carried.

Expiry is not automatic. Advancing the logical clock past an `expires_at` does not by itself change any quote's `status`; expiry is realised only by an ops-run checkpoint that a commercial manager invokes. That checkpoint moves every quote in `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock to `EXPIRED`, processing candidates in `expires_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, advancing each moved quote's `version` by 1, and answering with `expired_count` and `expired_quote_ids` — the ids listed in exactly the order they were processed. A quote whose id cannot be resolved answers `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.9. Turnaround Planning, Safety Review and the Outage Cascade

A turnaround is a multi-week outage on one reformer train, and it is the most procedurally guarded object in the domain: authored by one person, reviewed by a second, approved by a third, and executed only after all three signatures are in place.

A `ProcessEngineer` authors one with the `plant_id` whose outage crew and cranes are consumed, the `train_id` being taken down, a `turnaround_code`, a `scope` of `CATALYST_CHANGE`, `VESSEL_INSPECTION` or `COMBINED`, and a `window_start` and `window_end`. **Unlike campaign and movement windows, the outage window is closed**: both endpoints belong to the outage. `window_end` must still be strictly after `window_start`. The `plant_id` and the `train_id` are recorded independently and are never cross-checked against one another — the outage calendar of rung 20 is scoped to the `plant_id` while the down-train cascade follows the `train_id`, and an outage naming a train that belongs to some other plant is accepted. From those two instants the server derives `window_days` as the whole days from `window_start` to `window_end`, computed as their difference in seconds divided by 86400 and truncated toward zero, and it is at least 1. Beware the closed-interval arithmetic: an outage from the first of October through the twenty-seventh of October spans 27 calendar dates but only 26 whole elapsed days, and it is the 26 figure that every feasibility comparison uses. A new outage opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `psm_review_decision` `NONE`, `critical_path_days` at 0, and both `tasks_total_count` and `tasks_done_count` at 0; the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `authored_by_user_id`. Its `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `COMPLETED`, of which `COMPLETED` is terminal.

Work steps are added to an outage that is still `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`. Each step carries the `turnaround_id`, an optional `predecessor_task_id` naming the step that must finish first, a `task_code`, and a `duration_days` that must be at least 1 and integral. A new step opens at `status` `PENDING`; a step's `status` moves among `PENDING`, `READY`, `DONE` and `BLOCKED`. Because each step has at most one predecessor, the dependency structure is a forest rather than a general graph. The proposed `predecessor_task_id` must belong to the **same** outage and must not close a cycle — following the predecessor chain from the proposed step must never return to the proposed step — and either violation answers `ERR_TASK_CYCLE_DETECTED`. Cycle detection runs at creation time, before any schedule figure is written. Adding a step increments the outage's `tasks_total_count` by 1, advances the outage's `version` by 1, and re-derives the schedule.

The schedule walks the forest in topological order, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending. A step with a null `predecessor_task_id` has `earliest_start_day` of 0; every other step has `earliest_start_day` equal to its predecessor's `earliest_start_day` plus that predecessor's `duration_days`. The outage's `critical_path_days` is the greatest value, across all its steps, of that step's `earliest_start_day` plus its own `duration_days`, and is 0 when the outage has no steps at all. Two properties of that definition are load-bearing: **every step counts regardless of its `status`**, so `BLOCKED` and `DONE` steps still occupy their slot and rejecting an outage does not shorten it; and disconnected roots each start at day 0 with their chains scheduled independently, so the critical path is the longest single chain, never the sum of all durations. The figures are re-derived on step creation and on every read of a step or of its outage.

Submission moves the outage `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires at least one step — an outage carrying none at all is refused at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and a `critical_path_days` no greater than `window_days`; an infeasible window is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`. Because steps may still be added while the outage is `SUBMITTED`, and adding one re-derives `critical_path_days`, the same feasibility test must run again at approval.

The pre-startup safety review is a `SafetyOfficer`'s write carrying a `decision` of `ENDORSED` or `OBJECTED` together with a `findings_count`. It writes `psm_review_decision` from the decision, `psm_findings_count` from the count — which must not be negative — and `psm_reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. **The review is a field write, not a state transition**: the outage stays at `SUBMITTED`. Its `version` advances by 1.

From `SUBMITTED` the outage forks into two arms whose side-effect sets are entirely disjoint.

The **approve** arm moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and demands four things, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11 rather than in the order narrated here — so the segregation-of-duties refusal at rung 12 outranks the missing-review one at rung 15: `psm_review_decision` is `ENDORSED`, else `ERR_PSM_REVIEW_MISSING`; the acting `X-User-ID` differs from both `authored_by_user_id` and `psm_reviewed_by_user_id`; no other outage on the same `plant_id` in `APPROVED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` has a closed window intersecting this one, and every such neighbouring outage is separated from this window by at least **14 whole days** — the separation being the whole days, truncated toward zero, between the earlier window's `window_end` and the later window's `window_start`, measured exactly the way `window_days` is — else `ERR_TURNAROUND_BUFFER_CONFLICT`; and `critical_path_days` is no greater than `window_days`. On success `approved_by_user_id` is written from the acting `X-User-ID`, and both the outage's and the plant's `version` advance by 1. Approval blocks no tasks, voids no review and touches no train.

The **reject** arm moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, requires only that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `authored_by_user_id`, and does three things the approve arm does not: it writes `rejection_reason` from the request body; it sets `psm_review_decision` to `VOID` and clears `psm_findings_count` to null, so the safety review must be signed again from scratch; and it sets every step of that outage whose `status` is `PENDING` or `READY` to `BLOCKED`. The outage's `version` advances by 1 along with the `version` of every blocked step.

Revision closes the loop `REJECTED → DRAFT` and, uniquely in this service, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to **equal** `authored_by_user_id`. It clears `rejection_reason` to null, resets `psm_review_decision` to `NONE` with `psm_reviewed_by_user_id` cleared to null, and returns every `BLOCKED` step of that outage to `PENDING`, advancing the outage's `version` and every unblocked step's `version` by 1.

#### The outage cascade

Starting an approved outage moves it `APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS` and is the deepest cascade in the domain. It requires that the logical clock has reached or passed `window_start` — a clock still short of that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and that `psm_review_decision` is `ENDORSED`. In one request, five hops run in order:

1. The reformer train transitions to `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`, from `IDLE`, `RUNNING` or `DERATED` — whichever it held.
2. Because the train went down, every campaign on that train whose `status` is `RUNNING` transitions to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `TRAIN_DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND`, and every campaign on that train whose `status` is `APPROVED` reverts to `PLANNED` with `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` cleared to null. Both sweeps process candidates in `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order.
3. Because those campaigns left `RUNNING`, every order on an aborted campaign whose `allocation_status` is `RESERVED` transitions to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `RUN_ABORTED`, restoring that campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons`. The sweep orders candidates by the product line's `priority_rank` descending then the order's `created_at` ascending.
4. Because those orders released, every movement whose `production_order_id` is one of them and whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `BOOKED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `UPSTREAM_ALLOCATION_RELEASED`.
5. Because those movements left `BOOKED`, `reserved_tons` and `available_tons` are recomputed on every affected origin storage asset.

Alongside those five hops, every start step of the outage — every step whose `predecessor_task_id` is null and whose `status` is `PENDING` — transitions to `READY`. The outage's `version` advances by 1, as does that of every mutated train, campaign, order, movement, storage asset and step.

Executing the outage is then a matter of completing steps. Completing a step moves it `READY → DONE` and requires that the parent outage's `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` and that the step's `predecessor_task_id` is null or that predecessor is `DONE`; a step whose predecessor is unfinished is refused with `ERR_TASK_PREDECESSOR_NOT_DONE`, and as §2.11 notes that is also how a still-`PENDING` step inside an in-progress outage surfaces. Completion increments the outage's `tasks_done_count` by 1 and releases the step's successors: every step whose `predecessor_task_id` is this step and whose `status` is `PENDING` transitions to `READY`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The step's `version`, the outage's `version`, and the `version` of every released successor each advance by 1. A step identifier that cannot be resolved answers `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`.

Closing out the outage moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and requires that `tasks_done_count` equals `tasks_total_count` — an outage still carrying an unfinished step is refused at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and that `psm_review_decision` is still `ENDORSED`. The scope decides what closing out actually does, and the two scope branches compose when the scope is `COMBINED`:

- When the `scope` is `CATALYST_CHANGE` or `COMBINED`, the request must carry `new_charge_code` and `new_charge_design_life_hours`, and `new_charge_code` must be unique on that train. The train's current charge is retired — `ACTIVE → RETIRED` or `SPENT → RETIRED`, both arms behaving identically; the charge retired is the train's `ACTIVE` one where it has one and otherwise its earliest-created `SPENT` charge, and a train carrying neither simply takes the replacement with nothing retired — and a replacement charge is created on the same train with `charge_code` from `new_charge_code`, `design_life_hours` from `new_charge_design_life_hours`, `baseline_operating_hours` of 0, `poisoning_index` of 0.00, `regeneration_count` of 0, `installed_at` from the logical clock and `status` `ACTIVE`. The train's `active_charge_id` is set to the replacement charge's identifier.
- When the `scope` is `VESSEL_INSPECTION` or `COMBINED`, the plant's `last_vessel_inspection_at` is written from the logical clock.

Either way, closing out sets the plant's `psm_program_status` to `CURRENT` and re-derives the plant's `operating_posture` — which is how a plant restricted purely for an overdue safety program returns to `NORMAL`. The train then returns `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND → IDLE` when it ends the outage with an `ACTIVE` charge at `activity_pct` of at least 55.00, and `DOWN_FOR_TURNAROUND → DERATED` when it does not — which is exactly what a vessel-inspection-only outage leaves behind if it walked into the outage with a `SPENT` charge and never replaced it. The outage's `version`, the train's `version` and the plant's `version` each advance by 1, as does the `version` of the retired charge when the scope swapped the catalyst; the replacement charge is minted at `version` 1, and a `VESSEL_INSPECTION`-only close-out touches no charge at all.

### 3.10. Emissions Monitoring, Permit Standing and Posture Re-Derivation

Continuous-emissions monitoring is a `RegulatoryAdmin`'s feed. Each reading carries the monitored `plant_id`, the `pollutant` — one of `N2O`, `NH3`, `NOX` or `PM` — the measured mass `rate_lb_per_hour` over the averaging period, the length `interval_hours` of that averaging period, and `reading_at`, the instant the averaging period closed. The rate may be zero but not negative and carries three decimal places; the interval is strictly positive and carries one. A reading whose `reading_at` is after the logical clock is refused with `ERR_EMISSIONS_READING_IN_FUTURE`, so future-dated readings never enter the store at all. Readings are **immutable once written** and, per §2.4, carry only `id` and `created_at`; a reading identifier that cannot be resolved answers `ERR_EMISSIONS_READING_NOT_FOUND`.

Three figures are computed at ingestion. The reading's own `tons` is its `rate_lb_per_hour` multiplied by its `interval_hours`, divided by 2000 pounds per ton, rounded half up to three decimal places. Its `rolling_30d_tons` is the plant-and-pollutant rolling 30-day mass as at this ingestion, the reading being written to the store before the figure is taken so that its own `tons` counts toward the figure it reports. And its `exceedance` is a boolean, true when that rolling figure **strictly exceeds** the plant's `permit_limit_tons_30d` — a rolling mass exactly equal to the limit is compliant, not an exceedance.

**The 30-day window is left-exclusive and right-inclusive**, and that is part of the contract. It runs from the instant exactly 30 days before the logical clock, which is excluded, up to the logical clock itself, which is included. A reading sitting exactly 30 days before the clock has just aged out; one sitting exactly at the clock is inside. For each distinct `pollutant` present on the plant's readings, the rolling figure is the sum of that pollutant's in-window reading `tons` values, rounded half up to three decimal places — note that per-reading tonnage is rounded before the summation and the sum is rounded again. The plant's `exceeded_pollutants` is then the list of pollutant values whose rolling figure exceeds the ceiling, **sorted ascending by the enum spelling**. The same `permit_limit_tons_30d` applies independently to every pollutant.

Two enum writes sit alongside the feed. A `RegulatoryAdmin` records an air-permit standing change by supplying `permit_status`, either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. A `SafetyOfficer` records a process-safety-management standing change by supplying `psm_program_status`, one of `CURRENT`, `DUE` or `OVERDUE`. Each write stores the value, re-derives `operating_posture` per the ladder in §2.8, and advances the plant's `version` by 1. Neither write is gated on the value actually changing: recording the standing the plant already holds is accepted, is not a state error, and still advances the `version` by 1.

All three of those events — ingesting a reading, recording a permit standing, recording a safety-program standing — re-derive the posture, and so does advancing the logical clock, because sliding the 30-day window can drop stale readings out of it. The posture therefore moves along six arcs, and every one of them is a system re-derivation rather than an operator command: `NORMAL → RESTRICTED`, `NORMAL → FROZEN`, `RESTRICTED → FROZEN`, `RESTRICTED → NORMAL`, `FROZEN → RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN → NORMAL`. In every case the plant's `operating_posture` and `exceeded_pollutants` are rewritten together — the pollutant list being emptied entirely on any arc landing at `NORMAL` — and the plant's `version` advances by 1. The `version` bookkeeping across those four events is not uniform, though. Each of the three write events advances the plant's `version` by 1 whether or not the posture moves, because the write itself mutates the plant row: ingesting a reading bumps the plant even when its posture and its pollutant list both come out exactly as they went in. A re-derivation that lands on the posture the plant already holds is not itself a mutation, so on the clock-advance path — where nothing else touches the plant at all — a plant whose posture is unchanged keeps its `version` and its `updated_at` precisely as they stood, and a clock move bumps only those plants whose posture actually moved.

Degrading the posture withdraws the commercial book, and the cascade is driven by the **arc**, not by the destination alone: a re-derivation that merely confirms the posture the plant already holds cascades nothing at all, consistently with its not counting as a mutation above. **Whenever a re-derivation actually moves the plant to `RESTRICTED` or to `FROZEN`, every quote on that plant whose `status` is `ISSUED` transitions to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` set to `PLANT_POSTURE_DEGRADED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each withdrawn quote's `version` advancing by 1.** An arc that lands at `FROZEN` does more, because a frozen plant may not produce: every campaign on that plant's trains whose `status` is `APPROVED` reverts to `PLANNED` with `provisional_unit_cost_usd_per_ton` cleared to null, processed in `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order; and because those campaigns left `APPROVED`, every order against one of them whose `allocation_status` is `RESERVED` transitions to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `PLANT_FROZEN`, restoring the campaign's `unallocated_ammonia_tons`. The `version` of every mutated child advances by 1 alongside the plant's own.

Relaxation is deliberately **asymmetric**. When the posture improves — because a permit is reinstated, because a safety program returns to `CURRENT`, or purely because the clock advanced and an exceeding reading aged out of the window with no write to the plant at all — the posture and `exceeded_pollutants` are rewritten and the plant's `version` advances, but **already-withdrawn quotes are not reinstated**. A withdrawn quote must be revised back to `DRAFT` and re-issued explicitly, at whatever gas basis prevails then. Reverted campaigns are likewise not re-approved automatically, and released reservations are not re-reserved. Degradation is cheap; recovery is manual.

A last note on a `RESTRICTED` plant, because it is easy to over-restrict it: `RESTRICTED` blocks nothing. New quotes may still be issued while the plant sits at `RESTRICTED`; they simply carry the 1.06 posture premium of §3.8 instead of being refused. Only `FROZEN` closes the eight writes listed in §2.8.
