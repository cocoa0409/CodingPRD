# Employer Health Coverage Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every employer large enough to fall under the shared-responsibility rules has to answer three questions each year, in a fixed order, and defend the answers afterwards: was the aggregated group an applicable large employer at all, which employees were full time in which months, and what offer of coverage did each of those employees actually receive month by month. Today an ALE reporting team answers those questions in spreadsheets that cannot explain themselves. A headcount is amended, someone re-runs a full-time-equivalent count on a copy that is two revisions old, statements go out on a stale basis, and by the time the agency returns an error file nobody can reconstruct which figures produced which printed code.

This API replaces that spreadsheet with a system of record for the whole reporting season. It carries the aggregated group and its EIN-level members, the monthly headcounts that drive the applicable-large-employer determination, per-employee hours of service and look-back measurement cycles, offers of coverage, the generated statements with their twelve monthly code lines, the transmittal that carries them to the agency, the agency's error file and the correction rounds it triggers, and finally the response to a proposed penalty assessment together with the evidence and the member-level allocation of whatever the agency ultimately sustains.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as requirements rather than as background. First, **every derived number is reproducible**: rounding order, evaluation order, tie-breaks and input-set membership are pinned, so two runs on the same data produce the same cents. Second, **nothing silently overwrites work the caller has not seen**: determinations and submissions are guarded by version echoes, statements that have already left the building are corrected rather than edited, and a season cannot be closed while any correction is still open. The API is mounted under the `/ale-reporting` namespace, which is a namespace rather than a collection — every entity owns a flat top-level collection beneath it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` identifying the acting user and `X-User-Role` naming the capacity in which they act. A request that omits either header, sends either as an empty value, or names a role this system does not recognise is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. Five roles exist and their spellings are exact: `ComplianceOfficer`, `BenefitsAdmin`, `Preparer`, `FilingAgent`, and `EmployeeSelfService`. When the acting role is not among the roles listed for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The division of labour follows the reporting team's own segregation of duties. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns the season, the controlled-group structure, the applicable-large-employer determination and the entire proposed-penalty response workflow. A `BenefitsAdmin` owns workforce data: hires, wage updates, hours of service, leaves, transfers, terminations, rehires and offers of coverage. A `Preparer` produces and corrects the statements and assembles the submission — generating Forms 1095-C, regenerating them, opening transmittals, attaching and detaching forms, building submissions, starting correction cycles and resolving agency errors. A `FilingAgent` transmits submissions and records what the agency sent back, including additional agency errors. An `EmployeeSelfService` actor is the employee themselves, acting only on their own electronic-delivery consent.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the employee role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `BenefitsAdmin`, `Preparer`, or `FilingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands an audit actor — as `last_determination_user_id` on a controlled group, `recorded_by_user_id` on an employment event, `generated_by_user_id` and `furnished_by_user_id` on a form, `built_by_user_id` and `transmitted_by_user_id` on a transmittal, `resolved_by_user_id` on an agency error, and `filed_by_user_id`, `last_evidence_user_id` and `response_submitted_by_user_id` on penalty work — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `EmployeeSelfService`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Employee; ownership checks compare the consent's `employee_id` to `X-User-ID`. For that role the identity check is part of admission rather than a downstream business rule: an `EmployeeSelfService` actor granting or withdrawing a consent whose `employee_id` is not their own `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, at the same rung of the ladder as an ordinary role mismatch, not with a later conflict code.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending — which for penalty evidence is also the order in which a recomputation consumes it, and for employment events is the order in which they were applied.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside the request identifier and timestamp.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system is addressed by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Nine resource families additionally carry the full set of mutable-resource properties: the filing season, the controlled group, the group member, the employee, the furnishing consent, the Form 1095-C, the transmittal, the agency error row and the penalty notice each expose `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, plus a `version` integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that resource. Refreshing a derived counter is a mutation: when a cascade rewrites a group's correction count or a member's form counts, `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` advances.

The remaining seven families are append-only ledger rows and carry only `id` and `created_at` — monthly headcounts, measurement cycles, hours records, employment events, coverage offers, monthly coverage lines and penalty evidence have **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all.

All deadline, window and penalty-tier arithmetic reads a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any call to the clock endpoint the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that fixed default, never real time, is the startup value. An operator of any of the four operator roles may move the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the new instant as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity; it only changes how subsequent requests evaluate the furnishing window, the notice response window, the furnishing deadline and the late-furnishing tiers.

### 2.5. Flat Collections, Reverse Reachability and Path Identifier Validation

Every entity owns a flat top-level collection under `/ale-reporting`, joined to its neighbours by explicit foreign keys rather than by nesting. Each collection offers a single-resource read and a filtered list read, and that filtered list is the reverse-reachability channel for every foreign key in the model — including for rows that no create response can ever hand back, because they were produced by a cascade. Monthly coverage lines are only discoverable by filtering on `form_1095c_id` or `month`; agency error rows created by an agency response are only discoverable by filtering on `transmittal_id`, `form_1095c_id`, `corrected_form_id` or `resolution_status`; a correction form is only discoverable by filtering forms on `corrected_from_form_id`.

Path identifiers are validated strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the 404 that comes back. The identifiers in play are `season_id`, `group_id`, `member_id`, `employee_id`, `consent_id`, `form_id`, `transmittal_id`, `error_id` and `notice_id`, along with the bare `id` on single-resource reads. Each family has its own not-found code — `ERR_FILING_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTROLLED_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GROUP_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSMITTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSMITTAL_ERROR_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PENALTY_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` — while the single-row reads of the seven append-only ledger families answer with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved the same way and answer with the not-found code of the referenced family: `filing_season_id`, `controlled_group_id`, `group_member_id`, `single_member_id`, `employee_id`, `to_group_member_id`, `form_1095c_id`, `penalty_notice_id` and each entry of `form_ids`.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder

Every mutating endpoint runs one shared ladder from the top and returns the **first** rule it violates; lower numbers win. The ordering is not cosmetic — several pairs of rules can fire on the same request, and which code the client sees is part of the contract. Three consequences are worth naming up front: the request-shape rung sits **above** existence, so a body carrying both an out-of-range enum and a non-existent id answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and never a 404; the transmittal-state rung at 20 sits **above** the electronic-channel rung at 43, so a paper transmission attempt on a submission that is not built answers `ERR_TRANSMITTAL_NOT_BUILT`; and segregation of duties at 23 sits **above** every temporal window at 33 through 36, because it is a compliance control rather than a scheduling rule.

1. Both auth headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` a declared role — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), all endpoints.
2. The acting role is listed for the endpoint, and an `EmployeeSelfService` actor is the consent's own employee on grant and withdraw — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403), all endpoints.
3. Request shape: required fields present, correct types, enum values in range, declared integers not negative, `ownership_pct` above 0 and at most 100, `ssn_last4` exactly four digits, `ein` exactly nine digits, month values within 1 through 12, `months` arrays non-empty, `plan_start_month` within 0 through 12, dates well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD`, agency-response `errors` entries well-formed, and the intra-body date orderings (a coverage offer's `effective_end_date` strictly after its `effective_start_date`; a measurement cycle's `measurement_end_date` on or after `measurement_start_date`, `admin_period_end_date` on or after `measurement_end_date`, `stability_start_date` strictly after `admin_period_end_date`, `stability_end_date` on or after `stability_start_date`; a notice's `response_due_date` strictly after its `notice_date`) — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), all POST and PATCH endpoints.
4. Every path identifier exists, checked left to right — the matching not-found code (404), every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. Every foreign key in the body exists — the matching not-found code (404), every POST carrying a foreign key.
6. Cross-aggregate scope agreement — `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409), on attach-forms, ale-determination, measurement-cycle creation, agency-error creation, penalty evidence and employment events.
7. The governing filing season is `OPEN` — `ERR_SEASON_LOCKED` (409), on ale-determination, reopen, form generation, regenerate, furnish, transmittal creation, attach-forms, detach-form, build and transmit.
8. The governing controlled group is not `LOCKED` — `ERR_DETERMINATION_LOCKED` (409), on ale-determination, reopen, form generation, `POST /ale-reporting/group-members` and `PATCH /ale-reporting/group-members/{member_id}`.
9. The relevant controlled group is in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `CORRECTION_OPEN` — `ERR_GROUP_NOT_DETERMINED_ALE` (409), on form generation, regenerate and build.
10. The employment event is a declared transition out of the employee's current `employment_status` — `ERR_EMPLOYMENT_EVENT_NOT_APPLICABLE` (409), on employment events.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties

Three compliance controls compare `X-User-ID` against an actor id the system stored earlier, rather than against a role. The user who generated or last regenerated a statement may not be the user who furnishes it: on furnishing, `X-User-ID` must differ from that form's `generated_by_user_id`. The user who built a submission may not be the user who transmits it: on transmission, `X-User-ID` must differ from that transmittal's `built_by_user_id`. The user who most recently filed evidence on a penalty notice may not be the user who submits the response: on submission, `X-User-ID` must differ from that notice's `last_evidence_user_id`. All three violations answer `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403, and because they sit at rung 23 they are reported ahead of the furnishing window, the notice response window and every constraint below them.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this system legitimately supersede earlier work, and both are therefore guarded by an echo of the parent resource's `version`.

Running an applicable-large-employer determination requires `expected_controlled_group_version` in the body — a non-optional field carrying the `version` the client last read from the controlled group. When that value does not equal the group's current `version`, the determination is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GROUP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because a determination may legitimately replace an earlier one; without it, a client holding an outdated view of the group would overwrite counts derived from headcount amendments it never saw. The single-group read exposes the `version` value a caller must echo back.

Opening a transmittal requires `expected_group_member_version` in the body — a non-optional field carrying the `version` the client last read from the group member. A mismatch with the member's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newly built transmittal supersedes the member's prior built submission, so a stale view of the member — its `open_transmittal_id`, its attached and unattached form counts, or its reporting-year full-time months — must not become the basis of a new submission.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Operations not named here are processed without any version echo.

### 2.9. Counting, Rounding and Monetary Precision

Money in this system is always an integer number of cents; no monetary field is a decimal. Where a rule divides, the rounding method and the rounding *order* are both pinned, because the two produce different answers on the same data.

Two rounding operations are used. **Round half up to two decimal places** applies to the per-member part-time full-time-equivalent quotient, to the annual average full-time-equivalent figure before truncation, and to an employee's `measured_average_hours`. **Truncation toward zero (floor)** applies to each month's group full-time-equivalent total, to the annual average when it is reduced to a whole count, and to the monthly statutory penalty amounts derived from their annual figures. Threshold comparisons are inclusive unless stated otherwise: a count that exactly equals the applicable-large-employer threshold meets it, hours exactly equal to the monthly hours threshold make a month full time, a required contribution exactly equal to an affordability threshold is affordable, and an aggregate return count exactly equal to the electronic-filing threshold requires electronic transmission.

Two allocations distribute a whole across the group's active members and must sum exactly to that whole. The thirty-employee exclusion is distributed by the largest-remainder rule: each member first receives the whole-employee floor of its weighted share, and the units still undistributed are handed out one at a time to the members with the largest fractional shares, ties broken by the larger weight and then by the earlier member `created_at` — never by id. Cents are distributed the same way except that the entire leftover remainder goes to the single member with the largest weight, ties broken by the earlier member `created_at`, again never by id. In both cases the weight is the member's `reporting_year_ft_months`.

### 2.10. Season Closure and the Open-Correction Chain

The filing season is the outermost gate. While its `status` is `OPEN` the season's constants and deadlines govern every downstream calculation; once its `status` is `LOCKED` no filing or furnishing write is accepted anywhere beneath it, and there is no unlock — `LOCKED` is terminal. Locking sets `locked_at` from the logical clock and cascades: every controlled group of the season sitting in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `DETERMINED_NON_ALE` moves to `LOCKED`, after which no further determination, form generation or transmission is accepted for that group.

Between those two states runs a counter chain that exists so a season cannot be closed over an unresolved correction. When a statement of a group enters `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED` on its furnishing dimension or `CORRECTION_DRAFTED` on its filing dimension, that group's `open_correction_count` increments; if the group was `DETERMINED_ALE` and the count moves from zero to a positive value, the group itself moves to `CORRECTION_OPEN`, and that move increments the season's `open_correction_count`. The chain unwinds symmetrically: when the last outstanding correction on a group closes and its `open_correction_count` reaches zero, the group returns from `CORRECTION_OPEN` to `DETERMINED_ALE` and the season's `open_correction_count` decrements. A season whose `open_correction_count` is above zero cannot be locked.

### 2.11. Atomic Batch Writes

Three operations accept a set and are all-or-nothing. Recording headcounts creates one row per entry of the `months` array, all carrying the same counts; if any one of those member-year-month triples already exists, the entire call is rejected and nothing is created. Recording hours of service behaves identically over its own `months` array and its employee-year-month triples. Attaching statements to a submission takes a `form_ids` array; if any named form is not in `NOT_FILED`, the whole call is refused and no form is attached. Partial application is never acceptable in any of the three.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Season and Registering the Aggregated Group

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens the reporting year by creating a **FilingSeason** for one `tax_year`, which must fall between 2020 and 2035 inclusive and must not already be taken by another season. The season is the parameter block every later calculation reads. Three dates are mandatory at creation: `furnishing_deadline_date`, the last date on which a statement can be handed to an employee without penalty; `filing_deadline_date`, the last date a submission is on time; and `tier2_cutoff_date`, the date at or after which a late statement is priced in the highest ordinary tier rather than the middle one. Five further figures are optional and fall back to statutory defaults: `affordability_pct` defaults to 0.0902 and must lie strictly between 0 and 1 with four decimal places; `mainland_fpl_annual_cents` defaults to 1,560,000 cents; `penalty_a_annual_cents` defaults to 296,400 cents; `penalty_b_annual_cents` defaults to 444,000 cents; and `electronic_filing_threshold` defaults to 10 returns. A new season starts with `status` of `OPEN`, an `open_correction_count` of 0 and a null `locked_at`.

Inside a season the officer registers a **ControlledGroup**, supplying the season it belongs to through `filing_season_id` and a `name` for the aggregated group. A fresh group has `determination_state` of `NOT_DETERMINED`, `version` 1, `seasonal_exception_applied` false, `open_correction_count` 0, and null values for `determination_basis`, `ale_reason`, `single_member_id`, `determined_at` and `last_determination_user_id`.

Each EIN inside the group is registered as a **GroupMember** carrying its `controlled_group_id`, a nine-digit `ein` unique within that group, the `legal_name` printed on its 1094-C, its common-ownership `ownership_pct` above 0 and at most 100, and optionally its `measurement_method`, which defaults to `LOOK_BACK` and may instead be `MONTHLY`. Members start with `member_status` of `ACTIVE` and every counter at zero: `generated_form_count`, `unattached_form_count`, `attached_form_count`, `reporting_year_ft_months`, `allocated_exclusion_employees`, `allocated_penalty_cents` and `furnishing_penalty_total_cents`, with a null `open_transmittal_id`. The officer may later amend a member's `measurement_method` or flip its `member_status` to `INACTIVE`; either amendment increments the member's `version`, and employees who inherit the method pick the change up at their next full-time recompute. Deactivating a member removes it from every aggregate input set — it stops being counted in the determination, stops receiving penalty allocations and stops being examined when the season is checked for lockability. Because member creation and amendment are structural rather than filing writes, they are not covered by the season gate; they are refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_LOCKED` when the group has already reached `LOCKED`, which is the observable difference between that code and `ERR_SEASON_LOCKED`.

Headcounts arrive as **MonthlyHeadcount** rows, one per month, recorded by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `BenefitsAdmin` against a `group_member_id`. Each batch names a `count_year` — `PRIOR_YEAR` rows describe the calendar year one before the season's `tax_year` and are the only input to the applicable-large-employer determination, while `REPORTING_YEAR` rows describe the reporting year itself and feed the 1094-C Part III counts and the penalty arithmetic. A batch carries the `months` it covers plus the counts shared by those months: `full_time_employee_count`, the employees averaging at least 130 hours of service in the month; `part_time_hours_total`, the aggregate hours of everyone else, already capped at 120 hours per employee before submission; `total_employee_count`, everyone employed on any day of the month, which is what Part III column (b) prints; and optionally `seasonal_excess_count`, defaulting to 0, recording how many of the counted employees were seasonal workers. Each created row freezes its own `member_fte` at creation: the month's `full_time_employee_count` plus the month's `part_time_hours_total` divided by 120 hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. None of these rows is ever updated afterwards; they carry no `version`.

### 3.2. Determining Applicable Large Employer Status

The determination is a `ComplianceOfficer` action that moves a group out of `NOT_DETERMINED` (or out of `REOPENED`, or replaces an existing `DETERMINED_ALE` verdict with a fresh one) and writes every counting output on the group at once. The body carries `basis`, either `AGGREGATED` or `SINGLE_MEMBER`, the mandatory `expected_controlled_group_version` described in §2.8, and — under the single-member basis — the `single_member_id` naming the one member to count, which must belong to the group in the path or the call is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Under the aggregated basis the counted set is every `ACTIVE` member of the group; under the single-member basis it is exactly the named member. That choice is the whole point of the two bases: identical headcount data can yield `DETERMINED_NON_ALE` on a single member while the aggregated basis yields `DETERMINED_ALE`, because members individually under the threshold aggregate into it. Before any counting happens, every counted member must have all twelve prior-year months on file; a single missing member-month is fatal and answers `ERR_HEADCOUNT_INCOMPLETE`.

The count itself reads only prior-year headcounts of counted members — reporting-year rows and inactive members are never read here — and the **rounding order is pinned**. For each month from January through December of the year before the tax year, each counted member contributes its full-time employee count for that month plus its part-time hours total for that month divided by 120 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Those per-member values are added together at full two-decimal precision, the sum itself is rounded half up to two decimal places, and only then is the month's group figure truncated to a whole number and stored in `monthly_group_fte`, whose first element is January of the prior year. Truncating each member before summing is the wrong order and yields a smaller count. The twelve stored monthly figures are then summed, divided by 12, and rounded half up to two decimal places to give `annual_average_fte_raw`; truncating that value gives the whole-number `annual_average_fte` that is compared against the threshold of 50 full-time-equivalent employees.

The seasonal-worker exception is evaluated only when that comparison is met, and it has two conditions that must both hold. First, the number of months whose stored group figure is **strictly greater** than 50 must be at most four. Second, for each of those months, subtracting the counted members' seasonal excess counts for that month from the month's stored group figure must leave a result of at most 50. When `annual_average_fte` is at least 50, at least one month exceeded 50, and both conditions hold, the group becomes `DETERMINED_NON_ALE` with `ale_reason` of `SEASONAL_EXCEPTION` and `seasonal_exception_applied` set true. When `annual_average_fte` is at least 50 but that exception does not apply, the group becomes `DETERMINED_ALE` with `ale_reason` of `THRESHOLD_MET` and `seasonal_exception_applied` false. When `annual_average_fte` is below 50, the group becomes `DETERMINED_NON_ALE` with `ale_reason` of `BELOW_THRESHOLD` and `seasonal_exception_applied` false.

Whichever branch fires, the determination writes `monthly_group_fte`, `annual_average_fte_raw`, `annual_average_fte`, `determination_basis` (`AGGREGATED` or `SINGLE_MEMBER`), `ale_reason` and `seasonal_exception_applied`, stamps `determined_at` from the logical clock and `last_determination_user_id` from the acting officer, and increments the group's `version` by one. A determination that lands on `DETERMINED_ALE` additionally recomputes `reporting_year_ft_months` for every `ACTIVE` member as the sum over months 1 through 12 of that member's reporting-year full-time employee counts — the weight both later allocations divide by. Re-running a determination on a group already in `DETERMINED_ALE` is a legitimate self-loop that overwrites the previous outputs with the new basis and again advances `version`; this is precisely why the write is version-guarded.

Reopening is the officer's escape hatch. A group in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `DETERMINED_NON_ALE` can be pushed to `REOPENED` with an optional `reason`, which clears `determination_basis`, `ale_reason`, `annual_average_fte`, `annual_average_fte_raw`, `monthly_group_fte` and `determined_at` and increments `version`. Reopening a group that reached `DETERMINED_ALE` is refused once anything has gone to the agency: no transmittal of any member of the group may sit in `TRANSMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`. From `REOPENED` a new determination runs the same arithmetic and lands on either `DETERMINED_ALE` or `DETERMINED_NON_ALE`, giving the revision loop `NOT_DETERMINED` → `DETERMINED_ALE` → `REOPENED` → `DETERMINED_ALE`.

### 3.3. Workforce Records: Hires, Hours and Measurement Cycles

A `BenefitsAdmin` hires an **Employee** into a member by supplying `group_member_id`, `full_name`, a four-digit `ssn_last4`, a `hire_date`, an `employment_type` of `FULL_TIME`, `PART_TIME`, `VARIABLE_HOUR` or `SEASONAL`, and the `is_hourly` flag that decides which wage figure the rate-of-pay safe harbor reads. An hourly employee supplies `hourly_rate_cents`, the lowest hourly rate of pay in the reporting year; a salaried employee supplies `monthly_salary_cents`. `w2_box1_wages_cents` is optional and defaults to 0, and that zero is meaningful: it means the W-2 harbor has no data for this person and will be skipped. An optional `measurement_method_override` of `LOOK_BACK` or `MONTHLY` overrides the member's default for this one employee; left null, the employee inherits it. The resolved value is exposed as `effective_measurement_method` — the override when set, otherwise the member's `measurement_method` — and it is recomputed whenever the override changes or the employee moves to another member. A new hire starts `ACTIVE` with a null `termination_date`, a null `open_leave_type`, `full_time_month_count` of 0, empty `full_time_months`, a null `measured_average_hours` and a null `last_recomputed_at`. The admin may later amend `w2_box1_wages_cents`, `hourly_rate_cents`, `monthly_salary_cents` and `measurement_method_override`; each amendment re-resolves `effective_measurement_method` and increments the employee's `version`.

Hours arrive as **HoursRecord** batches, one row per month of a stated `measurement_year`, each carrying `hours_of_service` (paid working hours) and optionally `paid_leave_hours` (paid non-working hours, default 0) and `special_unpaid_leave_hours` (unpaid FMLA, USERRA or jury-duty hours, default 0). Two values are computed and frozen on each created row. `counted_hours` is `hours_of_service` plus `paid_leave_hours` — special unpaid leave is *never* added to the numerator. `excluded_from_denominator` is true exactly when `special_unpaid_leave_hours` is at least 130, and a month so marked drops out of the look-back average altogether, from numerator and denominator alike. Note the two different years in play: look-back cycles read the prior calendar year's months, while the monthly method reads the reporting year's months.

Look-back measurement is configured through a **MeasurementCycle**, created by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `BenefitsAdmin`. A `cycle_type` of `STANDARD` applies to every ongoing employee of the member and leaves `employee_id` null; a `cycle_type` of `INITIAL` applies to exactly one newly hired variable-hour or seasonal employee and names that `employee_id`, which must belong to the `group_member_id` in the body or the request is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The cycle declares four dates plus an optional threshold: `measurement_start_date` and `measurement_end_date` bound the measurement period inclusively, `admin_period_end_date` closes the administrative period (months lying wholly inside it are limited non-assessment months), and `stability_start_date` and `stability_end_date` bound the stability period the measurement verdict is projected onto. `hours_threshold_monthly` defaults to 130 and is the monthly average at or above which the employee is full time for the whole stability period.

Stability periods must not collide. For the same employee — or, for a standard cycle, for the same member with a null `employee_id` — the interval running from `stability_start_date` through `stability_end_date` is treated as **closed at both ends**, so a new cycle is refused with `ERR_STABILITY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` whenever its start falls on or before an existing period's end and its end falls on or after that period's start. Touching endpoints therefore collide: a new period may begin only strictly after the previous one ends. This is deliberately the opposite adjacency treatment from coverage offers in §3.6.

### 3.4. Employment Changes and the Termination Cascade

All employment changes flow through an append-only **EmploymentEvent** log written by a `BenefitsAdmin`, and this log is the only writer of an employee's `employment_status`, `termination_date` and `group_member_id`. An event names the `employee_id`, an `event_type` of `LEAVE_START`, `LEAVE_END`, `TRANSFER`, `TERMINATION` or `REHIRE`, an `effective_date`, and — depending on type — a `leave_type` of `FMLA`, `USERRA`, `JURY_DUTY` or `UNPAID_OTHER`, a destination `to_group_member_id`, and a free-text `note`. `leave_type` is required for a leave start and forbidden on every other type; `to_group_member_id` is required for a transfer and forbidden otherwise. Each event records the `resulting_status` it produced and the `recorded_by_user_id` who filed it. An event type that is not a declared transition out of the employee's current status — a leave start while already `ON_LEAVE`, a leave end while `ACTIVE`, a transfer or termination while `TERMINATED`, a rehire while not `TERMINATED` — is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYMENT_EVENT_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

A leave start moves the employee `ACTIVE` → `ON_LEAVE`, requires an effective date on or after the `hire_date`, and copies the event's leave type into `open_leave_type`. A leave end moves `ON_LEAVE` → `ACTIVE` and clears `open_leave_type`; it also stamps `special_situation` of `LEAVE` onto every monthly coverage line of the employee's non-voided statement whose month has an hours row flagged `excluded_from_denominator`. Because each leave round changes which months survive the look-back denominator, the `ACTIVE` → `ON_LEAVE` → `ACTIVE` cycle is expected to be walked more than once for the same person.

A transfer is a self-loop that leaves the employee `ACTIVE` while moving the EIN underneath them. The destination must exist, must be `ACTIVE`, must not be the employee's current member, and must belong to the same controlled group as the employee's current member — a target in another controlled group is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, while a target that is the current member or is `INACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INVALID`. The transfer sets `group_member_id` to the destination, re-resolves `effective_measurement_method` from the destination member's `measurement_method` when no override is set (so a transfer can flip a person between look-back and monthly measurement mid-year), and stamps `special_situation` of `TRANSFER` on the coverage line for the month containing the effective date on the employee's non-voided statement.

Termination is the system's longest cascade, and it is worth walking end to end. It is accepted from `ACTIVE` or from `ON_LEAVE`, requires an effective date on or after the `hire_date`, and produces the same five linked effects in both cases. **First**, the employee's `termination_date` is set to the effective date, `open_leave_type` is cleared, `employment_status` becomes `TERMINATED` and the event's `resulting_status` records it. **Second**, because the person is now terminated, every coverage line of the employee's non-voided statement for a month strictly after the termination month is rewritten to a `line14_code` of `1H`, a null `line15_amount_cents`, a `line16_code` of `2A`, `full_time_flag` false, `ft_source` of `NOT_EMPLOYED` and `assessable` false; the termination month itself receives `special_situation` of `TERMINATION` and, when its governing offer is not a `NO_OFFER`, a `line16_code` of `2B`. **Third**, because those lines changed, the parent statement recomputes `ft_month_count`, `offered_month_count`, `assessable_month_count` and `line15_total_cents`, and its two status dimensions move: a form already `FURNISHED_PAPER` or `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` goes to `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED`, and a form already `FILED_ACCEPTED` or `FILED_WITH_ERRORS` goes to `CORRECTION_DRAFTED`. **Fourth**, because the form entered `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED` or `CORRECTION_DRAFTED`, the controlled group's `open_correction_count` increments and a group in `DETERMINED_ALE` moves to `CORRECTION_OPEN`. **Fifth**, because the group entered `CORRECTION_OPEN`, the season's `open_correction_count` increments and the season can no longer be closed. A termination recorded late in the season therefore reaches all the way from one person's record to the season gate.

A rehire moves `TERMINATED` → `ACTIVE` and requires an effective date strictly after the `termination_date`. The break length decides whether the rehire is treated as a new hire: when the gap from termination to rehire is at least 91 days, `hire_date` is reset to the rehire's effective date, so the person's waiting period reopens and a fresh limited non-assessment period appears; when the gap is shorter than 91 days, `hire_date` is left exactly as it was and no new waiting period is created. Either way `termination_date` is cleared. The full cycle is `ACTIVE` → `TERMINATED` → `ACTIVE`, with the branch decided purely by day count.

### 3.5. Identifying Full-Time Months

Whether an employee is full time is decided month by month, and the two measurement methods can reach opposite verdicts on identical hours. Either a `BenefitsAdmin` or a `Preparer` can run the recompute for one employee on demand; it also runs automatically whenever a statement is generated or regenerated. The recompute writes `full_time_months`, `full_time_month_count`, `measured_average_hours`, `ft_source_by_month` and `last_recomputed_at` on the employee, and refreshes `full_time_flag`, `ft_source` and `assessable` on the coverage lines of the employee's non-voided statement. It is refused with `ERR_FORM_VOIDED` when the statement it would refresh has either status dimension at `VOIDED`.

Under the **monthly** method each month stands entirely alone. The input set is the employee's hours rows whose `measurement_year` equals the season's `tax_year`; the month is full time exactly when that month's `counted_hours` is at least 130, and `ft_source` for the month is `MONTHLY_ACTUAL`. A month with no hours row at all counts as zero hours and is not full time. `measured_average_hours` stays null under this method. A month at 100 counted hours is not full time even if the year as a whole averages 130.

Under the **look-back** method one average decides a whole stretch of the year. The governing cycle is the employee's own `INITIAL` cycle when one exists, and otherwise the `STANDARD` cycle of the employee's member; when neither exists the recompute is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_METHOD_CONFLICT`. The measurement months are those months whose first day falls within the cycle's measurement start and end dates inclusive, and the input set is the employee's hours rows for those months whose `excluded_from_denominator` is false. If that filtered set is empty the recompute is refused with `ERR_NO_HOURS_DATA`. Otherwise `measured_average_hours` is the sum of `counted_hours` across the surviving rows divided by the number of surviving rows, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the single stability verdict is that this average is at least the cycle's `hours_threshold_monthly`. Then, for each of the twelve months of the tax year: a month whose first day falls within the cycle's stability start and end dates inclusive takes that single verdict with `ft_source` of `LOOK_BACK_STABILITY`; any other month falls back to the monthly test against 130 counted hours with `ft_source` of `MONTHLY_ACTUAL`. Identical hours can therefore produce twelve full-time months under look-back where the monthly method produces none.

An employment overlay then runs on top of both methods, and the **year handling is pinned** so that month numbers from other calendar years cannot leak into the reporting year. The hire month is the calendar month of `hire_date` when that date falls in the tax year, and 0 when it falls in an earlier year — so a person hired before the reporting year is employed in all twelve months. The termination month is the calendar month of `termination_date` when that date falls in the tax year, 0 when it falls earlier, and 13 when it falls in a later year. Any month before the hire month, and any month after the termination month when a termination date exists, is forced to not-full-time with `ft_source` of `NOT_EMPLOYED`. `full_time_months` is the ascending list of the months that survive as full time, `full_time_month_count` is its length, and `ft_source_by_month` is the twelve source labels in calendar order starting with January, each one of `LOOK_BACK_STABILITY`, `MONTHLY_ACTUAL`, `LNAP` or `NOT_EMPLOYED`.

### 3.6. Offers of Coverage, Affordability and the Twelve Monthly Codes

A `BenefitsAdmin` records each offer of coverage as a **CoverageOffer** over a half-open interval: `effective_start_date` is the first date the offer is in force and `effective_end_date` is the first date it is no longer in force, so the end date itself is outside the offer. `covered_months` is computed as the ascending months of the reporting year every single day of which lies inside that interval. Because the interval is half-open, a new offer beginning exactly on an existing offer's `effective_end_date` is legal; an offer is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_OFFER_OVERLAP` only when its start falls strictly before an existing offer's end **and** its end falls strictly after that offer's start. This is the opposite adjacency treatment from measurement-cycle stability periods in §3.3, and the difference is intentional.

The offer records its shape through `offer_kind` — one of `NO_OFFER`, `MEC_NO_MV`, `MEC_MV_SELF_ONLY`, `MEC_MV_SELF_SPOUSE`, `MEC_MV_SELF_DEP`, `MEC_MV_FAMILY`, `MEC_MV_FAMILY_CONDITIONAL_SPOUSE`, `COBRA_TERMINATED` or `RETIREE_COVERAGE` — together with `minimum_value`, the per-month `lowest_cost_self_only_cents` the employee would have to contribute for the lowest-cost self-only minimum-value option, and four flags defaulting to false: `offers_spouse` and `offers_dependents` record who was covered, `conditional_spouse_offer` records that the spouse offer was conditional, and `enrolled` records that the employee actually took the coverage.

Generating a statement is a `Preparer` action against one `employee_id`, with the `plan_start_month` printed on the form (0 through 12) and an optional `safe_harbor_preference` of `W2`, `RATE_OF_PAY`, `FPL` or `NONE`, defaulting to `W2`. It creates a **Form1095C** with `form_type` of `ORIGINAL`, a `furnishing_status` of `NOT_FURNISHED` and a `filing_status` of `NOT_FILED` — the two status dimensions move independently of one another for the rest of the statement's life — and twelve **MonthlyCoverageLine** rows created together, one per month, each stamped with the parent statement's identity through `form_1095c_id` and its own `month`. The employee must not already have a live original — a statement with `form_type` of `ORIGINAL` whose `furnishing_status` is anything other than `VOIDED` blocks a second one with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FORM`, because corrections are produced by regeneration or error resolution and never by generating a second original. Generation also requires the employee's controlled group to be in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `CORRECTION_OPEN`, and it increments the member's `generated_form_count` and `unattached_form_count`.

**The seven steps run in a pinned order that is not the reading order of the form.** First the full-time verdicts of §3.5 are established. Second every month's `line14_code` is assigned. Third `offered_month_count` is computed as the number of months whose `line14_code` is not `1H`. Fourth that count becomes the divisor of the W-2 affordability threshold — which is what makes the W-2 harbor look circular: its threshold cannot be computed until every line 14 code exists. Fifth the affordability test selects the harbor and the threshold. Sixth every month's `line16_code` is assigned. Seventh assessability is derived. A caller who computes line 16 before line 14 will get different codes on the same data.

For each month, the governing offer is the employee's coverage offer whose `covered_months` contains that month; when several qualify, the one with the latest `effective_start_date` wins, ties broken by the later `created_at`; when none qualifies, the month is treated as having no offer at all.

**Step A, the employment overlay.** Using the same year-pinned hire and termination months as §3.5, a month before the hire month, or after the termination month when a termination date exists, is settled immediately: `line14_code` `1H`, `line15_amount_cents` null, `line16_code` `2A`, `full_time_flag` false, `ft_source` `NOT_EMPLOYED`, `special_situation` `NONE` and `assessable` false. No later step touches such a month.

**Step B, limited non-assessment periods.** A month is a limited non-assessment month when either the hire month is at least 1 and the month falls in the window running from the hire month through two months after it inclusive, or a governing `INITIAL` measurement cycle exists and the month's first day falls within that cycle's `measurement_start_date` through `admin_period_end_date` inclusive. A hire month of 0 — meaning the person was hired before the reporting year — creates **no** waiting-period month: the waiting period is only in play for a reporting-year hire. `special_situation` becomes `WAITING_PERIOD` when the waiting-period branch produced it, `INITIAL_MEASUREMENT` when the initial-cycle branch produced it, and `NONE` when neither did. The `LEAVE`, `TRANSFER` and `TERMINATION` values of `special_situation` are written later, by the employment-event cascades of §3.4, and overwrite whatever this step assigned.

**Step C, line 14, first match wins in this exact precedence.** A month with no offer, or an offer whose kind is `NO_OFFER`, is `1H`. Otherwise `COBRA_TERMINATED` is `1H`. Otherwise `RETIREE_COVERAGE` is `1G`. Otherwise an offer whose `minimum_value` is false is `1F`. Otherwise an offer whose `conditional_spouse_offer` is true is `1K`. Otherwise an offer of kind `MEC_MV_FAMILY` whose `lowest_cost_self_only_cents` is at most the federal-poverty-line threshold described below is `1A`. Otherwise `MEC_MV_FAMILY` is `1E`. Otherwise `MEC_MV_SELF_DEP` is `1C`. Otherwise `MEC_MV_SELF_SPOUSE` is `1D`. Everything remaining is `1B`.

**Step D, line 15.** `line15_amount_cents` is the offer's `lowest_cost_self_only_cents` when the month's `line14_code` is `1B`, `1C`, `1D`, `1E` or `1K`, and null for every other code.

**Step E, the affordability test**, whose result feeds line 16. Three thresholds are candidates, each rounded half up to whole cents. The W-2 threshold is the employee's `w2_box1_wages_cents` multiplied by the season's `affordability_pct` and divided by `offered_month_count`. The rate-of-pay threshold is, for an hourly employee, `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by 130 hours and then by `affordability_pct`; for a salaried employee it is `monthly_salary_cents` multiplied by `affordability_pct`. The federal-poverty-line threshold is `mainland_fpl_annual_cents` multiplied by `affordability_pct` and divided by 12, and it is the same flat monthly figure for every employee — it is also the figure the `1A` test in step C compares against. The W-2 harbor has data only when `w2_box1_wages_cents` is above 0 and `offered_month_count` is above 0; the rate-of-pay harbor has data only when the employee is hourly with `hourly_rate_cents` above 0, or salaried with `monthly_salary_cents` above 0; the federal-poverty-line harbor always has data. Selection begins at the statement's `safe_harbor_preference` and then falls through the remaining harbors in the pinned order W-2, then rate of pay, then federal poverty line, skipping any harbor whose data is missing; a preference of `NONE` selects `NONE` outright with no threshold at all. The chosen harbor is recorded per month as `safe_harbor_applied` and the figure it produced as `affordability_threshold_cents`, which is null when no harbor applied. A month is affordable exactly when the governing offer's `lowest_cost_self_only_cents` is at most that threshold; a month with no covering offer is never affordable, and when the applied harbor is `NONE` no month is affordable. The same contribution can be affordable under W-2 and unaffordable under rate of pay for the same employee, which is why the preference and the fall-through order are pinned.

**Step F, line 16, again first match wins in this exact precedence.** A month the employee was not employed in is `2A`. Otherwise a month whose offer has `enrolled` true is `2C`. Otherwise a limited non-assessment month is `2D`. Otherwise a month whose `full_time_flag` is false is `2B`. Otherwise the termination month itself, when a termination date exists and the governing offer is not a `NO_OFFER`, is `2B`. Otherwise an affordable month is `2F` when the applied harbor is `W2`, `2G` when it is `FPL`, and `2H` when it is `RATE_OF_PAY`. Anything left over gets a null `line16_code`, which means the month is exposed.

**Step G, assessability and roll-ups.** A month's `assessable` flag is true exactly when `full_time_flag` is true and `line16_code` is null. The statement then rolls up: `all_12_months_code` is the shared line 14 code when all twelve months agree and null otherwise; `ft_month_count` counts months with `full_time_flag` true; `offered_month_count` counts months whose `line14_code` is not `1H`; `assessable_month_count` counts months with `assessable` true; and `line15_total_cents` sums `line15_amount_cents` over the months where it is not null.

Regeneration recomputes the same twelve lines against current data and is also a `Preparer` action, taking an optional `correction_reason`. On a statement that has neither been delivered nor filed it simply rewrites the lines in place. On a statement that has already been delivered — `FURNISHED_PAPER` or `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` — or has already reached the agency, it does something quite different: a new statement is created with `form_type` of `CORRECTED` and `corrected_from_form_id` pointing at the original, the twelve lines are computed on the new statement, and the delivered original is pushed to `VOIDED` on its furnishing dimension rather than edited — the correction begins life at `NOT_FURNISHED` on its furnishing dimension and `NOT_FILED` on its filing dimension, the member's `generated_form_count` decrements for the voided original and increments for the correction, and `correction_reason` records why. When the original had also reached the agency, its filing dimension moves `FILED_ACCEPTED` → `CORRECTION_DRAFTED` rather than straight to `VOIDED`, because that dimension only voids once the correction is attached to a correction submission; either way the group and the season open a correction through the chain of §2.10. Regeneration is refused with `ERR_FORM_VOIDED` when either dimension is already `VOIDED`, and like generation it requires the group to be in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `CORRECTION_OPEN`.

### 3.7. Delivering Statements to Employees

Electronic delivery needs the employee's own consent, tracked as a **FurnishingConsent** created by the employee or by a `BenefitsAdmin` with the `employee_id` and the `electronic_address` statements go to. At most one consent exists per employee; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A new consent starts with `consent_status` of `PENDING`, `grant_count` of 0, and null `granted_at` and `withdrawn_at`.

Only the employee themselves may move that consent, and only for their own record. Granting takes the consent from `PENDING` to `GRANTED`, or from `WITHDRAWN` back to `GRANTED`; either way it stamps `granted_at` from the logical clock and increments `grant_count`, so the counter records how many rounds the consent has been through. Granting a consent that is already `GRANTED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_GRANTED`. Withdrawing takes `GRANTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `withdrawn_at`, and is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED` from any other state. Withdrawal is not a quiet bookkeeping change: every statement of that employee sitting at `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` is pushed back to `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED`, which increments the controlled group's `open_correction_count`, moves a `DETERMINED_ALE` group to `CORRECTION_OPEN` and increments the season's `open_correction_count`. The `GRANTED` → `WITHDRAWN` → `GRANTED` cycle can be walked repeatedly.

Furnishing a statement is available to a `Preparer` or a `BenefitsAdmin` and takes a `delivery_channel` of `PAPER` or `ELECTRONIC` plus an optional `intentional_disregard` flag. It is accepted only from `NOT_FURNISHED` or `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED`, so `furnishing_status` walks `NOT_FURNISHED` → `FURNISHED_PAPER` or `NOT_FURNISHED` → `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` on first delivery and `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED` → `FURNISHED_PAPER` or `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED` → `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` on redelivery. Electronic delivery additionally requires the employee to hold a consent at `GRANTED`, and the absence of one is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED`. The acting user may not be the user recorded in `generated_by_user_id`. And the calendar must have moved on: furnishing requires the logical clock's date to be strictly after the 31st of December of the season's `tax_year`, because a statement for a year that has not ended cannot be delivered — at the default clock instant this always fails for a season whose tax year is 2026. A successful delivery stamps `furnished_at` and `furnished_by_user_id`. A redelivery additionally decrements the controlled group's `open_correction_count`, and once that count reaches zero the group returns from `CORRECTION_OPEN` to `DETERMINED_ALE`, which decrements the season's counter in turn.

Lateness is priced at delivery. The day count is the whole-day difference between the logical clock's UTC date and the season's `furnishing_deadline_date`. A statement delivered on or before the deadline date carries no penalty at all — the deadline date itself is not late. From one through thirty days late the penalty is 6,000 cents. Beyond thirty days, a delivery whose date is still strictly before the season's `tier2_cutoff_date` costs 12,000 cents, and a delivery on or after that cutoff date costs 31,000 cents. `intentional_disregard` overrides the entire tier lookup with a flat 63,000 cents, even for a statement that is only one day late. The figure lands in the statement's `late_furnishing_penalty_cents`, and the member's `furnishing_penalty_total_cents` absorbs only the **delta** — the newly computed penalty minus whatever this statement had previously contributed — so a redelivery at a worse tier adds only the increase rather than double-counting the original charge.

### 3.8. Assembling, Transmitting and Correcting the Submission

A `Preparer` opens a **Transmittal**, the 1094-C for one member, naming the `group_member_id`, a `transmission_type` of `ORIGINAL`, `CORRECTION` or `REPLACEMENT`, the mandatory `expected_group_member_version` of §2.8, and optionally `authoritative_indicator`, which defaults to true and marks this as the member's authoritative transmittal carrying Part III. A new transmittal starts at `DRAFT` with `cycle_number` 1, every counter at zero and null `channel`, `receipt_id`, `built_at` and `transmitted_at`; creating it sets the member's `open_transmittal_id` and increments the member's `version`.

Statements are attached to a `DRAFT` transmittal in batches through `form_ids`. Every named form must currently be `NOT_FILED` and must belong to an employee whose `group_member_id` matches the transmittal's — a form from another member is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Each attached form moves `NOT_FILED` → `ATTACHED` and records the transmittal in its `transmittal_id`, the transmittal's `form_count` increments, and the member's `unattached_form_count` moves into `attached_form_count`; the member's `version` advances. Detaching is the exact compensating action for one `form_id`: the form moves `ATTACHED` → `NOT_FILED` with its `transmittal_id` cleared, `form_count` decrements, the member's `attached_form_count` moves back into `unattached_form_count`, and the member's `version` advances again. The conserved quantity across attach and detach is the member's `generated_form_count`, which neither operation changes. Detaching is permitted while the transmittal is `DRAFT` or `BUILT`, and detaching from a built transmittal reverts it `BUILT` → `DRAFT` and drops the build outputs it can no longer vouch for: `aggregate_return_count` back to 0, `required_channel` back to `ANY`, `monthly_ft_counts` back to an empty list and `built_at` cleared.

Building is what makes a submission transmissible. It is accepted from `DRAFT`, from `REJECTED` (a replacement round) and from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` (a correction round); it requires at least one attached form, an `OPEN` season, and a controlled group in `DETERMINED_ALE` or `CORRECTION_OPEN`; and from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` it additionally requires `open_error_count` of zero. Building from `REJECTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` increments `cycle_number` by one, and both of those rounds — like starting a correction cycle — are refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_CYCLE_LIMIT` once `cycle_number` has reached the limit of 3. A successful build stamps `built_by_user_id` and `built_at`, freezes `monthly_ft_counts` as the twelve reporting-year full-time employee counts of the member in calendar order starting with January, moves any *other* transmittal of the same member that was sitting at `BUILT` to `SUPERSEDED` (its attached forms keep their `ATTACHED` status and their `transmittal_id`), and computes the channel requirement.

That requirement is a threshold on returns, not on forms alone. `form_count` is this transmittal's attached statements whose filing dimension is not `VOIDED`. To it are added the frozen `form_count` values of every *other* transmittal of the same member sitting in `BUILT`, `TRANSMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` — draft, rejected, correction-required and superseded siblings are excluded — plus one more for this 1094-C itself. The sum is `aggregate_return_count`, and `required_channel` becomes `ELECTRONIC` when it is at or above the season's `electronic_filing_threshold`, otherwise `ANY`. With the default threshold of ten, a member with no counted sibling reaches it with nine attached statements; a member that already has a counted sibling can be pushed over by a two-form correction even though its original submission sat below.

Transmission belongs to the `FilingAgent`. It requires the transmittal to be `BUILT` and the season to be `OPEN`, refuses the user recorded in `built_by_user_id`, and takes the `channel` actually used, `ELECTRONIC` or `PAPER`. A `PAPER` channel against a `required_channel` of `ELECTRONIC` is refused with `ERR_ELECTRONIC_FILING_REQUIRED`; a `required_channel` of `ANY` accepts either. Success moves `DRAFT`-descended state `BUILT` → `TRANSMITTED`, stamps `channel`, `transmitted_by_user_id` and `transmitted_at`, and moves every attached statement `ATTACHED` → `PENDING_FILING`.

The agency's answer is recorded by the `FilingAgent` as a `response_status` of `ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, with an optional `receipt_id` and an optional `errors` array whose entries each name a `form_1095c_id`, an `error_code`, an `error_field` and a `severity` of `FATAL`, `ERROR` or `WARNING`. A clean acceptance requires the `errors` array to be empty; it records the receipt identifier, moves every attached statement `PENDING_FILING` → `FILED_ACCEPTED`, and clears the member's `open_transmittal_id`.

An acceptance with errors requires a non-empty `errors` array and runs the same five-link chain the termination cascade does. **First**, the receipt identifier is recorded and one **TransmittalError** row is created per entry, with `resolution_status` of `OPEN`, a null `resolution_note`, a null `corrected_form_id` and a null `resolved_by_user_id`; `error_count` and `open_error_count` are set from those rows. **Second**, because the transmittal entered `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS`, each attached statement named by an error of `FATAL` or `ERROR` severity moves `PENDING_FILING` → `FILED_WITH_ERRORS` while every unnamed statement moves `PENDING_FILING` → `FILED_ACCEPTED`. **Third**, because a statement entered `FILED_WITH_ERRORS`, any such statement already sitting at `FURNISHED_PAPER` or `FURNISHED_ELECTRONIC` also moves to `REDELIVERY_REQUIRED`. **Fourth**, that redelivery flag increments the controlled group's `open_correction_count` and moves a `DETERMINED_ALE` group to `CORRECTION_OPEN`. **Fifth**, the group's move increments the season's `open_correction_count` and the season can no longer be closed. A partial acceptance requires a non-empty `errors` array with at least one `FATAL` entry and behaves the same way, except that only the statements named by `FATAL` errors move to `FILED_WITH_ERRORS` while all other attached statements move to `FILED_ACCEPTED`; links three through five then fire identically for each of those errored statements that had already been delivered. A rejection creates the error rows too but moves every attached statement `PENDING_FILING` → `ATTACHED`, so the whole submission can be replaced from scratch.

A `FilingAgent` may also record an additional error against a submission already sitting in `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, naming the `transmittal_id`, the `form_1095c_id`, the `error_code`, the `error_field` and the `severity`. The named form must already belong to that transmittal or the request is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. This path touches counters only — `error_count` and `open_error_count` increment — and never moves a statement's filing dimension, because the agency-response cascade is the sole route into `FILED_WITH_ERRORS`.

Resolving an error is a `Preparer` action carrying a `resolution` of `CORRECT_FORM` or `ACCEPT_AS_FILED` plus an optional `resolution_note`, and the two branches carry equal weight. `CORRECT_FORM` requires the error row to be `OPEN`, to name a form through `form_1095c_id`, and to belong to a transmittal in `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`; it moves the error `OPEN` → `CORRECTED`, creates a statement with `form_type` of `CORRECTED` whose `corrected_from_form_id` is the named form, records that new statement in the error's `corrected_form_id`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note`, moves the named statement `FILED_WITH_ERRORS` → `CORRECTION_DRAFTED`, decrements the transmittal's `open_error_count`, and increments the group's `open_correction_count`, moving a `DETERMINED_ALE` group to `CORRECTION_OPEN`. `ACCEPT_AS_FILED` requires the error row to be `OPEN` and its `severity` to be exactly `WARNING`; it moves the error `OPEN` → `ACCEPTED_AS_FILED`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note`, and decrements `open_error_count` — but creates no correction statement and changes no group counter at all. Attempting to accept a `FATAL` or `ERROR` row as filed, or to resolve a row that is no longer `OPEN`, is refused with `ERR_ERROR_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.

Once errors exist, a `Preparer` starts the correction cycle, moving the transmittal from `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`. That move requires at least one open error and a `cycle_number` still below the limit of 3, and changes no counters — it simply marks the submission as needing another round, buildable again once every error has been resolved. The cycle that results is `BUILT` → `TRANSMITTED` → `ACCEPTED_WITH_ERRORS` → `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` → `BUILT` with `cycle_number` advancing, alongside the replacement cycle `BUILT` → `TRANSMITTED` → `REJECTED` → `BUILT`. A correction statement finally closes its predecessor: when a statement whose `corrected_from_form_id` points at an errored original reaches `ATTACHED` on a correction submission, the original moves `CORRECTION_DRAFTED` → `VOIDED`, the group's `open_correction_count` decrements, and the group returns from `CORRECTION_OPEN` to `DETERMINED_ALE` once that count reaches zero.

Closing the season is the last act, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may attempt it. The season must still be `OPEN`, its `open_correction_count` must be zero — meaning no group of the season sits in `CORRECTION_OPEN` — and every transmittal of every `ACTIVE` member of every group of the season must have settled into `ACCEPTED` or `SUPERSEDED`. A season failing either of the latter two conditions answers `ERR_SEASON_NOT_LOCKABLE`.

### 3.9. Answering a Proposed Penalty Assessment

When the agency proposes an assessment, a `ComplianceOfficer` records it as a **PenaltyNotice** against a `controlled_group_id`, with a `notice_number` unique across all notices, the `notice_date` printed on it, a `response_due_date` strictly after that date, the `proposed_amount_cents` asserted, the `asserted_a_months` the agency claims a section (a) failure for, and the `asserted_b_employee_months` — objects pairing an `employee_id` with a `month` — it claims a section (b) failure for. A new notice starts at `RECEIVED` with `evidence_count` 0, `allocated_total_cents` 0, `unallocated_amount_cents` 0, `applied_evidence_count` 0, and nulls in `a_month_penalty_cents`, `b_month_penalty_cents`, `recalculated_amount_cents`, `abated_amount_cents`, `determination`, `recomputed_at`, `last_evidence_user_id`, `response_submitted_by_user_id` and `response_submitted_at`.

The officer then files **PenaltyEvidence** against the notice, which moves it from `RECEIVED` to `EVIDENCE_GATHERING` (and is also accepted while already `EVIDENCE_GATHERING` or `RECOMPUTED`). Each piece names an `evidence_type`, the `month` it covers, a `document_ref` pointing at the archived document, and — for the employee-level types — the `employee_id` it concerns plus the `asserted_line14_code` and `asserted_line16_code` the employer asserts for that month. `MEC_OFFER_PROOF` proves the group-wide minimum-essential-coverage offer for a whole month and carries no employee, so its `employee_id` is null. `FT_STATUS_DISPUTE` denies an employee's full-time status for an employee-month. `OFFER_PROOF` proves an affordable offer for an employee-month. Evidence must line up with what the agency actually asserted: a `MEC_OFFER_PROOF` naming a month absent from `asserted_a_months`, or an `FT_STATUS_DISPUTE` or `OFFER_PROOF` naming an employee-month absent from `asserted_b_employee_months`, is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_MONTH_NOT_ASSERTED`. The employee named must sit under the notice's controlled group or the filing is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Each filing increments `evidence_count`, records `filed_by_user_id` on the row and `last_evidence_user_id` on the notice, and starts life with `applied` false. Filing evidence while the notice is already `RECOMPUTED` walks the notice back `RECOMPUTED` → `EVIDENCE_GATHERING` and invalidates the prior arithmetic: `a_month_penalty_cents`, `b_month_penalty_cents`, `recalculated_amount_cents` and `recomputed_at` are cleared, every `ACTIVE` member's `allocated_penalty_cents` is reset to 0, and both `allocated_total_cents` and `unallocated_amount_cents` are set to 0.

Recomputation moves `EVIDENCE_GATHERING` → `RECOMPUTED`, needs at least one piece of evidence, and needs every statement named by an `FT_STATUS_DISPUTE` or `OFFER_PROOF` row to have reached the agency — a filing dimension of `FILED_ACCEPTED`, `FILED_WITH_ERRORS` or `CORRECTION_DRAFTED` — or it is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_FORM_NOT_FILED`. **Its six steps run in a pinned order.**

The two monthly rates come first: the section (a) monthly amount is the season's `penalty_a_annual_cents` divided by 12 and truncated to whole cents, and the section (b) monthly amount is `penalty_b_annual_cents` divided by 12 and truncated the same way.

**Step 1 applies the evidence in `created_at` ascending order**, walking two working sets that begin as the asserted month list and the asserted employee-month pairs. A `MEC_OFFER_PROOF` whose month is still in the month set removes that month and is marked `applied` true. An `FT_STATUS_DISPUTE` whose employee-month pair is still in the pair set removes that pair, is marked `applied` true, and additionally records one full-time reduction for its month. An `OFFER_PROOF` whose employee-month pair is still in the pair set removes that pair and is marked `applied` true. Evidence whose target an earlier piece already removed — two minimum-essential-coverage proofs for the same month, or an offer proof filed after a full-time dispute on the same employee-month — is left `applied` false and changes nothing, which is why `applied_evidence_count` can be smaller than `evidence_count`.

**Step 2 recomputes the group's monthly full-time counts, and only after step 1 has run.** For each month the count is the sum of `full_time_employee_count` over the reporting-year headcount rows of the group's `ACTIVE` members, less the full-time reductions step 1 recorded for that month. Prior-year headcounts are never read here.

**Step 3 prices section (a).** For each month still surviving in the asserted month set, the charge is the recomputed group full-time count for that month less the thirty-employee exclusion — floored at zero, so a month whose count is at or below thirty contributes nothing — multiplied by the section (a) monthly amount. Their sum is `a_month_penalty_cents`. One surviving month can therefore cost more than a dozen individual employee-months.

**Step 4 prices section (b).** The two sections are mutually exclusive per month, so any surviving employee-month pair whose month also survives in the asserted month set is dropped here. The remaining pairs are counted and multiplied by the section (b) monthly amount to give `b_month_penalty_cents`, which charges per employee-month at the higher rate but only for the employees the agency actually named. `recalculated_amount_cents` is the sum of the two components.

**Step 5 allocates the thirty-employee exclusion** across the group's `ACTIVE` members using the largest-remainder rule of §2.9 with `reporting_year_ft_months` as the weight, writing each member's `allocated_exclusion_employees` so the units always total exactly thirty.

**Step 6 allocates the recalculated cents** the same way, writing each member's `allocated_penalty_cents`, with the whole leftover remainder going to the member with the largest weight. `allocated_total_cents` is the sum of those member allocations and equals `recalculated_amount_cents`, leaving `unallocated_amount_cents` at zero — that field is the conserved-quantity slack, and its being zero is what proves the allocation settled. `applied_evidence_count` and `recomputed_at` are written at the end.

An officer who wants to redo the split can reset the allocation, a compensating action for step 6 alone: it requires the notice to be `RECOMPUTED` with `allocated_total_cents` above zero, zeroes every `ACTIVE` member's `allocated_penalty_cents`, sets `allocated_total_cents` to 0 and moves the whole `recalculated_amount_cents` into `unallocated_amount_cents`. The notice stays `RECOMPUTED`; the conserved quantity is cents.

Submitting the response moves `RECOMPUTED` → `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. It requires a non-null `recalculated_amount_cents`, an `unallocated_amount_cents` of exactly zero (otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_SETTLED`), a logical clock date no later than the `response_due_date` — the due date itself is still inside the window — and an acting user who is not the notice's `last_evidence_user_id`. Success stamps `response_submitted_at` and `response_submitted_by_user_id`.

The agency's answer forks the notice into one of three terminal states, and each fork carries its own amount rule and its own allocation consequence. `ABATED_FULL` requires `abated_amount_cents` to equal `proposed_amount_cents` exactly; it records the `determination` and the amount, zeroes every `ACTIVE` member's `allocated_penalty_cents` and sets `unallocated_amount_cents` to 0. `ABATED_PARTIAL` requires `abated_amount_cents` to be strictly above 0 and strictly below `proposed_amount_cents`; it records the `determination` and the amount and leaves the member allocations exactly where they are, as the sustained split. `SUSTAINED` requires `abated_amount_cents` to be exactly 0; it records the determination and that zero, then reallocates the **full** `proposed_amount_cents` across the group's `ACTIVE` members by `reporting_year_ft_months` under the same largest-remainder rule, so `allocated_total_cents` ends up equal to `proposed_amount_cents`. Amounts that contradict the determination are refused with `ERR_ABATEMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`.
