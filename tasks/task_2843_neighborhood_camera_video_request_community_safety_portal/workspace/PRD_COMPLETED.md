# Neighborhood Safety Portal API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Residential doorbell and yard cameras have quietly become the densest surveillance network a neighbourhood has ever hosted, and law-enforcement agencies have learned to ask for their footage. The Neighborhood Safety Portal exists because that asking needs a process. Left informal, a detective knocking on twenty doors produces no record of what was requested, no evidence that a written justification ever existed, no way for a homeowner to say "not from my camera, ever", and no number anyone can publish at the end of the quarter. This product turns the whole exchange into an auditable, consent-first workflow: an agency is onboarded under a signed data-sharing agreement, an officer draws a bounded geographic canvass area and justifies it in writing, the portal itself decides which cameras are eligible and refuses to canvass a block too thin to anonymise its participants, and every camera owner answers for themselves — share, decline, or say nothing at all.

The portal is deliberately asymmetric in whose interests it protects. An agency's convenience is bounded by quotas, radius caps, repeat-canvass rules and an independent-audit trigger it does not control. A resident's refusal, by contrast, is absolute and is evaluated before any scheduling, window or quota consideration, so that a homeowner is never told "the agency is out of quota" when what actually happened is that they opted out. The same asymmetry governs the community feed that sits alongside the request workflow: neighbourhood posts are scored for discriminatory and unverified content before they ever reach a neighbour's phone, moderation is a two-person duty, and repeat offenders climb a strike ladder that eventually costs them the ability to post at all. Notification volume is treated as a harm in its own right — every resident sets their own alert radius and daily notification budget, and the portal suppresses rather than delivers once that budget is spent.

Two engineering constraints shape everything below. First, the system keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time, because every deadline, retention window and daily notification counter in this domain is a function of "now" and a benchmark must be able to move "now". Second, every derived counter in the portal is part of a published transparency figure, so counters are not conveniences — they are ledger entries, and the document specifies exactly when each one moves, in what order, and which invariant it must continue to satisfy afterwards. The API is mounted under `/neighborhood-safety`, with the clock-control capability living outside that mount at `/_clock/set_current_utc`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Callers, Roles and Identity

Every request to the portal carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, the identity of the caller, and `X-User-Role`, the caller's role. When `X-User-Role` is missing, or carries a value that is not one of the six recognised role names, or when `X-User-ID` is missing, empty or wholly whitespace for any role other than `Anonymous`, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The single exception is resident self-registration, which is exempt from the header requirement entirely — the account being created *is* the identity, so there is nothing to authenticate against yet; there a caller that omits `X-User-Role` is read as `Anonymous` and `X-User-ID` is never required, but a role header that *is* supplied must still name a role the operation admits, so an unrecognised or non-admitted value on that one endpoint is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. When the headers are well formed but the role is not one the target operation admits, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are spelled exactly `PlatformAdmin`, `AgencyAdmin`, `Officer`, `Resident`, `Moderator`, and `Anonymous`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` operates the portal itself: it registers agency applicants, records onboarding decisions, suspends and revokes agencies, compiles and certifies transparency reports, releases footage held under legal hold, runs the retention sweep, and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `compiled_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a transparency report — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- An `AgencyAdmin` administers exactly one law-enforcement agency: it attaches officers to that agency and may withdraw requests on their behalf. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's `agency_id` to `X-User-ID`. An `AgencyAdmin` may never define a canvass area, log a justification, submit a request, or audit one — those are sworn-officer duties.
- An `Officer` is a sworn officer of an onboarded agency. When `X-User-Role` is `Officer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Officer`; ownership checks compare the resource's officer foreign key — `created_by_officer_id` on a canvass area, `submitting_officer_id` or `auditor_officer_id` on a video request — to `X-User-ID`, and the acting officer's own `agency_id` must match the agency of the resource being touched.
- A `Resident` is a neighbourhood resident and camera owner, and holds every consent, retention and account-level data control in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resident`; ownership checks compare the resource's resident foreign key — `owner_resident_id` on a camera or a shared clip, `resident_id` on a solicitation or a dispatch, `author_resident_id` on a post — to `X-User-ID`.
- A `Moderator` opens and decides moderation cases on flagged community posts. When `X-User-Role` is `Moderator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque moderator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `opened_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a moderation case but never dereferences it or compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated caller whose only capability is self-registering a resident account. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque value that the server neither requires nor interprets.

When an entity-backed role acts on a resource that is not its own — a resident touching another resident's camera, solicitation, clip, post or alert settings, or an officer or agency administrator acting for an agency other than the one they belong to — the rejection is `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The same code covers an entity-backed identity that resolves to nothing at all: wherever a write dereferences the acting `X-User-ID` — an officer drawing, locking, requesting against, justifying, submitting, withdrawing or auditing, or a resident acting on a row of their own — an identifier matching no such row is reported as `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` and never as a not-found. Reads are not ownership-scoped at all, so any admitted role may read any single resource or collection.

Two duties in this portal are **separated**, and separation is enforced on `X-User-ID`, never on role name. The officer who audits a video request may never be the officer who submitted it, and the moderator who decides a moderation case may never be the moderator who opened it. Certification of a transparency report is separated even inside a single role: the `PlatformAdmin` who certifies a report may not be the `X-User-ID` that compiled it. Any of these three collisions, plus an officer attempting an audit review without the audit capability, is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so it echoes whatever instant the clock currently holds.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0, may be no smaller than 0, and has no upper bound — an offset past the end of the result set is legal and returns an empty page with the true `total_count`. A bare collection request with no query string at all is always legal and returns the first page under those defaults. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or that falls outside the bounds above, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); a parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent and falls back to its default.

Unless a specific collection documents a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock only moves when it is explicitly set, rows written in the same request or under an unmoved clock routinely share an identical `created_at`; wherever an ordering named anywhere in this document leaves two rows tied on every field it names, the tie is broken by creation order — the row written first comes first — and never by `id`. The pagination block echoed in `meta` carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the total number of rows matching the filters, not the number returned on this page — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Every filter parameter on every collection is optional and filters are combined conjunctively: supplying two filters returns only rows matching both. A filter naming an enumerated field is itself validated: a value outside that field's declared set is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than returning an empty page. A filter over a boolean field accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case and refuses anything else the same way, an identifier-valued filter is matched literally with no existence check, and any filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource in this portal is identified by a server-minted UUID exposed as `id`. Identifiers carry no type prefix, are never supplied by the client, and are never used as a sort key, ranking input or tie-break anywhere in the system — where a deterministic order is required, this document names the ordering fields explicitly, and `id` is never one of them.

Most resources carry the full set of common properties: `id`, `created_at` (the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, with three fractional digits), `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation), and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `Agency`, `Officer`, `Resident`, `Device`, `RequestArea`, `VideoRequest`, `ConsentSolicitation`, `SharedClip`, `ResidentPost`, `ModerationCase` and `TransparencyReport` all behave this way. Every instant-valued field the API returns is rendered in that same form — not only `created_at` and `updated_at` but every domain instant, `window_start_utc`, `locked_at`, `submitted_at`, `expires_at`, `respond_deadline_utc`, `responded_at`, `shared_at`, `retention_expires_at`, `purged_at`, `dispatched_at`, `decided_at`, `applied_at`, `certified_at`, `run_at_utc` and `current_utc` among them — carrying exactly three fractional digits, with any finer precision a client supplied truncated rather than rounded for display while the stored instant keeps its full microsecond precision for every comparison. Every field this document declares on a resource appears in that resource's representation on every response that carries it, holding `null` where it is unset rather than being omitted.

Two resources are **append-only receipts** and deliberately carry less. A `NotificationDispatch` and a `DataControlAction` each expose only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, because nothing in the system ever mutates them once written. They exist to record what the portal did, and a record that can be edited is not a record.

`version` is the currency of optimistic concurrency (§2.8). Because a cascade may mutate a row the caller never named, a caller cannot assume a resource's `version` is unchanged simply because it did not write to it — the cascade catalogue in §3 states, for every operation, which other rows it touches and therefore which other versions it advances.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The portal never reads real wall-clock time. It maintains a single logical clock, and every deadline, retention deadline, expiry comparison, daily notification counter and audit timestamp in this document is measured against that clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which sits at or before every default fixture instant in the system.

A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock to any instant by supplying `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and performs no resets of any kind. Moving it forwards across a UTC calendar-date boundary has exactly one consequence: every resident whose `notifications_sent_today` is non-zero has that counter set back to 0, and each such resident's `fatigue_state` is re-derived under the rules of §3.7 — judged against the reset counter of 0, so the tier penalty vanishes and the state lands on `MUTED` for a muted resident, `THROTTLED` for one whose `notification_budget_per_day` is 0, and `NORMAL` otherwise. That reset is deliberately **not** a versioned write: it rewrites `notifications_sent_today` and `fatigue_state` in place without advancing the resident's `version` and without restamping their `updated_at`, so the cascade bookkeeping of §2.11 does not apply to it. Affected residents are visited in ascending `created_at`. The clock response reports the new `current_utc` together with `residents_daily_counter_reset_count`, the number of residents whose daily counter was actually reset. Setting the clock performs no sweeping of its own: nothing expires and nothing is purged until the retention sweep is explicitly run (§3.6).

### 2.6. The Municipal Planning Grid

All positions in this portal — resident homes, camera installations, canvass-area centres and post incident locations — are expressed as whole-metre coordinates on a flat municipal planning grid. Eastings are `home_x_m`, `grid_x_m`, `center_x_m` and `post_x_m` depending on what is being placed; northings are `home_y_m`, `grid_y_m`, `center_y_m` and `post_y_m`. Every one of these coordinates is an integer between negative one million and one million inclusive.

Because the coordinates are whole metres, **every distance test in this system is performed on squared distances using exact integer arithmetic, and no square root is ever taken.** The squared distance between two grid positions is the square of the difference of their eastings plus the square of the difference of their northings. A point is inside a disc of radius R when its squared distance to the centre is less than or equal to R multiplied by itself — the disc boundary is **closed**, so a camera sitting exactly on the boundary of a canvass area is inside it, and a home sitting exactly at the edge of a resident's alert radius is alerted. Introducing a floating-point square root anywhere here would make those boundary cases ambiguous and is forbidden.

### 2.7. Exact Decimal Arithmetic, Rounding and Basis Points

All non-integer arithmetic in this portal is exact decimal arithmetic, never IEEE-754 binary floating point, and every operand must be constructed from its decimal string literal. This is not stylistic: two of the discrimination-score weights, thirteen thousandths and seventeen two-hundredths, are not exactly representable in binary, so a binary product such as one hundred fifteen thousandths multiplied by three lands just below the true value and rounds the wrong way. An implementation that uses binary floats will compute wrong scores for real inputs.

Where a decimal result is specified, the rounding mode is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the target precision is **two decimal places**. The discrimination score of a community post is the only two-decimal quantity in the system, and §3.8 specifies that its rounding is applied twice: once per weighted term and once to the sum of the rounded terms. Such a value is carried in a JSON response as a number and never as a string, so a trailing zero is not preserved in the rendering: a score of 0.60 appears as `0.6` and a score of zero as `0.0`. Every other quantity in the portal — counters, coordinates, squared distances, durations in seconds and share rates in basis points — is an exact integer, so no other field is subject to any rounding rule at all.

Share rates are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — as integers. A share rate is computed by multiplying the numerator by ten thousand, dividing by the denominator, and rounding the quotient half up to a whole integer. One share out of three solicitations is 3333 basis points; two out of three is 6667; two out of five is 4000; one out of four is 2500. When the denominator is zero the rate is defined to be 0 basis points; this is a documented outcome, never an error.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Three create operations in this portal read a parent resource, make a judgement based on what they read, and then write a child that supersedes or reshapes what other writers may have done to that parent in the meantime. All three therefore require the client to declare which `version` of the parent it observed, and all three reject the write when that declaration is stale. In each case the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one, and a rejection carries HTTP 412.

- Creating a video request against a canvass area requires `expected_request_area_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RequestArea`. A locked area may back any number of video requests, and each creation advances that area's `derived_request_count` and therefore its `version`, so a second creation that carries the area's *current* version legitimately succeeds. A creation still carrying a previously observed version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION`, because the officer's picture of the canvass area — device roster, request count, lock posture — predates a change another writer made.
- Opening a moderation case against a community post requires `expected_resident_post_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ResidentPost`. A new case legitimately supersedes any open case on the same post, so a second case creation carrying the post's current version succeeds and the earlier case is superseded. A creation still carrying a previously observed version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POST_VERSION`, because a moderator's triage judgement rests on post body, flag count and discrimination score that have since changed.
- Compiling a transparency report for an agency requires `expected_agency_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Agency`. An agency may publish many overlapping reporting periods and each compilation repoints `Agency.latest_report_id` and advances the agency's `version`, so a second compilation carrying the current version legitimately succeeds. A compilation still carrying a previously observed version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION`, because the request-volume and share-rate figures it is about to publish were derived from an agency snapshot that is no longer current.

No other operation in this portal accepts a version field. Every other write is guarded by state preconditions alone, and supplying a version to an operation that does not ask for one has no effect.

### 2.9. Order of Refusal

A single request can violate several rules at once, and which violation the portal *reports* is part of the contract. Rules are evaluated strictly in the order below, the first violated rule decides the response, and no later rule is evaluated once one has fired. The ordering is a civil-liberties ordering: authentication, then existence, then state legality, then consent and safety, then temporal validity, then constraint satisfaction, and finally the agency's own quotas. A resident's refusal must never be reported to an officer as "you are out of quota", and a quota breach must never mask a consent breach.

1. Role and identity headers are present and recognised — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role is admitted by the target operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Every `{id}` path segment resolves to an existing row, validated **left to right**, with the collection segment deciding which not-found code is raised: `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIDEO_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHARED_CLIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODERATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSPARENCY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.
4. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `agency_id`, `owner_resident_id`, `author_resident_id`, `parent_post_id`, `request_area_id` and `resident_post_id` — raising the matching not-found code from the list above with HTTP 404. This applies to officer creation, camera registration, canvass-area creation, video-request creation, post creation, moderation-case creation and transparency-report compilation. A foreign key supplied as anything other than a string identifier — a number, a list, an object — resolves to no row and is reported with the same not-found code rather than as a body-shape error; a *required* foreign key that is absent or null instead fails rule 5 as a missing field, while an optional one that is absent or null is simply never resolved.
5. The request body is well formed on every write endpoint: every required field present and non-null, every enum value a string inside its declared set, every integer a JSON integer inside its declared bounds — a boolean, a fractional number and a numeric string are none of them integers, and a field declared boolean must likewise arrive as a JSON boolean — every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and a resident `email` that is unique portal-wide — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An explicit `null` is read exactly as an omission throughout: it fails a required field, falls back to the declared default on an optional one, and leaves the stored value untouched on a partial update. An absent or empty body is read as an empty JSON object, which is why an operation with no required fields accepts no body at all; a body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or is JSON that is not an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as well. A datetime literal is accepted with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit numeric UTC offset which is normalised to UTC before anything else looks at it, or with no zone designator at all, in which case it is read as UTC; its seconds component may be omitted and then reads as zero, and one to nine fractional-second digits are accepted and retained to microsecond precision.
6. For entity-backed roles, the acting identity owns the resource being written — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.
7. Separated duties are respected — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. The acting resident's account is `ACTIVE`; a `DEACTIVATED` account may still read its own data but may not write anything — otherwise `ERR_RESIDENT_DEACTIVATED` (HTTP 409).
9. The agency owning the resource is `ONBOARDED` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_ONBOARDED` (HTTP 409).
10. The agency's own status transition is legal — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_STATUS_TRANSITION_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
11. A canvass area being locked is still in `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_AREA_ALREADY_LOCKED` (HTTP 409).
12. A canvass area backing a new video request is `LOCKED` — otherwise `ERR_AREA_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409).
13. A canvass area backing a submission has not become `INVALIDATED` — otherwise `ERR_AREA_INVALIDATED` (HTTP 409).
14. The video request is in lifecycle state `DRAFT` where the operation demands it — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_IN_DRAFT` (HTTP 409).
15. The video request is in lifecycle state `SOLICITING` where the operation demands it — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SOLICITING` (HTTP 409).
16. The video request's justification has been logged — otherwise `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_NOT_LOGGED` (HTTP 409).
17. The video request's compliance state is `AUDIT_FLAGGED` when an audit decision is recorded — otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409).
18. The consent solicitation is still open to the attempted answer — otherwise `ERR_SOLICITATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (HTTP 409).
19. The camera named by the solicitation is still `ACTIVE` at share time — otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_DECOMMISSIONED` (HTTP 409).
20. The shared clip has not already been purged — otherwise `ERR_CLIP_ALREADY_PURGED` (HTTP 409).
21. A clip under legal hold is being released legitimately — otherwise `ERR_CLIP_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD` (HTTP 409).
22. The post being flagged is `PUBLISHED` — otherwise `ERR_POST_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409).
23. The post a moderation case is opened against is `FLAGGED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_POST_NOT_FLAGGED` (HTTP 409).
24. The moderation case being decided is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409).
25. The post being revised is in `REVISION_REQUESTED` — otherwise `ERR_POST_NOT_REVISABLE` (HTTP 409).
26. The transparency report being certified is still `PROVISIONAL` — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409).
27. `expected_request_area_version` matches the area's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
28. `expected_resident_post_version` matches the post's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_POST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
29. `expected_agency_version` matches the agency's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
30. The resident answering a solicitation has not opted out of video requests at the account level — otherwise `ERR_RESIDENT_OPTED_OUT` (HTTP 409).
31. The resident creating or revising a post is not posting-suspended — otherwise `ERR_POSTING_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409).
32. The written justification meets the agency's minimum length and cites a statutory basis — otherwise `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422).
33. An account-level erasure control is not blocked by footage under legal hold — otherwise `ERR_DATA_CONTROL_BLOCKED_BY_HOLD` (HTTP 409).
34. Declared time windows are ordered and within their caps — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
35. The solicitation response deadline has not passed — otherwise `ERR_SOLICITATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 409).
36. The offered footage segment lies inside the justified footage window — otherwise `ERR_CLIP_OUTSIDE_REQUEST_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
37. The canvass radius is within the portal-wide cap — otherwise `ERR_AREA_RADIUS_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` (HTTP 422).
38. The new canvass area does not repeat-canvass ground the same agency has already covered — otherwise `ERR_AREA_CANVASS_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).
39. The frozen roster still clears the area's minimum-device threshold — otherwise `ERR_MIN_DEVICE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409).
40. The offered footage segment does not collide with footage already shared from the same camera — otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
41. A resident is not flagging their own post — otherwise `ERR_SELF_FLAG_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 409).
42. A resident is not flagging a post they have already flagged — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLAG` (HTTP 409).
43. A resident is not registering a second camera at a position they already occupy — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEVICE_LOCATION` (HTTP 409).
44. The agency is below its concurrent open-request quota — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_OPEN_REQUEST_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
45. The agency is below its lifetime request allotment — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_PERIOD_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

Note the placement of the two quota gates: they are the **last** two rules in the entire ordering, so a submission that is simultaneously over quota and aimed at an invalidated area reports the invalidated area, and a submission that is over quota and below the minimum-device threshold reports the threshold. Note equally that the two account-level consent rules — resident deactivation at position 8 and account-level opt-out at position 30 — both outrank every window, conflict and quota rule beneath them.

### 2.10. Flat Addressing and Path Resolution

This portal uses **flat collections**. Every resource is addressed directly under the API mount by its own identifier, and ownership is never expressed through URL nesting — a camera is not addressed beneath its owner, and a solicitation is not addressed beneath its request. Relationships are carried as explicit foreign-key fields on the row and are discoverable through collection filters instead.

Two collections are nevertheless reached through their parent's single-resource path, because the rows they contain are only ever created as a side effect of some other operation and would otherwise have no discovery channel at all: a resident's notification dispatches and a resident's data-control receipts. Where a path carries more than one identifier segment, segments are resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that fails to resolve decides the 404 code. A dispatch listing for a resident id that does not exist therefore reports `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND` and never a dispatch-level error.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Ledger Invariants

Many operations in this portal touch rows the caller never named. Cascades in this document are numbered by level — L1 is caused directly by the operation, L2 is caused by L1, and so on — and **every level runs to completion within the same request**; there is no deferred or eventual work anywhere in the portal except the retention sweep, which is an explicitly invoked operation. Each row a cascade mutates has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been written directly. That increment is counted once per row per API operation rather than once per write: a row that two different levels of the same cascade touch, or that the operation writes directly and a cascade then touches again, still ends the request exactly 1 above the `version` it started at, and a row created by the very same operation that afterwards mutates it is returned with `version` 1. `updated_at` is restamped by every one of those writes, which is invisible because the logical clock cannot move part-way through a request.

Cascade order is never left to the implementation. Wherever a cascade processes a set of rows, this document names the ordering field: video requests are processed in ascending `created_at`, consent solicitations in ascending `dispatch_rank`, community posts swept for author review in ascending `created_at`. Ordering matters because counters are read and written as the cascade walks, and because a dispatch batch's fatigue arithmetic depends on batch position.

Two ledger invariants must hold after **every** operation, and the specifications in §3 are written so that they do:

- **The solicitation ledger.** For every video request, `solicitation_count` equals the sum of `pending_count`, `shared_count`, `declined_count`, `expired_count` and `withdrawn_count`. `solicitation_count` is monotonic — it never decreases — so any operation that moves a solicitation out of one bucket must move it into another, and the compensating path that revokes an already-given consent moves one unit out of `shared_count` and into `withdrawn_count` rather than reducing the total.
- **The footage-seconds ledger.** For every resident, `lifetime_shared_footage_seconds` equals `retained_footage_seconds` plus `purged_footage_seconds`. `lifetime_shared_footage_seconds` is monotonic and is never decremented. Purging footage therefore moves seconds from the retained side to the purged side rather than destroying them, and footage awaiting purge still counts as retained exposure.

Derived counters are reconciled, not accumulated blindly. `Agency.open_request_count` is the number of that agency's video requests currently in lifecycle state `SOLICITING` and is recomputed on every lifecycle transition of any of them; `Agency.requests_submitted_count` counts every video request of that agency that has *ever* entered `SOLICITING` and is never decremented. `Device.pending_solicitation_count` is the number of that camera's solicitations still awaiting an answer, while `Device.solicitations_received_count` is monotonic. `RequestArea.derived_request_count` counts the video requests created against that area. `ConsentSolicitation.clip_count`, `VideoRequest.shared_clip_count`, `ResidentPost.flag_count` and `ResidentPost.revision_count` behave the same way — the monotonic ones are called out as monotonic where they appear in §3, and the reconciled ones are recomputed from their source rows. `VideoRequest.shared_clip_count` is reconciled as the number of shared clips carrying that `video_request_id` whatever their retention posture — clips already `PURGED` still count — which is what makes it monotonic in practice: purging footage never reduces it. Every one of these reconciliations is a compare-then-write: a recomputation that finds each figure already equal to what the row holds writes nothing at all, and therefore neither advances that row's `version` nor restamps its `updated_at`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Agency, Disciplining It, and Splitting Officer Duties

Nothing happens in this portal until a law-enforcement agency has been onboarded, and onboarding is a two-step act. A `PlatformAdmin` first registers an applicant by supplying its legal `name` (one to one hundred sixty characters) and the `jurisdiction_code` it files transparency returns under (one to thirty-two characters). The same payload may optionally pre-configure the five knobs the agency will later be measured against: `min_device_threshold`, the base number of eligible cameras it insists on before a canvass may be locked, which defaults to 5 and must be between 1 and 50 inclusive; `max_open_requests`, the ceiling on how many of its requests may be canvassing at one moment, which defaults to 3 and must be between 1 and 25 inclusive; `period_request_quota`, the total number of requests it may ever submit through the portal, which defaults to 20 and must be between 1 and 500 inclusive; `post_share_retention_days`, the agency-side retention window applied to every clip it receives, which defaults to 30 and must be between 1 and 365 inclusive; and `justification_min_chars`, the minimum length its own officers must write, which defaults to 80 and must be between 20 and 2000 inclusive. A newly registered applicant lands in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `version` 1, `open_request_count` and `requests_submitted_count` both at 0, and `latest_report_id`, `onboarding_agreement_version`, `reviewer_note` and `status_reason` all null.

The applicant is inert until the platform records its onboarding decision. That review carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with a `reviewer_note` — required, though an empty string is accepted and stored as one — and on approval it must also carry a non-empty `onboarding_agreement_version` — the version string of the signed data-sharing memorandum. Approval moves the agency `PENDING_REVIEW → ONBOARDED` and stores both the note and the agreement version on the row. Rejection moves it `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stores only the note, and is **terminal**: no officer, area, request or report may ever be created for a rejected agency. Either way the agency's `version` advances by 1. A review submitted against an agency that is not in `PENDING_REVIEW` is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_STATUS_TRANSITION_INVALID`.

Discipline runs through a separate enforcement action carrying a `target_status` and a non-empty `reason`, which is stored on the row as `status_reason`. Exactly three moves are legal: `ONBOARDED → SUSPENDED`, `SUSPENDED → ONBOARDED`, and `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`. `REJECTED` and `REVOKED` are terminal, and an onboarded agency must be suspended before it can be revoked — a direct move from `ONBOARDED` to `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_STATUS_TRANSITION_INVALID`, as is any target that is not reachable from where the agency currently stands.

Suspension is the portal's blunt instrument, and it cascades four levels deep. At level one, every canvass area of that agency still in `LOCKED` is driven to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` set to `AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `created_at`. At level two — caused by level one — every video request of that agency whose `lifecycle_state` is `DRAFT` or `SOLICITING` is driven to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` set to `AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, again in ascending `created_at`. At level three, caused by level two, every consent solicitation of those requests still sitting in `SOLICITED` is driven to `WITHDRAWN` with `responded_at` stamped at the logical clock instant, processed in ascending `dispatch_rank`, and the owning camera's `pending_solicitation_count` is decremented by 1 for each; each affected request recomputes `pending_count`, `withdrawn_count` and `provisional_share_rate_bps` so that the solicitation ledger of §2.11 is re-satisfied. At level four, caused by level two, the agency's `open_request_count` is recomputed to 0. The agency's own `version` advances by 1, as does the version of every row any level touched.

Reinstatement is deliberately not symmetrical. Moving `SUSPENDED → ONBOARDED` records the new `status_reason`, advances `version`, and re-opens the agency for fresh canvassing — but it resurrects nothing. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal on the lifecycle dimension and `INVALIDATED` is terminal for a canvass area, so requests and areas that suspension closed stay closed and the officers must start again. Revocation, `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`, records its `status_reason`, invalidates any canvass area still holding `LOCKED` with `invalidated_reason` `AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, and is terminal; by the time revocation runs the suspension has usually already emptied the agency of live work, so in practice there is little left to cascade.

Officers may only be attached to an agency that is already `ONBOARDED`; an attempt against an agency in `PENDING_REVIEW`, `SUSPENDED`, `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_ONBOARDED`. Attaching an officer supplies the `agency_id` they serve, a `badge_number` of one to thirty-two characters that must be unique within that agency — a collision with any existing officer of that same agency, whatever that officer's `duty_role`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the same badge may be reused freely at a different agency — a `full_name` of one to one hundred twenty characters that residents will see on solicitations, and optionally a `duty_role`. `duty_role` is the segregation-of-duties dial and takes one of `REQUESTER`, `AUDITOR` or `DUAL_ROLE`, defaulting to `REQUESTER`. A `REQUESTER` may define areas, lock them, create requests, log justifications and submit — but may never record an audit decision. An `AUDITOR` may record audit decisions but may not lock an area. A `DUAL_ROLE` officer may do both, and still may never audit a request whose `submitting_officer_id` is itself. The dial is consulted at exactly two moments in the whole portal — locking a canvass area and recording an audit decision — and nowhere else, so an `AUDITOR` is free to draw a canvass area, create a video request against a locked one, log its justification and submit it; it is only the lock and the audit that the dial governs. New officers begin with `requests_submitted_count` and `audits_completed_count` at 0; the first counts the requests they have pushed into `SOLICITING`, the second counts the requests they have cleared or escalated.

One agency field is pure bookkeeping and is called out here so it is not mistaken for logic: `latest_report_id` holds the identifier of the most recently compiled transparency report for that agency. It is an identity reference only and is never consulted in any ranking, tie-break or selector decision anywhere in the portal.

### 3.2. Resident Accounts, Cameras and Account-Level Data Controls

A resident joins the portal by self-registering, which is the one operation an unauthenticated caller may perform. Registration supplies a `display_name` of one to eighty characters, an `email` that must be unique across every resident account in the portal, and the home position on the planning grid as `home_x_m` and `home_y_m`. Three notification preferences may be set at registration or left to their defaults: `alert_radius_m`, the radius in metres around the resident's home within which they wish to hear about neighbourhood posts, defaulting to 800 and constrained to between 0 and 5000 inclusive, where 0 collapses that closed disc to the single point of their own home, so the only post that can still reach them is one whose incident position is exactly their `home_x_m` and `home_y_m`; `notification_budget_per_day`, the base number of notifications the resident is willing to receive per UTC calendar day, defaulting to 6 and constrained to between 0 and 100 inclusive, where 0 means every dispatch to them is suppressed for budget; and `notification_mute_enabled`, a hard mute that defaults to false. The new account is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `data_sharing_opt_out` false, `fatigue_state` `NORMAL`, `post_privilege_state` `UNRESTRICTED`, and every counter at zero — `notifications_sent_today`, `accuracy_strike_count`, `discrimination_strike_count`, `total_strike_score`, `lifetime_shared_footage_seconds`, `retained_footage_seconds` and `purged_footage_seconds`. The identifier returned is the `X-User-ID` the resident presents on every later call. A resident account has exactly two postures, `ACTIVE` and `DEACTIVATED`.

Reconfiguring alert preferences later is a partial update over the same three fields — `alert_radius_m`, `notification_budget_per_day` and `notification_mute_enabled` — where any field omitted from the payload is left untouched. Changing the radius changes which *future* posts may reach this resident and never rewrites the fan-out of posts already published. Every accepted update then re-derives `fatigue_state` under the fatigue rules of §3.7 — not only one that changed the daily budget: the tier penalty is taken from the resident's current `notifications_sent_today` and applied to whatever `notification_budget_per_day` the row now holds, so enabling the mute lands the resident in `MUTED` and clearing it returns them to whatever band their counter and budget imply. Both that re-derivation and the version advance are unconditional: the resident's `version` advances by exactly 1 on every accepted update, including one that omits all three fields, one that re-supplies the values already stored, and one whose re-derived `fatigue_state` is the state the row already held.

Registering a camera names its `owner_resident_id`, an owner-supplied `label` of one to eighty characters, and its grid position as `grid_x_m` and `grid_y_m`. Two optional fields shape its participation: `retention_window_days`, how many days of footage the camera keeps on device, defaulting to 30 and constrained to between 1 and 180 inclusive; and `auto_decline_requests`, a blanket refusal that excludes the camera from every eligible-device set, defaulting to false. A new camera is created with `status` `ACTIVE` — the three postures are `ACTIVE`, `OFFLINE` and `DECOMMISSIONED` — and with `solicitations_received_count` and `pending_solicitation_count` both at 0. A resident may not register a second camera at a grid position they already occupy: if any other camera owned by the same resident whose `status` is not `DECOMMISSIONED` already sits at exactly the submitted easting and northing, the registration is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEVICE_LOCATION`, because two co-located cameras would let a single household count twice toward a minimum-device threshold.

The camera also carries `auto_decline_source`, which records *what* set the blanket refusal so that a later opt-in reverts only what the opt-out itself did. It takes one of `NONE`, `OWNER_MANUAL` or `OPT_OUT` and starts at `NONE`. When the owner has already opted out of video requests at the account level, a newly registered camera is forced to `auto_decline_requests` true with `auto_decline_source` `OPT_OUT` regardless of what the payload asked for. When an owner who has *not* opted out submits `auto_decline_requests` as true, the source is recorded as `OWNER_MANUAL`.

Updating a camera is a partial write over `label`, `retention_window_days`, `auto_decline_requests` and `status`, and omitted fields are left untouched. Setting `auto_decline_requests` to true records `auto_decline_source` `OWNER_MANUAL` and excludes the camera from **future** eligible-device sets only; solicitations already dispatched to it are untouched and remain answerable. Setting it back to false records `auto_decline_source` `NONE` whatever the source had previously been, so an owner may lift a single camera's refusal even when it was the account-level opt-out that imposed it, and doing so leaves `data_sharing_opt_out` on the account exactly as it stood. Setting `status` to `DECOMMISSIONED` is deliberately just as inert: every solicitation on that camera stays in `SOLICITED`, `pending_solicitation_count` is left exactly where it was, and the owner is simply unable to share from the dead camera — the solicitations sit there until their response deadline lapses and the retention sweep expires them. That inertness stops at the canvass areas, however. A write that actually changes the stored `status` — in either direction, and no transition among `ACTIVE`, `OFFLINE` and `DECOMMISSIONED` is refused as illegal — runs the eligibility-collapse recount of §3.3 over every still-`LOCKED` area whose frozen roster names this camera, and may therefore drive such an area to `INVALIDATED`; a `status` re-supplied with the value the camera already holds changes nothing and runs no recount, and a write that touches only `label`, `retention_window_days` or `auto_decline_requests` runs none either. Either way the camera's `version` advances by 1.

Account-level data controls are the resident's ultimate lever, and each exercise of one produces an immutable receipt naming the `action_type` exercised, an optional free-text `reason`, the `applied_at` instant read from the logical clock, and four counts of what it touched: `affected_device_count`, `affected_solicitation_count`, `affected_clip_count` and `affected_request_count`. The `reason` is stored exactly as supplied, is left null when omitted, and is accepted and stored as an empty string when one is given. Each of the four counts is filled in only by the action that has something to say about it and reads 0 otherwise. For `OPT_OUT_OF_REQUESTS`, `affected_device_count` is every camera of the resident that was not `DECOMMISSIONED`, whether or not it was already auto-declining; `affected_solicitation_count` is the solicitations the action drove out of `SOLICITED`; `affected_request_count` is the number of distinct video requests that lost at least one of them; and `affected_clip_count` is 0. For `OPT_IN_TO_REQUESTS`, `affected_device_count` is the number of cameras whose `auto_decline_source` read `OPT_OUT`, counted across all of the resident's cameras including `DECOMMISSIONED` ones, and the other three counts are 0. For `PURGE_HISTORY`, `affected_clip_count` is every clip of theirs that was in `RETAINED`; `affected_solicitation_count` counts only those solicitations that thereby cascaded `SHARED → WITHDRAWN`, not every solicitation whose footage was erased; `affected_request_count` is the number of distinct requests behind those withdrawn solicitations; and `affected_device_count` is 0. For `DEACTIVATE_ACCOUNT`, `affected_device_count` is every camera that was not already `DECOMMISSIONED`, `affected_solicitation_count` is every solicitation of theirs that was in `SOLICITED`, `affected_clip_count` is every clip that was in `RETAINED`, and `affected_request_count` is the number of distinct requests that lost a solicitation. There are four `action_type` values.

`OPT_OUT_OF_REQUESTS` is an absolute withdrawal from the video-request programme and cascades five levels. Level one sets `data_sharing_opt_out` to true on the resident. Level two sets `auto_decline_requests` to true and `auto_decline_source` to `OPT_OUT` on every camera of that resident that is not `DECOMMISSIONED`. Level three drives every solicitation still in `SOLICITED` on those cameras to `DECLINED` with `decline_reason` `OWNER_OPT_OUT`, processed in ascending `created_at`. Level four recomputes the counters of every video request that lost a solicitation and auto-resolves each one out of `SOLICITING` when its `pending_count` reaches 0 — to `FULFILLED` when at least one solicitation of that request is `SHARED`, and to `EXHAUSTED` when none is. Level five decrements `Agency.open_request_count` once for every request that resolved.

`OPT_IN_TO_REQUESTS` reverses the opt-out surgically. It sets `data_sharing_opt_out` back to false, and clears `auto_decline_requests` on exactly those cameras whose `auto_decline_source` is `OPT_OUT`, resetting their `auto_decline_source` to `NONE`. Cameras the owner had manually set to auto-decline — those carrying `OWNER_MANUAL` — keep refusing, because the owner's own choice was not the opt-out's to undo. Nothing that the opt-out closed is reopened: declined solicitations stay declined, and a canvass area the opt-out invalidated stays `INVALIDATED`. The opt-in nevertheless runs the very same eligibility-collapse recount of §3.3 over the resident's cameras that the opt-out runs, and because that recount can only ever invalidate and never restore, an opt-in that hands eligibility back revives no area but may still invalidate a `LOCKED` one whose recomputed count was already short of its `effective_min_devices`.

`PURGE_HISTORY` erases the footage the resident has already shared. Every clip of theirs still in `RETAINED` is driven to `PURGED` with `purge_reason` `OWNER_REVOCATION`, processed in ascending `created_at`, and wherever that was the last non-purged clip of a solicitation, the solicitation itself cascades `SHARED → WITHDRAWN`. Only clips in `RETAINED` are reached: a clip already in `PURGE_PENDING` or `PURGED` is passed over, and a clip in `LEGAL_HOLD` cannot arise here at all because a single held clip refuses the whole control outright.

`DEACTIVATE_ACCOUNT` closes the account entirely. The resident moves `ACTIVE → DEACTIVATED`, `data_sharing_opt_out` is set to true, every camera of theirs that is not already `DECOMMISSIONED` is set to `DECOMMISSIONED`, every solicitation still in `SOLICITED` is driven to `DECLINED` with `decline_reason` `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` — every one of them, whatever posture its camera holds, unlike the opt-out branch above which reaches only the solicitations sitting on cameras it just touched — and every clip still in `RETAINED` is moved to `PURGE_PENDING` rather than purged outright, each of those three sets processed in ascending `created_at`. The counters of every video request that lost a solicitation are then recomputed and auto-resolved out of `SOLICITING` exactly as in the opt-out branch, in ascending request `created_at`, and the eligibility-collapse recount of §3.3 runs last. A deactivated resident may still read their own data but may not write anything — every write attempt is refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_DEACTIVATED`.

Two of the four controls are refused outright while the resident owns footage under legal hold. If any clip of theirs sits in `LEGAL_HOLD`, both `PURGE_HISTORY` and `DEACTIVATE_ACCOUNT` are rejected with `ERR_DATA_CONTROL_BLOCKED_BY_HOLD`, because evidence preserved under an audit escalation cannot be erased by an account-level control. `OPT_OUT_OF_REQUESTS` and `OPT_IN_TO_REQUESTS` are unaffected by holds — a resident may always stop receiving new requests. Every data control advances the resident's `version` by 1, and the receipt itself is append-only: it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from the common property set.

### 3.3. Drawing, Locking and Invalidating a Canvass Area

A canvass area is an officer's declaration of *where* and *when*, drawn before any camera owner is contacted. Creating one names the `agency_id` doing the canvassing, the disc centre as `center_x_m` and `center_y_m`, the `radius_m` of the disc in whole metres — at least 50, and a value below that floor is a malformed body answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while the ceiling of 1500 is not a declared body bound at all but the dedicated portal-wide cap described below — and the footage window being sought as `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. An optional `area_label` gives the area a human name; omitting it leaves the field null, and an empty string is accepted and stored as one. The acting officer's identity is stamped onto the row as `created_by_officer_id`, and the area is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `version` 1, `eligible_device_count` 0, `effective_min_devices` null, `derived_request_count` 0, `locked_at` null and `invalidated_reason` null. The three postures an area can hold are `DRAFT`, `LOCKED` and `INVALIDATED`. Creation additionally requires that the owning agency be `ONBOARDED`.

Three refusals guard area creation, and their relative order matters. The window is checked first: `window_start_utc` must fall strictly before `window_end_utc`, `window_end_utc` must fall at or before the current logical clock instant — the portal does not canvass for footage that has not been recorded yet — and the span between the two must not exceed 168 hours. Any of those three failures is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Next the radius: a `radius_m` greater than 1500 metres is refused with `ERR_AREA_RADIUS_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` regardless of how many cameras it would have found, because a wider disc sweeps in whole districts rather than a neighbourhood.

Last comes the repeat-canvass rule, and it is asymmetric on purpose. The new area is rejected with `ERR_AREA_CANVASS_OVERLAP` when there exists another area belonging to the **same** agency, in any status other than `INVALIDATED`, that satisfies both of the following. First, proximity: the squared grid distance between the two centres is less than or equal to 300 multiplied by 300 — a closed disc of 300 metres. Second, temporal collision: take the existing area's footage window and extend it by nothing at all before its `window_start_utc` and by 24 hours after its `window_end_utc`; if that extended interval and the new area's own window share at least one instant, treating both intervals as closed at both ends, the two collide. The buffer sits only on the trailing edge because an agency that has just canvassed a block is expected to work with what it received for a day before asking the same households again, whereas footage from *before* a previous canvass window is fresh ground.

Locking the area is the moment the portal decides who will be asked, and it happens exactly once. Locking requires the area to still be in `DRAFT` — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_AREA_ALREADY_LOCKED` — requires the owning agency to be `ONBOARDED`, requires the acting officer to be the one recorded in `created_by_officer_id`, and requires that officer's `duty_role` to be either `REQUESTER` or `DUAL_ROLE` — an officer holding `AUDITOR` alone is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, at that code's own position in the ladder of §2.9, which places it ahead of both the onboarding check and the `DRAFT` check. Locking writes `locked_at` from the logical clock, moves the area `DRAFT → LOCKED`, and advances `version` by 1.

The **eligible-device roster** is built at that instant, evaluated once over every camera in the portal, and frozen onto the area thereafter — it is never rebuilt when a request against the area is later submitted. A camera enters the roster only when all five of the following hold. Its `status` is `ACTIVE`, so `OFFLINE` and `DECOMMISSIONED` cameras are out. Its `auto_decline_requests` is false, so a blanket owner refusal is out. Its owner's `status` is `ACTIVE`, so cameras belonging to deactivated accounts are out. Its owner's `data_sharing_opt_out` is false, so account-level opt-outs are out. And geometrically, the squared grid distance from the camera's position to the area centre is less than or equal to `radius_m` multiplied by itself — the disc is closed, so a camera exactly on the perimeter is in. One further test filters for cameras that could still be holding the footage at all: the camera's `retention_window_days` multiplied by 86400 seconds per day must be greater than or equal to the number of seconds between the area's `window_start_utc` and its `locked_at` instant; a camera whose on-device retention is shorter than that gap has already overwritten the footage being asked for and is excluded.

The surviving cameras are ordered deterministically — nearest first by squared distance ascending, and where two cameras are equidistant, the one registered earlier first, by `created_at` ascending. A camera's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break, because random identifiers must not decide whose door is knocked on first. The number of cameras on this frozen roster is written to `eligible_device_count`, and their position in this ordering is the `dispatch_rank` each will carry when the request is actually sent.

Locking also freezes the area's **minimum-participation threshold** into `effective_min_devices`, and this number is never recomputed afterwards — the submission gate re-reads it, it does not re-derive it. Three terms compose additively. The base is the owning agency's own `min_device_threshold`. To that is added a radius bump, a step function over the disc: a `radius_m` of 500 metres or less adds nothing, a `radius_m` above 500 metres and up to and including 1000 metres adds 2, and a `radius_m` above 1000 metres adds 5. To that is added a window bump: take the elapsed time between `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, round it **up** to a whole number of hours, divide that by 12, round the quotient **up** to a whole number, subtract 1, and take the smaller of the result and 3. A six-hour window therefore contributes nothing, while a thirty-hour window contributes 2. Finally, and only after the three terms have been summed, the larger of that sum and the global floor of 3 is taken. The floor applies to the sum, never to the individual terms — flooring each term separately would produce a different answer. No camera rows participate in this calculation at all; a wider disc or a longer window means a broader intrusion, and the portal simply demands proportionally more voluntary participants before it will let the request leave draft.

Locking deliberately does **not** check the roster against the threshold. An area may legitimately lock with fewer eligible cameras than it will ever be allowed to canvass with; the minimum-device gate lives at submission time, which means an officer can lock an area, watch neighbours register cameras, and only then discover whether the canvass may proceed.

Two distinct events can drive a locked area to `INVALIDATED`, and their behaviour diverges sharply. The first is an **eligibility collapse**. Whenever a roster member or its owner loses eligibility — the camera's status changes, the owner opts out, the owner deactivates — the eligible count is recomputed against the frozen roster. That recomputation walks the frozen roster and re-tests exactly three things about each member: that the camera's own `status` is still `ACTIVE`, that its owner's account is still `ACTIVE`, and that its owner's `data_sharing_opt_out` is still false. Nothing else is evaluated a second time — a roster camera whose owner has since set `auto_decline_requests` still counts toward the recomputed total, and neither the disc-distance test nor the on-device retention test is re-run. If the recomputed count falls below the area's `effective_min_devices`, the area moves `LOCKED → INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` `ELIGIBILITY_BELOW_THRESHOLD` and its `version` advances by 1. That recount is **transient**: it serves the threshold comparison alone and is never written back, so the area's `eligible_device_count` keeps the value frozen onto it at lock time, and the recount by itself leaves the area's `version` and `updated_at` untouched — the version advance above belongs to the `LOCKED → INVALIDATED` move, and an area that stays `LOCKED` is left exactly as the check found it. Crucially, the lock froze the roster's *order*, not the eligibility of its members: while the recomputed count still clears the threshold, the area stays `LOCKED` and the newly ineligible camera stays on the roster. It will still be solicited, and the refusal comes later, at share time, from the camera-status rule. Requests already in `SOLICITING` against the area continue entirely unaffected by an eligibility collapse; only requests still sitting in `DRAFT` become unsubmittable.

The second event is **enforcement**, described in §3.1: the owning agency being suspended or revoked. That branch ignores `eligible_device_count` altogether, sets `invalidated_reason` to `AGENCY_SUSPENDED`, and — unlike the eligibility branch — does cascade onward, withdrawing the area's draft and soliciting video requests along with their unanswered solicitations.

### 3.4. Justifying, Auditing and Submitting a Video Request

A video request is the artefact an agency is ultimately held to account for, and it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `lifecycle_state` tracks solicitation progress through `DRAFT`, `SOLICITING`, `FULFILLED`, `EXHAUSTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which the last three are terminal. `compliance_state` tracks justification and audit posture through `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING`, `JUSTIFICATION_LOGGED`, `AUDIT_FLAGGED`, `AUDIT_CLEARED` and `AUDIT_ESCALATED`, of which the last two are terminal. The two dimensions advance independently: the compliance dimension moves while the lifecycle dimension is still sitting in `DRAFT`, and the lifecycle dimension moves without disturbing the compliance dimension. Only one crossing exists in each direction, and both are stated below.

Creating the request names the `request_area_id` of the locked canvass area it will draw on, a `case_number` of one to sixty-four characters recording the agency's own case reference, and `expected_request_area_version` as described in §2.8. The area must be in `LOCKED`; an area still in `DRAFT` has neither a frozen roster nor an `effective_min_devices`, so the creation is refused with `ERR_AREA_NOT_LOCKED`, and the owning agency must be `ONBOARDED`. On success the request is created with `lifecycle_state` `DRAFT` and `compliance_state` `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING`, its `agency_id` copied from the area, its `submitting_officer_id` stamped from the acting officer's identity, `auditor_officer_id` null, `justification_text`, `statutory_basis`, `justification_logged_at`, `audit_note`, `withdraw_reason`, `submitted_at` and `expires_at` all null, every solicitation counter — `solicitation_count`, `pending_count`, `shared_count`, `declined_count`, `expired_count`, `withdrawn_count` and `shared_clip_count` — at 0, and `provisional_share_rate_bps` at 0. The backing area's `derived_request_count` is incremented by 1 — a monotonic tally of requests ever created against that area, never decremented however those requests later end — and the area's `version` advances, which is precisely why the next officer to create a request against the same area must have read that new version.

Before the request may go anywhere, an officer must log why. The justification supplies `justification_text` and `statutory_basis`, the statute or ordinance cited as the legal basis. This is only permitted while the request is in `lifecycle_state` `DRAFT` and `compliance_state` `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING`, only by the officer recorded in `submitting_officer_id`, and only while the owning agency is still `ONBOARDED`. Both refusals on the state pair carry the same code: a request that has left `DRAFT` and a request whose justification has already been logged once are each answered with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_IN_DRAFT`, so there is no separate "already justified" error. The text is measured **after trimming surrounding whitespace** and must be at least as long, in characters, as the owning agency's `justification_min_chars`; `statutory_basis` must likewise still be non-empty once its own surrounding whitespace is trimmed away, so a basis of nothing but spaces is no basis at all. Failing either is `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422) — a request may not be logged against a token justification. On success both fields are stored exactly as they arrived, untrimmed, `justification_logged_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the compliance dimension moves `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING → JUSTIFICATION_LOGGED`.

The independent-audit trigger fires in the same operation, immediately after that move, and it is the one thing about this workflow the agency does not control. When the backing canvass area's `radius_m` is strictly greater than 1000 metres, **or** its frozen `eligible_device_count` is strictly greater than 4, the compliance dimension continues straight on `JUSTIFICATION_LOGGED → AUDIT_FLAGGED`: the request is wide or dense enough that an officer other than its author must sign off on it. When neither condition holds, the compliance dimension stops at `JUSTIFICATION_LOGGED` and no audit is ever required. Either way `lifecycle_state` is left untouched at `DRAFT` and the request's `version` advances by 1.

An audit decision carries a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `ESCALATE` together with an `audit_note`, which is required but may be an empty string. It is only accepted while `compliance_state` is `AUDIT_FLAGGED` — otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_PENDING` — and the acting officer must belong to the same agency as the request, must hold a `duty_role` of `AUDITOR` or `DUAL_ROLE`, and must not be the request's `submitting_officer_id`. A violation of that last condition, or an officer whose `duty_role` is `REQUESTER` attempting the review at all, is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `CLEAR` moves the compliance dimension `AUDIT_FLAGGED → AUDIT_CLEARED` and returns the request to ordinary handling; `ESCALATE` moves it `AUDIT_FLAGGED → AUDIT_ESCALATED`. Both outcomes store the `audit_note`, stamp `auditor_officer_id` from the acting officer's identity, increment that officer's `audits_completed_count` by 1, advance the request's `version` by 1, and leave `lifecycle_state` entirely alone — a request may be audited while it is still soliciting. The lifecycle dimension is in fact not consulted by this operation at all, so a request that has already reached `FULFILLED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `WITHDRAWN` may still have its outstanding audit recorded; and unlike creation, justification and submission, the audit does not check the owning agency's onboarding posture either, so an audit may be recorded for an agency that has since been suspended or revoked. Escalation is not a formality: it reaches across into the retention policy of the lifecycle dimension's own artefacts, because every clip shared while the compliance dimension reads `AUDIT_ESCALATED` is retained under a different, longer rule and starts life under legal hold (§3.6).

Submission is the fan-out point, and it carries no request body at all — everything it needs is already on the row. The gates are evaluated in the order of §2.9, which matters here more than anywhere else in the portal. The owning agency must be `ONBOARDED`, and the acting officer must be the request's `submitting_officer_id`. The backing area must then still be in `LOCKED`; an area that has since become `INVALIDATED` — whether through eligibility collapse or agency enforcement — can no longer authorise a canvass, and the answer is `ERR_AREA_INVALIDATED`. Only after that is the request's own lifecycle inspected: it must be in `lifecycle_state` `DRAFT`, or the answer is `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_IN_DRAFT`. That order is not cosmetic — a request that has already left `DRAFT` *and* sits behind an invalidated area is answered with `ERR_AREA_INVALIDATED`, exactly as positions 13 and 14 of §2.9 require. The compliance dimension must have moved off `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING`, or the answer is `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_NOT_LOGGED`. Then the area's frozen `eligible_device_count` must be greater than or equal to its frozen `effective_min_devices`; below that minimum the canvass would identify individual households rather than survey a neighbourhood, so **no solicitation is dispatched at all** and the answer is `ERR_MIN_DEVICE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`.

The agency's two quotas are checked **last**, after every state precondition and every other business rule, so a quota breach can never mask a consent, window or threshold failure. The first gate is concurrent load: when `open_request_count` has reached `max_open_requests` the submission is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_OPEN_REQUEST_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`; the comparison is inclusive, so an agency configured to allow one open request is blocked while exactly one of its requests is `SOLICITING` and is unblocked the instant that request resolves. The second gate is the lifetime allotment: when `requests_submitted_count` has reached `period_request_quota` the submission is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_PERIOD_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. On success both counters move, but only the first can ever come back down — `open_request_count` is incremented by 1 and is decremented again when the request resolves, whereas `requests_submitted_count` is incremented by 1 and is never decremented. The submitting officer's own `requests_submitted_count` is incremented by 1 at the same moment.

What submission actually does is send the request to every camera on the frozen roster. `lifecycle_state` moves `DRAFT → SOLICITING`, `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `expires_at` is set to exactly 72 hours after `submitted_at` — three days is the whole window a camera owner gets to answer. One consent solicitation is then created per roster camera, in the pinned roster order of §3.3 (squared distance ascending, then camera `created_at` ascending), each carrying its `video_request_id`, its `device_id`, the `resident_id` of that camera's owner as at this moment, a `status` of `SOLICITED`, its `distance_sq_m2` — the exact integer squared grid distance from the camera to the area centre — its 1-based `dispatch_rank` within the batch, and a `respond_deadline_utc` copied from the request's `expires_at`. Each new solicitation begins with `responded_at` and `decline_reason` null and `clip_count` at 0. For every camera solicited, `solicitations_received_count` and `pending_solicitation_count` are each incremented by 1. On the request itself, `solicitation_count` and `pending_count` are both set to the number of solicitations created, the other four bucket counters stay at 0, and `provisional_share_rate_bps` is 0. The request's `version` advances by 1.

Each solicitation is accompanied by exactly one notification dispatch, and this is where the fatigue rules of §3.7 bite. The batch emits one dispatch per solicitation with `kind` `CONSENT_SOLICITATION` and `batch_sequence_no` assigned in `dispatch_rank` order, and the fatigue arithmetic decides whether each one is delivered or suppressed. When a dispatch is suppressed, the solicitation it belongs to has `notification_suppressed` set to true and `suppression_reason` set to the matching cause — either `FATIGUE_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` or `RESIDENT_MUTED`. The solicitation itself still exists and may still be answered in the app: suppression silences the phone, it does not withdraw the ask. A solicitation whose notification was delivered keeps `notification_suppressed` false and `suppression_reason` null.

Withdrawal ends a canvass early. It carries a `reason` — required, though an empty string is accepted — which is recorded nowhere on the row: `withdraw_reason` is always written from the fixed vocabulary described here and never from the caller's own text. Withdrawal is available to the request's `submitting_officer_id` and to the agency's administrator. A request still in `DRAFT` may be withdrawn, and because no solicitation exists yet, nothing cascades — `withdraw_reason` is set to `OFFICER_WITHDRAWAL`, `lifecycle_state` moves `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, and `version` advances by 1. A request in `SOLICITING` may also be withdrawn — `lifecycle_state` moves `SOLICITING → WITHDRAWN` — and there the cascade runs two levels: at level one every solicitation of that request still in `SOLICITED` moves `SOLICITED → WITHDRAWN` in ascending `dispatch_rank` with `responded_at` stamped from the clock, and at level two each of those cameras has its `pending_solicitation_count` decremented by 1. The request then recomputes `pending_count` and `withdrawn_count` so that the solicitation ledger still balances, recomputes `provisional_share_rate_bps`, and the agency's `open_request_count` is decremented by 1. A request whose `lifecycle_state` is already terminal — `FULFILLED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `WITHDRAWN` — cannot be withdrawn and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SOLICITING`. Note what withdrawal does *not* do: solicitations already `SHARED` and the clips shared under them are untouched, because withdrawal ends canvassing and does not revoke consent already given. And when the withdrawal was driven by agency suspension rather than by an officer, the only difference is that `withdraw_reason` reads `AGENCY_SUSPENDED` instead of `OFFICER_WITHDRAWAL`.

### 3.5. Consent: Sharing, Declining and Lapsing

A consent solicitation is a question put to one camera owner about one camera, and it has exactly five answers: `SOLICITED` while it waits, then `SHARED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`. The last three are terminal. `SHARED` is not terminal, because one consent may cover several segments of footage.

Sharing supplies just two things: `recorded_start_utc`, the inclusive start instant of the footage segment being offered, and `duration_seconds`, its length in whole seconds, at least 1 and at most 3600. The segment occupies the **half-open** interval that begins at `recorded_start_utc` and ends `duration_seconds` later, excluding that end instant. Six conditions must hold. The acting resident must be the solicitation's `resident_id` and their account must be `ACTIVE`. Their `data_sharing_opt_out` must be false — an account-level opt-out is an absolute refusal to participate and is checked before any window, conflict or quota consideration, refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_OPTED_OUT`. The owning video request must be in `lifecycle_state` `SOLICITING`, or the answer is `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SOLICITING`. The solicitation itself must be in `SOLICITED` or `SHARED`; a `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN` solicitation is closed and the answer is `ERR_SOLICITATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. The camera named by the solicitation must still have `status` `ACTIVE` at this moment: a camera the owner has since decommissioned cannot produce footage even though the solicitation is still open, and the answer is `ERR_DEVICE_DECOMMISSIONED`. And the logical clock must be strictly before the solicitation's `respond_deadline_utc` — at or after that instant the owner may no longer answer even if the sweep has not yet marked the solicitation `EXPIRED`, and the answer is `ERR_SOLICITATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`.

Two rules then police the footage itself. First, containment: the offered half-open segment must lie entirely inside the backing canvass area's **closed** footage window running from `window_start_utc` to `window_end_utc` inclusive. An officer may only receive footage from the window they justified, so a segment that starts before the window or runs past its end is refused with `ERR_CLIP_OUTSIDE_REQUEST_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Second, non-collision: the offered half-open segment must not intersect the half-open interval of any existing clip on the **same camera** whose `status` is not `PURGED`. Intervals that merely touch at an endpoint do not conflict — a segment ending exactly where another begins is fine — but one second of true overlap is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A successful share creates a shared clip. The clip records its `consent_solicitation_id`, the `video_request_id` and `device_id` copied from the solicitation, the `owner_resident_id` of the camera's owner as at this moment — the anchor of the footage-seconds ledger — the supplied `recorded_start_utc` and `duration_seconds`, and `shared_at` stamped from the logical clock. Its `retention_variant`, `retention_expires_at` and initial `status` are decided by the retention rules of §3.6, and `purged_at` and `purge_reason` begin null.

The counter movements depend on whether this is the owner's *first* answer for that solicitation. On a first share, the solicitation moves `SOLICITED → SHARED`, its `responded_at` is stamped, and its `clip_count` becomes 1; on the request, one unit moves out of `pending_count` and into `shared_count`; and the camera's `pending_solicitation_count` is decremented by 1. On a subsequent share against an already-`SHARED` solicitation — the self-loop `SHARED → SHARED`, which is how one consent covers several non-overlapping segments from the same camera — every consent counter is left alone, because the consent decision was already counted; only `clip_count` on the solicitation and `shared_clip_count` on the request advance. In both cases `shared_clip_count` on the request is incremented by 1, the request's `provisional_share_rate_bps` is recomputed, the resident's `lifetime_shared_footage_seconds` and `retained_footage_seconds` are each increased by `duration_seconds` — preserving the footage-seconds ledger of §2.11 — and the solicitation's `version` advances by 1. All preconditions for the self-loop are identical to those of the first share except the status check itself.

Declining is the recorded refusal, and it is a strictly narrower operation than sharing: it is accepted only while the solicitation is in `SOLICITED`, only from the solicitation's own `resident_id`, only while the owning request is `SOLICITING`, and only strictly before `respond_deadline_utc`. It is narrower in those four respects and deliberately broader in two others: neither the camera's own `status` nor the resident's `data_sharing_opt_out` is consulted here at all, because positions 19 and 30 of §2.9 guard the share path alone. An owner may therefore still record a refusal from a camera they have since decommissioned, and still record one on a solicitation that outlived their account-level opt-out because the opt-out cascade never reached its decommissioned camera. The payload carries a `decline_reason`, but a resident-initiated refusal is always recorded as `OWNER_DECLINE`; the other two values of that field, `OWNER_OPT_OUT` and `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED`, are written only by the account-level data-control cascades of §3.2. The solicitation moves `SOLICITED → DECLINED`, `responded_at` is stamped from the clock, one unit moves out of `pending_count` and into `declined_count` on the request, the camera's `pending_solicitation_count` is decremented by 1, `provisional_share_rate_bps` is recomputed, and the solicitation's `version` advances by 1. Diverging from the share branch, no clip is created and no footage-seconds counter moves at all.

Expiry is silence, and it is not the same thing as a refusal. When the retention sweep of §3.6 finds a solicitation still in `SOLICITED` whose `respond_deadline_utc` is at or before the logical clock, it moves it `SOLICITED → EXPIRED`, stamps `responded_at`, and — diverging from the decline branch — leaves `decline_reason` null, because the owner said nothing and the portal records nothing on their behalf. One unit moves out of `pending_count` and into `expired_count`, the camera's `pending_solicitation_count` is decremented by 1, `provisional_share_rate_bps` is recomputed, and the solicitation's `version` advances by 1. A resident takes no strike or penalty of any kind for letting a solicitation lapse.

Withdrawal, the fifth outcome, arrives from above: the owning request leaving `SOLICITING`, whether by an officer's withdrawal or by the agency-suspension cascade. Each still-`SOLICITED` solicitation moves `SOLICITED → WITHDRAWN` with `responded_at` stamped, one unit moves out of `pending_count` and into `withdrawn_count`, the camera's `pending_solicitation_count` is decremented by 1, and the solicitation's `version` advances by 1.

There is one further path into `WITHDRAWN`, and it is the compensating action for a share that the owner later regrets. When a resident purges the last remaining non-purged clip of an already-`SHARED` solicitation — through an owner revocation or through the account-deactivation path — the solicitation moves `SHARED → WITHDRAWN` with `responded_at` stamped, one unit moves **out of `shared_count` and into `withdrawn_count`** on the request, leaving `solicitation_count` untouched so the solicitation ledger still balances, and `provisional_share_rate_bps` is recomputed. A purge driven by ordinary retention expiry does **not** do this: the solicitation stays `SHARED`, no request counter moves, and the consent stands as historically given.

Whenever any of these five paths leaves a request's `pending_count` at 0, the lifecycle dimension resolves itself immediately — provided the request is still in `SOLICITING`, which is the only state this resolution ever fires from. If at least one of the request's solicitations is `SHARED`, the request moves `SOLICITING → FULFILLED` and its `provisional_share_rate_bps` is recomputed. If none is, it moves `SOLICITING → EXHAUSTED` and `provisional_share_rate_bps` is set to 0. Either way the owning agency's `open_request_count` is decremented by 1 and the request's `version` advances by 1. A request that has once resolved is never resolved again and never re-labelled: the compensating `SHARED → WITHDRAWN` path above can empty a `FULFILLED` request's `shared_count` back to 0 long afterwards, and that request stays `FULFILLED` — it does not become `EXHAUSTED`, and no agency counter moves a second time. This is the second and last crossing between the two state dimensions: the compliance dimension is not consulted and does not move.

A request's `provisional_share_rate_bps` is the live share rate over that request's own solicitations, computed in basis points as described in §2.7: the numerator is the number of the request's solicitations in `SHARED`, the denominator is the number of the request's solicitations in total — every one of them, including those still `SOLICITED`, those `WITHDRAWN`, and those whose notification was suppressed. Unresolved and undelivered solicitations therefore dilute the denominator while contributing nothing to the numerator, which is exactly the point of calling this figure provisional. When a request has no solicitations at all the rate is 0.

### 3.6. Retention, Legal Hold and the Sweep

Footage that has been shared does not live forever, and how long it lives is decided **once**, at the instant of sharing, and never revisited — not even if the agency later changes its own `post_share_retention_days`. Three independent deadlines bound the same clip, and which of them is authoritative depends on the compliance posture of the request at the moment of sharing. That is the variant switch, recorded on the clip as `retention_variant`.

The three candidate instants are these. The **agency candidate** is the clip's `shared_at` instant advanced by the owning agency's `post_share_retention_days` whole days. The **device candidate** is the clip's `recorded_start_utc` advanced by the source camera's `retention_window_days` whole days. The **portal candidate** is the owning request's `expires_at` advanced by the portal grace period of 45 whole days. Whole-day offsets keep the time-of-day of the instant they are added to, down to the second. Ties between candidates are harmless, because the earliest and the latest of two equal instants are the same instant.

When the owning request's `compliance_state` reads `AUDIT_ESCALATED` at the moment of sharing, the clip is `CASE_ANCHORED`: its `retention_expires_at` is the **later** of the agency candidate and the portal candidate, the device candidate is **ignored entirely**, and the clip's initial `status` is `LEGAL_HOLD`. Evidence under audit escalation is meant to outlive the owner's own camera setting, and a clip under legal hold is never touched by the retention sweep however far past its nominal expiry the clock has travelled; only an explicit release ends the hold. In every other compliance posture — `JUSTIFICATION_PENDING`, `JUSTIFICATION_LOGGED`, `AUDIT_FLAGGED` or `AUDIT_CLEARED` — the clip is `DEVICE_ANCHORED`: its `retention_expires_at` is the **earliest** of all three candidates, so whichever of the resident's own camera retention, the agency's window and the portal grace period runs out first wins, and the clip's initial `status` is `RETAINED`. On identical input data the two variants therefore differ both in `retention_expires_at` and in whether the sweep may act on the clip at all.

A clip holds one of four retention postures: `RETAINED`, `LEGAL_HOLD`, `PURGE_PENDING` and `PURGED`, of which only `PURGED` is terminal. Purging a clip supplies a `purge_reason`, which takes one of `OWNER_REVOCATION`, `RETENTION_EXPIRED`, `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` or `LEGAL_RELEASE`. A clip already in `PURGED` cannot be purged again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLIP_ALREADY_PURGED`. A resident may purge a clip only when they are its `owner_resident_id`.

From `RETAINED`, a purge is accepted with `OWNER_REVOCATION`, `RETENTION_EXPIRED` or `LEGAL_RELEASE`. From `LEGAL_HOLD`, a purge is accepted **only** with `LEGAL_RELEASE` and **only** from a `PlatformAdmin`: an owner's revocation and the sweep's retention expiry are both refused with `ERR_CLIP_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD`, as is any attempt by a role other than `PlatformAdmin`. From `PURGE_PENDING`, a purge is accepted with `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` and with nothing else. A `purge_reason` that is a legal member of the enumeration but is not one the clip's current posture accepts — `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` offered against a `RETAINED` clip, or anything other than `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` offered against a `PURGE_PENDING` one — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Because that refusal depends on the posture rather than on the shape of the body, it does **not** sit at position 5 of §2.9: it is evaluated after the already-purged test of position 20 and after the legal-hold test of position 21, so an already-`PURGED` clip still answers `ERR_CLIP_ALREADY_PURGED` and a `LEGAL_HOLD` clip still answers `ERR_CLIP_UNDER_LEGAL_HOLD` however unsuitable the reason offered alongside. Whichever of the three paths applies — `RETAINED → PURGED`, `LEGAL_HOLD → PURGED` or `PURGE_PENDING → PURGED` — the clip's `status` becomes `PURGED`, `purged_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the supplied `purge_reason` is recorded, `duration_seconds` moves out of the owner's `retained_footage_seconds` and into their `purged_footage_seconds` — leaving `lifetime_shared_footage_seconds` unchanged, exactly as the footage-seconds ledger of §2.11 demands — and the clip's `version` advances by 1.

Whether a purge also revokes the underlying consent depends entirely on the reason. When `purge_reason` is `OWNER_REVOCATION` or `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` and no non-purged clip remains for that solicitation, the solicitation cascades `SHARED → WITHDRAWN` as described in §3.5, moving one unit from the request's `shared_count` into its `withdrawn_count` and recomputing `provisional_share_rate_bps`. When `purge_reason` is `RETENTION_EXPIRED`, the solicitation stays `SHARED`, no request counter moves at all, and the historical fact that consent was given survives the deletion of the footage. A `LEGAL_RELEASE` purge likewise leaves the solicitation in `SHARED`.

`PURGE_PENDING` exists for one reason: account deactivation. When a resident deactivates their account, every clip of theirs in `RETAINED` moves `RETAINED → PURGE_PENDING`, and — importantly — **no footage-seconds counter moves**, because a clip awaiting purge still represents retained exposure and the ledger must reflect that. The seconds move only when the clip actually reaches `PURGED`. Pending-purge clips are swept regardless of their `retention_expires_at`: deactivation overrides the retention window rather than waiting on it.

The retention sweep is a `PlatformAdmin` capability that runs the whole retention and expiry machine at the current logical clock instant, and it does four things. It moves every solicitation still in `SOLICITED` whose `respond_deadline_utc` is at or before the clock to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `respond_deadline_utc` and then ascending `created_at`, with the counter movements of §3.5. It moves every clip in `RETAINED` whose `retention_expires_at` is at or before the clock to `PURGED` with `purge_reason` `RETENTION_EXPIRED`, moving the seconds across and leaving the solicitation in `SHARED`. It then moves every clip in `PURGE_PENDING` to `PURGED` with `purge_reason` `ACCOUNT_DEACTIVATED` irrespective of `retention_expires_at`; each of those two clip passes walks its own clips in ascending `created_at`, and `clips_purged_count` is the two passes added together. And it counts every clip in `LEGAL_HOLD` whose `retention_expires_at` is at or before the clock — the same at-or-before comparison the retained pass uses, so a hold expiring exactly on the clock instant is counted — **without touching it**. Finally, every video request still in `SOLICITING` is revisited in ascending `created_at`, its solicitation buckets reconciled under §2.11, and each one whose `pending_count` has reached 0 is auto-resolved into `FULFILLED` or `EXHAUSTED` under the rule of §3.5, with the agency's `open_request_count` decremented for each. The sweep increments the `version` of every entity it mutates by 1.

The sweep reports what it did: `run_at_utc` echoing the logical clock instant it ran at, `solicitations_expired_count`, `clips_purged_count`, `clips_held_count` — the legal-hold clips it deliberately skipped — and `requests_auto_closed_count`, which counts the requests that were in `SOLICITING` when the sweep began and are no longer in it when the sweep ends. It is idempotent at a fixed clock in the sense that matters: run it twice without moving the clock and the second run mutates nothing at all, because everything it was entitled to act on has already moved, so `solicitations_expired_count`, `clips_purged_count` and `requests_auto_closed_count` each report 0. `clips_held_count` is the deliberate exception and does **not** fall to 0 on a repeat run — it is a census of clips the sweep never touches, so every run at the same clock instant reports the same figure.

### 3.7. Notification Dispatch and Fatigue Controls

Every notification the portal attempts produces a permanent record, delivered or not. Each dispatch names its recipient as `resident_id`, its `kind` — either `CONSENT_SOLICITATION` when it accompanies a consent solicitation or `POST_ALERT` when it accompanies a neighbourhood post — and its `source_ref_id`, a polymorphic reference holding the identifier of the consent solicitation for the first kind and of the community post for the second. Because the target entity varies by kind, `source_ref_id` is an identity reference only and is never dereferenced as a declared relationship. The dispatch also carries `batch_sequence_no`, its 1-based position within the emitting batch in that batch's pinned order, `dispatched_at` from the logical clock, `suppressed`, and `suppression_reason`. Dispatch records are append-only receipts: they expose `id` and `created_at` and carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Two further fields on each dispatch exist to make the fatigue arithmetic auditable after the fact. `recipient_budget_snapshot` is the recipient's `notifications_sent_today` as read at the **start** of the emitting batch, and `recipient_effective_budget` is the effective allowance the batch computed for that recipient. Both are identical across every dispatch of one batch aimed at the same recipient, which is the whole point.

The snapshot semantics are the difficult part of this rule and are worth stating plainly: **the fatigue tier is computed once per recipient per batch, from that recipient's `notifications_sent_today` as read at the start of the batch.** Dispatches emitted inside the batch draw down the remaining allowance but they do not re-tier the recipient part-way through. Re-deriving the tier per dispatch would yield a strictly smaller allowance and is wrong.

For each recipient in a batch, let the snapshot be that recipient's `notifications_sent_today` at batch start and let the budget be their `notification_budget_per_day`. A tier penalty is chosen from the snapshot in four bands: a snapshot below 5 incurs no penalty; a snapshot of 5 or more but below 10 incurs a penalty of 1; a snapshot of 10 or more but below 20 incurs a penalty of 3; and a snapshot of 20 or more incurs a penalty equal to the recipient's entire budget. The effective budget is the budget minus the penalty, clamped at a floor of zero. The batch allowance is then the effective budget minus the snapshot, again clamped at a floor of zero. Both clamps are mandatory: a penalty larger than the budget must yield an allowance of zero, never a negative one.

Dispatches are then decided one at a time, in ascending `batch_sequence_no`, with a running count of how many have been delivered so far in this batch to this recipient. Each dispatch first records `recipient_budget_snapshot` and `recipient_effective_budget`. Then: if the recipient's `notification_mute_enabled` is true, the dispatch is marked `suppressed` true with `suppression_reason` `RESIDENT_MUTED`. Otherwise, if the count already delivered in this batch has reached the batch allowance, the dispatch is marked `suppressed` true with `suppression_reason` `FATIGUE_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. Otherwise the dispatch is delivered — `suppressed` false and `suppression_reason` null — the delivered count for this batch rises by 1, and the recipient's `notifications_sent_today` rises by 1. A hard mute therefore beats a budget: a muted resident's dispatches are always attributed to the mute, never to the budget, however much allowance remained.

After the batch has finished, the recipient's `fatigue_state` is re-derived from the **post-batch** counter, which means it can move even in a batch where every single dispatch was suppressed. The counter is the post-batch one but the budget it is judged against is not: the derivation re-uses the very `recipient_effective_budget` the batch computed at its start and never re-tiers the recipient against their raised counter, so a recipient whose deliveries have pushed them over a *tier* boundary without reaching that batch-start effective budget is not thereby `THROTTLED`. That re-derivation is a write to the recipient's own row: every resident the batch aimed at least one dispatch at has their `version` advanced and their `updated_at` refreshed by the batch, whether or not any dispatch to them was delivered and whether or not the re-derived `fatigue_state` differs from the one they already held. A caller that submits a video request must therefore expect the `version` of every solicited camera owner to have moved (§2.11). The derivation is evaluated in order and takes the first branch that applies: a recipient whose `notification_mute_enabled` is true is `MUTED`; otherwise a recipient whose `notifications_sent_today` has reached or passed a strictly positive effective budget is `THROTTLED`; otherwise a recipient whose effective budget is zero is `THROTTLED`; otherwise a recipient whose `notifications_sent_today` has reached or passed 10 is `STRAINED`; otherwise the recipient is `NORMAL`.

`notifications_sent_today` counts that resident's delivered dispatches — those with `suppressed` false — whose `dispatched_at` falls on the current logical-clock UTC calendar date. It is reset to 0 for every resident holding a non-zero value whenever the clock advances across a UTC date boundary, at which point each affected resident's `fatigue_state` is re-derived (§2.5). Three operations emit dispatch batches: submitting a video request, which emits one `CONSENT_SOLICITATION` dispatch per solicitation in `dispatch_rank` order; publishing a community post that lands in the feed, which emits one `POST_ALERT` dispatch per selected neighbour; and the two deferred fan-outs of §3.8 — a moderation decision that upholds a post, and a revision that brings a post back into the feed.

### 3.8. The Neighbourhood Feed: Posting, Flagging, Moderation and Author Accountability

The feed is where residents talk to each other, and it is the part of the portal most likely to be used to accuse a neighbour of something on the basis of what they look like. The portal therefore scores every post before it is shown to anybody.

A resident publishing a post supplies their own `author_resident_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` after identity translation, or the write is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — a `body` of between 1 and 2000 characters, a `category` drawn from `SAFETY_REPORT`, `LOST_PET`, `SUSPICIOUS_ACTIVITY` and `GENERAL`, and the incident position as `post_x_m` and `post_y_m`, each a whole metre between −1000000 and 1000000 on the municipal planning grid. Optionally they may supply `parent_post_id`, the identifier of the post this one replies to, which is a self-reference into the feed and defaults to null for a top-level post; an `alert_fanout_cap` between 0 and 500, defaulting to 50; and the four classifier signals `protected_class_term_count`, `unverified_assertion_count`, `location_profiling_count` and `hostile_tone_count`, each an integer between 0 and 50 and each defaulting to 0. A resident whose `post_privilege_state` reads `POSTING_SUSPENDED` may not publish at all, and is refused with `ERR_POSTING_SUSPENDED`. Every post is created with `flag_count` 0, `revision_count` 0 and an empty `flagged_by_resident_ids`.

The four signals produce the post's `discrimination_score`, and this is the calculation that most often gets implemented wrongly. Each signal is multiplied by its own weight — 0.34 for `protected_class_term_count`, 0.17 for `unverified_assertion_count`, 0.115 for `location_profiling_count`, and 0.085 for `hostile_tone_count` — **and each of the four resulting terms is rounded to two decimals on its own**, half up. Only then are the four rounded terms added together, and the sum is rounded to two decimals again. Adding the raw products and rounding once at the end is a different calculation and gives a different answer for some inputs: with a `location_profiling_count` of 3 and a `hostile_tone_count` of 3, per-term rounding turns 0.345 into 0.35 and 0.255 into 0.26 for a total of 0.61, while rounding only the raw sum gives 0.60. The operands must be exact decimals, not binary floats, for the same reason set out in §2.7 — in IEEE-754, 0.115 multiplied by 3 is 0.34499999999999997, which rounds down to 0.34 and produces the wrong score. Nothing outside the post itself feeds this figure: it is a pure function of the post's own four signal counts.

A `discrimination_score` of 0.49 or higher — the comparison is inclusive — means the post never reaches the feed. It is created in `FLAGGED` with `auto_flag_reason` set to `DISCRIMINATION_SCORE`, it alerts nobody, and its three alert counters stay at 0. A score below 0.49 means the post is created in `PUBLISHED` with a null `auto_flag_reason` and the alert fan-out runs immediately. Either way the post is created and the response carries HTTP 201.

The fan-out decides which neighbours hear about a published post, and its defining property is that **the radius belongs to the recipient, not to the post**. Every resident is considered except the author themselves and except any resident whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`. For each remaining resident, the squared distance from the post's position to that resident's `home_x_m` and `home_y_m` is computed as an exact integer, and the resident is a candidate when that squared distance is no greater than the square of **their own** `alert_radius_m` — a closed disc, boundary inclusive, per §2.6. Because each recipient brings their own radius, the relation is asymmetric: a far-sighted resident may be alerted by a post at a neighbour's house without that neighbour being alerted by a post at theirs. An empty candidate pool is entirely legal, never an error: all three counters read 0, no dispatches are created, and the post is still returned with HTTP 201.

Candidates are then ordered by ascending squared distance and, for equal distances, by ascending `Resident.created_at` — nearest home first, then earliest account. The resident's identifier is never a tie-break input. `alert_candidate_count` records the size of the ordered list **before** the cap is applied, and the cap then truncates the **tail**, keeping the leading entries. Each surviving candidate receives exactly one notification dispatch, its `batch_sequence_no` assigned in that same order, with delivery or suppression decided by the fatigue rules of §3.7. `alert_fanout_count` counts the resulting dispatches that were delivered and `alert_suppressed_count` counts those that were suppressed, so the two together sum to the **lesser** of `alert_candidate_count` and `alert_fanout_cap` — not to `alert_candidate_count`. A cap of 0 is legal and yields a candidate count that may be large with both other counters at 0.

Any resident may flag a published post, supplying a `flag_reason` describing what is wrong with it — required, and unlike an audit or decision note it may not be the empty string, so an absent, null or empty `flag_reason` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the portal records who flagged, not the text they gave. The flagger is the acting `X-User-ID` and is never carried in the body. That identifier is recorded as given rather than resolved: a caller presenting role `Resident` under an identifier matching no resident row still flags successfully and that identifier still lands in `flagged_by_resident_ids`, while an identifier that does resolve, to an account whose `status` is `DEACTIVATED`, is refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_DEACTIVATED`. A post must be in `PUBLISHED` to be flagged, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_PUBLISHED`. An author may never flag their own post — `ERR_SELF_FLAG_NOT_ALLOWED` — and no resident may flag the same post twice, because their identifier already appears in `flagged_by_resident_ids` and the second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLAG`. `flagged_by_resident_ids` is used for exactly this duplicate test and for nothing else: it is never a ranking or ordering input. Each accepted flag increments `flag_count` by 1, appends the flagger, and advances the post's `version` by 1. The post itself moves `PUBLISHED → FLAGGED` with `auto_flag_reason` set to `RESIDENT_FLAG_THRESHOLD` only once `flag_count` has reached the resident-flag threshold of 2; a first, solitary flag leaves the post in `PUBLISHED` while still bumping the counter and the version. `flag_count` is monotonic — it is never decremented, not even when a revision cycle returns the post to the feed, so a post that has once been flagged twice carries that history for good.

A moderator opens a case against a post in `FLAGGED` or already in `UNDER_REVIEW`; a post in any other state is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_FLAGGED`. The case names its `resident_post_id`, an `opened_reason` of `RESIDENT_FLAGS`, `AUTO_SCORE` or `AUTHOR_SUSPENDED` — the last is the value the author-suspension cascade stamps on the cases it opens for itself, which is a convention and not a validation, so a moderator who supplies it by hand is not refused and the case is created carrying it — and the `expected_resident_post_version` the moderator observed. Opening is guarded by optimistic concurrency precisely because a second case on the same post is legitimate rather than forbidden: two moderators triaging the same post concurrently would both succeed and the first case would vanish into supersession unread, so the stale version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POST_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The new case is created in `OPEN` with `decision` `NONE`, `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `post_discrimination_score_at_open` snapshotted from the post's `discrimination_score` at that instant — a frozen figure that stays put however often the post is later revised. Any case already `OPEN` on the same post moves `OPEN → SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_case_created_at` set to the new case's `created_at` and its `decision` left at `NONE`. The post moves `FLAGGED → UNDER_REVIEW`, or `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW` when it was already under review — a re-triage that supersedes the open case without changing the post's state, and its `version` advances by 1 in either case.

Deciding a case requires the case to be `OPEN` — a `DECIDED` or `SUPERSEDED` case is terminal and is refused with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED` — and requires the post to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, a post in any other state being refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_FLAGGED`. Of those two the case check outranks the post check, so a case that has already been decided answers `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED` even when the post it names has by then left `UNDER_REVIEW` too. The decision carries a `decision` and a `decision_note`, the note being required but permitted to be the empty string, exactly as an audit note is. Crucially, **the deciding moderator may never be the moderator who opened the case**: the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the case's `opened_by_user_id`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. As with the audit duties of §3.1, the comparison is on the opaque caller identifier, not on the role: both parties are `Moderator`, and that is the point. A system-opened case, whose `opened_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`, can be decided by any moderator, since no human ever matches that value. On acceptance the case moves `OPEN → DECIDED` with `decided_by_user_id` set to the acting caller, `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` advanced by 1.

Four decisions are possible. `UPHELD_PUBLISH` returns the post `UNDER_REVIEW → PUBLISHED`, clears `auto_flag_reason`, and runs the fan-out **now** — the deferred fan-out, because a post that never reached the feed at creation alerted nobody then and must alert its neighbours the moment it is vindicated, rewriting all three alert counters. `REQUEST_REVISION` on a post whose `revision_count` is still below the ceiling of 2 sends it `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`; the counter is incremented by the author's revision, not by this decision. `REMOVE_INACCURATE` and `REMOVE_DISCRIMINATORY` both drive the post `UNDER_REVIEW → REMOVED`, which is terminal. So does `REQUEST_REVISION` on a post that has already used both its revision laps: a third request for revision is not a reprieve, it is a removal, and it is charged to the author as an accuracy strike.

The author revises a post that is in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and only the author may do so; a post in any other state is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_REVISABLE`, and an author whose privileges have since been suspended is refused with `ERR_POSTING_SUSPENDED`. The revision replaces the `body` and the four classifier signals, re-runs the score, and increments `revision_count` by 1 whichever branch it then takes. It is a replacement and not a partial update: `body` is required, and each of the four signals falls back to its declared default of 0 when omitted or sent as null rather than retaining the value the post already carried, so a revision naming none of them scores 0 and returns to the feed. No other field is revisable — neither `category`, nor `alert_fanout_cap`, nor the incident position. If the new score is below 0.49 the post returns `REVISION_REQUESTED → PUBLISHED`, `auto_flag_reason` is cleared, and a fresh fan-out batch is emitted with the three alert counters rewritten from scratch against the post's own unchanged position and whatever alert radii, home positions and account statuses the neighbourhood now holds. If the new score is 0.49 or above the post drops `REVISION_REQUESTED → FLAGGED` with `auto_flag_reason` set back to `DISCRIMINATION_SCORE` and no fan-out occurs at all, because the post is not in the feed. That is the second lap of the cycle, and it can be moderated again.

Removal triggers a five-level author-accountability cascade, and the levels must run in order because each is caused by the one above it. **L1** increments one strike counter on the author: `discrimination_strike_count` for a `REMOVE_DISCRIMINATORY` decision, `accuracy_strike_count` for a `REMOVE_INACCURATE` decision and equally for the over-limit `REQUEST_REVISION`. **L2** recomputes `total_strike_score` as `accuracy_strike_count` plus twice `discrimination_strike_count` — a discrimination strike weighs double. **L3** re-derives `post_privilege_state` from that score against two inclusive thresholds: a score of 3 or more is `POSTING_SUSPENDED`, a score of 2 is `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and anything below is `UNRESTRICTED`. One discrimination removal therefore lands a resident straight in `REVIEW_REQUIRED` at a score of 2, a second takes them to 4 and `POSTING_SUSPENDED`, while two accuracy removals are needed to reach `REVIEW_REQUIRED` — and the score of exactly 3 is reachable only by mixing one discrimination strike with one accuracy strike. **L4** fires only when L3 has just moved the author into `POSTING_SUSPENDED`, and only then: every **other** post by that author that is currently in `PUBLISHED` and whose `created_at` is no earlier than 30 days before the current logical-clock instant moves `PUBLISHED → UNDER_REVIEW` with `auto_flag_reason` set to `AUTHOR_UNDER_REVIEW`, processed in ascending `created_at`. Posts older than the 30-day lookback are left alone, as are posts already in any state other than `PUBLISHED`. **L5** follows from L4: one moderation case is auto-created for each swept post, in the same ascending `created_at` order, in state `OPEN` with `decision` `NONE`, `opened_reason` `AUTHOR_SUSPENDED`, `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`, and `post_discrimination_score_at_open` snapshotted from that swept post's own `discrimination_score` exactly as a hand-opened case would snapshot it. Every entity the cascade touches has its `version` advanced by 1. A removal that leaves the author below the suspension threshold stops after L3, creating no cases and sweeping no posts.

### 3.9. Transparency Reporting and Certification

The portal's public accountability artefact is the transparency report: a per-agency tally of what that agency asked for over a reporting window and how the neighbourhood answered. A `PlatformAdmin` compiles a report against an `agency_id`, a `period_start_utc` and a `period_end_utc`, and the `expected_agency_version` they observed. The window is half-open, inclusive of its start and exclusive of its end, and its start must be strictly before its end or the compilation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` and HTTP 422. Unlike a canvass area's footage window, a reporting period has no length cap and may lie wholly in the past or straddle the current clock instant. The agency must exist, or the compilation is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, and it must also currently be `ONBOARDED`: a report may not be compiled for an agency still in `PENDING_REVIEW`, nor for one that is `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, and that refusal is `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_ONBOARDED` sitting at its own position 9 in §2.9 — ahead of both the optimistic-concurrency check on `expected_agency_version` and the window check, so an agency in the wrong posture is reported as such in preference to either of those.

Compilation is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the agency, because it writes the agency's `latest_report_id`: a stale `expected_agency_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_AGENCY_VERSION` and HTTP 412. On success the new report's identifier becomes the agency's `latest_report_id` and the agency's `version` advances by 1. The report records `compiled_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` — an opaque caller identifier, never an entity identifier, and never dereferenced.

The scope of every count on the report is fixed the same way: it is the set of consent solicitations belonging to video requests whose `agency_id` matches the report's agency and whose `submitted_at` falls inside the half-open window. `requests_submitted_count` is the number of those in-window requests. `solicitations_dispatched_count` is the number of solicitations belonging to them, irrespective of status. `suppressed_dispatch_count` is how many of those had `notification_suppressed` true. `shared_count`, `declined_count`, `expired_count` and `withdrawn_count` count the in-scope solicitations sitting in `SHARED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` respectively, and `pending_count` counts those still in `SOLICITED`. `opt_out_excluded_count` counts the in-scope solicitations whose resident **currently** has `data_sharing_opt_out` true — a live reading of today's opt-out flag against a historical window, which is deliberate. `purged_clip_count` counts the shared clips belonging to those in-scope requests whose `status` is `PURGED`.

Both share-rate branches are computed at compilation so that the divergence between them is on the record before anybody certifies anything. Both are basis-point figures computed exactly as §2.7 describes, and both draw numerator and denominator from **the same** inclusion set, so neither can ever exceed 10000. What differs is only which solicitations enter that set — and that is what makes the two figures diverge on identical data.

`provisional_share_rate_bps` includes **every** in-scope solicitation: rows still `SOLICITED`, rows `WITHDRAWN`, and rows whose notification was suppressed by the fatigue controls all count in the denominator while contributing nothing to the numerator unless they are `SHARED`. Unresolved and undelivered solicitations therefore dilute the figure, which is the honest reading of "how much of what we asked for did we get".

`certified_share_rate_bps` includes only solicitations that both actually reached a resident **and** actually resolved: `status` in `SHARED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`, **and** `notification_suppressed` false. A suppressed notification never gave the owner a chance to answer and a `SOLICITED` or `WITHDRAWN` row never got an answer, so all of those drop out of the denominator. Note carefully that a solicitation in `SHARED` whose notification was suppressed drops out of the **numerator** as well: the certified rate is not merely the provisional rate over a smaller denominator, and it may land on either side of it. In both branches a denominator of zero yields 0, a documented outcome and not an error.

`published_share_rate_bps` is the figure the report puts its name to. It is `provisional_share_rate_bps` while the report's `variant` is `PROVISIONAL` and `certified_share_rate_bps` once the `variant` is `CERTIFIED`; a freshly compiled report is `PROVISIONAL` and publishes the provisional figure.

Certification is a one-way move `PROVISIONAL → CERTIFIED` performed by a `PlatformAdmin`, and it requires a non-empty `certification_note` — an absent, null, empty or wholly-whitespace note is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, the note being tested for emptiness after its surrounding whitespace is trimmed away, just as a `statutory_basis` is, yet stored exactly as it arrived and untrimmed. Unlike compilation, certification does not consult the owning agency at all: it neither requires the agency to be `ONBOARDED` nor touches the agency row, so a report may be certified for an agency that has since been suspended or revoked. A report that is already `CERTIFIED` is terminal and any further attempt is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`. **The certifying admin may not be the admin who compiled the report**: the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against `compiled_by_user_id`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both parties hold the same role, so once again it is the identity, not the role, that separates the duty.

Certification recomputes **both** branches over the same window rather than trusting the figures frozen at compilation, so a report certified after further solicitations have resolved publishes the current arithmetic and not the stale one. The recomputation is the whole tally and not merely the two rates: every count on the report — `requests_submitted_count`, `solicitations_dispatched_count`, `suppressed_dispatch_count`, the four resolution buckets, `pending_count`, `opt_out_excluded_count` and `purged_clip_count` — is rebuilt from today's rows over that same half-open window, so a request submitted into the window after compilation, or a clip purged since, is reflected in the certified report. It then sets `variant` to `CERTIFIED`, repoints `published_share_rate_bps` at `certified_share_rate_bps`, records the `certification_note`, stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock and `certified_by_user_id` from the acting caller, and advances the report's `version` by 1.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the `/neighborhood-safety` prefix, with the single exception of the clock control described in §2.5. Endpoints that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201; endpoints that modify, transition or read an existing resource respond with HTTP 200. Note that sharing footage responds with 201 because it creates a shared clip, while declining, purging, flagging, revising and every other transition responds with 200. Errors follow the catalogue in §5, and every response — success or failure — is wrapped in the envelope of §2.2.

Collection endpoints accept the pagination parameters `limit` and `offset` described in §2.3 alongside their own optional filters, all combined conjunctively. The officer collection filters on `agency_id` and `duty_role`. A resident's dispatch collection filters on `kind` and `suppressed`, and is ordered by ascending `dispatched_at` and then ascending `batch_sequence_no`. A resident's data-control collection filters on `action_type`. The camera collection filters on `owner_resident_id`, `status` and `auto_decline_requests`. The canvass-area collection filters on `agency_id`, `created_by_officer_id` and `status`. The video-request collection filters on `agency_id`, `request_area_id`, `submitting_officer_id`, `auditor_officer_id`, `lifecycle_state` and `compliance_state`. The solicitation collection filters on `video_request_id`, `device_id`, `resident_id`, `status` and `notification_suppressed`, and is ordered by ascending `created_at` and then ascending `dispatch_rank` — which within any single request, whose solicitations were all created at one clock instant, is exactly ascending `dispatch_rank`. The clip collection filters on `consent_solicitation_id`, `video_request_id`, `device_id`, `owner_resident_id`, `status` and `retention_variant`. The post collection filters on `author_resident_id`, `parent_post_id`, `state` and `category`. The moderation-case collection filters on `resident_post_id`, `state`, `decision` and `opened_reason`, and is ordered by ascending `created_at`. The report collection filters on `agency_id` and `variant`.

The order in which competing errors are chosen is fixed by the priority ladder in §2.9, not by this table.
