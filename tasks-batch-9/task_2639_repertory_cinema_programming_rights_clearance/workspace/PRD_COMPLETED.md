# Repertory Cinema Programming & Rights Clearance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A single-screen art house lives or dies by the arithmetic of one auditorium. Every slot given to a first-run art film is a slot taken from a repertory series; every licence that lapses un-cleared is a printed calendar that has to be reprinted; every distributor engagement carries a minimum-run promise that the box office must actually deliver before settlement day. Today the programming team runs this on spreadsheets, a shared inbox for distributor counter-proposals, a whiteboard for print traffic, and a separate ticketing system that knows nothing about rights windows. The consequence is predictable: screenings get scheduled onto licences that expired last week, KDMs arrive cut for the wrong validity window, minimum-run shortfalls surface only when the distributor's invoice disagrees with ours, and donors who paid for advance access find the seats already gone.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces all of it. The service owns the programming calendar, the distributor negotiation log, the repertory rights clearances, the print-traffic and digital-cinema-package pipeline, the membership and donor programme, the box office, and the distributor settlement ledger — as one consistent world in which a screening cannot exist unless a confirmed engagement, a cleared licence, an ingested package and a free screen block all agree that it may. The negotiation surface is deliberately conversational: engagements are opened with an offer, answered with counters, and confirmed only by the Executive Director, with every round retained as an append-only log. The scheduling surface is deliberately arithmetic: the service will place a screening itself, scoring every legal quarter-hour slot on a target date against prime-time value, spacing quality, fragmentation cost and the venue's audience-development target for repertory programming.

Two design constraints run through everything that follows. First, **time is logical, not real**: every window, expiry, sweep and advance-access decision is evaluated against a server-held logical clock that an Executive Director sets explicitly, so that a season can be rehearsed end to end without waiting for it. Second, **money is integral**: all monetary amounts are whole cents, and every rounding step in the document names both its position in the evaluation order and its rounding direction, because a settlement that disagrees with the distributor's by one cent is a settlement that has to be argued about.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every business resource in this service is addressed at the URL root — there is no nesting of one resource under another, and foreign keys travel in request bodies rather than in path segments. The one exception is the negotiation log of an engagement, which is read as a sub-collection of its booking. All timestamps that cross the wire, in either direction, are ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). That three-digit form is what the server always emits; on the way in the server is liberal, accepting any parseable ISO 8601 instant and normalising it to UTC — including one carrying an explicit non-zero offset such as `+02:00`, one carrying no zone marker at all (which is read as UTC), and one carrying fewer or more than three fractional digits — and a supplied precision finer than a millisecond is truncated rather than rounded when the instant is echoed back. Calendar dates cross the wire as plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings and venue-local times of day as `HH:MM` strings, both in exactly that width.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request identifies its caller with two headers. `X-User-Role` names the role the caller is acting as, and `X-User-ID` carries the caller's identifier. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent — or present but empty or whitespace-only, which is treated identically to absent — is treated as an anonymous walk-up caller and is admitted only on endpoints that explicitly list the anonymous role; a request whose `X-User-Role` header names a role that is not one of the seven roles below is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), and that rejection stands even on an endpoint that would have admitted an anonymous caller. A syntactically valid role that is simply not permitted on the addressed endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403), as is a request to an endpoint governed by segregation of duties that arrives without an `X-User-ID` header or with one that is empty or whitespace-only.

The seven roles are `Programmer`, `RightsManager`, `TechnicalManager`, `BoxOfficeManager`, `ExecutiveDirector`, `Member` and `Anonymous`. They are not a hierarchy: `ExecutiveDirector` is the sign-off authority but does **not** inherit the `Programmer`'s right to lodge negotiation rounds or schedule screenings, nor the `TechnicalManager`'s booth operations.

- A `Programmer` programmes the calendar: cataloguing titles, opening and negotiating engagements, scheduling screenings both by hand and through the optimiser, and planning special events.
- A `RightsManager` owns repertory rights clearance — lodging licences, re-lodging them after a change request, clearing them, sending them back for amendment, and revoking them.
- A `TechnicalManager` runs print traffic, package ingest, KDM key issue and revocation, and the booth playback test.
- A `BoxOfficeManager` runs ticketing, opens and closes sales on a screening, maintains membership records and donations, and drafts and submits settlements.
- An `ExecutiveDirector` owns the venue record and the logical clock, confirms and closes and withdraws engagements, confirms special-event productions, sweeps lapsed memberships, and finalises and resolves settlements.
- A `Member` is a cardholder acting on their own behalf.
- `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated walk-up purchaser or calendar browser.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's member foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a `Member` may read only their own membership record and their own ticket orders, and any order they create must carry a `member_id` equal to their own `id`. Failing one of those ownership comparisons is an authorisation refusal and not a not-found or a schema one: the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Where the member is named in the request body, as it is when a `Member` sells themselves seats, the comparison is made as soon as that `member_id` field has been read — ahead of the remaining body fields, ahead of resolving any foreign key, and ahead of every state, entitlement, seat, comp and credit check further down the ladder of §2.7; where the record is instead named by a path segment, the comparison is made once that path identifier has resolved to a row; and where the member appears as a list filter, the comparison is made against the supplied filter value. When `X-User-Role` is `Programmer`, `RightsManager`, `TechnicalManager`, `BoxOfficeManager`, `ExecutiveDirector` or `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it wherever a transition declares an actor field — `created_by_user_id` and `confirmed_by_user_id` on an engagement, `submitted_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a clearance, `submitted_by_user_id` on a negotiation round, `submitted_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on a settlement — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

One rule turns those opaque identifiers into a control. Clearing a rights licence is under **segregation of duties**: the `X-User-ID` that clears a licence must differ from the `X-User-ID` recorded as having lodged it. A caller who tries to clear their own submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), even though the role itself is permitted.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits; it is minted afresh for every response, error responses included, and is never repeated from the request.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in this service read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4: it bears no relation to the `created_at`/`updated_at` stamps on the resource in `data`, and two requests made at the same logical instant carry different `api_request_timestamp` values.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A supplied `limit` above 100, below 1, or of the wrong type is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a supplied `offset` below 0 or of the wrong type; `offset` has no upper bound, and either parameter omitted altogether or supplied as an empty string takes its documented default. The same discipline governs the filter parameters: a boolean filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` case-insensitively and an instant filter only a parseable ISO 8601 instant, anything else being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas a well-formed filter value that simply matches no row — an unknown id, or a status string no row holds — yields an empty page rather than an error. Paging parameters are validated after the addressed path has resolved, so on the one list nested under a path identifier a bad `limit` accompanying an unknown parent id is reported as that parent's not-found error.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless an endpoint's description says otherwise; because audit stamps are read from the logical clock (§2.4), rows frequently share a `created_at`, and rows sharing one are ordered by the sequence in which they were created, oldest first. The negotiation-round log of an engagement is likewise oldest-first, and the same oldest-first ordering governs any list of ids a batch capability returns, such as the swept membership ids of §3.7. For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond this page). Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Auditing and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation; at creation it equals `created_at`. Both of those instants are stamped from the logical clock described in the next paragraph rather than from the host's wall clock, so a mutation performed without the clock having been moved leaves `updated_at` still exactly equal to `created_at` even though the row's `version` has advanced. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations produced by a cascade rather than by a direct call to that resource. All thirteen resource types — auditoriums, distributors, titles, engagements, negotiation rounds, rights clearances, print/package assets, KDM keys, screenings, special events, ticket orders, members and settlements — carry all four. A read is never a mutation: it advances neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and that holds even on the endpoints whose response is re-derived from the clock while it is being rendered, such as a membership record whose standing is recomputed on every read.

Time in this service is a value the server holds, not a value it reads from the host. The logical clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real wall-clock time. An `ExecutiveDirector` may move it forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, whose response is not a resource at all but an object carrying the single field `current_utc` holding the instant just set; any parseable instant is accepted, whether it lies ahead of or behind the clock's present reading. Every rule in this document that speaks of "the clock" — licence-window expiry, offer expiry, key validity, ingest-window readiness, advance-access windows, the membership sweep, and the guard that a screening may not be scheduled into the past — reads that logical instant.

### 2.5. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents; the service never stores or returns fractional cents. Wherever a computation would produce a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and each formula in Section 3 names every position at which that rounding happens, because the positions are not interchangeable. Two share values, `distributor_share_pct` and `discount_pct`, are exact decimal fractions between 0 and 1 inclusive and are never rounded themselves; `proration_factor` is likewise carried as an exact fraction and only the product it multiplies is rounded.

Durations are whole minutes and are never rounded: a screening's on-screen length, the venue's `trailer_reel_minutes`, its `turnaround_minutes`, its `min_useful_gap_minutes` and a special event's `extra_minutes` are all integer minute counts. Seat counts, screening allowances, playing-day counts, negotiation-round counts and slot scores are integers. Instant comparisons are performed on the parsed instants themselves — at millisecond resolution for the three-fractional-digit form the wire uses, and at whatever finer resolution a client happened to supply.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Negotiation, Licences and Settlement

Four write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a **parent** resource, and on each of them the version field is a required, non-optional element of the request body. No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field.

- `POST /bookings/{id}/counter` must carry `expected_booking_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target booking. If that value does not equal the booking's current `version`, the counter is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Lodging a counter is legitimately repeatable — it is valid from both the offered and the countered state — so without this guard a caller answering a round that another writer has already superseded would silently succeed against a negotiation it has not seen.
- `POST /settlements` must carry `expected_booking_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the booking being settled. A mismatch with the booking's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A fresh draft settlement legitimately supersedes an earlier draft on the same engagement, so the guard is what catches the caller whose view of the closed engagement's playing days and shortfall penalty predates another writer's draft.
- `POST /showtimes` must carry `expected_clearance_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the rights clearance the screening will draw down. A mismatch with the clearance's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLEARANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Many screenings legitimately draw on one clearance, so a repeat call would otherwise succeed; the guard catches the caller whose remaining-screenings figure predates another writer's draw-down or a cancellation's restore.
- `POST /showtimes/auto-schedule` carries the same `expected_clearance_version` guard, with the same `ERR_STALE_CLEARANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rejection. The optimiser reads both the clearance's remaining allowance and the day's existing occupancy before it mints a slot, so a caller holding an out-of-date clearance version is refused before any slot is created.

### 2.7. The Validation Ladder

Checks are evaluated in one strictly ascending order, and **the first violated check wins**: once a rule fails, the request is rejected with that rule's error and no later rule is evaluated. This matters wherever a request is wrong in more than one way, because only one error identifier can be returned. The ladder, in order, is:

1. Authentication — the role header is present and names a declared role (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Authorisation — the named role appears in the addressed endpoint's permitted list, and `X-User-ID` is present where segregation of duties applies (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`).
3. Schema — on every write endpoint: the body parses as JSON, every required field is present and non-null, every field matches its declared type, every enumerated value is a declared member, every timestamp is ISO 8601 UTC, every amount in cents and every minute, day and seat count is a non-negative integer, `quantity` lies between 1 and 10 inclusive, and any interval's end is strictly after its start (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). Three details of this step are fixed. A write endpoint called with no body at all, or with an empty one, is read as having been called with an empty JSON object, so what is reported is the first required field found missing; a body that is not parseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A required string field must be a string that is still non-empty once surrounding whitespace is trimmed. And the type check is strict in both directions between booleans and integers: a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared, and an integer is never accepted where a boolean is, though an integer *is* accepted where one of the exact decimal share fractions is declared.
4. Existence — every id in the path and every foreign key in the payload resolves to an existing row of the declared type, yielding the per-entity not-found error for whichever reference failed.
5. Concurrency — on the four operations listed in §2.6, the supplied expected version equals the parent's current `version`, yielding that rule's declared stale-version error.
6. State — the addressed resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, yielding the per-entity invalid-state error. A terminal state admits nothing. This covers, among others, an engagement that is neither offered nor countered when a counter arrives, a negotiation round that is no longer pending when a confirmation arrives, a clearance that is not in the submitted state when a clear arrives, a print asset asked to skip a step in the traffic chain, a key that is not issued when a revoke arrives, a screening that is not scheduled when an order, a completion or a cancellation arrives, an order that is not reserved when a payment arrives, an order that is not paid when a refund arrives, and a settlement that is not a draft when a submission arrives.
7. Rights state — a screening may only be created against a clearance in the cleared state; a clearance that is submitted, awaiting changes, expired or revoked cannot carry a screening (`ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_CLEARED`). Applies to both the manual scheduler and the optimiser.
8. Licence window — the screening's venue-local date lies inside the clearance's licensed window, both ends inclusive (`ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_NOT_COVERED`). Applies to both schedulers.
9. Screening allowance — the referenced clearance has at least one screening remaining (`ERR_SCREENING_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`). Applies to both schedulers.
10. Format compatibility — formats agree the whole way down the chain (`ERR_FORMAT_INCOMPATIBLE`). Applies to both schedulers and to the creation of a print/package asset.
11. Dependency state — the booth test requires an ingested package and opening sales requires a confirmed or running engagement (`ERR_DEPENDENCY_STATE_INVALID`). Applies to the booth playback test and to opening sales.
12. Key coverage — the booth test requires an issued key covering the screening (`ERR_NO_COVERING_KDM`).
13. Segregation of duties — the caller clearing a licence is not the caller who lodged it (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Applies to clearing a licence.
14. Screen collision — the candidate screen block does not collide with another non-cancelled screening at the venue (`ERR_SCREEN_SLOT_CONFLICT`). Applies to both schedulers and to confirming a special event.
15. Key-window disjointness — a new key's closed validity interval is strictly disjoint from every other issued key for the same package-and-venue pair, and its start is strictly before its end (`ERR_KDM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`).
16. Ingest-lane capacity — admitting the new ingest window does not push concurrency past the venue's lane count at any evaluated instant (`ERR_INGEST_LANE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).
17. Showtime window — the venue-local start time lies inside the venue's operating start window, the venue-local date lies inside the engagement's inclusive date range, and the start instant is not earlier than the clock (`ERR_SHOWTIME_WINDOW_INVALID`). Applies to both schedulers.
18. Advance access — a purchase before public sales open is admitted only inside the buyer's advance window (`ERR_ADVANCE_ACCESS_NOT_OPEN`).
19. Seats — the requested seats fit in what remains (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`).
20. Comp allowance — comps fit inside the order and inside the member's remaining allowance, and a non-member order carries none (`ERR_COMP_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`).
21. Negotiation rounds — the engagement has not exhausted its round budget (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`).
22. Weekly balance — the addition keeps the venue's ISO-week first-run share inside its ceiling (`ERR_WEEKLY_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`). Applies to both schedulers.
23. Eligible slot — the optimiser's candidate pool is non-empty after filtering (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SLOT`).
24. Reference agreement — the referenced rows agree with one another (`ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`). Applies to both schedulers, key issue, clearance creation and the superseded-engagement reference on a newly opened engagement; drafting a settlement and selling a ticket resolve their references but impose no cross-row agreement of their own, so neither can ever return this identifier.
25. Sales open — the screening's sale dimension admits orders (`ERR_SHOWTIME_SALES_CLOSED`).
26. Settlement permission — the engagement is closed and no conflicting settlement already exists (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_PERMITTED`).
27. Credit honesty — credit redemption is only accepted with a member and only against a real balance (`ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`).
28. Offer liveness — the round being accepted has not expired (`ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`).
29. House-nut cap — the accepted round's proposed house nut does not exceed the board-approved weekly cap (`ERR_HOUSE_NUT_ABOVE_CAP`).
30. Guarantee requirement — a distributor demanding an advance guarantee gets a non-zero one (`ERR_GUARANTEE_REQUIRED`). Applies to opening an engagement, lodging a counter round and lodging a fresh cinema round.
31. Member email uniqueness — the email is not already held (`ERR_MEMBER_EMAIL_TAKEN`).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright. Because rights state (7), licence window (8) and allowance (9) all outrank screen collision (14), a screening request that is both unlicensed and colliding is reported as a licensing failure, not a scheduling one. And because seats (19) outranks the comp allowance (20) and the sales-open check (25), an order for more seats than exist on a screening whose sales are closed is reported as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`.

### 2.8. Venue-Local Time and the Programming Week

Instants are stored and exchanged in UTC, but the calendar is a local artefact. Each auditorium record carries `utc_offset_minutes`, a fixed offset (default 0) that is added to a UTC instant to obtain the venue-local date and time; every rule in this document phrased in terms of "the venue-local date" or "the venue-local time" derives it that way, and no daylight-saving arithmetic is applied. Licence windows, engagement windows, playing-day counts and the auditorium's own operating hours are all expressed in venue-local terms, whereas screen occupancy, key validity and ingest windows are compared as raw instants.

The auditorium's operating day is bounded by `open_local_time` and `last_start_local_time`, both venue-local `HH:MM` strings. A screening's advertised start must fall on or after the former and on or before the latter; the block is permitted to end after `last_start_local_time`, and it is permitted to run past midnight, in which case its occupancy still collides with the next day's first show, because occupancy is compared as instants and not as same-day clock readings.

The programming week is the ISO week: the venue-local span running from Monday at `00:00:00.000` through Sunday at `23:59:59.999` that contains the screening's start. Two balance rules key off it. The first is a ceiling: `max_first_run_share` (default 0.60) caps the share of a week's non-cancelled screenings that may be first-run. Concretely, when a candidate screening is evaluated, the service counts the venue's existing screenings in that ISO week whose status is not cancelled, adds one for the candidate to obtain the prospective total, counts how many of those existing screenings carry the `FIRST_RUN` strand and adds one more if the candidate itself is `FIRST_RUN`, and rejects the candidate with `ERR_WEEKLY_BALANCE_EXCEEDED` when the prospective first-run count is strictly greater than the prospective total multiplied by `max_first_run_share` and then rounded **up** to a whole number. The ceiling is applied to the product, never to the share, which makes the boundary directly observable: at a share of 0.60, one first-run screening out of one is admitted, two out of two are admitted, and the third first-run screening in a week of three is refused. The second is a target rather than a limit: `weekly_repertory_target` (default 6) is the number of `REPERTORY_SERIES` screenings the house wants per ISO week, and it influences only the optimiser's slot scoring — falling short of it never rejects anything.

### 2.9. Interval Geometry: Screen Blocks, Key Windows and Ingest Lanes

Three different intervals govern three different scarce resources, and their boundary conventions deliberately differ. Getting them confused is the single easiest way to produce a subtly wrong calendar.

**Screen occupancy is half-open.** A screening occupies the screen from its `start_at` up to but not including its `blocked_until`. Two screenings therefore collide when the candidate's start is strictly before the other's `blocked_until` **and** the other's start is strictly before the candidate's `blocked_until`; a candidate whose `start_at` falls exactly on an existing screening's `blocked_until` is legal, back-to-back programming. Because the reset buffer is appended to the tail only, a candidate that ends exactly when another begins is legal only if the candidate's whole block including its buffer finishes at or before the other's start.

**Key validity is closed.** An issued key covers the closed interval from its `valid_from` through its `valid_until` inclusive, and two issued keys for the same package-and-venue pair must be **strictly disjoint**: a candidate whose `valid_from` falls exactly on another issued key's `valid_until` is an overlap and is refused. Unlike screenings, keys may not touch.

**Ingest windows are closed and aggregated.** A package's ingest window runs from `ingest_window_start_at` through `ingest_window_end_at` inclusive, and overlap alone is not an error: the venue has `dcp_ingest_lane_count` parallel lanes, and only the window that would make more than that many windows concurrent at some instant is refused. Because both endpoints are inclusive, a window beginning at exactly the instant another ends counts as concurrent at that instant.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline, Recomputation and Conservation Invariants

Many operations in this service touch more than the resource they address, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Whenever a cascade mutates a row — a screening cancelled because a licence was revoked, an order voided because a key was pulled, a member's credit balance restored because a paid order was reversed — that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. Two refinements of that rule are directly observable. First, the unit of accounting is the operation and not the hop: a row advances its `version` by exactly one per request, however many hops of one cascade reach it, so a licence that a withdrawal revokes at one hop and recomputes the allowance of at a later hop ends the request one version higher, not two. Second, a row that a cascade merely recomputes is only touched when a stored field actually moves — a screening rollup, a licence's `screenings_used`/`screenings_remaining` pair or an engagement's `scheduled_showtime_count` that recomputes to the value it already held leaves that row's `version` and `updated_at` alone, and so does a released hold that had no credit and no comps to return, whose member is therefore not touched at all; a cascade that reverses a *paid* order, by contrast, always touches the member that order names.

Aggregates are always recomputed from their source rows rather than adjusted blindly, so that no cascade can leave a drifting counter behind. A screening's `seats_sold` is the sum of `quantity` across its orders whose status is reserved or paid; its `seats_available` is the venue's `seat_capacity` less the venue's `house_hold_seats` less `seats_sold`, and never falls below zero; its `gross_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` across its paid orders and its `admissions_count` the sum of their `quantity`. Voided and refunded orders contribute to none of those four. After every mutation, including every cascade, the identity holds that `seats_sold` plus `seats_available` plus the venue's `house_hold_seats` equals the venue's `seat_capacity` — the house hold seats are permanently withheld from sale and are never sellable.

The same discipline governs licensing capacity. A clearance's `screenings_used` is the count of screenings referencing it whose status is scheduled or completed, and `screenings_remaining` is `max_screenings` less `screenings_used`, never negative; a cancelled screening releases its allowance. The two always sum to `max_screenings`. Likewise an engagement's `scheduled_showtime_count` is the count of its screenings whose status is scheduled or completed, maintained across every creation and cancellation and cascade.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Reference Agreement

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. When an operation addresses one resource and its payload names others, the addressed resource is resolved first, then each foreign key in the payload; the specific not-found error names the entity type that was missing — `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DCP_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KDM_KEY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOWTIME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPECIAL_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The offer-not-found error also covers the case where an operation needs the engagement's currently pending round and there is none.

Resolving individually is not enough: the referenced rows must also agree with one another, and a disagreement is reported as `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. A clearance, its engagement and any screening standing on it must all name the same title; a screening's package must be for the same title as the screening; a screening, its engagement and its package must all name the same auditorium; a key must be bound to the same auditorium as the screening it is expected to cover; a settlement must name the engagement whose screenings it is settling; and when a new engagement supersedes an earlier one, the superseded engagement must itself be withdrawn.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the House, the Trading Partners and the Catalogue

Before anything can be programmed, an `ExecutiveDirector` registers the auditorium. The record carries the house `name` and its `seat_capacity`, which must be at least 1, together with the booth and scheduling parameters that every later rule reads: `trailer_reel_minutes`, the fixed pre-show reel added to every screening's on-screen duration (zero or more); `turnaround_minutes`, the clean-and-reset buffer appended after a screening ends before the screen is free again (zero or more); `dcp_ingest_lane_count`, the number of parallel ingest lanes on the venue's digital cinema server, at least 1; `open_local_time` and `last_start_local_time`, the earliest and latest permitted venue-local start times, each a well-formed `HH:MM` reading between `00:00` and `23:59` but neither constrained relative to the other at registration — the pair is stored exactly as posted and no ordering check is applied to it; and `supported_formats`, a non-empty list drawn from `DCP_2K`, `DCP_4K`, `PRINT_35MM`, `PRINT_16MM` and `DIGITAL_FILE_PRORES` naming the projection formats the booth can actually run.

Six further auditorium parameters are optional at registration and take documented defaults when omitted. `house_hold_seats` defaults to 0 and names the seats permanently withheld from sale for press and guests; it must be zero or more and strictly less than `seat_capacity`. `min_useful_gap_minutes` defaults to 45 and is the gap below which a hole left in a day's schedule is treated as fragmentation rather than as breathing room. `utc_offset_minutes` defaults to 0 and is the one minute-valued parameter in this service that may legitimately be negative — a house west of UTC posts a negative offset — with no bound imposed on its magnitude. `max_first_run_share` defaults to 0.60 and must be greater than 0 and no more than 1. `weekly_repertory_target` defaults to 6 and must be zero or more. And `base_house_nut_weekly_cents` defaults to 480000 — four thousand eight hundred dollars a week — and is the board-approved ceiling on the weekly house nut: no engagement may ever be confirmed at a house nut above it. Reading one auditorium record is open to every role, including anonymous browsers, since the public calendar needs the house name and capacity.

Distributors and licensors are registered by a `Programmer` or an `ExecutiveDirector` with a trading `name`, a booking-desk `contact_email` — checked only for being a non-empty string, never for address shape — and the terms this counterparty normally demands: `default_terms_variant`, which is one of `SLIDING_SCALE_90_10`, `PERCENTAGE_FLOOR` or `FLAT_FEE`; `default_min_run_days`, at least 1 playing day; `default_distributor_share_pct`, a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive; and `requires_advance_guarantee`, a flag defaulting to false. That flag is a hard fiscal constraint rather than documentation: when a distributor demands an advance guarantee, every engagement opened against them and every negotiation round lodged on such an engagement must carry a guarantee amount strictly greater than zero, and a zero guarantee is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_REQUIRED`. The defaults themselves are advisory — the programmer may open an engagement on different terms — but the guarantee flag is not.

Titles are catalogued by a `Programmer` or an `ExecutiveDirector` with the `title_name` as it will be printed in the calendar, the original theatrical `release_year`, a `runtime_minutes` of at least 1 excluding trailers, an `is_first_run` flag distinguishing first-run art films from repertory and archival titles, a `native_format` drawn from the same five-format enumeration, and `rights_holder_distributor_id` naming the party that controls theatrical rights. The catalogue is browsable: the title list may be narrowed to a single rights holder by supplying `rights_holder_distributor_id`, or to first-run titles only by supplying `is_first_run`, in either case paged with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Negotiating an Engagement, Round by Round

An engagement begins as an offer, not as a draft. When a `Programmer` opens one, they name the `title_id` being engaged, the `distributor_id` acting as counterparty and the `venue_id` whose screen the run will occupy, and they state the opening terms: a `terms_variant` of `SLIDING_SCALE_90_10`, `PERCENTAGE_FLOOR` or `FLAT_FEE`; a `house_nut_cents` of zero or more; a `distributor_share_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive; a `guarantee_cents` of zero or more, which is the film-rent floor payable regardless of what the screenings actually gross; a `min_run_days` of at least 1, being the number of distinct venue-local playing days promised to the distributor; and the inclusive venue-local `engagement_start_date` and `engagement_end_date` bounding the permitted playing dates, the former no later than the latter. Optionally the payload carries `supersedes_booking_id`, a self-reference to an earlier engagement that this one replaces; that earlier engagement must exist and must itself already be in the `WITHDRAWN` state, and a target in any other state is refused as a reference disagreement.

Creation lands the engagement directly in `OFFERED` and simultaneously lodges negotiation round 1. That first round is a **BookingOffer** whose `booking_id` points back at the engagement, whose `round_index` is 1, whose `proposed_by` is `CINEMA`, and whose `proposed_house_nut_cents`, `proposed_distributor_share_pct`, `proposed_min_run_days` and `proposed_guarantee_cents` mirror the opening terms just stated. Its `outcome` starts at `PENDING` and its `expires_at` is set to its own `created_at` plus 72 hours of offer validity. The engagement's own counters initialise at the same moment: `round_count` is set to 1, `scheduled_showtime_count` to 0, `min_run_compliant` to false and `min_run_penalty_cents` to 0, `actual_playing_days` to 0, `created_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `confirmed_by_user_id` remains null until sign-off.

Negotiation then alternates. Recording the distributor's answer moves the engagement `OFFERED → COUNTERED`, and recording a further distributor answer to the cinema's own counter is a self-loop `COUNTERED → COUNTERED`; both are lodged by a `Programmer` and both require the stale-version guard described in §2.6. Each such round supersedes whatever round it answers: the engagement's currently `PENDING` round has its `outcome` flipped to `COUNTERED`, a new round is created with `round_index` one greater than the engagement's `round_count`, `proposed_by` set to `DISTRIBUTOR`, `outcome` `PENDING` and the four posted `proposed_*` figures, the engagement's `round_count` increases by one, and the engagement's `version` increments. Answering a distributor counter with a fresh cinema proposal runs the mirror image, moving the engagement `COUNTERED → OFFERED` with `proposed_by` set to `CINEMA`; that operation carries no version guard, because a second sequential attempt is already refused by the from-state. Both operations require that the engagement's latest round still be `PENDING` — a requirement evaluated after the stale-version guard where one applies and after the engagement's own from-state, so an engagement whose status admits neither operation is reported as `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE` even when no pending round survives on it either — and every round records `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. A round that has been superseded is immutable — its `outcome` never changes again.

The negotiation budget is four rounds. A counter may only be lodged while the engagement's `round_count` is strictly less than four, so the fourth round is the last that may ever be recorded and an attempt to lodge a fifth is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The whole log is readable oldest-first as a sub-collection of the engagement, paged with `limit` and `offset`, and the engagement collection itself may be filtered by `title_id`, `distributor_id`, `venue_id`, `supersedes_booking_id` and `status`.

Sign-off belongs to the `ExecutiveDirector` alone, and it accepts the currently `PENDING` round rather than re-stating terms. It is available only while the engagement is itself `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED`, and that from-state is tested ahead of every condition below, so a confirmation aimed at an engagement in any other state — a `CONFIRMED`, `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN` one, on which no round is pending either — is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. Three further conditions then guard it. The engagement's latest round must still be `PENDING`. The logical clock must not be later than that round's `expires_at`; if it is, the round's `outcome` is moved to `EXPIRED` and the confirmation is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. And the round's `proposed_house_nut_cents` must not exceed the auditorium's `base_house_nut_weekly_cents`, otherwise the confirmation is refused with `ERR_HOUSE_NUT_ABOVE_CAP`. When all three hold, the round's `outcome` becomes `ACCEPTED`, its four proposed figures overwrite the engagement's `house_nut_cents`, `distributor_share_pct`, `min_run_days` and `guarantee_cents` — so the negotiated result, not the opening ask, is what settlement will later be priced from — `confirmed_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the engagement moves `OFFERED → CONFIRMED` or `COUNTERED → CONFIRMED` depending on which side lodged the accepted round.

Once confirmed, an engagement advances to `RUNNING` by itself. Nobody calls for it: the first screening on the engagement to reach completion bubbles the engagement `CONFIRMED → RUNNING`, changing no other field but incrementing the engagement's `version`. From either `CONFIRMED` or `RUNNING` the engagement can be closed, which is described in §3.9.

Withdrawal is the destructive exit, available to the `ExecutiveDirector` from `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RUNNING`, and it unwinds everything standing downstream in four hops. The caller supplies a `cancel_reason` recording why the run is being pulled; it is recorded against the engagement itself and is **not** propagated onto the screenings the cascade cancels, which take the fixed reason named at hop three instead. **Hop one:** every negotiation round on the engagement whose `outcome` is `PENDING` becomes `REJECTED`, and the engagement itself becomes `WITHDRAWN`. **Hop two:** because the engagement is gone, every rights clearance attached to it whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `CLEARED` becomes `REVOKED` with its `revoked_reason` set to `BOOKING_WITHDRAWN`. **Hop three:** because those licences are revoked, every screening standing on them whose status is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `CLEARANCE_REVOKED` and its `sale_status` forced to `SALES_CLOSED`. **Hop four:** because those screenings are cancelled, their ticket orders unwind — orders in `RESERVED` become `VOID`, orders in `PAID` become `REFUNDED` with `refund_destination` set to `MEMBER_CREDIT` when the order carries a `member_id` and `CASH` when it does not, each affected member's `credit_balance_cents` and `comp_tickets_remaining` are restored accordingly, and each affected clearance's `screenings_used` and `screenings_remaining` are recomputed from the surviving screenings. Every row touched at every hop has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

### 3.3. Clearing Repertory Rights

A repertory title cannot be screened on the strength of a booking alone; the exhibition licence has to be cleared, and clearing it is a two-person job. A `RightsManager` lodges the licence against an engagement, naming the `title_id` whose exhibition rights are being cleared and the `booking_id` the licence attaches to, the `licensor_name` of the archive, estate or distributor granting it, a `license_fee_cents` of zero or more as the flat licensing fee payable — a recorded obligation only, which like the guest honorarium of §3.6 enters no settlement figure — the licensed `territory` code such as `US-NY-METRO`, the inclusive venue-local `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` bounding permitted exhibition dates, a `max_screenings` of at least 1 as the contractual ceiling on licensed screenings, and the `source_format` the licence actually covers. Lodging requires that the engagement not be in the `WITHDRAWN` state, that `window_start_date` be no later than `window_end_date`, and that `source_format` either equal the title's `native_format` or be one of the auditorium's `supported_formats`. Creation *is* submission: the licence appears directly in `SUBMITTED`, its `submitted_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID` as the segregation-of-duties subject, its `screenings_used` at 0 and its `screenings_remaining` equal to `max_screenings`, with `cleared_by_user_id`, `change_request_note` and `revoked_reason` all null.

Licences frequently go back and forth with the licensor, and the revision loop is explicit. A `RightsManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector` may send a `SUBMITTED` licence back for amendment, which moves it `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and stores the supplied `change_request_note`. Re-lodging it after the licensor answers moves it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and overwrites `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — re-lodging resets the segregation subject, so whoever re-submitted becomes the person who may no longer clear it. The `change_request_note` that sent the licence back is left in place rather than cleared on re-lodging, as the standing record of what was amended. The loop may run as many times as the licensor needs.

Clearance itself moves the licence `SUBMITTED → CLEARED` and is available to a `RightsManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector`, subject to two conditions beyond the from-state. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the logical clock's venue-local date must be no later than `window_end_date`; a licence whose window has already run out can no longer be cleared and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Where both conditions fail at once — the caller who lodged the licence trying to clear it after the window has run out — it is the window that is reported, because that state refusal is evaluated ahead of the segregation one, exactly as the ladder of §2.7 orders them. On success `cleared_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`.

A cleared licence can still lapse, and it lapses lazily — there is no background job. When someone tries to place a screening on a licence whose `window_end_date` is already behind the clock's venue-local date, the licence is moved `CLEARED → EXPIRED` at that moment and, because the licence is then no longer in the cleared state, the screening request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_CLEARED`. The state flip persists even though the request failed. Both schedulers behave identically in this respect, and they are the only callers that discover the lapse: reading or listing a licence never flips it, so a `CLEARED` row whose window has passed keeps reporting `CLEARED` until a scheduler is pointed at it.

Revocation is the licensor pulling the rights, and it is available from `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `CLEARED` to a `RightsManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector`. **Hop one** records the supplied `revoked_reason` and moves the licence to `REVOKED`. **Hop two:** because the licence is gone, every screening carrying it whose status is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `CLEARANCE_REVOKED`, and each such screening's `sale_status` is forced to `SALES_CLOSED`. **Hop three:** because those screenings are cancelled, their `RESERVED` orders become `VOID` and their `PAID` orders become `REFUNDED`, restoring each affected member's `credit_balance_cents` and `comp_tickets_remaining`, and the owning engagement's `scheduled_showtime_count` is recomputed. Revoking a licence that never carried a screening — the usual case from `SUBMITTED` — completes the same cascade with nothing to do at hops two and three. Licences are readable individually with their live allowance, and the collection may be filtered by `title_id`, `booking_id` and `status`.

### 3.4. Print Traffic, Ingest Lanes, Keys and the Booth

Physical prints and digital packages arrive on a schedule of their own, and the `TechnicalManager` owns that pipeline. Booking an inbound asset names the `title_id` the package or print carries, the `source_distributor_id` shipping it, the `venue_id` whose ingest lanes the window will occupy, the `format` of this particular asset, an `is_encrypted` flag stating whether playback will require a KDM, an optional `carrier_ref` holding the print-traffic waybill or courier reference, and the booked server ingest window as `ingest_window_start_at` and `ingest_window_end_at`, the latter strictly after the former. The asset's own `format` must be one of the destination auditorium's `supported_formats`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FORMAT_INCOMPATIBLE`. The asset is created in `REQUESTED` with `received_at` and `ingested_at` both null.

Lane capacity is the interesting constraint at booking time, and it is an aggregate one rather than a pairwise one. Only four asset states occupy a lane — `REQUESTED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RECEIVED` and `INGESTING` — while `INGESTED` and `RETURNED` release it. To decide whether a candidate window fits, the service collects the instants that bound every window in play: the candidate's own start and end, plus the start and end of every occupying asset already booked at that auditorium, and it keeps only those instants that fall inside the candidate's own closed window, both ends included. At each of those instants it counts how many occupying assets at that auditorium have a window containing that instant, both ends inclusive, and adds one for the candidate itself. If the largest count reached at any evaluated instant is strictly greater than the auditorium's `dcp_ingest_lane_count`, the booking is refused with `ERR_INGEST_LANE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; otherwise it is admitted. Evaluating only the interval endpoints inside the candidate window is sufficient because concurrency does not change between them. Overlapping windows are perfectly normal — a two-lane server happily runs two at once, and it is only the third simultaneous window that fails.

Print traffic then advances one declared step at a time, each step supplied as a `new_status`, and the chain is strictly linear: `REQUESTED → IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED → INGESTING → INGESTED → RETURNED`. Asking for anything other than the single next step from the asset's current status is refused with `ERR_DCP_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. All six statuses of that chain, `REQUESTED` included, are declared values of `new_status`, so naming the asset's own current status, a status behind it or a step further ahead is that state refusal rather than a schema one, and only a string outside the chain altogether is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an asset already in `RETURNED` has no next step and therefore admits no traffic update at all. Moving to `IN_TRANSIT` optionally records a supplied `carrier_ref`; moving to `RECEIVED` stamps `received_at` from the logical clock; moving to `INGESTING` is additionally refused with the same invalid-state error while the clock is still earlier than `ingest_window_start_at`, since the server slot has not opened yet; moving to `INGESTED` stamps `ingested_at` from the logical clock and releases the lane; and moving to `RETURNED` optionally records a fresh `carrier_ref` but is refused, again with `ERR_DCP_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`, while any screening still standing on this asset has status `SCHEDULED` — a print cannot be shipped back while it is still booked to play. Assets are listable by `title_id`, `source_distributor_id`, `venue_id` and `status`.

Encrypted packages need keys. Issuing one names the `dcp_asset_id` it unlocks, the `venue_id` whose projection server it is bound to, the `server_ref` naming the projector or server certificate the KDM was cut for, and the closed validity interval `valid_from` through `valid_until`, the latter strictly after the former. The named package must be marked `is_encrypted`; a key requested for an unencrypted package is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The package must also belong to the same auditorium as the posted `venue_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The disjointness rule of §2.9 then applies: if any other key for the same package-and-venue pair whose status is `ISSUED` has a closed validity interval that touches or overlaps the candidate's, the request is refused with `ERR_KDM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A successful issue creates the key in `ISSUED` with a null `revoked_reason`, and it arms the calendar: every screening at that auditorium on that package whose status is `SCHEDULED` and whose `playback_status` is either `UNVERIFIED` or `PLAYBACK_FAILED`, and which the new key covers, moves to `KEY_PENDING`. Coverage means the key's `valid_from` is at or before the screening's `start_at` and its `valid_until` is at or after the screening's `end_at`; note the deliberate asymmetry with screen occupancy, which is measured to `blocked_until` — the projector is idle during turnaround, so a key need not span the reset buffer, and a key expiring at exactly `end_at` still covers.

The booth test is where those two dimensions meet. A `TechnicalManager` records the result of a playback test by supplying a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`. The screening's `playback_status` must be `KEY_PENDING` and its status `SCHEDULED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SHOWTIME_INVALID_STATE`. Before evaluating coverage, the service lazily retires stale keys: any candidate key whose `valid_until` is earlier than the logical clock is moved `ISSUED → EXPIRED` and stops being a coverage candidate for that verification; the retirement is performed only once the two state conditions above have held, and it persists — with that key's own `version` advanced — even when the verification then fails on the package or on coverage. The referenced package must then be in `INGESTED`, or the test is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENCY_STATE_INVALID`; and at least one surviving `ISSUED` key must cover the screening under the rule just stated, or the test is refused with `ERR_NO_COVERING_KDM`. A passing test moves `KEY_PENDING → PLAYBACK_VERIFIED`; a failing one moves `KEY_PENDING → PLAYBACK_FAILED`, from which cutting a fresh covering key re-arms the screening back to `KEY_PENDING` and the booth can test again — the retest cycle exists precisely because a failed test is usually a bad KDM rather than a bad print.

Revoking a key is available to a `TechnicalManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector` and only from `ISSUED`; a key that is already `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` yields `ERR_KDM_KEY_INVALID_STATE`. **Hop one** records the supplied `revoked_reason` and moves the key `ISSUED → REVOKED`. **Hop two:** because the key is gone, every screening at that auditorium on that package whose status is `SCHEDULED` and whose `playback_status` is `KEY_PENDING` or `PLAYBACK_VERIFIED`, and for which no *other* `ISSUED` key still provides coverage, falls back to `UNVERIFIED`; and if such a screening's `sale_status` is `ON_SALE` or `SOLD_OUT` it is forced to `SALES_CLOSED`, because a house cannot keep selling a show it can no longer project. Screenings that retain another covering key are untouched. **Hop three:** because those screenings' sales have been pulled, their ticket orders in `RESERVED` become `VOID` — unpaid holds are released — and each affected screening's `seats_sold` and `seats_available` are recomputed from the surviving orders. Keys are listable by `dcp_asset_id`, `venue_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Programming the Single Screen

Scheduling a screening is the point at which every other part of the system has to agree, and a `Programmer` does it in one of two ways: by naming an explicit start instant, or by asking the optimiser to find the best one.

Placing a screening by hand names the `venue_id` whose single screen it occupies, the `title_id` being screened, the `booking_id` the screening is billed against, the `clearance_id` whose licence allowance it consumes, the `dcp_asset_id` that will actually be projected, a `strand` of `FIRST_RUN`, `REPERTORY_SERIES`, `FESTIVAL` or `SPECIAL_EVENT` used for calendar-balance and audience-development rules, an optional free-text `series_label` printed in the calendar (it carries no referential meaning), the advertised `start_at` instant, a `base_price_cents` of zero or more as the advertised full-price admission before member discount and any event surcharge, the `public_sale_opens_at` instant at which non-member sales open, the projection `format` for this screening, and the `expected_clearance_version` guard of §2.6.

Ten conditions gate the placement, and the ladder of §2.7 determines which one is reported when several fail. The engagement must be `CONFIRMED` or `RUNNING`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. The licence must be `CLEARED` and must itself be attached to the posted `booking_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_NOT_CLEARED` or, where the licence belongs to a different engagement or the referenced rows otherwise disagree, with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The venue-local date of `start_at` must fall inside the licence's inclusive `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` span, otherwise `ERR_CLEARANCE_WINDOW_NOT_COVERED`. The licence must have at least one screening remaining, otherwise `ERR_SCREENING_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` — a `screenings_remaining` of exactly 1 still admits one more screening, and only 0 refuses. The screening's `format` must simultaneously be one of the auditorium's `supported_formats`, equal the package's `format`, and equal the licence's `source_format`, otherwise `ERR_FORMAT_INCOMPATIBLE`. The venue-local time of `start_at` must fall between `open_local_time` and `last_start_local_time` inclusive, the venue-local date of `start_at` must fall inside the engagement's inclusive `engagement_start_date` through `engagement_end_date` span, and `start_at` must not be earlier than the logical clock — any of the three failing yields `ERR_SHOWTIME_WINDOW_INVALID`. The screen block must not collide with another non-cancelled screening at the auditorium under the half-open geometry of §2.9, otherwise `ERR_SCREEN_SLOT_CONFLICT`. And the ISO-week first-run ceiling of §2.8 must still admit the candidate, otherwise `ERR_WEEKLY_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`.

The block itself is computed, never supplied. The screening's on-screen content runs for the auditorium's `trailer_reel_minutes` plus the title's `runtime_minutes` plus the screening's own `extra_minutes`, which is 0 at creation; `end_at` is `start_at` advanced by that total number of minutes, and `blocked_until` is `end_at` advanced by the auditorium's `turnaround_minutes`. Collision is tested against every other screening at the same auditorium whose status is not `CANCELLED` — that is, scheduled and completed screenings both block the screen, cancelled ones do not — and it is tested as instants, so a block spilling past midnight still collides with the next morning's first show. A successful placement creates the screening with status `SCHEDULED`, `sale_status` `DRAFT` and `playback_status` `UNVERIFIED`, with `surcharge_cents` at 0, `seats_sold` at 0, `gross_cents` at 0, `admissions_count` at 0, `cancel_reason` null and `slot_score` null. It then draws down the licence: `screenings_used` rises by one, `screenings_remaining` falls by one, the licence's `version` increments, and the engagement's `scheduled_showtime_count` rises by one.

The optimiser accepts the same payload with one substitution: instead of `start_at` it takes a venue-local `target_date` plus `earliest_local_time` and `latest_local_time` bounding the search, and it too carries `expected_clearance_version`. The two bounds may name the same reading, which searches a single slot; only a `latest_local_time` strictly earlier than `earliest_local_time` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It builds a candidate grid of every instant from the target date at `earliest_local_time` through the target date at `latest_local_time` inclusive, stepping in 15-minute increments. The conditions that do not vary from slot to slot are settled once for the whole request and keep their own refusals from the ladder of §2.7 rather than collapsing into an empty pool: the engagement's state, the licence's cleared state, the licence's remaining allowance, the format chain, and the `target_date` lying inside *both* the licence's window and the engagement's window. What is filtered slot by slot is narrower: a grid slot is eligible only if its venue-local time lies between the auditorium's `open_local_time` and `last_start_local_time` inclusive, its start instant is not earlier than the logical clock, its block collides with nothing under the same half-open geometry, and the ISO-week first-run ceiling still admits it — so a slot the clock has already passed, a slot the day's occupancy blocks and a slot the weekly ceiling refuses are all simply dropped from the pool. If no slot survives, nothing is created and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SLOT`. One temporal case is decided before the grid is built at all: when the target date's own `latest_local_time` already falls earlier than the clock, so that no candidate on that date could lie in the future, the request is refused with `ERR_SHOWTIME_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Surviving slots are then scored, and the winner's score is stored on the created screening as `slot_score` — a field that is only ever populated by the optimiser and stays null on a hand-placed screening. Four components make up the score, and throughout the scoring a screening counts as belonging to a venue-local day or an ISO week when its own `start_at` falls inside it, however far its block runs past midnight, while cancelled screenings are ignored in every component. The **prime component** is 60 when the slot's venue-local time falls between 18:00 and 21:00 inclusive, 35 when it falls at or after 15:00 but strictly before 18:00, and 10 otherwise. The **spacing component** rewards breathing room on either side: the gap before the slot is, when the same venue-local day already holds non-cancelled screenings whose `blocked_until` is at or before the slot's start, the whole number of minutes from the latest such `blocked_until` to the slot's start, capped at 120 minutes, and it is taken as the full 120 minutes when no such earlier screening exists; the gap after the slot is, when the same day already holds non-cancelled screenings whose `start_at` is at or after the slot's own `blocked_until`, the whole number of minutes from the slot's `blocked_until` to the earliest such `start_at`, again capped at 120, and again the full 120 when there is no later screening. Each gap is divided by 15 discarding any remainder, the quotient is doubled, and the result is capped at 12; the two capped values are added together. The **fragmentation penalty** subtracts 15 for a gap before the slot that is greater than zero but strictly less than the auditorium's `min_useful_gap_minutes`, and a further 15 for a gap after the slot in the same condition — a gap of exactly zero is perfect back-to-back programming and is not penalised, and a gap at or above the useful threshold is not penalised either. The **strand bonus** adds 10 when the candidate's `strand` is `REPERTORY_SERIES` and the count of `REPERTORY_SERIES` screenings already in that ISO week, excluding the candidate itself, is strictly below the auditorium's `weekly_repertory_target`. The slot's score is the prime component plus the spacing component, minus the fragmentation penalty, plus the strand bonus. The highest score wins; where two slots tie, the earlier `start_at` wins, and since grid instants are unique that tie-break always resolves. The winning slot is then created exactly as a hand-placed screening would be, drawing down the licence allowance and incrementing the licence's `version` in the same way.

Cancelling a screening is available to a `Programmer`, a `BoxOfficeManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector`, only from status `SCHEDULED`, and it takes a `cancel_reason` that is stored on the row. It moves the screening `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, frees its screen block for other programming, and forces `sale_status` to `SALES_CLOSED`. **Hop one:** its ticket orders in `RESERVED` become `VOID` and those in `PAID` become `REFUNDED`, with `refund_destination` set to `MEMBER_CREDIT` when the order carries a `member_id` and `CASH` when it does not. **Hop two:** because those orders were refunded, each affected member's `credit_balance_cents` rises by the refunded `total_cents` and their `comp_tickets_remaining` is restored for any comps on the order. The licence gets its allowance back — `screenings_used` falls by one, `screenings_remaining` rises by one, and the licence's `version` increments — and the engagement's `scheduled_showtime_count` falls by one. Any special event attached to the screening, whether `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, is cancelled along with it and takes the very `cancel_reason` the caller supplied for the screening; because the screening itself is gone, a `CONFIRMED` event collapsed this way is **not** compensated — the screening keeps the `extra_minutes` and `surcharge_cents` the event had added, and the `end_at` and `blocked_until` they produced. The screening collection is readable by every role including anonymous browsers, filterable by `venue_id`, `title_id`, `booking_id`, `clearance_id`, `dcp_asset_id`, `strand` and `status`, and narrowable to a start-instant window with `start_from` and `start_to`, both of which are compared against `start_at` inclusively.

### 3.6. Special Event Production and Displacement

A filmmaker Q and A, a panel, a festival slot or a themed introduction is produced *around* an existing screening rather than instead of it. A `Programmer` plans one by naming the `showtime_id` it wraps, an `event_type` of `FILMMAKER_QA`, `PANEL`, `FESTIVAL_SCREENING` or `THEMED_INTRO`, the `guest_name` of the visiting filmmaker or panellist, the `extra_minutes` of additional on-screen and on-stage time the production adds to the screening's block, the per-ticket `surcharge_cents` that will apply once the production is confirmed, and the `guest_fee_cents` honorarium payable to the guest — all three amounts zero or more. The honorarium is a cinema expense and never forms part of film rent, so it appears in no settlement figure. Planning requires the target screening to exist with status `SCHEDULED`, and it requires that no other special event on that same screening already be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`; a second live event on one screening is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. The event is created in `PLANNED` with an empty `displaced_showtime_ids` list, and — this is the part that surprises people — the screening's block is **not** yet extended. A planned event changes nothing about the calendar.

Confirmation is the `ExecutiveDirector`'s decision, and it is where the block grows. The caller supplies `displace_conflicts`, a boolean stating whether later screenings that the enlarged block now collides with should be displaced. Confirmation requires the event to be `PLANNED` and its screening to be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIAL_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`; and it requires the extended block to remain inside the auditorium's operating day — `start_at` is never moved by a confirmation, so only `blocked_until` travels, and a confirmation that would push the screening's occupancy outside the operating day is refused with `ERR_SHOWTIME_WINDOW_INVALID`. The operating day is measured here as the venue-local calendar date on which the screening starts: the enlarged `blocked_until` must not fall later than the midnight ending that date, a block finishing exactly at that midnight is admitted, and no comparison is made against `last_start_local_time`, which governs advertised start times only. A screening whose block already ran past that midnight before the event existed therefore admits no confirmation at all. When `displace_conflicts` is false and the enlarged block collides with anything, the request is refused with `ERR_SCREEN_SLOT_CONFLICT` and **nothing at all changes** — no partial extension is ever left behind.

When confirmation proceeds, four hops follow. **Hop one:** the event moves `PLANNED → CONFIRMED`, the screening's `extra_minutes` is set from the event's `extra_minutes` and its `surcharge_cents` from the event's `surcharge_cents`, and both `end_at` and `blocked_until` are recomputed and therefore both move later. **Hop two:** because `blocked_until` moved, every other screening at that auditorium whose status is `SCHEDULED` and whose own occupancy interval now intersects the enlarged block becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `DISPLACED_BY_SPECIAL_EVENT`, its `sale_status` forced to `SALES_CLOSED`, and its `id` appended to the event's `displaced_showtime_ids`, which therefore lists the displaced screenings oldest-first. **Hop three:** because those screenings are cancelled, their `RESERVED` orders become `VOID` and their `PAID` orders become `REFUNDED`, each displaced screening's licence gets `screenings_used` reduced by one with the licence's `version` incremented, and each displaced screening's engagement gets `scheduled_showtime_count` reduced by one. **Hop four:** because the paid member orders were refunded to member credit, each affected member's `credit_balance_cents` rises by the refunded `total_cents` and their `comp_tickets_remaining` is restored.

Cancelling an event is available to a `Programmer` or an `ExecutiveDirector` from either `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, with a `cancel_reason` supplied; an already-`CANCELLED` event yields `ERR_SPECIAL_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. Cancelling a `PLANNED` event has no side effects whatsoever, because such an event never touched its screening. Cancelling a `CONFIRMED` event compensates: the screening's `extra_minutes` and `surcharge_cents` both return to 0 and `end_at` and `blocked_until` are recomputed back to their pre-confirmation values, freeing the screen again. That compensation is applied to the wrapped screening whatever state the screening now holds — one that another cascade has since cancelled still has its minutes and surcharge returned to 0, its block recomputed and its `version` advanced with them. Two things are deliberately **not** compensated. Screenings already displaced are not resurrected — the calendar has moved on, the audience has been told, and `displaced_showtime_ids` is retained as the audit trail of what the production cost. And orders already sold keep the price they were sold at, surcharge included; no repricing occurs. Events are readable individually, including the ids they displaced, and the collection may be filtered by `showtime_id`, `status` and `event_type`.

### 3.7. Membership Standing and Donor Benefits

Membership at an art house is a donation relationship, not a subscription, and the service therefore derives every benefit from giving history rather than storing it as a plan. A `BoxOfficeManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector` opens a record with the `full_name` on the card, an `email` contact address and the `join_date` on which the membership was opened. The email must not already be held by a member whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` — the comparison is an exact string one, with no case folding and no trimming, and a `LAPSED` member's address is free to reuse — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_EMAIL_TAKEN`. A new record starts in `PENDING` with an empty `donations` list, a null `expires_at`, `rolling_donation_cents` and `fiscal_ytd_donation_cents` at 0, `rolling_tier`, `fiscal_tier` and `effective_tier` all at `PROSPECT`, `discount_pct` at 0.00, `advance_access_hours` at 0, `comp_tickets_granted` and `comp_tickets_remaining` at 0, and `credit_balance_cents` at 0.

Donations are lodged one at a time with an `amount_cents` and an explicit `received_at` instant, which lets a season of giving be replayed against the logical clock. Each donation is appended to the member's own `donations` list as an amount-and-instant pair; the list is append-only and donations are never shared between members. Every donation triggers a full re-derivation of standing, as does the membership sweep, as does any read of the member record — the tiers on a member you have just read are always current as of the clock, never as of the last write.

Standing is derived twice over, from two different windows, and the higher answer wins. The **rolling** figure, `rolling_donation_cents`, is the sum of the amounts of every donation whose `received_at` falls in the trailing 365 days ending at the clock, both boundary instants included, so a gift stamped exactly at the window's opening instant still counts. The **fiscal** figure, `fiscal_ytd_donation_cents`, is the sum of the amounts of every donation whose `received_at` falls on or after the fiscal year's start of `2026-07-01T00:00:00.000Z` and at or before the clock. Each figure is then mapped to a tier by the same thresholds: `DONOR_CIRCLE` at 50000 cents or more of giving, `SUPPORTER` at 15000 cents or more, `BASIC` at 6000 cents or more, and `PROSPECT` below that. The rolling figure yields `rolling_tier`, the fiscal figure yields `fiscal_tier`, and `effective_tier` is whichever of the two ranks higher on the ordering `PROSPECT`, then `BASIC`, then `SUPPORTER`, then `DONOR_CIRCLE`; when the two agree, that agreed value is the effective one. The two windows genuinely disagree in practice: a single 20000-cent gift made on 1 March 2026 makes `rolling_tier` `SUPPORTER` while `fiscal_tier` is still `PROSPECT`, and an 8000-cent gift on 15 July 2026 makes `fiscal_tier` `BASIC`.

Every benefit follows from `effective_tier` alone. `discount_pct` is exactly 0.00 for `PROSPECT`, 0.10 for `BASIC`, 0.15 for `SUPPORTER` and 0.25 for `DONOR_CIRCLE`, and is never rounded. `advance_access_hours` — the number of hours before a screening's `public_sale_opens_at` at which this member may already buy — is 0 for `PROSPECT`, 24 for `BASIC`, 48 for `SUPPORTER` and a full week, 168 hours, for `DONOR_CIRCLE`. `comp_tickets_granted` is 0 for both `PROSPECT` and `BASIC`, 2 for `SUPPORTER` and 6 for `DONOR_CIRCLE`, being the comp allowance for the current membership term. `comp_tickets_remaining` is `comp_tickets_granted` less the total `comp_quantity` already held on this member's orders whose status is `RESERVED` or `PAID` — voided and refunded orders release their comps. And `expires_at` is the latest `received_at` among the member's donations advanced by 365 days, or null when the member has never donated.

Membership state follows the money. A member moves `PENDING → ACTIVE` on a donation that leaves `rolling_donation_cents` at or above 6000 cents, at which point `expires_at` is re-derived exactly as the previous paragraph states — from the *latest* `received_at` on the record advanced by 365 days, which is the lodged donation's own instant whenever that gift is the most recent one on the record and an earlier gift's instant when a backdated one is lodged behind it. A lapsed member renews the same way, moving `LAPSED → ACTIVE` on a qualifying donation with `expires_at` re-derived identically; this is the intended cycle of the membership programme. Attempting a transition a member's state does not admit yields `ERR_MEMBER_INVALID_STATE`; in practice no membership operation refuses on that ground, because none of them has a from-state to violate — a donation is accepted whatever the member's status and simply leaves an `ACTIVE` record `ACTIVE` and a sub-threshold `PENDING` or `LAPSED` one where it was, and the sweep silently passes over every member that is not `ACTIVE`.

Lapsing is a batch operation the `ExecutiveDirector` runs: it moves every `ACTIVE` member whose `expires_at` is **strictly** earlier than the logical clock to `LAPSED`, so a member whose `expires_at` equals the clock exactly does **not** lapse. The sweep recomputes standing for each swept member at the sweep clock, so `discount_pct`, `advance_access_hours` and `comp_tickets_remaining` all follow from the recomputed `effective_tier` — in practice a member lapses because their giving has fallen out of both windows, so those benefits fall away — while `credit_balance_cents` and the `donations` history are retained untouched and are never zeroed. The sweep's response is not a member object but a summary carrying `swept_count`, the integer number of memberships lapsed, `lapsed_member_ids`, the list of their ids, and `evaluated_at`, the logical instant the sweep was evaluated against.

### 3.8. The Box Office: Sales, Comps, Credit and Refunds

A screening's ticketing dimension, `sale_status`, evolves entirely independently of its technical dimension, `playback_status`. A screening whose KDM has not yet been cut may still go on sale, and a screening that has passed its booth test may still be sitting in `DRAFT`. A `BoxOfficeManager` puts a screening on sale, moving `sale_status` `DRAFT → ON_SALE`; this requires the screening's status to be `SCHEDULED` and its `sale_status` to be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_SHOWTIME_INVALID_STATE`, requires the owning engagement to be `CONFIRMED` or `RUNNING`, otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_STATE_INVALID`, and requires at least one seat to be available, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`. Opening sales recomputes `seats_sold` and `seats_available` from the auditorium's `seat_capacity` and its `house_hold_seats`.

Closing the box office for a screening moves `sale_status` from `ON_SALE` or `SOLD_OUT` to `SALES_CLOSED` and releases every unpaid hold: every order on the screening whose status is `RESERVED` becomes `VOID`, and the screening's rollup is recomputed from what survives. Between opening and closing, the sale dimension oscillates on its own: the moment an order drives `seats_available` to exactly 0 the screening moves `ON_SALE → SOLD_OUT`, and the moment a refund or a void frees at least one seat again it moves `SOLD_OUT → ON_SALE`. Neither flip is ever requested by a caller.

Selling seats is open to a `BoxOfficeManager`, to a `Member` acting on their own behalf, and to an anonymous walk-up. The order names the `showtime_id` being sold, the `quantity` of seats including any comps, a `purchaser_email` for the confirmation, and optionally a `member_id` (which defaults to null and is absent for walk-up and anonymous purchases; a `BoxOfficeManager` may name any member and a `Member` only their own, while an `Anonymous` caller that supplies the field at all is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, decided as soon as that field has been read and therefore ahead of the rest of the body), a `comp_quantity` of seats to draw from the member's comp allowance (defaulting to 0 when omitted, and never greater than the order's own `quantity`), and an `apply_credit` flag (defaulting to false) asking that the member's refund credit be spent on this order. `quantity` must be at least 1 and no more than 10 seats per order. The screening must be `SCHEDULED` with a `sale_status` of `ON_SALE` or `SOLD_OUT`; an order against a screening in `DRAFT` or `SALES_CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_SHOWTIME_SALES_CLOSED`. The requested `quantity` must not exceed the screening's `seats_available`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SEATS`.

Two entitlement checks then apply. Comps are only for the upper tiers: `comp_quantity` must be zero unless the order carries a `member_id` whose `effective_tier` is `SUPPORTER` or `DONOR_CIRCLE`, and it must never exceed either the order's own `quantity` or the member's `comp_tickets_remaining`; a violation of any of those is refused with `ERR_COMP_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`, and an anonymous or walk-up order may never carry comps at all. Credit redemption must be honest: `apply_credit` is only accepted together with a `member_id`, and only when that member actually holds credit at request time; an `apply_credit` without a member, or against a `credit_balance_cents` of zero, is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.

Advance access is the donor benefit that bites hardest at the box office. When a screening's `public_sale_opens_at` is later than the logical clock, public sales have not opened, and the order is admitted only if the buyer's own advance window has: that is, only if the clock has reached `public_sale_opens_at` moved earlier by the buyer's `advance_access_hours`. Anonymous walk-ups and members at `PROSPECT` have a zero-hour window and are therefore refused outright before public sales open. A buyer whose window has not yet opened is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_ACCESS_NOT_OPEN`. Once the clock reaches `public_sale_opens_at`, the check is satisfied for everybody.

Pricing an admitted order is where step order is normative, because two plausible orderings differ by a cent. First the gross unit price is the screening's `base_price_cents` plus its `surcharge_cents`, the latter being non-zero only while a `CONFIRMED` special event is attached. Second the paid quantity is the order's `quantity` less its `comp_quantity`, since comp seats are admitted at zero admission. Third the applicable discount is 0.00 when the order carries no `member_id`, and otherwise the member's `discount_pct`. Fourth — and this is the step that must not be reordered — `unit_price_cents` is the gross unit price multiplied by one minus the discount, **rounded half up to a whole cent at this point, per ticket, before any multiplication**. Fifth `line_subtotal_cents` is that rounded `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the paid quantity. Sixth `facility_fee_cents` is 150 cents per seat multiplied by the order's full `quantity` — comp seats pay the facility fee too — and the fee is retained by the cinema rather than shared with the distributor. Seventh the order before credit is `line_subtotal_cents` plus `facility_fee_cents`. Eighth `applied_credit_cents` is, when `apply_credit` is true and a `member_id` is present, the lesser of the member's `credit_balance_cents` and that order-before-credit figure, and 0 otherwise — so credit can zero an order out but `total_cents` never goes negative. Ninth `total_cents` is the order before credit less `applied_credit_cents`. Discounting the whole line and rounding once at the end is explicitly wrong: at a base price of 1450 cents with a 0.15 discount over three paid seats, the correct figures round each ticket to 1233 cents for a subtotal of 3699 cents, where rounding after multiplication would yield one cent less.

An admitted order is created in `RESERVED` with a null `refund_destination`, and it moves the ledgers immediately: the member's `credit_balance_cents` falls by `applied_credit_cents` and the member's `comp_tickets_remaining` falls by `comp_quantity` — though the member row is itself touched, its `updated_at` and `version` moving, only when the order actually consumed credit or comps, so a plain full-price member sale leaves the membership record's `version` alone — the screening's `seats_sold` rises by `quantity`, `seats_available` is recomputed, and the screening flips `ON_SALE → SOLD_OUT` if that lands on exactly zero. Paying an order moves it `RESERVED → PAID`, which requires the screening still to be `SCHEDULED`, and adds the order's `total_cents` to the screening's `gross_cents` and its `quantity` to the screening's `admissions_count`; only paid orders ever count towards gross. An order that is not `RESERVED` when payment arrives, or not `PAID` when a refund arrives, yields `ERR_TICKET_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

Refunding is available to a `BoxOfficeManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector` from `PAID`, and only while the screening has not been completed. The caller supplies a `refund_destination` of `CASH` or `MEMBER_CREDIT`, which is stored on the order. The order moves `PAID → REFUNDED`, the screening's `gross_cents` falls by the order's `total_cents`, its `admissions_count` and `seats_sold` both fall by the order's `quantity`, and the screening flips `SOLD_OUT → ON_SALE` once at least one seat is free again. When the destination is `MEMBER_CREDIT` the member's `credit_balance_cents` rises by the order's `total_cents`; when it is `CASH` it does not. Independently of the destination, any credit the order had consumed is returned — the member's `credit_balance_cents` also rises by the order's `applied_credit_cents` — and the member's `comp_tickets_remaining` rises by the order's `comp_quantity`. A refund always touches the member an order names, even a `CASH` one on an order that consumed no credit and carried no comps. Orders voided by a cascade unwind the same way minus the cash: `seats_sold` falls by the order's `quantity`, `comp_tickets_remaining` rises by its `comp_quantity`, and `credit_balance_cents` rises by its `applied_credit_cents`. Where a cascade refunds a paid order rather than voiding a held one, `refund_destination` is set to `MEMBER_CREDIT` if the order carries a `member_id` and to `CASH` if it does not, and the same ledger reversals apply. Orders are readable individually with their pinned price breakdown and listable by `showtime_id`, `member_id` and `status`.

Marking a screening played is the last box-office act, available to a `BoxOfficeManager` or an `ExecutiveDirector`. Four conditions must hold, and any failure yields `ERR_SHOWTIME_INVALID_STATE`: the screening's status is `SCHEDULED`, its `sale_status` is `SALES_CLOSED`, its `playback_status` is `PLAYBACK_VERIFIED`, and the logical clock has reached or passed its `end_at`. Completion moves the screening `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and freezes `gross_cents` and `admissions_count` from its paid orders. If the owning engagement is still `CONFIRMED`, this is also the moment it bubbles up to `RUNNING`.

### 3.9. Closing the Engagement and Settling with the Distributor

Closing an engagement is the `ExecutiveDirector`'s act and is available from either `CONFIRMED` or `RUNNING`; from any other state it yields `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. Closing prices the minimum-run promise, and it does so in a strictly ordered sequence, because the dependency looks circular — the penalty is priced off a daily nut that is priced off a prorated nut that is priced off the very day count that defines the shortfall — and only the stated order makes the answer well defined.

The sequence runs as follows. **Step one** collects the engagement's screenings whose status is `COMPLETED`; scheduled and cancelled screenings are excluded even when they fall inside the engagement window. **Step two** sets `actual_playing_days` to the number of *distinct* venue-local calendar dates among those completed screenings' `start_at` instants, so two screenings on one date contribute a single playing day. **Step three** takes the shortfall as the greater of zero and the engagement's `min_run_days` less `actual_playing_days`. **Step four** derives the proration factor: it is exactly 0 when `actual_playing_days` is zero, and otherwise the lesser of 1 and the greater of 0.60 and the ratio of `actual_playing_days` to `min_run_days` — a floor of 0.60 and a ceiling of 1. **Step five** sets the applied house nut to the engagement's `house_nut_cents` multiplied by that proration factor, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Step six** derives the daily nut: when `actual_playing_days` is zero it is the engagement's `house_nut_cents` divided by its `min_run_days`, rounded half up; otherwise it is the applied house nut from step five divided by `actual_playing_days`, rounded half up. Dividing the *unprorated* nut by `min_run_days` is explicitly wrong here — it agrees with the correct figure whenever the 0.60 floor is not binding and disagrees precisely when it is, so for a nut of 18750 cents against a promise of 4 days with 1 day actually played the correct daily nut is 11250 cents, not 4688. **Step seven** sets `min_run_penalty_cents` to the shortfall in days multiplied by that daily nut multiplied by a penalty rate of 0.50 — the house forfeits half a day's nut for each day short — rounded half up to a whole cent. **Step eight** sets `min_run_compliant` to true exactly when the shortfall is zero. Rounding happens independently at steps five, six and seven. Closing an engagement on which nothing at all played is legal and exercises the zero-day branch throughout, yielding `actual_playing_days` of 0, `min_run_compliant` false, and a penalty priced off the unprorated daily nut.

A `BoxOfficeManager` then drafts the settlement, naming the `booking_id` being settled, a `variant` of `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, and the `expected_booking_version` guard of §2.6. The engagement must be `CLOSED`, and no other settlement on the same engagement may already be in `SUBMITTED`, `RECONCILED` or `PAID`; either failure is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_PERMITTED`. A fresh draft may legitimately supersede an earlier draft on the same engagement, which is exactly why the stale-version guard exists. Drafting computes and stores every figure, copies the engagement's `min_run_penalty_cents` onto the settlement, creates the settlement in `DRAFT` with `submitted_by_user_id`, `finalized_by_user_id` and `dispute_note` all null, and increments the engagement's `version`.

The `variant` selects the inclusion set, and everything after that is shared arithmetic. Under `PROVISIONAL` — the box office's forward-looking estimate at close — the included screenings are the engagement's screenings whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, and the included orders are those screenings' orders whose status is `RESERVED` or `PAID`. Under `FINAL` — the figure actually remitted — the included screenings are only those whose status is `COMPLETED`, and the included orders only those whose status is `PAID`. In both cases the whole engagement is in scope with no date truncation: every included screening counts regardless of `engagement_end_date`.

From the chosen set the figures follow. `box_office_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` across the included orders. `facility_fee_total_cents` is the sum of `facility_fee_cents` across the same orders. `adjusted_gross_cents` is `box_office_cents` less `facility_fee_total_cents`, and it is the basis on which film rent is computed — the facility fee never reaches the distributor. `playing_days` is the number of *distinct* venue-local calendar dates among the included screenings' `start_at` instants. `proration_factor` is exactly 0 when `playing_days` is zero, and otherwise the lesser of 1 and the greater of 0.60 and the ratio of `playing_days` to the engagement's `min_run_days`; it is the one figure stored on a settlement that is not a whole number, and it reaches the wire as a JSON number rather than a decimal string, so a ratio with no finite decimal expansion — two thirds, say — is emitted as the nearest double, `0.6666666666666666`, and only the products it multiplies are rounded. `house_nut_applied_cents` is the engagement's `house_nut_cents` multiplied by `proration_factor`, rounded half up to a whole cent.

Film rent then branches on the engagement's `terms_variant`. Under `SLIDING_SCALE_90_10` the raw rent is 0.90 multiplied by the greater of zero and `adjusted_gross_cents` less `house_nut_applied_cents`, rounded half up — the distributor takes ninety per cent of everything above the prorated nut and nothing below it. Under `PERCENTAGE_FLOOR` the raw rent is the engagement's `distributor_share_pct` multiplied by `adjusted_gross_cents`, rounded half up. Under `FLAT_FEE` the raw rent is simply the engagement's `guarantee_cents`. In all three cases `film_rent_cents` is the greater of that raw rent and the engagement's `guarantee_cents`: the guarantee is a **floor**, never a cap. Finally `cinema_retained_cents` is `adjusted_gross_cents` plus `facility_fee_total_cents` less `film_rent_cents`, which is to say the house keeps the facility fee in full plus whatever of the adjusted gross the distributor did not take. Rounding is applied independently to `house_nut_applied_cents` and to the raw rent. The two variants genuinely diverge on identical stored data: the provisional set counts scheduled screenings and unpaid holds, so both the gross **and** the playing-day count are larger, which raises the prorated house nut and therefore *lowers* the ninety-ten film rent relative to the final figure.

Settlement then moves through its own cycle. Submitting it to the distributor is a `BoxOfficeManager` act available from `DRAFT` or from `DISPUTED`, requires the engagement still to be `CLOSED`, records `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and moves the settlement to `SUBMITTED`. Finalising is the `ExecutiveDirector`'s act from `SUBMITTED`: it recomputes **every** figure under the final inclusion set regardless of the variant the draft was created under, overwrites `variant` to `FINAL`, records `finalized_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and moves the settlement to `RECONCILED`. Recording the distributor's disposition is likewise the `ExecutiveDirector`'s: an `outcome` of `DISPUTED`, which alone requires a `dispute_note` holding the distributor's rationale, moves the settlement from `SUBMITTED` or `RECONCILED` to `DISPUTED`, from which it can be adjusted and re-submitted — that is the settlement cycle; an `outcome` of `PAID` moves it from `RECONCILED` to `PAID`, which is terminal and freezes the figures for good, and needs no `dispute_note` — one sent with it is ignored, and whatever note an earlier dispute left on the row stays there. Any of these attempted from a state that does not admit it yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Settlements are readable individually and listable by `booking_id`, `variant` and `status`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is rooted: this service mounts its resources at the URL root rather than under a versioned or domain prefix. Creation endpoints — those that mint a new resource — respond **201 Created**; every other successful call, whether it reads a resource, drives a state transition or runs a batch capability, responds **200 OK**. Successful mutations return the created or updated resource in `data`, with the two exceptions noted in Section 3: the membership sweep returns its summary object, and the clock-setting capability returns the instant it was set to. Two cases are worth spelling out, because the resource created is not the resource addressed: lodging a negotiation round — whether as a counter or as a fresh cinema offer — creates a round and therefore responds **201 Created** returning that round rather than the engagement, and asking the optimiser to place a screening likewise responds **201 Created** returning the created screening; lodging a donation, by contrast, mutates the membership record rather than creating anything and therefore responds **200 OK** returning the updated member.
