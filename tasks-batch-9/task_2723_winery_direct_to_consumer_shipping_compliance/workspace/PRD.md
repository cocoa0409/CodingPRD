# Winery DTC Shipping Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Selling wine directly to a consumer across a state line is one of the most heavily conditioned transactions in American commerce. Before a single bottle leaves the cellar door, a winery must satisfy a destination state that has its own licensing regime, its own per-consumer and per-winery volume ceilings, its own excise and sales-tax arithmetic, its own dry counties and prohibited municipalities, its own rules about who may sign for the package, and its own reporting calendar. The rules are not merely numerous — they interact. A permit that lapses overnight retroactively invalidates orders that were lawful yesterday. A package that comes back return-to-sender must give the consumer's volume headroom back and must claw the excise accrual out of a tax return that may not have been filed yet. A wine club that ships automatically must know, on its own, when a subscriber's state has run out of room.

This document specifies the Winery DTC Shipping Compliance API, the service that carries that burden on behalf of a winery's compliance department. The API is mounted under `/dtc` and is organized around a single decisive act: **screening** an order against the destination state's rule set at the moment of shipment authorization. Everything upstream of screening — state configuration, permits, carrier enrollments, consumer identity verification — exists to make that screening decision defensible. Everything downstream — labels, carrier scans, the volume ledger, state remittances, the renewal calendar — exists to keep the record straight after the fact, including when the outcome has to be undone.

Two design commitments shape the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, an order carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**: a compliance dimension that answers "may this ship?" and a fulfillment dimension that answers "where is it?". Neither dimension constrains the other except where this document says so explicitly. Second, every volume movement is recorded as an **append-only ledger entry** rather than as a mutable counter, so that a return, a re-ship, or a manual correction is expressed as a new signed entry and never as an edit to history. Compliance officers can therefore reconstruct why any given order cleared or failed, months later, from the ledger alone.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, temporal semantics, identity and versioning, rounding discipline, evaluation ordering, and the cascade conventions the business sections rely on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — without exception, including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. This is the first check the server performs on any request.

The system recognizes exactly five roles, spelled precisely as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `WineryOperator`, `Consumer`, and `CarrierAgent`. `PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator; it seeds wineries, state rules, prohibited ZIPs, carrier programs and consumers, drives the logical clock, and never decides a compliance outcome. `ComplianceOfficer` is the licensing and tax authority; it files, activates, renews and revokes permits, screens orders, decides compliance exceptions, adjusts the volume ledger and files and remits state returns. `WineryOperator` is the winery acting for itself; it enrolls with carriers, files renewals, creates and screens its own orders, produces labels and runs its wine club. `Consumer` is the wine buyer; it may place its own orders and read its own records. `CarrierAgent` is the shipping carrier; it decides winery enrollments and posts the scan events that drive fulfillment, and can never create an order, screen one, produce a label or touch tax. If `X-User-Role` is not one of these five strings, or is a valid role that is not listed among the authorized roles of the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This check runs immediately after the presence check on the two headers.

Two of the five roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those two `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary string. When `X-User-Role` is `WineryOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Winery`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Winery` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which in practice means `Winery.id` itself, `StatePermit.winery_id`, `CarrierEnrollment.winery_id`, `Order.winery_id`, `ClubSubscription.winery_id`, `TaxRemittance.winery_id`, and — for a shipment, which holds no winery reference of its own — the `winery_id` of the order the shipment was drawn against. When `X-User-Role` is `Consumer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consumer`; ownership checks compare `Consumer.id` itself, `Order.consumer_id`, `ClubSubscription.consumer_id`, or `VolumeLedgerEntry.consumer_id` to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch on either family — a consumer reaching another consumer's order, subscription, ledger entry or profile, or a winery operator reaching another winery's permit, enrollment, order, shipment, subscription or remittance — is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check runs third, after role authorization and before any resource is looked up for existence.

The remaining three roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, or `CarrierAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `ComplianceException.requested_by_user_id` and `ComplianceException.decided_by_user_id`, as `CarrierEnrollment.decided_by_user_id`, and as `TaxRemittance.filed_by_user_id` and `TaxRemittance.remitted_by_user_id` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Recording those opaque identifiers is what makes segregation of duties enforceable, and the system does enforce it. The `ComplianceOfficer` who decides a compliance exception must not be the same `X-User-ID` that is recorded in that exception's `requested_by_user_id`, and the `ComplianceOfficer` who records payment of a tax remittance must not be the same `X-User-ID` recorded in that remittance's `filed_by_user_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that this is a check on the identifier, not on the role: two different officers are always acceptable, and the same officer is never acceptable. Separately, authority over compliance suspensions is reserved: only a `ComplianceOfficer` may lift a club subscription that the system suspended for compliance reasons, and a `WineryOperator` attempting it is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REINSTATE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). A `WineryOperator` may, however, release its own weather holds.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint in this API accepts two query parameters that control the window of results. `limit` sets the maximum number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets the number of items to skip before the window begins; when it is omitted it defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order. Ties are broken in favour of stable, repeatable output rather than insertion luck.

List responses report the window back to the caller in the `meta` object of the envelope, which carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of items matching the filter across the entire collection, not the number in the returned page, and `has_more` is true exactly when items remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock: One Source of "Now"

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every temporal judgement the API makes — whether a permit is inside its validity window, whether a consumer has reached legal purchasing age, whether a ledger entry falls inside a rolling window, whether a weather hold has lapsed, whether a reporting period has closed, what instant to stamp on `cleared_at`, `delivered_at`, `returned_at`, `decided_at`, `approved_at`, `filed_at`, `remitted_at`, `id_verified_at` or `renewal_filed_at` — resolves against a server-held **logical clock**. A `PlatformAdmin` moves that clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the administrative clock endpoint documented in §4, and every request thereafter evaluates its temporal rules against that instant.

The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Before any call has ever set it, the clock reads that default; it must not fall back to real wall-clock time, either at startup or at any later point. Where a rule speaks of "the clock date" rather than an instant, it means the UTC calendar date of the logical clock, and all such date arithmetic is genuine calendar-day arithmetic: adding a month means the same day-of-month in the following month, not a fixed thirty days.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Most resources in this system carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix — a winery's identifier and an order's identifier are indistinguishable by shape.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`), stamped from the logical clock when the row was created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative during a cascade.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation: when a ledger append forces the consumer's rolling volumes and risk scores to be recalculated, `Consumer.version` advances even though no client sent a field.

`Winery`, `StateRule`, `CarrierProgram`, `CarrierEnrollment`, `Consumer`, `StatePermit`, `ClubSubscription`, `Order`, `Shipment`, `ComplianceException` and `TaxRemittance` all carry the full set of four.

Three resource kinds are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for these, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely: **`ProhibitedZip`** (a prohibited-ZIP declaration is appended, never edited or withdrawn), **`OrderLine`** (a line is frozen with its computed money the moment its order is created), and **`VolumeLedgerEntry`** (the ledger is append-only by design; a correction is a new signed entry, never an edit).

### 2.6. Stale-Read Protection on the Two Headroom-Consuming Writes

Two write operations in this API consume shared headroom that another writer may have moved since the client last looked, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the *parent* resource's `version`.

Creating an order requires the request to carry `expected_consumer_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Consumer` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the consumer's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is specific to this domain: order creation reserves part of the consumer's rolling volume headroom, so a create issued from a stale read of the consumer would size its headroom against a superseded picture. Under the product's own semantics that second order may be perfectly lawful — which is exactly why it is the stale-version check, and not any state precondition, that must reject it.

Creating a carrier enrollment requires the request to carry `expected_winery_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Winery` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the winery's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Here the hazard is supersession: creating an enrollment demotes any enrollment the winery already holds on the same carrier program, so a create issued from a stale read of the winery would silently supersede an enrollment the client never saw.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind. Endpoints that are not one of these two do not take `expected_consumer_version`, `expected_winery_version`, or any equivalent, and must not require one.

### 2.7. Liters, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Two units of measure run through the whole system and each has one rounding rule.

**Volume** is carried in liters with exactly **3 decimal places**, rounded **half up**. This applies to a line's `line_liters`, an order's `total_liters`, a ledger entry's `liters`, the per-state figures inside `rolling_liters_by_state` and `rolling_liters_settled_by_state`, a remittance's `liters_total`, and every rolling or annual aggregate computed from them.

**Money** is carried in whole **cents** as integers, rounded **half up**. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the API, and no monetary value is ever represented as a string.

Two disciplines around rounding matter more than the rules themselves, because getting them wrong produces answers that are off by a cent or two and are therefore hard to notice. First, **every step that this document names as a rounding point rounds, and no intermediate is ever rounded twice**: if the charge chain says shipping is rounded to whole cents at one step and the tier multiplier is applied at a later step, the later step multiplies the already-rounded value and rounds again — it does not revisit the unrounded original. Second, where a tax is computed across several lines, the document states explicitly whether rounding happens per line and then sums, or on a summed base. These two orders give different totals and the difference is not negligible: two lines whose taxable bases are each 10,008 cents, taxed at 825 basis points, produce 826 cents plus 826 cents — 1,652 cents — when each line is rounded and then summed, but 1,651 cents when the bases are summed and the result rounded once. The per-line-then-sum order is the one this system uses for sales tax.

Rates expressed in **basis points** — `sales_tax_rate_bps` and the club shipping discount — are hundredths of a percent: a value of 825 means 8.25 percent, and the conversion is a division by 10,000.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Request Shape and Referential Integrity

Requests are admitted through three successive gates that all concern *whether the request can even be understood*, and they run in a fixed order.

Path identifiers are resolved first, and strictly **left to right**. Where a path nests one resource inside another — the prohibited-ZIP collection under a state rule, or the line, exception and screening operations under an order — the outer identifier is resolved before the inner one, and the request fails on the *first* segment that does not resolve to a stored row. The 404 that comes back names the entity of the failing segment, drawn from `ERR_WINERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATE_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARRIER_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARRIER_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSUMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLUB_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_VOLUME_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. A prohibited-ZIP creation aimed at a state rule that does not exist therefore returns `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, never a validation error about the ZIP itself.

The request body is checked second. Every required input field must be present and non-null; every field must carry its declared type; every enum-typed field must carry a declared member of that enum; and numeric fields must respect their ranges. The concrete bounds enforced here are: a line's `bottles` is at least 1; a line's `bottle_ml` is one of 375, 500, 750, 1000, 1500 or 3000; a line's `unit_price_cents` is at least 1; the `lines` array holds at least 1 and at most 20 elements; `sales_tax_rate_bps` lies between 0 and 2000 inclusive; a remittance's `period_start` is strictly before its `period_end`; `horizon_days` lies between 1 and 365 inclusive; and `run_date` parses as a valid ISO 8601 date. Unknown fields in a request body are ignored rather than rejected. Any failure at this gate is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Foreign keys named in the request *body* are resolved third. Every one of `winery_id`, `state_rule_id`, `consumer_id`, `carrier_program_id`, `carrier_enrollment_id`, `order_id`, `shipment_id`, `club_subscription_id`, `replacement_of_shipment_id` and `supersedes_remittance_id` must resolve to a stored row when supplied, and a supplied `ship_to_state_code` or `state_code` must have a `StateRule` row governing it. The 404 again names the entity that could not be found, using the same catalogue as path resolution; a `ship_to_state_code` with no configured state answers `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. Order of Evaluation

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the outcome the caller sees is determined entirely by which rule the server checks first. The full evaluation order, from first to last, is: header presence (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), role authorization (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), ownership scoping (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`), path-identifier existence (the 404 family), request-body shape (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), body foreign-key existence (the 404 family again), optimistic concurrency (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`), state-transition legality (the 409 family: `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SCREENED`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_CLEARED`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), winery operating status (`ERR_WINERY_SUSPENDED`), compliance-reinstatement authority (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_REINSTATE_FORBIDDEN`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and only then the domain rules of §3.

Within that final band of domain rules there is a further fixed ranking, and each rule below is listed in the order it is evaluated whenever more than one of them applies to the same request:

1. the destination state permits direct-to-consumer wine shipment at all — `ERR_STATE_DTC_PROHIBITED`;
2. the destination ZIP is not blocked — `ERR_PROHIBITED_DESTINATION`;
3. the purchaser is of legal age in the destination state — `ERR_CONSUMER_UNDERAGE`;
4. the purchaser's government ID has been verified — `ERR_ID_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`;
5. the winery holds some permit for the destination state — `ERR_PERMIT_MISSING_FOR_STATE`;
6. that permit is in force rather than merely on file — `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`;
7. that permit has not lapsed — `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`;
8. a new permit does not collide with an existing one — `ERR_PERMIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`;
9. a new remittance period does not collide with an existing one — `ERR_REMITTANCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`;
10. a weather hold respects the spacing rule — `ERR_HOLD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`;
11. the carrier-program requirement is satisfied — `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED`;
12. the enrolled program can actually offer an adult signature when one is required — `ERR_ADULT_SIGNATURE_NOT_SUPPORTED`;
13. a prohibited ZIP is not being declared twice — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROHIBITED_ZIP`;
14. a reroute stays inside the state the order cleared against — `ERR_REROUTE_STATE_CHANGE`;
15. a club run is not dated in the future — `ERR_RUN_DATE_IN_FUTURE`;
16. the consumer has volume headroom in the destination state — `ERR_CONSUMER_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`;
17. the winery has volume headroom in the destination state — `ERR_WINERY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`;
18. a filing has something to report — `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REMIT`;
19. the consumer is not flagged for elevated-risk review — `ERR_ELEVATED_RISK_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

Twelve of those nineteen rules form the **screening ladder** that compliance screening walks against an order: items 1 through 7, items 11 and 12, and items 16, 17 and 19. The remaining seven guard other operations and are described where those operations are.

The screening ladder has one property that distinguishes it from every other check in this document, and it is a deliberate product decision rather than an accident of implementation. **Screening never returns an error envelope.** It always answers HTTP 200 with the order body, and the outcome is expressed by moving the order's compliance dimension and writing the identifier of the *first* — that is, the highest-ranked — failing ladder rule into the order's `rejection_error_id`. A caller learns that screening failed by reading the order it gets back, not by catching an error. The very same identifiers, however, *are* raised as genuine HTTP errors by the label-time re-verification described in §3.7 and by the two other guarded writes noted alongside their rules, because at those points the caller is asking to take an irreversible action rather than asking for an assessment.

### 2.10. Cascades, Compensating Movements and Derived Counters

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource, and one operation — permit revocation — mutates three tiers. The convention is uniform: the server performs the whole cascade itself, in a defined order, without requiring a client request against each affected row, and **every row the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1**. A cascade never leaves a row half-updated: either the triggering operation succeeds and the whole cascade lands, or the operation fails and nothing moves.

Where a cascade replays over a set of rows rather than a single row, the processing order is stated so that the result is reproducible: suspended orders are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and club-run subscriptions are processed in the ranking given in §3.8.

Reversals follow a **compensating-entry** discipline rather than a delete-and-rewrite discipline. When an outcome has to be undone — a package comes back, a cleared order is re-screened into rejection — the system does not erase the original ledger entry or zero the original money field. It appends an offsetting ledger entry with the opposite sign, and it records the reversal in a dedicated field (`Order.excise_reversed_cents`, `Order.sales_tax_reversed_cents`, `Winery.excise_reversed_cents`) so that gross and net figures are both recoverable. Reversal fields on an order are all-or-nothing: each is either 0 or exactly equal to the corresponding gross figure, never a partial amount.

Finally, a family of fields on `Winery`, `Consumer` and `ClubSubscription` are **derived counters** — they are never supplied by a client and are never incrementally patched on faith. `Winery.excise_accrued_cents`, `Winery.excise_reversed_cents`, `Winery.suspended_subscription_count`, `Winery.open_exception_count`, `Consumer.rolling_liters_by_state`, `Consumer.rolling_liters_settled_by_state`, `Consumer.return_credit_count`, `Consumer.denied_exception_count`, `Consumer.risk_score_screening`, `Consumer.risk_score_audit`, `Consumer.screening_tier`, `ClubSubscription.missed_run_count`, `ClubSubscription.consumed_shipments` and `ClubSubscription.remaining_allotment` all exist because some other event moved, and each is described at the event that moves it. All of them start at their documented zero value — 0 for the integer counters, an empty map for the two liters maps, `STANDARD` for `screening_tier` — when the owning row is created.

### 2.11. One Order, Two Independent Status Dimensions

An `Order` row carries two status fields that are read and moved independently. `compliance_status` answers the licensing question and takes one of `DRAFT`, `CLEARED`, `REJECTED`, `OVERRIDE_PENDING` or `SUSPENDED_PERMIT`; it begins at `DRAFT` and `REJECTED` is its only terminal value. `fulfillment_status` answers the logistics question and takes one of `UNFULFILLED`, `LABELED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` or `RETURNED`; it begins at `UNFULFILLED` and `DELIVERED` is its only terminal value.

Neither dimension restricts the other's reachable values except where a rule in §3 says so in as many words. An order can be `CLEARED` and still `UNFULFILLED` for weeks; it can be `SUSPENDED_PERMIT` on the compliance axis while sitting at `UNFULFILLED` on the fulfillment axis; and — importantly for the risk model of §3.4 — a `CLEARED` order that has not yet been picked up is fully visible to volume limits but invisible to one of the two risk variants. Reading an order returns both dimensions, the recorded `rejection_error_id`, and every frozen money field.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Compliance Landscape

Before any order can be assessed, three kinds of reference data must exist: the winery that ships, the destination states that regulate, and the carriers licensed to carry alcohol.

A `PlatformAdmin` registers a **winery** by supplying its legal `name`, its federal basic permit number as `ttb_permit_number`, the two-letter code of its production state as `home_state_code`, and its `annual_production_gallons`. The TTB permit number must be unique across all wineries; `home_state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters; and `annual_production_gallons` is an integer of zero or more. That last figure looks like trivia but is load-bearing: it is the sole input that decides whether the winery qualifies for the small-producer excise credit described in §3.5. A freshly registered winery opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` null, with `excise_accrued_cents`, `excise_reversed_cents`, `suspended_subscription_count` and `open_exception_count` all at 0, and at `version` 1.

A winery's operating status has exactly two values, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and only a `PlatformAdmin` may move between them. Suspending an `ACTIVE` winery — an `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` move — accepts an optional free-text `suspension_reason` which is recorded on the row; reinstating a `SUSPENDED` winery — `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` — clears `suspension_reason` back to null. Neither move is available from the state it targets: reinstating a winery that is already `ACTIVE`, like suspending one that is already `SUSPENDED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. While a winery is `SUSPENDED`, five operations on its behalf are refused with `ERR_WINERY_SUSPENDED`: creating an order, producing a shipping label, opening a club subscription, filing a permit renewal, and running the wine-club scheduler. Reads are unaffected.

A **state rule** is the compliance configuration of one destination state, declared by a `PlatformAdmin`, and it is the single row that governs an entire order once that order names the state. Its `state_code` is exactly two uppercase letters and unique across all state rules. `allows_dtc` says whether direct shipment into the state is lawful at all. `per_consumer_limit_liters` caps how much wine one consumer may receive from one winery within the window named by `per_consumer_period`, which is one of `ROLLING_12_MONTHS`, `CALENDAR_YEAR` or `ROLLING_30_DAYS`; `per_winery_annual_limit_liters` caps how much one winery may ship into the state within a calendar year. Both limits are positive numbers carried to three decimal places. The tax parameters are `excise_rate_per_liter_cents` (zero or more cents charged on every liter), `large_format_surcharge_cents_per_liter` (zero or more additional cents per liter for oversized bottles), `excise_cap_cents_per_shipment` (a positive hard ceiling on a single order's excise), `sales_tax_rate_bps` (the destination sales-tax rate in basis points, between 0 and 2000 inclusive), and two booleans, `tax_on_shipping` and `excise_in_tax_base`, that decide whether shipping charges and excise respectively enter the sales-tax base. `adult_signature_required` says whether delivery demands an age-verified adult signature and `requires_carrier_program` says whether the winery must hold an approved carrier enrollment before shipping into the state. `reporting_frequency` is `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL` and sets the cadence of the state's mandatory report; `zero_filing_required` says whether a period with no qualifying orders must be filed anyway. Two fields have defaults and may be omitted: `min_purchaser_age`, the minimum purchaser age in whole years, defaults to 21, and `renewal_lead_days`, the number of days before a permit's expiration at which renewal becomes due, defaults to 60.

Dry counties and prohibited municipalities are not modelled as geography. They are enumerated as **prohibited ZIPs** hung beneath a state rule by either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, each carrying the owning `state_rule_id`, a `zip_code` of exactly five digits, a `reason` drawn from `DRY_COUNTY`, `PROHIBITED_MUNICIPALITY`, `PO_BOX_ONLY` or `CARRIER_UNSERVED`, and an `effective_from` date. A dry county is therefore enforced through the ZIPs someone has taken the trouble to list for it. The pair of `state_rule_id` and `zip_code` must be unique — a repeat declaration is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROHIBITED_ZIP` (HTTP 409). A prohibited-ZIP row is immutable once appended: there is no update operation, no withdrawal, and no `version` on the row. Screening honours a block only when its `effective_from` falls on or before the logical clock date; a block dated strictly after the clock date is ignored entirely, which is what lets a state pre-announce a block for a future date. The reverse lookup of every ZIP declared under a state rule can be filtered by `reason`.

Carriers are described in two layers. A **carrier program** is declared by a `PlatformAdmin` and represents one carrier's licensed alcohol offering: `carrier_code` is one of `UPS`, `FEDEX` or `GSO`, `program_name` is the display name, `states_served` is a non-empty array of two-letter state codes the program will deliver into, `requires_enrollment` says whether the carrier demands a per-winery enrollment before labels may be produced, and `adult_signature_service_code` is the carrier service code that enforces an age-verified adult signature. That last field is optional and defaults to null, and a null value means precisely that the program cannot offer an adult-signature service at all — a fact with teeth, as §3.3 shows. Programs may be listed filtered by `carrier_code`.

### 3.2. Destination-State Licensing: Permits, Renewals and Revocation

A **state permit** is the winery's licence to ship into one destination state, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may file one. Filing supplies the `winery_id` of the holder, the `state_code` the permit authorizes — which must match an existing state rule — the state-issued `permit_number`, a `permit_type` of `DIRECT_SHIPPER`, `WINE_SHIPPER` or `COMMON_CARRIER_ENDORSEMENT`, and the two dates that bound its authority: `effective_date`, the first date on which it authorizes shipment, and `expiration_date`, the last. Those two dates form a **closed interval** — both endpoints are inside the permit's authority.

Because two overlapping licences for the same state would make it ambiguous which one authorized a shipment, a new permit's closed interval must not overlap the closed interval of any non-revoked permit the same winery already holds for the same state. Sharing a single endpoint date already counts as an overlap: a new permit whose `effective_date` falls exactly on an existing permit's `expiration_date` collides, and the filing is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Revoked permits are excluded from this comparison, so a revoked licence never blocks a replacement.

A filed permit opens in `PENDING`, and a `PENDING` permit authorizes nothing. A `ComplianceOfficer` activates it — a `PENDING` → `ACTIVE` move — and that is only permitted once the permit's `effective_date` has arrived, meaning `effective_date` falls on or before the logical clock date. Activation from any state other than `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Once `ACTIVE`, the permit is what allows orders into its state to clear.

Renewal is a cycle that returns the same permit row to `ACTIVE` rather than creating a new row. Filing a renewal — available to both a `ComplianceOfficer` and the `WineryOperator` that holds the permit — records a `renewal_reference` supplied in the request and stamps `renewal_filed_at` from the logical clock. From `ACTIVE`, the filing has a timing precondition: the logical clock date must have reached the renewal window, that is, it must be on or after the permit's `expiration_date` minus the governing state rule's `renewal_lead_days` days. Filing earlier than that is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. From `EXPIRED`, by contrast, no lead-time precondition applies at all — a permit that has already lapsed may have its renewal filed at any time. Both filings land the permit in `RENEWAL_FILED`, and this is the subtle part: **a permit in `RENEWAL_FILED` no longer authorizes new clearances.** Screening an order into that state while its permit sits in `RENEWAL_FILED` records `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, exactly as a `PENDING` permit would. Filing a renewal is also one of the five operations blocked while the winery itself is `SUSPENDED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` closes the cycle by approving the filed renewal — a `RENEWAL_FILED` → `ACTIVE` move — supplying a new `expiration_date` in the request. The supplied date must be strictly later than the permit's current `expiration_date`; a date equal to or earlier than the current one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, as is any approval attempt against a permit that is not in `RENEWAL_FILED`. On success the permit's `expiration_date` is replaced by the supplied value, `renewal_cycle_count` is increased by 1, and both `renewal_reference` and `renewal_filed_at` are cleared back to null — leaving the row ready to repeat the same `ACTIVE` → `RENEWAL_FILED` → `ACTIVE` cycle at the next expiry.

Expiry is not something anyone requests; the system observes it. Whenever the logical clock date has moved strictly past an `ACTIVE` permit's `expiration_date`, that permit is driven `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED` — its `status` becomes `EXPIRED` and its `version` increments by 1 — at the moment the system next looks at it, which happens during order screening, during a wine-club run, and when the renewal calendar is rendered. The screening call that notices the lapse both performs the transition and records `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED` on the order it was screening; the calendar read performs the transition before it renders.

Revocation is the most consequential act in this section, reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, available from `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_FILED` and `EXPIRED` alike, and terminal — a `REVOKED` permit can never move again, and attempting to revoke one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The request supplies a `revocation_reason`, which is recorded on the row alongside the new `REVOKED` status. Revocation then fires a three-tier cascade, and each tier fires **because** the previous one did:

- **First**, every order of that winery bound for that state whose `compliance_status` is `CLEARED` and whose `fulfillment_status` is still `UNFULFILLED` is moved `CLEARED` → `SUSPENDED_PERMIT`, with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` written into its `rejection_error_id`. These orders are processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the cascade is replayable. Orders that have already been labelled, picked up, delivered or returned are left alone — the wine is already gone. Critically, the volume the suspended order consumed is **not** given back: the ledger entry that debited it stays exactly where it is, because suspension is not a terminal outcome and the liters remain reserved for an order that may yet be re-screened and cleared.
- **Second**, because those orders left `CLEARED`, any club subscription named by a suspended order's `club_subscription_id` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is moved `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE`, and its `next_run_date` is set to null so that it can never be picked up by a future run.
- **Third**, because those subscriptions changed status, the winery's `suspended_subscription_count` is recomputed as the number of that winery's subscriptions now sitting in `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE`, and `Winery.version` increments by 1.

### 3.3. Carrier Enrollment and the Adult-Signature Chain

Where a state rule sets `requires_carrier_program`, a winery cannot ship into that state on goodwill; it needs a live **carrier enrollment**. Either a `WineryOperator` acting for itself or a `ComplianceOfficer` acting on its behalf opens one, naming the `winery_id`, the `carrier_program_id` of the program being joined, the `account_number` the carrier issued to the winery, and — because the write is stale-read guarded per §2.6 — `expected_winery_version`. A new enrollment opens in `status` `PENDING` with `approved_at` and `decided_by_user_id` both null.

Enrollment creation carries a supersession side effect that is easy to overlook: at most one enrollment per winery-and-program pair may be live at a time, so any enrollment the winery already holds for the same `carrier_program_id` in `PENDING` or `APPROVED` is moved by the system to `SUSPENDED` as part of the same call, and `Winery.version` increments by 1. That supersession is precisely the hazard §2.6 guards — a create issued from a stale read of the winery would quietly demote an enrollment the client had never seen — which is why a mismatched `expected_winery_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before any of this happens.

Only a `CarrierAgent` decides an enrollment, submitting a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` together with an optional `note`. Approval is reachable from both `PENDING` and `SUSPENDED` — a `PENDING` → `APPROVED` first approval and a `SUSPENDED` → `APPROVED` reinstatement are both legitimate, and the reinstatement path is what closes the `APPROVED` → `SUSPENDED` → `APPROVED` cycle on a single enrollment row. Suspension is reachable only from `APPROVED`. Every decision records `decided_by_user_id` from the deciding agent's `X-User-ID`, and every decision that lands on `APPROVED` — first approval or reinstatement alike — stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock. A decision that merely repeats the enrollment's current standing, such as approving an already `APPROVED` enrollment, is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409). While an enrollment sits in `SUSPENDED`, no new label may be drawn against it; the attempt is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED`.

Two screening rules connect the carrier layer back to the order, and they sit adjacent in the evaluation order of §2.9 for a reason — the second only makes sense once the first has found an enrollment. When the destination state rule's `requires_carrier_program` is true, the winery must hold an enrollment in `APPROVED` whose program's `states_served` array contains the order's `ship_to_state_code`; otherwise the outcome is `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED`. Then, when the order's own `adult_signature_required` is true, that same enrollment's program must have a non-null `adult_signature_service_code`; a program that cannot offer an age-verified signature service cannot lawfully carry a signature-required shipment, and the outcome is `ERR_ADULT_SIGNATURE_NOT_SUPPORTED`. A program's `requires_enrollment` flag documents the carrier's own commercial demand for enrollment; it is the state rule's `requires_carrier_program` that decides whether screening insists on one.

### 3.4. Consumers, Identity Verification and the Volume Ledger

A **consumer** is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` with the purchaser's `full_name` as it will be printed on the adult-signature label, a unique contact `email`, a `birthdate`, and a default shipping address comprising `address_line`, `city`, `state_code` and `zip_code`. The default address is a convenience, not a constraint — an order may ship to a different state entirely, and the state rule that governs an order is chosen by the order's own destination, not by the consumer's home state. A new consumer opens with `id_verification_method` at `NONE`, `id_verified_at` null, `rolling_liters_by_state` and `rolling_liters_settled_by_state` both empty maps, `return_credit_count` and `denied_exception_count` at 0, `risk_score_screening` and `risk_score_audit` at 0, and `screening_tier` at `STANDARD`. Consumers may be listed filtered by `state_code` and by `screening_tier`.

Identity verification is a `ComplianceOfficer` act. The officer records the method used — `id_verification_method` set to `DOCUMENT` or `DATABASE`, the third declared value `NONE` being the unverified starting state — and the system stamps `id_verified_at` from the logical clock and increments `Consumer.version`. Until `id_verified_at` is non-null, screening records `ERR_ID_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` against any order for that consumer. The age gate is evaluated separately and against the destination state's own threshold: the consumer's completed whole years between `birthdate` and the logical clock date, computed as calendar anniversaries with the birthday itself counting as the day the year completes, must be at least the state rule's `min_purchaser_age`; falling short records `ERR_CONSUMER_UNDERAGE`.

Everything the system knows about how much wine a consumer has received is derived from the **volume ledger**, an append-only series of signed entries. Each entry names the `consumer_id` whose rolling volume it moves and the `winery_id` whose annual state volume it moves; carries the `state_code` the volume is attributed to; carries `liters` as a signed number to three decimal places, where a positive value consumes limit headroom and a negative value releases it; carries an `event_type` of `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED`, `RETURN_CREDIT` or `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`; carries `occurred_at`, the instant the movement is attributed to, which defaults to the logical clock; and optionally references the `order_id` and `shipment_id` that produced it, plus a `reason` narrative. `order_id` is null for a manual adjustment; `shipment_id` is null both for a manual adjustment and for a `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED` entry, since acceptance happens at clearance, before any label exists. `reason` carries the narrative for a manual adjustment and is null otherwise. Entries are immutable and unversioned, and they can be listed filtered by `consumer_id`, `winery_id`, `order_id`, `shipment_id`, `state_code` and `event_type` — that list is the audit trail behind every volume decision the system has ever made.

One entry type is created directly rather than as a side effect: a `ComplianceOfficer` may append a **manual adjustment**, which is the compliance department's tool for correcting historical volume and the only way to seed volume dated outside the current rolling window. The officer supplies `consumer_id`, `winery_id`, `state_code`, a signed `liters` value, an `occurred_at` instant and a `reason`; the system stamps `event_type` as `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`. A positive adjustment is not a free pass: if it would push the consumer past the destination state's `per_consumer_limit_liters` or push the winery past its `per_winery_annual_limit_liters`, the append is rejected outright with `ERR_CONSUMER_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_WINERY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` respectively — raised here as real HTTP 422 errors rather than merely recorded, because there is no order to record them on. A negative adjustment, which only releases headroom, is unconstrained by those two rules.

**Rolling volume** is recomputed for a consumer every time a ledger entry is appended — at order clearance, at a return-to-sender credit, at a re-ship re-debit, and at a manual adjustment. For each state, the window is chosen by that state's `per_consumer_period`. `ROLLING_12_MONTHS` admits entries whose `occurred_at` is on or after the logical clock instant minus 365 days and on or before the logical clock instant. `ROLLING_30_DAYS` admits entries whose `occurred_at` is on or after the logical clock instant minus 30 days and on or before it. `CALENDAR_YEAR` admits entries whose `occurred_at` is on or after January 1 at `00:00:00.000Z` of the logical clock's own year and on or before the logical clock instant. All three windows are **inclusive at the lower edge**: an entry whose `occurred_at` is exactly the clock instant minus 365 days is counted. `rolling_liters_by_state` then holds, for each state, the signed sum of that consumer's ledger entries for that state whose `occurred_at` falls in the window, rounded half up to three decimal places — every event type included, with `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED` adding, `RETURN_CREDIT` subtracting, and `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` doing either according to its sign. Aggregation keys on `occurred_at` and never on `created_at`, which is exactly what makes back-dated manual adjustments meaningful.

`rolling_liters_settled_by_state` is the same sum over the same window, restricted to entries whose `order_id` is null or whose order's `fulfillment_status` is one of `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` or `RETURNED`. The distinction is deliberate and consequential. The volume-limit check of §3.6 reads `rolling_liters_by_state`, so a cleared order that has not yet been labelled still consumes headroom. The settled map — the one that ignores orders still sitting at `UNFULFILLED` or `LABELED` — is consumed only by the screening variant of the risk score. The **winery's** annual state volume is a separate aggregate with a fixed window: for a winery-and-state pair it is the signed sum, rounded half up to three decimal places, of all ledger entries for that pair — across every consumer, every event type — whose `occurred_at` falls on or after January 1 at `00:00:00.000Z` of the logical clock's calendar year and on or before the logical clock instant. That window is always the calendar year regardless of what `per_consumer_period` the state has configured for consumers.

Two counters feed the risk model. `return_credit_count` is the number of that consumer's ledger entries with `event_type` `RETURN_CREDIT` falling inside the widest `per_consumer_period` window in play, and `denied_exception_count` is the number of compliance exceptions in status `DENIED` raised on that consumer's orders.

**Consumer risk** is scored out of 100 from three weighted factors, and the score is recomputed whenever a ledger entry is appended, whenever a compliance exception is denied, and whenever an order's `fulfillment_status` changes. The first factor is 10 points per in-scope return credit, capped at 40. The second factor is 15 points per in-scope denied exception, capped at 30. The third factor is a utilisation term: among the states present in the relevant liters map, the system selects the state with the greatest ratio of accumulated liters to that state's `per_consumer_limit_liters`, breaking a tie in favour of the alphabetically earlier `state_code`; the factor is then 30 multiplied by the selected state's liters divided by that state's per-consumer limit, truncated downward to a whole number, and capped at 30. An empty liters map yields a third factor of 0. The three factors are added and the sum is capped at 100.

That single formula is evaluated twice against two different scopes, and the two results live side by side on the consumer. The **screening** variant is written to `risk_score_screening` and is the only variant that gates anything: its scope is ledger entries inside the state's `per_consumer_period` window whose `order_id` is null or whose order's `fulfillment_status` is `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` or `RETURNED`, its utilisation term reads `rolling_liters_settled_by_state`, and its denial count includes only exceptions decided inside that same window. A cleared order still sitting at `UNFULFILLED` or `LABELED` is invisible to this variant. The **audit** variant is written to `risk_score_audit` and never gates any operation: its scope is every ledger entry of the consumer for all time and every `DENIED` exception for all time, regardless of the source order's `fulfillment_status`, and its utilisation term reads the all-time, all-status liters per state. The two therefore diverge routinely rather than exceptionally — a consumer holding a 22.500-litre adjustment dated outside the rolling window plus one cleared but unfulfilled 4.500-litre order against a 27.000-litre limit scores 0 on the screening variant and 30 on the audit variant.

From the screening score alone the system derives `screening_tier`, which is `STANDARD` while `risk_score_screening` is below 50 and becomes `ELEVATED` as soon as `risk_score_screening` reaches 50 or more. `ELEVATED` has two consequences, described where each applies: it makes screening record an overridable failure, and it blocks the opening of any new club subscription for that consumer.

A consumer's whole volume picture is available as a single computed read that reports the `consumer_id`, an `as_of` instant taken from the logical clock, a `per_state` map — keyed by state code, giving that state's accumulated rolling liters, the state's per-consumer limit, and the headroom remaining between them — and, side by side, `risk_score_screening`, `risk_score_audit`, `screening_tier`, `return_credit_count` and `denied_exception_count`. An optional `state_code` filter narrows the `per_state` map to one state.

### 3.5. Placing an Order: Lines, Liters and the Charge Chain

An order may be created by the buying `Consumer` acting for itself, by the `WineryOperator` shipping it, or by a `ComplianceOfficer` entering it on behalf of either. The request names the `winery_id` of the shipping licensee, the `consumer_id` of the purchaser, optionally a `club_subscription_id` when the order belongs to a wine-club run or is being attributed to a club member by hand, the destination as `ship_to_address_line`, `ship_to_state_code` and `ship_to_zip`, a `lines` array of one to twenty elements, and `expected_consumer_version` for the stale-read guard of §2.6. Each element of `lines` carries a product `sku`, a `bottle_ml` bottle size, a `bottles` count and a `unit_price_cents` per-bottle price. The destination state code is what selects the state rule that will govern the entire order — its limits, its tax parameters, its signature requirement, everything — and the `club_subscription_id` field is null for a one-off order.

Creation records one line per element of the `lines` array and assigns each an `order_id` pointing back at the order plus a `line_index` running from 1 upward in exactly the request's array order. That index is not decoration: it is the deterministic tie-break used when the sales-tax allocation below has whole cents left to hand out. The order itself opens with `compliance_status` `DRAFT` and `fulfillment_status` `UNFULFILLED`, with `rejection_error_id` null, `override_applied` false, `cleared_at` and `returned_at` null, and both `excise_reversed_cents` and `sales_tax_reversed_cents` at 0. Creation also increments `Consumer.version` by 1. The order's `adult_signature_required` flag is taken from the governing state rule's `adult_signature_required` at creation and re-asserted from the same state rule when the order's compliance dimension first reaches `CLEARED`; because the source is the same row in both cases, the recorded value is the state's requirement either way. Orders may be listed filtered by `winery_id`, `consumer_id`, `club_subscription_id`, `compliance_status` and `fulfillment_status`, and an order's lines are available as a reverse lookup that exposes each line's computed liters, allocated shipping and per-line sales tax.

Every monetary and volumetric field on the order and on each of its lines is computed exactly once, at creation, from the governing state rule and the winery, and then **frozen**. Nothing later in the order's life recomputes them — not screening, not an override, not a return. The computation is a twelve-step chain and the step order is pinned; two of the steps are pinned against a plausible alternative that produces different cents, and both are called out below.

1. For each line, in `line_index` order: `line_liters` is the bottle count multiplied by the bottle size in millilitres, divided by 1,000, rounded half up to three decimal places; and `line_subtotal_cents` is the bottle count multiplied by the per-bottle price in cents.
2. `total_liters` is the sum of every line's `line_liters`, rounded half up to three decimal places, and `subtotal_cents` is the sum of every line's `line_subtotal_cents`.
3. `shipping_gross_cents` is a flat base of 1,500 cents — fifteen dollars — plus 220 cents for every litre of `total_liters`, the whole expression rounded half up to whole cents.
4. A liter-tier multiplier is selected from `total_liters`: it is 1.00 when `total_liters` is at most 9.000 litres, 0.90 when `total_liters` is greater than 9.000 litres and at most 18.000 litres, and 0.80 when `total_liters` is greater than 18.000 litres.
5. `shipping_tiered_cents` is `shipping_gross_cents` multiplied by that tier multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents.
6. `shipping_discount_cents` is the wine-club shipping concession: when `club_subscription_id` is not null it is 2,500 basis points — one quarter — of `shipping_tiered_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; when `club_subscription_id` is null it is 0.
7. `shipping_cents` is `shipping_tiered_cents` less `shipping_discount_cents`.
8. `excise_tax_cents` is computed as described below; it needs only `total_liters` and the per-line bottle sizes.
9. `sales_tax_cents` is computed as described below; it needs `shipping_cents` from step 7 and `excise_tax_cents` from step 8, which is why it cannot run earlier.
10. `pre_fee_total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `shipping_cents` plus `excise_tax_cents` plus `sales_tax_cents`.
11. `compliance_handling_fee_cents` is 0 when `pre_fee_total_cents` is at least 20,000 cents — two hundred dollars — and otherwise 900 cents.
12. `total_cents` is `pre_fee_total_cents` plus `compliance_handling_fee_cents`.

The first pinning concerns step 11. The waiver comparison reads `pre_fee_total_cents`, and `pre_fee_total_cents` is the **only** quantity in the system ever compared against the two-hundred-dollar waiver threshold. Adding the fee first and then testing the resulting `total_cents` would flip the decision for every order whose `pre_fee_total_cents` lands between 19,100 cents inclusive and 20,000 cents exclusive: the fee would be waived and `total_cents` would come out 900 cents lower than it should. The chain looks circular precisely because step 11's decision reads a total that step 12 then grows, and it is well defined only because the comparison is pinned to the pre-fee figure. The second pinning concerns steps 5 and 6. The liter-tier multiplier is applied to the gross shipping charge **before** the club discount is taken, not after. Applying the club discount first and the tier multiplier second yields a different cent value whenever both are non-trivial, because each of the two steps rounds half up independently.

**Excise** is computed from the order's own lines and the destination state's rates. A base amount is `total_liters` multiplied by the state's `excise_rate_per_liter_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. A large-format surcharge is added on top: the system sums `line_liters` across only those lines whose `bottle_ml` is **strictly greater** than 1500 — a 1500 ml line contributes exactly zero surcharge litres — rounds that sum half up to three decimal places, multiplies it by the state's `large_format_surcharge_cents_per_liter`, and rounds half up to whole cents. Base plus surcharge is the gross excise. The small-producer credit is then applied: a winery whose `annual_production_gallons` is at most 250,000 earns a credit of 1,500 basis points — fifteen percent — of the gross excise, while a winery above that production figure earns no credit at all. The credit amount is rounded half up to whole cents **before** it is subtracted, and the difference is the credited excise. Finally, and strictly last, the state's `excise_cap_cents_per_shipment` is applied as a ceiling: `excise_tax_cents` is the lesser of the credited excise and the cap. The order matters — the cap is applied after the credit, never before.

**Sales tax** is computed per line and then summed, using a waterfall allocation of the taxable shipping-and-excise pool across the lines. First the pool is assembled: it consists of `shipping_cents` when the state's `tax_on_shipping` is true and nothing otherwise, plus `excise_tax_cents` when the state's `excise_in_tax_base` is true and nothing otherwise. The `compliance_handling_fee_cents` never enters the sales-tax base under any state's configuration. Each line's raw share of that pool is the pool multiplied by the line's `line_liters` divided by the order's `total_liters`; the line's `allocated_shipping_cents` is that raw share truncated downward to a whole cent. Truncating every line leaves a remainder — the pool less the sum of the truncated shares — and those remaining whole cents are handed out one at a time, walking the lines ordered by `line_liters` descending and then by `line_index` ascending, never by identifier, giving one extra cent to each line in turn until the remainder is exhausted. Each line's `line_taxable_cents` is then its `line_subtotal_cents` plus its final `allocated_shipping_cents`, and its `line_sales_tax_cents` is `line_taxable_cents` multiplied by the state's `sales_tax_rate_bps` divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. The order's `sales_tax_cents` is the sum of the per-line figures. Rounding happens per line and the rounded figures are then added — never the other way round, as §2.7 explains with a worked cent. Where a state's `sales_tax_rate_bps` is 0, every line's `line_sales_tax_cents` is 0, but the allocation still runs and each line's `allocated_shipping_cents` is still recorded for audit.

### 3.6. Screening, Rejection and the Override Path

Screening is the decisive act of the whole system. Either a `ComplianceOfficer` or the `WineryOperator` that owns the order may run it, and the outcome moves the order's compliance dimension. Only two starting states admit a screen: `DRAFT`, the ordinary case, and `SUSPENDED_PERMIT`, the re-screen of an order that a revocation had frozen. An order already sitting at `CLEARED`, `REJECTED` or `OVERRIDE_PENDING` cannot be screened again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SCREENED` (HTTP 409). Recall from §2.9 that a screen which *runs* always answers HTTP 200 with the order body; a failing check is recorded in `rejection_error_id`, never returned as an error envelope.

The ladder is walked in the fixed order established in §2.9, and the first failure decides everything:

- **The state must allow direct shipment.** If the governing state rule's `allows_dtc` is false, the recorded outcome is `ERR_STATE_DTC_PROHIBITED`.
- **The destination ZIP must not be blocked.** A prohibited-ZIP row of the destination state matches when its `zip_code` equals the order's `ship_to_zip` and its `effective_from` falls on or before the logical clock date; any `reason` value blocks, `DRY_COUNTY` included. A matching row records `ERR_PROHIBITED_DESTINATION`. Rows whose `effective_from` is strictly after the clock date are ignored.
- **The purchaser must be old enough.** Completed whole years from `birthdate` to the logical clock date, counted by calendar anniversary, must be at least the state's `min_purchaser_age`; otherwise `ERR_CONSUMER_UNDERAGE`.
- **The purchaser's identity must be verified.** `id_verified_at` must be non-null; otherwise `ERR_ID_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`.
- **A licence must exist for the destination state.** The winery must hold a state permit for the order's `ship_to_state_code` in any status other than `REVOKED`; if it holds none, `ERR_PERMIT_MISSING_FOR_STATE`.
- **That licence must be in force.** Its `status` must be `ACTIVE`. A permit in `PENDING`, `RENEWAL_FILED` or `REVOKED` authorizes no clearance and records `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`.
- **That licence must not have lapsed.** The logical clock date must fall inside the permit's closed window — on or after `effective_date` and on or before `expiration_date`, both endpoints inclusive. Outside it, the outcome is `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`, and if the lapse is being noticed for the first time the permit is driven `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED` first, before the identifier is recorded.
- **The carrier requirement must be satisfied**, exactly as §3.3 describes, failing with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED`.
- **The enrolled program must support an adult signature** when the order requires one, failing with `ERR_ADULT_SIGNATURE_NOT_SUPPORTED`.
- **The consumer must have volume headroom.** The consumer's accumulated rolling liters for the destination state, taken from `rolling_liters_by_state`, plus this order's `total_liters`, must be at most the state's `per_consumer_limit_liters`. Landing exactly on the limit clears; exceeding it by any amount at all fails with `ERR_CONSUMER_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. This failure is **overridable**.
- **The winery must have volume headroom.** The winery's calendar-year accumulated liters into the destination state plus this order's `total_liters` must be at most the state's `per_winery_annual_limit_liters`. Equality clears; exceeding fails with `ERR_WINERY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. This failure is **overridable**.
- **The consumer must not be flagged for review.** `screening_tier` must be `STANDARD`; an `ELEVATED` consumer, meaning `risk_score_screening` of 50 or more, requires manual compliance review and records `ERR_ELEVATED_RISK_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. This failure is **overridable**.

Three outcomes follow. If every check passes, the order clears. If the highest-ranked failing check is not one of the three overridable ones, the order is rejected and — since `REJECTED` is terminal on the compliance axis — the order is finished. If the highest-ranked failing check *is* one of `ERR_CONSUMER_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_WINERY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_ELEVATED_RISK_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, the order moves to `OVERRIDE_PENDING`, which is an invitation to the exception workflow rather than a refusal. Note that "highest-ranked" is what selects the recorded identifier: an order that both ships into a prohibited ZIP and exceeds its consumer limit is rejected outright on the ZIP, because the ZIP rule outranks the volume rule and the overridable rule is never reached.

A **`DRAFT` → `CLEARED`** clearance is the system's most consequential single mutation, and it does five things at once. It stamps `cleared_at` from the logical clock and clears `rejection_error_id` back to null. It affirms `adult_signature_required` from the governing state rule. It appends a volume-ledger entry with `event_type` `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED`, `liters` equal to positive `total_liters`, `occurred_at` at the logical clock, `order_id` pointing at the order, `shipment_id` null, and `state_code` equal to the order's `ship_to_state_code`. Because that entry now exists, the consumer's `rolling_liters_by_state`, `rolling_liters_settled_by_state`, `risk_score_screening`, `risk_score_audit` and `screening_tier` are all recomputed and `Consumer.version` increments by 1. The winery's `excise_accrued_cents` grows by the order's `excise_tax_cents` — that counter is the running sum of `excise_tax_cents` across every order of the winery that has reached `CLEARED` at least once — and `Winery.version` increments by 1. Finally, when the order names a `club_subscription_id`, that subscription's `consumed_shipments` grows by 1 and its `remaining_allotment` is recomputed as `annual_allotment` less `consumed_shipments`.

A **`DRAFT` → `REJECTED`** outcome writes the failing identifier into `rejection_error_id` and does nothing else: no ledger entry is appended, and `Winery.excise_accrued_cents` is untouched. A **`DRAFT` → `OVERRIDE_PENDING`** outcome likewise writes the overridable identifier into `rejection_error_id` and appends no ledger entry — the liters are not reserved while the exception is pending.

Re-screening a `SUSPENDED_PERMIT` order behaves differently in one crucial respect, because that order's liters were **never released** when the revocation froze it. A **`SUSPENDED_PERMIT` → `CLEARED`** re-clearance — which requires a fresh `ACTIVE` permit for the destination state and every other ladder check to pass — clears `rejection_error_id` to null and deliberately leaves `cleared_at` at its original value. No second `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED` entry is appended, and `Winery.excise_accrued_cents` is **not** incremented a second time, because the original debit and the original accrual were never reversed. A **`SUSPENDED_PERMIT` → `REJECTED`** outcome is where the reversal finally happens: the failing identifier is written to `rejection_error_id`, a `RETURN_CREDIT` ledger entry is appended with `liters` equal to negative `total_liters`, `order_id` set and `shipment_id` null, reversing the original debit; the order's `excise_reversed_cents` is set to its `excise_tax_cents` and its `sales_tax_reversed_cents` to its `sales_tax_cents`; the winery's `excise_reversed_cents` grows by the order's `excise_tax_cents`; and because a reversing entry now exists, the consumer's rolling and risk fields are recomputed with `Consumer.version` incrementing by 1. A **`SUSPENDED_PERMIT` → `OVERRIDE_PENDING`** outcome, reached when the highest-ranked failure is overridable, records the identifier and leaves the ledger untouched.

An order sitting in `OVERRIDE_PENDING` is resolved through a **compliance exception**, which only a `ComplianceOfficer` may raise. Raising one supplies `raised_rule` — one of `ERR_CONSUMER_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_WINERY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_ELEVATED_RISK_REVIEW_REQUIRED` — and a `justification` narrative that goes into the state compliance log. The supplied `raised_rule` must equal the order's currently recorded `rejection_error_id`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, which prevents an officer from overriding a rule the order never actually failed. The exception opens in `status` `OPEN` with `requested_by_user_id` set from the raising officer's `X-User-ID`, with `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `decided_at` all null, and the winery's `open_exception_count` — the number of `OPEN` exceptions on that winery's orders — grows by 1. Exceptions on an order can be listed filtered by `status`.

The decision is a fork with two branches carrying **disjoint** side-effect sets, and both branches share three preconditions: the exception's `status` must be `OPEN`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALREADY_DECIDED`; the deciding officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `requested_by_user_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the referenced order must be in `OVERRIDE_PENDING`. The request carries a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `DENIED` plus an optional `decision_note`, and either branch records `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `decided_at` on the exception.

An **`OPEN` → `APPROVED`** decision moves the order `OVERRIDE_PENDING` → `CLEARED`, sets its `override_applied` flag to true, clears its `rejection_error_id` to null, and stamps `cleared_at` from the logical clock. The full clearance chain then fires exactly as it does for a `DRAFT` → `CLEARED` screen: a `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED` entry is appended for the positive `total_liters`, the consumer's rolling and risk fields are recomputed with `Consumer.version` incrementing, the winery's `excise_accrued_cents` grows by the order's `excise_tax_cents`, and a named club subscription has its `consumed_shipments` and `remaining_allotment` moved. The winery's `open_exception_count` falls by 1.

An **`OPEN` → `DENIED`** decision moves the order `OVERRIDE_PENDING` → `REJECTED` and leaves `rejection_error_id` holding the overridable identifier that caused the referral in the first place — the record of *why* the order died is preserved rather than cleared. No ledger entry is appended and `Winery.excise_accrued_cents` is untouched, so the liters and the excise never accrue at all. Instead the consumer's `denied_exception_count` grows by 1, and because that counter moved, `risk_score_screening`, `risk_score_audit` and `screening_tier` are recomputed and `Consumer.version` increments by 1 — which is how a pattern of denied overrides eventually pushes a consumer to `ELEVATED` and starts blocking their orders at the risk gate. The winery's `open_exception_count` falls by 1 on this branch too.

### 3.7. Fulfillment: Labels, Scans, Reroutes and Returns

Producing the carrier label is reserved to the `WineryOperator` and is the moment at which the compliance decision becomes irreversible. The request names the `order_id` being shipped, the `carrier_enrollment_id` the label is drawn against, and, when re-shipping a package that came back, the `replacement_of_shipment_id` of the returned shipment.

The order must be `CLEARED` on the compliance axis and either `UNFULFILLED` or — for a re-ship — `RETURNED` on the fulfillment axis; any other combination is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409). The named enrollment must be in `APPROVED` status and must belong to the order's own winery, and the enrolled program's `states_served` must contain the order's `ship_to_state_code`; a suspended or foreign enrollment, or a program that does not serve the destination, is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED`. Label production is also one of the five operations blocked while the winery is `SUSPENDED`.

Beyond those preconditions, label production **re-verifies the safety, permit and carrier gates of the screening ladder and raises them as genuine HTTP errors** rather than recording them. This is the deliberate asymmetry described in §2.9: screening is an assessment, but a label is an act. Any of `ERR_STATE_DTC_PROHIBITED`, `ERR_PROHIBITED_DESTINATION`, `ERR_CONSUMER_UNDERAGE`, `ERR_ID_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PERMIT_MISSING_FOR_STATE`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`, `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ENROLLED` or `ERR_ADULT_SIGNATURE_NOT_SUPPORTED` may come back as an HTTP 422 error at this point, and the most common reason is elapsed time: the prohibited-ZIP list may have grown since the order cleared, and the permit may have lapsed.

A successful label creates a shipment in `status` `LABEL_CREATED` with a server-minted `tracking_number` formatted as `TRK-` followed by eight hexadecimal characters. The shipment's own `adult_signature_required` is copied from the order's `adult_signature_required`, and its `adult_signature_service_code` is copied from the enrolled program's `adult_signature_service_code` when a signature is required and is null when it is not. `exception_code`, `reroute_zip`, `signed_by`, `signer_age_verified`, `delivered_at` and `returned_at` all start null and `reroute_count` starts at 0. The order moves `UNFULFILLED` → `LABELED`. Shipments may be listed filtered by `order_id`, `carrier_enrollment_id`, `status` and by `replacement_of_shipment_id`, that last filter being the way to find the replacement drawn for a particular returned package.

A **re-ship** — a label whose `replacement_of_shipment_id` names a shipment in `RETURNED` status belonging to the same order — is the loop that makes the fulfillment dimension cyclic, running `UNFULFILLED` → `LABELED` → `IN_TRANSIT` → `RETURNED` → `LABELED`. Because the return credited the consumer's liters back, the re-ship must re-debit them, and it must therefore satisfy the per-consumer and per-winery volume limits all over again — a re-ship into a state whose room has since been taken by another order is refused. On success a new shipment row is created in `LABEL_CREATED` with `replacement_of_shipment_id` set, a `SHIPMENT_ACCEPTED` ledger entry is appended with `liters` equal to positive `total_liters`, `order_id` set and `shipment_id` null; the order's `excise_reversed_cents` and `sales_tax_reversed_cents` are both put back to 0 and the winery's `excise_reversed_cents` is reduced by the order's `excise_tax_cents`, undoing the reversal the return had recorded; and because the re-debit entry now exists, the consumer's rolling and risk fields are recomputed with `Consumer.version` incrementing by 1.

Everything after the label is driven by the `CarrierAgent` posting **scan events**. A scan carries a `scan_type` of `PICKUP`, `EXCEPTION`, `REROUTE_ACCEPTED`, `DELIVERED` or `RETURN_TO_SENDER`, plus whichever of `exception_code`, `reroute_zip`, `reroute_state_code`, `signed_by` and `signer_age_verified` that scan type needs. A scan type that is not declared from the shipment's current status — a pickup on a shipment already `IN_TRANSIT`, a reroute on a shipment that is not in `EXCEPTION`, a second reroute on a shipment that has already been rerouted once, or any scan at all on a shipment that has reached `DELIVERED` or `RETURNED` — is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

A **`PICKUP`** scan moves the shipment `LABEL_CREATED` → `IN_TRANSIT` and moves the order from `LABELED` to `IN_TRANSIT` with it. That second move has a consequence outside logistics: because the order has left `LABELED`, its liters now qualify as settled, so `rolling_liters_settled_by_state` grows, `risk_score_screening` and `screening_tier` are recomputed, and `Consumer.version` increments by 1.

An **`EXCEPTION`** scan moves the shipment `IN_TRANSIT` → `EXCEPTION` and requires an `exception_code` of `WEATHER`, `ADDRESS_UNDELIVERABLE`, `ADULT_SIGNATURE_UNAVAILABLE` or `REFUSED`, which is recorded on the shipment. The order's `fulfillment_status` deliberately **stays** `IN_TRANSIT`: an exception is a carrier-visible hiccup, not a change in where the wine is.

A **`REROUTE_ACCEPTED`** scan moves the shipment `EXCEPTION` → `IN_TRANSIT`, closing the `IN_TRANSIT` → `EXCEPTION` → `IN_TRANSIT` cycle, and carries a `reroute_zip` and a `reroute_state_code`. A shipment may be rerouted **at most once**, so the scan requires `reroute_count` to still be 0. The supplied `reroute_state_code` must equal the order's existing `ship_to_state_code`: a cross-state reroute is refused with `ERR_REROUTE_STATE_CHANGE` (HTTP 422) because it would invalidate the permit, tax and volume decisions already frozen on the order. The supplied `reroute_zip` must itself not be blocked by a prohibited-ZIP row of the destination state whose `effective_from` falls on or before the logical clock date; a blocked reroute destination is refused with `ERR_PROHIBITED_DESTINATION`. On success the shipment's `status` becomes `IN_TRANSIT`, `reroute_zip` is recorded, `reroute_count` grows by 1, and `exception_code` is cleared to null; on the order, `ship_to_zip` is rewritten to the accepted reroute ZIP and `Order.version` increments by 1. There is no ledger movement of any kind — the destination state has not changed, so neither tax nor volume attribution changes.

A **`DELIVERED`** scan moves the shipment `IN_TRANSIT` → `DELIVERED`. When the shipment's `adult_signature_required` is true the scan must carry a non-empty `signed_by` string **and** a `signer_age_verified` of true; a missing signer name or an unverified signer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the shipment's `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `signed_by` and `signer_age_verified` are recorded, and the order's `fulfillment_status` becomes `DELIVERED` — which is exactly what makes the order eligible for a delivery-basis reporting period, as §3.9 explains.

A **`RETURN_TO_SENDER`** scan is the system's compensating transaction and is reachable from both `IN_TRANSIT` and `EXCEPTION`, with an identical cascade either way — a package that is refused after an exception scan and a package that simply comes back with no exception recorded produce the same five movements:

- The shipment's `status` becomes `RETURNED` and its `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock.
- Because the shipment became `RETURNED`, the order's `fulfillment_status` becomes `RETURNED` and its `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock too.
- Because the order became `RETURNED`, a ledger entry with `event_type` `RETURN_CREDIT` is appended carrying `liters` equal to negative `total_liters`, `order_id` set, `shipment_id` set to this shipment, and `occurred_at` at the logical clock; the order's `excise_reversed_cents` is set to its `excise_tax_cents` and its `sales_tax_reversed_cents` to its `sales_tax_cents`; and the winery's `excise_reversed_cents` grows by the order's `excise_tax_cents`.
- Because a `RETURN_CREDIT` entry now exists, the consumer's `rolling_liters_by_state`, `rolling_liters_settled_by_state`, `return_credit_count`, `risk_score_screening` and `risk_score_audit` are all recomputed and `Consumer.version` increments by 1.
- Because `risk_score_screening` moved, `screening_tier` is re-derived — becoming `ELEVATED` if the new screening score has reached 50 or more, which is how a series of returns eventually starts gating that consumer's future orders.

Note what a return does **not** do: it leaves the order's `compliance_status` at `CLEARED`. The wine was lawfully authorized; it simply did not arrive. That is precisely why a re-ship is possible without a fresh screen, and why the reversal has to be recorded in the dedicated reversal fields rather than by rewriting the gross figures.

### 3.8. The Wine Club: Scheduling, Weather Holds and Missed Runs

A **club subscription** is a standing instruction to generate and screen an order on a recurring schedule, opened by a `WineryOperator` for its own club or by a `ComplianceOfficer` on its behalf. It names the `consumer_id` of the member and the `winery_id` of the club operator, a `cadence` of `MONTHLY` or `QUARTERLY`, and the shipment template that will be written onto every generated line: a `sku`, a `bottles_per_shipment` count of at least 1, a `bottle_ml` bottle size, and a `unit_price_cents` per-bottle price. It also names `next_run_date`, the date on or before which the next run should generate an order — supplied at creation as the first scheduled run and advanced by the system after every successful run — and `annual_allotment`, the number of shipments of at least 1 that the member has prepaid for the year. A new subscription opens in `status` `ACTIVE`, with `missed_run_count` and `consumed_shipments` at 0, `remaining_allotment` equal to `annual_allotment`, and `hold_start_date`, `hold_release_date`, `hold_reason` and `last_hold_release_date` all null. Two refusals guard the opening: a subscription may not be opened for a consumer whose `screening_tier` is `ELEVATED`, which is raised here as a real `ERR_ELEVATED_RISK_REVIEW_REQUIRED` error rather than recorded, and no subscription may be opened at all while the winery is `SUSPENDED`. Subscriptions may be listed filtered by `consumer_id`, `winery_id` and `status`.

Heat and frost are the reason a wine club needs a pause button. Placing a **weather hold** moves an `ACTIVE` subscription to `WEATHER_HELD` and records three fields from the request: `hold_start_date`, `hold_release_date` and a `hold_reason` of `WEATHER_HEAT`, `WEATHER_FREEZE` or `CONSUMER_REQUEST`. Two spacing conditions apply. The `hold_release_date` must be strictly after the `hold_start_date` — a zero-length or inverted hold is meaningless. And when the subscription already carries a `last_hold_release_date` from a previous hold, the new `hold_start_date` must fall on or after that date plus 3 days, so a club cannot chain back-to-back holds to stall a member's allotment indefinitely; when `last_hold_release_date` is null no spacing constraint applies. Either violation is refused with `ERR_HOLD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Placing a hold deliberately leaves `next_run_date` untouched — it is the run itself, not the hold, that defers the schedule. A hold is only available from `ACTIVE`; attempting to weather-hold a subscription already in `WEATHER_HELD` or in `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Releasing a hold serves two distinct purposes and its authorization differs between them. Releasing a `WEATHER_HELD` subscription early — a `WEATHER_HELD` → `ACTIVE` move available to a `WineryOperator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` — copies the current `hold_release_date` into `last_hold_release_date` and then clears `hold_start_date`, `hold_release_date` and `hold_reason` back to null; the optional `reset_missed_runs` flag, when true, additionally puts `missed_run_count` back to 0. Releasing a `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE` subscription is a different act entirely: it is a compliance reinstatement, it requires `reset_missed_runs` to be true, and it is **reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`** — a `WineryOperator` attempting it is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REINSTATE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). A reinstatement puts `missed_run_count` back to 0, sets `next_run_date` to the logical clock date so the subscription rejoins the schedule immediately, recomputes the winery's `suspended_subscription_count`, and increments `Winery.version` by 1. Attempting to release a hold on a subscription that is already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The **club run** is the scheduler, invoked by a `WineryOperator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` for one `winery_id` on one `run_date`. The `run_date` must fall on or before the logical clock date; a run dated in the future is refused with `ERR_RUN_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). A run for a `SUSPENDED` winery is refused with `ERR_WINERY_SUSPENDED`.

Candidate selection considers only subscriptions of the requested winery whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `WEATHER_HELD`, whose `next_run_date` is non-null and falls on or before the `run_date`, and whose `remaining_allotment` — always `annual_allotment` less `consumed_shipments`, and never negative — is at least 1. A subscription in `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE` is never a candidate, since its `next_run_date` is null by construction. A subscription with no allotment left is **silently skipped**: it is reported in the response's `skipped_subscription_ids` and its `next_run_date` is left exactly where it was. A `WEATHER_HELD` candidate is handled in one of two ways depending on its hold: if its `hold_release_date` falls on or before the `run_date`, the hold has lapsed, so the system performs the `WEATHER_HELD` → `ACTIVE` release itself — with the same field clearing as a manual release, including copying `hold_release_date` into `last_hold_release_date` — and the subscription then participates in the very same run; if its `hold_release_date` is strictly after the `run_date`, the subscription is skipped and its `next_run_date` is set to its `hold_release_date` so the next run picks it up as soon as the hold ends.

Surviving candidates are processed in a strictly defined order: by `next_run_date` ascending, then by `bottles_per_shipment` descending, then by `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break. Identifiers are never used for ordering. An empty candidate set is not an error: the run answers HTTP 200 with `generated_count` 0 and an empty `generated_order_ids` array.

For each processed subscription the run creates an order for that subscription's consumer and winery with `club_subscription_id` set, with `ship_to_address_line`, `ship_to_state_code` and `ship_to_zip` copied from the consumer's default address, and with exactly **one** line built from the subscription template — its `sku`, its `bottle_ml`, a `bottles` count equal to `bottles_per_shipment`, and its `unit_price_cents`. The full twelve-step charge chain of §3.5 runs on that order, including the club shipping discount, since the order carries a `club_subscription_id`. The run then screens the order immediately, applying exactly the same ladder, in exactly the same order, with exactly the same clearance side effects as a screen requested by hand — including the `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED` transition of any permit the run observes past its `expiration_date`.

What the run does next depends on the screening outcome, and the two branches are asymmetric by design. If the generated order landed in `REJECTED`, the subscription's `missed_run_count` grows by 1 and its `next_run_date` is left **unchanged**, so the same member is retried on the next run; and if that increment brings `missed_run_count` to 2 or more, the subscription is moved `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED_COMPLIANCE` with its `next_run_date` set to null, the winery's `suspended_subscription_count` is recomputed and `Winery.version` increments by 1. If the generated order landed in `CLEARED` or in `OVERRIDE_PENDING`, `next_run_date` is advanced by the subscription's `cadence`. Advancing a `MONTHLY` cadence means the same day-of-month one month later, clamped to that month's last day when the day-of-month does not exist — the 31st of January advances to the 28th or 29th of February — and advancing a `QUARTERLY` cadence means the same day-of-month three months later with the same clamping. Note carefully that the run does **not** touch `consumed_shipments` on either branch: a cleared order already incremented that counter as part of its own clearance side effects, and an order left in `OVERRIDE_PENDING` deliberately consumes no allotment at all until its exception is approved. The run therefore never double-counts a member's allotment.

The run answers with the `winery_id` and `run_date` it acted on, a `generated_count` and `skipped_count`, a `generated_order_ids` array listing the orders it created in processing order, and a `skipped_subscription_ids` array listing the candidates it passed over. Those generated orders carry no other announcement — they are discoverable afterwards only through the returned `generated_order_ids` or by filtering the order list on `club_subscription_id`.

### 3.9. State Reporting: Remittances, Amendments and the Renewal Calendar

Every destination state wants its money and its numbers on its own cadence, and a **tax remittance** is the API's record of one such filing. Only a `ComplianceOfficer` opens one, naming the `winery_id` of the remitting licensee, the `state_code` the money is owed to, a `period_type` of `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL`, the reporting interval as `period_start` and `period_end`, a `basis` of `ACCRUAL_BASIS` or `DELIVERY_BASIS`, and — when amending a filing already lodged with the state — the `supersedes_remittance_id` of the remittance being amended. The interval is always **half-open**: `period_start` is inclusive and `period_end` is exclusive, so an order whose key date falls exactly on `period_end` belongs to the *next* period, not this one. `period_start` must be strictly before `period_end`, and `period_end` must fall on or before the logical clock date — a period that has not closed yet cannot be reported.

Period exclusivity is enforced with three deliberate scopings. A new remittance's half-open interval must not overlap the interval of any existing remittance for the same combination of `winery_id`, `state_code`, `period_type` **and** `basis` whose `status` is anything other than `AMENDED`; a collision is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Exact adjacency is explicitly allowed — a period beginning precisely on an existing period's `period_end` is accepted, which is the whole point of the half-open convention. Because the check is scoped by `period_type`, a `MONTHLY` period nested inside an `ANNUAL` period never collides with it. Because the check is also scoped by `basis`, the two bases are separate filing families and the very same calendar period may legitimately be opened once on each. And a request carrying `supersedes_remittance_id` is exempt from the check against the specific row it supersedes, since replacing that row is the entire purpose of the call.

Supersession works as a state move on the old row rather than a deletion. When a request names a `supersedes_remittance_id` whose target is in `FILED`, that target is moved `FILED` → `AMENDED` by the system, and from then on it is excluded from the period-overlap comparison entirely. The new row is created in `OPEN` with amounts computed fresh from the orders — superseded rows never influence the numbers, which are always recomputed from orders rather than carried over.

All of a remittance's figures are computed once, at creation, from the `basis` field, and then frozen onto the row. The selection begins with orders of the named winery whose `ship_to_state_code` equals the named `state_code`, and the `basis` then decides which of them the period captures. Under **`ACCRUAL_BASIS`**, the selected orders are those whose `compliance_status` is `CLEARED` and whose `cleared_at` falls inside the half-open period. Under **`DELIVERY_BASIS`**, the selected orders are those that have a shipment in `DELIVERED` status whose `delivered_at` falls inside the half-open period. The two bases genuinely disagree, and the disagreement is the point: an order cleared on 2026-07-28 but delivered on 2026-08-03 **is** in the July monthly period on an accrual basis and is **not** in it on a delivery basis. For two otherwise identical 4.500-litre orders each carrying 211 cents of excise, cleared on 2026-07-10 and 2026-07-28 and delivered on 2026-07-20 and 2026-08-03, a July monthly period yields an `excise_due_cents` of 422 on `ACCRUAL_BASIS` and 211 on `DELIVERY_BASIS`.

From the selected set the remittance records: `excise_accrued_cents`, the sum of `excise_tax_cents` across the selected orders; `sales_tax_accrued_cents`, the sum of their `sales_tax_cents`; `excise_reversed_cents` and `sales_tax_reversed_cents`, the same two sums restricted to those selected orders whose `returned_at` is non-null and falls **strictly before** `period_end`; `excise_due_cents`, the accrued excise less the reversed excise; `sales_tax_due_cents`, the accrued sales tax less the reversed sales tax; `liters_total`, the sum of the selected orders' `total_liters` rounded half up to three decimal places; `shipment_count`, the count of selected orders; and `included_order_ids`, the identifiers of the selected orders ordered by `cleared_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Remittances may be listed filtered by `winery_id`, `state_code`, `period_type`, `status` and `supersedes_remittance_id`.

Filing lodges the return with the state, moving the remittance `OPEN` → `FILED`, and is available only from `OPEN` — filing an already-`FILED` remittance, or one that has been superseded to `AMENDED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Filing also has a substance requirement: the remittance's `shipment_count` must be at least 1, **unless** the governing state rule's `zero_filing_required` is true, in which case an empty period may and indeed must be filed. A filing with nothing to report in a state that does not demand zero filings is refused with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REMIT` (HTTP 422). On success, `filed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `filed_by_user_id` records the filing officer's `X-User-ID`.

Recording payment moves the remittance `FILED` → `REMITTED` and captures the state's confirmation reference in `payment_reference`, stamping `remitted_at` from the logical clock and `remitted_by_user_id` from the acting officer's `X-User-ID`. It is available only from `FILED`; a remittance still `OPEN`, or one already `AMENDED`, cannot be remitted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. And this is the second place segregation of duties bites: the officer recording payment must not be the officer whose `X-User-ID` is stored in `filed_by_user_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both `REMITTED` and `AMENDED` are terminal — a remittance in either state never moves again.

The last piece of the reporting picture is the **renewal calendar**, a computed read available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or the owning `WineryOperator` for a supplied `winery_id`, with an optional `horizon_days` look-ahead that defaults to 90 days and must lie between 1 and 365. It answers with the `winery_id`, an `as_of` instant from the logical clock, the effective `horizon_days`, and two ordered lists: `permits_expiring` and `reports_due`.

The permit side considers only that winery's permits whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_FILED` or `EXPIRED`; `REVOKED` permits are excluded entirely. Before anything is rendered, any permit the read observes past its `expiration_date` is driven `ACTIVE` → `EXPIRED`. For each remaining permit the system computes a `renewal_due_date` equal to the permit's `expiration_date` less the governing state rule's `renewal_lead_days` days, and then assigns a `calendar_status` by testing four conditions **in this order**: the status is `OVERDUE` when `renewal_due_date` falls strictly before the logical clock date and the permit is not in `RENEWAL_FILED`; otherwise it is `FILED` when the permit is in `RENEWAL_FILED`; otherwise it is `DUE` when `renewal_due_date` falls on or before the logical clock date plus `horizon_days` days; and otherwise it is `SCHEDULED`. Only permits whose `calendar_status` is `OVERDUE`, `FILED` or `DUE` appear in `permits_expiring`; `SCHEDULED` permits are outside the horizon and are omitted. The list is ordered by `renewal_due_date` ascending and then by `state_code` ascending.

The report side derives, for each such permit's state rule, the most recent complete reporting period that state's `reporting_frequency` implies. A `MONTHLY` state's `report_period` runs from the first day of the month before the logical clock's month, inclusive, to the first day of the logical clock's own month, exclusive, and its `report_due_date` is the 15th of the logical clock's month. An `ANNUAL` state's `report_period` runs from January 1 of the year before the logical clock's year, inclusive, to January 1 of the logical clock's own year, exclusive, and its `report_due_date` is January 31 of the logical clock's year. Each entry also carries `report_filed`, which is true exactly when a remittance already exists for that winery, that `state_code`, that `period_type`, and that period's `period_start` and `period_end` with a `status` of either `FILED` or `REMITTED`. The `reports_due` list is ordered by `report_due_date` ascending and then by `state_code` ascending. All of this date arithmetic is calendar-day arithmetic on the logical clock's UTC date, in whole days — never thirty-day months.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — registering a winery, declaring a state rule or a carrier program, appending a prohibited ZIP, filing a permit, opening a carrier enrollment, registering a consumer, opening a club subscription, creating an order, raising a compliance exception, producing a shipping label, appending a volume-ledger entry, or opening a remittance — answer with `201 Created`. Every other operation, including every state transition, every recorded decision, every scan, every computed read and every list, answers with `200 OK`. The screening endpoint answers `200 OK` even when the order fails its ladder, for the reason given in §2.9. Request and response payload details are not repeated here; they are established by the narratives of §3.
