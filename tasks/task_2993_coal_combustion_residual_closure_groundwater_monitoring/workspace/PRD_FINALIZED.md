# Coal Combustion Residual Closure API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Electric utilities that burned coal are left holding two kinds of liability at the same time: the ash itself, sitting in surface impoundments and landfills that must be closed on a statutory schedule, and the groundwater underneath it, which must be monitored well past the day the last load of ash stops arriving. These two obligations are usually tracked in different systems by different teams — a civil engineering group runs closure design and cost estimating, a hydrogeology group runs the well network and the laboratory data, and a compliance group publishes to the public operating record and answers to the state regulator. The result is that the facts which legally interlock are the facts nobody can see together: a statistically significant increase in boron at a downgradient well is supposed to stop ash from being trucked off site for reuse, and a cap that has eroded during post-closure care is supposed to lengthen the care period and quicken the sampling cadence. When those linkages live only in email, they are discovered late, in an inspection finding.

This document specifies the Coal Combustion Residual Closure and Groundwater Monitoring API, a single system of record that holds the ash inventory, the monitoring network, the statistical determinations, the closure plans and their two-signature certifications, the beneficial-reuse offtake ledger, the post-closure cap inspection history, and the public postings that the affected community reads. Every derived regulatory fact — whether a unit is disqualified by its siting, what its closure deadline is after extensions, whether its monitoring program has escalated out of detection monitoring, whether its ash mass balance still reconciles, and which public postings are overdue — is computed by the server from primary records rather than typed in by an operator. Nothing is cached: a read of a unit re-derives its compliance standing from the current state of its children and the current date.

The system is deliberately conservative. It refuses operations rather than recording them provisionally, it holds append-only records for anything a licensed professional signed, and it requires two different licensed identities on the two halves of a closure plan certification. All resources are mounted under the `/ccr` prefix, with a single administrative endpoint outside that prefix used to drive the server's regulatory clock during testing and rehearsal.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Access Control

Every request to the API — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent, or is present but sent as the empty string, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header whose value is the empty string is treated exactly as if it had never been sent. If both headers are present but the declared role is not one of the seven roles below, or is a recognised role that the target endpoint does not admit, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `UtilityAdmin`, `ProfessionalEngineer`, `Hydrogeologist`, `StatisticalReviewer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Regulator` and `PublicViewer`.

- A `UtilityAdmin` is the utility's ash-program administrator. They own the unit inventory, the professional roster, closure plan drafting, extension requests, offtake agreements and shipments, the beginning of closure construction, and the server's logical clock.
- A `ProfessionalEngineer` is a licensed engineer who signs structural stability assessments, the engineering and cost halves of a closure plan, closure completion, cap inspections, care completion, and the qualification decision on an ash reuse agreement.
- A `Hydrogeologist` is a licensed hydrogeologist who installs and decommissions monitoring wells, configures and certifies the monitoring network, and owns sampling-round data capture.
- A `StatisticalReviewer` is a licensed statistician who runs prediction-limit comparisons and issues the resulting groundwater verdicts.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` files the public operating record, the corrective-action financial assurance, and alternate source demonstrations.
- A `Regulator` is the state authority: it approves closure plans, returns certified plans to draft, decides and rescinds deadline extensions, and decides alternate source demonstrations.
- A `PublicViewer` is a member of the affected community with read-only access to the public postings and to a unit's compliance summary.

Three of these roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProfessionalEngineer`, `Hydrogeologist` or `StatisticalReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an acting `QualifiedProfessional` record, and the server resolves it against the professional roster before doing anything else with the request; if no such professional exists, the request fails with `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Because these identities are real records, the server stamps them onto the artifacts they sign — `certifying_professional_id` on a stability assessment, `network_certified_by` on a monitoring program, `submitted_by_professional_id` on a sampling round, `reviewing_professional_id` on a statistical evaluation, `engineering_certified_by` and `cost_certified_by` on a closure plan, `inspecting_professional_id` on a cap inspection, and `qualified_by` on a reuse agreement — always from the header, never from the request body. Ownership and separation checks on those artifacts compare the stored professional foreign key against the acting `X-User-ID`.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `UtilityAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Regulator` or `PublicViewer`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never resolves it against the professional roster and never compares it to any entity's `id`. It may still be recorded for audit purposes — a public posting stores the acting compliance officer's header value in `posted_by_user_id` — but that stored value is a bare operator string, not a reference.

Two further gates apply only to the entity-backed roles and are described in §2.9: the acting professional's licence must be in good standing, and their practice discipline must be one that is permitted to sign the artifact in question.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0, must be an integer, and must be at least 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where a list endpoint accepts filter parameters, all supplied filters are combined with logical AND, and unsupplied filters place no restriction.

For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` set true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Mutation Counters and Append-Only Records

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, set once when the record is first written and never changed.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer. `version` starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every persisted mutation of that resource, with `updated_at` refreshed to the moment of the mutation. The resources that carry `updated_at` and `version` are the disposal units, the qualified professionals, the monitoring wells, the monitoring programs, the sampling events, the statistical evaluations, the closure plans, the closure extension requests, the beneficial reuse agreements, the reuse shipments and the public postings.

Three record types are **append-only certification records**: the structural stability assessments, the laboratory analytical results, and the post-closure cap inspections. These are written once and are never mutated afterwards. Consequently they carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and those two fields must be absent from their representations entirely rather than present and null. A later record can supersede an earlier stability assessment, and a later cap inspection can change the unit's cover condition, but the earlier row itself is left exactly as filed.

One derived field is explicitly exempt from the mutation counter. A unit's `compliance_posture` is re-derived on every read of the unit and on every write that touches the unit or any of its children, and that recomputation on its own never advances the unit's `version` and never refreshes its `updated_at`. The same is true of the other read-time derivations described in §2.8 and §2.5: recomputing a value the server owns is not a mutation of the record.

### 2.5. The Logical Regulatory Clock

Every deadline, window, interval and overdue determination in this system reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real time. An administrative capability, available to a `UtilityAdmin`, moves the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and returns the new value as `current_utc`.

Moving the clock has no immediate write effect on stored records; it changes what the derived fields evaluate to. In particular, a unit's compliance posture, its overdue posting list, and its closure timeliness are re-derived from the new clock the next time that unit is read. A closure that began before its deadline and has not yet been completed will therefore flip its `closure_timeliness` from `ON_TIME` to `LATE` purely because the clock advanced past the deadline; no request against the unit is needed for that to happen.

Wherever this document speaks of "the current date" or "today", it means the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Dates supplied by clients are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. A group of event dates may never be dated in the future relative to the current date: a unit's `cessation_date`, a stability assessment's `assessed_on`, a well's `installed_on`, a sampling round's `sampled_on`, a shipment's `shipped_on`, a cap inspection's `inspected_on`, and a public posting's `posted_on` and `community_meeting_held_on`. Any of these carrying a date after the current date is rejected with `ERR_FUTURE_DATE_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). Window boundaries are deliberately exempt from that rule and may be dated in the future: a monitoring program's `background_window_start_on` and `background_window_end_on`, and a reuse agreement's `commitment_start_on` and `commitment_end_on`.

### 2.6. Money, Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in United States cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no currency field is ever transmitted as a decimal number or a string.

Wherever a computation produces a non-integral value, the rounding method is always **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude — and the number of decimal places is fixed per field. Analytical values used by the statistics carry 4 decimal places. A reported background mean carries 4 decimal places and a reported background standard deviation carries 6. A statistical prediction limit carries 4 decimal places. An ash reuse qualification score carries 2 decimal places. A closure cost variance percentage carries 2 decimal places. Discounted annual care payments are rounded to whole cents, meaning zero decimal places.

Two rounding *orders* are load-bearing and are stated here because getting them backwards produces a number that is wrong by a few cents or a few thousandths and is otherwise invisible. First, in the discounted closure cost, each individual care year's discounted payment is rounded to whole cents before any summation, and only the rounded per-year amounts are added together; summing the exact fractional amounts and rounding the total at the end is a different and incorrect figure. Second, in a background statistical limit, the mean, the variance and the standard deviation are carried at full unrounded precision into the limit expression, and only the final limit is rounded; the rounded mean and rounded standard deviation are display echoes and must never be fed back into the limit computation.

Input numbers are also bounded in precision. A separation, distance, screen depth, settlement or percentage figure accepts at most 2 decimal places; a seismic peak ground acceleration accepts at most 3; a laboratory concentration or reporting limit accepts at most 4. A payload that exceeds the declared precision for a field, or that falls outside a field's declared numeric bounds, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.7. Stale-Version Rejection on Tonnage and Laboratory Appends

Three write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* record's `version`. The guard exists because these three operations are individually valid when repeated — nothing in the parent's own state would refuse a second one — and the only way to make a client prove it has observed the parent's current contents is to make it quote the version it last read.

**Creating a reuse shipment** requires a non-optional `expected_unit_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning `DisposalUnit` — the unit that the shipment's agreement draws its ash from. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two shipments that are each individually within their agreements both draw down the same remaining-ash pool, so a writer working from a superseded unit balance would compute its remaining headroom from a number that no longer holds; the stale-version check forces it to re-read the unit first.

**Returning a reuse shipment** likewise requires `expected_unit_version` matching the owning unit's current `version`, and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on a mismatch. A return credits tonnage back to the unit, so a return quoting a superseded unit version is reasoning about a balance that another tonnage movement has already replaced.

**Appending an analytical result** requires a non-optional `expected_sampling_event_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SamplingEvent`. A mismatch with the event's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Appends to a round are legitimately repeatable — a round carries one result per well and parameter combination — so the event's own state will happily accept a second append; the version check is what forces the client to observe the round's current result set before adding to it.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field. Requests to any other write endpoint neither require nor honour an expected-version input.

### 2.8. The Compliance Posture Gate

Each disposal unit carries a single derived standing, `compliance_posture`, which is the system's one-word answer to "how much may this unit still do?". It takes one of four values — `COMPLIANT`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — and it is recomputed after every write that touches the unit, its monitoring program, its closure plans, its cap inspections or its public postings, as well as whenever the logical clock moves. It is never stored as an operator input and, as noted in §2.4, recomputing it does not advance the unit's `version`.

The posture is evaluated as three predicates and then resolved by strict precedence, first match winning: if the frozen predicate holds the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise if the restricted predicate holds it is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise if the watch predicate holds it is `WATCH`; otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. The precedence is absolute, so a unit that is simultaneously in assessment monitoring and past its cap repair window reports `FROZEN`, not `RESTRICTED`.

The **frozen** predicate holds when any one of four conditions is true: the unit's `governing_stability_verdict` is `DEFICIENT`; or the unit's `cap_integrity_state` is `DEFICIENT` and it has a non-null `cap_repair_due_date` and the current date is strictly after that repair due date; or the unit's `closure_state` is still `ACTIVE` and the current date is strictly after its `closure_deadline_date`; or the unit's `overdue_posting_types` list is non-empty. Note the second and third of those comparisons are strict — a unit evaluated exactly *on* its repair due date or exactly *on* its closure deadline is not yet frozen. Note also that a unit which already holds an approved closure plan, and has therefore moved out of `ACTIVE`, is never frozen by the deadline clause; it is simply late, and it is refused at the point where it tries to begin closure construction.

The **restricted** predicate holds when the unit's monitoring program is in either the `ASSESSMENT` or the `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` phase, or when the unit's `location_restriction_verdict` is `DISQUALIFIED`.

The **watch** predicate holds when the unit's `cap_integrity_state` is `DEFICIENT`, or when `demonstration_required` is true, or when the unit carries any pending public-posting obligation whose due date is on or after the current date.

Posture is a gate, not a label. A `FROZEN` unit accepts only remedial writes — filing a stability assessment, recording a cap inspection, posting to the public record, and capturing sampling rounds and laboratory results. Every other unit-scoped write against a frozen unit is refused with `ERR_UNIT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): approving a closure plan, beginning closure, creating an extension request, deciding an extension, qualifying or activating a reuse agreement, and creating a shipment. A `RESTRICTED` unit is less constrained but still cannot move ash off site: activating a reuse agreement and creating a shipment are refused with `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) while the posture is `RESTRICTED`. `WATCH` and `COMPLIANT` impose no gate of their own; `WATCH` exists so that an open obligation or an unrepaired cover is visible before it hardens into a refusal.

### 2.9. Licence Standing, Discipline Gating and Two-Signature Controls

Whenever an entity-backed role acts, the acting `QualifiedProfessional`'s `license_status` must be `ACTIVE` at the moment of the call. Both `SUSPENDED` and `LAPSED` fail, and the refusal is `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). Licence changes are not retroactive: an assessment or certification that was signed while the licence was active keeps its verdict and its signature after the licence lapses.

Discipline is the second gate. A `QualifiedProfessional` holds exactly one `discipline` — `GEOTECHNICAL`, `CIVIL`, `HYDROGEOLOGY` or `STATISTICS` — and each signing operation admits only certain disciplines. Filing a structural stability assessment and signing the engineering half of a closure plan both require `GEOTECHNICAL` or `CIVIL`. Signing the cost half of a closure plan requires `CIVIL` and nothing else. Certifying closure construction complete, recording a cap inspection, and certifying post-closure care complete all require `CIVIL` or `GEOTECHNICAL`. Every well operation, every sampling-round operation and the network certification require `HYDROGEOLOGY`. Issuing a statistical evaluation requires `STATISTICS`. A professional whose discipline is outside the admitted set for the operation is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 403).

Three controls in this system require two *different* professional identities, and in all three the second signer is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when the acting `X-User-ID` is the same identity that signed the paired step. On a closure plan, the cost certification may not be signed by the identity recorded in `engineering_certified_by`, and symmetrically the engineering certification may not be signed by the identity recorded in `cost_certified_by`. On a statistical evaluation, the professional named as the alternate source demonstration's certifier may not be the identity recorded in the evaluation's `reviewing_professional_id`. On a unit's care-completion certification, the acting engineer may not be the professional recorded as `inspecting_professional_id` on the unit's governing cap inspection — the one ranking highest by `inspected_on` descending, with a later `created_at` breaking a tie.

### 2.10. Reference Resolution and the Order of Refusals

Identifiers are resolved left to right: path identifiers first, in the order they appear in the URL, and only then identifiers carried in the request body. A request that names a non-existent resource in the path and also a non-existent resource in the body reports the path failure. Because the resource graph in this system is flat — every resource is addressed directly by its own identifier and parentage is expressed through body fields — the practical effect is that the addressed resource must exist before any of its references are examined.

Not-found refusals are grouped by family, and each family has its own error identifier. A missing `DisposalUnit` reference yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `MonitoringProgram`, `MonitoringWell`, `SamplingEvent`, `AnalyticalResult` or `StatisticalEvaluation` reference yields `ERR_MONITORING_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `ClosurePlan` or `ClosureExtensionRequest` reference yields `ERR_CLOSURE_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `BeneficialReuseAgreement` or `ReuseShipment` reference yields `ERR_REUSE_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `PublicPosting` reference yields `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`; and a missing `QualifiedProfessional` reference — whether it arrived as the acting header identity of an entity-backed role or as a professional identifier in a request body — yields `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_NOT_FOUND`. All six are HTTP 404.

When more than one rule would refuse the same request, exactly one refusal is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following precedence. The checks are evaluated in this order and the first failure is reported; no later check is evaluated once an earlier one has failed. This ordering is part of the contract, because a request can plausibly violate half a dozen rules at once and the caller must be able to predict which violation it hears about.

1. Both identity headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The declared role is one of the seven and is admitted by the target endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. For an endpoint open to an entity-backed role, the acting `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing professional, else `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_NOT_FOUND`.
4. On every write, the request body is well formed: all required fields present, each of its declared type, enum values within range, numbers within their declared bounds and decimal places, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `document_sha256` of exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, a `document_uri` beginning with `https://`, and no unknown fields carrying business meaning — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. On every list, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0, both integers, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. Every referenced unit identifier exists, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Every referenced monitoring-family identifier exists, else `ERR_MONITORING_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.
8. Every referenced closure plan or extension request identifier exists, else `ERR_CLOSURE_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.
9. Every referenced reuse agreement or shipment identifier exists, else `ERR_REUSE_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.
10. Every referenced posting identifier exists, else `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`.
11. Every professional identifier carried in a request body exists, else `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_NOT_FOUND`.
12. No enumerated event date lies in the future, else `ERR_FUTURE_DATE_NOT_ALLOWED`.
13. Business codes are unique in their declared scope, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.
14. The acting professional's licence is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`.
15. The acting professional's discipline is admitted for the operation, else `ERR_DISCIPLINE_NOT_QUALIFIED`.
16. `expected_unit_version` matches the owning unit's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION`.

18. The unit's `closure_state` admits the operation, else `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.
19. The monitoring program's `phase` admits the operation, else `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_PHASE`.

A collision in any of those scopes is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Disposal Unit into the Program

A `UtilityAdmin` opens the record for a coal combustion residual unit by registering it with the operator's own identifier in `unit_code` — 6 to 12 characters drawn from uppercase A–Z, the digits 0–9 and the hyphen, unique across the whole inventory — a human-readable `unit_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and the 4-to-8-character uppercase alphanumeric `plant_code` of the generating station that owns it. The regulatory category goes in `unit_type`, which is either `SURFACE_IMPOUNDMENT` or `LANDFILL` and which decides both the base closure timeline and the ceiling on deadline relief. Two engineered attributes follow: `liner_class`, one of `UNLINED`, `CLAY_LINED` or `COMPOSITE_LINED`, and `hazard_potential_class`, the dam-failure classification of the embankment, one of `LOW`, `SIGNIFICANT` or `HIGH`. The tonnage of ash sitting in the unit on the day it is registered goes in `original_ash_in_place_tons`, which must be at least 1 and which becomes the conserved total for the mass-balance invariant described in §3.7. Finally `cessation_date` records the calendar day the unit stopped receiving ash; it anchors the closure deadline and may not be later than the current date.

Five siting measurements arrive in the same payload and are the entire input to the location-restriction determination: `uppermost_aquifer_separation_ft`, the vertical separation in feet between the unit base and the uppermost aquifer; `wetland_distance_ft`, the horizontal distance in feet to the nearest wetland; `fault_distance_ft`, the horizontal distance in feet to the nearest Holocene fault; `seismic_pga_g`, the peak ground acceleration of the design earthquake expressed in units of g; and `unstable_area_flag`, true when documented karst, mine subsidence or slope instability underlies the site.

From those five values plus the liner and hazard classes, the server determines `location_restriction_verdict` by an ordered chain of clauses in which evaluation stops at the first clause that matches, so later clauses are never consulted even when they would also match. First, if `unstable_area_flag` is true the verdict is `DISQUALIFIED`. Otherwise, if `fault_distance_ft` is less than 200 feet the verdict is `DISQUALIFIED`. Otherwise, if `uppermost_aquifer_separation_ft` is less than 5 feet the verdict is `DISQUALIFIED`. Otherwise, if `wetland_distance_ft` is less than 500 feet **and** `hazard_potential_class` is either `SIGNIFICANT` or `HIGH`, the verdict is `RESTRICTED_DEMONSTRATION_REQUIRED`. Otherwise, if `seismic_pga_g` is greater than 0.10 g **and** `liner_class` is `UNLINED`, the verdict is again `RESTRICTED_DEMONSTRATION_REQUIRED`. If no clause matches, the verdict is `UNRESTRICTED`. All three of those numeric comparisons are strict: a fault at exactly 200 feet, a separation of exactly 5 feet and an acceleration of exactly 0.10 g each fail to trigger their clause. The supplied decimals are compared exactly as given, with no rounding. The boolean `demonstration_required` is set true exactly when the verdict came out `RESTRICTED_DEMONSTRATION_REQUIRED`, and false in the other two cases. This verdict is frozen at registration: it is stored once and is never recomputed, no matter what later happens to the unit.

Registration also seeds the rest of the unit's derived state. `ash_in_place_tons` starts equal to `original_ash_in_place_tons` and `ash_mass_balance_ok` starts true. `granted_extension_months` starts at 0 and `closure_deadline_date` is computed from `cessation_date` by advancing the calendar by the base allowance for the unit type — 60 months for a surface impoundment, 30 months for a landfill — plus the current granted extension total, which at registration is nothing. Calendar advancement keeps the day of month and clamps to the last day of a shorter target month, so advancing 31 January by one month lands on 28 February in a non-leap year. The two independent lifecycle dimensions start at `ACTIVE` for `closure_state` and `NOT_APPLICABLE` for `cap_integrity_state`; `governing_stability_verdict` starts `UNASSESSED`; `closure_timeliness` starts `PENDING`; `deficiency_extension_months` and `late_posting_count` start at 0 and `overdue_posting_types` starts empty. Every dated field the unit will later earn — `closure_initiated_on`, `closure_completed_on`, `post_closure_care_start_on`, `post_closure_care_end_date`, `cap_repair_due_date`, `ssi_notice_due_date`, `stability_posting_due_date`, `closure_plan_posting_due_date` and `extension_demonstration_due_date` — starts null.

One child record is created in the same transaction. Every unit gets exactly one groundwater monitoring program, cascade-created at registration in the `DETECTION` phase with `sampling_frequency` set to `SEMIANNUAL` and `network_certified` false, and the new program's identifier is returned on the unit as `monitoring_program_id`. There is no endpoint that creates a second program for a unit and no endpoint that deletes one. Finally the unit's `compliance_posture` is derived per §2.8: a unit whose siting came out `DISQUALIFIED` is immediately `RESTRICTED`, a unit that needs a demonstration is `WATCH`, and everything else is `COMPLIANT`.

Units can be listed and filtered by `plant_code`, `unit_type`, `closure_state`, `compliance_posture` and `monitoring_program_id`, that last filter being how a caller holding only a program identifier resolves it back to the unit that owns it. Reading a single unit returns every derived field re-evaluated against the current clock.

A separate compliance rollup exists for the public and for oversight readers, and it is the only unit read a `PublicViewer` may perform besides browsing the postings. Nothing in it is cached; every field is re-derived at read time from the unit, its monitoring program and its closure plans. It reports `unit_id`, `unit_code`, `closure_state`, `cap_integrity_state`, `compliance_posture`, `location_restriction_verdict`, `governing_stability_verdict`, the program's phase as `monitoring_phase`, `sampling_frequency`, `next_sampling_due_date`, `open_ssi_count`, `closure_deadline_date`, `granted_extension_months`, `post_closure_care_end_date`, `ash_in_place_tons`, the four cost figures `in_place_pv_usd_cents`, `removal_pv_usd_cents`, `in_place_nominal_usd_cents` and `removal_nominal_usd_cents`, the `recommended_method`, `overdue_posting_types`, `late_posting_count` and `ash_mass_balance_ok`. The four cost figures and the recommended method are taken from the unit's **approved** closure plan only; when the unit has no approved plan all five are null, and a plan still in draft or merely certified is not read. `open_ssi_count` is the number of the unit's statistical evaluations whose verdict is `SSI` or `SSL_EXCEEDANCE` and whose status is anything other than `ASD_ACCEPTED`.

Once a unit reaches `CARE_COMPLETE` its record is closed to further work: every unit-scoped write is refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE` (HTTP 409). That same error is how the system refuses a unit-scoped write whose required closure state does not hold — extension requests and rescissions require the unit to still be `ACTIVE`; beginning closure requires `ACTIVE` or `CLOSURE_PLANNED`; completing closure requires `CLOSING`; and cap inspections and care completion require `POST_CLOSURE_CARE`.

### 3.2. The Professional Roster and Structural Stability Certification

Before any licensed work can be signed, the professionals who will sign it must exist on the roster. A `UtilityAdmin` registers one with `full_name` (1 to 120 characters as it appears on the certification), `license_number` (4 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within its licensing state), `license_state` (exactly two uppercase letters, the USPS code), and `discipline`. An optional `license_status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the alternatives are `SUSPENDED` and `LAPSED`. The server stamps `license_status_effective_on` with the current date at registration. The identifier the roster mints is precisely what the professional then sends as `X-User-ID` when acting in one of the entity-backed roles.

Licence standing changes over time, and the administrator records a change by supplying the new `license_status` together with the `effective_on` date it took effect; the server stores that date into `license_status_effective_on`. Because the change is not retroactive, everything the professional already signed keeps its verdict. Professionals can be listed and filtered by `discipline`, `license_state` and `license_status`.

Structural stability is where the closure story actually begins, because no closure design may be certified for a unit whose embankment has not been shown to be sound. A `ProfessionalEngineer` files an assessment against a `unit_id`, dated `assessed_on` — a date no later than today — and carrying the three computed factors of safety of the critical slope section: `factor_of_safety_static`, `factor_of_safety_seismic` and `factor_of_safety_liquefaction`. All three must be strictly greater than zero and are accepted to at most 2 decimal places. The server sets `certifying_professional_id` from the acting header identity; the body has no field for it.

The `verdict` follows from three independent threshold tests, and the criteria that fail are collected into `deficient_criteria` in a fixed order — `STATIC` first, then `SEISMIC`, then `LIQUEFACTION`. A static factor below 1.50 contributes `STATIC`; a seismic factor below 1.00 contributes `SEISMIC`; a liquefaction factor below 1.20 contributes `LIQUEFACTION`. Every comparison is inclusive at the minimum, so a factor exactly equal to its minimum passes and does not appear in the list. If the list ends up empty the verdict is `ADEQUATE` and `deficient_criteria` is returned as an empty list; if any criterion failed the verdict is `DEFICIENT`. Assessments are append-only: once filed, the row is never edited.

What the unit acts on is not the newest assessment but the **governing** one. Across all of a unit's assessments — including deficient ones, none are excluded — the governing row is the one with the latest `assessed_on`, with a later `created_at` breaking a tie. Its verdict becomes the unit's `governing_stability_verdict`; a unit with no assessment at all reports `UNASSESSED`. The governing row carries `superseded` false and every other row of that unit carries `superseded` true. The identifier of the record plays no part in this ordering: two assessments sharing an `assessed_on` are separated by `created_at` and by nothing else. The practical consequence is that filing a back-dated assessment does not change what governs, and filing a newer one demotes the previous governing row.

Filing an assessment has three further effects on the unit. It sets `stability_posting_due_date` to the governing assessment's `assessed_on` plus 30 days — re-derived from whichever row now governs, not from the row just filed, so back-dating an assessment leaves the obligation date where the governing row put it. It does this on **every** assessment, adequate or deficient alike. And it recomputes the unit's compliance posture, which turns `FROZEN` the moment the governing verdict is `DEFICIENT`, halting closure approval, closure commencement, extension work and all ash movement until an adequate assessment supersedes it. Assessments may be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `certifying_professional_id`, `verdict` and `superseded`.

### 3.3. Designing and Certifying the Groundwater Monitoring Network

A unit's monitoring program exists from the moment the unit does, but it starts out unable to do anything: it has no wells, no configured background window, and `network_certified` is false. A `Hydrogeologist` builds it out.

Installing a well takes the owning `unit_id`, the `well_code` stencilled on the casing (3 to 16 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within that unit), the network position in `well_role` — either `BACKGROUND`, which contributes to the interwell background data set, or `DOWNGRADIENT`, which is where compliance measurements are taken — the screened hydrostratigraphic zone label in `aquifer_zone` (1 to 40 characters), the screened interval as `screen_top_ft` and `screen_bottom_ft` in feet below ground surface, and the completion date in `installed_on`. The top of the screen must be at least 0 feet and the bottom must be strictly deeper than the top; both accept at most 2 decimal places. The well is created with `well_status` `CERTIFIED`, meaning a licensed hydrogeologist stands behind its construction, and with `decommissioned_on` and `decommission_reason` null. Installing a well immediately re-derives the owning program's two network counts, `background_well_count` and `downgradient_well_count`, each of which counts only wells of that unit in the matching role whose `well_status` is `CERTIFIED` — decommissioned wells are excluded from both counts.

Before certification, the hydrogeologist configures how the statistics will be run. A configuration change may supply any subset of four fields — `statistical_method`, either `PARAMETRIC_PREDICTION_INTERVAL` (the default) or `NONPARAMETRIC_PREDICTION_INTERVAL`; `comparison_basis`, either `INTERWELL` (the default) or `INTRAWELL`; and the two inclusive bounds on which sampling dates count as background data, `background_window_start_on` and `background_window_end_on`, where the start must not be later than the end. Only the fields actually supplied are overwritten; anything omitted keeps its current value. Configuration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_PHASE` (HTTP 409) once the program's `phase` has left `DETECTION`, because rewriting the statistical basis mid-assessment would change the meaning of a determination already made. Reconfiguration never re-runs a past evaluation either — an issued verdict is immutable, and each evaluation carries its own snapshot of the settings that produced it.

Certifying the network takes a single `certified_on` date. The readiness test the server runs looks at four things at once: that at least 1 certified background well exists, that at least 3 certified downgradient wells exist, and that both background window bounds have been configured to non-null values. Both well comparisons are inclusive, so exactly three certified downgradient wells is sufficient. A network short of a background well is refused with `ERR_NO_BACKGROUND_WELL` (HTTP 422), and one short of downgradient wells with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DOWNGRADIENT_WELLS` (HTTP 422); when both are short, the background-well refusal is the one reported, as §2.10 position 36 precedes position 37. On success the program records `network_certified` true, `network_certified_on` from the supplied date, and `network_certified_by` from the acting hydrogeologist's header identity.

Certification is the gate on everything downstream: opening a sampling round or issuing a statistical evaluation against an uncertified program is refused with `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409).

Wells leave service only by decommissioning, which supplies `decommissioned_on` and a free-text `decommission_reason` and drives `well_status` `CERTIFIED → DECOMMISSIONED`. A well that is already decommissioned cannot be decommissioned again — that is `ERR_INVALID_WELL_STATE` (HTTP 409). Crucially, once a program has been certified the network minimums become a *floor* rather than a one-time hurdle: a decommission whose completion would leave fewer than 1 certified background well, or fewer than 3 certified downgradient wells, is refused with the same two errors used at certification. On an uncertified program no such floor applies. A successful decommission re-derives both network counts.

Wells can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `well_role` and `well_status`; programs by `unit_id`, `phase`, `network_certified`, `network_certified_by` and `last_validated_event_id`, that last filter resolving a validated round back to the program it advanced.

### 3.4. Sampling Rounds and Laboratory Data Capture

Groundwater data arrives in **rounds**. A `Hydrogeologist` opens a round against a `program_id` with a field-round identifier in `event_code` (3 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within the program), a classification in `event_type` — `BACKGROUND` for rounds that build the background data set, `DETECTION` and `ASSESSMENT` for rounds that supply comparison values — and the collection date in `sampled_on`, which may not be later than today. The round is created in `DRAFT` with `result_count` 0, `validated_on` and `rejection_reason` null, and `submitted_by_professional_id` taken from the acting header identity.

Field rounds are also paced. A new round's `sampled_on` must be at least 90 days after the `sampled_on` of the program's latest validated round; exactly 90 days later is accepted, and 89 days later is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INTERVAL_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). A program with no validated round yet is unconstrained.

Laboratory results are appended to a round one at a time, and only while the round is `DRAFT` — appending to a validated or rejected round is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Each append names the parent round in `sampling_event_id`, the well the aliquot came from in `well_id`, the monitored constituent in `parameter_code` — one of `BORON`, `SULFATE`, `CHLORIDE`, `ARSENIC`, `LITHIUM`, `MOLYBDENUM` or `RADIUM_226_228` — the reported `concentration` (at least 0, at most 4 decimal places), the `unit_of_measure`, which is `MG_PER_L` for every parameter except `RADIUM_226_228`, whose activity is reported as `PCI_PER_L`, and the `detection_flag`, either `DETECTED` or `NON_DETECT`. When the flag is `NON_DETECT` the laboratory's `reporting_limit` must accompany it, strictly greater than zero and at most 4 decimal places; for a detected result it may be omitted. The append also carries the `expected_sampling_event_version` guard described in §2.7.

Every result gets an `effective_value`, and that single field is the *only* value any statistical rule ever reads — the raw `concentration` is never used directly by the statistics. For a detected result, `effective_value` is the concentration rounded half up to 4 decimal places. For a non-detect, it is half the reporting limit, likewise rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Results are append-only laboratory records and are never edited after they are written; the only way to change a round's data set is to add to it.

Two integrity rules guard the append. The well must belong to the same disposal unit as the round's program; a well from another unit is refused with `ERR_RESULT_WELL_NOT_IN_PROGRAM` (HTTP 422). And a round may hold at most one result for any given combination of round, well and parameter — a second one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESULT_FOR_WELL_PARAMETER` (HTTP 409). Where more than one of these could fire, the ordering of §2.10 applies: the stale-version check at position 17 precedes the round-state check at 20, which precedes the well-membership check at 38, which precedes the duplicate check at 39. A successful append increments the round's `result_count` by 1 and its `version` by 1 — that version bump is exactly what makes a client's next append quote a fresh expected version.

Closing a round is a single operation with two outcomes, and both are reported as HTTP 200 with the round returned. The hydrogeologist supplies `validated_on`. If the round holds at least one result **and** none of those results references a well that has since been decommissioned, the round moves `DRAFT → VALIDATED`, stores the supplied `validated_on`, and advances the program's schedule. If the round holds no results at all, or if any of its results references a decommissioned well, the round moves `DRAFT → REJECTED` instead, and `rejection_reason` records which it was: `NO_RESULTS` when the round was empty, `WELL_DECOMMISSIONED` otherwise. A rejection leaves the program's `last_validated_event_id` and `next_sampling_due_date` exactly as they were — a rejected round never advances the schedule. Either outcome increments the round's `version`.

When a round validates, the program re-derives its schedule. Among all validated rounds of the program, the one with the latest `sampled_on` — a later `created_at` breaking a tie, and the record identifier never consulted — becomes `last_validated_event_id`. From that round, `next_sampling_due_date` is its `sampled_on` advanced by the cadence interval: 182 days while the program samples semiannually, 91 days once it has escalated to quarterly. A program with no validated round reports both `last_validated_event_id` and `next_sampling_due_date` as null. The same re-derivation runs whenever the cadence itself changes, so an escalation to quarterly pulls the next due date forward against the same last round.

A rejected round is not a dead end. Reopening it moves `REJECTED → DRAFT` and clears both `rejection_reason` and `validated_on` back to null, so the hydrogeologist can append the missing results and close it again. Only a rejected round may be reopened; a draft or validated round is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. Validation is terminal for a round in the sense that there is no transition out of `VALIDATED`. Rounds can be listed and filtered by `program_id`, `submitted_by_professional_id`, `status` and `event_type`; results by `sampling_event_id`, `well_id`, `parameter_code` and `detection_flag`, which is the only way to discover the results belonging to a round or a well.

### 3.5. Statistical Evaluation, the Monitoring Phase Cycle and Alternate Source Demonstrations

The determination that turns groundwater data into a regulatory event is made by a `StatisticalReviewer`, who compares one downgradient well's most recent measurement of one constituent against a statistical limit built from background data. The reviewer supplies the `program_id`, the `compliance_well_id` of the well under scrutiny, an `evaluation_code` (3 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within the program), the `parameter_code` under evaluation, and optionally `retest_confirmed`, a boolean that defaults to false and asserts that a verification resample reproduced the exceedance. The evaluation is only accepted when the program's network is certified, when the program's `phase` is `DETECTION` or `ASSESSMENT` — an evaluation attempted while the program is already in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` is `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_PHASE` (HTTP 409) — and when the compliance well is a `CERTIFIED` well (a decommissioned well is `ERR_INVALID_WELL_STATE`) belonging to the program's own unit and carrying `well_role` `DOWNGRADIENT`; a well from another unit, or a background well used as a compliance well, is `ERR_RESULT_WELL_NOT_IN_PROGRAM` (HTTP 422). The server stamps `reviewing_professional_id` from the acting header identity and `determined_on` with the current date, and it snapshots the program's settings at that instant into `method_used` and `comparison_basis_used`, so a later reconfiguration cannot rewrite history.

**Assembling the background data set.** Eligible data comes only from the program's own **validated** rounds whose `sampled_on` falls inside the closed background window — on or after `background_window_start_on` and on or before `background_window_end_on`. Rounds still in draft or rejected contribute nothing, and results for any other constituent are ignored. Which wells contribute depends on the program's comparison basis, and the two variants are genuinely different data sets. Under `INTERWELL`, the contributing wells are every well of the unit whose `well_role` is `BACKGROUND`, whether that well is still certified or has since been decommissioned, and the compliance well's own history is excluded entirely. Under `INTRAWELL`, the contributing well set is exactly the compliance well itself: the upgradient background wells are excluded and the well is compared against its own historical record inside the window. In both variants, a result whose well was decommissioned after the round was validated still counts — decommissioning does not retroactively delete data.

The values that enter the statistics are the `effective_value` figures of the selected results. Let their count be the `background_n` reported on the evaluation. Fewer than 4 values is a refusal: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BACKGROUND_DATA` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the server computes the arithmetic mean of the values and their sample standard deviation using the count less one as the denominator, both carried at full unrounded precision. Under `PARAMETRIC_PREDICTION_INTERVAL`, the limit is the mean plus a coverage multiplier times the standard deviation, where the multiplier depends on how many background values there were: 3.187 when there are 4 or 5 values, 2.752 when there are 6 or 7, and 2.355 when there are 8 or more. Under `NONPARAMETRIC_PREDICTION_INTERVAL`, no multiplier is used at all — the limit is simply the largest of the background values. The result is rounded half up to 4 decimal places and stored as `background_upper_limit`. The evaluation also echoes `background_mean` rounded half up to 4 decimal places and `background_stdev` rounded half up to 6, but those two are reporting conveniences only and must never be substituted back into the limit expression, as §2.6 insists.

**Choosing the comparison measurement.** The measurement compared against the limit comes from a validated round dated **strictly after** `background_window_end_on` — a round inside the window can build background but can never be the comparison. Among such rounds, only those that actually carry a result for the compliance well and the parameter under evaluation are candidates, and if none does the evaluation is refused with `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_MEASUREMENT` (HTTP 422). The candidate with the latest `sampled_on` wins, with a later `created_at` breaking a tie and the record identifier never consulted; its identifier is stored as `comparison_source_event_id` and its result's `effective_value` becomes `comparison_value`.

**Issuing the verdict.** Four parameters carry a groundwater protection standard: `ARSENIC` at 0.010 milligrams per litre, `LITHIUM` at 0.040, `MOLYBDENUM` at 0.100, and `RADIUM_226_228` at 5.0 picocuries per litre. `BORON`, `SULFATE` and `CHLORIDE` are detection-monitoring parameters with no such standard. The verdict is then determined in order. If `comparison_value` is less than or equal to `background_upper_limit`, the verdict is `NO_SSI` — a value exactly equal to the limit is *not* an increase. Otherwise, if the parameter has a protection standard **and** the comparison value strictly exceeds that standard **and** `retest_confirmed` is true, the verdict is `SSL_EXCEEDANCE`. In every other case the verdict is `SSI`. Two consequences follow from that shape and both are intended: a first exceedance without a confirming resample yields `SSI` and can never yield `SSL_EXCEEDANCE`, and boron, sulfate and chloride can never reach `SSL_EXCEEDANCE` at all, however large the exceedance. When the verdict is `SSI` or `SSL_EXCEEDANCE`, the server sets `asd_deadline_date` to `determined_on` plus 90 whole calendar days, the last day of that window being itself inside the window; for a `NO_SSI` verdict, `asd_deadline_date` is null. Every evaluation is created with `status` `ISSUED`.

**The escalation cascade.** An `SSI` or `SSL_EXCEEDANCE` verdict issued while the program is in `DETECTION` sets off a chain of four linked effects, and each one is caused by the one before it. First, the evaluation is stored with its verdict. Second, because that verdict is an increase, the program advances `DETECTION → ASSESSMENT`, stamps `assessment_entered_on` with the current date, escalates `sampling_frequency` to `QUARTERLY`, and re-derives `next_sampling_due_date` at the shorter 91-day interval. Third, because the phase became `ASSESSMENT`, the owning unit's `ssi_notice_due_date` is set to the evaluation's `determined_on` plus 30 days — creating a public-notification obligation — and the unit's `compliance_posture` is recomputed, which lands on `RESTRICTED`. Fourth, because the unit's posture became `RESTRICTED`, every beneficial reuse agreement of that unit whose status is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_on` set to the current date, halting ash movement off the site. An `SSL_EXCEEDANCE` verdict with `retest_confirmed` true issued while the program is already in `ASSESSMENT` drives a second escalation, `ASSESSMENT → CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, stamping `corrective_action_entered_on` with the current date and holding `sampling_frequency` at `QUARTERLY`; the unit's posture is recomputed and remains `RESTRICTED`.

**Filing the corrective-action cost basis.** Once a program is in `ASSESSMENT` or `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, a `ComplianceOfficer` files the remedy's cost basis: `remedy_base_cost_usd_cents`, the engineer's estimate of the selected remedy excluding contingency and oversight and at least 1 cent; `remedy_duration_years`, the expected years of remedy operation, between 1 and 60 inclusive; and `remedy_description`, a narrative of 1 to 500 characters. Filing it while the program is still in `DETECTION` is `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_PHASE`. The financial assurance amount is then built in three pinned steps from the base cost alone — the duration, despite being recorded, is not an input to the arithmetic. A contingency rate is selected by which band the base cost falls into: 20 percent when the base is below five million dollars, 15 percent when it is below twenty million dollars, and 10 percent at twenty million dollars or above. Both band ceilings are strict, so a base cost of exactly five million dollars falls into the 15 percent band and exactly twenty million dollars falls into the 10 percent band. The contingency amount is the base multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The subtotal is the base plus that contingency amount. The oversight fee is 2.5 percent of the **subtotal**, rounded half up to whole cents — never 2.5 percent of the base. `assurance_amount_usd_cents` is the subtotal plus the oversight fee. The band must be chosen from the base cost and never re-derived from the subtotal or the final amount: the total routinely lands in a higher band than the base it came from, and re-deriving it produces a smaller, incorrect assurance figure. Before the amount is filed, `assurance_amount_usd_cents` is null.

**Demonstrating an alternate source.** A utility that believes an increase came from something other than its ash may submit an alternate source demonstration, and that is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s filing: `submitted_on`, the `asd_narrative` explaining the alternate source, and `certifying_professional_id` naming the professional who stands behind it. The demonstration is accepted only for an evaluation whose verdict is `SSI` or `SSL_EXCEEDANCE`, only when the evaluation's `status` is `ISSUED` or `ASD_REJECTED` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_EVALUATION_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and only when `submitted_on` is on or before `asd_deadline_date`; a later submission is `ERR_ASD_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). The named certifier may not be the same professional recorded as the evaluation's `reviewing_professional_id`, per §2.9. A successful submission moves the evaluation `ISSUED → ASD_UNDER_REVIEW`, or `ASD_REJECTED → ASD_UNDER_REVIEW` on a resubmission, and stores `asd_submitted_on`, `asd_narrative` and `asd_certifying_professional_id`, overwriting whatever a previous submission left there. The 90-day window is measured from `determined_on` and a rejection does not extend it, so a rejected demonstration must be revised and resubmitted inside the original window.

A `Regulator` then decides, supplying `decision` — either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` — and `decided_on`, which is stored as `asd_decided_on` in both branches. The evaluation must be in `ASD_UNDER_REVIEW`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_EVALUATION_STATE`. A rejection moves `ASD_UNDER_REVIEW → ASD_REJECTED`, leaves the `verdict` exactly as it was, and leaves the program in whatever phase it is in — nothing is unwound. An acceptance moves `ASD_UNDER_REVIEW → ASD_ACCEPTED`, which is terminal for the evaluation, and rewrites the `verdict` to `NO_SSI_ALTERNATE_SOURCE`; that value is reachable in no other way. Acceptance also unwinds the escalation: the program returns to `DETECTION` with `sampling_frequency` back to `SEMIANNUAL` and `assessment_entered_on` cleared to null — and when the program was in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, `corrective_action_entered_on` is cleared to null as well, while `assurance_amount_usd_cents` is deliberately left in place as a historical record. Because the phase returned to `DETECTION`, the unit's `ssi_notice_due_date` is cleared to null and its `compliance_posture` is recomputed; and because the posture has left `RESTRICTED`, every `SUSPENDED` reuse agreement of that unit returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspended_on` cleared to null. Those two return paths — `ASSESSMENT → DETECTION` and `CORRECTIVE_ACTION → DETECTION` — are what make the monitoring phase a cycle rather than a ratchet: a unit can travel `DETECTION → ASSESSMENT → DETECTION`, or the longer `DETECTION → ASSESSMENT → CORRECTIVE_ACTION → DETECTION`, and then do it again.

Evaluations can be listed and filtered by `program_id`, `compliance_well_id`, `reviewing_professional_id`, `asd_certifying_professional_id`, `comparison_source_event_id`, `verdict` and `status`.

### 3.6. Closure Planning, Approval, Deadlines and Execution

Closure planning starts as an economic comparison. A `UtilityAdmin` drafts a plan against a `unit_id` with a `plan_code` (3 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within the unit), a proposed `selected_method` of either `CLOSURE_IN_PLACE` or `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL`, and the cost basis for **both** alternatives so that the two can be compared on equal footing: `in_place_capital_usd_cents` for dewatering, grading and final cover, `in_place_annual_care_usd_cents` for the annual care burden that a capped unit carries, `removal_capital_usd_cents` for excavation, transport and disposal, and `removal_annual_care_usd_cents` for the annual care burden after removal. Each of the four must be at least 1 cent. A single `care_period_years`, between 1 and 50 inclusive, sets the care horizon used by both variants. An optional `selection_justification` of 1 to 500 characters carries the written basis for departing from the system's own recommendation.

Two cost variants are computed on every draft, and they can rank the two methods in opposite orders. The **nominal** variant applies no discounting at all: each method's nominal total is its capital outlay plus its annual care cost multiplied by the number of care years, reported as `in_place_nominal_usd_cents` and `removal_nominal_usd_cents`. Because closure in place carries the larger care stream, the nominal variant systematically flatters removal. The **discounted** variant treats the capital outlay as a payment made in year zero and never discounts it, then discounts each of the care years 1 through the care horizon at 3 percent per year. The rounding order here is pinned and consequential: each individual year's discounted care payment is rounded half up to whole cents *first*, and only then are those rounded per-year amounts added together; the capital outlay is added to that sum last and exactly. The two results are `in_place_pv_usd_cents` and `removal_pv_usd_cents`.

The spread between the two methods is reported as `cost_variance_pct`, computed always from the **discounted** figures and never the nominal ones: it is the absolute difference between the two present values, expressed as a percentage of the larger of the two present values, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

From those figures and the unit's own attributes the server derives `recommended_method`, again by an ordered chain that stops at the first matching clause. If the unit's `location_restriction_verdict` is `DISQUALIFIED`, the recommendation is `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL`, because a siting failure cannot be remedied by capping the ash where it lies. Otherwise, if the unit's `liner_class` is `UNLINED` and its `hazard_potential_class` is `HIGH`, the recommendation is again `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL`. Otherwise, if `cost_variance_pct` is less than or equal to 5.00 the two methods are treated as economically indistinguishable and the recommendation is `CLOSURE_IN_PLACE`; the tie band is inclusive, so a variance of exactly 5.00 prefers closure in place while 5.01 falls through. Otherwise, if `in_place_pv_usd_cents` is strictly less than `removal_pv_usd_cents` the recommendation is `CLOSURE_IN_PLACE`. In every remaining case it is `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL`. That comparison reads the discounted fields only; comparing the nominal fields instead returns the opposite method whenever the care stream is large enough to reverse the ordering.

The operator is free to select a method other than the recommended one, but not silently. Whenever `selected_method` differs from the computed `recommended_method`, `selection_justification` must be present and at least 30 characters long, or the request is refused with `ERR_METHOD_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). That rule is enforced twice: once when the plan is drafted, and again when the regulator approves it, because the recommendation is recomputed at cost certification and a plan that was consistent when drafted can have drifted by the time it reaches approval. A new plan is created in `DRAFT` with `return_count` 0 and with both certification halves — `engineering_certified_by`, `engineering_certified_on`, `design_narrative`, `cost_certified_by`, `cost_certified_on`, `cost_basis_note` — all null.

**The certification diamond.** A plan reaches approvability only after two independent signatures, taken in either order, from two different licensed identities. The engineering half is signed by a `ProfessionalEngineer` whose discipline is `GEOTECHNICAL` or `CIVIL`, supplying `certified_on` and a `design_narrative`; the server records those into `engineering_certified_on` and `design_narrative` and stamps `engineering_certified_by` from the acting header identity. It is admitted only when the unit's `governing_stability_verdict` is `ADEQUATE` — both `UNASSESSED` and `DEFICIENT` fail, with `ERR_STABILITY_NOT_ADEQUATE` (HTTP 422) — which is why §3.2's stability filing is a prerequisite of closure design and not an afterthought. The cost half is signed by a `ProfessionalEngineer` whose discipline is `CIVIL` and nothing else, supplying `certified_on` and a `cost_basis_note`, recorded into `cost_certified_on` and `cost_basis_note` with `cost_certified_by` stamped from the header. Unlike the engineering half, the cost half **re-runs** both cost variants and the method recommendation, refreshing `in_place_pv_usd_cents`, `removal_pv_usd_cents`, `in_place_nominal_usd_cents`, `removal_nominal_usd_cents`, `cost_variance_pct` and `recommended_method`; the engineering half touches none of those.

The two signatures move the plan through a diamond. From `DRAFT`, the engineering signature takes the plan `DRAFT → ENGINEERING_CERTIFIED` and the cost signature takes it `DRAFT → COST_CERTIFIED`. From there the remaining signature closes the join: the cost signature completes `ENGINEERING_CERTIFIED → FULLY_CERTIFIED` and the engineering signature completes `COST_CERTIFIED → FULLY_CERTIFIED`. Whichever order is used, the second signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the identity already recorded on the other half, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A plan in the wrong status for the requested signature — a `FULLY_CERTIFIED` plan being signed again, or an `APPROVED` plan being touched at all — is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE` (HTTP 409).

A `Regulator` may send any certified plan back for rework. Returning to draft is admitted as `ENGINEERING_CERTIFIED → DRAFT`, `COST_CERTIFIED → DRAFT` and `FULLY_CERTIFIED → DRAFT`, takes no payload, and in all three cases clears **both** halves — `engineering_certified_by`, `engineering_certified_on`, `design_narrative`, `cost_certified_by`, `cost_certified_on` and `cost_basis_note` all return to null — sets the status to `DRAFT`, and increments `return_count` by 1. The join is not sticky: after a return, both halves must be signed again, and the two-different-identities rule applies afresh to the new pair.

**Approval.** A `Regulator` approves a `FULLY_CERTIFIED` plan by supplying `approved_on` and the `permit_reference` from the permit record. Approval is refused while the owning unit's posture is `FROZEN` (`ERR_UNIT_FROZEN`). Two method-specific gates then apply, and only to a plan whose `selected_method` is `CLOSURE_IN_PLACE`: such a plan cannot be approved for a unit whose `location_restriction_verdict` is `DISQUALIFIED`, which is `ERR_LOCATION_RESTRICTION_DISQUALIFIED` (HTTP 422), and it cannot be approved while the unit's monitoring program is in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, which is `ERR_REMEDY_REQUIRES_REMOVAL` (HTTP 409). A `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL` plan passes both gates untouched — a unit in corrective action may still be closed, provided the ash goes away. Approval also re-checks the justification rule described above. On success the plan moves `FULLY_CERTIFIED → APPROVED` with `approved_on` and `permit_reference` stored, and a second effect follows from it: the unit's `closure_state` advances `ACTIVE → CLOSURE_PLANNED`, the unit's `closure_plan_posting_due_date` is set to `approved_on` plus 60 days, creating a public-posting obligation, and the unit's `compliance_posture` is recomputed. `APPROVED` is terminal for the plan. Plans can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `engineering_certified_by`, `cost_certified_by`, `status` and `selected_method`.

**Deadlines and regulatory relief.** A unit's `closure_deadline_date` is always derived, never accumulated: it is `cessation_date` advanced by the base allowance for its `unit_type` — 5 years for a surface impoundment, two and a half years for a landfill — plus the unit's *current* `granted_extension_months` total, using the same day-clamping calendar arithmetic described in §3.1. Because it is always re-derived from those two inputs rather than nudged incrementally, granting and then rescinding an extension restores the previous date exactly, with no drift.

Relief is requested by a `UtilityAdmin` against a unit that is still `ACTIVE` and not `FROZEN`, supplying `requested_months` between 1 and 24 inclusive, a `justification_category` of `NO_ALTERNATIVE_CAPACITY`, `PERMANENT_CESSATION` or `TECHNICAL_INFEASIBILITY`, and a demonstration `narrative` of 1 to 500 characters. The request is created in `REQUESTED` with `granted_months` 0 and every decision field — `decided_on`, `decision_note`, `rescinded_on`, `rescission_reason` — null. Filing it also creates a public obligation: the unit's `extension_demonstration_due_date` is set to the current date plus 30 days, and the unit's posture is recomputed, which reads `WATCH` while that obligation is open and unmet.

A `Regulator` decides a `REQUESTED` extension by supplying `decision` — `GRANT` or `DENY` — a `decided_on` date, an optional `decision_note`, and, for a grant, `granted_months` between 0 and 24. A grant is subject to four further checks in this order, so that the community-engagement failures surface before the arithmetic ones. First, a posting of type `EXTENSION_DEMONSTRATION` with status `POSTED` must exist for the unit, or the grant is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_DEMONSTRATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Second, that posting must carry a non-null `community_meeting_held_on`, or the refusal is `ERR_COMMUNITY_ENGAGEMENT_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422) — a document on the website is not by itself community engagement. Third, `granted_months` may not exceed the request's `requested_months`, or the refusal is `ERR_GRANT_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); a regulator may grant less than was asked for, never more. Fourth, the unit's existing `granted_extension_months` plus the months now being granted may not exceed the cap for the unit type — 60 months in total for a surface impoundment, 24 months in total for a landfill — or the refusal is `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A grant is also refused while the unit is `FROZEN`.

A successful grant moves the request `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, stores `granted_months`, `decided_on` and `decision_note`, increases the unit's `granted_extension_months` by the granted amount and re-derives `closure_deadline_date`. A denial moves `REQUESTED → DENIED`, stores `decided_on` and `decision_note`, leaves `granted_months` at 0, and leaves the unit's `granted_extension_months` and `closure_deadline_date` completely untouched. `DENIED` is terminal.

A granted extension can be taken back while the unit is still `ACTIVE`. The regulator supplies `rescinded_on` and a `rescission_reason`; the request moves `GRANTED → RESCINDED`, its `granted_months` returns to 0, and the unit's `granted_extension_months` is reduced by the months that had been granted, after which `closure_deadline_date` is re-derived and lands exactly on its pre-grant value. `RESCINDED` is terminal, and a request in any status other than the one the operation requires is `ERR_INVALID_EXTENSION_STATE` (HTTP 409). Extension requests can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `status` and `justification_category`.

**Beginning and completing closure.** A `UtilityAdmin` starts closure construction by supplying `initiated_on`, which the server stores into `closure_initiated_on`. Four gates apply and their order is deliberate. The unit's `closure_state` check admits both `ACTIVE` and `CLOSURE_PLANNED`, so a unit with no approved plan gets past it and is then refused by the approved-plan gate with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_CLOSURE_PLAN` (HTTP 409) — the diagnosis a caller actually needs — rather than being masked by a generic invalid-state refusal. Before that, a `FROZEN` unit is refused with `ERR_UNIT_FROZEN`. And after it, the current date must be on or before the unit's `closure_deadline_date`, or the refusal is `ERR_CLOSURE_DEADLINE_MISSED` (HTTP 409); a unit sitting exactly on its deadline may still begin. On success the unit advances `CLOSURE_PLANNED → CLOSING`, `closure_timeliness` is set to `ON_TIME` — which the deadline gate has just guaranteed — and the posture is recomputed.

`closure_timeliness` is re-derived on every read of the unit and after every clock movement, and it has three values. It is `PENDING` while `closure_initiated_on` is still null. It is `ON_TIME` once closure has been initiated and either `closure_completed_on` has been set or the current date is still on or before `closure_deadline_date`. It is `LATE` when closure has been initiated, `closure_completed_on` is still null, and the current date is past `closure_deadline_date`. A closure that began on time and then overran therefore flips `ON_TIME → LATE` with nothing but the passage of the clock, and completing it does not retroactively restore `ON_TIME` unless the completion happened before the deadline.

Closure construction is certified complete by a `ProfessionalEngineer` of discipline `CIVIL` or `GEOTECHNICAL`, supplying `completed_on`, against a unit whose `closure_state` is `CLOSING`. One substantive gate applies: if the unit's approved plan selected `CLOSURE_BY_REMOVAL`, then the unit's `ash_in_place_tons` must be exactly 0 before completion is accepted, or the refusal is `ERR_ASH_REMOVAL_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). A closure-in-place plan carries no such requirement, since the ash is meant to stay. On success the unit stores `closure_completed_on` and `post_closure_care_start_on` both equal to the supplied `completed_on`, advances `closure_state` `CLOSING → POST_CLOSURE_CARE`, and — on the second, independent state dimension — advances `cap_integrity_state` `NOT_APPLICABLE → ADEQUATE`, because a certified closure means a cover has been built and accepted. The care period's end date is then computed as described in §3.8.

### 3.7. Beneficial Reuse of Ash and the Tonnage Ledger

Ash that meets the concrete industry's fly-ash specification is a product rather than a waste, and moving it to an offtaker is both a revenue line and the fastest route to emptying a unit destined for closure by removal. It is also the operation most tightly gated, because every ton that leaves changes a number the closure rules depend on.

A `UtilityAdmin` drafts an offtake agreement against a `unit_id` with an `agreement_code` (3 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique across all agreements), the end use in `product_type` — `CONCRETE_POZZOLAN`, `WALLBOARD`, `STRUCTURAL_FILL` or `ROADBASE` — the counterparty in `offtaker_name` (1 to 120 characters), the tonnage the offtaker undertakes to accept in `committed_tons` (at least 1), and the commitment window as `commitment_start_on` and `commitment_end_on`, an inclusive pair whose start must not be later than its end and either of which may be dated in the future. Four laboratory and commercial figures complete the draft: `loi_percent`, the loss on ignition; `retained_325_percent`, the percentage retained on a 45-micron sieve; `oxide_sum_percent`, the combined silica, alumina and iron oxide content; each between 0 and 100 inclusive with at most 2 decimal places; plus `market_price_usd_cents_per_ton`, the negotiated price per ton in cents, at least 0, and `haul_distance_miles`, the one-way haul distance to the offtaker's plant, at least 0.

**The specification test.** The boolean `meets_astm_c618_class_f` is true exactly when all three of the following hold: `loi_percent` is at most 6.00, `retained_325_percent` is at most 34.00, and `oxide_sum_percent` is at least 70.00. All three comparisons are inclusive at the limit, so 6.00, 34.00 and 70.00 respectively all meet the specification while 6.01, 34.01 and 69.99 all fail it.

**The qualification score.** Alongside the specification test, the server computes `qualification_score` out of a possible 100. Four components are derived first, each unrounded. The loss-on-ignition component is 100 reduced by ten points for every whole percentage point of `loi_percent`, floored at 0. The fineness component is 100 reduced by two points for every whole percentage point of `retained_325_percent`, floored at 0. The oxide component is `oxide_sum_percent` itself, capped at 100. The market component is `market_price_usd_cents_per_ton` divided by 50, capped at 100 — which is to say a price of fifty cents per ton scores one point and a price of fifty dollars per ton reaches the cap. Those four components are combined with fixed weights: 30 percent for the loss-on-ignition component, 20 percent for the fineness component, 35 percent for the oxide component and 15 percent for the market component. A product bonus is then added — 8 points for `CONCRETE_POZZOLAN`, 4 points for `WALLBOARD`, and nothing for `STRUCTURAL_FILL` or `ROADBASE` — and a haul penalty is subtracted: 10 points when `haul_distance_miles` is greater than 150, otherwise 5 points when it is greater than 75, otherwise nothing. Both haul bands are strict, so exactly 75 miles and exactly 150 miles incur no penalty while 76 and 151 miles do. The weighted sum plus the bonus less the penalty is then clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Both `meets_astm_c618_class_f` and `qualification_score` are stored on creation, and both are recomputed when the agreement is qualified. A new agreement is created in `DRAFT` with `shipped_tons_total` 0 and `qualified_on`, `qualified_by`, `activated_on` and `suspended_on` all null.

**Qualification.** A `ProfessionalEngineer` qualifies a `DRAFT` agreement by supplying `qualified_on`, against a unit that is not `FROZEN`. Ash that fails the Class F specification is refused outright with `ERR_ASH_QUALITY_FAILS_SPEC` (HTTP 422) and the agreement stays in `DRAFT` — the operator may revise nothing about it, but the record is not burned. Ash that meets the specification is also checked for over-commitment. The exposure test looks at the unit's *other* agreements whose status is `QUALIFIED` or `ACTIVE` — draft, disqualified, suspended and completed agreements contribute nothing — and keeps those whose commitment window intersects the candidate's. Both windows are closed intervals on both ends, so two agreements that merely touch on a single shared date, one ending on the 30th of June and the next starting on the 30th of June, do overlap and their tonnages are summed. The committed tonnages of those overlapping peers plus the candidate's own `committed_tons` form the aggregate exposure, and if that aggregate strictly exceeds the unit's current `ash_in_place_tons` the qualification is refused with `ERR_REUSE_COMMITMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The comparison is strict, so an exposure exactly equal to the remaining ash is permitted.

An agreement that clears both checks records `qualified_on` and `qualified_by` from the acting engineer's header identity, and then branches on its recomputed score: a score of at least 50.00 moves it `DRAFT → QUALIFIED`, while a score below 50.00 moves it `DRAFT → DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal. Both branches are successful outcomes reported as HTTP 200 with the agreement returned, and both stamp `qualified_on` and `qualified_by`; the difference is only which state the agreement lands in. This is worth distinguishing from the specification failure above, which is an error and leaves the agreement in `DRAFT` with neither field set.

**Activation and suspension.** A `UtilityAdmin` activates a `QUALIFIED` agreement by supplying `activated_on`, moving it `QUALIFIED → ACTIVE`. Activation is refused while the unit's posture is `FROZEN` (`ERR_UNIT_FROZEN`) or `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTED`). Suspension is never requested by anyone: as §3.5 describes, an agreement is driven `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_on` set to the current date by the cascade that follows a unit's posture becoming `RESTRICTED`, and it returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspended_on` cleared to null when the posture leaves `RESTRICTED` and is not `FROZEN`. A `SUSPENDED` agreement cannot ship, because shipping requires `ACTIVE`.

**Shipments.** Moving a load out is a `UtilityAdmin` operation against an `ACTIVE` agreement, supplying the `agreement_id`, a bill-of-lading identifier in `shipment_code` (3 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and hyphens, unique within that agreement), the load's `tons` (at least 1), the departure date in `shipped_on`, and the `expected_unit_version` guard of §2.7. Three quantitative gates apply after the posture gates, in this order. The departure date must fall inside the agreement's closed commitment window, inclusive of both endpoints, or the refusal is `ERR_SHIPMENT_OUTSIDE_COMMITMENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The agreement's `shipped_tons_total` plus this load's `tons` may not exceed its `committed_tons`, or the refusal is `ERR_COMMITTED_TONS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). And the load's `tons` may not exceed the owning unit's current `ash_in_place_tons`, or the refusal is `ERR_ASH_TONS_EXCEED_INVENTORY` (HTTP 422).

A successful shipment is created with `status` `SHIPPED` and with `returned_on` and `return_reason` null. It increases the agreement's `shipped_tons_total` by its tonnage, and when that total reaches the agreement's `committed_tons` exactly, the agreement bubbles up `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`. It also re-derives the unit's tonnage ledger and increments the unit's `version` by 1 — which is what makes the next shipment quote a fresh expected version.

The ledger itself is a single derivation, never an incremental adjustment. The unit's `ash_in_place_tons` is its `original_ash_in_place_tons` less the sum of `tons` over every shipment in `SHIPPED` status belonging to any agreement of that unit; a shipment in `RETURNED` status contributes nothing. The invariant `ash_mass_balance_ok` is true exactly when the current `ash_in_place_tons` plus that shipped-out sum equals `original_ash_in_place_tons`, and it must hold after every tonnage movement. Each agreement's `shipped_tons_total` is derived the same way from its own shipments in `SHIPPED` status. All of this is whole-ton integer arithmetic with no rounding anywhere.

**Returns as compensation.** A load can come back. A `UtilityAdmin` supplies `returned_on`, a `return_reason` and the `expected_unit_version` guard; the shipment moves `SHIPPED → RETURNED`, which is terminal for that shipment — a second return attempt is `ERR_INVALID_SHIPMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). The return reverses every tonnage effect of the original shipment: the agreement's `shipped_tons_total` decreases by the load's tonnage, a `COMPLETED` agreement is pulled back `COMPLETED → ACTIVE` because its commitment is no longer fully shipped, the unit's `ash_in_place_tons` is re-derived and rises by the returned tonnage, `ash_mass_balance_ok` is re-derived, and the unit's `version` increments by 1. Because the ledger is always re-derived from the original tonnage minus the shipped sum, a return restores the pre-shipment balance exactly with no drift. Returns are admitted against an agreement in `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` status; any other agreement status is `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Agreements can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `qualified_by`, `status` and `product_type`; shipments by `agreement_id` and `status`.

### 3.8. Post-Closure Care and Cap Integrity

Completing closure construction does not end the utility's obligation; it starts a decades-long watch over the final cover. The moment a `ProfessionalEngineer` certifies construction complete, `post_closure_care_start_on` is set, the unit enters `POST_CLOSURE_CARE`, and the second state dimension leaves `NOT_APPLICABLE` for `ADEQUATE` because there is now a cap to keep adequate.

**How long care lasts.** `post_closure_care_end_date` is null while `post_closure_care_start_on` is null. Once care has begun, the server counts the unit's cap inspections whose verdict is `DEFICIENT`, allows 12 months of extra care for each one, caps that allowance at 60 months, records the result in `deficiency_extension_months`, and adds a base 30 years — 360 months — plus that allowance to `post_closure_care_start_on`. The addition is calendar-month arithmetic with day clamping, so a care period that starts on the 31st of a month lands on the last day of the target month when that month is shorter. Because the allowance is capped at 60 months, the sixth and every subsequent deficient inspection lengthens nothing. Critically, the count includes deficient inspections that have since been remediated: a later passing inspection restores the cap's standing but never refunds care time already earned.

**Recording an inspection.** A `ProfessionalEngineer` whose discipline is `CIVIL` or `GEOTECHNICAL` files an inspection against a unit that is in `POST_CLOSURE_CARE` — any other closure state is `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE` — supplying the `unit_id`, the `inspected_on` date (not in the future), the worst observed erosion class in `erosion_severity` as `NONE`, `MINOR` or `MAJOR`, the maximum differential settlement in `settlement_inches` (at least 0, at most 2 decimal places), the established vegetative cover in `vegetation_cover_pct` (0 to 100 inclusive, at most 2 decimal places), and optionally a `note` of 1 to 500 characters. The server records `inspecting_professional_id` from the acting engineer's header identity. Inspections are append-only observations: once filed, a row is never edited, so it carries neither a mutation counter nor an `updated_at`.

**The verdict.** Three criteria are tested and the failing ones are collected into `deficient_criteria` in a fixed order — `EROSION` first, then `SETTLEMENT`, then `VEGETATION` — regardless of which failed worst or which was measured first. `EROSION` is recorded when `erosion_severity` is anything other than `NONE`, so a `MINOR` rill counts. `SETTLEMENT` is recorded when `settlement_inches` is strictly greater than 6.00, so exactly 6.00 inches passes and 6.01 does not. `VEGETATION` is recorded when `vegetation_cover_pct` is strictly less than 70.00, so exactly 70.00 percent passes and 69.99 does not. An empty list is a `PASS`; one or more entries make the `verdict` `DEFICIENT`.

**What a deficient verdict sets in motion.** A `DEFICIENT` verdict drives the cap dimension `ADEQUATE → DEFICIENT`, or `DEFICIENT → DEFICIENT` as a self-loop when the cap was already deficient, and sets `cap_repair_due_date` to `inspected_on` plus 60 days. The self-loop is not a no-op: it resets `cap_repair_due_date` from the new inspection's date, so a second deficient inspection moves the repair deadline forward rather than leaving the unit permanently past due. That state change then causes a further hop that the inspection itself does not: because the cap became — or remained — deficient, `deficiency_extension_months` increases by another 12 months up to the 60-month cap, `post_closure_care_end_date` is recomputed from the new allowance, and the unit's monitoring program has its `sampling_frequency` escalated to `QUARTERLY`. One inspection therefore rewrites the cap state, a repair deadline, the length of the care period and the groundwater sampling cadence. Posture is then recomputed, and the deficiency's effect on it is time-dependent: a unit with an unrepaired cap sits at `WATCH` while the current date is on or before `cap_repair_due_date` and hardens to `FROZEN` once the current date passes it, which is why the same unit's posture can change with nothing but a clock move.

**Returning to adequate.** A `PASS` verdict filed while the cap dimension is `DEFICIENT` drives `DEFICIENT → ADEQUATE` and clears `cap_repair_due_date` to null, and posture is recomputed. As stated above, `deficiency_extension_months` and `post_closure_care_end_date` are deliberately left untouched: the extended care period survives the repair. A `PASS` filed while the cap is already `ADEQUATE` simply records the observation and leaves the unit's cap fields as they are.

**Certifying care complete.** The last act in a unit's life is a `ProfessionalEngineer` in `CIVIL` or `GEOTECHNICAL` certifying that the care period is over. The request carries no body. The gates are checked in the order the platform's refusal chain fixes: the unit must be in `POST_CLOSURE_CARE`; the unit's monitoring program must be back in the `DETECTION` phase, because a unit still in assessment or corrective action has an unresolved groundwater problem and gets `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_PHASE`; the acting engineer must not be the same person as the inspecting professional of the unit's governing cap inspection — the one with the latest `inspected_on`, ties broken by the later `created_at` — or the refusal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the cap dimension must be `ADEQUATE`, or the refusal is `ERR_CAP_DEFICIENCY_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409); and the current date must be on or after `post_closure_care_end_date`, or the refusal is `ERR_CARE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 409). The date comparison is inclusive, so certification on the end date itself is permitted. Success moves the unit `POST_CLOSURE_CARE → CARE_COMPLETE` and recomputes posture. `CARE_COMPLETE` is terminal: no closure-state transition leads out of it, and every unit-scoped write is thereafter refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. Cap inspections can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, by `inspecting_professional_id` and by `verdict`.

### 3.9. The Public Operating Record and Community Engagement

The regulation's enforcement mechanism is not primarily an inspector; it is a publicly accessible operating record that the utility must keep current. Several of the workflows above set an obligation date as a side effect, and this section is where those obligations are discharged, missed, or reopened.

**Posting a document.** A `ComplianceOfficer` posts to a unit's record with the `unit_id`, the `posting_type` the document discharges — `ANNUAL_GW_REPORT`, `STABILITY_ASSESSMENT`, `CLOSURE_PLAN`, `SSI_NOTIFICATION` or `EXTENSION_DEMONSTRATION` — the `posted_on` date the document went live (not in the future), the document's public address in `document_uri` (8 to 300 characters, and it must begin with `https://`), and a tamper-evident `document_sha256` digest of exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. Uppercase hex, a 63- or 65-character digest, and a `http://` address are all shape failures. An optional `community_meeting_held_on` records the date of the supporting community meeting and, when present, must not be later than `posted_on`. The server stores `posted_by_user_id` from the acting officer's header identity — an opaque operator string, never a professional entity reference — and sets `status` to `POSTED`.

**Which obligation a posting discharges.** Each posting type maps to exactly one obligation date on the unit: `STABILITY_ASSESSMENT` to `stability_posting_due_date`, `CLOSURE_PLAN` to `closure_plan_posting_due_date`, `SSI_NOTIFICATION` to `ssi_notice_due_date`, and `EXTENSION_DEMONSTRATION` to `extension_demonstration_due_date`. `ANNUAL_GW_REPORT` is informational and maps to no obligation date at all. From that mapping the server derives two fields on the posting. `satisfied_obligation` is true exactly when the posting's `status` is `POSTED` and the unit carries a non-null obligation date for that type — which means an annual groundwater report never satisfies an obligation, and neither does a posting filed for a type whose obligation has not yet been triggered on that unit. `days_late` is `posted_on` minus the obligation date in whole calendar days, floored at 0, and is 0 whenever there is no obligation date or the posting is not `POSTED`; a posting that lands on the due date itself is 0 days late, not 1.

**Which obligations are outstanding.** Two roll-up fields live on the unit. `overdue_posting_types` lists every posting type for which the unit carries a non-null obligation date, no `POSTED` posting of that type exists, and the current date is strictly past the obligation date. The strict comparison matters: an obligation whose due date equals today is not yet overdue and appears nowhere in the list. The list is always emitted in the canonical order `ANNUAL_GW_REPORT`, `STABILITY_ASSESSMENT`, `CLOSURE_PLAN`, `SSI_NOTIFICATION`, `EXTENSION_DEMONSTRATION` — never in insertion order, identifier order or the order in which the obligations were triggered — although `ANNUAL_GW_REPORT` can never actually appear, having no obligation date. `late_posting_count` counts the unit's `POSTED` postings whose `days_late` exceeds 0. As §2.8 establishes, a non-empty `overdue_posting_types` makes the unit `FROZEN`, whereas an obligation that is still pending but whose due date has not yet passed — a due date on or after the current date — only reaches `WATCH`. `late_posting_count` is a reporting figure: it tells a regulator how many documents the utility filed after their deadlines, and it does not itself influence posture, because a late posting that has since been made is no longer an open obligation.

Because all four obligation windows are date arithmetic off a triggering event — 30 days from a stability assessment's `assessed_on`, 60 days from a closure plan's `approved_on`, 30 days from a statistically significant increase being confirmed, and 30 days from an extension request being filed — and because overdue status is evaluated against the logical clock, moving the clock forward past an unmet due date is sufficient on its own to freeze a unit. No write to the unit is required.

**Withdrawing a posting.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may withdraw a `POSTED` document by supplying `withdrawn_on` and a `withdrawal_reason`; the posting moves `POSTED → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and a second attempt — or an attempt against an already withdrawn posting — is `ERR_INVALID_POSTING_STATE` (HTTP 409). Withdrawal is a compensating action with consequences beyond the posting itself: `satisfied_obligation` is forced to false, and the unit's `overdue_posting_types`, `late_posting_count` and `compliance_posture` are all recomputed. Since the withdrawn document no longer counts as a `POSTED` posting of its type, the obligation it discharged becomes unmet again, and if its due date has already passed the type re-enters `overdue_posting_types` and the unit's posture hardens accordingly — a withdrawal can therefore push a compliant unit straight to `FROZEN` and, through the posture cascade of §2.8, suspend its active reuse agreements. Postings can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, by `posting_type` and by `status`.
