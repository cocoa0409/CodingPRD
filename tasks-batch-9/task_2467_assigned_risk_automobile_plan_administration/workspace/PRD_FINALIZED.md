# Assigned Risk Auto Plan API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every state that mandates compulsory automobile liability coverage needs a residual market: a mechanism that places drivers the voluntary market has declined with a carrier that is obliged to write them. This document specifies the administrative backbone of such a mechanism — an **Assigned Risk Automobile Plan** — as a REST API. The plan does not itself insure anyone. It certifies which applicants have genuinely exhausted the voluntary market, apportions those certified risks across participating carriers in proportion to each carrier's voluntary private-passenger market share, designates one carrier as the servicing carrier for each risk, and then polices that carrier's conduct through rating rules, claim-handling standards, complaint adjudication and end-of-period quota reconciliation.

The operational difficulty this system exists to solve is fairness under continuous drift. A carrier's obligation is a moving target: it grows as more applicants are certified into the current quota period, shrinks when the carrier writes a rehabilitated risk voluntarily, and reshuffles entirely when another carrier files notice of withdrawal and drops out of the apportionment denominator. Meanwhile the plan must hold carriers to service standards without punishing them for volume they never asked for, and must protect insureds from adverse action while a complaint against their carrier is still open. The API therefore treats quota apportionment, carrier standing and the premium ledger as derived, continuously recomputed state rather than as fields a client may set.

Four kinds of user act on the system: the plan's own administrator, a state insurance-department examiner, a participating carrier acting as servicing carrier, and the producer of record who brought the risk in. Their authorities are deliberately disjoint — the examiner who certifies eligibility cannot place the risk, the carrier that services the risk cannot certify it, and the examiner who investigates a complaint cannot dispose of it. Read this document as the complete specification of that separation, of the arithmetic behind every derived figure, and of the exact order in which the plan refuses a request it cannot honour.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/plan` prefix.

### 2.1. Plan Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role` names the authority under which it acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the four role names the plan recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403.

The four recognised roles are `PlanAdministrator`, `ComplianceExaminer`, `ServicingCarrier` and `ProducerAgent`. The `PlanAdministrator` is the plan's central administrator: it enrols carriers and producers, opens quota periods, schedules and runs assignment cycles, rescinds assignments, orders driver reclassifications and voluntary-market exits, and files quota reconciliations. The `ComplianceExaminer` is a state insurance-department examiner: it pulls and records motor-vehicle records, certifies or rejects applicant eligibility, returns defective applications for cure, and operates the two-examiner complaint process. A `ServicingCarrier` is a participating carrier acting on its own book: it accepts designated risks, issues policies, serves non-renewal notices and handles claims. A `ProducerAgent` is the producer of record: it submits applications, files voluntary-market declinations, records premium deposits, resubmits cured applications, and cancels at the insured's request.

Two of the roles are backed by persisted entities, and two are not. When `X-User-Role` is `ServicingCarrier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Carrier` compare the resource's carrier reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `ServicingCarrier` may only act on an `Assignment`, `Policy` or `Claim` whose servicing carrier is the carrier the caller is bound to — a `Claim` is scoped through the `Policy` it was reported under. A violation is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `ProducerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Producer`; ownership checks compare the producer of record recorded on the target `Application` (or, for a policy, on the application behind it) to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for instance as `certified_by_user_id` on an `Application` where a certification decision writes it — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceExaminer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque examiner identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `certified_by_user_id` on eligibility decisions and as `findings_recorded_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on the complaint trail, and compares those two recorded examiner identifiers against each other, but never against any entity's `id`.

The carrier-scope rule guards exactly seven operations: assignment acceptance, policy issuance, serving a non-renewal notice, policy cancellation, reporting a claim, acknowledging a claim, and closing a claim. The producer-scope rule guards exactly four: filing a market rejection against an application, recording a deposit against an application, resubmitting a cured application, and policy cancellation. Policy cancellation is the one operation both scope rules touch, because three different roles may initiate it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422.

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where the plan maintains an explicit ordinal for auditability, that ordinal is the tie-break within the same `created_at`: assignment lists break ties on `batch_sequence` ascending, application lists on `intake_sequence` ascending, and carrier lists on `enrollment_sequence` ascending. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Plan Sequences

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits, recorded at insertion.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. This includes mutations that only touch derived fields: when a cycle run raises a carrier's `assigned_risk_count`, or a complaint resolution lowers its `performance_score`, that carrier's `updated_at` and `version` both move. The single exception is the market-rejection record. A `MarketRejection` is an immutable evidentiary filing: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all.

Three monotonic sequences exist because the plan must be able to defend the order in which it did things, and because a UUID is not an ordering. Each `Carrier` receives an `enrollment_sequence` — a 1-based, strictly increasing, gap-free registration ordinal assigned server-side. Each `Application` receives an `intake_sequence` on the same terms, strictly increasing and gap-free across all applications. Each `Assignment` receives a `batch_sequence`, a 1-based creation ordinal within the originating request — the cycle run or the exit-notice cascade that produced it. Wherever a rule below calls for a deterministic tie-break, it names one of these sequences. No rule in this system ever tie-breaks on `id`.

### 2.5. The Logical Plan Clock

Every deadline, lookback window, ageing rule and eligibility window in this system reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any call is made, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. A `PlanAdministrator` may move the clock forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to an administrative test-driving capability; the call mutates no entity row. Advancing the clock immediately re-evaluates every clock-dependent derivation on the next read: `clean_years_credited`, `distinct_rejection_count`, the assignment acceptance window, the claim acknowledgement standard, the non-renewal notice deadline, and the maturation of a pending non-renewal into a completed one.

### 2.6. Territories and Nested Path Resolution

The plan divides the state into exactly three rating territories, identified by the codes `T01`, `T02` and `T03`. Every territory-typed field — an applicant's `territory_code`, a vehicle's `garaging_territory_code`, a carrier's `licensed_territories`, a producer's `appointed_territories`, a period's `underserved_territories` — draws from that set and nothing else. A carrier's `licensed_territories` must be non-empty, must contain no duplicates, and every element must be one of the three codes; a producer's `appointed_territories` must likewise be non-empty and drawn from the same set; a period's `underserved_territories` may be empty.

Several resources are addressed through a parent path — an applicant's vehicles, an application's market rejections, a period's assignment cycles, a policy's claims. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and only after path resolution succeeds does the server resolve identifiers carried in the request body, again left to right in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint. The first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error: an unresolvable carrier yields `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, a producer `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_FOUND`, an applicant `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, a vehicle `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, an application `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, a plan period `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, an assignment cycle `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, an assignment `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a policy `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, a claim `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, a complaint `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` and a quota reconciliation `ERR_QUOTA_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404. Note that a nested child is addressed under its parent but is not filtered by it beyond resolution: listing an applicant's vehicles returns only that applicant's vehicles, and listing an application's market rejections only that application's filings.

### 2.7. Money, Exposure and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent representations and no string-encoded amounts. Wherever a monetary quantity is derived by multiplication or division, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. The plan never rounds a monetary figure twice, and it never sums unrounded intermediates when the specification calls for rounding the intermediates first — the per-coverage-line premium calculation in §3.4 is the load-bearing case, and getting it wrong shifts the expense tier and the required deposit downstream.

Three non-monetary quantities carry declared decimal precision. Car-year exposure is carried to 4 decimal places, rounded half up: this governs a policy's `exposure_car_years_current_period`, a period's `total_assignable_car_years`, and both the obligation and credited figures of a car-year-basis reconciliation. Voluntary-writing credit is carried to 1 decimal place, because credit is granted in halves; this governs a carrier's `voluntary_credit_units`, its `quota_variance_units`, an assignment's `deficit_score_at_selection`, and the `voluntary_credit_units_applied` of a reconciliation. Surcharge points are whole integers. A surcharge factor is carried to 2 decimal places.

Where an interval is measured in days — days in force at cancellation, days of exposure inside a period window, days since a chargeable event — the measurement is in whole days. A "year" for rating and ageing purposes is exactly 365 days; the plan does not use calendar years and does not adjust for leap days.

### 2.8. The Deposit and Premium Ledger Invariant

Money enters this system exactly once, as a premium deposit recorded against an application, and it is thereafter conserved. Two fields hold the conserved quantity. On the application, `deposit_paid_total_cents` is the cumulative sum of every deposit amount ever recorded against that application and never decreases for any reason. `deposit_balance_cents` is the part of that total which has not yet been credited to a policy's premium or returned to the insured. On the policy, `premium_credited_cents` is the part that has been credited to premium.

When a policy is issued, deposit money sweeps from `deposit_balance_cents` into the policy's `premium_credited_cents` — as much as is available, but never more than the policy's `total_annual_charge_cents`. `deposit_balance_cents` falls by exactly the amount `premium_credited_cents` rises. If a further deposit is recorded after the policy already exists, the new money sweeps the same way, again capped at `total_annual_charge_cents`, and whatever the cap leaves behind stays in `deposit_balance_cents`. When an application is rejected outright, the money does not move: `deposit_paid_total_cents` stays where it is and remains wholly inside `deposit_balance_cents`.

The policy side of the ledger reports four derived figures. `additional_premium_due_cents` is `total_annual_charge_cents` less `premium_credited_cents` while the policy is unsettled, and is zero once the term charge is fully credited. `refund_paid_cents` accumulates every cent of unearned premium returned to the insured. `balance_due_cents` is earned premium in excess of the credited deposit at cancellation. `earned_premium_cents` is the premium the plan treats as earned at cancellation, computed on whichever `refund_basis` variant applies. Any operation that writes these figures must leave the invariant intact: no cent is created, and no cent vanishes.

### 2.9. Carrier Standing: Performance Score and the Posture Ladder

A carrier's fitness to receive assigned risks is expressed by two derived fields. `performance_score` is an integer that begins at 100 and is clamped to the inclusive range 0 through 100. Its adjustments are: a deduction of 5 for each claim the carrier acknowledged after the acknowledgement deadline, counted over the carrier's lifetime; a deduction of 20 for each complaint against the carrier that was resolved as upheld, counted over the carrier's lifetime; a deduction of 5 for each of the carrier's assignments the plan rescinded for a missed issuance deadline, counted over the carrier's lifetime; and a recovery of 10 for each quota period that was closed while the carrier was `ACTIVE`. The clamp is applied after each individual adjustment rather than once at the end, so a fresh carrier that suffers two upheld complaints moves 100 to 80 to 60.

`posture` is a three-valued gate — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` — derived from the score and from the carrier's current-period counters. It is evaluated as an ordered ladder in which the first matching rule wins, and no later rule can override an earlier one:

1. If the carrier's `status` is anything other than `ACTIVE`, the posture is `SUSPENDED`.
2. Otherwise, if `performance_score` is below 70, the posture is `SUSPENDED`.
3. Otherwise, if `performance_score` is at least 70 and at most 84, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if `breach_count_current_period` is at least 3, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise, if `overallocated` is true, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
6. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Because the ladder is ordered, an overallocated carrier whose score has fallen to 60 reports `SUSPENDED`, not `RESTRICTED`. The posture is recomputed after every mutation of the carrier's `status`, `performance_score`, `breach_count_current_period` or `overallocated`, which in practice means after every claim acknowledgement, complaint resolution, assignment rescission, exit notice, plan-period closure and quota recomputation. Only an `OPEN` carrier is a candidate for new assignments; a `SUSPENDED` carrier may neither accept a designated risk nor issue a policy, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED` and HTTP 409.

Two lifetime and two per-period counters feed this picture. `breach_count_current_period` counts the carrier's claims whose acknowledgement breached the standard since the last period closure. `non_renewal_count_current_period` counts the non-renewal notices the carrier has served since the last period closure. `upheld_complaint_count` is a lifetime count of complaints against the carrier resolved as upheld. `penalty_balance_cents` is the sum of `penalty_cents` across the carrier's reconciliation rows that are still `FINAL`.

### 2.10. Quota Apportionment by Largest Remainder

Each carrier's obligation for the current quota period is expressed as `quota_target_units`, an integer produced by a largest-remainder apportionment of the period's `total_assignable_units`. The apportionment considers only carriers whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a carrier that has filed notice of withdrawal drops out of the denominator entirely. Let the share denominator be the sum of `voluntary_market_share_bps` across those active carriers, and let the total be the current period's `total_assignable_units`. For each active carrier, its exact entitlement is the total multiplied by that carrier's `voluntary_market_share_bps` and divided by the denominator; its floor is that exact entitlement truncated downward to a whole unit, and its remainder is the exact entitlement less its floor. The leftover to distribute is the total less the sum of all floors. The active carriers are then ordered by remainder descending, then by `voluntary_market_share_bps` descending, then by `enrollment_sequence` ascending; the first as many carriers as there are leftover units each receive one additional unit, and every other carrier receives none. Exact entitlements are held unrounded for the purpose of the remainder comparison, and the resulting targets always sum to exactly `total_assignable_units`.

To make the tie-break concrete: with a total of 2 units and two active carriers holding 500 and 1500 basis points, the exact entitlements are 0.5 and 1.5, the floors are 0 and 1, one leftover unit remains, and the remainders tie at 0.5 each — so the tie is broken by market share and the 1500-basis-point carrier takes the leftover, giving targets of 0 and 2.

From the target follow two more derived fields. `quota_variance_units` is the carrier's `assigned_risk_count` plus its `voluntary_credit_units`, less its `quota_target_units`, carried to 1 decimal place; a positive variance means the carrier is over-served. `overallocated` is true exactly when `quota_variance_units` is greater than zero, and feeds the posture ladder above. `assigned_risk_count` is the number of the carrier's assignments whose status is `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, `ACCEPTED` or `POLICY_ISSUED`. The whole apportionment is recomputed on every mutation that changes the set of `ACTIVE` carriers, any carrier's `voluntary_market_share_bps`, or the period's `total_assignable_units` — which includes carrier enrolment, carrier exit notice, and every eligibility certification.

### 2.11. Optimistic Concurrency on Superseding Filings

Two write operations are lawful supersessions: the plan may legitimately file a second quota reconciliation for the same carrier and period on a different basis, and it may legitimately re-rate a policy a second time within the same term. Because each is lawful on its own terms, neither can be guarded by a uniqueness rule; instead each must pin the state it measured.

A quota-reconciliation filing must carry `expected_carrier_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Carrier`. If that value does not match the carrier's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — the carrier's `assigned_risk_count` or `voluntary_credit_units` moved after the filer read them, and computing a variance off that stale volume would misstate the penalty. A successful filing increments the carrier's `version` by 1.

A policy reclassification must carry `expected_policy_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Policy`. If that value does not match the policy's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — `premium_credited_cents` or `surcharge_points_applied` changed after the filer read them, and re-rating from that stale base would corrupt the deposit invariant. A successful reclassification increments the policy's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two are processed without any version check, and supplying a version field to them has no effect.

### 2.12. Cascade Semantics and Derived-State Propagation

The consistency model is immediate: when one write has consequences for other rows, those consequences are applied inside the same request, and a subsequent read observes them. There is no deferred settlement and no background reconciliation job. Three propagation patterns recur.

The first is counter-and-derivation propagation. Any change to a carrier's `assigned_risk_count`, `voluntary_credit_units`, `performance_score`, `breach_count_current_period` or `status` is followed immediately by recomputation of that carrier's `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture`, and by the `updated_at` and `version` bump described in §2.4.

The second is cascade-and-revert. When the plan pulls an assignment back, the risk returns to the assignment queue and the designated carrier's counters are decremented; when a carrier's posture falls to `SUSPENDED`, every assignment of that carrier still awaiting acceptance is pulled back the same way. These cascades are described in the workflow subsections where they arise, and each names the state each affected row lands in.

The third is aggregate bubble-up: a parent row reaches a state because of the aggregate condition of its children rather than because anyone called an endpoint on the parent. A carrier that has filed notice of withdrawal reaches `EXIT_COMPLETE` when the last of its live policies has been transferred away. A quota period in reconciliation reaches `CLOSED` when the last active carrier's reconciliation is filed. A policy that has been served a non-renewal notice reaches `NON_RENEWED` when the logical clock reaches its non-renewal effective date, observed on the next read of that policy.

### 2.13. Validation Order

When a request fails more than one rule, the plan returns the error of the highest-priority failing rule and evaluates no later rule. The order is fixed system-wide:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognised: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. **Authorisation** — the caller's role is listed for the endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. **Request shape** — required body fields present, enum values in range, integers non-negative where required, timestamps well-formed ISO 8601, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` not negative: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422.
4. **Existence** — every identifier in the path and then every identifier in the body resolves, left to right: the matching not-found error, 404.
5. **Ownership scope** — `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` or `ERR_PRODUCER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`, 403.
6. **State admissibility** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists (`ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`), and the carrier the operation designates has `status` equal to `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE`) — all with HTTP 409.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION`, 412.
8. **Business safety** — the eligibility and standing gates: `ERR_MVR_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MARKET_REJECTIONS`, `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Business referential** — cross-row agreement: `ERR_COMPLAINT_FREEZE_ACTIVE`, `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_EXIT`, `ERR_VEHICLE_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_REJECTING_CARRIER_UNKNOWN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REJECTING_CARRIER`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PRODUCER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PRODUCER_TERRITORY_NOT_APPOINTED`.
10. **Business temporal** — window and date rules: `ERR_CANCEL_REASON_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`, `ERR_POLICY_TERM_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`, `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_POLICY_TERM_INVALID`, `ERR_NON_RENEWAL_NOTICE_TOO_LATE`, `ERR_EXIT_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`.
11. **Business constraint** — plan limits and uniqueness: `ERR_CURE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`, `ERR_NON_RENEWAL_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_LICENSED_IN_TERRITORY`, `ERR_CLEAN_YEARS_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_RECONCILING`, `ERR_CYCLE_STILL_SCHEDULED`.
12. **Business fiscal** — money is checked last: `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_INVALID`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are observable. First, on an eligibility certification the documentation gates at priority 8 outrank the deposit gate at priority 12, so an applicant with an unverified motor-vehicle record or with too few documented declinations is refused before the plan looks at the money — the plan does not price an ineligible risk. Second, on a policy cancellation the complaint freeze at priority 9 outranks the permitted-reason check at priority 10, so a policy with an open complaint reports `ERR_COMPLAINT_FREEZE_ACTIVE` even when the submitted reason code would also have been impermissible for the elapsed term position.

The `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` rule at priority 11 covers six uniqueness constraints, each enforced across all rows of its entity: a carrier's `naic_code`, which must be exactly 5 digits; a producer's `license_number`; an applicant's `driver_license_number`; a vehicle's `vin`, which must be exactly 17 characters; a period's `period_code`; and a policy's `policy_number`. It is raised by carrier enrolment, producer registration, applicant creation, vehicle creation, period creation and policy issuance.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Risk to the Plan

Nothing enters the plan without a producer of record. A `PlanAdministrator` registers each producer with a `name`, a `license_number` and the `appointed_territories` it is permitted to submit applications for, and optionally with an explicit `status`; a producer whose `status` is `ACTIVE` may transact, while one whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` may neither submit nor resubmit an application. Enrolling a participating carrier is likewise administrator-only: a carrier is created with its legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, its five-digit `naic_code`, its `voluntary_market_share_bps` between 1 and 10000, and the `licensed_territories` it may write. A newly enrolled carrier starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `performance_score` 100 and `posture` `OPEN`, receives the next `enrollment_sequence`, and — because the apportionment denominator has just grown — triggers recomputation of `quota_target_units` for every `ACTIVE` carrier. Carriers can be listed and filtered by `status`, by `posture`, and by `territory_code`, the last matching against membership in `licensed_territories`; a single carrier read returns its live quota figures, score, posture and `version`, which is the value a reconciliation filer must later pin.

The risk itself is described by two rows. An applicant is created — by an administrator or by the producer — with a `full_name`, a `driver_license_number`, a residence `territory_code`, a plan-approved `driver_class` of `ADULT`, `YOUTHFUL_OPERATOR` or `SENIOR`, and a `first_licensed_date`. At creation, the applicant's `last_chargeable_event_date` is initialised to `first_licensed_date` and `clean_years_credited` is computed against the clock; `voluntary_market_eligible` starts false and turns true only when a policy of this applicant leaves the plan for the voluntary market. Each vehicle is added under its owning applicant, so the vehicle's `applicant_id` is taken from the addressed applicant rather than from the body, and is described by a 17-character `vin`, a `vehicle_class_code` of `PLEASURE`, `WORK_LT10`, `WORK_GE10` or `BUSINESS`, a `garaging_territory_code`, and a `model_year` between 1980 and 2027.

Submitting the application binds the three together. The payload carries the `applicant_id`, the `producer_id` of the producer of record, the `vehicle_id` to be insured, the `requested_effective_date` the applicant wants coverage to begin, and the applicant's own `declared_violations` — an array, possibly empty, of records each holding a `code` drawn from `SPEEDING_MINOR`, `SPEEDING_MAJOR`, `AT_FAULT_ACCIDENT`, `LICENSE_SUSPENSION`, `RECKLESS_DRIVING` and `DUI`, and an ISO 8601 UTC `conviction_date`. Three referential rules guard the submission. The vehicle must belong to the applicant named in the same payload, or the request is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_APPLICANT_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. The producer of record must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_SUSPENDED` and HTTP 409. And the applicant's `territory_code` must appear in that producer's `appointed_territories`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_TERRITORY_NOT_APPOINTED` and HTTP 422. On success the application lands in `SUBMITTED`, receives the next `intake_sequence`, and is immediately priced: `surcharge_points_estimated`, `estimated_annual_premium_cents`, `estimated_expense_charge_cents`, `estimated_total_charge_cents` and `required_deposit_cents` are all derived from the declared record at a rating date equal to the current clock. Applications can be listed and filtered by `applicant_id`, by `producer_id`, by `vehicle_id` and by `status`; the full `status` vocabulary is `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CURE`, `REJECTED`, `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT`, `DEFERRED_NO_CARRIER`, `ASSIGNED`, `POLICY_ISSUED`, `CLOSED_EXITED` and `CLOSED_CANCELLED`.

An assigned-risk applicant must prove the voluntary market genuinely turned the risk away, and the plan accepts only documented declinations. Each declination is filed against the application by the producer of record or by an administrator, and carries the `rejecting_carrier_naic_code` of the voluntary-market carrier that declined, the `rejection_date` on which it declined, a `reason_code` of `UNACCEPTABLE_DRIVING_RECORD`, `UNDERWRITING_GUIDELINES`, `NO_APPETITE_TERRITORY` or `PRIOR_CANCELLATION`, and the `document_reference` — the plan document control number that evidences the filing. The filing records the application it belongs to through its `application_id`. Declinations are filed by NAIC code rather than by carrier identifier, and the plan never joins the carrier row into rating; but the code must still be a real one, so a `rejecting_carrier_naic_code` matching no enrolled carrier's `naic_code` — regardless of that carrier's own `status` — is refused with `ERR_REJECTING_CARRIER_UNKNOWN` and HTTP 422. The plan also refuses a second declination from a NAIC code already on file for the same application inside the lookback window, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REJECTING_CARRIER` and HTTP 409.

Each accepted declination refreshes the application's `distinct_rejection_count`. That figure is the number of distinct `rejecting_carrier_naic_code` values among the application's declinations whose `rejection_date` falls inside a ninety-day lookback window ending at the current clock — that is, on or after the instant ninety days before the clock and on or at the clock itself. Both ends of the window are inclusive, so a declination dated exactly ninety days before the clock still counts. Duplicate codes collapse to one. The count is recomputed whenever a declination is filed, whenever a cured application is resubmitted, and again at certification, which matters because the clock can move between those moments and an ageing declination can silently fall out of the window.

Premium deposits are recorded against the application by the producer of record or by an administrator, one payment at a time, each carrying an `amount_cents`. A deposit of less than one cent is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_INVALID` and HTTP 422. A valid payment raises both `deposit_paid_total_cents` and `deposit_balance_cents` by `amount_cents`, and — if a policy for this application already exists — immediately sweeps the new money into that policy's `premium_credited_cents` up to the policy's `total_annual_charge_cents`, re-deriving the policy's money axis as described in §3.4. How much the plan requires before it will certify the risk is `required_deposit_cents`: one quarter of `estimated_total_charge_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, but never less than a fifteen-thousand-cent floor — so a small risk still posts a minimum deposit of 150 dollars. It is recomputed at submission, on every deposit, and on every resubmission.

### 3.2. Verification, Cure Rounds, and the Eligibility Decision

A `ComplianceExaminer` owns everything that happens to an application between submission and placement. The first act is the motor-vehicle-record pull: the examiner submits `verified_violations`, an array with the same element shape as `declared_violations`, which either confirms or corrects what the applicant declared. Recording the pull stores that array, stamps `mvr_verified_at` with the current clock, re-derives `surcharge_points_estimated` and the whole premium estimate from the verified record rather than the declared one, and recomputes the applicant's `last_chargeable_event_date` and `clean_years_credited`. Until the pull happens, `verified_violations` is null and `mvr_verified_at` is null.

Surcharge points are the plan's rating expression of a driving record, and they are computed the same way at every rating occasion. The source array is `verified_violations` when `mvr_verified_at` is not null, and `declared_violations` otherwise — the plan never blends the two. From that source, only convictions inside the three-year experience window count: a conviction counts when its `conviction_date` is strictly later than the instant 1095 days before the rating date and is no later than the rating date itself. The lower bound is strict, so a conviction dated exactly 1095 days before the rating date is excluded. Each counting conviction contributes points by its `code`: 1 point for `SPEEDING_MINOR`, 3 for `SPEEDING_MAJOR`, 3 for `AT_FAULT_ACCIDENT`, 4 for `LICENSE_SUSPENSION`, 5 for `RECKLESS_DRIVING` and 8 for `DUI`. To that sum the plan adds a driver-class adder: nothing for `ADULT`, 1 point for `SENIOR`, and 3 points for `YOUTHFUL_OPERATOR`. The result is then capped, and the capped figure is the lesser of 15 and the uncapped sum.

The same arithmetic runs against two different rating dates, and the difference is observable. The **estimated** variant runs at certification with the rating date equal to the clock at that moment, and is written to the application's `surcharge_points_estimated`; at that point no reclassification has occurred and the driver class is the applicant's own. The **applied** variant runs at policy issuance and again at every reclassification, with the rating date equal to the policy's `effective_date`, and is written to the policy's `surcharge_points_applied`, which the plan constrains to the inclusive range 0 through 15. Because the applied variant anchors its 1095-day window to the effective date rather than to the certification instant, a conviction can age out of the window between certification and issuance, and the policy will then rate lower than the application estimated.

If the examiner finds a documentation defect rather than an eligibility failure, the application can be returned to the producer for cure: the examiner supplies a `cure_note`, and the application moves `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_CURE`. The return itself does not touch `cure_count` — that counter increments on the way back. The plan tolerates at most two cure round-trips, so a return is only admissible while `cure_count` is less than 2; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_CURE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409. The producer of record — or an administrator — then resubmits, optionally supplying a corrected `declared_violations` array and a revised `requested_effective_date`. Resubmission moves the application `RETURNED_FOR_CURE → SUBMITTED`, increments `cure_count` by 1, and requires the producer of record to still be `ACTIVE`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_SUSPENDED`. Critically, resubmission resets `verified_violations` to null and `mvr_verified_at` to null: the motor-vehicle record must be pulled again, because the cure may have changed the very record the examiner verified. The premium estimate, `required_deposit_cents` and `distinct_rejection_count` are all re-derived against the current clock.

The eligibility decision is a single examiner action carrying a `decision` of either `CERTIFY` or `REJECT`, and an optional `rejection_reason_code` used with the latter. Both branches require the application to be in `SUBMITTED`, and both require `mvr_verified_at` to be non-null — the plan may not dispose of an unverified driving record in either direction, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_MVR_NOT_VERIFIED` and HTTP 422.

A `CERTIFY` decision imposes three further gates in the order §2.13 fixes. The application's `distinct_rejection_count` must be at least 2 — two different voluntary-market carriers must have declined the risk inside the ninety-day lookback — failing which the decision is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MARKET_REJECTIONS` and HTTP 422. A quota period whose `status` is `OPEN` must exist whose closed window contains the current clock, failing which the decision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE` and HTTP 409. And `deposit_paid_total_cents` must be at least `required_deposit_cents`; the comparison is inclusive, so posting exactly the required amount passes, and posting less is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_REQUIRED` and HTTP 422. When all three pass, the application moves `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT`, `certified_at` is stamped with the clock, `certified_by_user_id` records the examiner's `X-User-ID`, the premium estimate and `surcharge_points_estimated` are recomputed at a rating date equal to that clock, the containing period's `total_assignable_units` rises by 1, and — because the apportionment total just moved — every `ACTIVE` carrier's `quota_target_units` is recomputed.

A `REJECT` decision moves the application `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal. The submitted `rejection_reason_code` is recorded on the application and `certified_by_user_id` records the deciding examiner's `X-User-ID` exactly as a certification would. No money moves: `deposit_paid_total_cents` stays put and remains wholly inside `deposit_balance_cents`, so the deposit invariant of §2.8 continues to hold on a rejected risk.

### 3.3. Quota Periods, Assignment Cycles, and Designating a Servicing Carrier

The plan measures obligation in closed quota periods. A `PlanAdministrator` opens one with a `period_code` label such as "2026-P3", an inclusive `window_start` and an inclusive `window_end` that must be no earlier than the start, and optionally the `underserved_territories` whose voluntary writings attract enhanced credit this period. A new period starts with `status` `OPEN` and `total_assignable_units` at zero, and its closed window may not intersect the window of any existing period — an intersection is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409. The period's `status` moves through `OPEN`, `RECONCILING` and `CLOSED`. A period read exposes `total_assignable_units` — the count of applications whose `certified_at` falls inside the period's inclusive window and whose `status` is any of `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT`, `DEFERRED_NO_CARRIER`, `ASSIGNED`, `POLICY_ISSUED`, `CLOSED_EXITED` or `CLOSED_CANCELLED` — alongside `total_assignable_car_years`, described in §3.9.

Within a period the administrator schedules assignment cycles, each addressed under its parent period and carrying that period through its `plan_period_id`, with its own inclusive intake `window_start` and `window_end`, the latter no earlier than the former. Only applications certified inside the cycle's window are queued by that cycle's run. Two temporal rules constrain scheduling. The cycle window must lie inside the parent period's window, or the request is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PERIOD` and HTTP 422. And the cycle window must be separated from every sibling cycle window of the same period by at least one clear day: the new window's start must be at least two days after a sibling's end, or the new window's end must be at least two days before a sibling's start. Unlike policy terms, cycle windows are closed intervals and mere adjacency is refused — the plan requires a clear settlement day between runs — so an overlap or a mere abutment is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409. A scheduled cycle starts in `SCHEDULED` and its `status` reaches `COMPLETED` exactly once. Cycles can be listed under their period and filtered by `status`, and a cycle read reports `assigned_count`, `deferred_count` and `run_at`.

Running a cycle is the plan's central act of apportionment, and it advances the cycle `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` while stamping `run_at` with the clock. It is admissible only while the cycle is still `SCHEDULED`, only once the clock has reached the cycle's `window_start` — an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED` and HTTP 409 — and only while the parent period's `status` is `OPEN`.

The run first builds its queue: every application whose `status` is `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT` or `DEFERRED_NO_CARRIER` and whose `certified_at` falls inside the cycle's inclusive window, ordered by `certified_at` ascending and, where two applications share a certification instant, by `intake_sequence` ascending. Deferred applications from an earlier run are therefore retried by a later cycle whose window still contains their certification instant, and a retry that succeeds advances the application `DEFERRED_NO_CARRIER → ASSIGNED` with exactly the same consequences as a first-time placement.

For each queued application in that order, the run assembles a candidate pool of carriers. A carrier is a candidate when its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `posture` is `OPEN`, and the garaging territory of the application's vehicle appears in that carrier's `licensed_territories`. When the run is the reassignment cascade of a carrier withdrawal rather than a cycle run, the withdrawing carrier is additionally excluded from its own pool. If the candidate pool is empty, the application moves to `DEFERRED_NO_CARRIER`, the cycle's `deferred_count` rises by 1, no assignment row is created, no error is raised, and the run proceeds to the next application — an empty market is a fact to be recorded, not a request failure.

If the pool is non-empty, the winner is the candidate with the greatest unmet quota, measured as its `quota_target_units` less its `assigned_risk_count`. Ties are broken by `voluntary_market_share_bps` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `enrollment_sequence` ascending, and never by `id`. A deficit of zero or below does not disqualify a carrier: when it is the only licensed candidate it takes the risk and duly becomes overallocated. The winning carrier's `assigned_risk_count` rises by 1 and its `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture` are recomputed *before the next queued application is ranked*, so the ordering of the queue is observable in the outcome — two applications in a single run can land on different carriers even though the candidate set never changed.

Each placement writes an assignment row and advances its application `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT → ASSIGNED`, while the cycle's `assigned_count` rises by 1. The row records the risk through its `application_id` and the designated servicing carrier through its `carrier_id`; both are written by the selector and are never accepted from a client. It records the run that produced it through `assignment_cycle_id`, its `assignment_kind` is `INITIAL`, and its `predecessor_assignment_id` is null. Its `status` begins at `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, and the full `status` vocabulary is `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, `ACCEPTED`, `POLICY_ISSUED`, `RESCINDED`, `REASSIGNED` and `CLOSED`. Its `batch_sequence` is its 1-based creation position within this run. Its `deficit_score_at_selection` preserves the winning carrier's deficit at the instant of selection — the audit trail of the ranking decision, carried to 1 decimal place — and its `provisional_premium_cents` snapshots the application's `estimated_total_charge_cents` as it stood at selection. Its `acceptance_deadline` is ten days after the assignment's `created_at`. `rescission_reason_code` is null unless and until the plan pulls the assignment back. Assignments can be listed and filtered by `application_id`, by `carrier_id`, by `assignment_cycle_id`, by `predecessor_assignment_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Acceptance, Policy Issuance, and Final Rating

A designated risk is not yet a policy. The `ServicingCarrier` named on the assignment accepts it, and acceptance is admissible only while the assignment is `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, only while the clock is at or before the `acceptance_deadline` — a later attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and HTTP 409 — and only while the accepting carrier's `posture` is not `SUSPENDED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. The caller's own carrier identity must match the assignment's designated carrier, or §2.1's scope rule refuses the call with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`. Either way, `accepted_at` is stamped with the clock.

Where acceptance lands depends on `assignment_kind`, and the two branches are genuinely different. Accepting an `INITIAL` assignment advances it `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE → ACCEPTED`; the policy does not exist yet and the carrier must still issue it. Accepting a `REASSIGNMENT` assignment advances it `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE → POLICY_ISSUED` directly, because the policy already exists and is merely being transferred — the consequences of that transfer are set out in §3.8.

Issuing the policy is the servicing carrier's own act. The payload carries the `assignment_id` that authorises issuance, the carrier-assigned `policy_number`, the `effective_date` that opens the annual term inclusively, and the `expiration_date` that closes it exclusively. Issuance requires the assignment to be in `ACCEPTED`, requires that no policy already references that assignment, and requires the carrier's `posture` not to be `SUSPENDED`. The `expiration_date` must be exactly 365 days after the `effective_date` and the `effective_date` must not be before the clock, or the request is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERM_INVALID` and HTTP 422. The issuing carrier must be licensed in the vehicle's `garaging_territory_code`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_LICENSED_IN_TERRITORY` and HTTP 422. And no other policy on the same vehicle may have a term that intersects the requested one: terms are treated as half-open intervals running from the `effective_date` inclusive to the `expiration_date` exclusive, so exact adjacency — a new `effective_date` equal to a prior policy's `expiration_date` — is permitted, while any true intersection is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERM_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409. On success the policy's `carrier_id` is copied from the assignment, the assignment advances `ACCEPTED → POLICY_ISSUED`, and the application advances `ASSIGNED → POLICY_ISSUED`.

Final rating happens at issuance, at a rating date equal to the policy's `effective_date`. The plan starts from a territory base annual charge selected by the vehicle's `garaging_territory_code`: 96475 cents for `T01`, 148337 cents for `T02` and 231909 cents for `T03`. It multiplies that base by a class factor selected by the vehicle's `vehicle_class_code` — 1.00 for `PLEASURE`, 1.10 for `WORK_LT10`, 1.25 for `WORK_GE10` and 1.45 for `BUSINESS` — and by a plan-approved surcharge factor selected as a step function of the applied surcharge points: 1.00 at exactly 0 points, 1.15 for 1 through 3 points, 1.35 for 4 or 5 points, 1.60 for 6 through 9 points, and 1.90 at 10 points or more. The chosen factor is stored on the policy as `surcharge_factor_applied`, carried to 2 decimal places.

The product is then split across three coverage lines and — this is the part that must not be simplified — **each line is rounded before anything is added**. The bodily-injury line takes 0.55 of the product, the property-damage line 0.30 and the uninsured-motorist line 0.15; each of the three is rounded half up to whole cents independently, and the three rounded figures are stored in the policy's `premium_lines` object under the keys `BI`, `PD` and `UM`. The policy's `final_premium_cents` is the sum of those three already-rounded lines. Summing the unrounded lines and rounding once at the end is wrong and diverges: for territory `T02`, class `PLEASURE` and 6 applied points the three rounded lines are 130537, 71202 and 35601, giving 237340, whereas rounding the unrounded sum yields 237339 — and that single cent propagates into the expense tier and the deposit requirement.

The plan expense charge is then layered on top. Its rate is chosen from `final_premium_cents` *before* the charge is added, never from the charge-inclusive total: 0.06 when `final_premium_cents` is below 75000 cents, 0.05 when it is at least 75000 but below 150000 cents, and 0.04 when it is 150000 cents or more. Both tier ceilings are exclusive, so a final premium of exactly 150000 cents sits in the lowest-rate tier. The rate is applied to `final_premium_cents` and rounded half up to whole cents to give `plan_expense_charge_cents`, and `total_annual_charge_cents` is `final_premium_cents` plus `plan_expense_charge_cents`. The same layering produces the application's estimate fields at submission, verification, resubmission and certification: `estimated_annual_premium_cents` is the per-line-rounded premium sum, `estimated_expense_charge_cents` is the expense charge on it, and `estimated_total_charge_cents` is the two added together.

Issuance closes with the ledger and the exposure figure. The applicant's `deposit_balance_cents` sweeps into the policy's `premium_credited_cents`, capped at `total_annual_charge_cents`, per §2.8; `additional_premium_due_cents` is restated as whatever remains uncredited. The money axis is initialised from the result of that sweep — `premium_settlement_status` becomes `PAID_IN_FULL` when `premium_credited_cents` is at least `total_annual_charge_cents`, and `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING` otherwise. And `exposure_car_years_current_period` is computed against whichever period is currently `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`, by the rule in §3.9.

Every policy carries two independent state axes that never write each other. The coverage axis is `policy_status`, beginning at `IN_FORCE` and ranging over `IN_FORCE`, `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING`, `NON_RENEWAL_PENDING`, `NON_RENEWED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXITED_TO_VOLUNTARY`, of which the last three are terminal. The money axis is `premium_settlement_status`, ranging over `PAID_IN_FULL`, `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING`, `REFUND_ISSUED` and `BALANCE_DUE_AT_CANCELLATION`, of which the last two are terminal. A move on one axis never implies a move on the other: when a deposit sweep or a downward re-rating brings `premium_credited_cents` up to at least `total_annual_charge_cents`, the money axis moves `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING → PAID_IN_FULL` and sets `additional_premium_due_cents` to zero while `policy_status` is not touched at all; and when a re-rating pushes `total_annual_charge_cents` back above `premium_credited_cents`, the money axis moves `PAID_IN_FULL → PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING` and restates `additional_premium_due_cents` as the difference, again leaving `policy_status` untouched. Policies can be listed and filtered by `carrier_id`, by `assignment_id`, by `policy_status` and by `premium_settlement_status`. The policy also carries `rating_revision`, which is 1 at issuance, and `reassignment_required`, which is false at issuance.

The plan may also pull an assignment back. Rescission is an administrator action carrying a `reason_code` of `ISSUANCE_DEADLINE_MISSED`, `CARRIER_INELIGIBLE` or `ASSIGNMENT_ERROR`, which is recorded on the assignment as `rescission_reason_code`. It is admissible while the assignment is `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` or `ACCEPTED`, and only while no policy references that assignment. A `reason_code` of `ISSUANCE_DEADLINE_MISSED` additionally requires the clock to be strictly past the `acceptance_deadline`. A rescission moves the assignment to `RESCINDED`, returns its application to `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT` so the risk re-enters the queue and can be placed by the next cycle run, lowers the designated carrier's `assigned_risk_count` by 1, and recomputes that carrier's `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture`. When and only when the reason was `ISSUANCE_DEADLINE_MISSED`, the carrier additionally suffers a 5-point deduction to `performance_score`.

### 3.5. Claim Handling Standards

Losses are reported by the servicing carrier itself. A claim is filed against a policy through its `policy_id` and carries the `loss_date`, an `at_fault` boolean stating whether the insured was at fault, a `coverage_line` of `BI`, `PD` or `UM`, and the carrier's own `reserve_cents`, which must be zero or greater. The `loss_date` must fall on or after the policy's `effective_date` and no later than the current clock; a loss dated before the term opened or in the future is refused with `ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM` and HTTP 422. A filed claim begins in `OPEN`, records `reported_at` as the current clock, and derives its `acknowledgement_deadline` as ten days after `reported_at` — that ten-day window *is* the plan's claim-handling standard. Filing a claim does not touch the policy's `complaint_freeze_active` flag, but it does immediately block the policy's voluntary-market exit for as long as the claim stays live. Claims are listed under their policy and filterable by `status`, whose full vocabulary is `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `CLOSED_PAID` and `CLOSED_DENIED`; a claim read exposes its `standard_breach` flag.

Acknowledgement is the moment the standard is tested. The servicing carrier of the claim's policy acknowledges it, moving the claim `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamping `acknowledged_at` with the current clock. If `acknowledged_at` is later than `acknowledgement_deadline`, three things follow in the same request: `standard_breach` is set to true, the carrier's `breach_count_current_period` rises by 1, and the carrier's `performance_score` falls by 5 — floored at 0 — after which its `posture` is recomputed against the ladder in §2.9. An acknowledgement inside the window sets `standard_breach` to false and leaves the carrier's score and counters alone. Because `breach_count_current_period` at 3 or above forces `RESTRICTED`, three late acknowledgements in one period take a carrier out of the candidate pool for new assignments even while its score remains comfortable.

Closing a claim requires an explicit `disposition`, and the two dispositions are treated symmetrically but differently. A `PAID` closure moves the claim `ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED_PAID` and records the supplied `paid_cents` as the indemnity paid; `paid_cents` may not exceed the claim's `reserve_cents`, and an overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` and HTTP 422. A `DENIED` closure moves the claim `ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED_DENIED`, requires a `denial_reason` to be present, and leaves `paid_cents` at 0. Whichever disposition applies, if the claim's `at_fault` flag is true the closure recomputes the applicant's `last_chargeable_event_date` and `clean_years_credited`, because an at-fault loss resets the applicant's clean-years clock.

Clean years are the plan's measure of rehabilitation, and they are recomputed on claim closure, on motor-vehicle-record verification, on policy issuance, and on every read of the applicant. The plan gathers two kinds of chargeable event: the `conviction_date` of every *verified* violation on any of the applicant's applications, and the `loss_date` of every claim of the applicant whose `at_fault` flag is true. Declared-but-unverified violations are ignored entirely. `last_chargeable_event_date` is the latest of those event dates, or — when the applicant has no chargeable event at all — the applicant's `first_licensed_date`. `clean_years_credited` is then the lesser of 5 and the number of whole 365-day years between `last_chargeable_event_date` and the current clock, computed by taking the whole days elapsed, dividing by 365, and truncating downward. The measurement in whole floored days makes the boundary sharp: read at a clock of 2026-09-15, a chargeable event on 2023-09-16 is exactly 1095 days back and credits 3 clean years, so a voluntary-market exit is allowed, while an event on 2023-09-20 is 1091 days back and credits only 2, so the exit is refused. Note the deliberate interaction with surcharge rating: an event exactly 1095 days old grants the third clean year and at the same instant falls outside the strict points window described in §3.2.

### 3.6. Complaints and the Two-Examiner Review

Complaints are docketed by a `PlanAdministrator` or a `ComplianceExaminer` against the servicing carrier of a policy. The filing carries the `policy_id` complained about, the `carrier_id` the complaint runs against, a `complaint_type` of `CLAIM_HANDLING_DELAY`, `RATING_DISPUTE`, `CANCELLATION_DISPUTE` or `SERVICE_STANDARD`, and the complainant's `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters. The named `carrier_id` must equal the policy's current servicing carrier at the moment of filing, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_CARRIER_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422 — a complaint cannot be aimed at a carrier that has already handed the risk on. A docketed complaint begins in `OPEN` with `review_round` at 1, and its `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, `findings_note`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `disposition` all begin null.

Docketing has an immediate protective effect on the insured. The policy's `open_complaint_count` — the number of that policy's complaints whose `status` is `OPEN` or `FINDINGS_RECORDED` — rises by 1, and `complaint_freeze_active`, which is true exactly when `open_complaint_count` is greater than zero, becomes true. While the freeze is on, the plan refuses both adverse actions against that insured: cancellation and the service of a non-renewal notice, each with `ERR_COMPLAINT_FREEZE_ACTIVE` and HTTP 409. Complaints are listable and filterable by `policy_id`, by `carrier_id` and by `status`, whose full vocabulary is `OPEN`, `FINDINGS_RECORDED`, `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and `RESOLVED_NOT_UPHELD`.

Investigation and disposition are deliberately split between two different examiners. The first examiner records the investigation outcome by supplying a `findings_note`, which moves the complaint `OPEN → FINDINGS_RECORDED`, stores the note, and writes that examiner's `X-User-ID` into `findings_recorded_by_user_id`. A second examiner then disposes of it by supplying a `disposition` and, optionally, a `resolution_note`; the resolving examiner's `X-User-ID` is written to `resolved_by_user_id`. The segregation is enforced literally: if the `X-User-ID` presented on the resolution equals the value already stored in `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409, so one examiner can never both investigate and dispose of the same complaint.

Three dispositions are available, and all three act from `FINDINGS_RECORDED`. A `disposition` of `UPHELD` moves the complaint `FINDINGS_RECORDED → RESOLVED_UPHELD` and is the expensive one for the carrier: its `upheld_complaint_count` rises by 1, its `performance_score` falls by 20 with a floor of 0, and its `posture` is recomputed. If that recomputation lands the carrier in `SUSPENDED`, a cascade follows in the same request — every assignment of that carrier still in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` is moved to `RESCINDED` with a `rescission_reason_code` of `CARRIER_INELIGIBLE`, and each of those assignments' applications returns from `ASSIGNED` to `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT` so the risks re-enter the queue; each rescission also lowers the carrier's `assigned_risk_count` and re-derives its `quota_variance_units` and `overallocated`. Because two upheld complaints cost 40 points in total and the clamp is applied after each adjustment, a fresh carrier's first upheld complaint leaves it `RESTRICTED` at 80 and its second leaves it `SUSPENDED` at 60. Finally, the policy's `open_complaint_count` falls by 1 and `complaint_freeze_active` is re-derived from it.

A `disposition` of `NOT_UPHELD` moves the complaint `FINDINGS_RECORDED → RESOLVED_NOT_UPHELD`. The carrier's `performance_score`, `upheld_complaint_count` and `posture` are entirely unaffected — there is no deduction and no cascade — but the policy side behaves identically to the upheld case: `open_complaint_count` falls by 1 and `complaint_freeze_active` is re-derived, so the freeze lifts once no complaint of that policy remains unresolved.

A `disposition` of `RETURNED_FOR_FURTHER_REVIEW` sends the complaint back for another investigation round, moving it `FINDINGS_RECORDED → OPEN`. `review_round` rises by 1, and both `findings_recorded_by_user_id` and `findings_note` are cleared so that a fresh examiner may record findings without tripping the segregation rule. The policy's `open_complaint_count` is deliberately left unchanged, so the freeze stays on across the round trip. The plan bounds this cycle: a further review round is only admissible while `review_round` is less than 3, and a request on a complaint already at its third round is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT` and HTTP 409. Note that this bound is checked at business-constraint priority, below the segregation check — so a complaint at round 3 presented by the wrong examiner reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` first.

### 3.7. Mid-Term Change: Reclassification, Non-Renewal, Cancellation, and the Voluntary Exit

Driver reclassification is an administrator-ordered re-rating of an in-force policy. The request carries the `expected_policy_version` demanded by §2.11, the `reclassified_driver_class` the applicant is being moved to, and the `reclassification_effective_date` on which the reclassification takes effect. On success the policy's `version` increments by 1 and its `rating_revision` rises by 1, and `surcharge_points_applied`, `surcharge_factor_applied`, `premium_lines`, `final_premium_cents`, `plan_expense_charge_cents` and `total_annual_charge_cents` are all recomputed by the §3.4 arithmetic with the new driver class substituted into the class adder. The applicant's own `driver_class` is updated to the `reclassified_driver_class`. The ledger then absorbs the difference: when the recomputed `total_annual_charge_cents` is below `premium_credited_cents`, the excess moves into `refund_paid_cents` and `premium_credited_cents` drops to the new total; otherwise `additional_premium_due_cents` is restated as `total_annual_charge_cents` less `premium_credited_cents`. Either way `premium_settlement_status` is re-derived on the money axis and `policy_status` is not touched at all.

A servicing carrier that does not want the risk beyond the current term serves a non-renewal notice with a `reason_note`. Four preconditions apply. The policy's `non_renewal_notice_count` must be 0 — the plan allows at most one notice per term. The policy's `complaint_freeze_active` must be false, or the notice is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_FREEZE_ACTIVE`. The clock must be at or before 45 days ahead of the policy's `expiration_date`, so that the insured has at least 45 days' warning; a later notice is refused with `ERR_NON_RENEWAL_NOTICE_TOO_LATE` and HTTP 422. And the carrier's `non_renewal_count_current_period` must be below 3, since the plan caps each carrier at three non-renewals per period; a fourth is refused with `ERR_NON_RENEWAL_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409. A served notice moves the policy `IN_FORCE → NON_RENEWAL_PENDING`, sets `non_renewal_notice_count` to 1, records `non_renewal_effective_date` as the policy's `expiration_date`, and raises the carrier's `non_renewal_count_current_period` by 1. The notice then matures without anyone calling anything: once the clock reaches or passes `non_renewal_effective_date`, the policy is `NON_RENEWAL_PENDING → NON_RENEWED` as observed on the next read of that policy, its assignment moves to `CLOSED`, and the carrier's `assigned_risk_count` falls by 1 with the usual re-derivation of `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture`.

Cancellation is the one operation three roles may perform, and the caller's role *is* the initiating party: a `ServicingCarrier` caller yields a `cancellation_initiated_by` of `CARRIER`, a `PlanAdministrator` caller yields `PLAN`, and a `ProducerAgent` caller — acting for the insured — yields `INSURED`. The request carries a `cancellation_reason_code` drawn from `NON_PAYMENT_OF_PREMIUM`, `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`, `MATERIAL_MISREPRESENTATION`, `VEHICLE_NOT_GARAGED_IN_STATE`, `INSURED_REQUEST` and `PLAN_ORDERED`, together with the `cancellation_effective_date` that closes coverage. The policy must be `IN_FORCE` and its `complaint_freeze_active` must be false. The `cancellation_effective_date` must lie inside the term, on or after `effective_date` and on or before `expiration_date`, and must not be earlier than the current clock; a date outside those bounds is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM` and HTTP 422.

Reason codes are further restricted by how far into the term the cancellation falls. When the whole days between the policy's `effective_date` and the `cancellation_effective_date` exceed 60, the `cancellation_reason_code` must be one of `NON_PAYMENT_OF_PREMIUM`, `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`, `MATERIAL_MISREPRESENTATION` or `INSURED_REQUEST`; `VEHICLE_NOT_GARAGED_IN_STATE` and `PLAN_ORDERED` are first-sixty-day reasons only, and using either later is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_REASON_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 422. The boundary is strict: a cancellation exactly 60 days into the term still permits the restricted reasons, and one 61 days in does not. This reason restriction is evaluated only after the term-bound date rule of the preceding paragraph has passed: both sit at business-temporal priority, and the enumeration at §2.13 priority 10 lists which errors share that level rather than fixing their order within it, so a `cancellation_effective_date` that is itself outside the term or behind the clock reports `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM` even when the submitted reason code would also have been impermissible for the elapsed term position. Recall from §2.13 that the complaint freeze is checked before both, so a frozen policy reports the freeze error even when the reason code would also have been impermissible.

Settlement then computes what the carrier keeps. Days in force is the whole days between the policy's `effective_date` and the `cancellation_effective_date`, treating the coverage interval as half-open — the effective date counts, the cancellation effective date does not. On the **pro-rata** basis, `earned_premium_cents` is `total_annual_charge_cents` multiplied by days in force, divided by 365, and rounded half up to whole cents; the insured keeps every unearned cent, with no penalty and no minimum retained premium. On the **short-rate** basis, the plan first takes that same product — the term charge multiplied by days in force and divided by 365 — multiplies it by a penalty multiplier of 1.10 *before* rounding, and rounds the whole thing half up to whole cents; it then raises the result to a minimum retained premium of 5000 cents if it fell below that, and finally clamps it down to `total_annual_charge_cents` so the plan can never earn more than the full term charge. The multiplier is applied inside the rounding rather than to a pre-rounded pro-rata figure. Which basis applies is decided by the initiating party and recorded on the policy as `refund_basis`: a `cancellation_initiated_by` of `CARRIER` or `PLAN` gives `refund_basis` `PRO_RATA`, while `INSURED` gives `SHORT_RATE`. The divergence is material — on a policy whose `total_annual_charge_cents` is 246834, cancelled 91 days in, the pro-rata basis earns 61539 and refunds 185295, while the short-rate basis earns 67693 and refunds only 179141.

With `earned_premium_cents` fixed, the ledger settles in three moves: `refund_paid_cents` increases by the credited deposit less the earned premium when that difference is positive and by nothing otherwise; `balance_due_cents` becomes the earned premium less the credited deposit when that difference is positive and 0 otherwise; and `premium_credited_cents` is reduced to the lesser of its current value and `earned_premium_cents`. The money axis then follows from the arithmetic. When the refund is positive the policy moves to `REFUND_ISSUED` — from `PAID_IN_FULL` and from `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING` alike, and in the latter case `balance_due_cents` settles at 0. When earned premium exceeds the credited deposit the policy moves `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING → BALANCE_DUE_AT_CANCELLATION` with `balance_due_cents` carrying the shortfall and `refund_paid_cents` unchanged. When earned premium exactly equals the credited deposit the policy moves `PREMIUM_OUTSTANDING → PAID_IN_FULL` with `refund_paid_cents` unchanged and `balance_due_cents` at 0. On the coverage axis the policy moves `IN_FORCE → CANCELLED`, its `cancellation_reason_code`, `cancellation_effective_date` and `cancellation_initiated_by` are recorded, its assignment moves to `CLOSED`, its application moves `POLICY_ISSUED → CLOSED_CANCELLED`, and the servicing carrier's `assigned_risk_count` falls by 1 with `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture` recomputed.

The happier ending is a voluntary-market exit, ordered by a `PlanAdministrator` with an optional `exit_note`. It is the plan's declaration that a risk has been rehabilitated and no longer needs the residual market. The policy must be `IN_FORCE`, the applicant's `clean_years_credited` must be at least 3 — fewer is refused with `ERR_CLEAN_YEARS_INSUFFICIENT` and HTTP 422 — and no claim of the policy may be in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, since an unsettled loss is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_EXIT` and HTTP 409. On success the policy moves `IN_FORCE → EXITED_TO_VOLUNTARY`, the applicant's `voluntary_market_eligible` becomes true, the assignment moves to `CLOSED`, and the application moves `POLICY_ISSUED → CLOSED_EXITED`. The carrier's `assigned_risk_count` falls by 1, and its `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture` are recomputed.

Exiting a risk also earns the carrier credit against its quota, which is how the plan rewards carriers for absorbing residual-market business into their voluntary books. The credit is territory-weighted against the period that is `OPEN` or `RECONCILING` at the moment of exit: `voluntary_credit_units` rises by 1.5 when the vehicle's `garaging_territory_code` appears in that period's `underserved_territories`, and by 1.0 otherwise. Credit is carried to 1 decimal place and accrues to the carrier that was servicing the policy at the moment of exit, which after a reassignment is not necessarily the carrier the risk was originally assigned to.

### 3.8. Carrier Withdrawal and Reassignment of the Book

A carrier leaves the plan by filing notice, not by disappearing. A `PlanAdministrator` files the notice with an `exit_effective_date`, which is recorded on the carrier and must be at least 90 days after the current clock; a shorter notice is refused with `ERR_EXIT_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` and HTTP 422. The carrier's `status` must be `ACTIVE` at the time of the notice, and a carrier already withdrawing is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE` and HTTP 409. While a carrier is `ACTIVE` its `exit_effective_date` is null.

Filing the notice moves the carrier `ACTIVE → EXIT_NOTICED` and sets off the largest cascade in the system, all inside the one request. First, because `status` other than `ACTIVE` is the top rung of the posture ladder, the carrier's `posture` is recomputed to `SUSPENDED`. Second, every policy of the carrier whose `policy_status` is `IN_FORCE` moves `IN_FORCE → REASSIGNMENT_PENDING` with `reassignment_required` set to true. Third, for each such policy — processed in ascending policy `created_at` order and stamped with `batch_sequence` running from 1 upward across the cascade — the plan creates a successor assignment whose `assignment_kind` is `REASSIGNMENT`, whose `assignment_cycle_id` is null because no cycle produced it, and whose `predecessor_assignment_id` points at the assignment the policy currently sits on. The receiving carrier for each successor is chosen by exactly the selector of §3.3, with the withdrawing carrier excluded from its own candidate pool, and each receiving carrier's `assigned_risk_count` rises by 1 with `quota_variance_units`, `overallocated` and `posture` recomputed before the next policy is placed. Fourth, every assignment of the withdrawing carrier still in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` is moved to `RESCINDED` with a `rescission_reason_code` of `CARRIER_INELIGIBLE`, and each of those applications returns to `CERTIFIED_AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT`. Fifth, the withdrawing carrier leaves the apportionment denominator, so `quota_target_units` is recomputed for every remaining `ACTIVE` carrier.

The receiving carrier completes the transfer by accepting the successor assignment, which as noted in §3.4 lands directly on `POLICY_ISSUED` because the policy already exists. That acceptance rewrites the policy's `carrier_id` to the accepting carrier, moves the policy `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING → IN_FORCE`, and sets `reassignment_required` back to false. The predecessor assignment moves `POLICY_ISSUED → REASSIGNED`, and the predecessor's carrier has its `assigned_risk_count` lowered by 1 with the usual re-derivations. Then the bubble-up: when the predecessor's carrier has `status` `EXIT_NOTICED` and now holds no policy at all whose `policy_status` is `IN_FORCE`, `REASSIGNMENT_PENDING` or `NON_RENEWAL_PENDING`, that carrier moves `EXIT_NOTICED → EXIT_COMPLETE`, which is terminal. At that point its `assigned_risk_count` is 0 and its `posture` stays `SUSPENDED` — a completed exit never returns to `OPEN`.

A separate, much narrower administrator action records a state filing that changes which territories a carrier may write. It replaces the carrier's `licensed_territories` wholesale with the supplied set, and is admissible only while the carrier's `status` is `ACTIVE`; a withdrawing carrier is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Deliberately, the plan does *not* reassign the book: existing assignment and policy rows are left exactly where they are, so a carrier can end up servicing a risk garaged in a territory it may no longer write, and only *future* candidate filtering reflects the amendment. The carrier's `posture` is recomputed, but `quota_target_units` figures are unchanged across the whole plan, because neither the set of `ACTIVE` carriers nor the market-share denominator moved.

### 3.9. Period Closure, Exposure, and Quota Reconciliation

Closing the intake side of a quota period is an administrator action with three preconditions: the period's `status` must be `OPEN`, the clock must have reached or passed the period's `window_end` — an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED` and HTTP 409 — and no assignment cycle of that period may still be `SCHEDULED`, since an unrun cycle would still be entitled to place risks; an outstanding cycle is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_STILL_SCHEDULED` and HTTP 409. Closure moves the period `OPEN → RECONCILING` and freezes both `total_assignable_units` and `total_assignable_car_years` at the closure instant. It also turns the page for every carrier whose `status` is `ACTIVE`: `breach_count_current_period` is reset to 0, `non_renewal_count_current_period` is reset to 0, `performance_score` is raised by 10 but never above 100, and `posture` is recomputed — which is how a carrier restricted by breaches or by a mediocre score can earn its way back to `OPEN`.

Car-year exposure is the plan's second, earned measure of volume, and it is computed per policy before it is aggregated. For one policy against one period, the exposure interval starts at the later of the policy's `effective_date` and the period's `window_start`, and ends at the earliest of three instants: the policy's `expiration_date`, the day after the period's `window_end`, and the current clock. The period window is inclusive at both ends, which is why the interval is allowed to run to the day *after* `window_end`; the running clock caps it so that future exposure is never counted. The number of whole days between those two instants, floored at 0, is divided by 365 and rounded half up to 4 decimal places to give that policy's `exposure_car_years_current_period`. A carrier's exposure for the period is the sum of those *already-rounded* per-policy figures across the carrier's policies whose `policy_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and the period's `total_assignable_car_years` is in turn the sum of those already-rounded carrier figures across carriers whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. To make the layering concrete: for a period running 2026-09-01 through 2026-11-30 read at a clock of 2026-11-30, a policy effective 2026-11-24 contributes 6 days, or 0.0164, and a policy effective 2026-09-01 contributes 90 days, or 0.2466, for a total of 0.2630.

Reconciliation is filed one carrier and one period at a time by a `PlanAdministrator`, naming the `carrier_id` being reconciled, the `plan_period_id` being reconciled, the `basis` of measurement, and the `expected_carrier_version` required by §2.11. The carrier's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The period's `status` must be `RECONCILING`; a filing against a period still open or already closed is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_RECONCILING` and HTTP 409.

The `basis` selects between the plan's two approved measures, and they produce genuinely different answers. On the `POLICY_COUNT` basis — the plan's headcount measure — `obligation_units` is simply the carrier's `quota_target_units`, a whole number, and `credited_units` is the carrier's live `assigned_risk_count` plus its `voluntary_credit_units`, carried to 1 decimal place because voluntary credit is granted in halves. A carrier that took exactly its whole target therefore reconciles to a variance of zero and owes nothing. On the `CAR_YEAR_EXPOSURE` basis — the plan's earned-exposure measure — `obligation_units` is the period's `total_assignable_car_years` multiplied by the carrier's `voluntary_market_share_bps`, divided by the sum of `voluntary_market_share_bps` across all carriers whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and rounded half up to 4 decimal places; `credited_units` is the carrier's own car-year exposure for that period, computed as above, plus its `voluntary_credit_units`, likewise carried to 4 decimal places. Because exposure is earned over time, a carrier that took its full headcount late in the period is still deficient on this basis: on the same figures a carrier can reconcile to a variance of 0 and no penalty on `POLICY_COUNT` while showing a variance of -0.1151 and a penalty of 2878 cents on `CAR_YEAR_EXPOSURE`.

Whichever basis applies, `variance_units` is `credited_units` less `obligation_units`, so a negative variance means the carrier fell short of its obligation. `penalty_cents` is then the shortfall — that is, the negation of `variance_units` when the variance is negative, and 0 when it is zero or positive — multiplied by 25000 cents per deficient unit and rounded half up to whole cents. `voluntary_credit_units_applied` records the carrier's `voluntary_credit_units` as folded into `credited_units` by this filing, so the audit trail shows how much of the credited volume was voluntary rather than assigned.

Filing has four consequences. The carrier's `version` increments by 1. Any prior reconciliation row for the same carrier and the same period moves `FINAL → SUPERSEDED`, leaving the new row as the only `FINAL` one — the `status` vocabulary is exactly `FINAL` and `SUPERSEDED` — and the carrier's `penalty_balance_cents` is recomputed from its `FINAL` rows only, so a superseded penalty stops counting. Third, when every carrier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` now holds a `FINAL` reconciliation for the period, the period bubbles up `RECONCILING → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and no further reconciliation row may be created for that period. Reconciliations are listable and filterable by `carrier_id`, by `plan_period_id`, by `basis` and by `status`.
