# Hormone-Pellet & Wellness-Injection Clinic API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hormone-pellet clinic sits at an unusual intersection. Clinically it is a surgical practice: a physician implants milligrams of testosterone or estradiol under a patient's skin, and once those pellets are in the body nothing can be un-done — not a billing correction, not a recall, not a change of heart. Operationally it is a controlled-inventory warehouse: every pellet arrives in a manufacturer lot, sits in quarantine until a release decision is made, is reserved against a named patient's slot, and must be accounted for down to the individual pellet whether it ends up implanted, broken on the table, returned to the shelf, or pulled out of circulation by a recall. Commercially it is a subscription business: memberships with monthly fees and tiered discounts, prepaid injection series, self-pay installment plans, cancellation fees keyed to how much notice the patient gave. This API is the system of record for all three faces of that clinic simultaneously, and most of its difficulty comes from the places where the three collide.

The product exists because those collisions are currently managed by hand, and manual reconciliation fails in exactly the ways that matter most. A physician's standing order delegates injection authority to a nurse within a typed, dose-capped, time-bounded scope; when a severe adverse event suspends that order, every procedure booked under it must freeze — including procedures belonging to patients who have nothing to do with the event — and the pellets those procedures had reserved must flow back into the shared allocation pool, changing which lot the next patient draws from. A supplier recall must chase pellets forward through allocations, procedures, patient notifications and safety surveillance, five hops deep, and must distinguish pellets still on a shelf from pellets already inside a person. A single laboratory number has to be read twice against two different rulers — the laboratory's wide reference interval and the clinic's narrower therapeutic target — because one ruler decides whether the patient is medically normal and the other decides whether they qualify for another dose.

What follows specifies that system completely: sixteen record types, fourteen independent state axes, nineteen named calculations, a thirty-nine-rung validation priority ladder that fixes exactly which complaint a rejected request hears first, three optimistic-concurrency guards on the writes that commit irreversible facts, and three conservation identities that must hold after every single mutation. Every threshold, every rounding order, every tie-break and every cascade hop is stated. Where two rules could plausibly fire on the same request, the document says which one wins.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All resources are mounted under the `/clinic` prefix.

### 2.1. Clinic Personnel, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven declared roles, the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` is returned — an unknown role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a permission problem. Only once the role is recognised does the system ask whether that role is permitted on the addressed endpoint; if it is not, the answer is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ClinicAdmin`, `Physician`, `NurseInjector`, `InventoryManager`, `FrontDesk`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `Patient`.

A `ClinicAdmin` is the clinic's operations administrator. They onboard clinicians, publish protocols, drive the ops run, move the logical clock, and are the only role that can lift a financial hold. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any row's `id`.

A `Physician` is a licensed physician who authors standing orders, records consults, reviews adverse events, acknowledges critical laboratory values, voids procedures and voided injection visits, and may perform insertions on their own licence without any delegation at all. When `X-User-Role` is `Physician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and the general ownership check is exactly that `X-User-ID` resolves to a persisted `Provider` row and nothing more — it is never compared to the addressed row's `provider_id`, so a physician may open, perform, void or allocate against a procedure or visit booked under a different operator. Only two rules compare the acting identity to a specific foreign key: withdrawing or reinstating a standing order requires that `X-User-ID` equal that order's `physician_id`, and reviewing an adverse event compares `X-User-ID` against `reported_by_provider_id` and `reviewed_by_provider_id`.

A `NurseInjector` is a non-physician injector. Their identity works the same way: when `X-User-Role` is `NurseInjector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`. Every administration a `NurseInjector` performs is gated by a valid physician standing order, and nothing else in the API depends on that gate.

An `InventoryManager` owns the pellet supply chain — receiving lots, releasing them from quarantine, running the allocation planner and executing recalls. A `FrontDesk` operator runs the scheduling, membership, series and money desk; they can neither administer anything nor touch inventory. A `ComplianceOfficer` provides quality and safety oversight: they may execute recalls and read every clinical record, but may not schedule, administer, allocate or bill. For all three of these roles `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as the acknowledging identity on a record whose transition writes it) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

A `Patient` acts on their own records only. When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient` record, and ownership is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against the addressed row's `patient_id`. A `Patient` who addresses a row belonging to somebody else is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNERSHIP` (HTTP 403) — the same code a physician receives when acting on a standing order they did not author. Ownership is evaluated after existence, so a `Patient` asking about a record that does not exist hears the not-found code, not the ownership code. That comparison is made only where the request addresses one identified row — a row named by a path segment, or a `patient_id` carried in the request body. Collection list endpoints address no single row and are therefore deliberately **not** narrowed by the acting identity: a `Patient` listing procedures, consents, consults, lab panels, injection visits, memberships, series packs, adverse events, financing plans, protocols or notifications receives every matching row in the system and must supply a `patient_id` filter to narrow the result themselves.

Two provider-shaped requirements are worth stating up front because they cut across several workflows. A `Provider` carries a `provider_type` of either `PHYSICIAN` or `NURSE_INJECTOR`, and only a `PHYSICIAN` may be named as the author of a protocol publication, the physician on a consult, or the delegating physician on a standing order. A request naming a provider of the wrong licensure class in one of those positions is rejected as a request-shape violation with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The check reads the resolved `Provider` row, so despite that classification it is evaluated **after** rung 7 has resolved every foreign key and **before** rung 8's ownership sweep: a `physician_id` or `published_by_provider_id` naming no provider at all reports `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` first, and only an id that resolves to a `NURSE_INJECTOR` reaches `ERR_VALIDATION`. The positions class-checked are exactly `physician_id` and `published_by_provider_id`; a procedure's or an injection visit's `provider_id` is never class-checked, so a `NURSE_INJECTOR` may be booked as the operator on either.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time, so it repeats exactly until the clock is driven.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints **only**. A response whose `response_type` is `array` but which is not a paginated list — the HTTP 201 array of newly created allocation rows returned by the allocation run — carries a `meta` block holding `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` alone.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no smaller than 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative below says otherwise. Because the logical clock moves only when it is driven, identical `created_at` values are the norm rather than the exception, and every such tie is broken by **server write order** — the order in which the rows were inserted into the store — never by `id` and never by any domain field. That tie-break governs the two allocation listings as well, so allocation rows come back in creation order rather than in `allocation_rank` order; the two coincide for a plan as it was written, and diverge only for the sibling row a wastage split mints last, which lands at the end of the page despite carrying a middling rank. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before the window was applied, and `has_more` set true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Every list endpoint also accepts the filters named in its description; a filter that is supplied narrows the result set by exact match on the named field, and filters combine conjunctively. Filtering never changes the ordering rule. Filter values are compared as raw strings against the string rendering of the stored field, and are never checked for enum membership: a filter carrying a value that is not a declared member of the field's enum is accepted and simply matches nothing, answering HTTP 200 with an empty `data`, `total_count` 0 and `has_more` false rather than an error. A row whose filtered field is null matches no filter value at all, so there is no way to filter *for* null. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored outright rather than rejected.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock, Day Arithmetic and Datetime Rendering

Nothing in this system reads the operating system's wall clock. The server holds a **logical UTC clock** whose value at startup is exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. `POST /clinic/_clock/set_current_utc` moves that clock to the instant supplied in `to_iso8601` and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; only a `ClinicAdmin` may drive it. Every rule in this document that speaks of "now" reads the logical clock at the moment the request is handled.

Day arithmetic is defined once and used everywhere. The number of days between two instants is the difference between them expressed in seconds, divided by 86400, then rounded down to a whole number — whole twenty-four-hour periods only, never calendar-date subtraction. Every threshold in this document whose name ends in days is compared using that function. Hour arithmetic is different: the number of hours between two instants is their difference in seconds divided by 3600, kept as an exact real number and never floored. Every threshold expressed in hours is compared using that function.

Every datetime value in every response body is rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. This applies uniformly to values the server stamps itself (`created_at`, `updated_at`, `resulted_at`, `performed_at`, `signed_at`, `effective_at`, `purchased_at`, `period_start`, `administered_at`, `recalled_at`, `paused_at`, `acknowledged_at`) and to values echoed back from request input (`drawn_at`, `scheduled_start`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `expiry_date`, `onset_at`, `first_due_date`). Input may arrive with or without fractional digits; the server normalises to three digits on output.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Versioning and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp. Records additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation, and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — with one exception. The `Protocol` record is immutable once published: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and neither `updated_at` nor `version` appears on it at all.

The `version` field is not decorative. It is the observation token that the three stale-version guards in §2.7 compare against, and it is also the visible evidence that a cascade touched a row the caller did not address. When a request writes to more than one record, every record it writes gets its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed — the addressed row and every collaterally-affected row alike. The increment is once per row per **request**, never once per write: a row that a cascade touches on two separate hops — a lot moved in hop one of a recall and moved again in hop two, a patient whose open-notification counter and whose `clinical_hold` are both written, a procedure that crosses its recall axis in hop three-a and is then notified in hop four — ends the request with its `version` exactly 1 higher than it started, while its `updated_at` is refreshed by each of those touches. The response body of a mutating request carries the state of the addressed resource **after** the commit, which means the `version` in that body is the post-increment value, not the value the row held when the request arrived.

Paths that nest one resource inside another are resolved left to right. A request addressing the allocations held by a procedure resolves the procedure first: if the procedure id does not exist, the answer is `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_FOUND` and the nested segment is never considered. The same left-to-right discipline governs foreign keys inside a request body, and §2.9 rung 7 fixes the exact order in which the body's keys are resolved so that a payload with two bad references always reports the same one.

### 2.6. Money, Milligrams and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is integer cents. There are no fractional-cent balances anywhere, and no floating-point money is ever persisted. Where a rule invokes rounding, it is always **round half up** applied to a non-negative magnitude: a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent.

Rounding *order* is load-bearing and is pinned individually by every calculation that uses it. Three orderings recur and must not be interchanged. When a charge is composed of several priced lines, each line is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded lines are then summed; summing raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong. When a rate is multiplied by a count, the unrounded rate is multiplied first and the product is rounded once; rounding the rate first and then multiplying is wrong. When a discount applies, the discount amount is rounded and then subtracted from the exact subtotal, rather than rounding the discounted result. Each calculation in §3 restates which of these applies to it.

Milligrams are exact integers throughout. Every rung of both pellet ladders is a whole number of milligrams, so planned and delivered milligram totals are exact integer sums with no rounding step at all. Pellet counts are whole pellets; there is no partial pellet anywhere in the model.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on the Three Committing Writes

Three writes in this API commit facts that cannot be reconstructed from a later read, and each is guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against a parent record's `version`. In each case the named field is a **required, non-optional** input on that request, it must carry the client's last observed `version` of the named parent, and any mismatch with the parent's current `version` is rejected. Endpoints not listed here take no version field at all.

`POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform` requires `expected_patient_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the procedure's `Patient`. Performing an insertion is the moment the clinic commits milligrams to a body, and every gate it depends on lives on the patient row — `clinical_hold`, `financial_hold`, `cumulative_mg_testosterone`, `cumulative_mg_estradiol`, `last_insertion_at`. Any of those may have moved under a legitimate write the client has not yet seen: a severe adverse event filed against a sibling procedure, a financing default, or an earlier insertion that already consumed the rolling milligram allowance. If `expected_patient_version` does not equal the patient's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

`POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/allocations` requires `expected_procedure_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `Procedure`. A fresh allocation request legitimately supersedes whatever allocation set the procedure already holds — the prior reserved rows are released and a new plan is minted — so the operation is never refused by rung 10 on origin-state grounds, and the stale-version check is the only thing standing between the caller and silently discarding an allocation set that was produced from a newer plan. Rung 11 does still apply: an allocation run against a procedure already in `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and that check precedes the version comparison. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROCEDURE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

`POST /clinic/memberships/{id}/tier-change` requires `expected_membership_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `Membership`. A tier change legitimately supersedes the prior tier, restarts the period and resets the included-visit counter, so it would otherwise succeed from any active state; the stale-version check is what prevents a change computed against a pre-pause fee basis from overwriting a period that a pause or an included-visit redemption has since moved. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBERSHIP_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. The Delegated-Authority Write Gate

The clinic's regulatory model is delegation: a physician's standing order lets a nurse administer therapy the nurse could not authorise alone, inside a scope the physician chose. That delegation is enforced as a single global write gate, and it is deliberately narrow.

The gate fires **only** when `X-User-Role` is `NurseInjector`, and **only** on three endpoints — `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/open-supply`, `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`, and `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. On those three requests, the `StandingOrder` referenced by the target row must satisfy every one of four conditions: its `status` must be `ACTIVE` (both `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED` fail); the logical clock must be at or after `valid_from` and at or before `valid_until`, with both bounds inclusive; the target's therapy type must be in scope, meaning the procedure's `hormone_type` is a member of `scope_hormone_types` or the visit's `injection_type` is a member of `scope_injection_types`; and, for a procedure, `planned_total_mg` must not exceed the order's `max_dose_mg`. Failure of any of the four is `ERR_STANDING_ORDER_INVALID` (HTTP 403).

The gate sits above state, above concurrency and above every business rule. A `NurseInjector` acting under a lapsed order therefore sees `ERR_STANDING_ORDER_INVALID` even when the target row is also in the wrong state, also stale, and also short of inventory. Four exclusions keep the rest of the ladder reachable and are as binding as the gate itself. First, the same three endpoints performed by a `Physician` are outside the gate entirely — a physician acts on their own licence, so no delegation is needed. Second, scheduling a procedure is outside the gate for every role, because scheduling administers nothing. Third, running an allocation plan is outside the gate, because allocation is an inventory movement. Fourth, recording lab panels, recording consults, and every cancel or void endpoint are outside the gate, because they are clinical data entry and reversals rather than administration.

### 2.9. The Validation Priority Ladder

When a request could fail several checks at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed by a global thirty-nine-rung ladder. Lower rung numbers pre-empt higher ones. The full order is:

1. **Rung 1** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Rung 2** — `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Rung 3** — the acting role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorised-role list, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
4. **Rung 4** — every required input field for the endpoint is present, of the declared type, and within its declared numeric bounds, on every endpoint. This rung also covers `limit` being an integer from 1 to 100 and `offset` being an integer of at least 0; `prescribed_dose_mg` being greater than 0; `symptom_score` being an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive; `qty_received` being greater than 0; `principal_cents` being greater than 0; `installment_count` being an integer; and `valid_until` being strictly after `valid_from` and no more than 365 days after it. A request body that is absent, empty, or not a JSON object at all is treated as an empty object, so its required fields simply report missing here. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
5. **Rung 5** — every enum-valued input field carries a declared member. The fields routed here are exhaustively: `sex_at_birth` on a patient; `provider_type` on a provider; `hormone_type` on a protocol, on a consent, on a consult and on a pellet lot; `analyte` on a lab panel; the members of `scope_hormone_types` and of `scope_injection_types` on a standing order; `procedure_type` on a procedure; `injection_type` on an injection visit; `tier` on a membership; `series_type` on a series pack; `severity` on an adverse event, where the accepted input members are `MILD`, `MODERATE` and `SEVERE` only, because `SURVEILLANCE` is system-generated and is rejected here; `severity_class` on the lot-recall request; `hold_kind` and `hold_action` on the eligibility-hold request; `disposition` on the adverse-event review; and `variant` and `assume_severity` on the recall-impact read. No other field routes here — an out-of-range integer or a malformed datetime is `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 4, never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`; a list endpoint's filter parameters are never enum-checked at all (§2.3); and the `hormone_type` of the therapy-status read is deliberately outside this list, so an unrecognised hormone type on that one read answers `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 4 rather than `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, unlike the identically named field on a protocol, a consent, a consult or a pellet lot. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400).
6. **Rung 6** — every path-segment resource id resolves, validated left to right, on every endpoint that has a path parameter. The code is the per-entity one: `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSULT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDING_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INJECTION_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERIES_PACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINANCING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404.
7. **Rung 7** — every non-null foreign key in the request body resolves, on every endpoint that accepts foreign keys, checked in this fixed order: `patient_id` first; then the provider-shaped keys `provider_id`, `physician_id`, `published_by_provider_id`, `reported_by_provider_id` and `ordered_by_provider_id`, in that order; then `consult_id`; then `standing_order_id`; then `consent_record_id`; then `lab_panel_id`; then `lot_id`; then `procedure_id`; then `injection_visit_id`; then `series_pack_id`; then `membership_id`. The codes are the same per-entity not-found set as rung 6, at HTTP 404.
8. **Rung 8** — for the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` owns the addressed row. A `Patient` may only read or act on rows whose `patient_id` equals `X-User-ID`, on the endpoints that address one identified row — collection list endpoints are not narrowed by the acting patient (§2.1); a `Physician` or `NurseInjector` must supply an `X-User-ID` that resolves to a `Provider` row, and nothing further is compared; and for standing-order revoke and reinstate the acting `X-User-ID` must additionally equal that order's `physician_id`. Applies to every endpoint whose acting role is `Patient`, `Physician` or `NurseInjector`. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNERSHIP` (403).
9. **Rung 9** — the delegated-authority write gate of §2.8, on `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/open-supply`, `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`, and only when `X-User-Role` is `NurseInjector`. Otherwise `ERR_STANDING_ORDER_INVALID` (403).
10. **Rung 10** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared on the relevant axis, on every action endpoint. Rungs 10 and 11 read the same row on the same axis and are therefore evaluated as one gate, in which rung 11 is tested **first** despite its higher number: a row already sitting in a terminal state hears `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, even though that terminal state is equally an undeclared origin for the requested transition, and `ERR_INVALID_STATE` is left to describe a non-terminal state from which the transition is not declared. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
11. **Rung 11** — the target row is not already in a terminal state for the axis being moved, on every action endpoint. The terminal sets are: a procedure's `status` in `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED`; a lot's `sterility_status` at `RECALLED`; a standing order's `status` at `REVOKED`; a series pack's `status` in `EXPIRED` or `REFUNDED`; an injection visit's `status` in `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED`; a membership's `status` at `CANCELLED`; a financing plan's `status` in `DEFAULTED`, `PAID_OFF` or `CANCELLED`; an adverse event's `status` at `CLOSED`; and a notification's `status` at `ACKNOWLEDGED`. That list is exhaustive for this rung. Axes absent from it — the lab-panel status axis, the consent status axis, the allocation status axis and the procedure's `recall_status` axis — carry no terminal set here, so an undeclared origin on one of those answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 10 even where §3 calls the state terminal in the narrative sense. Otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409).
12. **Rung 12** — the supplied `expected_patient_version`, `expected_procedure_version` or `expected_membership_version` equals its parent row's current `version`, on `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`, `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/allocations` and `POST /clinic/memberships/{id}/tier-change` respectively. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_PROCEDURE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_MEMBERSHIP_VERSION` (412).
13. **Rung 13** — segregation of duties on adverse-event review, on `POST /clinic/adverse-events/{id}/review`: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_provider_id`, and when the event is already `ESCALATED` it must additionally differ from the `reviewed_by_provider_id` recorded by the escalating review. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
14. **Rung 14** — `clinical_hold` is false on the patient, on `POST /clinic/procedures`, `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. Otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_CLINICAL_HOLD` (409).
15. **Rung 15** — the patient holds no `LabPanel` in status `CRITICAL_FLAGGED`, on `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. Acknowledging the panel clears the block; recording a newer panel does not, because critically flagged panels are never superseded. Otherwise `ERR_CRITICAL_LAB_UNACKNOWLEDGED` (409).
16. **Rung 16** — the consent attached to the procedure exists, has status `ACTIVE`, belongs to the same patient, covers the same `hormone_type`, and its `protocol_version` equals the highest published protocol version for that hormone type; at perform time the procedure's `consent_record_id` must additionally be non-null and still `ACTIVE`. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/consent-attach`, `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/open-supply` and `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`. Otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (409).
17. **Rung 17** — the consent's `consented_max_dose_mg` is greater than or equal to the procedure's `planned_total_mg`, on `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/consent-attach`. Otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_DOSE_EXCEEDED` (422).
18. **Rung 18** — the patient's most recent non-superseded `HEMATOCRIT` panel has a `value` no greater than 52.0 percent, on `POST /clinic/procedures` and `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`. A patient with no hematocrit panel at all passes this rung. Otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (422).
19. **Rung 19** — `planned_total_mg` is no greater than 2000 mg for a testosterone procedure or 150 mg for an estradiol procedure, on `POST /clinic/procedures`. Equality passes. Otherwise `ERR_DOSE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
20. **Rung 20** — the rolling 365-day exposure plus the milligrams this request would commit is no greater than 4800 mg for testosterone or 400 mg for estradiol, on `POST /clinic/procedures` and `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`. Equality passes in both cases. Otherwise `ERR_ROLLING_MG_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Rung 21** — re-dose factor three, laboratory freshness: no more than 45 days separate the supporting panel's `drawn_at` from now. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures` when `procedure_type` is `REDOSE`. A panel exactly 45 days old still supports the decision; 46 days fails and forces a re-draw. Otherwise `ERR_LAB_STALE` (422).
22. **Rung 22** — re-dose factor two, the trough window: the supporting panel's `is_trough_draw` is true and the days between the anchoring insertion and the panel's `drawn_at` fall inside the hormone's trough window with both bounds inclusive — 70 through 98 days for testosterone, 56 through 84 days for estradiol. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures` when `procedure_type` is `REDOSE`. Otherwise `ERR_TROUGH_WINDOW_VIOLATION` (422).
23. **Rung 23** — re-dose factor one, the minimum interval: at least 84 days for testosterone, or at least 70 days for estradiol, separate the anchoring insertion from the requested `scheduled_start`. Equality passes. A patient with no completed procedure of that hormone at all also fails here. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures` when `procedure_type` is `REDOSE`. Otherwise `ERR_REDOSE_INTERVAL_NOT_MET` (422).
24. **Rung 24** — re-dose factor five, the analyte band: the referenced panel's `analyte` is the hormone's primary analyte and its `clinic_optimal_band` is `BELOW_OPTIMAL`. A laboratory-normal but not-below-optimal value fails here even though its `lab_reference_band` reads `IN_REF`. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures` when `procedure_type` is `REDOSE`. Otherwise `ERR_ANALYTE_NOT_BELOW_OPTIMAL` (422).
25. **Rung 25** — re-dose factor four, symptom burden: the consult's `symptom_score` is at least 40. Equality passes. Applies to `POST /clinic/procedures` when `procedure_type` is `REDOSE`. Otherwise `ERR_SYMPTOM_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (422).
26. **Rung 26** — the post-insertion restriction, on `POST /clinic/procedures`: reject when `scheduled_start` falls strictly earlier than the patient's `last_insertion_at` plus 14 days. The comparison is a strict less-than, so a slot exactly 14 days after the last insertion is allowed. This is a point-anchored forward exclusion over any `hormone_type`, distinct from the same-hormone interval at rung 23. Otherwise `ERR_POST_INSERTION_RESTRICTION` (409).
27. **Rung 27** — provider double-booking with a symmetric 20-minute buffer, on `POST /clinic/procedures` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits`. Otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409).
28. **Rung 28** — room double-booking with no buffer and half-open intervals, on `POST /clinic/procedures` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits`. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_CONFLICT` (409).
29. **Rung 29** — a lot's `strength_mg` is a member of the pellet ladder for its `hormone_type`: 200 mg or 100 mg for testosterone, 25 mg or 10 mg for estradiol. Applies to `POST /clinic/pellet-lots`. Otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
30. **Rung 30** — the allocation walk satisfies both strength legs in full, on `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/allocations`. An empty candidate set and a short walk both land here, and nothing is mutated. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PELLET_INVENTORY` (409).
31. **Rung 31** — the series pack's `patient_id` equals the injection visit's `patient_id`, on `POST /clinic/injection-visits` and `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. Packs are never transferable between patients. Otherwise `ERR_SERIES_NOT_TRANSFERABLE` (403).
32. **Rung 32** — now is no later than the pack's `expires_at`, on `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. Otherwise `ERR_SERIES_EXPIRED` (409), with the forfeiture behaviour described in §2.11.
33. **Rung 33** — the pack's `remaining_visits` is at least 1, on `POST /clinic/injection-visits/{id}/administer`. Otherwise `ERR_SERIES_EXHAUSTED` (409).
34. **Rung 34** — the patient holds no other membership in status `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, on `POST /clinic/memberships`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_MEMBERSHIP` (409).
35. **Rung 35** — `pause_days_used` plus the days elapsed since `paused_at` is no greater than 90, on `POST /clinic/memberships/{id}/resume`. Equality passes, and the check runs before any mutation so a rejected resume leaves the membership completely untouched. Otherwise `ERR_PAUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
36. **Rung 36** — `down_payment_cents` is at least 20 percent of `principal_cents` rounded up to the next whole cent, and is strictly less than `principal_cents`, on `POST /clinic/financing-plans`. Otherwise `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_TOO_LOW` (422).
37. **Rung 37** — `installment_count` is between 2 and 12 inclusive, on `POST /clinic/financing-plans`. Otherwise `ERR_INSTALLMENT_COUNT_INVALID` (422).
38. **Rung 38** — the payment's `amount_cents` exactly equals the addressed schedule entry's `amount_cents`, and that entry's status is `PENDING` or `MISSED`; partial payments and over-payments are both rejected. Applies to `POST /clinic/financing-plans/{id}/payments`. Otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422).
39. **Rung 39** — `financial_hold` is false on the patient, on `POST /clinic/procedures` and `POST /clinic/procedures/{id}/perform`. This sits at the very bottom of the ladder deliberately, so that a patient carrying both holds always hears `ERR_PATIENT_CLINICAL_HOLD` from rung 14 rather than the financial complaint. Otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD` (402).

Two ordering subtleties inside this ladder are consequential enough to restate. First, the five re-dose factors are evaluated in the fixed order 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 — freshness, then trough window, then interval, then band, then symptom score — so a request failing several of them hears exactly the lowest-numbered one. Laboratory freshness is checked before the trough window even though both read the same panel, because a stale panel is a data-quality problem while a mistimed panel is a clinical one, and the clinic reports the data-quality problem first. Second, when the patient has no completed procedure of the relevant hormone at all, there is no anchoring insertion for rungs 21 and 22 to measure against; in that case rungs 21 and 22 are skipped and the request lands on rung 23, which is what makes rung 23's no-prior-insertion condition reachable.

### 2.10. Three Conservation Identities

Three quantities in this system are conserved, and each identity must hold after every single request that could disturb it — not merely at the end of a workflow.

**Pellets against a lot.** For every `PelletLot`, `qty_received` always equals the sum of `qty_on_hand`, `qty_reserved`, `qty_consumed`, `qty_wasted` and `qty_returned`. `qty_received` is fixed at creation and never changes; the other five buckets move against each other. This identity must hold after lot creation, lot release, an allocation run, an allocation release triggered by a procedure cancellation, opening a sterile tray, performing an insertion (which splits pellets into inserted and wasted), voiding a procedure, and executing a recall. Note carefully that `qty_reserved` covers allocation rows in status `RESERVED` **and** in status `OPENED` alike: opening the tray changes the allocation's status only, and never moves a lot bucket. The compensating pair is exact — running an allocation moves pellets from `qty_on_hand` to `qty_reserved`, and cancelling that procedure while it is still `CONSENTED` moves them straight back, leaving all five buckets at their pre-allocation values.

**Entitlements in a series pool.** For every `SeriesPack`, `purchased_visits` always equals the sum of `remaining_visits`, `redeemed_visits`, `refunded_visits` and `forfeited_visits`. This holds after pack purchase, after an injection is administered against the pack, after such an injection is voided, after a pack refund, and after an expiry forfeiture. The compensating pair here is administering against the pack (moving one entitlement from remaining to redeemed) and voiding that visit (moving it back); the round trip restores `remaining_visits` exactly and leaves `refunded_visits` and `forfeited_visits` untouched.

**Cents of financed principal.** For every `FinancingPlan`, two legs hold simultaneously. The first: `principal_cents` equals `down_payment_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` across every entry of the `schedule`. The second: that same schedule sum equals `paid_installment_cents` plus `outstanding_cents` plus `waived_cents`. Both legs hold after plan creation, after activation, after each installment payment, after each write-off, after the delinquency sweep, and after the plan is cancelled by a procedure cancellation. `late_fee_cents` and `refunded_cents` sit deliberately **outside** both legs: a late fee is new money owed rather than a redistribution of principal, and a refund is money returned to the patient that never re-enters the schedule.

### 2.11. Cascades, Collateral Writes and Mutate-Then-Reject

Several requests in this system fan out well beyond the record they address. A single lot recall reaches five hops deep — through allocations, then procedures on two different axes, then patient notifications, then safety surveillance events. A single severe adverse event reaches four hops — through consents, then a standing order, then every procedure booked under that order regardless of which patient it belongs to, then those procedures' reserved pellets. A single protocol publication reaches four hops — through consents, then consented procedures, then re-consent notifications, then the patients' open-notification counters. Every hop of every cascade is a real write: the affected rows have their `version` incremented and their `updated_at` refreshed exactly as if they had been addressed directly, and each is independently readable afterwards through the corresponding list endpoint.

Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggered them. There is no deferred queue and no eventual consistency: by the time the response is written, every hop has committed. One consequence is deliberate and must be reproduced rather than smoothed over — a procedure's recall exposure passes through the intermediate value `RECALL_AFFECTED` and out the other side to `NOTIFIED` inside the same recall request, so `RECALL_AFFECTED` is never observable at rest.

Finally, one rule commits a write and then fails the request anyway. When an administration is attempted against a series pack whose validity has expired while the pack still reads `ACTIVE`, the forfeiture bookkeeping commits first — the remaining entitlements move into `forfeited_visits`, `remaining_visits` drops to zero and the pack's `version` increments — and only then is the request rejected with `ERR_SERIES_EXPIRED`. A second identical attempt finds the pack already in status `EXPIRED`, is rejected by the same rung with the same error, and forfeits nothing further. This is the one place in the system where a 4xx response leaves persistent state changed, and it is intentional.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Patient Record, the Two Holds and the Eligibility Projection

Registration is the clinic's lightest operation and its most consequential one, because everything else hangs off the row it creates. A `ClinicAdmin` or a `FrontDesk` operator supplies the patient's `full_name`, their `date_of_birth` as an ISO calendar date in year-month-day form — carried, stored and echoed back as an opaque non-empty string, never parsed as an instant, never normalised and never format-checked — an `email` that must be unique across all patients, with a duplicate rejected as a request-shape violation carrying `ERR_VALIDATION` (400), and a `sex_at_birth` of either `MALE` or `FEMALE`. The server initialises the rest: `clinical_hold` and `financial_hold` both start false, `eligibility_status` starts at `CLEAR`, `cumulative_mg_testosterone` and `cumulative_mg_estradiol` both start at 0, `last_insertion_at` and `last_insertion_hormone_type` both start null, and `open_notification_count` starts at 0.

Two independent booleans decide whether the clinic will treat a patient. `clinical_hold` is true while a severe safety concern is unresolved; `financial_hold` is true while the patient has at least one financing plan that has defaulted. Both are **persisted**, not re-derived on read. What *is* derived, on every request that writes either boolean, is `eligibility_status`, a compound projection over the pair: `DUAL_HOLD` when both booleans are true, `CLINICAL_HOLD` when only the clinical one is true, `FINANCIAL_HOLD` when only the financial one is true, and `CLEAR` when neither is. There is no endpoint that writes `eligibility_status` directly.

The two holds are set and cleared by different actors and have different reach. `clinical_hold` is set true by three things: filing an adverse event with severity `SEVERE`, the surveillance hop of a class-one lot recall, and an explicit eligibility-hold action carrying `hold_kind` of `CLINICAL` with `hold_action` of `SET`. It is set false by two things: a review that leaves the patient with no adverse event whose `severity` is `SEVERE` or `SURVEILLANCE` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, and an explicit eligibility-hold action carrying `hold_kind` `CLINICAL` with `hold_action` `CLEAR`. `financial_hold` is set true by the delinquency default cascade or by an eligibility-hold action carrying `hold_kind` `FINANCIAL` with `hold_action` `SET`; it is set false **only** by an eligibility-hold action carrying `hold_kind` `FINANCIAL` with `hold_action` `CLEAR`, because a defaulted plan never self-cures. Every eligibility-hold action may carry an optional `reason` for the audit trail, recomputes `eligibility_status` and increments the patient's `version` by 1. A `Physician` may drive the clinical hold; only a `ClinicAdmin` may drive the financial one, and a `Physician` attempting an action with `hold_kind` `FINANCIAL` is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. That split is an extension of rung 3 rather than a rule of its own: it reads the raw `hold_kind` value straight off the request body the instant the endpoint's role list has been satisfied, and therefore **before** rungs 4, 5 and 6 run. A `Physician` sending `hold_kind` `FINANCIAL` consequently hears `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` even when `hold_action` is missing, when `hold_action` carries an undeclared member, or when the patient in the path does not exist at all.

Clearing the financial hold has a reach the clinical clear does not: every procedure of that patient sitting in `SUPPLY_HOLD` whose `hold_reason` reads `PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD` returns to `SCHEDULED`, with `hold_reason` cleared to null and `consent_record_id` cleared to null so the procedure must be re-consented and re-allocated before it can proceed. This is the only route by which a financially held procedure recovers, and it is what makes the `SUPPLY_HOLD → SCHEDULED` leg reachable at all. The release is keyed on the clear action alone rather than on the resulting eligibility state, so it fires identically on the `FINANCIAL_HOLD → CLEAR` leg and on the `DUAL_HOLD → CLINICAL_HOLD` leg, freeing the financially frozen procedures even while the patient's `clinical_hold` remains true and the patient therefore still cannot be treated. A `FINANCIAL`/`CLEAR` action against a patient who was carrying no financial hold at all is likewise accepted: it releases nothing, changes no boolean, and still recomputes `eligibility_status` and increments the patient's `version`.

The eligibility axis therefore moves through a small compound state machine driven entirely by side effects. `CLEAR → CLINICAL_HOLD` fires whenever `clinical_hold` flips true while `financial_hold` is false, and its effect is that scheduling, performing and injection administration are all blocked at rung 14. `CLEAR → FINANCIAL_HOLD` fires on the delinquency default cascade when `financial_hold` flips true while `clinical_hold` is false, and its effect is narrower: scheduling and performing a procedure are blocked at rung 39 with HTTP 402, while injection administration is deliberately **not** blocked, because a financial hold must never withhold care the patient has already paid for through a series or a membership. `CLINICAL_HOLD → DUAL_HOLD` fires when the delinquency cascade reaches an already clinically held patient, and `FINANCIAL_HOLD → DUAL_HOLD` fires when a severe adverse event reaches an already financially held one; in both directions the ladder decides what the patient hears, and the clinical complaint at rung 14 always pre-empts the financial one at rung 39. Coming back down, `DUAL_HOLD → FINANCIAL_HOLD` fires when the last non-closed adverse event for that patient is closed; `CLINICAL_HOLD → CLEAR` fires either from that same closure or from an explicit clinical clear; `FINANCIAL_HOLD → CLEAR` and `DUAL_HOLD → CLINICAL_HOLD` both fire from a financial clear. Every one of these legs increments the patient's `version` by 1.

Clinicians are registered separately and only by a `ClinicAdmin`, who supplies the provider's `full_name`, a `provider_type` of `PHYSICIAN` or `NURSE_INJECTOR`, an `npi` that must be unique across providers — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) — and an optional `active` flag that defaults to true. `active` is persisted and echoed on the provider projection, but no endpoint in this API ever consults it: booking a procedure or an injection visit against a provider whose `active` is false succeeds exactly as it would against an active one, and there is no endpoint that changes the flag after registration.

### 3.2. Protocols, Versioned Consent, and the De-Consent Cascade

The clinic's clinical protocols are versioned per hormone, and consent is pinned to a version. A `Physician` or a `ClinicAdmin` publishes a protocol by supplying its `hormone_type` — `TESTOSTERONE` or `ESTRADIOL` — a human-readable `title`, and the `published_by_provider_id` of the authoring physician, who must be a provider whose `provider_type` is `PHYSICIAN`. The server assigns `protocol_version` as one more than the highest version already published for that same hormone type, so the first testosterone protocol is version 1, the second is version 2, and estradiol numbers independently. `effective_at` is stamped from the logical clock at publication. A protocol is an immutable historical record: it carries `id` and `created_at` but neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Publication is not a quiet act. It fires a four-hop cascade whose purpose is to make sure nobody is treated under a protocol they never agreed to. Hop one is the publication itself. Hop two supersedes consent: every `ConsentRecord` for that hormone type whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, incrementing its `version` — and if hop two supersedes nothing, because no `ACTIVE` consent for that hormone exists, hops three and four are skipped outright and the publication is a lone write with no collateral effect whatever. Hop three reaches the schedule: every procedure sitting in `CONSENTED` that holds one of the consents just superseded moves back `CONSENTED → SCHEDULED`, with its `consent_record_id` set to null and its `version` incremented — this is a cyclic leg, and a procedure may be consented, de-consented by a protocol bump, and consented again under the new version without limit. Hop four notifies: for each such procedure the server mints a `PatientNotification` with `notification_type` of `CONSENT_REQUIRED`, `status` `PENDING`, a `severity_class` of null because no recall is involved, a human-readable `message`, and a `due_at` seven days after the moment of creation; the patient's `open_notification_count` rises by 1 and their `version` increments.

Signing a consent is available to `FrontDesk`, `Physician` and `Patient`. The signer supplies `patient_id`, `hormone_type`, the `protocol_version` under which the patient is consenting, and `consented_max_dose_mg`, the largest delivered total the patient agrees to receive in a single procedure. The server stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock and sets `status` to `ACTIVE`. `protocol_version` is taken at face value at signing time: no check is made that it names a protocol that has actually been published, or that it is the current one, so a consent signed under a stale or wholly fictitious version is created happily and simply fails rung 16's currency test later when somebody tries to attach it. Signing supersedes: any prior consent for the same `patient_id` and the same `hormone_type` still in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented. A consent has exactly two states, `ACTIVE` and `SUPERSEDED`, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — there is no revoked value. Three distinct events supersede a consent: a newer consent for the same patient and hormone, the protocol-publication cascade described above, and the filing of a severe adverse event, which supersedes every `ACTIVE` consent belonging to the affected patient regardless of hormone type.

Attaching a consent to a booked procedure is what moves it `SCHEDULED → CONSENTED`, and is available to `FrontDesk`, `Physician` and `Patient`. The request names the `consent_record_id` to attach. Five conditions must all hold: the procedure is in `SCHEDULED`; the referenced consent's `status` is `ACTIVE`; its `patient_id` matches the procedure's patient; its `hormone_type` matches the procedure's; and its `protocol_version` equals the highest protocol version published for that hormone type, failing which the answer is `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. Separately, the consent's `consented_max_dose_mg` must be at least the procedure's `planned_total_mg`; a consent that covers too little dose is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_DOSE_EXCEEDED`. On success the procedure's `consent_record_id` is set and its `version` increments by 1. Consent validity is re-examined twice more downstream — once when the sterile tray is opened and once at the moment of insertion — and at those later checkpoints the procedure's `consent_record_id` must be non-null and the referenced consent must still read `ACTIVE`.

### 3.3. Consults, the Pellet Ladders, and the Laboratory's Two Rulers

Only a `Physician` may record a consult. The consult carries `patient_id`, the `physician_id` of the consulting physician (who must be a provider whose `provider_type` is `PHYSICIAN`), the `hormone_type` being prescribed, a `prescribed_dose_mg` greater than zero, a `symptom_score` that is an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive, and a free-text `indication`. Recording the consult immediately converts the prescribed dose into a discrete pellet plan, because pellets do not come in arbitrary sizes.

Each hormone has a two-rung ladder of pellet strengths, and the conversion is greedy on the larger rung first. For testosterone the ladder is 200 mg for the large strength and 100 mg for the small; for estradiol it is 25 mg large and 10 mg small. The server divides the prescribed dose by the large strength and rounds that quotient **down** to a whole number, giving `planned_large_count`. It then subtracts the milligrams those large pellets supply from the prescribed dose, leaving a remainder. If the remainder is exactly zero, `planned_small_count` is zero; otherwise `planned_small_count` is the remainder divided by the small strength and rounded **up** to a whole number. `planned_pellet_count` is the two counts added together, and `planned_total_mg` is the large count multiplied by the large strength plus the small count multiplied by the small strength. Because the remainder rounds up, the planned total is always greater than or equal to the prescribed dose and the plan never under-delivers. A testosterone prescription of 1150 mg therefore resolves to five 200 mg pellets covering 1000 mg, a remainder of 150 mg, two 100 mg pellets, seven pellets in all and a `planned_total_mg` of 1200. An estradiol prescription of 80 mg resolves to three 25 mg pellets covering 75 mg, a remainder of 5 mg, one 10 mg pellet, four pellets and a planned total of 85. An estradiol prescription of exactly 100 mg resolves to four 25 mg pellets, a remainder of zero, no small pellets at all, four pellets and a planned total of 100. Counts are whole integers and the planned total is exact integer milligrams, because every ladder rung is a whole number. This conversion never clamps to any cap; the per-procedure ceiling is a separate gate applied when the procedure is booked, and it rejects the booking outright rather than trimming the plan.

Laboratory results are recorded by a `Physician` or a `NurseInjector`, who supply the `patient_id`, the `ordered_by_provider_id` of whoever ordered the draw, an `analyte` of `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE`, `ESTRADIOL` or `HEMATOCRIT`, the measured `value`, the instant `drawn_at` at which the specimen was taken, and an optional `is_trough_draw` flag that defaults to false. The server derives the `unit` from the analyte — `ng/dL` for `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE`, `pg/mL` for `ESTRADIOL`, `percent` for `HEMATOCRIT` — and stamps `resulted_at` from the logical clock. The `acknowledged_by_provider_id` stays null until a critical value is acknowledged.

The clinically interesting part is that one number is read against two rulers, and both readings are computed and persisted on the same row. The first ruler is the standard laboratory reference interval, recorded in `lab_reference_band`: the value is `BELOW_REF` when it falls strictly below the analyte's reference low, `ABOVE_REF` when it rises strictly above the analyte's reference high, and `IN_REF` in every other case, which includes sitting exactly on either bound. The reference lows and highs are 264 and 916 ng/dL for `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE`, 30 and 400 pg/mL for `ESTRADIOL`, and 38.0 and 50.0 percent for `HEMATOCRIT`. The second ruler is the clinic's own, narrower therapeutic target, recorded in `clinic_optimal_band`: the value is `BELOW_OPTIMAL` when it falls strictly below the optimal low, `ABOVE_OPTIMAL` when it rises strictly above the optimal high, and `IN_OPTIMAL` in every other case including the bounds themselves. Those optimal lows and highs are 600 and 900 ng/dL for `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE`, 80 and 200 pg/mL for `ESTRADIOL`, and 40.0 and 46.0 percent for `HEMATOCRIT`. Comparisons run on the raw numeric value with no rounding.

The consequence is that the two rulers routinely disagree, and each one drives a different subsystem. A total testosterone of 400 ng/dL is `IN_REF` — the laboratory would call that patient normal — yet it is `BELOW_OPTIMAL`, which is precisely the condition the re-dose gate is looking for. Run the same measurement up to 1000 ng/dL and it becomes `ABOVE_REF` and `ABOVE_OPTIMAL` at once, which blocks a re-dose without triggering any safety diversion at all.

The safety diversion is a third threshold, higher than both rulers. `critical_flag` is set true when the value rises **strictly above** the analyte's critical ceiling — 1500 ng/dL for `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE`, 500 pg/mL for `ESTRADIOL`, 54.0 percent for `HEMATOCRIT` — and false otherwise. A panel's `status` starts at `RECORDED`, and a panel whose `critical_flag` is true is diverted `RECORDED → CRITICAL_FLAGGED` within the very request that creates it, so it is never observable at rest in `RECORDED`. That divert is the branch point of the panel lifecycle. Merely exceeding the laboratory reference interval, or merely exceeding the clinic's optimal band, leaves the panel in `RECORDED`; only the critical ceiling diverts it. While any panel of that patient sits in `CRITICAL_FLAGGED`, both insertion and injection administration are refused with `ERR_CRITICAL_LAB_UNACKNOWLEDGED`.

Only a `Physician` can clear that block, by acknowledging the critical value with an optional `note`. Acknowledgement moves the panel `CRITICAL_FLAGGED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, writes the acting physician's `X-User-ID` verbatim into `acknowledged_by_provider_id`, and increments the panel's `version` by 1. `CRITICAL_FLAGGED` is the only origin the acknowledgement declares: a panel already in `RECORDED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `SUPERSEDED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), because the panel axis is absent from rung 11's terminal set and so never produces `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` even though `SUPERSEDED` is terminal in the narrative sense. Recording a newer panel does **not** clear it: when a new panel is written for the same `patient_id` and the same `analyte`, any prior panel of that patient and analyte moves `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented, but a `CRITICAL_FLAGGED` panel is deliberately left alone. A critical value must be seen and acknowledged by a physician, never silently displaced by a fresher draw. `SUPERSEDED` is the terminal state of the panel axis.

One further reading of the hematocrit ruler operates independently of all of the above. Before a procedure may be booked, and again before it may be performed, the patient's most recent non-superseded `HEMATOCRIT` panel must read no higher than 52.0 percent; a higher value is rejected with `ERR_SAFETY_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. "Most recent" here means latest by the panel row's own `created_at` — the order the results were entered, with identical `created_at` values broken by write order — and explicitly **not** by `drawn_at`, so a specimen drawn earlier but recorded later is the one that governs. Every non-superseded panel counts, whatever its status: `RECORDED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `CRITICAL_FLAGGED` panels are all candidates and only `SUPERSEDED` ones are skipped. Note that this safety ceiling of 52.0 sits between the reference high of 50.0 and the critical ceiling of 54.0, so a hematocrit of 53.0 blocks treatment without ever flagging as critical. A patient with no hematocrit panel on file at all passes the ceiling check.

### 3.4. Booking an Insertion: Dose Ceilings, the Re-Dose Gate and Occupancy

Booking a pellet insertion is available to `FrontDesk` and `Physician`, and it is the single most heavily gated write in the system. The booking supplies `patient_id`, the `provider_id` of the operator who will perform the insertion, the `consult_id` whose plan the procedure will carry out, the `standing_order_id` of the order that would govern a nurse-performed insertion, a `procedure_type` of `INITIAL` or `REDOSE`, a `room_code` naming the treatment room, and the `scheduled_start` instant of the slot. Two optional references may accompany it: a `lab_panel_id` naming the trough panel that supports a re-dose, which is null for an `INITIAL` procedure, and a `membership_id` naming the membership whose discount should apply to the eventual charge, which is null for non-members. Each of these references is checked for existence at rung 7 and for nothing else: no rule verifies that the named consult, lab panel or membership actually belongs to the named patient, nor that the named `provider_id` has any particular `provider_type` or `active` value, so a booking that crosses those references is accepted and the consult's `hormone_type`, `planned_pellet_count` and `planned_total_mg` are copied across regardless of whose consult it was.

On success the server copies `hormone_type`, `planned_pellet_count` and `planned_total_mg` from the referenced consult onto the procedure, sets `scheduled_end` to 45 minutes after `scheduled_start`, sets `status` to `SCHEDULED` and `recall_status` to `NONE`, and initialises `pellets_allocated`, `pellets_opened`, `pellets_inserted`, `pellets_wasted`, `delivered_total_mg`, `charge_cents` and `cancel_fee_cents` all to 0, with `consent_record_id`, `cancel_tier`, `performed_at` and `hold_reason` all null. Booking is deliberately held **outside** the delegated-authority gate for every role, because scheduling administers nothing to anybody; that exclusion is what keeps the scheduling checks reachable even when every standing order in the database is suspended.

Two ceilings apply to the dose. The first is per-procedure: `planned_total_mg` may not exceed 2000 mg for a testosterone procedure or 150 mg for an estradiol one, with equality passing, and a violation answers `ERR_DOSE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The second is cumulative over a rolling 365-day window, and it is computed differently depending on when it is asked. At booking, the system computes the patient's **including-scheduled** exposure for that hormone: the sum of `delivered_total_mg` over the patient's procedures of that hormone whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `performed_at` falls within the window running from 365 days before now up to now, both ends inclusive, **plus** the sum of `planned_total_mg` over the patient's procedures of that hormone whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONSENTED` or `SUPPLY_OPENED` and whose `scheduled_start` falls strictly after now and no later than 365 days after now. That figure plus the milligrams this booking would commit must not exceed 4800 mg for testosterone or 400 mg for estradiol; equality passes, and a violation answers `ERR_ROLLING_MG_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Procedures in `VOIDED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPPLY_HOLD` are excluded from both halves of the sum. The window boundary is exact: a completed procedure performed 366 days ago is out, one performed exactly 365 days ago is in.

At insertion time the same cap is re-tested against a **completed-only** exposure, which drops the forward-looking half entirely and counts only milligrams actually in bodies. The procedure being performed is not yet `COMPLETED` and so is not in its own sum; the milligrams about to commit are added separately. The two variants deliberately return different numbers for the same patient at the same instant, and the including-scheduled variant can refuse a booking that the completed-only variant would happily allow. The interaction runs the other way too: breakage at insertion lowers `delivered_total_mg`, which lowers the completed-only exposure, which can let a later procedure through a ceiling the planned figure would have blocked.

An `INITIAL` procedure skips the re-dose gate entirely. A `REDOSE` runs all five of its factors, in the fixed ladder order set out in §2.9, against the panel named by `lab_panel_id` and an **anchor** — the `performed_at` of the most recent `COMPLETED` procedure for this patient whose `hormone_type` matches the consult's. Note that the anchor is hormone-specific and is *not* the patient's `last_insertion_at`, which ignores hormone type entirely. Factor three, checked first, is freshness: no more than 45 days may separate the panel's `drawn_at` from now, so a panel exactly 45 days old still supports the decision while one 46 days old is rejected with `ERR_LAB_STALE`. Factor two, checked second, is the trough window: the panel's `is_trough_draw` must be true, and the days between the anchor and the panel's `drawn_at` must fall inside the hormone's window with **both** bounds inclusive — from 70 through 98 days for testosterone, from 56 through 84 days for estradiol — failing which the answer is `ERR_TROUGH_WINDOW_VIOLATION`. Factor one, checked third, is the minimum interval: at least 84 days for testosterone or at least 70 days for estradiol must separate the anchor from the requested `scheduled_start`, with equality passing; a patient who has no completed procedure of that hormone at all also fails here, with `ERR_REDOSE_INTERVAL_NOT_MET`. Factor five, checked fourth, is the band: the panel's `analyte` must be the hormone's primary analyte — `TOTAL_TESTOSTERONE` for testosterone, `ESTRADIOL` for estradiol — and its `clinic_optimal_band` must read `BELOW_OPTIMAL`, failing which the answer is `ERR_ANALYTE_NOT_BELOW_OPTIMAL`. Factor four, checked last, is symptom burden: the consult's `symptom_score` must be at least 40, with equality passing, failing which the answer is `ERR_SYMPTOM_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. A patient is eligible for a re-dose only when all five factors hold at once. When the anchor does not exist, factors three and two have nothing to measure and are skipped, so the request lands squarely on the interval factor. When the anchor *does* exist but no `lab_panel_id` was supplied at all, the two panel-reading factors are not skipped: factor three has no panel to age, fails, and a `REDOSE` booked without a supporting panel therefore answers `ERR_LAB_STALE`.

Below the re-dose gate sit three occupancy rules. The **post-insertion restriction** is anchored on the patient and spans every hormone: a booking is refused when its `scheduled_start` falls strictly earlier than `last_insertion_at` plus 14 days, which means a slot exactly 14 days after the last insertion of any hormone is permitted. The rejection code is `ERR_POST_INSERTION_RESTRICTION`. For a same-hormone re-dose the 84-day or 70-day interval always subsumes this 14-day window, so the restriction bites in practice on cross-hormone bookings — a patient who completed a testosterone insertion on day zero and books an estradiol `INITIAL` insertion on day ten skips the whole re-dose gate and lands here.

The **provider conflict** rule and the **room conflict** rule use deliberately different interval geometry on different resources. For the provider, expand the new booking by 20 minutes on each side and test that expanded interval against the raw intervals of the provider's existing bookings: there is a conflict when the new start minus twenty minutes falls strictly before an existing booking's end **and** that booking's start falls strictly before the new end plus twenty minutes. For the room, use no buffer at all and test half-open overlap on the raw intervals: there is a conflict when the new start falls strictly before an existing end **and** the existing start falls strictly before the new end — so two bookings that touch exactly at a boundary in the same room are allowed, while the same pair on one provider is not. Both rules draw from the same candidate set: every procedure of that `provider_id` (or in that `room_code`) whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONSENTED`, `SUPPLY_OPENED` or `COMPLETED`, plus every injection visit of that provider (or room) whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ADMINISTERED`. Rows in `CANCELLED`, `VOIDED` or `SUPPLY_HOLD` never occupy anybody. The codes are `ERR_PROVIDER_SLOT_CONFLICT` and `ERR_ROOM_CONFLICT`, and the provider rule is checked first.

Finally, a booking is refused outright while the patient's `clinical_hold` is true, with `ERR_PATIENT_CLINICAL_HOLD`, and — at the very bottom of the ladder — while their `financial_hold` is true, with `ERR_PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD` at HTTP 402.

Because reconstructing all of this by hand is unreasonable, the system offers a read-only therapy-status projection for a patient, open to `ClinicAdmin`, `Physician`, `NurseInjector`, `FrontDesk` and the `Patient` themselves. The caller names the `hormone_type` of interest and may optionally supply a `consult_id`, a `lab_panel_id` and a `scheduled_start` so that the five factors can be evaluated against a specific hypothetical booking. The response reports `patient_id` and `hormone_type` as asked, then `rolling_mg_completed_only` and `rolling_mg_including_scheduled` side by side with the applicable `rolling_mg_cap`, the `anchor_performed_at` instant the re-dose factors are measured from, the five booleans `f1_interval_ok`, `f2_trough_ok`, `f3_freshness_ok`, `f4_symptom_ok` and `f5_band_ok`, the conjunction of all five as `redose_eligible`, and the patient's current `clinical_hold`, `financial_hold` and `eligibility_status`. The read mutates nothing.

### 3.5. Pellet Supply: Lots, Earliest-Expiry Allocation, the Tray, and the Insertion

Pellets enter the clinic in manufacturer lots, and only an `InventoryManager` may receive one. The receipt names a `lot_code` unique across all lots, the `hormone_type` the pellets contain, the `strength_mg` of each pellet, an `expiry_date` after which the lot may not be allocated, and a `qty_received` greater than zero. A duplicate `lot_code` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400), and that uniqueness test runs **before** the strength test below, so a receipt that is both a duplicate and off-ladder reports the duplicate. The strength must be a rung of the ladder for that hormone — 200 mg or 100 mg for testosterone, 25 mg or 10 mg for estradiol — and anything else is rejected with `ERR_LOT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. On receipt, `qty_on_hand` is set equal to `qty_received` while `qty_reserved`, `qty_consumed`, `qty_wasted` and `qty_returned` are all set to 0, satisfying the pellet conservation identity from the first instant. `sterility_status` starts at `QUARANTINED`, and `recall_severity`, `recalled_at` and `recall_reason` are all null. `qty_received` is immutable thereafter.

A quarantined lot is inert. Releasing it — again an `InventoryManager` action — moves `QUARANTINED → RELEASED`, increments the lot's `version` by 1, and makes it a candidate for allocation. There is no manual quarantine endpoint in the other direction: a lot leaves `RELEASED` only by a severe adverse event naming it, which moves it back `RELEASED → QUARANTINED`, or by a recall, which moves it to the terminal `RECALLED`.

Allocation is a planner, not a picker: the client never names a lot. An `InventoryManager` or a `Physician` runs the plan against a procedure, supplying only `expected_procedure_version`. The requirement is decomposed into at most two legs in a fixed order — first the large-strength leg, needing the consult's `planned_large_count` pellets at the large strength, then the small-strength leg, needing `planned_small_count` pellets at the small strength. For each leg whose need is greater than zero, in that order, the candidate set is every lot whose `hormone_type` matches the procedure's, whose `strength_mg` equals that leg's strength exactly, whose `sterility_status` is `RELEASED`, whose `expiry_date` falls **strictly after** the procedure's `scheduled_start` (a lot expiring exactly at the slot start is not a candidate), and whose `qty_on_hand` is greater than zero. Candidates are ranked by `expiry_date` ascending — earliest expiry first — and ties on identical expiry are broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id` and never by `lot_code`. A residual tie on identical `created_at` — routine, since the logical clock only moves when it is driven — is broken by the order the lots were received. The planner walks that ranked list taking, from each lot in turn, the smaller of the pellets still needed and that lot's `qty_on_hand`, until the leg is satisfied.

Two failure modes land in the same place. If a leg's candidate set is empty, or if the walk exhausts the candidates with pellets still needed, the **whole** request fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PELLET_INVENTORY` and neither a lot bucket nor an allocation row is touched — including the leg that had already succeeded. The plan is strength-exact and never substitutes two small pellets for one large; quarantined and recalled lots are never candidates at all.

On success, one `PelletAllocation` row is written per lot draw. Each carries the `procedure_id` it serves and the `lot_id` it drew from — both chosen by the server, never supplied by the client — the `strength_mg` copied from the chosen lot, the `quantity` drawn, an `allocation_rank` assigned 1, 2, 3 and onward across the concatenated walk order with the large-strength leg's draws numbered before the small-strength leg's, and a `status` of `RESERVED`. Each drawn lot moves that quantity from `qty_on_hand` into `qty_reserved`, and the procedure's `pellets_allocated` is recomputed as the sum of `quantity` over its allocation rows whose status is `RESERVED`, `OPENED`, `CONSUMED`, `WASTED` or `RECALL_FLAGGED`. The procedure's `version` increments by 1. The request answers HTTP 201 with `response_type` `array`, and `data` holds only the allocation rows this run created, in ascending `allocation_rank`; rows the run superseded are not echoed, and — as §2.2 notes — the `meta` block of that array response carries no pagination fields.

Re-running the planner supersedes rather than adds. Any allocation row this procedure already holds in `RESERVED` or `OPENED` is moved to `RELEASED` with its quantity credited back from `qty_reserved` to `qty_on_hand`, and the new plan is computed against that refreshed inventory. The two halves commit together or not at all: the walk is run first against inventory that merely *assumes* the credit, and if it comes up short the request fails at rung 30 with nothing written — the supersession is **not** committed either, so a failed re-plan leaves the procedure holding exactly the allocation set it already had. Because supersession always succeeds on state grounds, the stale-version guard described in §2.7 is the only protection the caller has, and a mismatch answers `ERR_STALE_PROCEDURE_VERSION`. Allocation rows are readable from both ends — by the procedure that holds them and by the lot they were drawn from — and both listings accept a `status` filter, which is how allocation rows created by a cascade rather than by a direct call are discovered.

An allocation row's own lifecycle has exactly seven declared legs, and each moves the lot buckets differently, so they are worth stating together. `RESERVED → OPENED` fires when the sterile tray is opened for a procedure sitting in `CONSENTED`, and moves **no** bucket at all. `OPENED → CONSUMED` fires at insertion and moves the row's `quantity` from `qty_reserved` into `qty_consumed`. `OPENED → WASTED` fires either at insertion with a non-zero wastage count or when a procedure is cancelled out of `SUPPLY_OPENED`, and moves the quantity from `qty_reserved` into `qty_wasted`. `RESERVED → RELEASED` fires when a procedure is cancelled out of `SCHEDULED` or `CONSENTED`, when a fresh plan supersedes an old one, or when a cascade freezes the procedure, and returns the quantity from `qty_reserved` to `qty_on_hand`. `OPENED → RELEASED` fires from a cascade acting on a procedure in `SUPPLY_OPENED`, and equally from a fresh allocation plan superseding an already-opened set, and returns the quantity the same way. `RESERVED → RECALL_RELEASED` and `OPENED → RECALL_RELEASED` both fire from a recall of the row's lot and move the quantity from `qty_reserved` into `qty_returned`. `CONSUMED → RECALL_FLAGGED` also fires from a recall of the row's lot but moves nothing, because those pellets are inside a patient. Every one of the seven increments the allocation row's `version` by 1, and `CONSUMED`, `WASTED`, `RELEASED`, `RECALL_RELEASED` and `RECALL_FLAGGED` are all terminal.

Opening the sterile tray moves the procedure `CONSENTED → SUPPLY_OPENED` and may be done by a `NurseInjector` or a `Physician`; when the actor is a nurse, the delegated-authority gate of §2.8 applies. The procedure's `pellets_allocated` must already equal its `planned_pellet_count`, meaning the plan is complete; a procedure whose plan is short, over-full, or missing altogether is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), tested after the `CONSENTED` origin gate of rungs 10 and 11 and before rung 16 re-examines the consent. Every allocation of that procedure in `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → OPENED`, the procedure's `pellets_opened` is set to `pellets_allocated`, and every affected allocation and the procedure itself increment their `version`. Crucially, **no lot bucket moves at all**: `qty_reserved` already covers reserved and opened pellets alike, so opening a tray is a status change and nothing more.

The insertion itself moves the procedure `SUPPLY_OPENED → COMPLETED`. It is available to a `NurseInjector` — under the delegation gate — or to a `Physician` on their own licence, and it requires two inputs: `expected_patient_version`, matched against the patient's current `version` as described in §2.7, and `pellets_wasted`, the count of opened pellets destroyed on the table. That count may not exceed the procedure's `pellets_opened`; a larger figure is rejected as a request-shape violation with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Because the comparison needs the procedure row, it runs immediately after rung 6 has resolved the path id and **before** rung 8, rung 9's delegation gate, the state gate of rungs 10 and 11, and the stale-version guard of rung 12 — so an over-large `pellets_wasted` pre-empts all of them, and in particular a procedure that is not yet in `SUPPLY_OPENED` (whose `pellets_opened` is still 0) answers `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE` whenever any non-zero wastage count is sent. Before anything is written the patient must have `clinical_hold` false, `financial_hold` false, and no panel in `CRITICAL_FLAGGED`; the attached consent must still be valid; the hematocrit ceiling of 52.0 percent must still hold; and the completed-only rolling exposure plus the milligrams about to commit must still sit within the hormone's rolling ceiling.

Wastage is taken from the **highest** `allocation_rank` downward. Because ranks were assigned in earliest-expiry order, walking downward means the earliest-expiring lot is consumed and the breakage lands on the latest-expiring lot. Rows are walked from the top rank toward rank 1, taking pellets as waste until the requested `pellets_wasted` is satisfied; every remaining pellet moves to `CONSUMED`. When the wastage boundary falls part-way through a row, that row is **split**: the original row keeps its `id`, keeps its `allocation_rank`, and takes the consumed remainder as its `quantity` with status `CONSUMED`, while a new allocation row is created against the same `procedure_id`, the same `lot_id`, the same `strength_mg` and the same `allocation_rank`, carrying the wasted quantity with status `WASTED`. A row wasted in full is simply flipped to `WASTED` with no split at all. For every lot touched, `qty_reserved` decreases by that lot's opened quantity while `qty_consumed` rises by its consumed share and `qty_wasted` by its wasted share, so the pellet conservation identity survives the split intact.

The procedure then records the outcome. `pellets_inserted` and `pellets_wasted` are written; `delivered_total_mg` is recomputed as the sum, over this procedure's allocation rows whose status is exactly `CONSUMED`, of `quantity` multiplied by `strength_mg` — rows in `OPENED`, `WASTED`, `RELEASED`, `RECALL_RELEASED` or `RECALL_FLAGGED` contribute nothing. This is the actual delivered figure, and any non-zero wastage makes it strictly smaller than the planned figure copied at booking. `performed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `charge_cents` is computed as described below, the patient's `cumulative_mg_testosterone` or `cumulative_mg_estradiol` rises by the delivered total, `last_insertion_at` and `last_insertion_hormone_type` are updated to this procedure's values, and both the patient's `version` and the procedure's `version` increment by 1.

The charge is built in two stages. **Stage one, the pellet subtotal.** The consumed allocation rows are grouped by `strength_mg` into one line per distinct strength, ordered large strength first and then small strength. Each line's cents figure is the line's pellet count multiplied by that strength in milligrams multiplied by the hormone's price per milligram — 21.5 cents per milligram for testosterone, 176.25 cents per milligram for estradiol — with the result rounded half up to whole cents **per line**. The pellet subtotal is the sum of the rounded lines, and the procedure subtotal is a fixed insertion base fee of 25000 cents plus that pellet subtotal. Rounding order here is load-bearing and must not be rearranged: an estradiol procedure that consumed three 25 mg pellets and one 10 mg pellet produces a first line of 13218.75 cents rounding to 13219 and a second line of 1762.5 cents rounding to 1763, summing to 14982 — whereas summing the raw products first and rounding once would give 14981, which is wrong. Wasted pellets never enter this subtotal; they are billed separately by the refund settlement.

**Stage two, the discount and the facility fee**, evaluated in exactly this order. First take the procedure subtotal from stage one. Second, determine the discount rate: it is the membership's `discount_pct` when the procedure names a `membership_id` and that membership's `status` is `ACTIVE` at the moment of insertion, and zero otherwise. Third, compute the discount amount as the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents — this is the only rounding step in stage two. Fourth, subtract the discount amount from the subtotal to get the discounted subtotal. Fifth, decide the facility fee: it is zero when the **discounted** subtotal is greater than or equal to 42625 cents, and 7500 cents otherwise. Sixth, `charge_cents` is the discounted subtotal plus the facility fee.

The dependency looks circular — the fee depends on the discounted subtotal, which depends on the discount, which is keyed on the subtotal — and the numbered order above is what resolves it. It also produces a consequence the implementation must reproduce rather than smooth over: a discount can cost a patient the waiver and leave them paying more overall. An estradiol procedure delivering 100 mg has a subtotal of exactly 42625 cents; with no membership the waiver is granted precisely on the boundary and the total is 42625, while a `PLUS` member's ten-percent discount of 4262.5 rounds to 4263, drops the discounted subtotal to 38362, loses the waiver, and totals 45862.

### 3.6. Holds, Cancellation, Voiding and the Two Refund Numbers

A procedure that has not yet been performed can leave the pipeline in two ways: the clinic can freeze it, or somebody can cancel it. Freezing is never a client action — `SUPPLY_HOLD` is reached only by cascade, over three legs: `SCHEDULED → SUPPLY_HOLD`, `CONSENTED → SUPPLY_HOLD` and `SUPPLY_OPENED → SUPPLY_HOLD`. The `hold_reason` written on arrival names which cascade did it: `STANDING_ORDER_SUSPENDED` when a severe adverse event suspended the governing order, `LOT_RECALL` when a recall reached the pellets, and `PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD` when a financing plan defaulted. Freezing from `SCHEDULED` or from `CONSENTED` releases every allocation of that procedure in `RESERVED` or `OPENED` back to `RELEASED`, returning each quantity from `qty_reserved` to `qty_on_hand`; freezing from `CONSENTED` preserves the attached `consent_record_id`, while freezing from `SUPPLY_OPENED` additionally resets `pellets_opened` to 0. That last case is a deliberate asymmetry worth dwelling on: when the clinic itself puts an opened tray on hold, the pellets are credited back to stock rather than charged as waste, because a systemic hold is not the patient's doing. Every freeze increments the procedure's `version` by 1.

Coming back out of `SUPPLY_HOLD` is possible for exactly two of the three hold reasons. Reinstating a suspended standing order returns every procedure held under it with `hold_reason` `STANDING_ORDER_SUSPENDED` to `SCHEDULED`; clearing a patient's financial hold returns every procedure of that patient with `hold_reason` `PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD` to `SCHEDULED`. In both cases `hold_reason` is set back to null and `consent_record_id` is cleared to null, so the procedure must be re-consented and re-allocated before it can proceed, and the procedure's `version` increments. A procedure held for `LOT_RECALL` matches neither trigger: a recall hold is permanent, and cancellation is that procedure's only exit.

Cancellation reaches the terminal `CANCELLED` over four declared legs — `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, `CONSENTED → CANCELLED`, `SUPPLY_OPENED → CANCELLED` and `SUPPLY_HOLD → CANCELLED`. It may carry an optional `reason`, and is open to `FrontDesk`, `Physician` and the `Patient` themselves — except from `SUPPLY_OPENED` and `SUPPLY_HOLD`, where the sterile tray is already committed or the clinic itself imposed the freeze, and only `FrontDesk` and `Physician` may act. That narrowing needs the procedure's current status, so it is an extension of rung 3 evaluated after rung 6 has resolved the path id and **before** rung 8 and the state gate: a `Patient` cancelling their own procedure out of `SUPPLY_OPENED` or `SUPPLY_HOLD` hears `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, not `ERR_FORBIDDEN_OWNERSHIP` and not `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. What happens to the pellets depends entirely on where the procedure was. From `SCHEDULED` or `CONSENTED`, every allocation in `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED` and its quantity returns from `qty_reserved` to `qty_on_hand`; a consent attached before the cancel stays `ACTIVE` and remains reusable. From `SUPPLY_OPENED`, every allocation in `OPENED` moves to `WASTED` and its quantity moves from `qty_reserved` to `qty_wasted` — an opened sterile tray cannot go back on the shelf, so `qty_on_hand` does not rise — and the procedure's `pellets_wasted` is set to its `pellets_opened`. From `SUPPLY_HOLD` there are no reserved or opened pellets left to move, because the freeze already released them.

The cancellation fee is a lead-time ladder. Let the lead be the exact number of hours from now to the procedure's `scheduled_start`, kept unfloored. When the lead is 48 hours or more, `cancel_tier` is `NONE` and the base fee is 0. When it is at least 24 hours but less than 48, the tier is `TIER1` and the base fee is 5000 cents. When it is greater than zero but less than 24 hours, the tier is `TIER2` and the base fee is 12500 cents. When the lead is zero or negative — the slot has arrived or passed — the tier is `NO_SHOW` and the base fee is 20000 cents. A membership discount then applies: take the membership's `discount_pct` when the procedure names a `membership_id` and that membership's `status` is `ACTIVE`, or zero otherwise, multiply the base fee by one minus that rate, and round half up once to whole cents. That figure is written to `cancel_fee_cents` and the tier to `cancel_tier`. The financing plan is never consulted by this calculation.

Cancelling from `SUPPLY_HOLD` overrides the ladder entirely: `cancel_tier` is forced to `NONE` and `cancel_fee_cents` to 0 regardless of lead time, because the clinic put the procedure on hold and the patient must not pay for it. The divergence is stark — the identical procedure cancelled two hours before its slot yields 12500 cents from `SCHEDULED` and 0 from `SUPPLY_HOLD`.

Cancellation also settles the money. If the procedure carries a financing plan in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`, that plan moves to `CANCELLED`, every schedule entry still in `PENDING` or `MISSED` flips to `WAIVED` with `waived_cents` recomputed so that the second conservation leg still balances, and `refunded_cents` is written from the **final settlement** figure. Accrued `late_fee_cents` is retained on a plan cancelled out of `DELINQUENT` and is never refunded.

Two refund numbers exist and they are not interchangeable. Both start from the same base: the amount paid to date, which is zero when the procedure has no financing plan and otherwise is the plan's `down_payment_cents` plus its `paid_installment_cents`; and the cancellation fee computed by the lead-time ladder against the logical clock at the moment of the call. The **provisional quote** is the paid-to-date amount minus the cancellation fee, clamped at zero from below. The **final settlement** subtracts one further term: the wasted-pellet charge, computed as the sum over this procedure's allocation rows whose status is exactly `WASTED` of that row's `quantity` multiplied by its `strength_mg` multiplied by the hormone's price per milligram (21.5 cents for testosterone, 176.25 cents for estradiol), rounded half up **per allocation row** and then summed. The final settlement is paid-to-date minus cancellation fee minus wasted-pellet charge, again clamped at zero from below.

The divergence between them is structural rather than temporal — both read the same rows at the same instant. At quote time a procedure sitting in `SUPPLY_OPENED` has no wasted allocations at all, and it is the very act of cancelling from `SUPPLY_OPENED` that converts the whole opened tray to `WASTED`, so the settlement subtracts a charge the quote could not have seen. Cancelling from `SUPPLY_OPENED` therefore always settles at or below the quote, while cancelling from `SCHEDULED` or `CONSENTED` settles exactly at the quote. The provisional number is available as a read-only quote to `FrontDesk`, `Physician` and the `Patient` in **every** procedure state — the read carries no state gate at all, so it answers just as readily for a procedure already `CANCELLED`, `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, and where several financing plans name the same procedure it reads the earliest-created one — reporting `procedure_id`, a `variant` of `PROVISIONAL_QUOTE`, `paid_to_date_cents`, the `cancel_tier` and `cancel_fee_cents` the ladder would produce right now, `wasted_pellet_charge_cents`, and the resulting `refund_cents`. It writes nothing.

Voiding is different from cancelling and only a `Physician` may do it. A void moves a procedure `COMPLETED → VOIDED`, requires a `reason`, and is a **billing correction, not a stock return**. The procedure's `delivered_total_mg` is reset to 0; the patient's `cumulative_mg_testosterone` or `cumulative_mg_estradiol` is reduced by the pre-void delivered figure; and `last_insertion_at` together with `last_insertion_hormone_type` are recomputed from whatever completed procedures remain, falling back to null when none do. Allocation rows stay `CONSUMED` and every lot bucket is left exactly as it was, because the pellets are physically inside the patient and no accounting entry brings them back. `performed_at`, `charge_cents`, `pellets_inserted`, `pellets_wasted` and `pellets_allocated` are likewise all left exactly as the insertion wrote them: a void corrects the milligram ledger and the status axis, and nothing else on the row. Both the patient's `version` and the procedure's `version` increment by 1. `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal states of the procedure lifecycle.

### 3.7. Wellness Injections, Memberships and Prepaid Series

Alongside pellet therapy the clinic sells wellness injections, and a visit for one is booked by `FrontDesk`, `NurseInjector` or `Physician`. The booking names `patient_id`, the administering `provider_id`, the `standing_order_id` that would govern a nurse-administered visit, an `injection_type` of `B12`, `LIPOTROPIC`, `VITAMIN_D` or `GLUTATHIONE`, a `room_code` and a `scheduled_start`; it may optionally name a `series_pack_id` when the visit should draw a prepaid entitlement and a `membership_id` when an included-visit allowance or tier discount should apply. `scheduled_end` is set to 15 minutes after `scheduled_start` and `status` starts at `SCHEDULED`. Injection bookings run the same provider-conflict and room-conflict geometry as insertions — twenty minutes of buffer on the provider, none on the room — and a pack named for a different patient is refused at once with `ERR_SERIES_NOT_TRANSFERABLE`.

Administering the injection moves it `SCHEDULED → ADMINISTERED` and is done by a `NurseInjector` under the delegation gate or by a `Physician` on their own licence. The patient's `clinical_hold` must be false and no panel of theirs may sit in `CRITICAL_FLAGGED`; a financial hold, by contrast, does **not** block administration. When a `series_pack_id` is present, the pack must belong to the same patient, must not be past its `expires_at`, and must have at least one `remaining_visits`.

How the visit is paid for is decided by a fixed three-step precedence, evaluated top to bottom with the first match winning, and written to `payment_source`. Step one: if `series_pack_id` is non-null, the source is `SERIES`. Step two: if `membership_id` is non-null and that membership's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `included_visits_used` is strictly less than its `included_visits_allowance`, the source is `MEMBERSHIP_INCLUDED`. Step three: otherwise the source is `SELF_PAY`. The precedence is absolute — a patient holding both a usable pack and an unused included-visit allowance always burns the pack.

Each source pays differently. For `SERIES`, `charge_cents` is 0 and the value comes out of the pack instead: `series_debit_cents` is normally the pack's floored `per_visit_value_cents`, but on the **final** genuine redemption it is the pack's whole remaining book value, namely `price_cents` minus `per_visit_value_cents` multiplied by one fewer than `purchased_visits`. That final-residual rule applies if and only if, immediately before this draw, `redeemed_visits` equals `purchased_visits` minus one — that is, every other entitlement was actually redeemed. If `remaining_visits` happens to be 1 because entitlements were refunded or forfeited rather than redeemed, the ordinary floored value applies instead. On a `B12` pack the ordinary debit is 3287 cents and the final-residual debit is 3291 cents, four cents more on the identical pack. The pack's `remaining_visits` decrements, `redeemed_visits` increments, and its `version` increments; when the draw takes the last entitlement the pack moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`. For `MEMBERSHIP_INCLUDED`, `charge_cents` is 0, `series_debit_cents` stays 0, the membership's `included_visits_used` increments and its `version` increments by 1. For `SELF_PAY`, `series_debit_cents` stays 0 and `charge_cents` is the injection's list price multiplied by one minus the applicable discount rate, rounded half up once — where the list prices are 3500 cents for `B12`, 4500 for `LIPOTROPIC`, 5500 for `VITAMIN_D` and 8500 for `GLUTATHIONE`, and the discount rate is the membership's `discount_pct` when a `membership_id` is named and that membership is `ACTIVE`, and zero otherwise. A paused membership supplies neither the allowance nor the discount, so a paused `ELITE` member pays the full 8500 for a glutathione injection while an active one pays 6800. `administered_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the visit's `version` increments.

A booked visit that never happens can be cancelled by `FrontDesk` or the `Patient` with an optional `reason`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`; nothing is drawn or returned, because nothing was consumed at scheduling time. An administered visit can be voided by a `Physician` with a required `reason`, moving it `ADMINISTERED → VOIDED`, and this is the compensating action for the entitlement identity: a `SERIES` visit gives the pack its entitlement back, decrementing `redeemed_visits` and incrementing `remaining_visits` and returning the pack `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` whenever the pack currently reads `EXHAUSTED`, whichever visit had emptied it; a `MEMBERSHIP_INCLUDED` visit decrements `included_visits_used` and increments the membership's `version`; a `SELF_PAY` visit simply resets `charge_cents` to 0. The voided visit's own `payment_source` is left as recorded rather than cleared, and on a `SERIES` void so is `series_debit_cents` — the visit keeps the debit figure it was administered under even though the entitlement has gone back into the pack. `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are the visit's terminal states.

Memberships are enrolled by `FrontDesk` or `ClinicAdmin` and name only a `patient_id` and a `tier` of `BASIC`, `PLUS` or `ELITE`. Everything else is derived: `monthly_fee_cents` is 4900 for `BASIC`, 9900 for `PLUS` and 19900 for `ELITE`; `discount_pct` is 0.00, 0.10 and 0.20 across the same three tiers and applies both to procedure subtotals and to cancellation fees; `included_visits_allowance` is 0, 2 and 4 injection visits per period. `period_start` is stamped from the logical clock, `period_end` is 30 days after it, `status` starts `ACTIVE`, and `included_visits_used`, `pause_days_used` and `pause_credit_cents` all start at 0 with `paused_at` null. A patient may hold only one live membership: enrolling while another membership of theirs is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_MEMBERSHIP`.

Pausing, available to `FrontDesk` or the `Patient`, moves the membership `ACTIVE → PAUSED` and banks a prorated credit computed in seven strictly ordered steps. First, elapsed days are the days between `period_start` and now. Second, the period length in days is the days between `period_start` and `period_end`. Third, unused days are the period length minus the elapsed days, floored at zero so a pause after the period has run out banks nothing. Fourth, the daily rate is `monthly_fee_cents` divided by the period length, kept as an exact unrounded rational. Fifth — and this is the only rounding step — the credit is the daily rate multiplied by the unused days, rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth, the credit is capped at `monthly_fee_cents`. Seventh, that credit is added to the membership's `pause_credit_cents`. The order matters: an `ELITE` membership of 19900 cents over 30 days paused on elapsed day 7 has 23 unused days and a daily rate of 663.3333…, giving a correct credit of 15257, whereas rounding the daily rate to 663 first and then multiplying gives 15249, which is wrong. `paused_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `included_visits_used` is **frozen at its current value** — neither reset nor forfeited — and the membership's `version` increments.

Resuming moves it `PAUSED → ACTIVE` and is checked before it is applied: `pause_days_used` plus the days elapsed since `paused_at` must be no greater than 90, with equality passing, and a rejected resume answers `ERR_PAUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` while leaving the membership entirely untouched. On success `pause_days_used` rises by the days elapsed since `paused_at`, `period_end` is pushed out by that same number of days, `pause_credit_cents` is reduced by the smaller of itself and `monthly_fee_cents`, `paused_at` returns to null, and the `version` increments. A membership can cycle between active and paused any number of times.

Changing tier is available to `FrontDesk` or `ClinicAdmin`, names the new `tier` and requires `expected_membership_version` as described in §2.7. It is declared from `ACTIVE` and from `PAUSED` alike — only the terminal `CANCELLED` refuses it, and it does so at rung 11 with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — and it leaves `paused_at`, `pause_days_used` and `pause_credit_cents` exactly as it found them, so a paused membership stays paused, with its banked credit intact, on its new tier. It rewrites `tier`, `monthly_fee_cents`, `discount_pct` and `included_visits_allowance` from the new tier, resets `included_visits_used` to 0 — the only thing that ever resets it — restamps `period_start` from the logical clock with `period_end` set 30 days later, and increments the `version`. Cancelling, open to `FrontDesk` or the `Patient`, reaches the terminal `CANCELLED` over either `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` or `PAUSED → CANCELLED`. From `ACTIVE`, the `discount_pct` is frozen at its current value but stops applying to new charges. From `PAUSED`, the banked `pause_credit_cents` is forfeited and reset to 0, because credit is redeemable only by resuming.

A prepaid series pack is sold by `FrontDesk` or bought by the `Patient`, and names a `patient_id` and a `series_type` of `B12`, `LIPOTROPIC`, `VITAMIN_D` or `GLUTATHIONE`. Every pack carries exactly 8 visits. Its `price_cents` is eight times the injection's list price multiplied by one minus a pack discount rate of 0.15 (fifteen percent), rounded half up once, with a flat enrolment fee of 2500 cents then added as an exact integer. `per_visit_value_cents` is `price_cents` divided by `purchased_visits` and rounded **down**, so a residual of a few cents always remains, and it rides on the final genuine redemption rather than being spread. The four packs price out as follows: `B12` at 26300 cents with a per-visit value of 3287; `LIPOTROPIC` at 33100 with 4137; `VITAMIN_D` at 39900 with 4987; `GLUTATHIONE` at 60300 with 7537 — each leaving a four-cent residual. `purchased_visits` and `remaining_visits` are both set to 8 while `redeemed_visits`, `refunded_visits`, `forfeited_visits` and `refunded_cents` all start at 0; `purchased_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `expires_at` is set 365 days later, and `status` starts `ACTIVE`. A pack is bound to its patient and is never transferable.

A pack can end in three terminal ways. It can be refunded by `FrontDesk` with an optional `reason`, which moves it `ACTIVE → REFUNDED`: `refunded_visits` absorbs whatever `remaining_visits` held, `remaining_visits` drops to 0, and `refunded_cents` is set to the number of entitlements bought back multiplied by the floored `per_visit_value_cents` — deliberately with no residual adjustment, so a `B12` pack refunded before any redemption returns eight times 3287, which is 26296 against a price of 26300, and the clinic keeps the four cents. That is intended, not drift: the entitlement identity is stated over visits, not over cents. It can be exhausted and then, if a visit is voided, come back to `ACTIVE` — `EXHAUSTED` is the one non-terminal resting place on this axis. Or it can move `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, which happens only when somebody attempts an administration against it after `expires_at` has passed, with the mutate-then-reject behaviour set out in §2.11: `forfeited_visits` absorbs `remaining_visits`, `remaining_visits` drops to 0, the pack's `version` increments, and the administration is then refused with `ERR_SERIES_EXPIRED`. The forfeiture write fires only for a pack still reading `ACTIVE`: an already-expired pack — one whose `status` has reached `EXPIRED`, or which reads `EXHAUSTED` or `REFUNDED` — is refused with the same `ERR_SERIES_EXPIRED` and nothing at all is written, which is exactly what makes the second identical attempt forfeit nothing further. A pack with no entitlements left but still inside its validity window is refused with `ERR_SERIES_EXHAUSTED` instead — and because the expiry check sits above the exhaustion check, a pack that is both expired and exhausted reports the expiry.

### 3.8. Delegated Authority, Adverse Events, and the Recall Blast Radius

A standing order is how a physician lends their licence. Only a `Physician` may author one, and it names the delegating `physician_id` (who must be a provider whose `provider_type` is `PHYSICIAN`), a `scope_hormone_types` array that is a subset of testosterone and estradiol and may be empty, a `scope_injection_types` array that is a subset of `B12`, `LIPOTROPIC`, `VITAMIN_D` and `GLUTATHIONE` and may likewise be empty, a `max_dose_mg` greater than zero capping the planned milligrams a nurse may deliver under it, and the inclusive window `valid_from` to `valid_until`. That window must be non-degenerate and bounded: `valid_until` must fall strictly after `valid_from` and no more than 365 days after it. A new order starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `suspended_reason` null. There is deliberately **no expired status**: lapse is evaluated at gate time by comparing the logical clock against the window, so an order that still reads `ACTIVE` but whose window has closed still fails the gate.

An order moves out of `ACTIVE` in two directions. Suspension is never a client action — it is hop two of the severe-adverse-event cascade, moving the order `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `SEVERE_ADVERSE_EVENT`. Reinstatement, available only to the authoring physician (the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `physician_id`), moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, but only when **every** adverse event referencing that order has reached `CLOSED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), tested after the `SUSPENDED` origin gate of rungs 10 and 11; it clears `suspended_reason` back to null and releases every procedure the suspension held. Revocation, again restricted to the authoring physician, may be applied with an optional `reason` over either of two legs, `ACTIVE → REVOKED` or `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`, reaching the terminal `REVOKED`. Revocation and suspension differ in a way that matters operationally: revocation is **forward-looking only**, so already-scheduled procedures are *not* frozen and simply lose their delegated cover for future administrations, whereas suspension freezes them. Revoking an order that was already suspended leaves the procedures it held frozen forever, since reinstatement is no longer available and cancellation becomes their only exit.

Adverse events are filed by a `Physician` or a `NurseInjector` and carry `patient_id`, the `reported_by_provider_id` of the reporter, a `severity`, a free-text `narrative`, and the `onset_at` instant symptoms began. Up to four optional references pin the event to what is implicated: a `procedure_id`, an `injection_visit_id`, a `lot_id` and a `standing_order_id`, in any combination including none. `source` is set to `CLINICAL_REPORT` for staff-filed events, `status` starts `OPEN`, and `reviewed_by_provider_id` and `disposition_note` start null.

Severity is a routing key, and the three filable severities behave very differently. `MILD` logs and nothing more: `review_due_at` stays null and no hold is placed. `MODERATE` sets `review_due_at` to 14 days after the moment of filing — note that the deadline is anchored on the filing instant, not on `onset_at` — places no hold, and increments the patient's `version` by 1. `SEVERE` fires a four-hop cascade and also increments the patient's `version`. Hop one sets `review_due_at` to 24 hours after filing, sets the patient's `clinical_hold` to true with `eligibility_status` recomputed, supersedes **every** `ACTIVE` consent belonging to that patient regardless of hormone type, and, when a `lot_id` was named and that lot is `RELEASED`, moves the lot back `RELEASED → QUARANTINED` so it stops being an allocation candidate — existing reserved and opened allocations against it are left untouched. Hop two suspends the referenced standing order, and it fires **only** when a `standing_order_id` was actually named and that order's `status` is still `ACTIVE`: a severe event naming no order at all, or naming one already `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, stops after hop one, and hops three and four never run. Hop three freezes every procedure booked under that order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONSENTED` or `SUPPLY_OPENED` into `SUPPLY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` `STANDING_ORDER_SUSPENDED` — including procedures belonging to patients who have nothing whatever to do with the event. Hop four releases those procedures' pellets: every allocation of theirs in `RESERVED` or `OPENED` moves to `RELEASED` with its quantity returned from `qty_reserved` to `qty_on_hand`, which changes which lot the next unrelated patient's plan will draw from. The fourth severity value, `SURVEILLANCE`, is system-generated only; a client attempting to file it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

Review is a `Physician`-only action carrying a `disposition` of `CLOSED` or `ESCALATED` and a `disposition_note`. Segregation of duties is absolute: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_provider_id`, and a violation answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. From `OPEN` the event branches — `OPEN → CLOSED` when the disposition is `CLOSED`, `OPEN → ESCALATED` when it is `ESCALATED` — and both branches write `reviewed_by_provider_id` and `disposition_note` and increment the event's `version`. Escalation explicitly leaves the patient's `clinical_hold` true regardless of severity. From `ESCALATED` the event converges over the leg `ESCALATED → CLOSED`, and here the segregation rule tightens: the closing physician's `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_provider_id` **and** from the `reviewed_by_provider_id` written by the escalating review, so three distinct people are required to close an escalated event — reporter, escalating reviewer, closing reviewer. The closing review overwrites `reviewed_by_provider_id` with the closing physician. Whenever a review reaches `CLOSED` and leaves that patient with no adverse event whose `severity` is `SEVERE` or `SURVEILLANCE` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, the patient's `clinical_hold` is set false, `eligibility_status` is recomputed, and the patient's `version` increments by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

A lot recall is the deepest cascade in the system, executable by an `InventoryManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` over either of two legs, `RELEASED → RECALLED` or `QUARANTINED → RECALLED`, which are identical in effect. The request names a `severity_class` of `CLASS_I` or `CLASS_II` and a `recall_reason`. Hop one moves `sterility_status` to the terminal `RECALLED`, increases `qty_returned` by whatever `qty_on_hand` held and then sets `qty_on_hand` to 0, and records `recall_severity`, `recall_reason` and `recalled_at` from the logical clock. Hop two chases the allocations: every allocation of this lot in `RESERVED` or in `OPENED` moves to `RECALL_RELEASED`, with its quantity moved from `qty_reserved` into `qty_returned`; every allocation in `CONSUMED` moves to `RECALL_FLAGGED` with **no** bucket movement at all, because those pellets are inside a patient; and allocations already in `RELEASED`, `WASTED` or `RECALL_RELEASED` are left untouched. Hop three splits in two directions. Hop three-a runs on the procedures' recall axis: every procedure whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and which owns an allocation just flagged moves `NONE → RECALL_AFFECTED`, incrementing its `version`; procedures in `VOIDED` or `CANCELLED` are explicitly skipped, and allocations that had already been released before the recall produce nothing. A procedure whose `recall_status` already reads `RECALL_AFFECTED` or `NOTIFIED` from an earlier recall of some other lot is skipped here as well, so it never mints a second notification. Hop three-b runs on the procedures' primary axis: every procedure in `SCHEDULED`, `CONSENTED` or `SUPPLY_OPENED` owning an allocation just moved to `RECALL_RELEASED` is frozen into `SUPPLY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` `LOT_RECALL`. A procedure owning such an allocation but sitting in any other status is **not** frozen; its `pellets_allocated` is simply recomputed to drop the recalled pellets, and its `version` increments only if that number actually moved.

Hop four notifies. Each recall-affected procedure mints a `PatientNotification` whose `notification_type` is `RECALL`, whose `severity_class` carries the lot's `recall_severity` through, and whose `patient_id`, `procedure_id` and `lot_id` are all set; its `status` starts `PENDING`, its `due_at` is 7 days after the moment of creation, `acknowledged_at` is null, and the patient's `open_notification_count` rises by 1. The procedure then moves `RECALL_AFFECTED → NOTIFIED`, which is terminal on that axis. Because hops four and five run inside the same request as hop three-a, `RECALL_AFFECTED` is never observable at rest and every affected procedure ends the request at `NOTIFIED`. Hop five runs **only** for a `CLASS_I` recall: for each such notification the server mints an `AdverseEvent` with `severity` `SURVEILLANCE`, `source` `RECALL_SURVEILLANCE`, `status` `OPEN` and `reported_by_provider_id` set to the affected procedure's `provider_id`, and sets that patient's `clinical_hold` to true. That surveillance event carries `onset_at` stamped from the logical clock, a `review_due_at` of **null** — the twenty-four-hour review deadline belongs to a filed `SEVERE` event, never to surveillance — `procedure_id` and `lot_id` naming the affected procedure and the recalled lot, `injection_visit_id` and `standing_order_id` null, and `reviewed_by_provider_id` and `disposition_note` null. A `CLASS_II` recall notifies without holding anybody.

Two independent axes on the same procedure row make this workable: the primary `status` axis and the `recall_status` axis run entirely independently, so the same row can read `COMPLETED` with `NONE`, `COMPLETED` with `RECALL_AFFECTED`, or `COMPLETED` with `NOTIFIED`, and status transitions never touch the recall axis.

Before pulling the trigger, an `InventoryManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `ClinicAdmin` can size the blast radius with a read-only impact report, which is answered under one of two `variant` values. Under `PROJECTION`, available at any time, the report is a deliberately over-inclusive worst-case planning number: `released_allocation_count` is the count of this lot's allocations in `RESERVED` or `OPENED`; `flagged_allocation_count` is the count in `CONSUMED`; `affected_procedure_count` is the number of distinct procedures owning those consumed allocations **with no procedure-status filter at all**; `held_procedure_count` is the number of distinct procedures owning reserved or opened allocations of this lot whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONSENTED` or `SUPPLY_OPENED`; `notification_count` equals `affected_procedure_count`; and `surveillance_event_count` equals `affected_procedure_count` when the optional `assume_severity` parameter is `CLASS_I` and is 0 otherwise — including when `assume_severity` is omitted entirely. `assume_severity` is meaningful under `PROJECTION` alone; supplied alongside `ACTUAL` it is still enum-checked at rung 5 and then ignored. Under `ACTUAL`, meaningful once the lot has actually been recalled, the same six response fields are filled from what the cascade really wrote: allocations now in `RECALL_RELEASED`, allocations now in `RECALL_FLAGGED`, procedures with `recall_status` of `NOTIFIED` owning an allocation of this lot, procedures in `SUPPLY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` `LOT_RECALL` owning an allocation of this lot, notifications carrying this `lot_id`, and adverse events carrying this `lot_id` whose `source` is `RECALL_SURVEILLANCE`. The two variants diverge because the projection counts procedures the cascade skips: a lot consumed by two completed procedures and one voided one projects three affected procedures and actually reaches two. The report also echoes the `lot_id` and the `variant` it answered under, and writes nothing.

Notifications close the loop with the patient. Every notification carries a `notification_type` of `RECALL`, `ADVERSE_EVENT` or `CONSENT_REQUIRED`, a `message`, a `due_at` seven days after the moment of minting whatever the type, and a `status` that starts `PENDING`; the listing is filterable by `patient_id`, `procedure_id`, `lot_id`, `notification_type` and `status`, with `procedure_id`, `lot_id` and `severity_class` left null on whichever notifications have no such subject. Acknowledgement by `FrontDesk` or by the `Patient` moves it `PENDING → ACKNOWLEDGED` or `OVERDUE → ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock, decrements the patient's `open_notification_count` by 1 and increments the patient's `version` by 1, and increments the notification's own `version`. `ACKNOWLEDGED` is terminal. The only other movement on this axis is ageing: the ops run flips any notification still `PENDING` whose `due_at` lies strictly before now to `OVERDUE`. The patient's `open_notification_count` counts notifications in `PENDING` and `OVERDUE` together, so ageing a notification does not change it.

### 3.9. Self-Pay Financing and the Delinquency Ops Run

A patient who cannot pay for a procedure outright is offered an installment plan, created by `FrontDesk` alone. The plan names the `patient_id`, the `procedure_id` being financed, a `principal_cents` greater than zero, the `down_payment_cents` paid up front, an `installment_count`, and the `first_due_date` on which installment number one falls due. Two fiscal gates apply, in this order: the down payment must be at least twenty percent of the principal — computed as the principal multiplied by 0.20 and rounded **up** to the next whole cent — and must be strictly less than the principal, failing which the answer is `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_TOO_LOW`; and `installment_count` must be an integer between 2 and 12 inclusive, failing which the answer is `ERR_INSTALLMENT_COUNT_INVALID`.

The schedule is built at creation. The financed amount is the principal minus the down payment. The base installment is that financed amount divided by the installment count and rounded **down** — floor division, never rounding to nearest — and the residual is whatever the base installments leave uncovered, namely the financed amount minus the base multiplied by the count. Every installment from the first through the second-to-last carries exactly the base amount; the **final** installment carries the base plus the entire residual. The residual is never spread a cent at a time across the earliest installments and never lands on the first. Each `schedule` entry is an object carrying an `index` numbered from 1, a `due_date` set to `first_due_date` plus thirty days multiplied by one less than the index, an `amount_cents`, and a `status` that starts `PENDING` and can later read `PAID`, `MISSED` or `WAIVED`. Worked through: a principal of 58300 cents with a down payment of 11660 over six installments finances 46640, gives a base of 7773 and a residual of 2, so installments one through five are 7773 each and installment six is 7775 — and 11660 plus five times 7773 plus 7775 is exactly 58300, as the first conservation leg requires.

A new plan starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `paid_installment_cents`, `waived_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `missed_count` all 0 and `outstanding_cents` equal to the sum of the schedule. Activating it, again a `FrontDesk` action, moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and makes the schedule payable; activation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) if the financed procedure has already reached `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED`, tested after the plan's own `DRAFT` origin gate at rungs 10 and 11.

Payments are made by `FrontDesk` or by the `Patient` and address one installment at a time by `installment_index`, supplying an `amount_cents` that must **exactly** equal that entry's `amount_cents`; the entry must be in `PENDING` or `MISSED`, and any partial payment, over-payment or payment against an already-settled entry is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success the entry moves to `PAID`, `paid_installment_cents` is recomputed as the sum of amounts over `PAID` entries, and `outstanding_cents` as the sum over entries still in `PENDING` or `MISSED`, keeping the second conservation leg balanced. Two state consequences follow. A plan in `DELINQUENT` returns `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE` the moment no schedule entry remains in `MISSED` — that is the cure path, and it is cyclic, so a plan may fall delinquent and cure repeatedly. A plan in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → PAID_OFF` when the payment brings `outstanding_cents` to 0. The two legs are evaluated in exactly that order inside the same request, so a payment that both clears the last `MISSED` entry and empties the schedule carries the plan `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE → PAID_OFF` in one go and answers with `PAID_OFF`. Either way the plan's `version` increments — once, as §2.5 requires, however many legs it crossed.

The ops run is a single system-epoch advance driven by `FrontDesk` or `ClinicAdmin`, and it does two things in a fixed order. **Phase one sweeps financing**, taking plans in ascending order of their `created_at` and, within each plan, its schedule entries in ascending `index`. Only plans in `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` are considered; plans in `DRAFT`, `DEFAULTED`, `PAID_OFF` or `CANCELLED` are skipped entirely. For each entry still `PENDING`, if more than 10 days have elapsed between its `due_date` and now, the entry flips to `MISSED` and 2500 cents are added to the plan's `late_fee_cents`. The boundary is strict: an installment exactly 10 days late is still `PENDING`, and it flips at eleven. A late fee accrues once per entry, at the moment it flips, so re-running the sweep without advancing the clock adds nothing further. Each plan's `missed_count` is then recomputed as the number of its entries currently in `MISSED`. A plan whose `missed_count` has reached 3 or more moves to `DEFAULTED` — from `DELINQUENT`, and equally directly from `ACTIVE` when three or more of its entries flip inside a single sweep; a plan whose `missed_count` is at least 1 but no more than 2 moves `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`; a plan with no missed entries keeps its status. A plan's `version` increments only when the sweep actually changed something on it — an entry flipped to `MISSED`, `missed_count` moved, or the status advanced. A plan that is scanned and found to need nothing is left entirely untouched, with neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` disturbed.

Default fires its own cascade. The patient's `financial_hold` is set true, `eligibility_status` is recomputed, and the patient's `version` increments; then every procedure of that patient whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` is frozen into `SUPPLY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` `PATIENT_FINANCIAL_HOLD`, releasing its `RESERVED` allocations back to `qty_on_hand` — which, like the adverse-event cascade, changes allocation outcomes for entirely unrelated patients. A defaulted plan is terminal and never self-cures; only an explicit financial-hold clear by a `ClinicAdmin` lets the patient's frozen procedures resume.

**Phase two ages notifications**, and begins only after phase one has completely finished. Taking notifications in ascending `created_at`, every one whose `status` is `PENDING` and whose `due_at` lies strictly before now moves `PENDING → OVERDUE` with its `version` incremented. The run answers with `response_type` `object` whose `data` is nothing but seven counters describing what this one run did — no resource is echoed. Each counts only what the run itself changed: `plans_scanned` is the number of plans found in `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` and considered, whether or not anything moved; `entries_missed` is the number of schedule entries flipped `PENDING → MISSED` in this run; `plans_delinquent` and `plans_defaulted` count the plans that *entered* `DELINQUENT` and `DEFAULTED` respectively during this run, so a plan already sitting in either status contributes nothing; `patients_held` counts the distinct patients whose `financial_hold` this run flipped from false to true, so a patient already financially held is not recounted; `procedures_held` counts every procedure frozen into `SUPPLY_HOLD` by the default cascades of this run, summed across all defaulting plans; and `notifications_overdue` counts the notifications phase two aged.

One last financing leg belongs to the cancellation story of §3.6. When the financed procedure is cancelled, a plan in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → CANCELLED` with every `PENDING` entry flipped to `WAIVED` and `refunded_cents` written from the very same **final settlement** the other two legs use — paid-to-date, less the cancellation fee, less the wasted-pellet charge, clamped at zero — which for a `DRAFT` plan reduces to the down payment less the fee only when nothing was wasted, and is reduced further by the wastage when the procedure was cancelled out of `SUPPLY_OPENED`; a plan in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` with every `PENDING` or `MISSED` entry flipped to `WAIVED`, `waived_cents` recomputed so the second conservation leg still balances, and `refunded_cents` written from the final settlement; and a plan in `DELINQUENT` moves `DELINQUENT → CANCELLED` on identical terms except that accrued `late_fee_cents` is retained and never refunded. `DEFAULTED`, `PAID_OFF` and `CANCELLED` are the plan's three terminal states.

Every endpoint listed below is mounted under the `/clinic` prefix and follows the envelope of §2.2.
