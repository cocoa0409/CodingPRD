# Crisis Communications Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a corporate reputation event breaks, the corporate communications department is asked to do three contradictory things at once: move fast, say only what legal has cleared, and speak to five different stakeholder audiences in the right order. Today those three demands live in three different places — a monitoring spreadsheet, an email approval chain, and a shared drive of half-reviewed holding templates — and the failure mode is always the same. A holding statement misses its service-level window because nobody noticed the severity tier had shifted; a draft carrying privileged legal analysis gets approved by someone outside the privilege circle; the media notice goes out before employees have been told.

This document specifies the Crisis Communications Program API, the system of record for the whole arc of a reputation event: monitoring issue signals across media, social and regulatory surfaces; escalating them by a computed severity score; activating a crisis team with named seats and explicit decision authority; developing holding statements and follow-up messages through an approval quorum that tightens as severity rises; sequencing stakeholder notices across employees, customers, regulators, investors and media; designating and preparing spokespeople for interviews; tracking and correcting misinformation with platform escalation paths; and closing out with a scored post-crisis assessment that feeds the playbook. Every business path is mounted under `/crisis`.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, the system is a **gate**, not a workflow suggestion: each incident carries a derived communications posture that can silently freeze or restrict every outbound write, and no operation is permitted to route around it. Second, the system is **auditable by arithmetic**: severity tiers, containment priorities, reach indices, readiness decay and reputation deltas are all computed by pinned formulas with pinned evaluation order, so two reviewers reading the same incident after the fact agree on every number. All time-sensitive behaviour reads a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, which makes the entire time-pressure model deterministically testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the caller is acting under. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Surrounding whitespace in either header is insignificant: it is stripped before the value is examined or resolved, so a header holding nothing but whitespace counts as empty, and `X-User-ID` is matched against `TeamMember` identifiers in its trimmed form. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `IncidentLead`, `LegalCounsel`, `CommsLead`, `Spokesperson`, `SocialListener`, and `ExecSponsor`.

`ProgramAdmin` is the communications program administrator. It owns the static registries — the personnel roster, the monitored-channel registry and the prepared-material library — plus the logical clock, and it never drafts, approves or publishes content. `IncidentLead` is the crisis team incident lead: it opens incidents, seats the team, and activates and resolves the response. `LegalCounsel` is in-house counsel and the sole holder of privilege authority; it may withdraw content on legal grounds and may sit as a veto seat in approval routing, but may never draft or publish. `CommsLead` is the communications lead and owns message development, approval routing, audience sequencing, interview booking and correction publication. `Spokesperson` fronts interviews and may stand down a booking, but holds no drafting, approval or release authority. `SocialListener` is the social listening analyst: it records signals and misinformation claims, triages correction windows and drives platform escalation, but cannot author or publish statements. `ExecSponsor` is the executive sponsor: it carries the highest decision authority in approval routing and is the only role that may close out the post-crisis assessment.

Identity resolution differs between `ProgramAdmin` and the other six roles, and this distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it in an operational log but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is performed against it. When `X-User-Role` is any of `IncidentLead`, `LegalCounsel`, `CommsLead`, `Spokesperson`, `SocialListener` or `ExecSponsor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TeamMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `TeamMember` compare the resource's member-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, opening an incident records the acting `IncidentLead`'s `X-User-ID` as the incident's `created_by_member_id`; creating a statement records the acting `CommsLead`'s `X-User-ID` as that statement's `drafted_by_member_id`; acknowledging a crisis-team seat requires `X-User-ID` to equal the seat's `member_id`; and deciding an approval task requires `X-User-ID` to equal the task's `assignee_member_id`. On any endpoint whose authorised roles include one of those six entity-backed roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing `TeamMember` is rejected with `ERR_TEAM_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in the system read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, because it records when the response was assembled rather than anything a business rule reasons about.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a value for either parameter that is not a base-ten integer, such as `10.5`, an empty string, or any non-numeric text.

Unless a listing endpoint's own narrative says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties — which the logical clock makes common, since any number of records can share a single instant — broken by the order in which the records were created, so the ordering is always total and stable. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching record remains beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource this API persists — `TeamMember`, `MonitoredChannel`, `IssueSignal`, `Incident`, `TeamAssignment`, `Statement`, `ApprovalTask`, `StakeholderNotice`, `PreparedMaterial`, `MediaInterview`, `MisinformationClaim` and `PostCrisisReview` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, rendered with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format; on a freshly created resource it equals `created_at`. Both instants are read from the logical clock described in the next paragraph, never from wall-clock time. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. A recomputation that leaves a value exactly where it already stood is not a mutation: when a `TeamMember`'s derived counters are recomputed, that member's `updated_at` and `version` move only if `scheduled_interview_minutes` or `active_assignment_count` actually changes, so a cascade that recomputes a member without altering either counter leaves the member's `version` untouched. All twelve resource types carry all four properties — none of them is a create-only record.

Every instant this system reasons about — deadlines, embargoes, decay windows, breach latching — is read from a server-side **logical clock** rather than from wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until a `ProgramAdmin` repositions it, every reference to the current instant reads that default, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. Repositioning the clock is done by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the call answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep of any kind: time-driven state advances only when an operator explicitly asks for it, which is what makes the entire time-pressure model reproducible.

### 2.5. Flat Topology, Path Resolution and Collection Filters

The resource graph is deliberately **flat**. No entity is nested inside another entity's URL path; every collection sits directly under `/crisis/<collection>`, and every parent-child relationship is discovered by filtering a collection rather than by walking a nested path. A caller who wants an incident's statements lists statements filtered by `incident_id`; a caller who wants a statement's approval tasks lists approval tasks filtered by `statement_id`.

Because paths are flat, each action path carries exactly one identifier: `member_id`, `channel_id`, `incident_id`, `signal_id`, `assignment_id`, `statement_id`, `task_id`, `notice_id`, `material_id`, `interview_id`, `claim_id` or `review_id`. Path identifiers are resolved left to right before any body field is inspected, and an identifier that does not resolve produces the resource-specific not-found error at HTTP 404 — `ERR_TEAM_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIGNAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` respectively. The same catalogue governs foreign keys carried in a request body: an `incident_id`, `channel_id`, `member_id`, `statement_id`, `spokesperson_member_id`, `supersedes_statement_id` or `correction_statement_id` that does not resolve produces the not-found error belonging to the referenced resource type, also at HTTP 404. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to every creation endpoint and additionally to signal linking and to correction issuance.

Filters available on the listing endpoints follow the entity's own vocabulary. Roster listings filter on `privilege_cleared` and `media_training_status`. Channel listings filter on `channel_type` and `escalation_path_state`. Incident listings filter on `response_state`, `privilege_state`, `comms_posture`, `severity_tier` and `created_by_member_id`. Seat listings filter on `incident_id`, `member_id`, `team_role` and `state`. Signal listings filter on `incident_id`, `channel_id` and `state`. Statement listings filter on `incident_id`, `drafted_by_member_id`, `supersedes_statement_id`, `statement_type` and `state`. Approval-task listings filter on `statement_id`, `assignee_member_id`, `revision_round` and `state`, and return tasks in their pinned creation order. Notice listings filter on `incident_id`, `statement_id`, `audience` and `state`. Prepared-material listings filter on `scenario_code`, `material_type` and `readiness_state`. Interview listings filter on `incident_id`, `spokesperson_member_id`, `channel_id` and `state`. Claim listings filter on `incident_id`, `channel_id`, `correction_statement_id` and `state`. Post-crisis-review listings filter on `incident_id` and `state`. All of them additionally accept `limit` and `offset`. An unknown field in any request body, or an enum filter value outside the declared set, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an unknown *query* parameter, by contrast, is simply ignored rather than rejected. `privilege_cleared` is the one boolean-valued filter and accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case, rejecting anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `revision_round` is the one integer-valued filter and must be a base-ten integer or it is rejected the same way; and every identifier-valued filter is an exact string match that is never itself resolved, so an identifier no record holds yields an empty page with a `total_count` of 0 rather than a not-found error.

### 2.6. The Communications Posture Gate

Each incident carries a derived field, `comms_posture`, that is the single most consequential gate in the system. It is never supplied by a client. It is recomputed after every mutation of the incident itself and after every mutation of any of its children, and its value is decided by a three-rule precedence in which the **first matching rule wins**. Rule one: the posture is `FROZEN` when `legal_hold_active` is true, or when `response_state` is `RESOLVED`. Rule two, evaluated only when rule one does not match: the posture is `RESTRICTED` when `sla_breached` is true, or when `blocked_notice_count` — the number of that incident's stakeholder notices sitting in state `BLOCKED` — is at least 1. Rule three, evaluated only when neither of the first two matches: the posture is `OPEN`. A fresh incident is created with the posture `OPEN`.

What each posture blocks is equally precise. A `FROZEN` posture blocks every child mutation on that incident, with exactly four exemptions: waiving privilege, running the service-level checkpoint, scoring the post-crisis review, and recording a decision on an approval task. That last exemption follows from the guard list of §3.5 being complete: an approval decision carries no posture test and no response-dimension test at all, so a counsel-cleared assignee may still approve or reject a statement that is `IN_REVIEW` on an incident sitting under a legal hold. The rejection carries `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A `RESTRICTED` posture permits statement drafting, submission, publication and notice release, but it additionally blocks confirmation of a media interview — interview confirmation is the one operation that demands a fully `OPEN` posture, and it too is refused with `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` when the posture is anything other than `OPEN`.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and the Three Conserved Ledgers

Unless a specific rule says otherwise, every monetary-style or score-style number in this system is carried to **two decimal places and rounded half up**. Those quantities are rendered in the JSON envelope as plain JSON numbers rather than as quoted strings, so the wire form does not preserve trailing zeros: a score held internally as 100.00 appears as `100.0`, and one held as 0.00 appears as `0.0`. Two values are exceptions and carry four decimal places: an incident's exposure factor as snapshotted onto the post-crisis review, and the correction ratio computed during reputation scoring. Elapsed-time quantities — interview durations, correction-window lengths, approval latencies, decay day counts — are whole numbers computed by discarding any fractional remainder, that is, by taking the floor of the division. Service-level windows are expressed in whole minutes.

Three quantities in this system are **conserved ledgers**: an incident's `open_approval_task_count`, a monitored channel's `reserved_correction_minutes`, and a team member's `scheduled_interview_minutes`. Each is recomputed from scratch — never incrementally patched — every time a transition says it changes, which guarantees that any forward action followed by its compensating action leaves the ledger exactly where it started. An incident's `open_approval_task_count` is the number of approval tasks in state `PENDING` whose statement belongs to that incident. A channel's `reserved_correction_minutes` is the sum of `correction_window_minutes` over that channel's claims in state `CORRECTION_ISSUED`. A member's `scheduled_interview_minutes` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over interviews where that member is the spokesperson and the interview is in state `CONFIRMED`. Every filter in those three definitions is an explicit state-enumeration membership test, never a judgement call.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Two write operations in this system reach across into a parent record whose state other callers are also changing, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency on that parent's `version`.

Creating a statement requires the request to carry `expected_incident_version` as a non-optional field, holding the `version` of the target `Incident` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the incident's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INCIDENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard matters precisely because no state precondition covers the case: a new `HOLDING` draft legitimately supersedes an earlier one, so a second create is structurally permitted. The only defect is the writer's stale view of the incident, and the version check is what detects it.

Issuing a correction against a misinformation claim requires the request to carry `expected_channel_version` as a non-optional field, holding the `version` of the referenced `MonitoredChannel` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the channel's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHANNEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again, two different claims on the same channel may each legitimately hold a correction window while capacity remains, so no state precondition catches the writer whose picture of the channel's reserved airtime is out of date; the version check does.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. A caller must not send `expected_incident_version` or `expected_channel_version` anywhere else; doing so is an unknown field and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.9. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is deterministic: checks are evaluated in the order below and the **first failure wins**. Implementers must honour this ordering, because a mid-flight hold or a stale version must surface as itself rather than being masked by a later business check.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised role list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. For endpoints authorising an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `TeamMember` — `ERR_TEAM_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` (404).
4. Request-body schema: every required field present, every value of the declared type, every enum value a declared member, every number inside its declared range, every datetime well formed, every datetime interval satisfying end strictly after start (this covers `scheduled_end_at` against `scheduled_start_at` and `correction_window_end` against `correction_window_start`), no unknown fields, and `limit`/`offset` inside the pagination bounds — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Several details of this step are pinned. A request carrying no body at all is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose payload is entirely optional — or which declares no payload — may be called with no body; a body that is present but parses to anything other than a JSON object is rejected here. An explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omission: for an optional field the documented default applies, and for a required field the answer is the same missing-field rejection. A datetime is well formed when it is an ISO 8601 instant — a trailing `Z` and an explicit numeric offset are both accepted, an offset other than UTC is converted to UTC, and a value carrying no offset at all is read as already being UTC. And a string is measured against its length limit and stored exactly as supplied, without trimming, so a value consisting only of whitespace fails the non-empty test while a value with significant leading or trailing spaces is persisted verbatim; an integer-valued field additionally refuses booleans and any number with a fractional part, whereas a number field documented as carrying two decimals accepts a more precise value and rounds it half up to two decimals on ingest rather than refusing it.
5. Every path identifier resolves, validated left to right — the resource-specific not-found error (404).
6. Every foreign key in the body resolves — the resource-specific not-found error (404). Several payloads carry more than one, and they are resolved in the order the catalogue of §2.5 lists them: `incident_id` first, then `channel_id`, then the member reference (`member_id` or `spokesperson_member_id`), then any statement reference. A payload naming two unresolvable identifiers therefore reports the earlier one in that order — seating a member on a non-existent incident answers `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_TEAM_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, and a signal naming neither a known incident nor a known channel answers `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Ownership binding: for seat acknowledgement, `X-User-ID` equals the seat's `member_id`; for an approval decision, `X-User-ID` equals the task's `assignee_member_id` — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
8. On statement creation, `expected_incident_version` equals the incident's `version` — `ERR_STALE_INCIDENT_VERSION` (412).
9. On correction issuance, `expected_channel_version` equals the channel's `version` — `ERR_STALE_CHANNEL_VERSION` (412).
10. The target resource sits in a from-state the requested transition declares — one of `ERR_INVALID_SIGNAL_STATE`, `ERR_STATEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_APPROVAL_TASK_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MATERIAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, all at HTTP 409. This check applies to every action endpoint.
11. The owning incident sits in the response dimension the operation requires — `ACTIVATED` for statement submission and publication, claim triage and notice release, giving `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED`; and not `RESOLVED` for signal linking, seating, seat acknowledgement and privilege *assertion*, giving `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED` — privilege *waiver* is the one privilege transition that carries no such check, precisely so that a hold can still be lifted on a resolved file. Both are HTTP 409, and the check applies to every child-mutating endpoint. The two are ordered against each other: an operation that requires `ACTIVATED` tests the resolved case first, so on a `RESOLVED` incident it always answers `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, and `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` is reserved for an incident still sitting in `MONITORING`.
12. The incident's `comms_posture` does not gate the operation — `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409), applied to every child-mutating endpoint under the rules of §2.6.
13. When the operation touches privileged material, the acting `TeamMember` has `privilege_cleared` true — `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED` (403).
14. Segregation of duties: when deciding an approval task, `X-User-ID` differs from the statement's `drafted_by_member_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
15. The crisis-team seat the operation depends on exists in state `ACKNOWLEDGED` — `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (409).
16. The notice's linked statement is `PUBLISHED` — `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
17. The referenced correction statement is of the right type, state and incident — `ERR_CORRECTION_STATEMENT_REQUIRED` (409).
18. Every required approver seat has a non-empty eligible pool — `ERR_APPROVER_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
19. No other seat on the incident already holds the same `team_role` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT_ROLE` (409).
20. The review deadline has not passed — `ERR_REVIEW_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409).
21. The notice embargo has been reached — `ERR_EMBARGO_NOT_REACHED` (409).
22. The spokesperson calendar has room including the preparation buffer — `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).
23. The signal clears the escalation threshold — `ERR_ESCALATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (422).
24. The incident has at least one escalated signal — `ERR_NO_ESCALATED_SIGNAL` (422).
25. The statement has revision rounds left — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
26. The audience sequence is satisfied — `ERR_NOTICE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (409).
27. The spokesperson's media training is current — `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT` (422).
28. The anticipated-question pack is complete — `ERR_QUESTION_PACK_INCOMPLETE` (422).
29. The channel's escalation path is verified — `ERR_ESCALATION_PATH_UNVERIFIED` (422).
30. The claim clears the virality threshold — `ERR_VIRALITY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (422).
31. No notice on the incident is still open — `ERR_OPEN_NOTICES_BLOCK_RESOLUTION` (409).
32. No misinformation claim on the incident is still open — `ERR_OPEN_CLAIMS_BLOCK_RESOLUTION` (409).
33. The channel has correction capacity for the requested window — `ERR_CHANNEL_CORRECTION_CAPACITY` (409).

The specific trigger conditions behind checks 8 through 33 are described in the workflow narratives of §3, alongside the business rules they protect.

### 2.10. The Privilege Circle, Decision Authority and Segregation of Duties

Two orthogonal ideas govern who may decide what, and confusing them is the most common source of implementation error.

The **privilege circle** is a property of people. A `TeamMember` carries `privilege_cleared`, a boolean defaulting to false, which is true exactly when that person sits inside the attorney-client privilege circle. Five operations test it: asserting privilege on an incident, waiving privilege on an incident, deciding an approval task on a statement whose `privilege_marked` flag is true, publishing a statement whose `privilege_marked` flag is true, and issuing a correction that cites a statement whose `privilege_marked` flag is true. In all five, a caller outside the circle is refused with `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 403).

**Decision authority** is a property of a seat, not a person. Each crisis-team seat carries `decision_authority`, either `FULL` or `LIMITED`, and `FULL` outranks `LIMITED` when the server chooses which seat-holder receives an approval task. Authority never substitutes for privilege clearance: a `FULL`-authority seat held by someone outside the privilege circle still cannot decide a privileged statement.

**Segregation of duties** is a relationship between two records. The `TeamMember` recorded as a statement's `drafted_by_member_id` may never record a decision on any approval task belonging to that statement, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because `drafted_by_member_id` is pinned at creation and is never rewritten when the statement is revised, this separation survives the entire revision cycle.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a crisis from the quiet before it — a maintained roster, a verified channel registry, a library of reviewed dark-site material — through detection, activation, message development, publication, misinformation control and finally the scored retrospective. Rules that cut across several stages are stated where the business first needs them, so a reader following one workflow end to end will encounter each constraint at the moment it bites.

### 3.1. Standing Readiness: The Roster, the Channel Registry and the Dark Site

Nothing in a crisis can be improvised, so the program administrator maintains three registries in peacetime. Registering a person on the crisis roster requires a `full_name` and an `org_title`, each non-empty and no longer than 120 characters; the `org_title` is what appears beside the person in the war-room roster. Two optional attributes decide what that person may later do. `privilege_cleared` is a boolean that defaults to false, and it is true only for people inside the attorney-client privilege circle. `media_training_status` defaults to `NONE` and may be set to `CURRENT`; when it is `CURRENT`, `media_training_expires_at` records the instant at which that currency lapses, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and it is null whenever the status is `NONE` — an expiry supplied alongside a `NONE` status is silently discarded and stored as null rather than rejected. That expiry field is itself optional and defaults to null even when the status is `CURRENT`, so a `CURRENT` member registered without one carries a null expiry and can never satisfy the interview-confirmation training test of §3.7. A newly registered member begins with `scheduled_interview_minutes` of 0 and `active_assignment_count` of 0. The latter is maintained by the server as the number of crisis-team seats held by that member in state `ASSIGNED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Monitoring surfaces are registered separately. A monitored channel needs a `name` — non-empty, at most 120 characters — and a `channel_type` that is one of `MEDIA`, `SOCIAL` or `REGULATORY`, naming whether the surface is an outlet, a social platform or a regulator docket. A `platform_escalation_contact` may optionally be recorded, defaulting to null; it is the trust-and-safety reference the team will lean on when a false claim needs a takedown. `max_concurrent_corrections` expresses how many overlapping correction windows the channel tolerates at once; it defaults to 1 and must be at least 1 and no more than 5. Every new channel starts with `escalation_path_state` of `UNVERIFIED`, a null `verified_contact_reference`, `reserved_correction_minutes` of 0 and `open_escalation_count` of 0, where the latter counts that channel's claims sitting in state `PLATFORM_ESCALATED`.

A channel's escalation path is not usable until an administrator has verified it. Verifying the escalation path supplies a mandatory non-empty `verified_contact_reference`, which the server records verbatim, moves `escalation_path_state` from `UNVERIFIED` to `VERIFIED`, and increments the channel's `version` by 1. No from-state is declared for this operation, and none is enforced: re-verifying a channel that already sits in `VERIFIED` is accepted rather than refused, overwriting `verified_contact_reference` with the newly supplied value, leaving the state at `VERIFIED` and incrementing `version` again. Until that happens, the platform-escalation branch of misinformation handling is closed.

The dark site is the third registry. A prepared material is filed against a `scenario_code`, one of `PRODUCT_RECALL`, `DATA_BREACH`, `EXEC_MISCONDUCT`, `PLANT_INCIDENT` or `REGULATORY_PROBE`, and a `material_type`, one of `DARK_SITE_PAGE`, `FAQ`, `HOLDING_TEMPLATE` or `EXEC_BRIEF`. It also carries a `title` of at most 160 non-empty characters and an optional `dark_site_url` pointing at the staging location of the unpublished asset, defaulting to null. Freshly filed material sits in `readiness_state` `DRAFT` with a `readiness_score` of 0.00, a null `last_reviewed_at` and null `reviewer_notes`; it is not yet counted as ready for anything.

Material readiness decays, and this is the mechanism that keeps the library honest. A content review by the communications lead — permitted from `readiness_state` `DRAFT` or `STALE`, and refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_STATE_INVALID` otherwise — stamps `last_reviewed_at` with the current instant, records the supplied `reviewer_notes`, which is a mandatory non-empty string on this call, and resets `readiness_score` to exactly 100.00, which lands the material in `READY`. From then on the score erodes in whole-day steps rather than continuously: the number of elapsed days is the whole number of complete 24-hour periods between `last_reviewed_at` and the current instant, discarding any partial day, and the score is 100.00 less 2.50 for each of those whole days, rounded half up to two decimals and never allowed below 0.00. That floor is the only clamp; no ceiling is imposed, so if the logical clock is repositioned to an instant earlier than `last_reviewed_at` the whole-day count goes negative and a re-score lifts the value above 100.00. The material is `READY` while that score is at least 60.00 and `STALE` once it falls below. Concretely, sixteen whole days after review the score is 60.00 and the material is still `READY`, because the threshold is inclusive; on the seventeenth day the score is 57.50 and the state becomes `STALE`. Re-reviewing a `STALE` asset restarts the curve at 100.00 and returns it to `READY`, so the cycle between `READY` and `STALE` may repeat indefinitely. Materials still in `DRAFT` are never touched by the decay pass — only `READY` and `STALE` material is re-scored, and only for the scenario the sweeping incident is filed under. A re-score that lands on exactly the `readiness_score` and `readiness_state` the material already held is not a mutation, so it leaves that material's `updated_at` and `version` untouched; only a material whose score or state actually moves is written and bumped.

### 3.2. Issue Monitoring: Signals and the Escalation Score

A social listening analyst — or the program administrator — records an observed issue against a monitored channel by supplying the `channel_id` of the surface it appeared on, a `headline` of at most 240 non-empty characters describing it in one line, a `reach_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive giving observed audience reach, a `sentiment_score` between -1.0 and 1.0 inclusive where -1.0 is hostile and 1.0 supportive — this is the one score-style input that is *not* quantised to two decimals on ingest: it is stored at exactly the precision the client supplied, and the sentiment penalty below is computed from that unrounded value — and a `source_credibility_tier` of `A`, `B` or `C`. Two more inputs are optional: `regulatory_flag`, a boolean defaulting to false that is true once a regulator has publicly referenced the issue, and `incident_id`. Supplying `incident_id` at creation links the signal to that incident immediately, so it starts life in state `LINKED`; omitting it leaves the signal in `OPEN`, and it must be linked before it can ever be escalated. Creation-time linking is deliberately ungated: unlike the standalone link operation described below it tests neither the incident's `response_state` nor its `comms_posture`, so a signal may be filed straight onto a `MONITORING`, `RESOLVED` or `FROZEN` incident. It does recompute that incident's `linked_signal_count`, its other query-derived counters and its `comms_posture`, but it does **not** increment the incident's `version` — creating a signal is the one linking path that leaves the parent's version where it stood. Either way the server stamps `detected_at` with the current instant, and that instant is the chronological ordering key used later when the final severity is computed.

The signal's `escalation_score` is computed once, at creation, and is **frozen forever** — no later operation ever recomputes it. Four influences combine. First, the credibility multiplier is selected by the tier: 1.00 for `A`, 0.85 for `B`, 0.60 for `C`. The base component is the `reach_score` multiplied by that credibility multiplier, rounded half up to two decimals. Second, the sentiment penalty takes the negation of `sentiment_score`, keeps it only if it is greater than 0.0 and treats it as 0.0 otherwise, multiplies the result by a scale of 30, and rounds half up to two decimals; positive sentiment therefore contributes exactly nothing and never reduces the score. Third, a channel bump is added according to the surface: 8 points for a `MEDIA` channel, 4 for a `SOCIAL` channel, and 15 for a `REGULATORY` channel. Fourth, a flat regulatory bump of 12 points is added when `regulatory_flag` is true, and nothing when it is false. The evaluation order is pinned: the base component and the sentiment penalty are each rounded to two decimals **before** anything is summed; the four contributions are then added; the sum is rounded half up to two decimals again; and finally the result is capped so that `escalation_score` never exceeds 100.00. The stored value always sits between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals.

Linking an `OPEN` signal to an incident carries the target `incident_id` in the payload and advances the signal `OPEN → LINKED`. Three conditions guard it beyond the signal's own state: the target incident must exist, its `response_state` must be either `MONITORING` or `ACTIVATED`, and its `comms_posture` must not be `FROZEN`. On success the signal's `incident_id` is written, the incident's `linked_signal_count` — the number of that incident's signals in state `LINKED` or `ESCALATED` — is recomputed, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1. Attempting to link a signal that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SIGNAL_STATE` (HTTP 409), and attempting to link against an incident already in `RESOLVED` is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.

Escalation is the gate that turns noise into a crisis. A signal advances `LINKED → ESCALATED` only when its frozen `escalation_score` is at least 70.00; a score of exactly 70.00 passes and 69.99 does not, and a score below the threshold is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). The incident's posture must not be `FROZEN`, and that posture test is the only response-dimension gate escalation carries — there is no separate resolved check, so because a `RESOLVED` incident is always `FROZEN` under §2.6, escalating against a resolved file answers `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` rather than `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. The posture test is evaluated before the threshold test, so a below-threshold signal on a frozen incident answers `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN`. An `OPEN` signal can never be escalated — the unlinked case is a declared rejection, not a shortcut, and it produces `ERR_INVALID_SIGNAL_STATE` (HTTP 409) with no state change whatsoever. On success the signal's `escalated_at` is stamped with the current instant and the incident's `escalated_signal_count`, the number of its signals in state `ESCALATED`, is recomputed. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1 on every successful escalation — including one against an incident still in `MONITORING`, where the severity and deadline fields (`severity_tier`, `severity_score`, `severity_basis`, `holding_statement_due_at`) and the whole containment family are left exactly as they stood, which on a never-activated file means still null. `ESCALATED` is terminal for a signal.

### 3.3. Activation, Severity Derivation and the Holding-Statement Clock

An incident lead opens an incident file with a `title` of at most 160 non-empty characters and a `scenario_code` drawn from the same five-member playbook enumeration the prepared-material library uses — `PRODUCT_RECALL`, `DATA_BREACH`, `EXEC_MISCONDUCT`, `PLANT_INCIDENT` or `REGULATORY_PROBE` — which is how an incident is joined to its dark-site assets, by simple equality of that code. An optional `reputation_index_before` captures the baseline reputation reading at the moment the file is opened; it defaults to 50.00 and must sit between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals. The opening lead's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `created_by_member_id`. A new incident begins in `response_state` `MONITORING`, `privilege_state` `NON_PRIVILEGED`, `comms_posture` `OPEN`, with `legal_hold_active` false, `sla_breached` false, a null `privilege_basis`, and every counter — `linked_signal_count`, `escalated_signal_count`, `open_approval_task_count`, `blocked_notice_count`, `released_notice_count`, `open_claim_count`, `confirmed_interview_count` — at 0, a `stakeholder_reach_index` of 0.00, and nulls in `activated_at`, `resolved_at`, `severity_tier`, `severity_basis`, `severity_score`, `final_severity_tier`, `final_severity_score`, `holding_statement_due_at`, `holding_statement_published_at`, `base_containment_score`, `amplification_rate_applied`, `containment_priority_score`, `reputation_index_after` and `reputation_delta_reported`.

Seating the team is the lead's next act. A crisis-team seat is created by naming the `incident_id`, the `member_id` of the person filling it, the `team_role` — one of `INCIDENT_LEAD`, `LEGAL_COUNSEL`, `COMMS_LEAD`, `SPOKESPERSON`, `SOCIAL_LISTENER` or `EXEC_SPONSOR` — and the `decision_authority` rank, either `FULL` or `LIMITED`. Each seat is unique per incident: if another seat on the same incident already holds the same `team_role` in state `ASSIGNED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSIGNMENT_ROLE` (HTTP 409). A new seat starts in state `ASSIGNED` with null `acknowledged_at` and null `stood_down_at`, and the filling member's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed. Only the seated person may accept the seat: acknowledgement advances `ASSIGNED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `acknowledged_at`, and recomputes the member's `active_assignment_count`; a caller whose `X-User-ID` differs from the seat's `member_id` is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403), a seat that is not in `ASSIGNED` yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and acknowledgement is impossible once the incident has reached `RESOLVED` or while its posture is `FROZEN`. Because both `ASSIGNED` and `ACKNOWLEDGED` count toward `active_assignment_count`, that recomputation always lands on the value it already held, so acknowledgement never moves the member's own `version`; seating a fresh seat, by contrast, raises the count and does bump the member. Neither seating nor acknowledgement touches the incident at all — the incident's counters and its `version` are left exactly as they stood.

Activation moves the incident `MONITORING → ACTIVATED` and is the moment the crisis clock starts. Three preconditions guard it. The incident must have at least one signal in `ESCALATED`, otherwise no provisional tier could be derived and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ESCALATED_SIGNAL` (HTTP 422). A seat with `team_role` `INCIDENT_LEAD` must exist on the incident in state `ACKNOWLEDGED`, otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (HTTP 409). And when the provisional tier the server is about to compute turns out to be `SEV1`, a seat with `team_role` `LEGAL_COUNSEL` in state `ACKNOWLEDGED` must exist as well, or the same `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` is returned — the most severe crises may not be activated without counsel in the room. The posture must not be `FROZEN`, and `MONITORING` is the only from-state activation accepts: an incident already in `ACTIVATED`, as much as one already in `RESOLVED`, cannot be activated again, and **both** cases surface as `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (HTTP 409) — activation declares no separate already-activated identifier. These guards are evaluated in the §2.9 order rather than in the order narrated here, so the from-state and posture checks come first, then the unacknowledged-`INCIDENT_LEAD`-seat refusal (check 15), and only then the missing-escalated-signal refusal (check 24); the `SEV1` counsel-seat refusal is necessarily last of all, because the provisional tier it keys on can only be computed once at least one escalated signal is known to exist.

Severity is banded identically in both places it is computed: the tier is `SEV1` when the governing score is at least 90.00, otherwise `SEV2` when the score is at least 70.00, otherwise `SEV3` when the score is at least 50.00, and otherwise `SEV4`. What differs between the two computations is which score is banded. At activation, and on every later escalation while the incident is `ACTIVATED`, the **provisional** reading is used: the candidate set is every signal on the incident in state `LINKED` or `ESCALATED` — deliberately forward-looking, because a merely linked signal still represents live exposure — and the governing score is the largest `escalation_score` in that set. The result is written to `severity_tier` and `severity_score` with `severity_basis` set to `PROVISIONAL`. Because activation requires at least one escalated signal, the candidate set can never be empty.

Activation also fixes the holding-statement deadline. `holding_statement_due_at` is `activated_at` plus the service-level window belonging to the current `severity_tier`: half an hour for `SEV1`, one hour for `SEV2`, three hours for `SEV3`, and eight hours for `SEV4`. Note carefully that `activated_at` is the **only** base instant this deadline is ever computed from. When the tier tightens later in the response, the window shortens retroactively; the deadline is never re-based on the instant of the escalation that changed the tier. An incident is considered in breach — `sla_breached` latched true — once the current instant has reached or passed `holding_statement_due_at` while `holding_statement_published_at` is still null. Equality at the deadline is already a breach, not a grace moment. Only publishing a holding statement clears the latch.

Alongside the tier, activation computes the containment priority family, which is how the team decides where to spend attention. The exposure component is the sum of `virality_score` across every misinformation claim on the incident in state `DETECTED`, `TRIAGED`, `PLATFORM_ESCALATED` or `CORRECTION_ISSUED`, rounded half up to two decimals and then capped so it never exceeds 100.00. `base_containment_score` is seven tenths of the tier's point value plus three tenths of that exposure component, rounded half up to two decimals, where the tier point values are 100 for `SEV1`, 75 for `SEV2`, 50 for `SEV3` and 25 for `SEV4`. The base score is then placed into an amplification band: `HIGH` when it is at least 80.00, otherwise `MEDIUM` when it is at least 55.00, otherwise `LOW`. `amplification_rate_applied` is the rate belonging to that band — 0.20 for `HIGH`, 0.10 for `MEDIUM`, 0.05 for `LOW`. The amplification fee is the base score multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals, and `containment_priority_score` is the base score plus the fee, rounded half up to two decimals and capped so it never exceeds 150.00. **The evaluation order is pinned and the band is keyed on the pre-fee base score only.** The total is never re-banded after the fee is added: a base score of 52.50 sits in `LOW`, takes the 0.05 rate, and yields a total of 55.13 — a figure that falls inside the `MEDIUM` band while keeping the `LOW` rate. All three of `base_containment_score`, `amplification_rate_applied` and `containment_priority_score` are stored on the incident, and all three are refreshed at activation, on every signal escalation, on every claim triage and on every claim close.

Escalating a linked signal while the incident is already `ACTIVATED` therefore sets off a four-hop cascade, and every hop matters. First, `severity_tier`, `severity_score` and the `PROVISIONAL` basis are recomputed from the enlarged candidate set — this recomputation happens only when the incident is in `ACTIVATED`; escalating a signal on a still-`MONITORING` incident leaves the tier untouched. Second, because the tier may have changed, `holding_statement_due_at` is recomputed as `activated_at` plus the new tier's window. Third, because the deadline may have moved earlier, the breach test is re-evaluated: if the current instant now sits at or past the new `holding_statement_due_at` and `holding_statement_published_at` is still null, `sla_breached` latches true. Fourth, because `sla_breached` just became true, `comms_posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED` — unless `legal_hold_active` is already forcing `FROZEN`, in which case the posture precedence of §2.6 keeps it `FROZEN`. The containment family is recomputed as well, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Time does not advance by itself. An operator checkpoint — available to the program administrator, the incident lead and the communications lead — advances every time-driven consequence at the current logical clock reading for one incident. It declares no from-state of its own and is never refused on the response dimension: it may be run against an incident sitting in `MONITORING`, `ACTIVATED` or `RESOLVED` alike, and on a file whose deadline fields are still null each of its passes simply finds nothing to do while the closing recomputation and version bump still happen. It latches `sla_breached` when the current instant has reached `holding_statement_due_at` and no holding statement has been published. It expires approval tasks that have outrun their review deadline, pushing their statements into the revision loop and thereby blocking the notices those statements carry. It re-scores every prepared material whose `scenario_code` equals the incident's `scenario_code`, moving any `READY` asset whose decayed score has fallen below 60.00 into `STALE`. Finally it recomputes `comms_posture` and increments the incident's `version` by 1. This checkpoint is one of the three operations a `FROZEN` posture does not block.

### 3.4. Legal Privilege as an Independent Dimension

An incident advances along two state dimensions that do not constrain each other. `response_state` tracks the response itself, running `MONITORING → ACTIVATED → RESOLVED`. `privilege_state` tracks the legal posture, running `NON_PRIVILEGED → PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED → PRIVILEGE_WAIVED`, with `PRIVILEGE_WAIVED` terminal. Privilege may be asserted while the incident is still `MONITORING` and waived while it is `ACTIVATED`; neither privilege transition ever changes `response_state`, and neither response transition ever changes `privilege_state`.

Asserting privilege is reserved to in-house counsel and is the sharpest instrument in the system. The payload carries a `privilege_basis` — the text recording why the assertion holds — and the incident must currently be `NON_PRIVILEGED` and not yet `RESOLVED`. The resolved case is tested first and answers `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. A file that has already moved off `NON_PRIVILEGED` — whether it sits in `PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED` or has been carried on to `PRIVILEGE_WAIVED` — is refused with `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), the same identifier the posture gate itself uses, because the privilege dimension declares no state-invalid error of its own; the two conditions are in fact one test, so a `PRIVILEGE_WAIVED` incident whose posture is perfectly `OPEN` still answers `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Two authorisation conditions apply on top of the role check: the acting `TeamMember` must have `privilege_cleared` true, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 403); and that same member must hold a seat on this incident with `team_role` `LEGAL_COUNSEL` in state `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (HTTP 409). Counsel who has not taken their seat cannot assert privilege over a file.

On success the incident advances `NON_PRIVILEGED → PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED`, `legal_hold_active` is set to true, the supplied `privilege_basis` is stored, and `comms_posture` recomputes to `FROZEN` — the highest-precedence posture rule. Three further hops follow, and they are where privilege assertion becomes visibly destructive. First, every approval task on this incident's statements that is still `PENDING` and whose assignee has `privilege_cleared` false transitions to `VOIDED` with `decided_at` stamped at the current instant; approvers outside the circle lose their pending decisions outright. Second, because those tasks were voided, `open_approval_task_count` is recomputed. Third, because a statement in `IN_REVIEW` can lose every one of its outstanding tasks this way, any such statement that is left with zero remaining `PENDING` tasks and fewer `APPROVED` tasks than its `quorum_required` transitions to `REVISION_REQUESTED`. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Waiving privilege reverses the hold. The payload carries a mandatory non-empty `waiver_reason`, which the server validates but does not persist — the incident record carries no waiver field, so the text is used only to force the caller to state a ground. The incident must currently be `PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED`, and any other privilege state is refused with `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) exactly as an out-of-state assertion is. Unlike assertion, waiver carries no response-dimension check whatever, so a hold may be lifted on an incident that has already reached `RESOLVED`; and unlike assertion it demands no `ACKNOWLEDGED` `LEGAL_COUNSEL` seat — clearance alone suffices. The acting member must again have `privilege_cleared` true or be refused with `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED`. The incident advances `PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED → PRIVILEGE_WAIVED`, `legal_hold_active` becomes false, the `privilege_basis` recorded at assertion is left in place rather than cleared, and the posture is recomputed from scratch under the precedence of §2.6: it becomes `RESTRICTED` if `sla_breached` is true or `blocked_notice_count` is at least 1, `OPEN` if neither holds, and `FROZEN` regardless if the incident has already reached `RESOLVED`. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1. Nothing the assertion destroyed is restored by the waiver: approval tasks it voided stay `VOIDED`, statements it pushed into `REVISION_REQUESTED` stay there, and notices those statements blocked stay `BLOCKED` until the statement is approved again — so a `RESTRICTED` posture may well be what the waiver reveals. Waiving privilege is the second of the three operations a `FROZEN` posture does not block — otherwise a legal hold would be impossible to lift.

### 3.5. Message Development, Quorum Routing and the Revision Loop

Drafting is the communications lead's exclusive province. A draft names the `incident_id` it belongs to, a `statement_type` of `HOLDING`, `UPDATE`, `CORRECTION` or `FINAL`, and a `body` of at most 4000 non-empty characters. Two optional inputs shape everything downstream: `privilege_marked`, a boolean defaulting to false that is true when the draft carries privileged legal analysis, and `supersedes_statement_id`, a self-reference to the earlier message this one replaces, null for the first message on an incident. The mandatory `expected_incident_version` described in §2.8 travels with every draft creation. The incident must be `ACTIVATED` — drafting against a still-`MONITORING` file yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED`, and against a `RESOLVED` one `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED` — and the posture must not be `FROZEN`. A new draft lands in state `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `revision_locked` false, `quorum_required` 0, `approvals_recorded` 0, nulls in `submitted_at`, `review_deadline_at`, `approved_at`, `published_at`, `approval_latency_minutes` and `withdraw_reason`, and `drafted_by_member_id` pinned to the drafting lead's `X-User-ID`. That pin is never rewritten, not even by a later revision, which is exactly what keeps segregation of duties intact across the whole revision cycle. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Submitting for review advances the statement into `IN_REVIEW` and is accepted from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; from any other state the answer is `ERR_STATEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and a statement whose revision budget is exhausted — which at submission means exactly that its `revision_locked` flag has latched true — is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Per the §2.9 ordering that refusal is evaluated *after* the approver-pool check described below, so a locked statement whose required seats are also unfillable answers `ERR_APPROVER_UNAVAILABLE` rather than `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. The incident must be `ACTIVATED`, giving `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` otherwise, and the posture must not be `FROZEN`, giving `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` otherwise.

The quorum is derived at the moment of submission and tightens with severity. The list of required approver seats always begins with `INCIDENT_LEAD`. `LEGAL_COUNSEL` is appended when the incident's `severity_tier` is `SEV1` or `SEV2`, or when the statement's `privilege_marked` flag is true — meaning a privileged draft always draws counsel in, no matter how mild the incident. `EXEC_SPONSOR` is appended when and only when the tier is `SEV1`. `quorum_required` is the number of seats in that list, so it is 1, 2 or 3. The order of the list is fixed as written — incident lead first, then counsel, then executive sponsor — and approval tasks are created in exactly that order, which is also the order in which they are returned by the approval-task listing. Alongside the quorum, `submitted_at` is stamped with the current instant and `review_deadline_at` is set to `submitted_at` plus the review window belonging to the incident's current `severity_tier`: fifteen minutes for `SEV1`, half an hour for `SEV2`, an hour and a half for `SEV3`, and four hours for `SEV4`. Both are recomputed on every submission, so each new round gets a fresh clock. `approvals_recorded` resets to 0, `open_approval_task_count` rises by exactly the number of tasks created, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Choosing who receives each task is a server-side selection, never a client input. For each required seat the server assembles a candidate pool of crisis-team seats on the statement's own incident whose `team_role` equals the required seat and whose state is `ACKNOWLEDGED`; when the statement's `privilege_marked` flag is true, the pool is further narrowed to seats whose member has `privilege_cleared` true, because a privileged draft may only be decided inside the circle. Candidates are then ranked by `decision_authority`, with `FULL` ranking above `LIMITED`, and ties are broken by the seat's own `created_at` ascending — never by identifier; and because the logical clock lets several seats share one `created_at`, a tie that survives that comparison is broken by the order in which the seats were created. The first candidate after ranking and tie-break becomes the task's `assignee_member_id`. If the pool for **any** required seat is empty, the entire submission fails with `ERR_APPROVER_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) and no approval task is created for any seat at all; the operation is atomic. One subtlety is deliberate: the drafter is **not** filtered out of the candidate pool. If the only `ACKNOWLEDGED` holder of a required seat happens to be the statement's `drafted_by_member_id`, the task is still created and assigned to that member, and the segregation-of-duties refusal surfaces later, when they try to decide it. Such a round can only end by sweep expiry or by withdrawal of the statement, which is the intended pressure to seat the team properly in the first place.

Each created task records the `statement_id` it guards, the routed `assignee_member_id`, the `required_team_role` it must be decided from — one of `INCIDENT_LEAD`, `LEGAL_COUNSEL` or `EXEC_SPONSOR` — and the `revision_round` of the statement at the moment of creation. `is_veto_seat` is true for the counsel task and false for the others; it is a record of provenance, not a special power, as the next paragraph makes clear. The task begins in `PENDING` with a null `decision_comment` and a null `decided_at`.

Recording a decision requires a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and accepts an optional `decision_comment`. Six conditions guard both outcomes identically: the task must still be `PENDING`, or `ERR_APPROVAL_TASK_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409); the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the task's `assignee_member_id`, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403); the statement must be `IN_REVIEW`, or `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW` (HTTP 409); the current instant must not have passed `review_deadline_at`, so a decision made exactly at the deadline still counts and anything later yields `ERR_REVIEW_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 409); the caller must not be the statement's `drafted_by_member_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403); and when the statement's `privilege_marked` flag is true the acting member must have `privilege_cleared` true, or `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 403). Those six are the complete guard set, evaluated in the §2.9 order rather than the order just listed: recording a decision is the one child mutation that tests neither the incident's `comms_posture` nor its `response_state`, so a `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED` posture — and even a `RESOLVED` response — leaves an outstanding decision perfectly recordable. The omitted `decision_comment` is stored as null.

An approval advances the task `PENDING → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at`, stores the `decision_comment`, recomputes the statement's `approvals_recorded` as the number of that round's tasks now in `APPROVED`, and recomputes the incident's `open_approval_task_count`. When no task of the current round remains `PENDING` and the count of `APPROVED` tasks equals `quorum_required`, the statement itself advances `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`. That transition stamps `approved_at`, latches `approval_latency_minutes` as the whole number of complete minutes between the current round's `submitted_at` and `approved_at` — the fractional remainder is discarded — returns every stakeholder notice that carries this statement and is sitting in `BLOCKED` back to `QUEUED` (the unblocking cascade, like the blocking one, is keyed on the notice's `statement_id` alone and not on the notice's own `incident_id`), recomputes `blocked_notice_count`, recomputes `open_approval_task_count`, and recomputes `comms_posture`. That last recomputation is how a `RESTRICTED` incident can return to `OPEN` once the blockage clears. Unlike a rejection, an approval never increments the incident's `version` — the counter recomputations it performs are not counted as a mutation of the incident record, whether or not the statement reaches quorum — while the statement's own `version` does increment on every approval, including one that merely raises `approvals_recorded` without advancing the state.

A rejection is far more consequential, and the rule is uniform across seats. The task itself advances `PENDING → REJECTED` with `decided_at` stamped and the `decision_comment` stored, and the statement advances `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` **immediately**, regardless of how many sibling tasks in the round have already been approved. Veto semantics apply to every seat; `is_veto_seat` merely records that the rejection came from legal rather than conferring the power. Four hops follow. Every sibling task of the same `revision_round` still in `PENDING` transitions to `VOIDED` with `decided_at` stamped, and `approvals_recorded` is recomputed as the number of that round's tasks now standing in `APPROVED` — so siblings that had already approved before the rejection keep counting toward it, and the figure is not reset to 0 until the statement is actually revised into a new round. Because the round has collapsed, `open_approval_task_count` is recomputed back down to its pre-submission value — the conserved-ledger invariant of §2.7 in action. Because the statement is no longer approvable, every stakeholder notice carrying it that sits in `QUEUED` transitions to `BLOCKED`, and `blocked_notice_count` is recomputed. And because `blocked_notice_count` has become at least 1, `comms_posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`, unless `legal_hold_active` is forcing `FROZEN`. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Silence is treated as rejection. When the operator checkpoint runs while a `PENDING` task's statement is `IN_REVIEW` and the current instant is strictly past `review_deadline_at`, the task advances `PENDING → EXPIRED` with `decided_at` stamped. Because every task of a round shares its statement's single `review_deadline_at`, the trigger is satisfied by all of them at once and the checkpoint expires them all: **every** `PENDING` task of that statement's current `revision_round` advances `PENDING → EXPIRED`, in creation order, not merely the first one found. The statement is then pushed `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` exactly as a rejection would push it — its sibling-voiding pass simply finds nothing still `PENDING` to void, so a task the checkpoint reached ends as `EXPIRED` rather than `VOIDED`. This is the time-pressure escalation the review windows exist to create. Separately, tasks reach `VOIDED` in three other ways: withdrawal of their statement, the revision-requested cascade above, and the privilege assertion that voids assignees outside the circle. In every voiding case `decided_at` is stamped and `open_approval_task_count` is recomputed.

Revision closes the loop. Rewriting a statement is permitted only from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and it replaces the `body` wholesale with the supplied text — `body` is the operation's only field, it is mandatory, and it is measured against the same at-most-4000 non-empty-characters rule that governs it at creation. The revision budget is three rounds: `revision_locked` must be false and `revision_round` must be strictly less than 3, or the request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The posture must not be `FROZEN`. On success the statement returns `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, `revision_round` increases by 1, `revision_locked` latches to true the moment `revision_round` reaches 3, `quorum_required` resets to 0, and `approvals_recorded` is recomputed for the new round — which lands it at 0, because the freshly incremented round owns no tasks yet. `submitted_at` and `review_deadline_at` are left standing as the previous round left them, to be overwritten at the next submission. The statement's `version` is incremented by 1; the incident's is not, because revision moves none of the incident's derived counters. The full cycle is therefore `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` and so on, bounded at a `revision_round` of 3, and each new round re-derives the quorum from the tier and privilege flag as they stand at that moment and fans out a completely fresh set of approval tasks.

### 3.6. Publication, Withdrawal and Stakeholder Sequencing

Publishing is what makes a message real. A statement advances `APPROVED → PUBLISHED` when the incident is `ACTIVATED` — otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` — the posture is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` — and, when the statement's `privilege_marked` flag is true, the publishing member has `privilege_cleared` true, failing which the answer is `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED`. On success `published_at` is stamped. Two conditional effects follow. If the `statement_type` is `HOLDING` and the incident's `holding_statement_published_at` is still null, that field is set to the publication instant and `sla_breached` is cleared to false — publishing the first holding statement is the only thing in the system that clears the breach latch. And if `supersedes_statement_id` is set and the statement it names is itself in `PUBLISHED`, that predecessor advances `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`. The posture is recomputed and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Withdrawal exists because messages sometimes have to be taken back, and it is available both to the communications lead and to counsel acting on legal grounds. The payload carries a `withdraw_reason`, which is stored on the statement. Withdrawal is accepted from `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `PUBLISHED`; from `SUPERSEDED` or an already-`WITHDRAWN` statement it is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The posture must not be `FROZEN`. Two withdrawal paths carry distinct compensations, and both must be implemented. Withdrawing from `IN_REVIEW` advances `IN_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN` and compensates the review round: every approval task of the current round still in `PENDING` transitions to `VOIDED`, and `open_approval_task_count` is recomputed back to its exact pre-submission value, preserving the conserved-ledger invariant. Withdrawing from `PUBLISHED` advances `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN` and compensates the downstream audience work instead: every stakeholder notice carrying that statement in state `QUEUED` or `BLOCKED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal string `AUTO_STATEMENT_WITHDRAWN`, and `blocked_notice_count` is recomputed. Additionally, when the withdrawn statement's `statement_type` is `HOLDING` and the incident's `holding_statement_published_at` equals that statement's `published_at`, the incident's `holding_statement_published_at` reverts to null and `sla_breached` is re-evaluated against the current instant — pulling back the holding statement can therefore put the incident straight back into breach. In every path — including a withdrawal from `DRAFT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, which carries no compensation of its own — the posture is recomputed and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Stakeholder notices are the delivery vehicles. A notice is queued by naming the `incident_id`, the `statement_id` of the approved message it will carry, the `audience` — one of `EMPLOYEE`, `CUSTOMER`, `REGULATOR`, `INVESTOR` or `MEDIA` — and an `estimated_reach` giving the expected audience size in persons, which must be at least 0. An optional `embargo_until` names the earliest instant the notice may go out; null means no embargo. The incident must be `ACTIVATED`. Queuing imposes no further constraint on the named statement beyond its resolving: the statement may sit in any state and it need not belong to the notice's own `incident_id`, because the publication test is deferred entirely to release. Queuing moves none of the incident's derived counters, so the incident's `version` is left untouched. Every notice starts in `QUEUED` with a `reach_contribution` of 0.00, a null `released_at`, `priority_override_used` false and a null `cancel_reason`. The server derives `sequence_rank` from the audience and it is never client-supplied: `EMPLOYEE` ranks 1, `CUSTOMER` 2, `REGULATOR` 3, `INVESTOR` 4 and `MEDIA` 5, so `sequence_rank` always sits between 1 and 5.

That rank is the sequencing contract: employees hear first, then customers, then regulators, then investors, and media last. Releasing a notice advances it `QUEUED → RELEASED` and is guarded by six conditions, evaluated in the §2.9 order. The notice must be `QUEUED`, or `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The incident must be `ACTIVATED`, or `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` (HTTP 409). The posture must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The statement the notice carries must be in `PUBLISHED`, or `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). Either `embargo_until` is null or the current instant has reached or passed it, otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). And every notice on the same incident whose `sequence_rank` is strictly lower must already be settled, meaning in `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED`; otherwise the release is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).

The sequence has exactly one legitimate bypass, and it exists because statutory notification deadlines outrank internal courtesy. The statutory override applies only when all four of the following hold at once: the request sets `priority_override` — the release payload's one field, an optional boolean defaulting to false — to true, the notice's `audience` is `REGULATOR`, the acting role is `LegalCounsel` — the communications lead cannot invoke it even though it may otherwise release notices — and the current instant has reached or passed the incident's `activated_at` plus two hours. When the override applies, the lower-ranked employee and customer notices may still be unsettled and the release proceeds, with `priority_override_used` recorded as true on the released notice. That flag records *use*, not mere eligibility: it is set true only where the override was actually needed, meaning at least one lower-ranked notice really was unsettled at that instant. A release that meets all four override conditions but finds nothing unsettled below it simply passes the ordinary sequencing rule and records `priority_override_used` as false. If any one of the four conditions fails, the ordinary sequencing rule governs and the rejection is `ERR_NOTICE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`.

Release computes reach. Each audience carries a weight — 1.00 for `EMPLOYEE`, 1.25 for `CUSTOMER`, 1.50 for `REGULATOR`, 1.75 for `INVESTOR` and 2.00 for `MEDIA` — and a released notice's `reach_contribution` is its audience weight multiplied by its `estimated_reach`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up to two decimals. Notices that are not `RELEASED` carry a `reach_contribution` of 0.00. The incident's `stakeholder_reach_index` is then rebuilt from scratch on every release: taking only the notices on that incident in state `RELEASED` — `QUEUED`, `BLOCKED` and `CANCELLED` notices are excluded entirely — ordered by `released_at` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, each notice's contribution is rounded to two decimals **first**, those rounded contributions are then summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimals again. This ordering is pinned and it changes the cents: three notices whose unrounded contributions are 1.006, 1.00625 and 1.0065 yield 3.03 under the pinned per-notice-first order, whereas summing the unrounded values and rounding once would give 3.02. The pinned answer is 3.03. Release also recomputes `released_notice_count`, the number of that incident's notices in state `RELEASED`, and increments the incident's `version` by 1.

Cancelling a notice takes a `cancel_reason` and is accepted from either `QUEUED` or `BLOCKED`, with the posture not `FROZEN`; from any other state it yields `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`. The reason is stored, `blocked_notice_count` is recomputed, `comms_posture` is recomputed, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1. Cancelling the last blocked notice is a common way out of a `RESTRICTED` posture: when `blocked_notice_count` falls to 0 and `sla_breached` is false, the posture returns to `OPEN`. The two system-driven notice movements complete the picture, and they are symmetric: a `QUEUED` notice transitions to `BLOCKED` when the statement it carries is pushed into `REVISION_REQUESTED`, recomputing `blocked_notice_count` and driving the posture to `RESTRICTED`; and a `BLOCKED` notice returns to `QUEUED` when that same statement is approved again in a later revision round, again recomputing `blocked_notice_count` and the posture. `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for a notice.

### 3.7. Spokesperson Designation, Media Training and Interview Preparation

A spokesperson's calendar is the scarcest resource in a crisis, and the booking rules protect it. Booking an interview is the communications lead's job and requires the `incident_id` the interview addresses, the `spokesperson_member_id` of the designated speaker, the `channel_id` of the outlet conducting it, an `outlet_name` of at most 160 non-empty characters naming the outlet or programme in free text, and the two endpoints of the booking, `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`. Those two instants describe a closed interval whose end must be strictly after its start; an end at or before the start is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An optional `anticipated_question_count` records how many questions the preparation pack anticipates; it defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. The incident must be `ACTIVATED`. The server derives `duration_minutes` as the whole number of complete minutes between the two endpoints, discarding any fractional remainder, and a booking always spans at least 1 minute. A new booking lands in state `SCHEDULED` with a null `confirmed_at`, a null `cancel_reason` and `high_risk_answers_approved_count` of 0. Booking moves none of the incident's derived counters — a merely `SCHEDULED` interview is invisible to `confirmed_interview_count` — so the incident's `version` is left untouched, and so is the spokesperson's.

Double booking is prevented with a preparation buffer built into the comparison. A booking is refused when the same spokesperson already has any interview in state `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` that clashes once both intervals are padded by thirty minutes on each side. Concretely, the clash test is satisfied when the requested `scheduled_start_at` is earlier than the existing interview's `scheduled_end_at` plus thirty minutes **and** the requested `scheduled_end_at` plus thirty minutes is later than the existing interview's `scheduled_start_at`; when both halves hold, the request is refused with `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409). A gap of exactly thirty minutes between two bookings is therefore permitted, since neither strict comparison holds at the boundary. Note that a merely `SCHEDULED` interview still occupies the calendar for this test even though it contributes nothing to `scheduled_interview_minutes`, which tracks confirmed load only.

Confirmation is where media training and question preparation are enforced. The payload supplies `high_risk_answers_approved_count`, the number of high-risk answers legal has signed off, which must be at least 0. Four conditions guard the advance `SCHEDULED → CONFIRMED`. The interview must be in `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The spokesperson's `media_training_status` must be `CURRENT` **and** their `media_training_expires_at` must be strictly after the current instant, so training that lapses exactly at the confirmation instant does not count; failure gives `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT` (HTTP 422). The preparation pack must be complete, meaning `anticipated_question_count` is at least 8 and `high_risk_answers_approved_count` is at least 3; failure gives `ERR_QUESTION_PACK_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). And the incident's `comms_posture` must be exactly `OPEN` — this is the one operation for which a `RESTRICTED` posture is as fatal as a `FROZEN` one, and either way the rejection is `ERR_COMMS_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). On success `confirmed_at` is stamped, the spokesperson's `scheduled_interview_minutes` increases by exactly this interview's `duration_minutes`, the incident's `confirmed_interview_count` is recomputed as the number of that incident's interviews in state `CONFIRMED`, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

Cancellation may be initiated either by the communications lead or by the spokesperson standing themselves down, and it takes a `cancel_reason` that is stored on the booking. No ownership binding applies here: any caller holding the `Spokesperson` role may cancel any booking, not merely one where they are the recorded `spokesperson_member_id`. The compensation depends on which state the booking is leaving, and the two branches must be treated with equal care. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` advances `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, decreases the spokesperson's `scheduled_interview_minutes` by exactly this interview's `duration_minutes` — restoring the spokesperson-load ledger to where it stood before confirmation — recomputes the incident's `confirmed_interview_count`, and increments the incident's `version` by 1. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` advances `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and frees the interval for later overlap checks, but leaves `scheduled_interview_minutes` unchanged, precisely because that ledger only ever tracked confirmed load; with no counter moving on either side, that branch also leaves the incident's `version` and the spokesperson's `version` untouched. `CANCELLED` is terminal for an interview.

### 3.8. Social Listening: Misinformation, Platform Escalation and Corrections

Only a social listening analyst may log a misinformation claim. The record names the `incident_id` the false claim attaches to, the `channel_id` of the platform hosting it and holding the correction capacity, a `claim_text` of at most 500 non-empty characters stating the false or misleading assertion, and a `virality_score` between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals expressing spread intensity. The incident must be `ACTIVATED`. A new claim begins in `DETECTED` with a null `correction_statement_id`, null `correction_window_start` and `correction_window_end`, a `correction_window_minutes` of 0, a null `resolution_branch`, `takedown_confirmed` false, `corrected` false and a null `outcome_note`, and the incident's `open_claim_count` — the number of its claims in `DETECTED`, `TRIAGED`, `PLATFORM_ESCALATED` or `CORRECTION_ISSUED` — is recomputed. That recomputation is not itself treated as a mutation of the incident, so logging a claim leaves the incident's `version` untouched; nor is the containment family re-scored, which waits until the claim is triaged.

Triage is the analyst's assessment step and the point at which the claim starts costing the incident attention. It advances `DETECTED → TRIAGED`, refusing anything else with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); the incident must be `ACTIVATED`, giving `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` otherwise, and the posture must not be `FROZEN`. On success `open_claim_count` is recomputed and the containment family — `base_containment_score`, `amplification_rate_applied` and `containment_priority_score` — is recomputed, because this claim's `virality_score` now counts toward the exposure component described in §3.3. The incident's `version` is incremented by 1.

From `TRIAGED` the claim forks, and the two branches are genuinely different responses rather than variants of one. Both eventually join at `CLOSED`, but their close-time effects diverge.

The **platform branch** escalates to the channel's trust-and-safety contact and is open to the analyst or the communications lead. It advances `TRIAGED → PLATFORM_ESCALATED` under two substantive conditions: the channel's `escalation_path_state` must be `VERIFIED`, since an unverified path has nowhere to send the escalation, and failure gives `ERR_ESCALATION_PATH_UNVERIFIED` (HTTP 422); and the claim's `virality_score` must be at least 60.00, because trust-and-safety escalation is reserved for claims with real spread, and failure gives `ERR_VIRALITY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). On success the channel's `open_escalation_count` is recomputed and the channel's `version` is incremented by 1. The incident side is left alone: `open_claim_count` does not move because `TRIAGED` and `PLATFORM_ESCALATED` both count as open, the containment family is not re-scored, and the incident's `version` does not advance. The branch the claim has taken is settled at this point, though `resolution_branch` itself is written only when the claim closes.

The **correction branch** binds a published correction message and reserves airtime on the channel; it is exclusive to the communications lead. The payload carries `correction_statement_id`, `correction_window_start`, `correction_window_end` and the mandatory `expected_channel_version` of §2.8. The claim must be `TRIAGED`. The stale-version check of §2.8 is evaluated before the from-state, referential and capacity checks, so a stale `expected_channel_version` presented against a claim still sitting in `DETECTED` answers `ERR_STALE_CHANNEL_VERSION` rather than `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The referenced statement must exist and must simultaneously have `statement_type` `CORRECTION`, be in state `PUBLISHED`, and belong to the same incident as the claim; any of those failing gives `ERR_CORRECTION_STATEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). The requested `correction_window_end` must be strictly after `correction_window_start`, or the request is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). And when the referenced statement's `privilege_marked` flag is true, the acting member must have `privilege_cleared` true, or `ERR_PRIVILEGE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 403).

Channel capacity is the last gate on that branch. Correction windows are **half-open** intervals: a window covers its start instant and every instant up to but not including its end. The server counts the claims on the same channel already in state `CORRECTION_ISSUED` whose window overlaps the requested one — overlap meaning the existing window's start is earlier than the requested end and the existing window's end is later than the requested start — and that count must be strictly less than the channel's `max_concurrent_corrections`. If it is not, the request is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_CORRECTION_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409). Because the arithmetic is half-open, a window that starts at exactly the instant another one ends does not overlap it and does not consume capacity. On success the claim advances `TRIAGED → CORRECTION_ISSUED`, the three supplied values are stored, `correction_window_minutes` is derived as the whole number of complete minutes between the window's start and end with any remainder discarded, the channel's `reserved_correction_minutes` increases by exactly that many minutes, and the channel's `version` is incremented by 1 — unconditionally, even for a sub-minute window whose `correction_window_minutes` floors to 0 and therefore moves the ledger not at all. The incident's `version` is again left untouched, since `CORRECTION_ISSUED` counts as open exactly as `TRIAGED` did.

Closing joins the fork and takes an `outcome_note` recording how it ended. Closing from `PLATFORM_ESCALATED` sets `resolution_branch` to `PLATFORM`, sets `takedown_confirmed` to true, leaves `corrected` false, recomputes the channel's `open_escalation_count` and increments the channel's `version` by 1. Closing from `CORRECTION_ISSUED` sets `resolution_branch` to `CORRECTION`, sets `corrected` to true — which is what makes the claim count toward correction efficacy in the retrospective — leaves `takedown_confirmed` false, and compensates the airtime ledger by decreasing the channel's `reserved_correction_minutes` by exactly this claim's `correction_window_minutes`, restoring it to its pre-correction value, again incrementing the channel's `version` by 1. Both branches then recompute the incident's `open_claim_count`, recompute the containment family because the closed claim no longer contributes exposure, and increment the incident's `version` by 1. Closing from any state other than `PLATFORM_ESCALATED` or `CORRECTION_ISSUED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.9. Resolution, Post-Crisis Assessment and Playbook Updates

Resolution is deliberately hard to reach, because a crisis that is closed on paper while notices are still queued is not closed at all. Resolving an incident supplies the observed `reputation_index_after` and advances `ACTIVATED → RESOLVED`. An incident that was never activated cannot be resolved: attempting it from `MONITORING` is a declared rejection with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` (HTTP 409) and no state change, and attempting it on an already-`RESOLVED` incident gives `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (HTTP 409). Beyond that, two closure conditions apply: the incident must have no stakeholder notice in `QUEUED` or `BLOCKED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_NOTICES_BLOCK_RESOLUTION` (HTTP 409); and its `open_claim_count` must be exactly 0, meaning every misinformation claim has reached `CLOSED`, or the refusal is `ERR_OPEN_CLAIMS_BLOCK_RESOLUTION` (HTTP 409). Those two, tested in that order after the from-state, are the only business gates resolution carries: resolution is deliberately **not** posture-gated, so an incident sitting under a legal hold — posture `FROZEN`, `privilege_state` `PRIVILEGED_ASSERTED` — may be resolved without waiving privilege first, and the privilege dimension is left exactly where it stood.

On success the supplied `reputation_index_after` is stored, `resolved_at` is stamped with the current instant, and the **final** severity reading is computed and written to `final_severity_tier` and `final_severity_score` with `severity_basis` moving to `FINAL`. The final reading uses the same tier bands as the provisional one — at least 90.00 is `SEV1`, at least 70.00 is `SEV2`, at least 50.00 is `SEV3`, otherwise `SEV4` — but bands a different score over a different candidate set. Only signals in state `ESCALATED` are considered, ordered by `detected_at` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; merely `LINKED` signals are excluded, in contrast to the forward-looking provisional set. The peak is the largest `escalation_score` in that set. The average is the sum of the `escalation_score` values divided by the number of signals in the set, rounded half up to two decimals. The final score is six tenths of the peak plus four tenths of that rounded average, rounded half up to two decimals. Identical signal data can therefore land in a different tier than the provisional reading did — whenever a linked-but-never-escalated signal held the peak, or whenever the escalated set is not uniform — and both readings are retained on the incident side by side so the retrospective can see the divergence.

Resolution also opens the retrospective and stands the team down. One post-crisis review is created for the incident in state `OPEN`, snapshotting the `incident_id`, both `reputation_index_before` and `reputation_index_after` as they stand at resolution, an `exposure_factor`, a `total_claim_count` giving the number of misinformation claims ever logged on the incident, and a `corrected_claim_count` giving how many of those had `corrected` true. The `exposure_factor` is the incident's `stakeholder_reach_index` divided by one hundred, capped so it never exceeds 0.50, and rounded to four decimals. It is a snapshot: no live child row is ever re-read later. The review begins with a null `reputation_delta_raw`, a null `reputation_delta_adjusted`, a null `scoring_method`, a null `reported_delta`, a `playbook_update_count` of 0 and a null `scored_at`.

Three cascade hops complete the resolution, and each depends on the one before it. First, every crisis-team seat on this incident in state `ACKNOWLEDGED` transitions `ACKNOWLEDGED → STOOD_DOWN` with `stood_down_at` stamped at the current instant, and each affected member's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed. Second, because the `SPOKESPERSON` seat has stood down, every interview on this incident whose `spokesperson_member_id` is that seat's member and which sits in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal string `AUTO_CASCADE_INCIDENT_RESOLVED`. Third, because those interviews have left `CONFIRMED`, that spokesperson's `scheduled_interview_minutes` is decreased by each cancelled interview's `duration_minutes`, and the incident's `confirmed_interview_count` is recomputed. The member-side recomputations of the first and third hops are performed together, once per affected member, over the union of the members who stood down and the spokespeople whose bookings were cancelled: both counters are rebuilt in a single pass, so a spokesperson who both loses a seat and loses a booking has their `version` advance by exactly 1 rather than 2, and a member whose figures happen not to move is not bumped at all. Finally `comms_posture` recomputes to `FROZEN`, since a `RESOLVED` response forces the highest-precedence posture rule, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1. `RESOLVED` is terminal on the response dimension.

Scoring the retrospective is the executive sponsor's exclusive act and the third operation a `FROZEN` posture does not block. The payload supplies a `scoring_method` of either `RAW` or `ADJUSTED` and a `playbook_update_count` recording how many playbook amendments the review produced, which is mandatory and must be at least 0. The review must be in `OPEN`, or `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and its incident must be in `RESOLVED`, or the refusal reuses the response-dimension error `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVATED` (HTTP 409) rather than minting an identifier of its own — checked in that order, so an already-`SCORED` review on a still-`ACTIVATED` incident answers `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. Both delta variants are **always** computed and both are always stored, whichever method is chosen. The raw delta is the review's snapshotted `reputation_index_after` less its snapshotted `reputation_index_before`, rounded half up to two decimals. The correction ratio is exactly 0.0000 when `total_claim_count` is 0, and otherwise `corrected_claim_count` divided by `total_claim_count`, rounded to four decimals. The efficacy bonus is a maximum of 2.50 multiplied by that correction ratio, rounded half up to two decimals. The adjusted delta is the raw delta multiplied by one less the `exposure_factor`, plus the efficacy bonus, rounded half up to two decimals. The raw value is stored in `reputation_delta_raw` and the adjusted value in `reputation_delta_adjusted`. `reported_delta` is then the raw variant when `scoring_method` is `RAW` and the adjusted variant when it is `ADJUSTED`. The two variants diverge materially on identical reputation indices: at an `exposure_factor` of 0.0303 with one of two claims corrected, a raw delta of -7.30 reports as -5.83 when adjusted, because heavy media exposure damps the swing and successful corrections earn part of the bonus back.

The review then advances `OPEN → SCORED`, `scored_at` is stamped, the supplied `playbook_update_count` is stored, and the ledger is synchronised: the incident's `reputation_delta_reported` is set to the review's `reported_delta` so the incident record and the retrospective never disagree, and the incident's `version` is incremented by 1. `SCORED` is terminal for a review.
