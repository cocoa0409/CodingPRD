# Employer On-Site & Near-Site Clinic API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Large employers increasingly buy primary care, occupational health and wellness services directly, either by hosting a clinic on their own premises or by contracting a shared near-site clinic alongside other employers. The commercial promise is straightforward — a visit delivered in the workplace clinic is cheaper than the same visit bought through the health plan, and the employer sees the saving. The operational reality is not straightforward at all. The employer funds the clinic and needs utilization and return-on-investment reporting to justify the spend, but the employer must never be able to read an individual's clinical record. The clinic vendor is paid either per completed visit or per staffed hour, against a contractual minimum guarantee and a fixed visit quota, and both sides need a single trustworthy count of what actually happened in a period. Wellness incentives are paid through payroll and are capped by regulation as a percentage of the cost of coverage, with a separate, higher cap when a tobacco-cessation component is in play, and a participant who cannot meet an outcome standard is legally entitled to a reasonable alternative that grants the same credit retroactively.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that program end to end. It covers the eligible population and the enrollment file that produces it, the clinic site calendar and its slot capacity, vendor contracts and their quotas, provider credentialing and staffed shifts, appointment booking through check-in, the encounter itself on two independent axes (what happened clinically, and what — if anything — the employer is allowed to learn), biometric screening and health-risk scoring, specialist referral with steerage tiers and navigation service levels, reasonable alternative standards, incentive awards through payroll settlement, and the reporting period that freezes the whole picture so vendor fees can be trued up and a utilization and ROI report can be produced.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is that the **confidentiality wall** is not a filter applied at the end of a query — it is an access gate evaluated near the top of a single global validation ladder, ahead of even the question of whether the record being asked about exists, because a "not found" answer to an employer-side caller would itself disclose that a clinical record does or does not exist. The second is that **the reporting period is the unit of financial truth**: once a period is finalized, records dated inside it stop accepting writes, its visits lock, its vendor contracts true up, and the money that was booked against it cannot drift. Every collection in this API is mounted flat at the service root, so a resource is addressed as its collection name and, where applicable, its identifier; there is no nesting of one collection inside another.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. No endpoint is anonymous, including the logical-clock endpoint.

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `ClinicManager`, `Clinician`, `CareNavigator`, `EmployerBenefitsAdmin`, `Participant` and `PayrollProcessor`. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles permitted to invoke it; a caller whose role is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. Two groupings recur throughout this document and are worth fixing now. The **employer-side roles** are exactly `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` and `PayrollProcessor` — these are the roles that answer to the paying employer, and they are the roles the confidentiality wall of §2.7 and the small-cell suppression of §3.9 exist to constrain. The **clinic-side roles** are exactly `Clinician`, `CareNavigator` and `ClinicManager`. `ProgramAdmin` belongs to neither group: it is the platform operator, it is never subject to the confidentiality wall, and it receives reporting figures unsuppressed.

`X-User-ID` means different things to different roles, and the difference is what makes ownership checks possible.

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `approved_by_user_id` or `exported_by_user_id` on an `IncentiveAward` where a transition writes it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded where a transition writes it but never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollProcessor`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is recorded as `exported_by_user_id` when an award is exported to payroll, and is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Clinician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and that provider's `provider_function` must be `CLINICIAN`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's provider reference to `X-User-ID` — advancing an encounter, for instance, requires the visit's `provider_id` to equal `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CareNavigator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and that provider's `provider_function` must be `NAVIGATOR`. The segregation-of-duties rule of §3.7 compares `X-User-ID` against a referral's `raised_by_provider_id` on exactly this basis.
- When `X-User-Role` is `EmployerBenefitsAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employer`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employer` compare the resource's employer reference to `X-User-ID`, so a benefits administrator may load eligibility, enrol members, run incentives and read reports only for its own employer.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Participant` compare the resource's `participant_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a member may book, reschedule, cancel, self-attest and request alternatives only for itself.

A caller that presents a recognised role and is routed to an endpoint its role may use, but then acts outside the scope its identity grants — a `Participant` operating on another participant's record, an `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` operating on another employer's record, a `Clinician` whose backing `Provider` carries `provider_function` of `NAVIGATOR`, or a `CareNavigator` whose backing `Provider` carries `provider_function` of `CLINICIAN` — is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403.

Three capabilities are restricted more narrowly than the endpoint's role list alone suggests, and the restriction is enforced as an authorization matter with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. First, appointment cancellation is open to `Participant`, `CareNavigator` and `Clinician`, but only a `Clinician` may supply a `cancellation_reason` of `NO_SHOW`; a `Participant` or `CareNavigator` supplying that reason is refused. Second, health-assessment creation is open to `Clinician` and `Participant`, but only a `Clinician` may create a row whose `source_type` is `CLINIC_MEASURED`; a `Participant` attempting it is refused. Third, a `Participant` creating a `SELF_ATTESTED` row may name only its own `participant_id` — naming another member's identifier is a scope matter and is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` instead.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. Both are supplied as query-string parameters and both are omitted from the request in the common case, in which the server applies the stated defaults.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise, and that ordering is applied before `limit` and `offset` are taken, so paging through a collection walks it oldest-first. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective skip actually applied), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before paging) and `has_more` (true when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page, false otherwise).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource in this system — `Employer`, `Site`, `VendorContract`, `Provider`, `StaffingShift`, `EligibilityRecord`, `Participant`, `Appointment`, `Visit`, `HealthAssessment`, `Referral`, `AlternativeStandard`, `IncentiveAward` and `ReportingPeriod` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created.

The versioning rule is uniform and deliberately strict, because three endpoints in §2.9 make the client echo a version back. Whenever a request causes the server to write any stored attribute of a resource, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant and its `version` is incremented by exactly one. This holds for resources touched only as the downstream target of a cascade just as it holds for the resource the request names directly: when terminating an enrollment cancels a booked appointment and that cancellation in turn changes a site's slot counters, the site's `version` advances too. Within the handling of a single request a given resource's `version` advances at most once, no matter how many cascade hops touch it. A request that is rejected by any rung of the validation ladder writes nothing at all — no counters move, no cascade fires, no `version` advances, and no partial state survives the rejection.

Every stored attribute of a resource is response-visible: input fields, server-computed fields and system-maintained fields alike all appear in the resource payload returned by a read and by any operation that mutates the resource. When the operation is one that mutates the resource, the payload it returns shows the resource as it stands at the end of that request: the `version` and `updated_at` it carries are the post-write values, already reflecting whatever single increment and timestamp refresh this request produced under the rule above. Values that exist only as intermediate steps inside a formula — a metric's band points, a category's avoided-cost subtotal, a cap ceiling — are not resource attributes and are not returned anywhere.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right. When a path names more than one identifier, the leftmost is resolved first, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines which not-found error is returned; the remaining identifiers are not consulted. Body identifiers that reference other resources are resolved after every path identifier has resolved successfully.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is a logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` advances it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc` together with the identifiers of everything the advance swept.

Advancing the clock runs three system sweeps, in this fixed order. **First**, every `Provider` whose `credential_status` is `CREDENTIALED` and whose `license_expiry_date` falls strictly before the new clock date moves to `LAPSED`; a provider is still credentialed on the expiry date itself, because the expiry bound is closed at the top. The identifiers of the providers moved are returned as `lapsed_provider_ids`. **Second**, every `Referral` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `AUTHORIZED` and whose `sla_due_at` falls strictly before the new clock instant moves to `ESCALATED` with `escalated_at` set to the new instant; a referral standing at exactly its `sla_due_at` does not escalate. The identifiers moved are returned as `escalated_referral_ids`. **Third**, every `Participant` whose `enrollment_status` is `TERMINATED` and whose `termination_effective_date` plus thirty days falls strictly before the new clock date moves to `CLOSED`, and on the parallel authorization axis that participant's `authorization_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` whatever it was before. The identifiers moved are returned as `closed_participant_ids`.

Each of the three lists is present in the response even when nothing was swept, in which case it is an empty array. Because the sweeps are ordered, a single advance can lapse a provider, escalate a referral and close an enrollment in one call, and each sweep observes the new clock instant rather than the old one. Nothing else about the system is recomputed by a clock advance: appointments do not expire, periods do not finalize themselves, and awards do not change status.

### 2.6. The Ordered Validation Ladder

Validation in this system is **one global ordered engine**, not a collection of per-handler checks. Every request, whatever handler it is ultimately bound for, walks the same twenty-five rungs in ascending order and returns the **first** failure it encounters; no later rung is evaluated once an earlier one has failed, and no later rung can mask or override an earlier one. Implementations that scatter these checks into independent handler code will produce the right answer for a request with one defect and the wrong answer for a request with two, because with two defects it is the rung number that decides which error the caller sees.

Two rungs are placed deliberately high so that they **pre-empt** rungs below them, including rungs a reader would ordinarily expect to fire first. Rung 3, the confidentiality wall, sits above existence (rung 4), scope (rung 5) and every payload check, so an employer-side role asking about a clinical record that does not exist receives the wall error and never a 404 — the 404 would itself leak whether a clinical record exists. Rung 6, the reporting-period freeze, sits above payload well-formedness (rung 7), enum membership (rung 8), optimistic concurrency (rung 9), state (rung 10) and every business rung, so a malformed amendment of a visit dated inside a finalized period reports the freeze, not the malformed body. §2.7 and §2.8 state the exact endpoint families each of those two gates covers.

The ladder is as follows.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint role list.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint. This rung also carries the three action-level restrictions named in §2.1: a `NO_SHOW` cancellation reason from a non-`Clinician`, and a `CLINIC_MEASURED` health assessment from a `Participant`, are refused here.
3. **Confidentiality wall.** An employer-side role may not touch an individual-level clinical resource. Otherwise `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_WALL`, HTTP 403. The covered surface is enumerated in §2.7.
4. **Existence.** Every identifier in the path exists, resolved left to right, and then every foreign-key identifier in the body exists. Otherwise the matching 404: `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for an `EligibilityRecord`, `VendorContract`, `StaffingShift` or `AlternativeStandard` identifier. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier or a foreign key in its body.
5. **Identity scope.** The entity-backed roles act only inside their own scope, exactly as §2.1 describes. Otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint acted on by `Participant`, `EmployerBenefitsAdmin`, `Clinician` or `CareNavigator`.
6. **Reporting-period freeze.** A write whose governing date falls inside a `ReportingPeriod` of that employer whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED` is refused. Otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_FINALIZED`, HTTP 409. The covered writes and the governing date each of them uses are enumerated in §2.8.
7. **Payload well-formedness.** Every required field is present, every value carries the declared type, every integer declared non-negative is non-negative, every ISO date and every ISO instant parses, and every conditionally-required field is honoured. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400. Applies to every write endpoint. The conditional requirements are: `fitness_conclusion` must be supplied when completing a visit whose `visit_type` is `OCC_HEALTH`; `health_assessment_id` must be supplied for an award whose `award_type` is `OUTCOME_BASED` and must be null for any other award type; `visit_id` must be supplied for an assessment whose `source_type` is `CLINIC_MEASURED` and must be null when `source_type` is `SELF_ATTESTED`; a `CLINIC_MEASURED` assessment must carry all six biometric metrics (`systolic_mmhg`, `diastolic_mmhg`, `bmi`, `a1c_pct`, `ldl_mg_dl` and `cotinine_ng_ml`); a `SELF_ATTESTED` assessment must carry `bmi` and `self_reported_tobacco`; `consult_note_reference` must be supplied when closing a referral loop; and `payroll_reference` must be supplied when exporting an award. This rung also carries the three supplied-date range constraints that have no dedicated error code: a `termination_effective_date` on an enrollment termination that falls strictly earlier than the clock date minus thirty days, a `termination_date` on an eligibility-record termination that falls strictly earlier than the clock date minus thirty days, and a `license_expiry_date` on a credential renewal that falls strictly earlier than the clock date, are each refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. So is a `shift_end` that is not strictly after its `shift_start`.
8. **Enum membership.** Every enum-typed field carries a declared member of its enumeration — `visit_type`, `site_type`, `fee_model`, `tier`, `coverage_role`, `source_type`, `steerage_tier`, `urgency`, `award_type`, `alternative_type`, `cancellation_reason`, `decision` and `provider_function`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, HTTP 400. Applies to every write endpoint carrying an enum field.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_site_version`, `expected_participant_version` or `expected_visit_version` equals the parent resource's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Applies to appointment booking, incentive-award creation and visit amendment, and to nothing else. §2.9 gives the full rule.
10. **Declared transition.** The target resource stands in a state from which the requested transition is declared in this document, and the transition's own caps are respected — the alternative-standard reassignment cap, the visit amendment cap and the reporting-period restatement cap all report here. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409. Applies to every state-changing endpoint.
11. **Reschedule cap.** The appointment's `reschedule_count` is strictly less than two. Otherwise `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED`, HTTP 409. Applies only to appointment rescheduling.
12. **Enrolled and covered on the governing date.** The participant's `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE` and the backing `EligibilityRecord` covers the governing date under the closed-interval rule of §2.10. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE`, HTTP 422. The governing date is the appointment's `service_date` for booking, rescheduling and check-in; the clock date for enrollment and for reinstatement; and `measurement_date` for a health assessment. Applies to enrollment, reinstatement, appointment booking, appointment rescheduling, check-in and health-assessment creation.
13. **Disclosure authorization in force.** The participant's `authorization_status` is `GRANTED` at the moment of release. Otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`, HTTP 403. Applies only to the disclosure release.
14. **Disclosure permitted for this visit.** For a release, the visit's `disclosure_status` is `RELEASE_PENDING`; for an employer read, it is `FITNESS_RELEASED` or `PARTICIPATION_RELEASED`. Otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PERMITTED`, HTTP 422. Applies to the employer disclosure read and to the disclosure release.
15. **Provider credentialed.** The named `Provider` carries `credential_status` of `CREDENTIALED` at the moment of the write; both `LAPSED` and `DEACTIVATED` fail. Otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to appointment booking, appointment rescheduling, check-in and staffing-shift creation.
16. **Site not closed.** The appointment's `service_date` does not appear in the site's `closure_dates`. Otherwise `ERR_SITE_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
17. **Inside operating hours.** The appointment interval sits inside the site's daily operating window under the closed containment rule of §2.10, and the ISO weekday of `service_date` appears in the site's `operating_weekdays`. Otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS`, HTTP 422. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
18. **Staffing coverage.** Some `StaffingShift` for the same `site_id` and the same `provider_id` closed-contains the appointment interval. Otherwise `ERR_STAFFING_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
19. **Provider not double-booked.** No other appointment of the same provider whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CONSUMED` or `NO_SHOW` overlaps the candidate interval under the half-open rule of §2.10. Otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_SLOT_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
20. **Site day capacity.** The site's non-cancelled appointment count on that `service_date` is strictly less than the site's `daily_slot_capacity` before the write. Otherwise `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
21. **Employer quota.** A vendor contract selected by the waterfall of §3.4 exists, with `quota_visits_consumed` strictly less than `quota_visits_total`. Otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYER_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409. Applies to appointment booking and rescheduling.
22. **Segregation of duties.** The care navigator authorizing a referral is not the provider who raised it. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403. Applies only to referral authorization.
23. **Eligibility-row uniqueness.** No other `EligibilityRecord` of the same `employer_id` and the same `member_external_id` has a coverage span overlapping the new span under the closed-interval rule. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELIGIBILITY`, HTTP 409. Applies only to eligibility-record creation.
24. **Incentive program cap.** The participant's committed incentive cents for the period plus the candidate award is less than or equal to the program cap. Otherwise `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. Applies to incentive-award creation and to alternative-standard completion.
25. **Wellness budget.** The employer's `wellness_budget_committed_cents` plus the candidate award is less than or equal to the employer's `wellness_budget_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_WELLNESS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. Applies to incentive-award creation and to alternative-standard completion.

Note the consequences of this ordering at a few points that matter. An appointment request that is both outside operating hours and over the site's day capacity reports `ERR_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS`, because rung 17 precedes rung 20. A booking that carries a stale site version and also names a lapsed provider reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, because rung 9 precedes rung 15. An incentive award that breaches both the program cap and the employer's wellness budget reports `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, because rung 24 precedes rung 25. And a third reschedule of an appointment that is also outside operating hours reports `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED`, because rung 11 precedes every scheduling rung.

### 2.7. The Confidentiality Wall (Ladder Rung 3)

The employer funds this clinic and is entitled to aggregate reporting, but it is not entitled to individual-level clinical detail. That separation is enforced at rung 3 of the ladder, above existence and above scope, and it applies to the two employer-side roles — `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` and `PayrollProcessor` — and to no other role.

The walled surface is exactly these eight read routes, and a reader must not infer the set from any other property of the endpoint:

- `GET /visits` and `GET /visits/{visit_id}`
- `GET /health-assessments` and `GET /health-assessments/{assessment_id}`
- `GET /referrals` and `GET /referrals/{referral_id}`
- `GET /alternative-standards` and `GET /alternative-standards/{standard_id}`

An employer-side role reaching any of those eight is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_WALL` and HTTP 403. The check runs **before** the path identifier is resolved, so a walled read of an identifier that does not exist also returns the wall error rather than a not-found error — the whole point of placing the rung this high is that the shape of the answer must not vary with whether the record exists. It follows that `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` are reachable only by callers that are not employer-side.

The two employer-side roles reach the wall by different routes and this difference is observable. `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` is listed among the authorized roles on all eight walled routes, so it passes rung 2 and is then refused at rung 3 with `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_WALL`. `PayrollProcessor` is not listed on any of the eight, so it never reaches rung 3 at all and is refused earlier, at rung 2, with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

Nothing outside those eight routes is walled. In particular the sanctioned employer channel, `GET /visits/{visit_id}/employer-disclosure`, is deliberately *not* on the walled list: it is the one place an employer-side caller may learn something about an individual encounter, and what it may learn is constrained by the projection rules of §3.5 rather than by rung 3. Reads of employers, sites, vendor contracts, eligibility records, participants, incentive awards and reporting periods are likewise unwalled — those are administrative or financial records, not clinical ones. Free-text clinical content is never disclosed to an employer-side role under any circumstances: a visit's `clinical_note` appears in no employer-facing payload under any `disclosure_status`.

### 2.8. The Reporting-Period Freeze (Ladder Rung 6)

Once a `ReportingPeriod` reaches `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED`, the operational record underneath it stops moving. That is enforced at rung 6, above payload well-formedness, enum membership, optimistic concurrency, state and every business rung, so that a request with several defects against a frozen period reports the freeze rather than the defect a lower rung would have found.

Seven write endpoints are covered, and each has its own **governing date** — the date the server compares against the employer's periods:

- Visit amendment — governed by the visit's `service_date`.
- Visit disclosure release — governed by the visit's `service_date`.
- Appointment check-in — governed by the appointment's `service_date`.
- Appointment cancellation — governed by the appointment's `service_date`.
- Health-assessment creation — governed by the supplied `measurement_date`.
- Alternative-standard completion — governed by the `measurement_date` of the backing `HealthAssessment`.
- Incentive-award creation — governed by the reporting period the request names directly in `reporting_period_id`.

If any `ReportingPeriod` of the relevant employer whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED` contains the governing date within its closed span from `period_start` through `period_end`, the write is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_FINALIZED` and HTTP 409. A period whose `status` is `RESTATING` does **not** freeze — reopening a period for restatement is precisely the mechanism by which late corrections become writable again — and neither does a period whose `status` is `OPEN`.

Endpoints outside that list of seven are not gated by rung 6 at all. Appointment booking and rescheduling, for instance, are not frozen by a finalized period, and neither is referral work, alternative-standard assignment, award approval, export, mark-paid or reversal.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints require the client to prove it is acting on a view of the world it has actually observed. Each of the three names a parent resource, a request field carrying that parent's `version` as the client last read it, and a rejection when the two disagree. All three reject with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 at rung 9 of the ladder. Endpoints not listed here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

**Booking an appointment** — `POST /appointments` — requires `expected_site_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Site`. Every booking, cancellation, no-show, reschedule and check-in at a site changes that site's day-level slot accounting and advances the site's `version`, so a booking still carrying an earlier value has priced its remaining capacity from a stale view and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. A client recovers by re-reading the site and retrying with the current value.

**Creating an incentive award** — `POST /incentive-awards` — requires `expected_participant_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Participant`. A new award legitimately adds to that participant's `incentive_awarded_cents` and consumes program-cap headroom, so a create carrying an earlier value has priced the headroom from a stale view and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

**Amending a visit** — `POST /visits/{visit_id}/amend` — requires `expected_visit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Visit` being amended. Here the parent is the resource itself: an amendment supersedes previously recorded encounter content, so an amendment carrying an earlier value would overwrite content the client never saw, and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

All three fields are non-optional on their endpoints; omitting one is a payload defect and is caught earlier, at rung 7, with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 2.10. Interval and Boundary Conventions

This domain deliberately runs **three different interval conventions at once**, and a reader must not generalise one to another. Each is stated at its own site in §3 as well, but the three are collected here because confusing them is the single easiest way to produce a wrong implementation.

**Eligibility coverage spans are closed at both ends.** A member is covered on the `effective_date` itself and is *still* covered on the `termination_date` itself; a null `termination_date` means the span is open-ended into the future. Two eligibility spans overlap for uniqueness purposes when they share at least one date under that same closed reading.

**Reporting-period spans are closed at both ends.** A date on `period_start` and a date on `period_end` both fall inside the period.

**Staffing containment is closed.** An appointment is covered by a staffing shift when the shift starts at or before the appointment's start and ends at or after the appointment's end; an appointment ending at exactly the shift's `shift_end` **is** covered.

**Site operating windows are closed.** The appointment's start time of day must be at or after the site's `open_time_utc` and the appointment's end time of day must be at or before the site's `close_time_utc`; an appointment ending exactly at closing time is inside hours.

**Provider occupation intervals are half-open.** An appointment occupies the interval from its start instant up to but not including its end instant. Two of the same provider's appointments collide only when the candidate starts strictly before the existing one ends *and* the existing one starts strictly before the candidate ends. There is no buffer between appointments: two appointments that merely touch, one ending at exactly the instant the next begins, do **not** collide.

Thresholds elsewhere are stated inclusively or exclusively at their own site, and the reader should take each literally. Site day capacity is a strict-less-than test taken before the write, so the booking that brings the day's count up to exactly the capacity succeeds and the next one fails. Referral escalation is strict: a referral standing at exactly its `sla_due_at` has not breached. Credential expiry is closed at the top: a provider whose licence expires today is credentialed today and lapses tomorrow.

### 2.11. Money, Rates and Rounding

All money in this system is carried as a whole number of cents in an integer field; there are no decimal currency values anywhere in the API. Wherever a money value is produced by multiplication or division, the result is rounded **half-up** to a whole cent — a value exactly at the half-cent rounds away from zero, upward.

Rates and ratios are decimals. Engagement rates, the visits-per-utilizer figure and the return-on-investment ratio are all rounded half-up to exactly four decimal places. Staffed hours are carried to two decimal places, rounded half-up.

Two places in this system pin **where** rounding happens relative to summation, because summing first and rounding once produces a different answer from rounding each term and summing the rounded values. Both of them round first. A vendor contract's staffed-hour total rounds each individual shift's hours to two decimals before adding them together. The avoided-cost figure on the utilization report rounds each visit-type category to whole cents before adding the three categories together. §3.9 states both rules again where they are used.

Divisions with an empty denominator never raise an error and never return null. An engagement rate over an empty population is exactly 0.0; a visits-per-utilizer figure with no utilizers is exactly 0.0; a return-on-investment ratio against a zero program cost is exactly 0.0; a per-employee-per-month cost with no enrolled population or no months is exactly 0.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants

Three quantities in this system partition exactly, and every operation is designed to preserve the partition rather than to recompute it independently. They are stated here as invariants a correct implementation will satisfy at every observable moment.

**Incentive money conserves within a reporting period.** A period's `incentive_awarded_cents` always equals the sum of its `incentive_pending_cents`, `incentive_paid_cents`, `incentive_reversed_cents` and `incentive_forfeited_cents`. Each award status maps to exactly one bucket: `PENDING`, `APPROVED` and `EXPORTED` all count toward pending; `PAID` counts toward paid; `REVERSED` toward reversed; `FORFEITED` toward forfeited. Approval, export, mark-paid, reversal and forfeiture only move cents between buckets — none of them ever changes `incentive_awarded_cents`.

**Slots conserve within a site.** A site's `slots_requested_total` always equals the sum of its `slots_held_count`, `slots_consumed_count`, `slots_no_show_count` and `slots_released_count`. Every appointment ever created at that site sits in exactly one of those four buckets — held while `BOOKED`, consumed once checked in, no-show when marked so, released when cancelled. A reschedule moves an appointment in time but leaves it counted as held.

**Employer quota conserves within a vendor contract.** A contract's `quota_visits_requested_total` always equals the sum of its `quota_visits_consumed` and `quota_visits_released`, and its `quota_visits_remaining` always equals `quota_visits_total` minus `quota_visits_consumed`. A cancelled appointment releases the employer's quota back; a no-show does not, because the employer still paid for the reserved capacity. At a near-site clinic shared by several employers each contract conserves its own quota independently, while all of them draw on the one site-level `daily_slot_capacity`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program: Employers, Clinic Sites and Vendor Contracts

Everything in this system hangs off a sponsoring employer. A `ProgramAdmin` registers one by supplying its `legal_name`, a short `employer_code` unique across all employers and between two and sixteen characters long, the `annual_total_cost_of_coverage_cents` that will later serve as the base of the incentive cap, and the `wellness_budget_cents` the employer has set aside to fund awards. Both money figures are non-negative integers in cents. An optional `tobacco_program_enabled` flag declares whether the wellness program includes a tobacco-cessation component; when the caller omits it the server stores `false`. A second employer registered with an `employer_code` already in use is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Three employer figures are maintained by the server and never supplied by a client. `wellness_budget_committed_cents` is the sum of `award_amount_cents` across every incentive award of this employer whose `status` is `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `EXPORTED` or `PAID`; awards that have been reversed or forfeited release their commitment and drop out of that sum. `wellness_budget_remaining_cents` is `wellness_budget_cents` less `wellness_budget_committed_cents`. `program_cost_to_date_cents` is the running total of vendor contract cost booked at each period finalization, and it starts at 0. At registration the committed figure is 0, the remaining figure equals the full budget, and the program cost to date is 0. An employer's `status` is `ACTIVE` from creation and never changes through any operation this API exposes.

A clinic **site** is where care is delivered. A site carries a `site_name`, a `site_type` of either `ON_SITE` — a clinic on a single employer's premises — or `NEAR_SITE`, a shared clinic several employers buy into. An `ON_SITE` clinic normally names its landlord through `host_employer_id`; for a `NEAR_SITE` clinic that field is normally omitted, which is entirely legitimate and stores null. The weekly calendar is described by `open_time_utc` and `close_time_utc`, both twenty-four-hour wall times in UTC written as hours and minutes, and by `operating_weekdays`, an array of ISO weekday numbers where 1 is Monday and 7 is Sunday. Holidays and one-off shutdowns go into `closure_dates`, an array of ISO dates on which the site is shut regardless of the weekday pattern; when the caller omits it the server stores an empty array. `daily_slot_capacity` caps how many capacity-holding appointments the site will accept on any single service date and must be at least 1.

Five counters travel with every site and are maintained entirely by the server, all starting at 0: `slots_held_count` counts that site's appointments currently `BOOKED`, `slots_consumed_count` counts those checked in, `slots_no_show_count` counts those marked as no-shows, `slots_released_count` counts those cancelled, and `slots_requested_total` counts every appointment ever created at the site in any status. The conservation rule of §2.12 ties the five together.

Site calendars change. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `ClinicManager` may revise a site's `open_time_utc`, `close_time_utc`, `operating_weekdays`, `closure_dates` or `daily_slot_capacity`; every one of those inputs is optional on that update and only the fields actually supplied are written. Any such revision increments the site's `version` by one, which by construction invalidates any booking still holding the older value. Sites can be listed and filtered by `host_employer_id` and by `site_type`; reading a single site returns its live counters together with the `version` a booking must echo back.

A **vendor contract** is the commercial instrument that lets one employer buy capacity at one site. Created by a `ProgramAdmin`, it names its `site_id`, its `employer_id` and the operating `vendor_name`, and it fixes the pricing. `fee_model` is either `PER_VISIT`, in which case `per_visit_fee_cents` prices each billable visit, or `FTE_HOUR`, in which case `fte_hourly_rate_cents` prices each staffed hour; both fee fields are supplied at creation whatever the model, and the unused one simply does not participate in pricing. `minimum_guarantee_cents` is the floor the employer owes the vendor per reporting period however little the clinic is used, and `quota_visits_total` is the number of appointment slots at that site reserved for this employer over the life of the contract. All four money-and-quota inputs are non-negative integers.

The contract's own counters start at zero and are maintained by the booking workflow: `quota_visits_consumed` counts appointments pinned to this contract whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CONSUMED` or `NO_SHOW`; `quota_visits_released` counts those that were cancelled; `quota_visits_requested_total` counts every appointment ever pinned to the contract in any status; and `quota_visits_remaining` is `quota_visits_total` less `quota_visits_consumed`, so at creation it equals the full quota. Four further fields stay at zero until the employer's reporting period is finalized, at which point §3.9 fills them in: `billable_visit_count`, `staffed_hours`, `gross_fee_cents`, `true_up_amount_cents` and `contract_cost_cents`. A contract's `status` is `ACTIVE` from creation and becomes `TRUED_UP` when a finalization prices it. Contracts can be listed and filtered by `site_id`, by `employer_id` and by `status`, and a single contract read returns both its quota counters and, once finalized, its fee breakdown.

### 3.2. Staffing the Clinic: Providers, Credentials and Shifts

A `ClinicManager` registers each person who works the clinic as a `Provider`, naming the `site_id` that is their primary base, their `full_name`, their `provider_function` — `CLINICIAN` for those who deliver encounters, `NAVIGATOR` for those who run referral and navigation work — and the `license_expiry_date` on which their licence runs out. A new provider starts `CREDENTIALED` with a `renewal_count` of 0.

Credentialing is a cycle rather than a straight line. The expiry bound is **closed at the top**: a provider is still `CREDENTIALED` on the `license_expiry_date` itself and only lapses the first time the clock reads a date strictly after it. When a clock advance crosses that line the provider moves `CREDENTIALED → LAPSED`, and from that moment on any booking, reschedule, check-in or staffing-shift creation naming that provider is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`. A `ClinicManager` restores the provider by recording a renewal with a fresh `license_expiry_date`, moving the provider `LAPSED → CREDENTIALED`, replacing the stored expiry date and incrementing `renewal_count` by one. The replacement date must not fall strictly earlier than the current clock date; one that does is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. This loop may repeat indefinitely — a provider may lapse and be renewed any number of times, and `renewal_count` records how many times.

Deactivation is the way out of the cycle. A `ClinicManager` may deactivate a provider from either `CREDENTIALED` or `LAPSED`, moving it to `DEACTIVATED`, after which the provider can no longer be named on a booking and can no longer be renewed. `DEACTIVATED` is terminal: a renewal attempt against a deactivated provider, or against one that is already `CREDENTIALED`, finds no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is a second deactivation. Because rung 15 tests for `CREDENTIALED` specifically, a deactivated provider named on a booking is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` exactly as a lapsed one is.

Coverage is published as **staffing shifts**. A `ClinicManager` publishes one by naming the `site_id` being staffed, the `provider_id` working it, the `shift_start` and `shift_end` instants in ISO 8601 UTC, and the `coverage_role` the shift provides — one of `PRIMARY_CARE`, `OCC_HEALTH`, `WELLNESS` or `NAVIGATION`. The end instant must be strictly after the start instant; a shift that ends at or before it starts is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The server derives `staffed_hours` as the elapsed time between the two instants expressed in hours and rounded half-up to two decimal places — a shift running from eight in the morning to a quarter past four in the afternoon records 8.25 staffed hours. That per-shift rounding matters later: §3.9 sums the already-rounded per-shift figures rather than rounding a raw total once.

Shifts are what make an appointment bookable at all. Rung 18 requires that some shift for the same `site_id` and the same `provider_id` **closed-contains** the appointment interval — the shift must start at or before the appointment starts and end at or after the appointment ends, and an appointment finishing at exactly `shift_end` is covered. A provider with no published shift covering the requested window cannot be booked, however open the site is. Note that `coverage_role` is recorded for reporting and does not have to agree with the appointment's `visit_type`; nothing in the booking ladder compares the two.

Providers can be listed and filtered by `site_id`, by `credential_status` and by `provider_function`; staffing shifts can be listed and filtered by `site_id` and by `provider_id`. Both collections are readable by the operational roles, and both list endpoints page under the common rules of §2.3.

### 3.3. The Eligibility File, Enrollment and the Authorization Axis

An `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` loads its employer's eligibility file one row at a time. Each row names the `employer_id` it belongs to, the `subscriber_external_id` of the contract holder and the `member_external_id` of the covered person as the employer's own systems know them, the relationship `tier` — `EMPLOYEE`, `SPOUSE`, `DEPENDENT` or `RETIREE` — and the `effective_date` on which coverage begins. An optional `termination_date` closes the span; omitting it stores null and leaves the span open-ended.

**The coverage span is closed at both ends.** A member is covered on the `effective_date` itself, and — this is the part implementations most often get wrong — a member is *still covered on the `termination_date` itself*. From that reading the server derives `coverage_status`: `FUTURE` when the clock date falls strictly before `effective_date`, `EFFECTIVE` when the clock date falls on or after `effective_date` and either there is no `termination_date` or the clock date falls on or before it, and `EXPIRED` when a `termination_date` exists and the clock date falls strictly after it. The status is derived, not stored by the client, and it is recomputed whenever the row is read or written.

The same closed reading governs uniqueness. A new row is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELIGIBILITY` when another row of the same `employer_id` and the same `member_external_id` has a coverage span sharing at least one date with the new span — two spans that merely abut, one ending the day before the other begins, do not overlap and are both accepted.

Coverage ends by recording a termination on the row. The administrator supplies a new `termination_date`, the server recomputes `coverage_status` against it, and if the supplied date falls strictly before the current clock date the row is flagged `retro_terminated` as true — a retroactive termination, which employers do issue and which the clinic needs to see. Retroactivity is bounded: a `termination_date` falling strictly earlier than the clock date minus thirty days is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, while a date exactly thirty days before the clock date is accepted, the grace bound being closed. A termination date on or after the clock date leaves `retro_terminated` false. Eligibility rows can be listed and filtered by `employer_id`, by `member_external_id` and by `coverage_status`.

**Enrollment** turns an eligible person into a `Participant`. The administrator names the `employer_id`, the `eligibility_record_id` the enrollment rests on and a `display_name`. Enrollment is refused with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE` when the named row does not cover the current clock date under the closed rule above — a `FUTURE` row and an `EXPIRED` row are both unenrollable. On success the server copies `tier` from the backing row, stamps `enrolled_on` with the clock's date, and starts the participant on both of its axes: `enrollment_status` at `ACTIVE` and `authorization_status` at `NOT_REQUESTED`. `termination_effective_date` and `authorization_revoked_at` both start null, and the two derived figures `completed_visit_count` — this participant's visits whose `encounter_status` is `COMPLETED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED` — and `incentive_awarded_cents` — the sum of `award_amount_cents` across this participant's awards in any status — both start at 0. Participants can be listed and filtered by `employer_id`, by `eligibility_record_id` and by `enrollment_status`, and a single participant read returns the current `version` that award creation must echo back.

The two axes advance **independently**. Terminating enrollment does not by itself change the authorization axis, and granting or revoking authorization never changes the enrollment axis. Only the final closure of an enrollment reaches across, and it reaches in one direction only.

**Terminating an enrollment** moves the participant `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and stores the supplied `termination_effective_date`. As with eligibility, retroactivity is bounded at thirty days: an effective date strictly earlier than the clock date minus thirty days is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, and a date exactly thirty days back is accepted. Termination then fires a cascade in two independent branches, and both branches complete before the response is returned.

Down the scheduling branch, every appointment of this participant standing at `BOOKED` moves to `CANCELLED` with a `cancellation_reason` of `ENROLLMENT_TERMINATED`. Each of those cancellations reaches the appointment's site, which recomputes `slots_held_count` and `slots_released_count` and advances its own `version` by one. Each cancellation then reaches the vendor contract the appointment had been pinned to, which recomputes `quota_visits_consumed`, `quota_visits_released` and `quota_visits_remaining` — the released quota returns to the employer.

Down the money branch, every incentive award of this participant standing at `PENDING` moves to `FORFEITED`. **Awards already `APPROVED`, `EXPORTED` or `PAID` are explicitly not forfeited** — they have passed the point at which the employer can take them back, and this branch leaves them exactly as it found them. Each forfeiture reaches the award's reporting period, which recomputes `incentive_pending_cents` and `incentive_forfeited_cents` while `incentive_awarded_cents` stays put, and the period in turn reaches the employer, which recomputes `wellness_budget_committed_cents` and `wellness_budget_remaining_cents` because forfeited money is no longer committed.

**Reinstatement** reverses the enrollment axis, moving the participant `TERMINATED → ACTIVE` and clearing `termination_effective_date` back to null. It is available only inside the grace window: the clock date must fall on or before `termination_effective_date` plus thirty days, and the thirtieth day itself still permits it. A reinstatement outside that window finds no available transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a reinstatement whose backing eligibility row is not `EFFECTIVE` at the clock date is refused with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE`, and because the state rung sits above the eligibility rung, a request that fails both reports the state error. Reinstatement is **not compensating**: the appointments the termination cancelled stay cancelled, the site and contract counters keep their post-cancellation values, and forfeited awards stay forfeited. The `ACTIVE → TERMINATED → ACTIVE` loop may repeat any number of times.

Beyond the grace window the enrollment closes for good. When a clock advance finds a `TERMINATED` participant whose `termination_effective_date` plus thirty days falls strictly before the new clock date, the participant moves `TERMINATED → CLOSED` and reinstatement is no longer possible. That closure is the one place the enrollment axis reaches the authorization axis: whatever `authorization_status` the participant held — `NOT_REQUESTED`, `GRANTED` or `REVOKED` — it becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and no further authorization decision is accepted from that participant.

**The authorization axis** is the participant's own control over what the employer may be told. A `Participant` records a decision of `GRANT` or `REVOKE` on itself. A grant from `NOT_REQUESTED` or from `REVOKED` moves the axis to `GRANTED`, and is available only while `enrollment_status` is not `CLOSED`; a grant out of `REVOKED` also clears `authorization_revoked_at` back to null. Reaching `GRANTED` is what opens the `WALLED → RELEASE_PENDING` leg for encounters completed afterwards.

A revoke from `GRANTED` moves the axis to `REVOKED`, stamps `authorization_revoked_at` with the clock instant, and reaches across to the participant's visits: every visit whose `disclosure_status` is `WALLED` or `RELEASE_PENDING` moves to `SUPPRESSED`. **Revocation is prospective only.** Visits already sitting at `FITNESS_RELEASED` or `PARTICIPATION_RELEASED` are left untouched — a disclosure the employer has already lawfully received is never retracted — and this non-firing branch is as much a part of the specification as the firing one. Nor is suppression undone later: a subsequent grant restores the axis to `GRANTED` and opens the leg for future encounters, but visits already `SUPPRESSED` stay `SUPPRESSED` permanently. Any decision recorded against a participant whose axis is already `WITHDRAWN`, a grant against an already-`GRANTED` axis, or a revoke against an axis that is not `GRANTED`, finds no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `decision` value outside `GRANT` and `REVOKE` is refused earlier, with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

### 3.4. Booking a Slot: Geometry, Quota and the Life of an Appointment

A `Participant` books for itself, or a `CareNavigator` books on a member's behalf, by naming the `participant_id`, the `site_id`, the `provider_id` who will deliver the encounter, the `visit_type` — `PRIMARY_CARE`, `OCC_HEALTH` or `WELLNESS` — the `scheduled_start` instant in ISO 8601 UTC, and the `expected_site_version` that proves the caller has seen the site's current capacity picture. Appointments are a fixed half hour: the server derives `scheduled_end` as `scheduled_start` plus thirty minutes and `service_date` as the UTC date part of `scheduled_start`. A new appointment starts at `BOOKED` with a `reschedule_count` of 0 and a null `cancellation_reason`.

Four geometric tests stand between a request and a booked slot, and each uses its own boundary convention. **Site closure** comes first: a `service_date` listed in the site's `closure_dates` is refused with `ERR_SITE_CLOSED`. **The operating window** is closed containment — the appointment's start time of day must be at or after the site's `open_time_utc`, its end time of day must be at or before the site's `close_time_utc`, and the ISO weekday of `service_date` must appear in the site's `operating_weekdays`; a half-hour slot that ends exactly at closing time is inside hours, and a violation of either the window or the weekday pattern is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS`. **Staffing coverage** is also closed containment, but against a published shift rather than the site calendar: some `StaffingShift` of the same site and the same provider must start at or before `scheduled_start` and end at or after `scheduled_end`, with an appointment ending exactly at `shift_end` counting as covered; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`. **Provider occupation**, by contrast, is deliberately half-open. The candidate interval runs from `scheduled_start` up to but not including `scheduled_end`, and it collides with an existing appointment of the same provider standing at `BOOKED`, `CONSUMED` or `NO_SHOW` only when the candidate starts strictly before that appointment ends and that appointment starts strictly before the candidate ends. There is no buffer whatsoever between consecutive appointments, so two half-hour slots back to back — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do not collide, while any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Cancelled appointments have released their hold and are never counted as occupying a provider.

**Site day capacity** is the fifth test and is a strict-less-than comparison taken before the write: count the site's appointments on the same `service_date` whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CONSUMED` or `NO_SHOW` — cancelled ones released their slot and are excluded — and the booking is permitted only while that count is strictly less than the site's `daily_slot_capacity`. The booking that raises the count to exactly the capacity therefore succeeds, and the next one is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`.

**Whose quota did this consume?** The request never names a vendor contract; the server chooses one and pins the answer permanently as `quota_contract_id` so it never has to be re-derived. The selection is a waterfall with four fully pinned elements. First the eligible set: vendor contracts whose `site_id` equals the appointment's site, whose `employer_id` equals the *participant's* employer, whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `TRUED_UP`, and whose `quota_visits_consumed` is strictly less than `quota_visits_total` — a contract sitting at exactly its quota is not eligible. Then the ranking: ascending `quota_visits_remaining`, so the tightest contract is drained first. Then the tie-break: ascending `created_at`, so the earliest-created contract wins; the entity identifier is never consulted. And finally the empty case: when no contract qualifies the booking is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`, a single error that covers both "this employer has no contract at this site at all" and "its quota is used up". At a near-site clinic each employer drains only its own contract, so two employers booking the same site consume different quotas while competing for one shared `daily_slot_capacity`.

A successful booking moves five counters. On the site, `slots_held_count` and `slots_requested_total` each rise by one and the site's `version` rises by one — which is why a client that books twice in a row from one cached read will have its second booking refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. On the pinned contract, `quota_visits_consumed` and `quota_visits_requested_total` each rise by one and `quota_visits_remaining` is recomputed.

**Rescheduling** moves a booked appointment to a new `scheduled_start`; the new instant is the only input. The appointment stays at `BOOKED` — this is a capped self-loop, not a state change — and the entire geometry above is re-evaluated against the new interval exactly as at booking, including site closure, operating hours, staffing coverage, provider occupation and site day capacity. When re-checking provider occupation and day capacity the appointment being moved is not counted against itself. A reschedule is capped at two: `reschedule_count` rises by one on each success, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED`. The site's `version` rises by one because day-level occupancy changed, but the site's slot counters do not move — the appointment was held before and is held after, which is exactly what the slot conservation rule of §2.12 requires. Rescheduling carries no version field of its own. The contract selector runs again as part of the reschedule, and because neither the site nor the participant's employer has changed it normally re-pins the same `quota_contract_id`, in which case no quota counter moves; in the unusual case where the selector's winner is a different contract, one unit of quota travels with the appointment — the previously pinned contract's `quota_visits_consumed` and `quota_visits_requested_total` each fall by one and the newly pinned contract's each rise by one — so that both contracts continue to satisfy quota conservation. An eligible contract must still exist at reschedule time; if none does, the reschedule is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`.

**Cancellation** takes a `cancellation_reason` and forks on it. `PARTICIPANT_REQUEST` and `CLINIC_REQUEST` both move the appointment `BOOKED → CANCELLED`: the site's `slots_held_count` falls by one, its `slots_released_count` rises by one and its `version` rises by one, and on the pinned contract `quota_visits_consumed` falls by one, `quota_visits_released` rises by one and `quota_visits_remaining` is recomputed — the employer gets its reserved capacity back. `NO_SHOW` moves the appointment `BOOKED → NO_SHOW` instead, may be supplied only by a `Clinician`, and treats the quota very differently: the site's `slots_held_count` falls by one, its `slots_no_show_count` rises by one and its `version` rises by one, but **the vendor contract's quota counters are not touched at all**, because a member who fails to turn up has still consumed the employer's reserved capacity. The fourth member of the reason enumeration, `ENROLLMENT_TERMINATED`, is written only by the termination cascade of §3.3; a caller supplying it directly is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**Check-in** is the boundary between scheduling and clinical care and belongs to the `Clinician`. It requires the appointment to stand at `BOOKED`, the participant's `enrollment_status` to be `ACTIVE`, the backing eligibility row to cover the appointment's `service_date` under the closed-span rule, and the named provider to be `CREDENTIALED`; failures report `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE`, `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE` and `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` respectively. The acting clinician does not have to be the appointment's own provider — check-in is a front-desk act — but the visit it produces is stamped with the appointment's `provider_id`, and from that point on only that provider may advance the encounter. Check-in moves the appointment `BOOKED → CONSUMED`, lowers the site's `slots_held_count` by one, raises its `slots_consumed_count` by one and raises its `version` by one, and leaves the contract's quota counters untouched, since booking already counted the visit as consumed. It creates the `Visit` described in §3.5, and the newly created visit — not the appointment — is what the response returns.

Appointments can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `site_id`, by `provider_id`, by `quota_contract_id`, by `status` and by `service_date`, and reading a single appointment shows which vendor contract's quota it consumed.

### 3.5. The Encounter and Its Two Independent Axes

Check-in creates a `Visit`, and a visit is tracked on **two axes that advance independently of one another**. The clinical axis, `encounter_status`, records what happened in the room. The confidentiality axis, `disclosure_status`, records what — if anything — the employer may be told about it. Neither axis is a function of the other, and an implementation that models the visit as a single status enumeration will not be able to express the combinations this domain requires.

At creation the visit copies `appointment_id`, `participant_id`, `provider_id`, `site_id`, `visit_type` and `service_date` from the appointment that produced it, and starts with `encounter_status` at `CHECKED_IN`, `disclosure_status` at `WALLED`, `amendment_count` at 0, and `fitness_conclusion`, `clinical_note`, `chargeable_party` and `consumes_benefit_entitlement` all unset.

**The clinical axis.** Only the visit's own provider may advance it: the acting `Clinician`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the visit's `provider_id`, and any other clinician is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Starting the encounter moves it `CHECKED_IN → IN_PROGRESS` and has no effect on anything outside the visit. Completing it moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and accepts two optional inputs, `fitness_conclusion` and `clinical_note` — except that on a visit whose `visit_type` is `OCC_HEALTH` a `fitness_conclusion` is conditionally required and its absence is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The conclusion is one of `FIT_FOR_DUTY`, `FIT_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` or `NOT_FIT`, and it is meaningful only on occupational-health encounters, being the employer-directed answer the employer commissioned the encounter to obtain.

Completion is where the visit learns who pays, and the answer forks cleanly on `visit_type`. A `PRIMARY_CARE` encounter sets `chargeable_party` to `HEALTH_PLAN`; an `OCC_HEALTH` encounter sets it to `EMPLOYER_DIRECT`, because the employer commissioned it directly; a `WELLNESS` encounter sets it to `WELLNESS_BUDGET`. Alongside it the server writes `consumes_benefit_entitlement`, which is true **only** for `PRIMARY_CARE` — occupational-health and wellness encounters deliberately do not draw down the member's health-plan benefit, which is a large part of why employers run them on site. Completion also refreshes the participant's `completed_visit_count`.

**The confidentiality axis fires as part of that same completion**, and it forks on two things at once: the visit's `visit_type` and the participant's current `authorization_status`. A `PRIMARY_CARE` encounter moves `WALLED → NOT_DISCLOSABLE` **regardless of authorization** — primary care is never disclosable to an employer at the individual level, and a participant's grant cannot make it so; from that point the employer disclosure read on that visit is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PERMITTED` forever. An `OCC_HEALTH` or `WELLNESS` encounter completed while the participant's `authorization_status` is `GRANTED` moves `WALLED → RELEASE_PENDING`, which is the only state from which a release can later be made. An `OCC_HEALTH` or `WELLNESS` encounter completed while the participant's `authorization_status` is `NOT_REQUESTED` or `REVOKED` moves `WALLED → SUPPRESSED` instead, and no disclosure channel is ever opened for that visit — a later grant does not reopen it. A participant whose axis reads `WITHDRAWN` is by construction a closed enrollment and has no live encounters to complete.

**Amending** a completed encounter is how late clinical corrections are recorded. It moves the visit `COMPLETED → AMENDED` the first time and then loops `AMENDED → AMENDED`, and it is capped at three amendments: `amendment_count` rises by one on each success and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An amendment carries `expected_visit_version` as a mandatory field, matching the visit's current `version`, and may revise `clinical_note`, `fitness_conclusion`, or both. It is blocked whenever the visit's `service_date` falls inside a reporting period of that employer standing at `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED`, and because that freeze sits at rung 6 it is reported as `ERR_PERIOD_FINALIZED` even when the amendment is also malformed or carries a stale version. Each successful amendment increments the visit's `version` by one, which means a client that amends twice from one cached read has its second amendment refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Amending does **not** touch the disclosure axis: a visit sitting at `FITNESS_RELEASED` stays released while its clinical content is revised.

**Releasing a disclosure** is the deliberate act by which a clinician opens the employer-visible channel, and it takes no input at all — everything it needs is already on the visit. It requires the visit to stand at `RELEASE_PENDING`; a visit at `WALLED`, `NOT_DISCLOSABLE` or `SUPPRESSED`, or one already released, is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PERMITTED`. It requires the participant's `authorization_status` to be `GRANTED` at that moment; if the participant has revoked in the meantime the release is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`, and because rung 13 sits above rung 14 an attempt that fails both reports the revocation. It is covered by the reporting-period freeze on the visit's `service_date`. And it forks on `visit_type`: an `OCC_HEALTH` visit — which must by now carry a non-null `fitness_conclusion` — moves `RELEASE_PENDING → FITNESS_RELEASED`, while a `WELLNESS` visit moves `RELEASE_PENDING → PARTICIPATION_RELEASED`. There is no third destination; `PRIMARY_CARE` visits never reach `RELEASE_PENDING` in the first place.

**What the employer actually sees** is the narrow projection served by the employer disclosure read, available to `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` and `ProgramAdmin`, and it too is dual-variant. On a visit at `FITNESS_RELEASED` the payload carries `visit_id`, `visit_type`, `service_date`, `disclosure_status`, the visit's actual `fitness_conclusion`, `participation_recorded` set to true, and `clinical_detail_included` set to false. On a visit at `PARTICIPATION_RELEASED` the payload carries the same seven keys but `fitness_conclusion` is **null even when the visit row happens to hold a conclusion value** — a wellness encounter tells the employer that the member participated and nothing more — with `participation_recorded` true and `clinical_detail_included` false. Any other `disclosure_status`, meaning `WALLED`, `RELEASE_PENDING`, `NOT_DISCLOSABLE` or `SUPPRESSED`, is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The visit's `clinical_note` never appears in this payload under any status whatsoever. One subtlety is deliberate: this read does **not** re-consult the participant's current `authorization_status`. A disclosure that was lawfully released stays readable afterwards even if the participant later revokes, which is the read-side counterpart to revocation being prospective only.

**Locking** is the terminal state of the clinical axis and it is never requested directly. When a reporting period is finalized, every visit of that employer whose `service_date` falls inside the period's closed span and whose `encounter_status` is `COMPLETED` or `AMENDED` moves to `LOCKED`; §3.9 describes what that locking sets in motion. A locked visit accepts no further amendment, and a visit still at `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_PROGRESS` when a period finalizes is simply left where it is — the lock reaches completed and amended encounters only.

Visits can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `provider_id`, by `site_id`, by `appointment_id`, by `visit_type` and by `encounter_status` — but both the list and the single read are behind the confidentiality wall of §2.7, so an employer-side caller reaching either is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_WALL`.

### 3.6. Biometrics, Health-Risk Scoring and the Reasonable Alternative Standard

A `HealthAssessment` records a set of health metrics for one participant and scores them into a risk band. It names the `participant_id` assessed, a `source_type`, and the `measurement_date` on which the values were taken — that date, and not the date of the request, is what decides which reporting period any resulting outcome belongs to. `source_type` is either `CLINIC_MEASURED`, meaning biometrics verified in the clinic, or `SELF_ATTESTED`, meaning values the participant entered themselves. A `CLINIC_MEASURED` row must name the in-clinic `visit_id` that produced the measurements; a `SELF_ATTESTED` row must leave `visit_id` null. Only a `Clinician` may create a `CLINIC_MEASURED` row; a `Participant` may create `SELF_ATTESTED` rows and only for itself.

The metrics are `systolic_mmhg` and `diastolic_mmhg` in millimetres of mercury, `bmi`, `a1c_pct` as a percentage, `ldl_mg_dl` in milligrams per decilitre, `cotinine_ng_ml` as serum cotinine in nanograms per millilitre, and the boolean `self_reported_tobacco`. Which of them are mandatory depends on the source. A `CLINIC_MEASURED` assessment requires all six numeric metrics — the two blood-pressure values, the body-mass index, the A1c, the LDL and the cotinine — and omitting any one is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A `SELF_ATTESTED` assessment requires only `bmi` and `self_reported_tobacco`; the rest may be omitted, and their absence is scored rather than rejected. Serum cotinine is meaningful only on a clinic-measured row and the self-reported flag only on a self-attested one.

**Scoring runs once, at creation, and writes five derived fields.** Each of five metric groups contributes band points, and within each group the bands are read from the most severe downward so that the first matching band wins.

Blood pressure scores 3 points when the systolic value is at least 140 or the diastolic value is at least 90; failing that, 2 points when the systolic value falls between 130 and 139 with both ends included, or the diastolic value falls between 80 and 89 with both ends included; failing that, 1 point when the systolic value falls between 120 and 129 with both ends included *and* the diastolic value is strictly below 80; and 0 points otherwise, that is when the systolic value is strictly below 120 and the diastolic value is strictly below 80. Body-mass index scores 3 points at 35.0 or above, 2 points from 30.0 inclusive up to but not including 35.0, 1 point from 25.0 inclusive up to but not including 30.0, and 0 points strictly below 25.0. A1c scores 3 points at 6.5 or above, 1 point from 5.7 inclusive up to but not including 6.5, and 0 points strictly below 5.7 — there is no 2-point A1c band. LDL scores 2 points at 160 or above, 1 point from 100 inclusive up to but not including 160, and 0 points strictly below 100.

**Tobacco is the first place the two source variants diverge.** On a `CLINIC_MEASURED` assessment tobacco scores 3 points when `cotinine_ng_ml` is 10.0 or above and 0 points otherwise, the answer coming from the assay. On a `SELF_ATTESTED` assessment tobacco scores 3 points when `self_reported_tobacco` is true and 0 points otherwise, the answer coming from the participant's own declaration.

**Absent metrics are the second divergence, and they apply only to self-attested rows.** On a `SELF_ATTESTED` assessment, the blood-pressure pair counts as one metric and is absent only when both `systolic_mmhg` and `diastolic_mmhg` are omitted; each of the blood-pressure pair, `a1c_pct` and `ldl_mg_dl` that is absent contributes exactly 1 point instead of the 0 it would otherwise have scored, so an assessment with none of the three supplied carries 3 points of missing-metric penalty. Where only one member of the blood-pressure pair is supplied, the pair is not absent and is scored through the band table above, with the omitted member simply not satisfying any comparison of its own. On a `CLINIC_MEASURED` assessment this rule never engages, because every metric is mandatory there.

`risk_points_raw` is the straight sum of the blood-pressure points, the body-mass-index points, the A1c points, the LDL points, the tobacco points and any missing-metric points. **The third and sharpest divergence follows.** On a `CLINIC_MEASURED` assessment `risk_points_total` is simply `risk_points_raw`, unadjusted. On a `SELF_ATTESTED` assessment `risk_points_total` is `risk_points_raw` multiplied by 1.25 and then rounded half-up to a whole number — unverified numbers are discounted by loading the score. Identical measurements therefore score higher when self-attested than when clinic-measured, and that difference can by itself flip a participant from meeting the outcome standard to not meeting it.

From the total the server derives the rest. `risk_band` is `LOW` when `risk_points_total` is 3 or less, `MODERATE` when it falls between 4 and 7 with both ends included, and `HIGH` when it is 8 or more. `outcome_standard_met` is true when `risk_points_total` is 5 or less, the bound being inclusive, and false otherwise. `outcome_credit_status` starts as `MET_DIRECT` when the standard was met and `PENDING_ALTERNATIVE` when it was not; the third value, `CREDITED_VIA_RAS`, is reachable only through the alternative-standard cascade below and never at creation. Assessment creation is covered by the reporting-period freeze on `measurement_date` and by the eligibility rung on the same date, so an assessment dated into a finalized period is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_FINALIZED` and one dated outside the member's coverage span is refused with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_ON_DATE`. Assessments can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `visit_id` and by `source_type`, and both assessment reads sit behind the confidentiality wall.

**The reasonable alternative standard** exists because a participant may be unable to meet an outcome standard for reasons the program must accommodate. A `Participant` requests one for itself by naming its `participant_id`, the `health_assessment_id` whose outcome standard is at issue, and a `requested_reason` explaining why meeting the standard is unreasonably difficult or medically inadvisable. The request is accepted against any assessment the participant owns; the system does not additionally gate creation on the value of `outcome_standard_met`. A new request starts at `REQUESTED` with `alternative_type`, `assigned_at` and `due_date` all null, `reassignment_count` at 0 and `credit_granted` false.

A `CareNavigator` then either assigns or denies. **Assignment** moves the request `REQUESTED → ASSIGNED`, stores the chosen `alternative_type` — one of `COACHING_PROGRAM`, `EDUCATION_MODULE` or `PHYSICIAN_ALTERNATIVE` — stamps `assigned_at` with the clock instant, and sets `due_date` to the assignment date plus ninety days. Exactly one re-assignment is permitted: an already-`ASSIGNED` request may be assigned again, which replaces `alternative_type`, resets `assigned_at` and `due_date` from the new assignment date and raises `reassignment_count` to 1, and a second re-assignment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. **Denial** moves the request `REQUESTED → DENIED`, and its consequence is stated as much by what it does not do as by what it does: the backing assessment keeps `outcome_credit_status` at `PENDING_ALTERNATIVE`, no credit is granted, no incentive award is created, and no period or employer money moves.

**Completion is the deepest cascade in this system.** A `CareNavigator` completes an `ASSIGNED` request; the clock date must not fall strictly after `due_date`, with completion on the due date itself accepted, and the reporting period containing the backing assessment's `measurement_date` must not stand at `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED`. Before anything is written, the incentive program cap of §3.8 and the employer's wellness budget are both tested against the award this completion is about to create, in that order; a breach is refused with `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_WELLNESS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` and **nothing at all is written — the request stays `ASSIGNED`, `credit_granted` stays false and the backing assessment is untouched.**

On success five effects land in order. The request moves to `COMPLETED` and `credit_granted` becomes true. Caused by that, the backing `HealthAssessment` moves `outcome_credit_status` to `CREDITED_VIA_RAS` — while `outcome_standard_met` **keeps its original false value**, because the participant earned credit through the alternative rather than by meeting the standard. Caused by that, an `IncentiveAward` of `award_type` `OUTCOME_BASED` is created for the participant with `retroactive` true, `status` `PENDING` and `award_amount_cents` of 60000, and it is created in the reporting period whose closed span contains the assessment's `measurement_date` — **the original measurement period, not the period containing the completion date.** Caused by that, that reporting period recomputes `incentive_awarded_cents` and `incentive_pending_cents`. And caused by that, the employer recomputes `wellness_budget_committed_cents` and `wellness_budget_remaining_cents`. If no reporting period of that employer contains the measurement date at all, the first two effects still land and the last three do not — there is no period for the award to belong to, so no award is created.

### 3.7. Referral Steerage, Navigation Service Levels and Loop Closure

Not everything can be handled in the clinic. A `Clinician` or a `CareNavigator` raises a `Referral` out of an encounter, naming the `participant_id` being referred, the `visit_id` of the encounter that generated it, the requested `specialty` as free text, the `steerage_tier` of the intended destination and the navigation `urgency`. Referrals are raised from the encounter recorded in `visit_id`; the system does not additionally gate creation on that encounter's `encounter_status`. The server stamps `raised_by_provider_id` with the acting provider's identifier — this is the audit fact the segregation-of-duties rule later depends on — and leaves `authorized_by_provider_id`, `escalated_at`, `loop_closed_at` and `consult_note_reference` null.

`steerage_tier` is one of `CENTER_OF_EXCELLENCE`, `PREFERRED`, `STANDARD` or `OUT_OF_NETWORK`, and it decides whether a human has to look at the referral at all. A referral to a `CENTER_OF_EXCELLENCE` or a `PREFERRED` destination is auto-authorized by the system at the moment of creation, moving `SUBMITTED → AUTHORIZED` immediately; `authorized_by_provider_id` stays null on that path precisely because no human authorized it. A referral to a `STANDARD` or `OUT_OF_NETWORK` destination rests at `SUBMITTED` and waits for a navigator.

**The navigation service level** is computed at creation and stored as `sla_due_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, and it is dual-variant on `urgency`. For `EMERGENT` and `URGENT` referrals the deadline is pure wall-clock arithmetic from the logical clock instant at creation: four calendar hours for `EMERGENT`, forty-eight calendar hours for `URGENT`. Site closures, weekends and opening hours are all ignored on this branch — an `EMERGENT` referral raised on a Friday evening is still due four hours later, in the middle of the night. For a `ROUTINE` referral the deadline is measured in the site's own business days instead, the site being the one the referred encounter took place at, reached through the visit named in `visit_id`. Counting begins with the day **after** the creation date — the creation date itself never counts, whether or not the site is open on it — and advances through dates that are site-open, meaning dates whose ISO weekday appears in that site's `operating_weekdays` and which do not appear in that site's `closure_dates`. The tenth such site-open date is the due date, and `sla_due_at` is that date at the site's `close_time_utc`. The two variants therefore produce completely different answers from the same starting instant, and no single formula serves both.

**Authorization** of a `SUBMITTED` referral belongs to the `CareNavigator` and is available only for the `STANDARD` and `OUT_OF_NETWORK` tiers — the two auto-authorized tiers have already left `SUBMITTED`, so an attempt against them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Success sets `authorized_by_provider_id` to the acting navigator's provider identifier and moves the referral `SUBMITTED → AUTHORIZED`. The segregation-of-duties rule governs it: the acting navigator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the referral's `raised_by_provider_id`, because whoever raised a referral may not also authorize it, and a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

**Declining** moves a `SUBMITTED` referral to `DECLINED` and has no effect on any other resource. From `DECLINED` a navigator may **redirect**, which is where the referral graph becomes cyclic. A redirect supplies a fresh `specialty`, `steerage_tier` and `urgency`; it moves the declined referral to `REDIRECTED`, which is terminal for it, and creates a successor referral that copies `participant_id` and `visit_id` from its predecessor, takes the three supplied values, and records `redirected_from_referral_id` pointing back at the referral it came from. The successor computes its own `sla_due_at` afresh from the clock instant at redirect time, under the same dual-variant rule. Two properties of the successor are load-bearing. Its `raised_by_provider_id` is **copied unchanged** from the predecessor, so a navigator cannot launder a referral it raised through a redirect and then authorize the successor — the segregation-of-duties constraint survives the whole chain. And it starts at `SUBMITTED` **whatever its `steerage_tier`**: the auto-authorization that applies at original creation does not apply to a redirect successor, which must be authorized by a navigator like any other submission. Because a redirect can follow a decline, and the successor can itself be declined and redirected, the chain may loop indefinitely. For counting purposes only chain heads count — a referral whose `redirected_from_referral_id` is null — so a redirected chain is counted once however long it grows.

**Escalation** is not requested by anyone; it is swept by the clock. A referral standing at `SUBMITTED` or `AUTHORIZED` whose `sla_due_at` falls strictly before a new clock instant moves to `ESCALATED` with `escalated_at` stamped to that new instant. The comparison is strict on purpose: a referral standing at exactly its `sla_due_at` has not breached and does not escalate. Referrals that have already been declined, redirected or loop-closed are not swept.

**Loop closure** is the point of the whole exercise: the specialist's note has come back. A `CareNavigator` closes the loop on a referral standing at `AUTHORIZED` or at `ESCALATED` by supplying the `consult_note_reference` of the returned note, which moves the referral to `CLOSED_LOOP` and stamps `loop_closed_at` with the clock instant. Closing an escalated referral **preserves** its `escalated_at` — the breach happened and the record of it survives closure, because that is exactly what navigation performance reporting needs to see. A closure attempt against a referral at `SUBMITTED`, `DECLINED` or `REDIRECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and one that omits `consult_note_reference` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Referrals can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `visit_id`, by `redirected_from_referral_id`, by `raised_by_provider_id`, by `status` and by `urgency`. Both referral reads sit behind the confidentiality wall, so an employer-side caller reaching either is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_WALL` and never learns whether a given referral exists.

### 3.8. Incentive Awards: Amounts, the Regulatory Cap, and Payroll Settlement

Wellness incentives are money the employer pays a member through payroll, and they are the most heavily constrained object in this system. An `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` creates one by naming the `participant_id` earning it, the `reporting_period_id` it belongs to, the `award_type`, the `expected_participant_version` that proves the caller has seen the participant's current state, and — for one award type only — a `health_assessment_id`. The server copies `employer_id` from the participant, sets `retroactive` to false on a directly created award, starts `status` at `PENDING`, leaves `approved_by_user_id`, `exported_by_user_id` and `payroll_reference` null, and raises the participant's `version` by one.

**The amount is fixed by type, not negotiated.** An `OUTCOME_BASED` award is 60000 cents. A `PARTICIPATORY` award is 15000 cents. A `TOBACCO_CESSATION` award is 45000 cents. No rounding occurs — these are whole cents by construction — and `award_amount_cents` is written by the server from `award_type` alone.

The two families behave quite differently. `OUTCOME_BASED` is **health-contingent**: it requires `health_assessment_id`, and a direct creation is accepted only when the named assessment's `outcome_credit_status` reads `MET_DIRECT`. An assessment still at `PENDING_ALTERNATIVE` has not earned anything yet, and an assessment at `CREDITED_VIA_RAS` has already had its award created for it by the alternative-standard cascade; naming either one on a direct creation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and so is a second `OUTCOME_BASED` award for the same participant in the same reporting period. `PARTICIPATORY` and `TOBACCO_CESSATION` are **participation-based**: `health_assessment_id` must be null on both, no outcome standard is consulted at all, and a participant sitting in the `HIGH` risk band can earn either of them exactly as readily as anyone else. Supplying an assessment identifier on a participation-based award, or omitting one on an outcome-based award, is refused earlier with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**The program cap** is the regulatory ceiling on health-contingent incentives and it is dual-variant on the employer's own configuration. When the employer's `tobacco_program_enabled` is false the cap is thirty percent of `annual_total_cost_of_coverage_cents`; when it is true the cap rises to fifty percent of that same base, so an award set that breaches the standard cap is accepted unchanged under the tobacco-inclusive one. The cap is a whole number of cents, obtained by multiplying the annual cost of coverage by the applicable percentage and rounding half-up. Against that ceiling the server measures the participant's committed cents — the sum of `award_amount_cents` across awards for **the same participant and the same reporting period** whose `status` is `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `EXPORTED` or `PAID`. Awards that have been `REVERSED` or `FORFEITED` release their headroom and are excluded from the sum. The candidate award is accepted when the committed cents plus the candidate amount is less than or equal to the cap, the bound being **inclusive** — an award that lands exactly on the cap is allowed — and is refused with `ERR_INCENTIVE_CAP_EXCEEDED` only when the sum is strictly greater.

**The wellness budget** is the employer's own funding ceiling and is tested immediately after the program cap. The employer's `wellness_budget_committed_cents` plus the candidate amount must be less than or equal to `wellness_budget_cents`, again inclusively; a strictly greater sum is refused with `ERR_WELLNESS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Because the cap rung sits above the budget rung, an award that breaches both reports the cap error. On success the award's reporting period recomputes its five money buckets and the employer recomputes `wellness_budget_committed_cents` and `wellness_budget_remaining_cents`.

**Settlement runs through four roles' hands, and each hand is a separate transition.** Approval by an `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` moves the award `PENDING → APPROVED` and stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; it is available only while the award's reporting period stands at `OPEN` or `RESTATING`, and an approval against a period at `FINALIZED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The money does not move buckets on approval — an `APPROVED` award still counts toward `incentive_pending_cents`. Export by a `PayrollProcessor` moves the award `APPROVED → EXPORTED`, requires a `payroll_reference` naming the payroll batch, and stamps both `payroll_reference` and `exported_by_user_id`; an exported award still counts toward the pending bucket. Marking paid, also by a `PayrollProcessor`, moves the award `EXPORTED → PAID` and is the first transition that actually moves money: the amount leaves `incentive_pending_cents` and enters `incentive_paid_cents`, while `incentive_awarded_cents` stays exactly where it was.

**Reversal** is available to the `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` from two states and behaves symmetrically from both. From `APPROVED` it moves the award to `REVERSED` and shifts the amount out of `incentive_pending_cents` into `incentive_reversed_cents`. From `PAID` it moves the award to `REVERSED` and shifts the amount out of `incentive_paid_cents` into `incentive_reversed_cents`. In both cases `incentive_awarded_cents` is unchanged, and in both cases the employer recomputes `wellness_budget_committed_cents`, because a reversed award no longer commits budget and its cap headroom is released for a later award. A reversal attempt from `PENDING`, `EXPORTED`, `FORFEITED` or `REVERSED` finds no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Forfeiture** is never requested; it is the money branch of the enrollment-termination cascade in §3.3. It reaches only awards standing at `PENDING`, moving them to `FORFEITED` and shifting the amount out of `incentive_pending_cents` into `incentive_forfeited_cents` while `incentive_awarded_cents` holds steady, after which the employer recomputes `wellness_budget_committed_cents`. Awards at `APPROVED`, `EXPORTED` or `PAID` are pointedly left alone by that cascade. `PAID`, `REVERSED` and `FORFEITED` are all terminal.

Awards can be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `employer_id`, by `reporting_period_id`, by `health_assessment_id` and by `status`. The award collection is not behind the confidentiality wall — an incentive is a payroll fact, not a clinical one — and `PayrollProcessor` may read it even though it may read almost nothing else.

### 3.9. Reporting Periods, Finalization, Restatement and the Utilization Report

A `ReportingPeriod` is the unit of financial truth. A `ProgramAdmin` opens one for an employer by naming the `employer_id`, the `period_start` and the `period_end`; **the interval is closed at both ends**, so a record dated on either boundary falls inside the period. A new period starts at `OPEN` with `restatement_count` at 0, `finalized_at` null and all five incentive money buckets — `incentive_awarded_cents`, `incentive_pending_cents`, `incentive_paid_cents`, `incentive_reversed_cents` and `incentive_forfeited_cents` — at 0. Periods can be listed and filtered by `employer_id` and by `status`.

**Finalization** moves the period out of `OPEN` and sets everything downstream in motion. From `OPEN` it requires the clock date to fall strictly after `period_end` — a period cannot be finalized while it is still running — and it moves the period `OPEN → FINALIZED`. Four effects follow in a fixed order. First, `finalized_at` is stamped with the clock instant and the period's aggregates are frozen. Caused by that, every visit of this employer whose `service_date` falls inside the closed span and whose `encounter_status` is `COMPLETED` or `AMENDED` moves to `LOCKED`; visits still at `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_PROGRESS` are not touched. Caused by that lock, every `ACTIVE` vendor contract of this employer is priced and moves to `TRUED_UP`. And caused by that pricing, the employer's `program_cost_to_date_cents` rises by the sum of those contracts' `contract_cost_cents`.

**Contract pricing has a pinned evaluation order**, because the quantities are otherwise circular — the true-up depends on the gross fee, the program cost depends on the true-up, and both the per-employee-per-month figure and the return on investment depend on the program cost. The order is: freeze the visit set; compute the gross fee; compute the true-up from the gross fee; add the two into the contract cost; and only then compute the report-level program cost, per-employee-per-month cost and ROI ratio.

The frozen visit set for one contract is the visits of that employer's participants at the contract's own `site_id` whose `service_date` falls inside the period's closed span and whose `encounter_status` is `LOCKED` after the locking step above; `billable_visit_count` is the size of that set. `staffed_hours` on the contract is the sum, over every staffing shift at the contract's `site_id` whose `shift_start` falls inside the period from `period_start` at midnight UTC through `period_end` at one second before the following midnight UTC — both bounds inclusive — of that shift's own `staffed_hours`, where **each shift's hours are rounded half-up to two decimals first and the already-rounded values are then added together.**

`gross_fee_cents` is dual-variant on `fee_model`. Under `PER_VISIT` it is `billable_visit_count` multiplied by `per_visit_fee_cents`, and staffing hours, although recorded, do not price the contract at all. Under `FTE_HOUR` it is `staffed_hours` multiplied by `fte_hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents, and the visit count, although recorded, does not price the contract — which is why a period with no visits at all still bills the full staffed hours under this model, and why the minimum-guarantee true-up fires far less often here than it does under per-visit pricing. `true_up_amount_cents` is then whichever is larger of zero and `minimum_guarantee_cents` less `gross_fee_cents`, so it is zero whenever the gross fee already meets or exceeds the guarantee. `contract_cost_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` plus `true_up_amount_cents`.

**Restatement** reopens a finalized period for late corrections. It moves the period `FINALIZED → RESTATING`, raises `restatement_count` by one, and returns every `LOCKED` visit whose `service_date` falls inside the period to `AMENDED` — note that a visit that entered the lock from `COMPLETED` comes back as `AMENDED`, not as `COMPLETED`. Restatement is available only while `restatement_count` is strictly less than two, so a period may be reopened at most twice; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is any restatement of a period that is `OPEN`, `RESTATING` or `CLOSED`. Restatement does **not** unwind pricing: vendor contracts stay `TRUED_UP` holding their previously computed figures, and the employer's `program_cost_to_date_cents` keeps the amount already booked until the next finalization adjusts it.

Finalizing a `RESTATING` period runs the same four effects, with two differences from the `OPEN` path. There is **no** requirement that the clock be past `period_end` — that condition guards only the first finalization. And contracts that are already `TRUED_UP` recompute in place rather than being skipped, with the employer's `program_cost_to_date_cents` adjusted by the difference between the newly computed contract costs and the previously booked ones rather than by the full amount again. Where the restatement allowance still has room — `restatement_count` strictly less than two — the period returns to `FINALIZED` and may be reopened once more. Where the allowance is exhausted — `restatement_count` exactly two — the same finalization lands in `CLOSED` instead, running the identical four effects and rendering the period permanently immutable: no further restatement is possible from `CLOSED`, and the rung-6 freeze applies to `CLOSED` periods exactly as it does to `FINALIZED` ones.

**The utilization report** is computed fresh on every read and is never stored. It is available to `ProgramAdmin` and `EmployerBenefitsAdmin`, and it echoes the period it describes as `reporting_period_id`.

Three population figures anchor it. `eligible_population_count` counts the eligibility rows of the period's employer whose closed coverage span — from `effective_date` through `termination_date`, with a null termination meaning unbounded into the future — shares at least one date with the period's closed span; people who were eligible but never enrolled are counted here. `enrolled_population_count` counts the participants of that employer whose `enrolled_on` falls on or before `period_end`, in any `enrollment_status` — `ACTIVE`, `TERMINATED` and `CLOSED` all count — that is, everybody who was enrolled at any point up to the last day of the period. `utilizing_participant_count` counts the **distinct** participants over the employer's visits whose `service_date` falls inside the period's closed span and whose `encounter_status` is `COMPLETED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED`, and `completed_visit_count` counts those same visits without deduplication. Cancelled and no-show appointments never enter any of the four figures, because they never produced a visit.

From those the report derives three ratios, each rounded half-up to four decimal places, and each with a different denominator, so that no single formula can produce all three. `engagement_rate_over_eligible` divides `utilizing_participant_count` by `eligible_population_count` — the largest denominator and therefore the smallest rate, since it includes people who never enrolled — and is exactly 0.0 when the eligible population is zero. `engagement_rate_over_enrolled` divides the same numerator by `enrolled_population_count`, so once-enrolled people who have since terminated still count in the denominator while never-enrolled eligible people do not, and it is exactly 0.0 when the enrolled population is zero. `visits_per_utilizer` is not a rate at all but an intensity: its numerator switches from distinct participants to the raw `completed_visit_count`, its denominator is `utilizing_participant_count`, and it is exactly 0.0 when there are no utilizers.

Six cell figures break the visits down by track: `primary_care_visit_count` and `primary_care_participant_count`, `occ_health_visit_count` and `occ_health_participant_count`, and `wellness_visit_count` and `wellness_participant_count`. Each visit count is the number of in-period visits of that `visit_type` in `COMPLETED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED`, and each participant count is the distinct participants behind them.

**Avoided cost** is the business case for the clinic, and its rounding order is pinned. For each visit type in the fixed order `PRIMARY_CARE`, `OCC_HEALTH`, `WELLNESS`, take the count of in-period visits of that type in `COMPLETED`, `AMENDED` or `LOCKED`, multiply by that track's diversion rate — 0.62 for primary care, 0.40 for occupational health, 0.15 for wellness, these being the shares of visits deemed to have displaced an outside encounter — and then by that track's benchmark outside cost, which is 185.33 dollars, that is 18533 cents, for primary care, 240.11 dollars, that is 24011 cents, for occupational health, and 90.07 dollars, that is 9007 cents, for wellness. **Round each of the three category products half-up to whole cents first, and only then add the three rounded values together** to obtain `avoided_cost_cents`; summing the raw products and rounding once at the end produces a different total and is wrong.

`program_cost_cents` is the sum of `contract_cost_cents` over the employer's vendor contracts whose `status` is `TRUED_UP`; contracts still `ACTIVE`, because the period has not been finalized, contribute zero. `roi_ratio` divides the already-rounded `avoided_cost_cents` by `program_cost_cents` and rounds half-up to four decimal places, and is exactly 0.0 when the program cost is zero.

`pepm_cents` — the per-employee-per-month cost — is computed after the ROI figures and reuses their inputs rather than recomputing them. Count the distinct calendar months the period touches, both bounds inclusive: a period running from the first of January to the thirty-first of March touches three, and a period running from the fifteenth of January to the second of February touches two. Then divide `program_cost_cents` by the enrolled population multiplied by that month count, rounding half-up to whole cents. When the enrolled population is zero, or the month count is zero, `pepm_cents` is exactly 0.

**Small-cell suppression is applied last, after every figure above has been computed, and only when the caller is an employer-side role.** A `ProgramAdmin` sees the report exactly as computed, with `suppressed_cell_keys` an empty array. For an `EmployerBenefitsAdmin` the three visit-type cells are examined in the fixed order `PRIMARY_CARE`, `OCC_HEALTH`, `WELLNESS`, using each cell's distinct participant count as the test. A cell with zero distinct participants is reported as it stands, with its visit count and participant count both 0, and is **not** suppressed — an empty cell re-identifies nobody. A cell with at least one and strictly fewer than six distinct participants has both its visit count and its participant count replaced by null, and the visit-type name is appended to `suppressed_cell_keys`. A cell with six or more distinct participants is reported unchanged. The utilizing cell is protected on the same rule: when `utilizing_participant_count` is at least one and strictly fewer than six, both `utilizing_participant_count` and `visits_per_utilizer` are replaced by null and the key `UTILIZING` is appended to `suppressed_cell_keys` **after** any visit-type keys. Everything else survives suppression untouched — `engagement_rate_over_eligible`, `engagement_rate_over_enrolled`, `eligible_population_count`, `enrolled_population_count`, `completed_visit_count`, `avoided_cost_cents`, `program_cost_cents`, `roi_ratio` and `pepm_cents` are never suppressed. Suppression replaces a number with null; it never rounds, bands or approximates a figure.
