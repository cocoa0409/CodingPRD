# Public Procurement Protest Debarment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state procurement office buys on behalf of dozens of agencies, and every dollar it commits is subject to challenge. The office advertises a solicitation, answers written questions, issues addenda that move the bid due date and add mandatory requirements, opens sealed bids in public, decides which bids are responsive, reviews whether the apparent low bidder is responsible, and only then issues a notice of award. At any point in that sequence a disappointed or prospective bidder may file a protest, and a timely protest freezes the award automatically. Separately, and on a slower clock, the office runs suspension and debarment proceedings against vendors for statutory cause, maintains a public debarred-vendor list, screens proposed subcontractors against it, and entertains reinstatement petitions once a portion of the debarment period has been served.

Today this work is spread across spreadsheets, email threads and a document management system, and the two things that matter most — *did we compute the deadline correctly* and *did the same official decide both sides of the question* — are enforced by habit rather than by software. The consequences are expensive: a protest sustained on a procedural defect voids an award that has already been executed, and every downstream determination has to be unwound by hand. Vendors, meanwhile, cannot tell whether their protest was filed in time until an officer tells them, and the debarred-vendor list is only as fresh as the last person who remembered to edit it.

This document specifies the API that replaces that arrangement. It is a system of record and a system of enforcement at once: it computes filing fees, timeliness deadlines, evaluated bid prices, responsibility scores, debarment severity and reinstatement eligibility from pinned formulas; it propagates the consequences of a sustained protest or a vendor debarment through bids, awards and solicitations in the same request that caused them; and it refuses operations that would put one official on both sides of an adjudication. Because deadlines are the substance of the domain rather than an incidental detail, the server runs on a logical clock that tests and operators drive explicitly, and every window, expiry and occupancy check is evaluated against that clock rather than against wall time. The whole surface is mounted under `/procurement`, with a single administrative clock endpoint outside that mount.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority the caller is exercising. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before anything else, including body shape validation, so a malformed payload sent by an unauthenticated caller yields the authentication error rather than a validation error.

The six roles are `ProcurementOfficer`, `ResponsibilityReviewer`, `HearingOfficer`, `DebarmentAuthority`, `Vendor` and `SystemAdmin`.

A `ProcurementOfficer` is the state procurement office buyer. Officers draft, advertise and amend solicitations, close the question period, open bids, record responsiveness determinations, issue and execute awards, cancel and resolicit procurements, file the agency report into a protest record, screen subcontractors, and may override an automatic stay on written urgency findings. A `ResponsibilityReviewer` belongs to the responsibility review unit and scores the apparent low bidder's financial capacity, past performance and business integrity. A `HearingOfficer` presides over protest hearings: scheduling, convening, continuing and concluding them, and issuing the protest decision. A `DebarmentAuthority` is the suspension and debarment official who issues notices of proposed debarment, holds debarment hearings, decides them on the merits and rules on reinstatement petitions. A `SystemAdmin` maintains the vendor file, credits protest-fee balances and drives the logical clock, and holds no adjudicative authority whatsoever. A `Vendor` is a bidder or a respondent: it submits written questions and sealed bids, files and withdraws protests, responds to debarment notices, petitions for reinstatement, deposits protest-fee funds and may request subcontractor screenings.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the vendor role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProcurementOfficer`, `ResponsibilityReviewer`, `HearingOfficer`, `DebarmentAuthority` or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `issued_by_user_id` on a solicitation when an officer advertises it, as `responsiveness_decided_by_user_id` on a bid when an officer determines responsiveness, as `reviewer_user_id` on a responsibility review, and as `submitted_by_role` (the caller's `X-User-Role` rather than its id) on an agency record item — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Vendor`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` row, and ownership checks on any resource that references a vendor compare the resource's vendor-bearing field to `X-User-ID`: `vendor_id` on a bid, `vendor_id` on a question submission, `protester_vendor_id` on a protest, `vendor_id` on a debarment proceeding, and `prime_vendor_id` on a subcontractor screening. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Read access is deliberately wide: every listed role, including `Vendor`, may read any single resource and any list. Write authority is narrow and is stated per endpoint in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Result Ordering

Collection endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position, defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current window.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three collections override that default because their domain order is different. The addenda of a solicitation are returned in ascending `sequence_number`. The agency record of a protest is returned in ascending `sequence_number`, which is the order in which documents were filed. The debarred-vendor list has its own snapshot ordering, described in §3.9.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits, stamped once at creation and never changed thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. Vendors, solicitations, bids, responsibility reviews, protests, protest hearings, awards, debarment proceedings, reinstatement petitions and subcontractor screenings all carry both. Four resource types are append-only journal rows and carry neither: a `MandatoryRequirement`, an `Addendum`, a `QuestionSubmission` and an `AgencyRecordItem` expose only `id` and `created_at`. For those four, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely — not null, absent.

Timekeeping is explicit rather than ambient. The server maintains a logical clock, and every deadline, window, expiry, occupancy and eligibility comparison in this specification is evaluated against that clock and never against real wall-clock time. Before any call to the clock capability, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`, and every subsequent evaluation uses the new instant. Because the clock is the only source of "now", a test can place a solicitation's bid due date in the past, advance the clock past a debarment's end instant, or file a protest at exactly the deadline instant, all deterministically.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Resolution Order

Nested URLs are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request naming a solicitation that does not exist fails with `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND` before the server ever looks at the child resource named further right in the path. The per-segment mapping is: a vendor segment yields `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, a solicitation segment `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`, a bid segment `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, a protest segment `ERR_PROTEST_NOT_FOUND`, a hearing segment `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`, an award segment `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, a debarment-proceeding segment `ERR_PROCEEDING_NOT_FOUND`, a reinstatement-petition segment `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND` and a subcontractor-screening segment `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND`. All nine are HTTP 404.

A child resource created through a nested path stores its parent link as a persisted field rather than inferring it from the URL, and that field is present in the child's payload: a responsibility review carries `bid_id`, a protest hearing and an agency record item each carry `protest_id`, a reinstatement petition carries `proceeding_id`, and a mandatory requirement, an addendum and a question submission each carry `solicitation_id`. Those fields are never supplied by the client on a nested create; the server sets them from the resolved path segment.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved. A `solicitation_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `vendor_id`, `prime_vendor_id`, `sub_vendor_id` or `protester_vendor_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `winning_bid_id` or `challenged_bid_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`; a `source_protest_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROTEST_NOT_FOUND`; and a member of `answers_question_ids` that does not resolve yields `ERR_QUESTION_NOT_FOUND`. Resolution is separate from relationship checking: a bid that exists but belongs to a different solicitation resolves successfully and then fails the referential check with `ERR_BID_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION` (HTTP 422), and a question that exists but hangs off another solicitation fails with `ERR_QUESTION_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION` (HTTP 422).

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Bid Filing and Reinstatement Filing

Two create operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a second create against identical state would itself legitimately succeed and so cannot be distinguished from a lost update by shape alone.

A `POST /procurement/bids` request must carry `expected_solicitation_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `Solicitation` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the stored solicitation's current `version` — because an addendum was issued, because the question period was closed, or because another bid create advanced it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SOLICITATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a vendor may legitimately replace its own earlier bid on the same solicitation, so the create cannot be made idempotent; pinning the version guarantees that the bidder saw the current mandatory requirements and the current bid due date before it priced its offer.

A `POST /procurement/debarment-proceedings/{id}/reinstatement-petitions` request must likewise carry `expected_proceeding_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `DebarmentProceeding` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the proceeding's stored `version` — most commonly because a prior petition decision advanced it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROCEEDING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A vendor may file a fresh petition after an earlier one was `DENIED`, so again the create is legitimately repeatable and the version pin is what prevents a client from filing against a proceeding it has not re-read.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be rejected for the absence of one.

### 2.7. Monetary and Score Arithmetic

All money in this system is carried as integer cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or a response payload. Where a computation produces a fractional cent, it is resolved with round-half-up to a whole cent, and the specification always pins *where* in the computation that rounding happens, because the alternatives differ by one cent on exact-half boundaries. Two such orderings are contract, not convenience: the protest filing fee rounds each of its three components to a whole cent and then adds the rounded components, and the award obligation applies one rounding to the administrative fee and a second rounding to the reserve computed on the already-rounded fee. Neither computation is permitted to sum unrounded parts and round once at the end.

Two scores are carried as decimal numbers rather than integers. A responsibility review's `composite_score` is a number in the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00 with two decimal places, produced by a single round-half-up applied once at the end of its derivation. A debarment proceeding's `severity_score` is likewise a number carried to two decimal places with round-half-up. Time arithmetic uses two units: whole calendar days, added to a UTC instant while preserving its time-of-day component, and whole calendar months, added while preserving the day-of-month and clamping to the last day of the target month at the same time of day when the target month is shorter than the source day-of-month. Instants are compared at millisecond precision.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Two adjudicative pairings are structurally forbidden, and both are enforced at HTTP 403 rather than as a business-rule conflict, because they are questions of authority rather than of state.

The official who advertised a solicitation may not sit on a protest against it. The `hearing_officer_user_id` supplied when a hearing is scheduled must differ from the `issued_by_user_id` recorded on the protested solicitation, and the `X-User-ID` of the `HearingOfficer` who issues the decision must likewise differ from it. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`. Note that the check applies at both moments independently: a hearing can be scheduled with a clean officer and still fail at decision time if a different, conflicted `HearingOfficer` attempts to decide.

The officer who determined a bid's responsiveness may not also review that bidder's responsibility. The `X-User-ID` of the acting `ResponsibilityReviewer` must differ from the `responsiveness_decided_by_user_id` stamped on the bid; if they match, the review is rejected with `ERR_REVIEWER_CONFLICT`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and the Immediate-Consistency Rule

Consistency in this system is immediate. When one operation implies consequences for other rows, those consequences are applied inside the same request, before the response is returned; there is no deferred queue and no eventual reconciliation. Two named cascade chains carry most of that weight, and both are described end to end in §3 where the business events that trigger them live. Cascade A begins with a protest sustained on corrective-action grounds and walks outward through the award, the solicitation, the bids and the responsibility reviews. Cascade B begins with a vendor becoming debarred and walks outward through that vendor's bids, the affected solicitations and their pending awards.

Three mechanical rules govern every cascade. First, each row that a cascade mutates has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by exactly 1 — a cascade that touches a bid, its solicitation and an award increments three separate version counters, not one. Second, a computed aggregate is always recomputed from its inputs rather than adjusted incrementally, so a solicitation's `apparent_low_bid_id`, `cumulative_extension_days`, `addendum_count`, a protest's `record_item_count` and a vendor's `debarment_strike_count` are each re-derived from the rows they summarize whenever any of those rows changes. Third, a cascade never partially applies: if any step of the originating operation fails a check, nothing is written at all.

One asymmetry deserves emphasis because it is the most common source of surprise. Compensating actions restore fiscal and eligibility state but never restore a responsiveness determination. When a debarment proceeding is dismissed and its respondent returns to `ELIGIBLE`, the strike is returned and blocked subcontractor screenings are un-blocked, but bids that were already marked `NONRESPONSIVE` by the suspension cascade stay `NONRESPONSIVE` — nonresponsiveness is final once determined.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed evaluation order rather than by the order the checks happen to appear in an implementation. The pipeline is:

1. Authentication of the headers, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request-body shape: every required input field present, every enum member recognised, every integer inside its declared range, a hearing window whose `scheduled_end_utc` is strictly after its `scheduled_start_utc` and whose length is a whole number of minutes, an `original_question_deadline_utc` strictly before the `original_bid_due_utc`, and every array declared non-empty actually non-empty — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Distinctness of the code lists: `aggravating_factor_codes`, `mitigating_factor_codes` and `remediation_evidence_codes` must each contain no repeated member. This applies when a debarment proceeding is opened, when a respondent files its written response, and when a reinstatement petition is filed — `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACTOR_CODE`.
5. Existence of every path resource, left to right, per §2.5.
6. Resolution of every body foreign key, per §2.5.
7. Uniqueness: a vendor's `tax_id` is unique across the whole vendor file (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID` when a vendor is registered); a `solicitation_number` is unique across all solicitations (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SOLICITATION_NUMBER` when a solicitation is drafted or a successor is created by resolicitation); a `requirement_code` is unique within one solicitation (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT_CODE` when a solicitation is drafted or an addendum introduces requirements).
8. Vendor ownership, per §2.1 — `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. This applies to bid submission, question submission, protest filing, protest withdrawal, fee deposits, a debarment response, a reinstatement petition and a subcontractor screening request.
9. Version pinning, per §2.6 — `ERR_STALE_SOLICITATION_VERSION` on bid submission, `ERR_STALE_PROCEEDING_VERSION` on reinstatement-petition filing.
10. Solicitation state admissibility for the requested operation — `ERR_INVALID_SOLICITATION_STATE`, applying to issuance, addendum issuance, question-period closure, bid opening, cancellation, resolicitation, bid submission, responsiveness determination and award creation.
11. Protestability of the solicitation: a protest is accepted only when the solicitation's `status` is one of `ISSUED`, `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `BIDS_OPENED`, `EVALUATION`, `AWARD_PENDING` or `AWARDED`; a `DRAFT`, `CANCELLED` or `RESOLICITED` solicitation accepts no protest — `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_PROTESTABLE`.
12. Bid state admissibility, on both the responsiveness determination and the responsibility review — `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE`.
13. Protest state admissibility, on hearing scheduling, decision issuance, withdrawal, stay override and record-item filing — `ERR_INVALID_PROTEST_STATE`.
14. Hearing state admissibility, on convening, continuing and concluding — `ERR_INVALID_HEARING_STATE`.
15. Proceeding state admissibility, on the response, the hearing, the decision and petition filing — `ERR_INVALID_PROCEEDING_STATE`.
16. Petition state admissibility, on a petition decision — `ERR_INVALID_PETITION_STATE`.
17. Period gates: a question submitted once the solicitation has reached `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` or beyond (`ERR_QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`); closing the question period while the clock is before `question_deadline_utc` (`ERR_QUESTION_PERIOD_OPEN`); opening bids while the clock is before `effective_bid_due_utc` (`ERR_BID_PERIOD_OPEN`); submitting a bid once the clock is strictly after `effective_bid_due_utc` (`ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED`).
18. Vendor eligibility: the acting or referenced vendor's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`, on bid submission and on a `RESPONSIBLE` responsibility determination — `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
19. A cancelled solicitation must not already have a successor, on resolicitation — `ERR_ALREADY_RESOLICITED`.
20. The clock must have reached the hearing's `scheduled_start_utc`, on convening — `ERR_HEARING_NOT_YET_STARTED`.
21. Hearing-officer segregation of duties, per §2.8, on hearing scheduling and on decision issuance — `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`.
22. Reviewer segregation of duties, per §2.8, on the responsibility review — `ERR_REVIEWER_CONFLICT`.
23. Protester standing, on protest filing — `ERR_PROTESTER_LACKS_STANDING`.
24. Agency-record sufficiency: convening requires at least one record item; concluding a hearing and issuing a protest decision each require at least two — `ERR_AGENCY_RECORD_INCOMPLETE` on all three operations.
25. A protest decision requires a concluded hearing — `ERR_HEARING_NOT_CONCLUDED`.
26. A debarment hearing taken from `NOTICE_ISSUED` requires the response window to have expired — `ERR_DEBARMENT_HEARING_RIGHTS_NOT_EXHAUSTED`.
27. Material addendum requirements must be acknowledged before a bid may be found responsive — `ERR_MATERIAL_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`.
28. Interim suspension requires its severity threshold — `ERR_INTERIM_SUSPENSION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`.
29. A debarment outcome requires its severity floor — `ERR_SEVERITY_BELOW_DEBARMENT_FLOOR`.
30. A responsible determination requires its composite-score threshold — `ERR_RESPONSIBILITY_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.
31. Protest timeliness — `ERR_PROTEST_UNTIMELY`.
32. Existence of the trigger event the protest's grounds require — `ERR_PROTEST_TRIGGER_EVENT_MISSING`.
33. Reinstatement-petition ripeness — `ERR_PETITION_PREMATURE`.
34. The debarment response window — `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
35. Hearing-room occupancy including the changeover buffer, on hearing scheduling and on a continuance — `ERR_HEARING_ROOM_CONFLICT`.
36. Hearing-officer daily docket capacity, on hearing scheduling and on a continuance — `ERR_OFFICER_DOCKET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
37. Respondent-vendor compliance-docket occupancy, when a new proceeding is opened — `ERR_ACTIVE_PROCEEDING_EXISTS`.
38. The cumulative bid-due extension cap, on addendum issuance — `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
39. The continuance limit — `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
40. At least one mandatory requirement before issuance — `ERR_NO_MANDATORY_REQUIREMENTS`.
41. At least one non-withdrawn bid before opening — `ERR_NO_BIDS_RECEIVED`.
42. An identified apparent low bidder before award creation — `ERR_NO_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`.
43. No active stay, on award creation and on award execution — `ERR_AWARD_STAYED`.
44. Subcontract tier range and the self-subcontract prohibition, on a screening request — `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_TIER_UNSUPPORTED` and `ERR_SELF_SUBCONTRACT`.
45. Cross-parent referential checks: a supplied `challenged_bid_id` must belong to the protested solicitation and a `winning_bid_id` to the awarded solicitation (`ERR_BID_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION`); members of `answers_question_ids` must belong to the addendum's solicitation (`ERR_QUESTION_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION`).
46. Apparent-low-bidder identity, on the responsibility review and on award creation — `ERR_NOT_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`.
47. A respondent must not already be debarred when a new proceeding is opened (`ERR_VENDOR_ALREADY_DEBARRED`), and no petition may already be pending on the proceeding when a new one is filed (`ERR_PETITION_ALREADY_PENDING`).
48. Sufficiency of the protester's fee balance, on protest filing — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEE_BALANCE` (HTTP 402).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Vendors and Funding the Protest Purse

Before anything else can happen, a `SystemAdmin` registers each vendor in the state vendor file, supplying the vendor's registered `legal_name` (between 1 and 200 characters), its `tax_id` and its notice-of-record `contact_email`. The `tax_id` is a state taxpayer identifier consisting of exactly nine digits and must be unique across the whole vendor file; a second registration reusing an existing identifier is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID`. A newly registered vendor starts life with `eligibility_status` set to `ELIGIBLE`, a `protest_fee_balance_cents` of 0, a `debarment_strike_count` of 0, a null `active_debarment_id`, a null `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc`, a null `suspension_reason_code` and `version` 1.

Protest filing in this jurisdiction is fee-bearing and pre-funded, so the vendor file doubles as a purse. A deposit credits the vendor's `protest_fee_balance_cents` by the supplied `amount_cents` and bumps the vendor's `version`; the amount must be strictly positive, and a zero or negative deposit is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A deposit brings no deposit row of its own into existence — there is no `FeeDeposit` entity anywhere in this system, only the mutation of the vendor — so the endpoint answers with the modify-endpoint status of §4, HTTP 200, carrying the updated vendor object in `data`. Either a `SystemAdmin` or the owning `Vendor` may make a deposit, but a `Vendor` may only deposit into its own purse — a mismatch between `X-User-ID` and the vendor named in the path is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The balance is unencumbered by construction: filing a protest debits it, a refund re-credits it, and it may never go below zero.

Callers can search the vendor file by `eligibility_status`, by `tax_id` and by `active_debarment_id`, which is useful for asking "which vendors are currently attached to this proceeding". A vendor's own question history across every solicitation is also retrievable in one place, optionally narrowed by question `status`.

### 3.2. Drafting and Advertising a Solicitation

A `ProcurementOfficer` drafts a solicitation by supplying its publicly advertised `solicitation_number` (unique across all solicitations, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOLICITATION_NUMBER`), a short `title`, the `issuing_agency_code` of the agency being bought for, the agency's `estimated_value_cents` (strictly greater than zero), the `original_bid_due_utc` at which bids will be due as first advertised, the `original_question_deadline_utc` at which the question period will first close, and a `mandatory_requirements` list. An optional `predecessor_solicitation_id` may be supplied when the draft is being created as the successor of an earlier procurement; on an originally advertised solicitation it is null. The question deadline must be strictly before the bid due date, and a draft violating that ordering is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Each entry of `mandatory_requirements` becomes a `MandatoryRequirement` row carrying a `requirement_code` unique within the solicitation, a prose `description`, a `materiality` of either `MATERIAL` or `INFORMALITY`, and an `is_waivable` flag that defaults to false and records whether the procurement officer may waive non-compliance with that requirement. Requirements created at draft time carry `introduced_by_addendum_sequence` of 0, meaning they were part of the original solicitation rather than added later. A repeated `requirement_code` within the same solicitation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT_CODE`. The solicitation itself is created in `status` `DRAFT`.

Advertising the solicitation moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED` and is available only to a `ProcurementOfficer`. Two preconditions guard it: the solicitation must carry at least one mandatory requirement, otherwise `ERR_NO_MANDATORY_REQUIREMENTS`; and the `original_question_deadline_utc` must still lie strictly in the future relative to the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SOLICITATION_STATE` — there is no point advertising a question period that has already expired. Issuance stamps `issued_by_user_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, which is the value the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.8 will later compare against, sets `effective_bid_due_utc` equal to `original_bid_due_utc`, sets `cumulative_extension_days` to 0, sets `question_deadline_utc` equal to `original_question_deadline_utc`, and increments the solicitation's `version` by 1. Neither `original_bid_due_utc` nor `original_question_deadline_utc` is ever mutated again; they are the historical record of what was first advertised.

Solicitations can be listed and filtered by `status`, by `issuing_agency_code`, by `predecessor_solicitation_id` and by `apparent_low_bid_id`.

### 3.3. The Question Period, Addenda and the Moving Bid Due Date

Once a solicitation is `ISSUED`, prospective bidders may submit written questions. A `Vendor` submits a question by supplying its own `vendor_id` and the `question_text`; the server stamps `submitted_at_utc` from the logical clock. A question filed while the clock is at or before the solicitation's current `question_deadline_utc` is created in `status` `PENDING`. A question filed after that instant, while the solicitation is still `ISSUED`, is created directly in `status` `REJECTED_LATE` and can never be answered — the row exists so that the record shows the question was asked, and its `answer_summary` and `answered_via_addendum_sequence` both stay null forever. Once the solicitation has advanced to `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` or beyond, a question submission is refused outright with `ERR_QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` and no row is created at all. Questions on a solicitation can be listed and filtered by `status`.

Addenda are the officer's instrument for amending an advertised solicitation, and a single addendum can do three things at once. Issuing one requires the solicitation to be `ISSUED`, and the solicitation stays `ISSUED` afterwards. The officer supplies a prose `summary` of what changes, optionally an `extends_bid_due_by_days` count of whole calendar days to add to the bid due date (defaulting to 0, and individually constrained to no fewer than 0 and no more than 21), optionally an `acknowledgment_required` flag (defaulting to true) stating whether bidders must acknowledge this addendum, optionally a `new_requirement_codes` array of requirement definitions to introduce, and optionally an `answers_question_ids` array naming the pending questions this addendum answers.

The addendum is appended with a `sequence_number` equal to the solicitation's current `addendum_count` plus 1, giving a 1-based issuance order within the solicitation, and an `issued_at_utc` taken from the logical clock. Each entry in `new_requirement_codes` — each carrying its own `requirement_code`, `description`, `materiality` and `is_waivable` — becomes a fresh `MandatoryRequirement` whose `introduced_by_addendum_sequence` is the new sequence number, so the record always shows which amendment brought a requirement into being. The addendum's own `adds_material_requirement` flag is set to true when at least one requirement it introduced has `materiality` of `MATERIAL`, and false otherwise; that single boolean is what later decides whether an unacknowledged addendum is a fatal defect or merely a priced informality.

Every question named in `answers_question_ids` moves `PENDING → ANSWERED`, receives an `answer_summary` copied verbatim from the addendum's `summary`, and records `answered_via_addendum_sequence` as the new sequence number. A named question must belong to the same solicitation as the addendum, else `ERR_QUESTION_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION`, and must resolve at all, else `ERR_QUESTION_NOT_FOUND`.

The bid due date then moves. The solicitation's `cumulative_extension_days` is recomputed as the sum of `extends_bid_due_by_days` over every addendum of that solicitation, with no status filter of any kind, evaluated in ascending sequence order; `addendum_count` is recomputed as the number of addendum rows on the solicitation; and `effective_bid_due_utc` is set to `original_bid_due_utc` plus that cumulative number of whole calendar days, preserving the original time of day. A ceiling protects bidders from indefinite deferral: cumulative extension may not exceed three weeks. If adding the requested `extends_bid_due_by_days` to the existing cumulative total would push it past 21 days, the addendum is rejected in full with `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` — no partial extension is applied, no requirement rows are created, and `addendum_count` is not incremented. The cap check is evaluated once per addendum in issuance order, which is why an addendum that would individually fit can still be refused when earlier addenda have already consumed the allowance.

The question deadline tracks the bid due date, but only while the question period is still open and only when the addendum actually moved the bid due date. If the solicitation is `ISSUED` and the addendum's `extends_bid_due_by_days` is strictly greater than zero, `question_deadline_utc` is recomputed as the new `effective_bid_due_utc` less one week. Otherwise `question_deadline_utc` is left exactly as it was. Every successful addendum increments the solicitation's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a bidder holding a stale version cannot submit a bid (see §2.6).

Closing the question period moves the solicitation `ISSUED → QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` on an officer's action, and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `question_deadline_utc`; an attempt made too early is refused with `ERR_QUESTION_PERIOD_OPEN`. Closure freezes `question_deadline_utc` permanently: later addenda continue to move `effective_bid_due_utc` and `cumulative_extension_days`, but the frozen question deadline is never recomputed again. The solicitation's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.4. Sealed Bidding, the Public Opening and the Responsiveness Determination

A `Vendor` submits a sealed bid by supplying the `solicitation_id`, its own `vendor_id`, its as-read `total_price_cents` (strictly greater than zero), optionally an `acknowledged_addendum_sequences` array listing the addendum sequence numbers it acknowledges (defaulting to empty), and the mandatory `expected_solicitation_version` described in §2.6. The `vendor_id` in the body must equal the acting vendor's `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Bids are accepted only while the solicitation is `ISSUED` or `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` — before it is advertised or after bids have been opened there is no such transition, and the attempt fails with `ERR_INVALID_SOLICITATION_STATE`. The clock must not yet have passed the current `effective_bid_due_utc`; a bid arriving strictly after that instant is refused with `ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A vendor whose `eligibility_status` is anything other than `ELIGIBLE` cannot bid at all, and is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

A new bid is created with `submitted_at_utc` from the logical clock and with both of its parallel state dimensions at their initial values: `responsiveness_status` is `UNREVIEWED` and `responsibility_status` is `NOT_REVIEWED`. These two dimensions evolve independently of one another for the entire life of the bid, which is why a bid can be `RESPONSIVE` and still `NOT_REVIEWED`, or `RESPONSIVE` and `NONRESPONSIBLE`. Its `evaluated_price_cents` starts null, its `corrected_total_price_cents` starts null, its `informality_loading_cents` starts at 0, its `nonresponsive_reason_codes` starts empty and its `superseded_by_bid_id` starts null.

Replacement is explicit and automatic. When a vendor submits a second bid on a solicitation that is still `ISSUED` or `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED` — that is, whose bids have not yet been opened — its earlier bid on that same solicitation moves to `WITHDRAWN` — from either `UNREVIEWED` or `RESPONSIVE` — and has its `superseded_by_bid_id` set to the id of the replacement, with its own `version` incremented by 1. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for the responsiveness dimension: a withdrawn bid is never evaluated, never ranked and never restored. Every bid create, replacement or not, increments the owning solicitation's `version` by 1. Bids can be listed and filtered by `solicitation_id`, `vendor_id`, `responsiveness_status`, `responsibility_status` and `superseded_by_bid_id`.

Bid opening moves the solicitation `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED → BIDS_OPENED` on an officer's action. It requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `effective_bid_due_utc`, else `ERR_BID_PERIOD_OPEN`, and it requires at least one bid on the solicitation whose `responsiveness_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, else `ERR_NO_BIDS_RECEIVED` — the empty-collection edge is a hard stop, not a no-op. Opening stamps `bids_opened_at_utc` from the logical clock, which becomes the trigger event for one flavour of protest timeliness, and prices every non-withdrawn bid under the **as-read variant** of the evaluated-price rule: each such bid's `evaluated_price_cents` is set to exactly its `total_price_cents`, and its `informality_loading_cents` is set to 0. Arithmetic corrections and unacknowledged-addendum loadings are deliberately ignored at this stage, because at opening no officer has yet examined anything; the consequence, which is intended, is that the ranking announced at opening can name a different apparent low bidder than the ranking that emerges after determinations are recorded. The solicitation then recomputes its `apparent_low_bid_id` and increments its `version` by 1.

Ranking is a pinned procedure, and it re-runs on bid opening, on every responsiveness determination, on every responsibility review and inside every cascade that changes any bid's `responsiveness_status` or `responsibility_status`. The candidate set is the bids of that one solicitation whose `responsiveness_status` is either `UNREVIEWED` or `RESPONSIVE`, whose `responsibility_status` is not `NONRESPONSIBLE`, and whose `evaluated_price_cents` is not null. Candidates are ordered by `evaluated_price_cents` ascending, and ties on price are broken by `created_at` ascending. No further tie-break is needed or permitted, because `created_at` carries millisecond precision and the pair of price and creation instant is therefore unique within a solicitation; the bid's `id` is never consulted for ordering. The lowest-ranked candidate's id becomes `apparent_low_bid_id`. When the candidate set is empty, `apparent_low_bid_id` becomes null; a solicitation sitting in `AWARD_PENDING` whose candidate set empties falls back to `EVALUATION`, and an attempt to create an award while `apparent_low_bid_id` is null is refused with `ERR_NO_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`. Withdrawn and nonresponsive bids are excluded permanently — they never re-enter the candidate set.

A `ProcurementOfficer` records a responsiveness determination on one bid at a time, supplying the `determination` itself, optionally a `reason_codes` array and optionally a `corrected_total_price_cents` figure representing the arithmetically corrected as-read price. The bid's `responsiveness_status` must be `UNREVIEWED` — a bid that is `WITHDRAWN` or already determined is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE` — and the owning solicitation must be in `BIDS_OPENED` or `EVALUATION`, else `ERR_INVALID_SOLICITATION_STATE`.

A determination of `RESPONSIVE` carries one additional precondition, and it is the mechanism by which addenda bind bidders. Every mandatory requirement of the solicitation that has `materiality` of `MATERIAL` **and** was introduced by an addendum — that is, whose `introduced_by_addendum_sequence` is greater than zero — must have its introducing sequence number present in the bid's `acknowledged_addendum_sequences`. If any such requirement is unacknowledged, the bid cannot be found responsive and the request is refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 422). Requirements that were part of the original solicitation, and requirements marked `INFORMALITY`, are not subject to this gate.

Where the material gate is a bar, the informality gate is a price. A `RESPONSIVE` determination recomputes the bid under the **corrected variant** of the evaluated-price rule. The base price is the `corrected_total_price_cents` supplied on this request when one was supplied, and otherwise the bid's stored `total_price_cents`. The server then counts the addenda of the bid's solicitation — again with no status filter, since an addendum has no lifecycle of its own — that satisfy all three of these conditions: `acknowledgment_required` is true, `adds_material_requirement` is false, and the addendum's `sequence_number` does not appear in the bid's `acknowledged_addendum_sequences`. That count of unacknowledged non-material addenda is multiplied by the base price and by a loading rate of twenty-five hundredths of one percent — that is, 0.0025 — and the product is rounded half-up to a whole cent exactly once to give `informality_loading_cents`. One rounding is applied to the whole product; the rule is emphatically *not* one rounding per addendum. The bid's `evaluated_price_cents` becomes the base price plus that loading. Because a bid with at least one unacknowledged non-material addendum therefore prices strictly higher than the same bid with none, the corrected variant can reorder the field relative to the as-read ranking.

A determination of `NONRESPONSIVE` requires only that the bid be `UNREVIEWED` and the solicitation be in `BIDS_OPENED` or `EVALUATION`. It appends the supplied `reason_codes` to the bid's `nonresponsive_reason_codes`, defaulting to a single `OFFICER_DETERMINATION` entry when no codes were supplied. The recognised reason codes are `MATERIAL_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`, `ADDENDUM_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `LATE_SUBMISSION`, `VENDOR_DEBARRED`, `VENDOR_SUSPENDED` and `OFFICER_DETERMINATION`.

Both determinations share the remaining side effects, in equal measure. Each stamps `responsiveness_decided_at_utc` from the logical clock — the trigger event for responsiveness-ground protest timeliness — and `responsiveness_decided_by_user_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, which is the value the reviewer-conflict rule will later police. Each fires the solicitation's automatic `BIDS_OPENED → EVALUATION` transition when the solicitation is still `BIDS_OPENED`, inside the very same request; that auto-transition increments the solicitation's `version` by 1 and fires only on the first determination recorded on that solicitation, since afterwards the solicitation is no longer `BIDS_OPENED`. Each recomputes the solicitation's `apparent_low_bid_id`. And each increments the bid's own `version` by 1.

### 3.5. Responsibility Review of the Apparent Low Bidder

Responsibility is reviewed by a `ResponsibilityReviewer`, and only ever against the bid that is currently the apparent low bidder. The reviewer supplies three factor scores — `financial_capacity_score`, `past_performance_score` and `integrity_score`, each an integer between 0 and 100 inclusive — the `determination` of either `RESPONSIBLE` or `NONRESPONSIBLE`, and optional `notes`. The server stamps `reviewer_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock; `decided_at_utc` is the trigger event for responsibility-ground protest timeliness. A review's `superseded_at_utc` starts null and stays null while the review is operative.

Five preconditions guard the review, in the order given in §2.10. The bid's `responsibility_status` must be `NOT_REVIEWED`. The bid's `responsiveness_status` must be `RESPONSIVE`, else `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE`. The bid must be the one named by the solicitation's `apparent_low_bid_id`, else `ERR_NOT_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the bid's `responsiveness_decided_by_user_id`, else `ERR_REVIEWER_CONFLICT`. For a `RESPONSIBLE` determination only, the bidding vendor's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`, else `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and the computed `composite_score` must reach the responsible threshold of 60.00, else `ERR_RESPONSIBILITY_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422).

The `composite_score` is derived in six steps from the three factor scores, the solicitation's `estimated_value_cents` and the vendor's `debarment_strike_count` as stored at the moment the review is created.

First, the `weight_profile` is selected on contract size. When the solicitation's `estimated_value_cents` is at or above 500,000,000 cents — five million dollars — the profile is `HIGH_VALUE`; below that boundary it is `STANDARD`. The boundary is inclusive on the high-value side: an estimated value of exactly 500,000,000 cents selects `HIGH_VALUE`.

Second, a raw weighted score is formed. Under the `STANDARD` profile, financial capacity carries a weight of three tenths, past performance four tenths, and integrity three tenths. Under the `HIGH_VALUE` profile, the emphasis shifts to the balance sheet: financial capacity carries five tenths, past performance three tenths, and integrity two tenths. The raw score is the sum of each factor score multiplied by its profile weight.

Third, `strike_penalty_points` is computed as seven points for every strike currently attributed to the vendor, capped at twenty points however many strikes there are — so a vendor with two strikes loses fourteen points, and a vendor with three or more loses exactly twenty.

Fourth, the raw score less the strike penalty is clamped into the range from 0 to 100 inclusive: anything above 100 becomes 100 and anything below 0 becomes 0.

Fifth, an integrity failure imposes a hard ceiling. If the reviewer's `integrity_score` is strictly below 40, the clamped value is further capped at 49 — which, being below the responsible threshold, makes a `RESPONSIBLE` determination arithmetically impossible for a bidder with an integrity failure no matter how strong its other factors. If `integrity_score` is 40 or above, the clamped value passes through unchanged. The result is then rounded half-up to two decimal places, once, at this point and nowhere earlier.

Sixth and last, the threshold gate of the preceding paragraph is applied.

A `RESPONSIBLE` determination moves the bid's `responsibility_status` from `NOT_REVIEWED` to `RESPONSIBLE`, without touching its `responsiveness_status` at all, and fires the solicitation's automatic `EVALUATION → AWARD_PENDING` transition in the same request — a transition available only when the solicitation is in `EVALUATION` and the reviewed bid is the apparent low bidder. That auto-transition increments the solicitation's `version` by 1; the bid's own `version` also increments by 1.

A `NONRESPONSIBLE` determination moves `NOT_REVIEWED → NONRESPONSIBLE`, creates the review row with its determination recorded, and leaves the solicitation in `EVALUATION` — it does *not* advance to `AWARD_PENDING`. Because the ranking procedure excludes bids whose `responsibility_status` is `NONRESPONSIBLE`, the solicitation immediately recomputes `apparent_low_bid_id`, which now names the next-lowest eligible candidate or becomes null when none remains. The bid's `version` increments by 1. The reviews recorded against a bid can be listed in their own right.

### 3.6. Notice of Award, the Automatic Stay and Execution

An award is created by a `ProcurementOfficer` who supplies the `solicitation_id` and the `winning_bid_id`. The winning bid must be the solicitation's current `apparent_low_bid_id`, else `ERR_NOT_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`; it must belong to the named solicitation, else `ERR_BID_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION`; the solicitation must have an apparent low bidder at all, else `ERR_NO_APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER`; and no protest on the solicitation may have an active stay, else `ERR_AWARD_STAYED`. The award is created in `status` `PENDING_STAY`, with `executed_at_utc` and `void_reason_code` both null, and copies `vendor_id` from the winning bid.

The award's money is computed in five steps from the winning bid's `evaluated_price_cents` under the corrected variant. Step one sets `base_obligation_cents` to that evaluated price. Step two selects `administrative_fee_rate` from a descending ladder read off the **provisional** total, which is the base obligation alone: one and a half percent — 0.0150 — when the base obligation is at or below 100,000,000 cents; one and one tenth percent — 0.0110 — when it is above 100,000,000 but at or below 500,000,000 cents; and three quarters of one percent — 0.0075 — above 500,000,000 cents. The rate is selected once, on the base obligation, and is never re-selected on the final total even though that total is larger and may fall in a different tier; pinning the selection this way is what stops the definition being circular. Step three sets `administrative_fee_cents` to the base obligation multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to a whole cent. Step four computes the `protest_risk_reserve_cents`: the solicitation is examined for the existence of any protest whose `status` is `SUSTAINED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and if at least one such resolved protest exists, the reserve is half of one tenth of one percent — 0.0050 — of the sum of the base obligation and the already-rounded administrative fee, rounded half-up to a whole cent. Protests still open, in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, do not trigger the reserve, and when no resolved protest exists the reserve is 0. The reserve percentage applies to the base plus the rounded fee, never to the unrounded product from step three. Step five sets `award_total_obligation_cents` to the base obligation plus the administrative fee plus the reserve.

Execution is the moment the state actually commits. Executing an award moves it `PENDING_STAY → EXECUTED` and drives the solicitation `AWARD_PENDING → AWARDED` in the same request. It requires the award to be in `PENDING_STAY`, the solicitation to be in `AWARD_PENDING`, and again that no protest on the solicitation has an active stay, else `ERR_AWARD_STAYED`. Execution stamps `executed_at_utc` from the logical clock and increments both the award's `version` and the solicitation's `version` by 1. `AWARDED` is a terminal state of the solicitation machine under normal operation, and so is `RESOLICITED`; only a sustained protest can pull a solicitation back out of `AWARDED`.

An award never leaves `PENDING_STAY` or `EXECUTED` by anyone's direct action other than execution. It leaves them only by cascade, into one of its two terminal states. From `PENDING_STAY` it becomes `VOID`, with `void_reason_code` set to `PROTEST_SUSTAINED`, `APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER_CHANGED` or `SOLICITATION_CANCELLED` according to which cascade originated the change, and that departure is itself what pushes the solicitation out of `AWARD_PENDING`. From `EXECUTED` it becomes `RESCINDED` with `void_reason_code` set to `PROTEST_SUSTAINED`, and that departure pushes the solicitation out of `AWARDED` and back to `EVALUATION`. Either way the award's `version` increments by 1. Awards can be listed and filtered by `solicitation_id`, `winning_bid_id`, `vendor_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Filing a Protest: Standing, Timeliness, the Fee and the Stay

A protest is filed by a `Vendor`, which supplies the `solicitation_id`, its own `protester_vendor_id`, the statutory `grounds_code`, a `narrative` stating the grounds, and — for two of the five grounds — a `challenged_bid_id`. The five grounds are `SOLICITATION_IMPROPRIETY`, `RESPONSIVENESS_DETERMINATION`, `RESPONSIBILITY_DETERMINATION`, `AWARD_IMPROPRIETY` and `EVALUATION_ERROR`. A `challenged_bid_id` is required for `RESPONSIVENESS_DETERMINATION` and `RESPONSIBILITY_DETERMINATION` and must be null for the other three grounds. When supplied, the challenged bid must belong to the protested solicitation, else `ERR_BID_NOT_ON_SOLICITATION`. The `protester_vendor_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The solicitation must be protestable per §2.10, else `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_PROTESTABLE`. The server stamps `filed_at_utc` from the logical clock and creates the protest with `status` `FILED`, `stay_status` `NONE`, `decision_outcome` null, `remedy_code` null, `decided_at_utc` null, `record_item_count` 0 and `fee_refunded_cents` 0.

**Standing** is computed, not asserted, and lands in `standing_basis`. A protester that has a bid on the solicitation whose `responsiveness_status` is `UNREVIEWED`, `RESPONSIVE` or `NONRESPONSIVE` has standing as an `ACTUAL_BIDDER` — note that having been found nonresponsive does not strip standing, since the determination itself is frequently what is being protested. Alternatively, and only for the `SOLICITATION_IMPROPRIETY` ground, a protester that filed a question submission on the solicitation whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ANSWERED` has standing as a `PROSPECTIVE_BIDDER`. A vendor whose only bid on the solicitation is `WITHDRAWN` has no standing at all. Where neither basis is satisfied, the filing is refused with `ERR_PROTESTER_LACKS_STANDING` (HTTP 403).

**Timeliness** is likewise computed, into `timeliness_deadline_utc`, and each ground names its own trigger event and its own window. For `SOLICITATION_IMPROPRIETY`, the trigger event is the solicitation's `effective_bid_due_utc`, and the deadline is one day *before* that instant — improprieties in the solicitation itself must be raised before bids are due, not after. For `RESPONSIVENESS_DETERMINATION`, the trigger event is the challenged bid's `responsiveness_decided_at_utc`, and the deadline is seven days after it. For `RESPONSIBILITY_DETERMINATION`, the trigger event is the `decided_at_utc` of the responsibility review of the challenged bid that has the greatest `decided_at_utc` among those rows whose `superseded_at_utc` is null, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`; if no such operative review exists, the filing is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_TRIGGER_EVENT_MISSING` (HTTP 422). The deadline is seven days after that instant. For `AWARD_IMPROPRIETY`, the trigger event is the `created_at` of the award on the solicitation with the greatest `created_at` among awards whose `status` is `PENDING_STAY` or `EXECUTED`; if no such award exists, the filing is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_TRIGGER_EVENT_MISSING`. The deadline is ten days after that instant. For `EVALUATION_ERROR`, the trigger event is the solicitation's `bids_opened_at_utc`, and the deadline is seven days after it. A filing is timely when the logical clock is at or before `timeliness_deadline_utc`; filing at exactly the deadline instant is timely. A filing after it is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_UNTIMELY`.

**The fee** is computed into `filing_fee_cents` from exactly two inputs — the protested solicitation's `estimated_value_cents` and this protest's own `grounds_code` — and no other protest on the solicitation affects it. Three components are formed. The first is the flat statutory base of 25,000 cents, which is already integral. The second is an ad valorem component on a descending rate ladder: the solicitation's estimated value multiplied by seventy-five thousandths of one percent — 0.00075 — when that value is at or below 10,000,000 cents; by fifty thousandths of one percent — 0.00050 — when it is above 10,000,000 but at or below 100,000,000 cents; and by twenty-five thousandths of one percent — 0.00025 — above 100,000,000 cents. The third is a grounds surcharge, formed as the 25,000-cent base fee multiplied by the ground's own multiplier: 0.1255 for `SOLICITATION_IMPROPRIETY`, 0.3335 for `RESPONSIVENESS_DETERMINATION`, 0.2955 for `RESPONSIBILITY_DETERMINATION`, 0.5015 for `AWARD_IMPROPRIETY` and 0.2135 for `EVALUATION_ERROR`. The rounding order is pinned and is part of the contract: each of the three components is individually rounded half-up to a whole cent, and only then are the three rounded components added together. Summing the unrounded components and rounding the sum once is explicitly not the rule, and yields a different number of cents whenever two or more components carry a fractional part of exactly one half.

The fee is then moved. Timeliness is evaluated before the fee is debited. The protester's `protest_fee_balance_cents` must be at least the computed `filing_fee_cents`, otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEE_BALANCE` (HTTP 402). On success the fee is subtracted from the protester's `protest_fee_balance_cents` and added to the solicitation's `protest_escrow_cents`, and both the vendor's `version` and the solicitation's `version` increment by 1.

**The stay** is the protest's second, independent state dimension, and it is what gives a protest teeth. When the protest is filed while the solicitation is in `ISSUED`, `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `BIDS_OPENED`, `EVALUATION` or `AWARD_PENDING` and no award on that solicitation is `EXECUTED`, `stay_status` moves `NONE → ACTIVE`, and while it is `ACTIVE` both award creation and award execution on that solicitation are refused with `ERR_AWARD_STAYED`. A protest filed once an award has already been executed keeps `stay_status` at `NONE` — there is nothing left to freeze. A `ProcurementOfficer` may override an active stay on written urgency findings by supplying an `urgency_justification`; the override moves `stay_status` `ACTIVE → OVERRIDDEN`, which re-permits award creation and execution while the merits track continues untouched, requires the protest's `status` to be `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, and increments the protest's `version` by 1. Both a decision on the merits and a withdrawal move `stay_status` to `LIFTED` from either `ACTIVE` or `OVERRIDDEN`, incrementing `version` by 1. Protests can be listed and filtered by `solicitation_id`, `protester_vendor_id`, `challenged_bid_id`, `status` and `stay_status`.

Withdrawal is the protester's own escape hatch and is available from `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, with an optional `withdrawal_reason`. It requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `protester_vendor_id`, else `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. From every one of those three states the effects are identical: `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, `stay_status` moves to `LIFTED` from `ACTIVE` or `OVERRIDDEN`, and the filing debit is fully compensated — `fee_refunded_cents` is set to the whole `filing_fee_cents`, that amount is subtracted from the solicitation's `protest_escrow_cents` and added back to the protester's `protest_fee_balance_cents`. Every hearing on the protest whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. The protest's `version` increments by 1. `WITHDRAWN`, `SUSTAINED` and `DENIED` are the three terminal states of the protest's merits dimension.

### 3.8. The Agency Record, the Hearing, the Decision and the Ordered Remedy

A protest is decided on a documentary record, and the record is built one item at a time by a `ProcurementOfficer`, a `HearingOfficer` or a `Vendor`. Each filing supplies an `item_type` and a `document_ref`, an opaque reference to the filed document. The recognised item types are `SOLICITATION_DOCUMENT`, `ADDENDUM`, `BID_ABSTRACT`, `RESPONSIVENESS_MEMO`, `RESPONSIBILITY_MEMO`, `PROTESTER_SUBMISSION` and `AGENCY_REPORT`. The server appends the item with a `sequence_number` equal to the protest's current `record_item_count` plus 1, stamps `submitted_at_utc` from the logical clock and `submitted_by_role` with the caller's `X-User-Role`, then recomputes the protest's `record_item_count` and increments the protest's `version` by 1.

One filing has a state consequence. The first item filed with `item_type` `AGENCY_REPORT` by a caller acting as `ProcurementOfficer` advances the protest `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW` in the same request — the agency report is the pleading that opens the review. An `AGENCY_REPORT` filed by any other role, or a later `AGENCY_REPORT` after the protest has already advanced, appends normally without moving the merits dimension.

Scheduling a hearing is a `HearingOfficer` action that advances the protest `UNDER_REVIEW → HEARING_SCHEDULED` and creates a `ProtestHearing` in `status` `SCHEDULED`. The officer supplies the `hearing_officer_user_id` who will preside, the `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` bounding the hearing window, and the `room_code` of the hearing room the window occupies. The end must be strictly after the start and the window's length must be a whole number of minutes, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The protest must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, its `record_item_count` must be at least 1, and the presiding officer must not be the official who issued the solicitation, else `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`. A new hearing starts with `continuance_count` 0, `convened_at_utc` null, `concluded_at_utc` null and `record_items_reviewed_count` 0; the protest's `version` increments by 1.

Two capacity checks then apply to the candidate window, and they apply identically when a continuance re-schedules an existing hearing — with the one difference that the row being re-scheduled excludes itself from both checks.

The **room** check enforces a changeover buffer of thirty minutes. Treat each hearing's window as the half-open interval from its `scheduled_start_utc` up to but not including its `scheduled_end_utc`. For the same `room_code`, the candidate conflicts with any *other* hearing whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONVENED` when the candidate's start is earlier than that hearing's end extended by thirty minutes *and* that hearing's start is earlier than the candidate's end extended by thirty minutes. Because the buffer is applied on both sides in this form, two exactly adjacent bookings in the same room *do* conflict, and so does any booking that comes within half an hour of another. A conflict is refused with `ERR_HEARING_ROOM_CONFLICT`.

The **docket** check enforces a daily ceiling of eight hours on one officer's time. The relevant day is the half-open UTC calendar day containing the candidate's `scheduled_start_utc`: from that instant floored to UTC midnight, up to but not including the following UTC midnight. Booked minutes are the sum, in whole minutes, of the window lengths of every other hearing sharing the candidate's `hearing_officer_user_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONVENED` and whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls inside that day window; hearings in `CONCLUDED` or `CANCELLED` release their minutes and are not counted. The candidate's own window length in whole minutes is added to that sum, and if the total exceeds 480 minutes the request is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_DOCKET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A total of exactly 480 minutes is permitted; only a strictly greater total is refused. Adjacency is permitted on this axis: unlike the room check, no buffer applies between an officer's back-to-back hearings.

Convening moves the hearing `SCHEDULED → CONVENED`, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `scheduled_start_utc` (else `ERR_HEARING_NOT_YET_STARTED`), requires the protest's `record_item_count` to be at least 1 (else `ERR_AGENCY_RECORD_INCOMPLETE`), stamps `convened_at_utc` from the logical clock, and increments the hearing's `version` by 1.

A continuance is the hearing machine's cycle. It moves the hearing `CONVENED → SCHEDULED`, requires `continuance_count` to be strictly less than 2 (else `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, so at most two continuances are ever granted and a third attempt fails), and requires the new window to pass the same room and docket checks as an original scheduling. The officer supplies the new `scheduled_start_utc`, `scheduled_end_utc` and `room_code` along with a `continuance_reason`. On success the hearing's window and room are replaced by the supplied values, `continuance_count` increases by 1, `convened_at_utc` is cleared back to null, and the hearing's `version` increments by 1.

Concluding moves the hearing `CONVENED → CONCLUDED`, requires the protest's `record_item_count` to be at least 2 (else `ERR_AGENCY_RECORD_INCOMPLETE`), stamps `concluded_at_utc` from the logical clock, snapshots `record_items_reviewed_count` as the protest's `record_item_count` at that moment, and drives the protest `HEARING_SCHEDULED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Both the hearing's `version` and the protest's `version` increment by 1. That return to `UNDER_REVIEW` is what makes the protest's merits dimension cyclic: a second hearing may be scheduled on the same protest, convened, continued and concluded again, before any decision issues. `CONCLUDED` and `CANCELLED` are the hearing machine's terminal states.

The decision is issued by a `HearingOfficer` from `UNDER_REVIEW`, supplying the `outcome`, a `rationale`, and — with a sustained outcome — a `remedy_code`. Four preconditions apply identically to both outcomes: the protest must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`; at least one hearing on the protest must have `status` `CONCLUDED`, else `ERR_HEARING_NOT_CONCLUDED`; the protest's `record_item_count` must be at least 2, else `ERR_AGENCY_RECORD_INCOMPLETE`; and the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the solicitation's `issued_by_user_id`, else `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_CONFLICT`.

A **denied** protest moves `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`. Its `decision_outcome` is set to `DENIED`, its `remedy_code` is recorded as `NONE`, `decided_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, and `stay_status` moves to `LIFTED` from `ACTIVE` or `OVERRIDDEN`. Fiscally, the denial is final against the protester: `fee_refunded_cents` remains 0, the escrowed fee is forfeited to the solicitation, and the solicitation's `protest_escrow_cents` is left unchanged. No award, bid or responsibility review row is mutated in any way. The protest's `version` increments by 1.

A **sustained** protest moves `UNDER_REVIEW → SUSTAINED` and begins Cascade A. Hop 1 records the outcome: `status` and `decision_outcome` both become `SUSTAINED`, `remedy_code` takes the supplied remedy, `decided_at_utc` is stamped, `stay_status` moves to `LIFTED` from `ACTIVE` or `OVERRIDDEN`, and the fee is returned in full — `fee_refunded_cents` is set to the whole `filing_fee_cents`, that amount is subtracted from the solicitation's `protest_escrow_cents` and added back to the protester's `protest_fee_balance_cents`.

The remaining hops depend on the remedy. With `remedy_code` `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, hop 2 follows from hop 1: the award on the same solicitation leaves its current state, moving `PENDING_STAY → VOID` with `void_reason_code` `PROTEST_SUSTAINED`, or `EXECUTED → RESCINDED` with the same reason code when the award had already been executed. Hop 3 follows from hop 2: because the award left `PENDING_STAY` or `EXECUTED`, the solicitation transitions `AWARD_PENDING → EVALUATION` or `AWARDED → EVALUATION` respectively, and recomputes `apparent_low_bid_id`. Hop 4 follows from hop 3: because the solicitation re-entered `EVALUATION`, every bid on it whose `responsibility_status` is `RESPONSIBLE` or `NONRESPONSIBLE` reverts to `NOT_REVIEWED`. Hop 5 follows from hop 4: because those bids reverted, every responsibility review whose reviewed bid is one of them has its `superseded_at_utc` stamped with the logical clock, which is exactly what removes it from consideration as a protest trigger event. Each row touched along the chain — the award, the solicitation, each bid — has its own `version` incremented by 1, and so does the protest.

With `remedy_code` `CANCELLATION`, hop 3 instead drives the solicitation to `CANCELLED`. With `remedy_code` `RESOLICITATION`, hop 3 likewise drives the solicitation to `CANCELLED` and leaves it eligible for resolicitation. `NONE` is the remedy recorded with a denial and is not a remedy a sustaining officer supplies; it is nonetheless one of the four recognised members of the `remedy_code` enum, alongside `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, `CANCELLATION` and `RESOLICITATION`, so a decision body that carries `remedy_code` `NONE` — as the body of a denial ordinarily does — clears the body-shape check of §2.10 step 3 rather than being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the later preconditions of this section are reached and evaluated normally.

A `ProcurementOfficer` may cancel a live procurement, supplying a `cancellation_reason`, from any of `ISSUED`, `QUESTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `BIDS_OPENED`, `EVALUATION` and `AWARD_PENDING`; from any other state there is no such transition and the attempt fails with `ERR_INVALID_SOLICITATION_STATE`. The `cancellation_reason` is an input of that request and nothing more: it is not persisted as a field of the solicitation and does not appear in the solicitation payload the cancellation returns. The same `CANCELLED` destination is reached by system cascade when a protest is sustained with a `CANCELLATION` or `RESOLICITATION` remedy, from `EVALUATION` or `AWARD_PENDING`.

Cancellation clears the field in three moves. Every bid on the solicitation whose `responsiveness_status` is `UNREVIEWED` or `RESPONSIVE` becomes `NONRESPONSIVE` with `OFFICER_DETERMINATION` appended to its `nonresponsive_reason_codes`, each bid's `version` incrementing by 1. Every award on the solicitation whose `status` is `PENDING_STAY` becomes `VOID` with `void_reason_code` `SOLICITATION_CANCELLED`, its `version` incrementing by 1. The solicitation's `apparent_low_bid_id` is set to null and its own `version` increments by 1.

Resolicitation is how a cancelled procurement is put back out to market, and it is the only path out of `CANCELLED`. The officer supplies a `new_solicitation_number` for the successor, together with the successor's `original_bid_due_utc` and `original_question_deadline_utc`. The predecessor must be in `CANCELLED`, and no solicitation may already exist whose `predecessor_solicitation_id` names it, else `ERR_ALREADY_RESOLICITED` — a procurement gets exactly one successor. On success the predecessor moves `CANCELLED → RESOLICITED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments by 1. A successor solicitation is created in `DRAFT` with its `predecessor_solicitation_id` pointing at the predecessor and its `solicitation_number` taken from the supplied `new_solicitation_number` — conventionally the predecessor's number suffixed with `-R` and the successor generation number — which must not already be in use, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOLICITATION_NUMBER`. Every mandatory requirement of the predecessor whose `introduced_by_addendum_sequence` is 0, that is every requirement from the original advertisement, is copied onto the successor; requirements that arrived by addendum are not carried over, since the successor's own amendment history starts empty. The successor then follows the ordinary lifecycle from §3.2 onward: it must be issued before it accepts questions or bids.

### 3.9. Suspension, Debarment, the Debarred-Vendor List and Reinstatement

A `DebarmentAuthority` opens a proceeding by issuing a notice of proposed debarment. It supplies the respondent's `vendor_id`, the statutory `cause_code`, a non-empty `aggravating_factor_codes` array of distinct members, optionally a `mitigating_factor_codes` array of distinct members (defaulting to empty), optionally a `source_protest_id` naming the protest whose record supplied the cause (null when the cause arose outside a protest), and optionally an `interim_suspension` flag. The recognised causes are `FRAUD`, `BID_RIGGING`, `REPEATED_NONPERFORMANCE`, `FALSE_CERTIFICATION`, `SAFETY_VIOLATION` and `ADMINISTRATIVE_DEFAULT`. Aggravating factors are drawn from that same set of six; mitigating factors are drawn from `SELF_DISCLOSURE`, `RESTITUTION_PAID`, `COMPLIANCE_PROGRAM` and `COOPERATION`. A repeated member in either list is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACTOR_CODE`. A respondent whose `eligibility_status` is already `DEBARRED` cannot be the subject of a new proceeding and the attempt is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_ALREADY_DEBARRED`.

A vendor may also not be double-docketed. Each proceeding occupies a closed interval on its respondent's compliance docket, running from its `notice_issued_at_utc` to an occupancy end that depends on its `status`: `debarment_end_utc` once the proceeding is `DEBARRED`, `decided_at_utc` once it is `DISMISSED`, and its own `notice_issued_at_utc` plus ten years — 3,650 days — while it is still live in `NOTICE_ISSUED`, `RESPONSE_RECEIVED` or `HEARING_HELD`. If the new notice instant falls inside any other proceeding's occupancy interval for the same vendor, the proceeding is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_PROCEEDING_EXISTS`. Both endpoints of the interval are inclusive, so a notice issued at exactly a prior proceeding's `debarment_end_utc` is rejected.

The proceeding is created in `status` `NOTICE_ISSUED` with `interim_status` `NONE`, `notice_issued_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, and `response_deadline_utc` set to two weeks — fourteen days — after that notice instant. Its `response_narrative` starts null, its `decided_at_utc` starts null, its `debarment_period_months` starts at 0, its `debarment_start_utc` and `debarment_end_utc` start null, and its `strikes_applied` starts at 0.

**Severity** is computed at creation and again at decision, under two different variants of one rule. The aggravating total is always the sum of the weights of the distinct members of `aggravating_factor_codes`, stored as `aggravating_total_points`: `FRAUD` weighs 40 points, `BID_RIGGING` 35, `FALSE_CERTIFICATION` 25, `REPEATED_NONPERFORMANCE` 20, `SAFETY_VIOLATION` 15 and `ADMINISTRATIVE_DEFAULT` 5.

Under the **interim-suspension variant**, which reflects the adequate-evidence standard applicable at notice time, mitigation is ignored entirely and prior strikes do not escalate: `mitigating_credit_points` is 0 and `severity_score` is the aggravating total, capped at 100. Interim suspension is permitted only when that severity reaches 35; below it the request is refused with `ERR_INTERIM_SUSPENSION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).

Under the **final-debarment variant**, which reflects the preponderance standard applicable at the merits decision, three adjustments apply. A raw mitigation total is formed as the sum of the weights of the distinct members of `mitigating_factor_codes` — `SELF_DISCLOSURE` credits 15 points, `RESTITUTION_PAID` 12, `COMPLIANCE_PROGRAM` 10 and `COOPERATION` 8 — and `mitigating_credit_points` is the lesser of that raw total and half of the aggravating total, so mitigation can never erase more than 50 percent of the aggravation. Prior strikes are counted as the respondent's `debarment_strike_count` less this proceeding's own `strikes_applied`, which is what stops a proceeding's own interim suspension from escalating its own severity; only other proceedings of the same vendor whose `status` is `DEBARRED` or whose `interim_status` is `INTERIM_SUSPENDED` contribute. Each prior strike adds 10 points. `severity_score` is then the aggravating total less the mitigation credit plus the strike escalation, floored at 0 and capped at 100. Because the same factor codes therefore yield a materially different score under the two variants, the interim severity recorded at notice time is not the number the decision uses.

**The period** is read off a tier table from the final severity: 60 months at a severity of 80 or more, 36 months at 60 or more, 24 months at 40 or more, and 12 months at 20 or more. Below 20 the period would be 0 months, and a debarment outcome is therefore refused with `ERR_SEVERITY_BELOW_DEBARMENT_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). `debarment_period_months` is an integer read directly off that table.

**The hearing-rights track** forks at the notice and rejoins at `HEARING_HELD`. On one branch, the respondent `Vendor` files a written response, supplying a `response_narrative` and optionally further `mitigating_factor_codes`; this moves the proceeding `NOTICE_ISSUED → RESPONSE_RECEIVED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the respondent's `vendor_id` (else `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`) and requires the logical clock to be at or before `response_deadline_utc` (else `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`). The supplied codes are merged into the proceeding's `mitigating_factor_codes`, and the proceeding's `version` increments by 1. From `RESPONSE_RECEIVED`, the authority holds a contested hearing, supplying a `hearing_summary`; this moves `RESPONSE_RECEIVED → HEARING_HELD`, records that the respondent was heard on the merits, and increments `version` by 1. On the other branch the respondent never answers, and the authority takes the hearing directly from `NOTICE_ISSUED → HEARING_HELD` — but only once the response window has actually expired, that is once the logical clock is strictly after `response_deadline_utc`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_DEBARMENT_HEARING_RIGHTS_NOT_EXHAUSTED`. That branch records that hearing rights were exhausted by default and likewise increments `version` by 1. Both branches converge on `HEARING_HELD`, but they carry different mitigation into the decision, because only the response branch can have merged additional mitigating codes.

**Interim suspension** is the proceeding's second, independent state dimension. When the notice is issued with `interim_suspension` set to true and the interim severity clears 35, `interim_status` moves `NONE → INTERIM_SUSPENDED`, `strikes_applied` is set to 1, and the respondent moves `ELIGIBLE → SUSPENDED`. That vendor transition sets `suspension_reason_code` to the proceeding's `cause_code` and `active_debarment_id` to the proceeding's id, and recomputes `debarment_strike_count` as the sum of `strikes_applied` over that vendor's proceedings whose `status` is `DEBARRED` or whose `interim_status` is `INTERIM_SUSPENDED`. Two fan-outs follow. Every bid of that vendor on a solicitation in `BIDS_OPENED`, `EVALUATION` or `AWARD_PENDING` whose `responsiveness_status` is `UNREVIEWED` or `RESPONSIVE` becomes `NONRESPONSIVE` with `VENDOR_SUSPENDED` appended to `nonresponsive_reason_codes`, and each affected solicitation then recomputes its `apparent_low_bid_id`. And every subcontractor screening naming that vendor as the subcontractor whose `result` is `CLEARED` becomes `BLOCKED_SUSPENDED` with its `rescreened_count` incremented. The vendor's `version` increments by 1, as does each affected row's.

**The merits decision** is issued by the `DebarmentAuthority` from `HEARING_HELD`, supplying the `outcome` and a `rationale`.

A **debarment** outcome moves the proceeding `HEARING_HELD → DEBARRED`, which is terminal, and begins Cascade B. Hop 1 recomputes `severity_score` and `mitigating_credit_points` under the final-debarment variant, sets `debarment_period_months` from the severity tier, stamps `debarment_start_utc` and `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock, computes `debarment_end_utc` as the start instant plus `debarment_period_months` calendar months, sets `strikes_applied` to 1, and drives the respondent to `DEBARRED` — recomputing that vendor's `debarment_strike_count`, `active_debarment_id`, `suspension_reason_code` and `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc`. The strike bookkeeping differs by the vendor's prior posture, and the difference is deliberate: a vendor moving `SUSPENDED → DEBARRED` was already carrying this proceeding's strike, so `interim_status` moves `INTERIM_SUSPENDED → INTERIM_LIFTED` while `strikes_applied` stays 1 and `debarment_strike_count` is unchanged; a vendor moving `ELIGIBLE → DEBARRED` with no interim suspension in force has this proceeding's `strikes_applied` move from 0 to 1, so `debarment_strike_count` increases by 1. Hop 2 follows from hop 1: because the vendor's `eligibility_status` became `DEBARRED`, every bid of that vendor whose solicitation is in `BIDS_OPENED`, `EVALUATION` or `AWARD_PENDING` and whose `responsiveness_status` is `UNREVIEWED` or `RESPONSIVE` becomes `NONRESPONSIVE` with `VENDOR_DEBARRED` appended. Hop 3 follows from hop 2: each affected solicitation recomputes `apparent_low_bid_id`, and a solicitation in `AWARD_PENDING` whose apparent low bidder changed transitions to `EVALUATION` — which in turn reverts that solicitation's bids from `RESPONSIBLE` or `NONRESPONSIBLE` back to `NOT_REVIEWED` and stamps `superseded_at_utc` on their responsibility reviews, exactly as in Cascade A. Hop 4 follows from hop 3: because that solicitation left `AWARD_PENDING`, its award in `PENDING_STAY` becomes `VOID` with `void_reason_code` `APPARENT_LOW_BIDDER_CHANGED`. Separately from the chain, every subcontractor screening naming the vendor as subcontractor whose `result` is `CLEARED` fans out to `BLOCKED_DEBARRED`. Every row touched has its own `version` incremented by 1, including the proceeding's.

A **dismissal** outcome moves the proceeding `HEARING_HELD → DISMISSED`, also terminal, and its side-effect set diverges sharply from the debarment branch. It stamps `decided_at_utc`, leaves `debarment_period_months` at 0 and `debarment_start_utc` null, and performs no bid cascade of any kind. Where an interim suspension was in force, the dismissal compensates it: `strikes_applied` moves from 1 back to 0, `interim_status` moves `INTERIM_SUSPENDED → INTERIM_LIFTED`, the respondent moves `SUSPENDED → ELIGIBLE` with `active_debarment_id` and `suspension_reason_code` both cleared to null, and `debarment_strike_count` is recomputed and so decreases by 1. Every subcontractor screening naming that vendor as subcontractor whose `result` is `BLOCKED_SUSPENDED` returns to `CLEARED`, with `rescreened_count` incremented and `blocked_reason_code` cleared. Bids already marked `NONRESPONSIVE` by the suspension cascade are not restored, per §2.9. The proceeding's `version` increments by 1. Proceedings can be listed and filtered by `vendor_id`, `source_protest_id`, `status` and `interim_status`.

**The debarred-vendor list** is the public artifact the whole regime exists to maintain, and it is a snapshot rather than a simple filter. The reader may supply an `as_of_utc` instant, and when it is omitted the current logical clock instant is used instead. The reader may also supply `include_suspended`: when it is false or omitted, only vendors whose `eligibility_status` is `DEBARRED` are in scope; when it is true, vendors whose `eligibility_status` is `SUSPENDED` are in scope as well. Within that status scope, a vendor appears only when its `active_debarment_id` is not null and the proceeding that identifier names either has a null `debarment_end_utc` or has a `debarment_end_utc` strictly greater than the as-of instant — a debarment whose end instant equals the as-of instant has expired and drops off the list. Rows are ordered by the referenced proceeding's `debarment_end_utc` descending, with rows whose `debarment_end_utc` is null sorting first, and ties broken by the vendor's `created_at` ascending. The `limit` and `offset` window is applied last, after that ordering.

Reinstatement is not available on demand: a debarred vendor must serve part of its period first. When a debarment is decided, the respondent's `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc` is stamped as the proceeding's `debarment_start_utc` plus a number of calendar months equal to half the `debarment_period_months`, rounded up to a whole month. A twelve-month debarment therefore admits a petition after six months served, and a 36-month debarment after eighteen. Calendar-month addition preserves the day-of-month, clamping to the last day of the target month at the same time of day when the target month is shorter. The same convention gives `debarment_end_utc` as the start instant plus the full `debarment_period_months`. While a vendor's `eligibility_status` is anything other than `DEBARRED`, its `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc` is null.

A respondent `Vendor` files a petition against the proceeding, supplying a non-empty `remediation_evidence_codes` array of distinct members drawn from `RESTITUTION_COMPLETE`, `MANAGEMENT_REPLACED`, `COMPLIANCE_AUDIT_PASSED` and `TRAINING_COMPLETED`, a `narrative` stating the remediation, and the `expected_proceeding_version` required by §2.6. A repeated evidence code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACTOR_CODE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the proceeding's `vendor_id`, else `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The proceeding's `status` must be `DEBARRED`, else `ERR_INVALID_PROCEEDING_STATE`. No petition on that proceeding may already be in `FILED`, else `ERR_PETITION_ALREADY_PENDING`. And the logical clock must have reached or passed `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc` — the boundary is inclusive, so filing at exactly that instant is accepted — otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_PETITION_PREMATURE`. The petition is created in `status` `FILED`, with `filed_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, `months_served_at_filing` computed as the number of whole calendar months elapsed from the proceeding's `debarment_start_utc` to that filing instant, and `decided_at_utc` and `decision_rationale` both null. The proceeding's `version` increments by 1. Petitions can be listed per proceeding, filtered by `status`, and read individually.

The `DebarmentAuthority` decides a petition from `FILED`, supplying the `outcome` and a `decision_rationale`; the proceeding must still be `DEBARRED`. A petition already `GRANTED` or `DENIED` cannot be decided again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PETITION_STATE`. A denial moves the petition `FILED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_at_utc` and `decision_rationale`, leaves the vendor `DEBARRED`, and allows a further petition to be filed later — which is exactly the situation the version pin of §2.6 protects. A grant moves the petition `FILED → GRANTED`, stamps the same two fields, and drives the vendor `DEBARRED → ELIGIBLE`: the proceeding's `strikes_applied` moves from 1 back to 0 so the vendor's `debarment_strike_count` decreases by 1, and `active_debarment_id`, `suspension_reason_code` and `reinstatement_earliest_petition_utc` are all cleared to null. Every subcontractor screening naming that vendor as subcontractor whose `result` is `BLOCKED_DEBARRED` returns to `CLEARED`, with `rescreened_count` incremented and `blocked_reason_code` cleared. The vendor's `version`, the petition's `version` and each affected screening's `version` each increment by 1. Because the vendor machine runs `ELIGIBLE → SUSPENDED → DEBARRED → ELIGIBLE`, reinstatement returns a vendor to the same posture it started in, strikes and all.

Subcontractor screening is how the debarred-vendor list bites on parties who never bid. A `ProcurementOfficer` or a `Vendor` requests a screening by supplying the `solicitation_id` the subcontract sits under, the `prime_vendor_id` of the prime contractor proposing the subcontractor, the `sub_vendor_id` of the proposed subcontractor being screened, and the subcontract `tier` below the prime. The tier must be 1, 2 or 3; a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_TIER_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). A vendor may not subcontract to itself, so a request whose `prime_vendor_id` equals its `sub_vendor_id` is refused with `ERR_SELF_SUBCONTRACT` (HTTP 422). When the caller acts as `Vendor`, its `X-User-ID` must equal the `prime_vendor_id`.

The screening is evaluated in the same request that creates it, so it never lingers in `PENDING`. When the subcontractor's `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `result` becomes `CLEARED` and `blocked_reason_code` is null. When it is `SUSPENDED`, `result` becomes `BLOCKED_SUSPENDED` and `blocked_reason_code` is set to the subcontractor's `suspension_reason_code`. When it is `DEBARRED`, `result` becomes `BLOCKED_DEBARRED` with `blocked_reason_code` set the same way. In all three cases `screened_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock and `rescreened_count` starts at 0.

Screenings are then kept live by the vendor cascades rather than by re-request. A `CLEARED` screening becomes `BLOCKED_DEBARRED` when its subcontractor's `eligibility_status` becomes `DEBARRED`, and `BLOCKED_SUSPENDED` when that status becomes `SUSPENDED`; a `BLOCKED_DEBARRED` screening returns to `CLEARED` when the subcontractor is reinstated by a granted petition, and a `BLOCKED_SUSPENDED` screening returns to `CLEARED` when the proceeding that suspended it is dismissed. Each of those four re-evaluations increments `rescreened_count` by 1, refreshes `screened_at_utc` from the logical clock, sets or clears `blocked_reason_code` as the new result requires, and increments the screening's `version` by 1, so `rescreened_count` reads as an audit trail of how many times the list has moved under a given subcontract. Screenings can be listed and filtered by `solicitation_id`, `prime_vendor_id`, `sub_vendor_id` and `result`.
