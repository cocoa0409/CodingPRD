# Retina Intravitreal Buy-And-Bill API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A retina specialty practice occupies an unusually uncomfortable seam in outpatient medicine: the clinical decision to inject is driven by optical coherence tomography read minutes before the needle goes in, while the economics of that same decision are governed by a payer contract negotiated months earlier, an inventory of biologic vials each worth more than a day of clinic revenue, and a prior authorization that may or may not still be valid. Under **buy-and-bill**, the practice purchases the drug with its own capital, stores it, injects it, and only then bills the payer — so every hour a vial sits on the shelf is financing cost, every expired vial is a write-off, and every denied drug line is a loss the practice absorbed before it ever knew the claim would be refused. This document specifies the API that carries that whole chain: imaging interpretation, treat-and-extend interval steering, step therapy and prior authorization with biosimilar preference, lot and vial custody, injection documentation with single-dose-vial wastage billing, remittance posting against two independent claim lines, patient assistance coordination, endophthalmitis surveillance, and the recall of a lot with patient notification and manufacturer credit reconciliation.

The system's guiding obligation is **auditability under conservation**. Vials are never created or destroyed outside a lot receipt: they move between custody states, and a voided encounter quarantines the vial it consumed rather than pretending the injection never happened. Money is likewise conserved — the allowed amount on a claim is fully accounted for across payer cash, patient responsibility, foundation support, and practice write-off, and when an appeal succeeds the write-off unwinds rather than persisting as a phantom loss. Clinical safety is enforced by refusing the operation, not by warning about it: a stale OCT, an interval shorter than the product's labelled minimum, an unconfirmed aseptic prep, or a clinician whose privileges were pulled all block encounter creation or documentation outright.

Three human safeguards are structural rather than procedural, and the API enforces them on the acting identity rather than trusting workflow convention. The clinician who injects may not attest their own wastage. The clinician who injected may not adjudicate an adverse event on that encounter. The compliance officer who opened a lot recall may not be the one who closes it. Because much of the domain is time-sensitive and the practice needs deterministic, replayable behaviour for testing and reconciliation, all temporal reasoning reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every endpoint in this API is mounted at the root path — collections are addressed at the top level (for example `/vials`, `/claims`, `/drug-lots`) and there are no nested collection paths. A parent-to-child relationship is therefore never walked through the URL; it is discovered by filtering the child collection on the parent's identifier. The rules in this section apply to every request unless a later section names a narrower exception.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or present but consisting only of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401); the role name is matched exactly and case-sensitively against those six spellings, and is not trimmed before matching, so `practiceadmin` or a value padded with a leading space is an unknown role rather than a recognised one. If the role is recognised but the endpoint does not list it among its authorised roles, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `RetinaSpecialist`, `PharmacyTech`, `BillingSpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PracticeAdmin`, and `Patient`. Their remits are as follows.

- A `RetinaSpecialist` is the treating retina physician. This role exclusively interprets imaging studies, opens and steers treatment plans (activation, suspension, resumption, discontinuation), creates injection encounters and performs their documentation, wastage attestation and voiding, files adverse-event reports, and adjudicates adverse events. A `RetinaSpecialist` may read every collection but may not perform any inventory, billing, assistance or recall write.
- A `PharmacyTech` is the buy-and-bill inventory custodian. This role exclusively receives drug lots, quarantines a lot, runs the inventory expiration sweep, and ships a recall return. It may read everything, including the lot exposure computation, but performs no clinical or billing write and neither initiates nor closes a recall.
- A `BillingSpecialist` is the revenue-cycle operator. This role exclusively creates prior authorizations and drives their submission, decision and appeal steps; creates claims and drives their submission, remittance posting and drug-line appeal; creates, approves and terminates assistance enrollments; and reconciles the manufacturer credit on a recall. It may read everything, including lot exposure, and performs no clinical or inventory write.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` is the quality and regulatory officer. This role exclusively initiates a lot recall, sends its patient notifications, closes it, releases a quarantined lot back to available stock, starts an adverse-event investigation, and marks a notification unreachable. It may read every collection, but the lot exposure computation of §3.5 is not among its reads and is refused to it with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. It performs no clinical or billing write, does not receive lots, and does not adjudicate adverse events.
- A `PracticeAdmin` is the reference-data administrator and clock operator. This role exclusively creates payers, drugs, payer drug policies, patients and clinicians, suspends a clinician, and sets the logical clock. It may read everything, including lot exposure, and performs no clinical, inventory, billing or recall write anywhere in the system.
- A `Patient` is a notified patient acting on their own recall notifications. This role's only write is acknowledging a recall notification. Its only reads are the recall-notification collection and a single recall notification. Every other endpoint in the API is denied to it.

Identity resolution differs by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `RetinaSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`; the server stamps it onto `ImagingStudy.interpreted_by_clinician_id`, onto `InjectionEncounter.clinician_id` at encounter creation, onto `InjectionEncounter.attested_by_clinician_id` at wastage attestation, onto `AdverseEvent.reported_by_clinician_id` at report time and onto `AdverseEvent.adjudicated_by_clinician_id` at adjudication, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `Clinician` compare the resource's clinician foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient` row, and a notification is the acting patient's only when its `patient_id` equals `X-User-ID`. For both of these entity-backed roles the header value must resolve to an existing row; a `RetinaSpecialist` whose `X-User-ID` matches no `Clinician`, or a `Patient` whose `X-User-ID` matches no `Patient`, is rejected with `ERR_ACTING_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 403). This existence check runs on every endpoint whose authorised roles include `RetinaSpecialist` or `Patient`.

When `X-User-Role` is `PharmacyTech`, `BillingSpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `PracticeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a lot recall stores the initiating officer's identifier in `initiated_by_user_id` and the closing officer's in `closed_by_user_id`, and the segregation-of-duties comparison between those two values is a comparison of opaque strings — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`, and it never requires the identifier to resolve to a row.

An acting `Patient` is additionally scoped: a patient may only touch `RecallNotification` rows whose `patient_id` equals their `X-User-ID`. Acknowledging another patient's notification, or reading the notification collection or a single notification belonging to another patient, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_NOTIFICATION_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` to `Z` and the digits `0` to `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one temporal value in the API that reads real wall-clock time rather than the logical clock of §2.5, so it never echoes an instant the client has set on that clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless the narrative for a specific listing says otherwise. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, rows written without an intervening clock move legitimately share the same `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were written into the store, earliest write first, so the ordering is total and stable and the row's `id` is never used as a tie-breaker. Four pagination fields appear in the `meta` object of every list response: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filter across all pages, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads and write operations return a `meta` object without those four fields.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Version Counter

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client influence — a client-supplied `id` in a request body is never honoured. It is a random (version 4) UUID rendered in the canonical lowercase hyphenated 8-4-4-4-12 form. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written, and its value is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time — so rows created while the clock stands still all carry the identical `created_at`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `Payer`, `Drug`, `PayerDrugPolicy`, `Patient`, `Clinician`, `TreatmentPlan`, `DrugLot`, `Vial`, `PriorAuthorization`, `InjectionEncounter`, `Claim`, `AssistanceEnrollment`, `AdverseEvent`, `LotRecall` and `RecallNotification` all carry the full set of four properties.

An `ImagingStudy` is the one exception. An OCT reading is an immutable clinical observation: once written it is never edited, and consequently an `ImagingStudy` row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither key appears in its response payload.

A mutation that changes only a status enum — for example an adverse event moving from `REPORTED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, where the scaffolded transition changes nothing else — still refreshes `updated_at` and still advances `version`. The reverse is also true: any operation described later as "bumping the plan's version" is refreshing `updated_at` on the same row in the same breath. `updated_at` is likewise read from the logical clock, so a mutation taken while the clock has not moved since creation leaves `updated_at` equal to `created_at` even though `version` has advanced. The counter is scoped to the request rather than to the individual field write: when a single request touches the same row more than once — as the multi-hop cascades of §2.11 routinely do — `updated_at` is refreshed on each hop but `version` advances by exactly 1 for that request as a whole, never once per hop. A row that carries no `version` field at all, such as an `ImagingStudy`, is never version-bumped or `updated_at`-stamped by any request.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Rather than reading wall-clock time, every temporal rule in this system reads a server-held logical clock. Before any client sets it, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PracticeAdmin` may move it by supplying a required `current_utc` instant to the clock endpoint, which responds with HTTP 200 and a `data` object carrying the single key `current_utc` holding the newly effective instant — that one-field object, not a resource row, is the whole payload, so it has no `id`, `created_at` or `version`. The clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. Setting the clock mutates no domain row: lazily evaluated transitions — an authorization aging past its expiration, an assistance enrollment aging past its benefit year — are not swept on the clock call itself but are evaluated on the next domain request that needs them.

Where this document says "the logical clock", it means the value that endpoint most recently established (or the default if it was never called). Comparisons against the clock are exact instant comparisons; where a rule is expressed in whole days, the day count is computed from the two instants and truncated toward zero, so two instants 47 hours apart are one whole day apart, and a count taken in the reverse direction is the negative of the same truncated magnitude. Every instant the API accepts from a client — `current_utc` and every other ISO 8601 field or datetime query parameter — is parsed permissively and then normalised to UTC before anything compares it: a trailing `Z` in either letter case is accepted, an explicit numeric offset such as `+02:00` is accepted and converted to UTC, a value carrying no zone designator at all is taken to already be UTC, and any number of fractional-second digits (including none) is accepted. Surrounding whitespace is trimmed, and an empty or whitespace-only string, or any string that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Instants are always rendered back in responses normalised to UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a `Z` suffix, whatever precision the client sent.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Treatment Plan

Two write operations take an optimistic-concurrency guard, and both guard the same parent entity — the `TreatmentPlan`. Both require a non-optional integer field named `expected_treatment_plan_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` value of the plan the client last observed. The field is declared as an integer of at least 1 and is therefore checked at §2.8 step 5: a zero, a negative value or a non-integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than a version mismatch, and only a well-formed value reaches the comparison below.

The first is injection-encounter creation. Creating an encounter legitimately mutates its plan — `last_encounter_id` is re-derived and `version` advances at creation itself, and the documentation that follows moves `current_interval_days` and `next_due_date` too — so a client holding an older snapshot of the plan is reasoning about a superseded interval schedule. If `expected_treatment_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the rejection happens before any vial is reserved, so no inventory is disturbed by a stale request.

The second is prior-authorization creation. A new authorization supersedes the plan's earlier authorization record for renewal purposes, so a create whose `expected_treatment_plan_version` is behind the plan's current `version` is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412): the requester has not observed the authorization that already superseded theirs. A successful prior-authorization create itself advances the plan's `version`.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Documentation, wastage attestation, voiding, plan suspension, plan resumption, plan discontinuation, claim operations, inventory operations and recall operations all carry no `expected_treatment_plan_version` and are never rejected for a version mismatch.

### 2.7. Money, Volumes and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this API is an integer count of cents. There are no fractional cents and no floating-point currency fields; a field name ending in `_cents` is always a whole number. Wherever a monetary value is produced by multiplication — a coinsurance share, a per-encounter cap scaled by a tier factor — the product is rounded half up to a whole cent as it is produced, and it is that rounded integer, never a full-precision intermediate, that every later step consumes. Where one amount is instead the result of a chain of factors, as the carrying-cost accrual of §3.5 is, the chain is carried at full precision and rounded half up to whole cents exactly once at its end. Half up means a value exactly halfway between two whole cents rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Volumes are millilitres carried to at most three decimal places. A drug's `vial_fill_ml` and `label_dose_ml` are each accepted with at most three decimal places, and computed wastage volume is rounded half up to three decimal places. Concentrations (`mg_per_ml`) and billing-unit sizes (`billing_unit_mg`) are positive numbers with no declared decimal limit. A payer's `coinsurance_rate` is a fraction accepted with at most four decimal places.

Billing units are whole numbers. The rounding order used to derive them is normative and is spelled out in the documentation narrative: each billed line is rounded to whole billing units on its own before the lines are summed. Counts — injections administered, quiescent visits, vials in a custody state, notifications acknowledged, documented step-therapy failures — are non-negative integers. Intervals and reporting deadlines are expressed in whole days. OCT thickness is a whole number of micrometres and is never rounded, because it arrives as an integer.

### 2.8. Request Validation Order and Failure Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error of the *first* violated rule in the order below, and evaluation stops there. This ordering is normative: an implementation that reports a later error while an earlier one also applies is wrong, even though both conditions hold. The sequence is:

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The role is authorised for this endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. For the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row (`RetinaSpecialist` to a `Clinician`, `Patient` to a `Patient`) — otherwise `ERR_ACTING_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `RetinaSpecialist` or `Patient`.
5. The request body and query string carry every required field, each with the declared type, a recognised enum member, a value inside its numeric range, and pagination values inside their bounds (`limit` from 1 to 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0) — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint. Several conventions of this step are uniform across the whole API. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so a body-less POST fails only on the required fields it is missing; a body that is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON that is not an object, fails here. A key present with the JSON value `null` is treated exactly as though it were absent: an optional field sent as `null` therefore takes its declared default rather than being stored as null, and a required field sent as `null` fails as missing. Keys the endpoint does not declare, and query parameters the endpoint does not declare, are ignored silently rather than rejected — including a client-supplied `id` or any server-derived field. A JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is declared, and a string is never accepted where a boolean, an integer or a number is declared, but a JSON float whose fractional part is zero (`3.0`) is accepted where an integer is declared. A declared string field is rejected if the supplied value is not of string type, or falls outside its declared length bounds; an identifier field additionally rejects a string that is empty or whitespace-only. Finally, cross-field range checks stated in §3 — a `label_dose_ml` above its own `vial_fill_ml`, a `max_interval_days` below its own `min_interval_days` — are evaluated in this step too, and therefore ahead of every foreign-key resolution, uniqueness check and business gate on the same request.
6. Every identifier in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — otherwise the 404 belonging to the entity that failed to resolve, drawn from `ERR_PAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRUG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_IMAGING_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECALL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that has a path identifier.
7. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint in §4's companion narrative — otherwise the same catalogue of 404s. Applies to every endpoint whose input carries a foreign key.
8. An acting `Patient` is touching only their own notifications — otherwise `ERR_NOT_NOTIFICATION_OWNER` (403). Applies to notification acknowledgement, the notification collection listing, and the single-notification read.
9. The wastage attester is not the injecting clinician — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to wastage attestation.
10. The adjudicating clinician is not the source encounter's injecting clinician — otherwise `ERR_ADJUDICATOR_IS_INJECTOR` (403). Applies to adverse-event adjudication.
11. The closing operator's `X-User-ID` differs from the recall's `initiated_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_RECALL_CLOSER_CONFLICT` (403). Applies to recall closure.
12. `expected_treatment_plan_version` equals the plan's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (412). Applies to injection-encounter creation and prior-authorization creation.
13. The target resource is in a state the requested transition allows — otherwise the state error belonging to that resource: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_TREATABLE` or `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RECALL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`, or `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every state-changing endpoint. `ERR_VIAL_STATE_INVALID` (409) completes that family in the catalogue of §5, but no endpoint addresses a transition at a `Vial` — every vial status change is a side effect of a lot, encounter or recall operation — so no request is ever refused with it.
14. The acting clinician is eligible for the act — otherwise `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation, where the injector must be in `ACTIVE` status with `injection_privilege` true; to wastage attestation, where the attesting clinician must be in `ACTIVE` status and their `injection_privilege` is not consulted; and — because a `Clinician` has no state error of its own in the step 13 catalogue — this same identifier also carries the clinician's own transition guard: suspending a clinician who is already `SUSPENDED` is `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
15. The imaging study belongs to the plan's patient and its eye matches the plan's eye — otherwise `ERR_IMAGING_EYE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to injection-encounter creation and plan activation.
16. The imaging study is inside its validity window — otherwise `ERR_IMAGING_STUDY_STALE` (422). Applies to injection-encounter creation and plan activation.
17. The gap since the plan's previous encounter is at least the effective drug's minimum interval — otherwise `ERR_INJECTION_INTERVAL_VIOLATION` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
18. The acting clinician has capacity in the rolling day window — otherwise `ERR_CLINICIAN_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
19. `override_reason` is present and non-empty whenever `medical_necessity_override` is true — otherwise `ERR_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
20. A `PayerDrugPolicy` exists for the patient's payer paired with the effective drug — otherwise `ERR_PAYER_POLICY_MISSING` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation, claim creation, prior-authorization submission and the `APPROVED` branch of a prior-authorization decision; on the two authorization steps the pairing looked up is the authorization's own copied `payer_id` and `drug_id` rather than a freshly re-derived one.
21. That policy's `coverage_tier` is not `EXCLUDED` — otherwise `ERR_DRUG_EXCLUDED_BY_PAYER` (422). Applies to injection-encounter creation and prior-authorization submission.
22. When the policy requires prior authorization, the plan has one in `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED` status — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
23. The selected authorization's effective window contains the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
24. The selected authorization's `drug_id` equals the encounter's `effective_drug_id` — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_DRUG_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
25. The selected authorization has capacity left — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
26. Vial selection finds at least one eligible vial — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VIAL` (409). Applies to injection-encounter creation.
27. At documentation time the reserved vial's lot is still `AVAILABLE` — otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Applies to encounter documentation.
28. The administered dose is greater than zero and does not exceed the effective drug's vial fill volume — otherwise `ERR_DOSE_EXCEEDS_VIAL_FILL` (422). Applies to encounter documentation.
29. `injection_site_prep_confirmed` is true — otherwise `ERR_PREP_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to encounter documentation.
30. The attested wastage volume matches the computed wastage volume to three decimal places — otherwise `ERR_ATTESTED_WASTAGE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to wastage attestation.
31. Counted prerequisite-drug failures reach the policy's required number — otherwise `ERR_STEP_THERAPY_NOT_SATISFIED` (422). Applies to prior-authorization submission.
32. `approved_injections` does not exceed the policy's `pa_max_injections` — otherwise `ERR_APPROVED_INJECTIONS_EXCEED_POLICY` (422). Applies to prior-authorization decision.
33. The new authorization window does not overlap another live window on the same plan — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to prior-authorization decision.
34. The appeal is filed inside the payer's appeal window — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to prior-authorization appeal and claim drug-line appeal.
35. `lot_number` is unique within its `drug_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER` (409). Applies to drug-lot receipt.
36. The patient has no open plan for the same eye — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_PLAN` (409). Applies to treatment-plan creation.
37. The encounter has no existing claim — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409). Applies to claim creation.
38. A manufacturer copay card is not being enrolled for a federal-program patient — otherwise `ERR_COPAY_CARD_FEDERAL_INELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to assistance-enrollment creation.
39. The patient has no other `ACTIVE` assistance enrollment — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT` (409). Applies to assistance-enrollment approval.
40. The lot has no recall in any status other than `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_RECALL_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). Applies to lot-recall creation and lot release.
41. The lot has at least one `QUARANTINED` vial — otherwise `ERR_NO_QUARANTINED_VIALS` (409). Applies to recall return shipment.
42. `credit_received_cents` does not exceed `credit_expected_cents` — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED` (422). Applies to recall credit reconciliation.
43. Acknowledged plus unreachable notifications equal the required count — otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_NOTIFICATIONS_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to recall closure.
44. No adverse event on the plan's encounters is still open — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_ADVERSE_EVENT` (409). Applies to plan resumption.
45. Cross-row uniqueness holds for `Payer.name`, `Drug.hcpcs_code`, `Patient.mrn`, `Clinician.npi` and the payer-and-drug pair of a `PayerDrugPolicy` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). Applies to payer creation, drug creation, patient creation, clinician creation and payer-drug-policy creation.
46. A supplied `reference_drug_id` points at a non-biosimilar drug, and a drug declaring itself a biosimilar supplies a non-null `reference_drug_id` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE_DRUG` (422). Applies to drug creation.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because role authorisation (step 3) precedes payload validation (step 5), a wrong-role request with a malformed body reports the role failure. Because the stale-version guard (step 12) precedes every state and business check (steps 13 onward), a request that is both stale and pointed at an untreatable plan reports the version mismatch. And because the segregation-of-duties checks (steps 9 through 11) precede state validation, a self-attestation attempt on an already-attested encounter reports the duties conflict rather than the state conflict.

### 2.9. Vial Conservation and Lot Counter Integrity

The number of vials belonging to a lot is fixed at receipt and is never afterwards changed. Whatever `vials_received` was booked in, exactly that many `Vial` rows exist for that lot forever; vials move between custody states but are never created, deleted or transferred to another lot. This is the conservation invariant that makes the inventory auditable, and it is why a voided injection does not return a consumed vial to stock.

Six per-lot counters mirror that population and are recomputed — never incrementally guessed — whenever any of the lot's vials changes state: `vials_on_hand` counts the lot's vials in `IN_STOCK`, `vials_reserved` counts those in `RESERVED`, `vials_administered` counts those in `ADMINISTERED`, `vials_quarantined` counts those in `QUARANTINED`, `vials_returned` counts those in `RETURNED`, and `vials_expired` counts those in `EXPIRED`. At every instant those six counters sum to `vials_received`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three same-person prohibitions are enforced on the acting `X-User-ID` and cannot be waived by any role or override. First, the clinician recorded as `clinician_id` on an injection encounter may not be the clinician who attests its wastage; the attester must be a different `Clinician` and must additionally be in `ACTIVE` status at the moment of attestation. Second, the clinician who performed an injection may not adjudicate an adverse event filed against that encounter. Third, the compliance officer whose opaque identifier was captured as `initiated_by_user_id` when a lot recall was opened may not be the officer who closes it.

### 2.11. Cascade Propagation and Compensating Reversal

Several operations in this system mutate more than one entity, and the propagation is always immediate and fully applied within the request that triggered it — there is no deferred queue and no eventual convergence. Where a cascade crosses an aggregate boundary the narrative in §3 names every hop, and an implementation must apply all of them or none.

Equally important are the *compensating* edges. Releasing a reservation returns a vial to `IN_STOCK` so the lot's counters stay conserved. Voiding an encounter that had consumed an authorization slot recomputes `injections_used` and pulls an `EXHAUSTED` authorization back to `APPROVED` when capacity is genuinely freed. A successful drug-line appeal unwinds the payer shortfall previously parked in `practice_writeoff_cents`. A re-run of the assistance waterfall that lowers an enrollment's consumed benefit pulls an `EXHAUSTED` enrollment back to `ACTIVE`. Each of these exists so that a reversal restores the ledger rather than leaving a permanent scar.

### 2.12. Resource Addressing and Filtered Discovery

Because the topology is flat, path identifiers are shallow — at most one identifier followed by an action segment. Where a path does contain an identifier it is validated before the body is interpreted for referential integrity purposes, in the left-to-right order in which the segments appear, and the first unresolvable segment produces its entity's 404.

Relationships are traversed by filtering. Every list endpoint exposes optional query parameters over its own foreign keys and status enums, so the vials of a lot are found by filtering the vial collection on `drug_lot_id`, the encounters of a plan by filtering the encounter collection on `treatment_plan_id`, and the notifications of a recall by filtering the notification collection on `lot_recall_id`; the complete filter set available on each collection is given with that collection's listing endpoint in §4. Filters combine conjunctively: supplying two filters returns only rows matching both, each by exact equality against the row's stored value. A filter over a string or identifier column matches by exact, case-sensitive equality with no partial or prefix matching; a boolean filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case; and a query parameter a collection does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, so an unrecognised parameter name narrows nothing. An unknown enum value supplied to a status filter, or an unparseable value supplied to a boolean, integer or instant filter, is a malformed request under `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than an empty result set. Routing itself is tolerant of a trailing slash: `/payers` and `/payers/` address the same collection and the server neither redirects nor rejects for the difference. A request whose path matches no endpoint at all, and a request whose path exists but whose HTTP method the endpoint does not support, are both answered in the standard error envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — carrying HTTP 404 in the first case and HTTP 405 in the second, the only two situations in which that identifier does not carry HTTP 400.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below follow the practice's own working order: reference data is loaded, a patient is imaged, a plan is opened and steered, the payer is asked for authorization, inventory is received and custody-tracked, an encounter is booked and documented, the claim is billed and remitted, and — when something goes wrong — an adverse event or a lot recall is worked to closure. Rules that govern more than one of those stages are stated where the practice first meets them.

One convention applies throughout. Where a transition described here carries a precondition for which no more specific error is named, the rejection is the target entity's state error at HTTP 409 — a plan precondition that is not otherwise catalogued surfaces as `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, an authorization precondition as `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID`, and likewise `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RECALL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` for their own entities. `ERR_VIAL_STATE_INVALID` belongs to the same family and is catalogued in §5, but no transition described here is gated on a vial's own status, so it is not an error any request receives.

### 3.1. Reference Data Foundations: Payers, Products and Coverage Policies

A `PracticeAdmin` seeds the world before any clinical work is possible. A payer is registered with a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique across all payers, a `payer_type` drawn from `COMMERCIAL`, `MEDICARE_ADVANTAGE`, `MEDICARE_PART_B` and `MEDICAID`, and a `coinsurance_rate` expressing the fraction of the allowed amount that lands on the patient — at least 0, at most 1, and carried to at most four decimal places. Three further attributes are optional at registration and carry defaults: `pa_required` defaults to true and expresses the payer's house rule on prior authorization; `biosimilar_preference` defaults to `NONE` and may instead be `PREFERRED` or `MANDATORY`; and `appeal_window_days` defaults to 60 and must fall between 1 and 365 inclusive, fixing how long after a denial an appeal is still accepted. A payer's `status` is never client-supplied: it is fixed at `ACTIVE` when the payer is registered and stays `ACTIVE` for the lifetime of the row, because the API exposes no endpoint that transitions it. `INACTIVE` is nevertheless an accepted value of the payer `status` filter, and simply matches no payer.

One payer attribute is never supplied by the client. `federal_program` is derived from the contract family: it is true when `payer_type` is `MEDICARE_PART_B`, `MEDICARE_ADVANTAGE` or `MEDICAID`, and false when `payer_type` is `COMMERCIAL`. That single boolean is what later blocks a manufacturer copay card from being applied to a federally funded patient, and because it is derived rather than declared, the practice cannot accidentally opt a Medicare patient into an ineligible assistance program. The payer collection may be filtered on `name`, `payer_type`, `federal_program`, `biosimilar_preference` and `status`.

Products are registered next. A drug carries a `brand_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `hcpcs_code` of 5 to 8 characters that must be unique across all drugs and is the J-code billed on the drug line, and a `vial_type` that is either `SINGLE_DOSE` — for which discarded volume is a billable wastage line — or `MULTI_DOSE_COMPOUNDED`, for which no wastage line is ever produced. The physical description of the product is three numbers: `vial_fill_ml`, the total volume supplied in one vial, which must be greater than zero and carry at most three decimal places; `label_dose_ml`, the labelled intravitreal dose volume, which must be greater than zero, must not exceed `vial_fill_ml`, and likewise carries at most three decimals; and `mg_per_ml`, the concentration, which must be greater than zero and is what converts an injected volume into milligrams. Billing description adds `billing_unit_mg`, the milligrams represented by one HCPCS billing unit, which must be greater than zero, and `asp_cents_per_billing_unit`, the published average sales price per billing unit in cents, which must be at least 0 and serves as the standard-cost basis for provisional margin.

Safety bounds live on the product too. `min_interval_days` is the shortest safe gap between two injections of this product in one eye and must be at least 1; `max_interval_days` is the longest treat-and-extend interval permitted for the product and must be greater than or equal to `min_interval_days`. Biosimilar lineage is expressed by two more fields: `is_biosimilar`, which defaults to false, and `reference_drug_id`, which defaults to null and, when populated, names the reference product this biosimilar copies — a drug pointing at another drug in the same collection. The two must agree, and the agreement is enforced in both directions: a supplied `reference_drug_id` must resolve to a drug whose own `is_biosimilar` is false, and a drug declaring `is_biosimilar` true must supply a non-null `reference_drug_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE_DRUG` (HTTP 422); a `reference_drug_id` that resolves to nothing at all is instead `ERR_DRUG_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Like a payer, a drug carries a `status` the client never supplies: it is fixed at `ACTIVE` at registration and no endpoint transitions it, while `INACTIVE` stays an accepted value of the drug `status` filter that matches no drug. The drug collection may be filtered on `hcpcs_code`, `reference_drug_id`, `is_biosimilar`, `vial_type` and `status`.

Coverage is the join between a payer and a drug, and exactly one such policy may exist for a given pairing — a second attempt at the same `payer_id` and `drug_id` combination is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409), the same error that guards a duplicate payer `name`, a duplicate `hcpcs_code`, a duplicate patient `mrn` and a duplicate clinician `npi` — every one of those uniqueness comparisons being exact, case-sensitive string equality over the whole stored value, so `Aetna` and `aetna` are two distinct payers and neither trimming nor case folding is applied before comparing. Each policy names its `payer_id` and `drug_id`, then a `coverage_tier` of `PREFERRED`, `NON_PREFERRED` or `EXCLUDED`, and two contracted allowables in cents, each at least 0: `drug_allowed_cents_per_billing_unit` for the drug line and `admin_allowed_cents` for the administration line. `wastage_reimbursed` defaults to false and states whether the payer will actually pay the wastage line — note carefully that this governs reimbursement, not billing, and the practice bills wastage regardless. `pa_required` on the policy defaults to true and *overrides* the payer-level default for this specific pairing; it is the policy value, not the payer value, that decides whether an authorization is demanded. `pa_validity_days` defaults to 180 and must fall between 1 and 730 inclusive, fixing how long an approved authorization stays effective. `pa_max_injections` defaults to 3, must be at least 1, and caps how many injections one authorization may cover. Step therapy is expressed by `step_therapy_prerequisite_drug_id`, which defaults to null and, when set, names the product that must first be documented as having failed, together with `required_prior_failures`, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A `PayerDrugPolicy` carries no `status` field of its own; policies may be listed filtered by `payer_id`, `drug_id`, `step_therapy_prerequisite_drug_id`, `coverage_tier`, `pa_required` and `wastage_reimbursed`. The three foreign keys a policy supplies are resolved in the order `payer_id`, then `drug_id`, then `step_therapy_prerequisite_drug_id`, and only once all three resolve is the duplicate-pairing check run.

Patients and clinicians complete the reference layer. A patient carries an `mrn` — the practice medical record number, 4 to 24 characters, unique across patients — a `full_name`, the `payer_id` of their current payer, the payer-issued `member_id`, a `notification_channel` of `SMS`, `EMAIL` or `PHONE` defaulting to `PHONE`, and a `contact_point` holding the phone number or email address matching that channel — which is required, must be a non-empty string, and is stored verbatim, the server never checking that its shape agrees with the chosen channel. A patient row carries no diagnosis field and no federal-program flag of its own; the federal-program determination is always read from the patient's payer. A patient's `status` behaves the same way: fixed at `ACTIVE` at registration, transitioned by no endpoint, with `INACTIVE` accepted only as a filter value; patient listings may be filtered on `mrn`, `payer_id` and `status`. Because neither a patient nor a drug can leave `ACTIVE`, the `ACTIVE`-status preconditions that plan activation (§3.3) and biosimilar candidate selection (§3.6) place on them are stated for completeness and cannot in fact fail. A clinician carries a `full_name`, an `npi` of exactly ten digits that must be unique across clinicians, a `credential` of either `MD` or `DO`, an `injection_privilege` flag defaulting to true that records whether the practice has granted intravitreal injection privileges, and `max_injections_per_day` defaulting to 12 and required to be at least 1, which caps aggregate injection volume inside any closed twenty-four-hour window. Clinician listings may be filtered on `npi`, `status`, `credential` and `injection_privilege`. A `status` filter on the payer, drug or patient collection accepts only `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, while a `status` filter on the clinician collection accepts only `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

A clinician's own `status` begins `ACTIVE` and has exactly one transition: a `PracticeAdmin` may move `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, supplying a required, non-empty free-text `suspension_reason` that is stored on the row and which remains null for as long as the clinician is `ACTIVE`. The transition answers HTTP 200 with the full updated clinician, and there is no reinstatement edge — `SUSPENDED` is terminal for a clinician. Suspension is forward-looking only. It deliberately does **not** void, alter or unwind any of that clinician's existing encounters — documented injections stay documented and billed claims stay billable — but a suspended clinician can no longer create a new encounter and can no longer serve as the second-clinician wastage attester. Attempting the suspension transition on a clinician who is already `SUSPENDED` is rejected under the state-precedence rule of §2.8 with `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409), the identifier that stands in for the `Clinician` entity's missing state error.

### 3.2. Imaging Interpretation and the Disease-Activity Verdict

Every treatment decision in this practice begins with an OCT. A `RetinaSpecialist` records an imaging study naming the `patient_id` imaged, the `eye` — `OD` for the right eye or `OS` for the left — and the `study_datetime`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant the scan was captured. The clinical read is four findings plus one measurement: `central_subfield_thickness_um`, an integer count of micrometres that must lie between 100 and 900 inclusive; `intraretinal_fluid`, `subretinal_fluid` and `new_hemorrhage`, three booleans recording fluid on OCT and new haemorrhage on examination; and `va_letter_score`, the ETDRS letter score, an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive. The acting specialist's own identifier is stamped onto the study as `interpreted_by_clinician_id`; a client may not nominate the interpreter.

The study's `disease_activity` verdict is computed by the server and a client-supplied value is ignored outright. The verdict is `ACTIVE` when the central subfield thickness is at least 300 micrometres, or intraretinal fluid is present, or subretinal fluid is present, or a new haemorrhage is present — any one of those four conditions suffices. When none of the four holds, the verdict is `QUIESCENT`. The thickness threshold is inclusive at 300: a reading of 299 micrometres with no fluid and no haemorrhage is `QUIESCENT`, while a reading of exactly 300 micrometres with no fluid and no haemorrhage is `ACTIVE`. Nothing outside the study being created participates in the verdict — no prior study, no plan history, no drug attribute. Imaging studies may be listed filtered on `patient_id`, `interpreted_by_clinician_id`, `eye` and `disease_activity`, and two of those — the interpreter and the verdict — each answer to a second accepted spelling: `interpreting_clinician_id` filters the stored `interpreted_by_clinician_id` and `activity_classification` filters the stored `disease_activity`, so six parameter names are recognised on this collection and any other name is ignored under §2.12.

Because a study is an immutable observation it is never edited after creation, and as §2.4 establishes it therefore carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. That immutability matters downstream: the verdict recorded at read time is the verdict the plan will act on weeks later, and a study that has aged out of its fourteen-day validity window cannot be refreshed — a new scan must be taken.

### 3.3. Opening a Treatment Plan and Steering the Treat-and-Extend Interval

A `RetinaSpecialist` opens a plan for one patient and one eye, naming the `patient_id`, the `drug_id` of the planned product before any biosimilar substitution, and the `eye`. `loading_doses_required` is optional, defaults to 3, and must fall between 1 and 6 inclusive; it fixes how many injections must be administered before the plan graduates out of loading. A new plan is born in `DRAFT` with `version` 1, and at most one plan per patient and eye may be open at a time: if that patient already has a plan for the same `eye` whose status is `DRAFT`, `LOADING`, `MAINTENANCE`, `INTENSIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`, creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_PLAN` (HTTP 409). Only a plan that reached `DISCONTINUED` frees the eye for a fresh plan. Plans may be listed filtered on `patient_id`, `drug_id`, `baseline_imaging_study_id`, `last_encounter_id`, `status` and `eye`.

Activation is the decision that treatment is justified, and it moves the plan `DRAFT → LOADING`. The specialist supplies `baseline_imaging_study_id`, naming the study that justifies treatment, and four things must hold beyond the plan being in `DRAFT`: the patient's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the drug's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the nominated study belongs to the plan's patient and its `eye` equals the plan's `eye`, and that study's `disease_activity` is `ACTIVE`. A study whose patient or eye disagrees with the plan is rejected with `ERR_IMAGING_EYE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The study must also be fresh: the logical clock must be at or after `study_datetime` and no more than fourteen days — two weeks — after it, and a study outside that window is rejected with `ERR_IMAGING_STUDY_STALE` (HTTP 422). That fourteen-day limit is an exact instant difference of 336 hours rather than a truncated whole-day count, so a clock standing fourteen days and one second after `study_datetime` is already stale. A study that resolves to nothing is `ERR_IMAGING_STUDY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and an inactive patient, an inactive drug or a baseline whose verdict is `QUIESCENT` falls to `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) under the convention stated at the head of this section. Those `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` conditions are step-13 checks, taken in the order plan status, patient status, drug status, baseline verdict, and all four precede the eye-mismatch and staleness checks of steps 15 and 16 — so a `QUIESCENT` baseline that also belongs to the wrong eye reports `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_IMAGING_EYE_MISMATCH`.

A successful activation writes `baseline_imaging_study_id` onto the plan, sets `current_interval_days` to 28 — four weeks, the loading cadence — sets `next_due_date` to the logical clock advanced by those 28 days, and advances the plan's `version` by 1. Before activation, `current_interval_days` and `next_due_date` are both null, as are `baseline_imaging_study_id` and `last_encounter_id`; `injections_administered` and `consecutive_quiescent_visits` both start at 0.

**Treat-and-extend steering.** Every time an encounter is documented, the plan's schedule is recomputed as a side effect, and the recomputation is a single pass with exactly one branch taken. Three inputs are read: the count of doses this plan will have administered once the current documentation lands, which is the plan's `injections_administered` increased by one; the `disease_activity` verdict of the one imaging study the encounter was built on; and the plan's `consecutive_quiescent_visits` *as it stood before this visit*. The branches are evaluated in this order.

- If the incremented dose count is still strictly fewer than `loading_doses_required`, the plan is still loading. `current_interval_days` is pinned to 28 days regardless of what the imaging showed. The quiescent run still accumulates: it becomes the prior run increased by one when the driving study was `QUIESCENT`, and is reset to 0 when the study was `ACTIVE`.
- Otherwise, if the driving study was `QUIESCENT`, the interval extends. The extension step is 14 days — two weeks — but it doubles to 28 days when the prior quiescent run was already at least 3, rewarding a patient who has held quiet for three consecutive visits with a full extra month of freedom. The new `current_interval_days` is the smaller of the prior interval plus that step and the effective drug's `max_interval_days`, so the product's labelled ceiling always wins. `consecutive_quiescent_visits` becomes the prior run increased by one.
- Otherwise the driving study was `ACTIVE` and the interval contracts. The new `current_interval_days` is the larger of the prior interval less 14 days and the effective drug's `min_interval_days`, so the product's labelled floor always wins. `consecutive_quiescent_visits` resets to 0.

Whichever branch fires, the plan is then published in one step: `current_interval_days` and `consecutive_quiescent_visits` take their new values, `next_due_date` becomes the encounter's `encounter_datetime` advanced by the new interval in whole days while keeping the encounter's time of day, `injections_administered` takes the incremented count, and `last_encounter_id` points at the encounter just documented. `injections_administered` is maintained rather than recomputed on this path: documentation simply stores the incremented count, without consulting the plan's other encounters. It is re-derived only when a `DOCUMENTED` encounter is voided, and the population re-derived over is this plan's encounters whose status is `DOCUMENTED`, `ATTESTED`, `BILLED` or `CLOSED` — `DRAFT` and `VOIDED` encounters excluded — replayed in ascending `encounter_datetime` order with ascending `created_at` breaking ties. The two rules never disagree: documentation is the only edge into that population and the void of a `DOCUMENTED` encounter the only edge out, so the stored count always equals the size of the population. `last_encounter_id` is likewise always the plan's non-`VOIDED` encounter with the greatest `encounter_datetime`, with the greatest `created_at` breaking a tie, and is null before the plan's first encounter. That greatest-datetime rule is what the re-derivations at encounter creation, at a void and inside every cascade enforce; the documentation step itself instead pins `last_encounter_id` directly at the encounter it just documented, so documenting a back-dated encounter moves the pointer onto that encounter even while another non-`VOIDED` encounter carries a later `encounter_datetime`.

The plan's own status moves alongside that arithmetic. A plan in `LOADING` graduates `LOADING → MAINTENANCE` on the documentation whose incremented dose count exactly equals `loading_doses_required` — and note that this graduating dose is no longer in the loading branch, so its interval is already extended or reduced from 28 days according to the driving study's verdict rather than pinned. While the incremented count is still below the requirement, the plan stays in `LOADING`. Once in maintenance the plan oscillates: an `ACTIVE` study drives `MAINTENANCE → INTENSIFIED` with the reduction branch and the quiescent run reset to 0, a `QUIESCENT` study drives `INTENSIFIED → MAINTENANCE` with the extension branch and the run incremented, and a `QUIESCENT` study on a plan already in `MAINTENANCE` re-enters `MAINTENANCE` with the same extension branch and the same increment. There is no edge out of `INTENSIFIED` for an `ACTIVE` study, so such a documentation leaves the status at `INTENSIFIED` while still applying the reduction branch to the interval and resetting the run. The `MAINTENANCE` and `INTENSIFIED` pair can therefore cycle indefinitely for the life of the plan, and each of these documentations advances the plan's `version` by 1.

**Interrupting a plan.** A specialist may suspend a plan that is in `LOADING`, `MAINTENANCE` or `INTENSIFIED`, supplying a `suspension_reason` drawn from `ADVERSE_EVENT`, `CLINICIAN_HOLD`, `LOT_RECALL` and `PATIENT_REQUEST`; that value is stored and remains null unless the plan is `SUSPENDED`. Suspension is a three-hop cascade. First the plan itself moves to `SUSPENDED` and its `next_due_date` is cleared to null. Second, every vial currently `RESERVED` for one of this plan's `DRAFT` encounters returns to `IN_STOCK` and each of those encounters moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` recorded as `PLAN_SUSPENDED`; because they have left the non-`VOIDED` population, the plan's `last_encounter_id` is re-derived in the same breath so the pointer lands on a surviving encounter — a re-derivation that every cascade voiding a plan's `DRAFT` encounters performs, and that carries no version bump of its own beyond the one the triggering operation already books. Third, because those encounters became `VOIDED`, `injections_used` is recomputed on every prior authorization that lost an encounter, and any authorization sitting in `EXHAUSTED` whose recomputed usage now falls below its `approved_injections` returns to `APPROVED`. The plan's `version` advances by 1. Suspension may also arrive as a system cascade rather than a specialist action, in which case `suspension_reason` is `ADVERSE_EVENT`; that path is described in §3.9 and its side effects are identical.

Resumption moves the plan `SUSPENDED → MAINTENANCE` and takes no request body. It is blocked while any adverse event linked to one of the plan's encounters is still `REPORTED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` — that rejection is `ERR_OPEN_ADVERSE_EVENT` (HTTP 409), and the encounters scanned are all of the plan's encounters whatever their own status, `DRAFT` and `VOIDED` ones included, so an open event filed against an encounter that a cascade later voided still holds the plan shut. Vial availability, by contrast, does **not** block resumption: even when no eligible vial exists for the plan's product, the resume succeeds and `next_due_date` is set anyway. On success, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null and `next_due_date` becomes the logical clock advanced by the plan's `current_interval_days` in whole days, and `version` advances by 1. One consequence is worth stating plainly: a plan that was `INTENSIFIED` when it was suspended always re-enters `MAINTENANCE`, never `INTENSIFIED`, and carries its pre-suspension interval with it.

Discontinuation is terminal and is reachable from `DRAFT`, `LOADING`, `MAINTENANCE`, `INTENSIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`. The specialist supplies a free-text `discontinuation_reason`, which is required and must be a non-empty string, and which is stored and is otherwise null. The plan moves to `DISCONTINUED`, `next_due_date` is cleared to null, every vial reserved by one of the plan's `DRAFT` encounters is released back to `IN_STOCK` while those encounters move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` recorded as `PLAN_DISCONTINUED` — the one cascade that writes that value, where suspension writes `PLAN_SUSPENDED` — and — because those voids free authorization capacity — `injections_used` is recomputed with the same `EXHAUSTED → APPROVED` revival. `version` advances by 1. Nothing leaves `DISCONTINUED`: any transition attempted on a discontinued plan is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), as is activation of a plan that is not in `DRAFT`, suspension of a plan that is in `DRAFT` or already terminal, and resumption of a plan that is not `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.4. Payer Authorization: Step Therapy, Decisions and Appeals

Authorization work belongs to the `BillingSpecialist`, and it starts from the plan. Creating an authorization takes only two inputs — the `treatment_plan_id` it covers and the `expected_treatment_plan_version` guard described in §2.6 — and the server copies the rest so that later reference-data edits cannot retroactively rewrite history: `patient_id` is copied from the plan, `drug_id` is copied from the plan's planned product and is never re-derived afterwards, and `payer_id` is copied from the patient's current payer. The new authorization begins in `DRAFT` with `injections_used` at 0, `submission_count` at 0, `step_therapy_failures_documented` at 0, and `auth_number`, `approved_injections`, `effective_date`, `expiration_date`, `clinical_justification`, `information_requested`, `denial_reason` and `appeal_basis` all null. Creating the authorization advances the plan's `version` by 1. The plan's own status is never consulted at creation: an authorization may be opened against a plan in any status, `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` and `DISCONTINUED` included, so beyond a payload or foreign-key failure the only rejection the create can raise is the version mismatch. Authorizations may be listed filtered on `treatment_plan_id`, `patient_id`, `drug_id`, `payer_id` and `status`.

Submission moves the authorization from either `DRAFT` or `INFO_REQUESTED` into `SUBMITTED` and carries the `clinical_justification` narrative the payer will read, which is required and must be a non-empty string. Three things must hold. The payer-and-drug pairing must have a coverage policy — when none exists the submission cannot be priced or evaluated and is rejected with `ERR_PAYER_POLICY_MISSING` (HTTP 409). That policy's `coverage_tier` must not be `EXCLUDED`, since a payer that excludes a product will never authorize it; an excluded tier is rejected with `ERR_DRUG_EXCLUDED_BY_PAYER` (HTTP 422). And step therapy must be satisfied.

**Counting step-therapy failures.** The count is taken fresh at every submission. When the governing policy's `step_therapy_prerequisite_drug_id` is null, no step therapy applies: `step_therapy_failures_documented` is written as 0 and the gate is skipped entirely, so a policy whose `required_prior_failures` happens to be greater than zero without naming a prerequisite drug never blocks anything. When a prerequisite drug is named, the server counts this patient's injection encounters — across every plan the patient has ever had, with no time window at all, so the whole treatment history counts — restricted to encounters whose status is `DOCUMENTED`, `ATTESTED`, `BILLED` or `CLOSED`, whose `effective_drug_id` is the prerequisite product (the product actually injected, not whatever the plan nominated), and whose driving imaging study carried a `disease_activity` verdict of `ACTIVE`. That count — a documented failure being an injection of the prerequisite that nonetheless left the eye active — is written to `step_therapy_failures_documented`. The gate then requires the count to be at least the policy's `required_prior_failures`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_STEP_THERAPY_NOT_SATISFIED` (HTTP 422). Because the result is a count, no ordering or tie-break is involved.

A first submission out of `DRAFT` stores the `clinical_justification`, writes `step_therapy_failures_documented`, and increases `submission_count` by 1. A resubmission out of `INFO_REQUESTED` does slightly more: it *replaces* `clinical_justification` with the new narrative, clears `information_requested` back to null, recomputes `step_therapy_failures_documented` against whatever the history now shows, and again increases `submission_count` by 1. Submission is rejected with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) from any status other than `DRAFT` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — notably from `APPROVED`.

The payer's answer arrives as a decision, which is accepted only while the authorization sits in `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`. The request carries a `decision` value and the fields that decision requires; each of those conditional requirements is enforced inside the payload validation of §2.8 step 5, so a decision arriving without the field its branch demands is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and because step 5 precedes step 6 that rejection is reported even when the path identifier names no authorization at all. `approved_injections`, whenever supplied, must be an integer of at least 1. A decision of `INFO_REQUESTED` requires `information_requested`, the payer's outstanding-information note, and stores it while moving the authorization to `INFO_REQUESTED` — which the specialist answers by resubmitting, so `SUBMITTED` and `INFO_REQUESTED` may cycle as many times as the payer keeps asking. A decision of `DENIED` requires a `denial_reason` drawn from `STEP_THERAPY`, `NOT_MEDICALLY_NECESSARY`, `NON_FORMULARY` and `INCOMPLETE_DOCUMENTATION`; it stores that reason and deliberately leaves `approved_injections` null. When a denial arrives on an authorization that was in `APPEALED`, the effect is the same except that the stored `denial_reason` replaces the earlier one.

A decision of `APPROVED` is the substantial one and requires all three of `auth_number` — the payer-issued authorization number — `approved_injections` and `effective_date`, the first instant the authorization covers. This branch alone re-reads the coverage policy for the authorization's copied `payer_id` and `drug_id`, and a pairing whose policy no longer exists is rejected with `ERR_PAYER_POLICY_MISSING` (HTTP 409) ahead of both gates below. Two gates then apply. `approved_injections` may not exceed the governing policy's `pa_max_injections`; exceeding it is `ERR_APPROVED_INJECTIONS_EXCEED_POLICY` (HTTP 422). And the coverage window this decision would create — the closed interval running from `effective_date` through `effective_date` advanced by the policy's `pa_validity_days` in whole days — may not overlap the equivalent interval of any *other* authorization on the same plan whose status is `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED`. Touching endpoints count as an overlap, so a new window must begin at least one full day after the previous window's last day; a collision is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). On success the decision stores `auth_number`, `approved_injections` and `effective_date`, computes `expiration_date` as `effective_date` advanced by `pa_validity_days` whole days, and clears any `denial_reason` back to null — which is equally true whether the approval arrived from `SUBMITTED` or from `APPEALED`, closing the denial-and-appeal cycle.

A denial may be appealed, which moves the authorization `DENIED → APPEALED` and stores the `appeal_basis` narrative, itself required and non-empty. The appeal must be filed in time: the logical clock must be at or before the authorization's `updated_at` advanced by the payer's `appeal_window_days` in whole days, and a late filing is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The window is read from the payer the authorization copied into `payer_id` at creation, not from whatever payer the patient carries now, and the `updated_at` it starts from is the one the denial itself stamped. Appealing anything that is not `DENIED` is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Two of the authorization's status moves are made by the system rather than by an operator, and both are driven by encounter activity. `injections_used` is defined as the count of non-`VOIDED` injection encounters whose `prior_authorization_id` names this authorization. When creating an encounter pushes that count up to exactly `approved_injections`, the authorization moves `APPROVED → EXHAUSTED`, and nothing on the row changes beyond its status and `updated_at`. The reverse edge is the compensating one: when an encounter consuming this authorization is voided — whether by an explicit void or by a cascade from plan suspension or discontinuation — `injections_used` is recomputed, and if the recomputed value is strictly below `approved_injections` and the logical clock is still at or before `expiration_date`, the authorization moves `EXHAUSTED → APPROVED`, restoring the freed slot. An authorization whose window has already closed does not revive.

`EXPIRED` is the authorization's terminal status and is reached lazily. When an encounter creation finds no authorization on the plan whose coverage window contains the logical clock, the single fallback authorization it lands on — the `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED` one carrying the greatest `expiration_date`, selected as §3.6 details — is flipped to `EXPIRED` from either of those two statuses, that flip is persisted, and only then is the triggering encounter creation rejected with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). The ordering matters: a client that retries after such a rejection will find the authorization already recorded as `EXPIRED`.

### 3.5. Buy-and-Bill Inventory Custody: Lots, Vials, Exposure and the Sweep

A `PharmacyTech` books drug into inventory one lot at a time, supplying the `drug_id` of the product, the manufacturer's `lot_number` (3 to 24 characters, unique within that drug — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER`, HTTP 409), the `expiration_date` and `received_date` instants, the `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` the practice paid for one vial from this lot, which must be at least 0, and `vials_received`, which must fall between 1 and 500 inclusive. Neither instant is constrained against the other or against the logical clock, so a lot may be booked in already past its `expiration_date` and still begins life `AVAILABLE` — only the sweep below moves it to `EXPIRED`. Receipt is the only moment vials come into existence: exactly `vials_received` vial rows are created, all in `IN_STOCK`, each labelled with a practice-minted `serial_number` formed from the lot number, a hyphen, and a zero-padded three-digit sequence beginning at `001` and ascending in creation order, each vial born with `reserved_for_encounter_id`, `reserved_at` and `administered_at` all null. Immediately after receipt `vials_on_hand` equals `vials_received` and every other vial counter on the lot is 0. A lot begins in `AVAILABLE` with `quarantine_reason` null, `carrying_cost_accrued_cents` at 0, `last_swept_at` null, and `related_endophthalmitis_count` at 0. Lots may be listed filtered on `drug_id`, `lot_number`, `status` and `expiration_date` — the last of these matching by exact instant equality against the stored value; vials may be listed filtered on `drug_lot_id`, `serial_number`, `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `status`.

A vial's own life is a custody chain. It starts `IN_STOCK`; it becomes `RESERVED` when an encounter claims it, at which point `reserved_for_encounter_id` names that encounter and `reserved_at` records the logical clock; it becomes `ADMINISTERED` when the injection is documented, at which point `administered_at` is stamped with the encounter's `encounter_datetime` rather than with the clock. `QUARANTINED` is the holding state for stock that must not be used, `RETURNED` is the state of stock shipped back to the manufacturer under a recall, and `EXPIRED` is the state of stock that aged out on the shelf. `ADMINISTERED`, `RETURNED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` is the compensating edge that keeps the conserved count of §2.9 intact when a reservation is abandoned; taking it clears both `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `reserved_at` back to null.

Quarantine is the tech's tool for holding suspect stock. A lot in `AVAILABLE` moves to `QUARANTINED` with a stored `quarantine_reason` — required, and rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) when empty — and every one of its vials that was `IN_STOCK` moves to `QUARANTINED` in the same operation; `vials_on_hand` and `vials_quarantined` are recomputed and the lot's `version` advances by 1. Vials already `RESERVED` are left alone by a plain quarantine — only a recall reaches into reservations. Quarantining a lot that is not `AVAILABLE` is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Releasing a quarantine is a `ComplianceOfficer` decision rather than the tech's, precisely because it puts high-cost biologic back into clinical circulation. The lot must be `QUARANTINED`, no recall on that lot may exist in any status other than `CLOSED` — an open recall makes release impossible and is rejected with `ERR_RECALL_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — and the lot's `expiration_date` must be strictly after the logical clock, so an expired lot can never be released back into stock. Those three preconditions are evaluated in exactly that written order, which places the open-recall check of §2.8 step 40 ahead of the expiration check even though the expiration failure reports a step-13 identifier: a quarantined lot that is both under an open recall and already past its `expiration_date` reports `ERR_RECALL_ALREADY_OPEN`, not `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. On success `quarantine_reason` is cleared to null, every vial of the lot that is `QUARANTINED` **and** whose `reserved_for_encounter_id` is null returns to `IN_STOCK`, `vials_on_hand` and `vials_quarantined` are recomputed, and the lot's `version` advances by 1. A quarantined vial still carrying a reservation pointer stays quarantined.

**Exposure, read two ways.** A `PracticeAdmin`, `PharmacyTech` or `BillingSpecialist` may ask a lot what it is costing the practice, supplying a `basis` of either `CARRYING` or `EXPIRATION`. `basis` is a required query parameter checked at §2.8 step 5, so omitting it altogether is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) exactly as supplying an unrecognised value is, and because step 5 precedes step 6 that rejection is reported even when the path identifier names no lot. The read persists nothing. Its response echoes the `drug_lot_id` and the `basis` and reports `days_held`, `unconsumed_vials`, `unconsumed_value_cents`, `exposure_cents`, `projected_burn_vials` and `at_risk_vials`.

The `CARRYING` basis measures the financing cost of capital still sitting on the shelf, and it ignores the expiration date entirely — it grows every single day the lot goes unused. `days_held` is the whole number of days from the lot's `received_date` to the logical clock, truncated toward zero exactly as §2.5 prescribes, and is therefore a negative count when the clock stands before the lot's `received_date`. Only vials in `IN_STOCK` are counted, because capital behind a `RESERVED` vial is treated as already committed; that count is reported as `unconsumed_vials`, and multiplying it by `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` gives `unconsumed_value_cents`. The accrual itself takes that same unconsumed value, applies the practice's annual financing rate of 2000 basis points — twenty percent, expressed as the basis points divided by ten thousand — multiplies by `days_held`, divides by a 365-day year, and rounds the result half up to whole cents exactly once at the very end rather than at any intermediate step. That figure is `exposure_cents`. On this basis `projected_burn_vials` and `at_risk_vials` are both reported as 0.

The `EXPIRATION` basis instead asks how much acquisition value will never be injected, and it ignores how long the lot has been held. The practice's recent consumption rate is drawn from a thirty-day trailing window that is half-open: it begins at the logical clock moved back thirty days, includes that lower endpoint, and runs up to but does *not* include the clock itself. Inside that window the server counts vials whose status is `ADMINISTERED` and whose `administered_at` falls in the window, across every lot of the **same** product — the burn rate is a property of the drug, not of this one lot. Dividing that count by thirty gives a per-day burn rate carried as an exact rational value with no rounding. Days remaining are the whole days from the logical clock to the lot's `expiration_date`, truncated downward and floored at 0 so an already-expired lot contributes no remaining days. Multiplying the burn rate by the days remaining and taking the floor gives the projected consumable count, reported as `projected_burn_vials`. The vials at stake are those in `IN_STOCK` plus those in `RESERVED` — reported here as `unconsumed_vials`, with `unconsumed_value_cents` again being that count times `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` — and the shortfall between that population and the projected consumable count, floored at 0, is the unusable count reported as `at_risk_vials`. Multiplying the unusable count by `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` gives `exposure_cents` on this basis. `days_held` is reported on this basis too, with the same meaning as before. Both bases are insensitive to the order in which rows are examined.

**The expiration sweep.** A `PharmacyTech` may run a sweep over the whole inventory; it takes no request body. Every lot is visited. For each visited lot the carrying accrual described above is recomputed and *persisted* into `carrying_cost_accrued_cents` — this endpoint is the only writer of that field, so it is stale by exactly as much time as has passed since the last sweep — and `last_swept_at` is set to the logical clock. That accrual write is itself a mutation of the lot, so every visited lot has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced by 1 whatever its status and whether or not the sweep changes anything else about it. Any lot whose status is `AVAILABLE` and whose `expiration_date` is at or before the logical clock moves `AVAILABLE → EXPIRED`, every vial of that lot still in `IN_STOCK` moves to `EXPIRED`, and `vials_on_hand` and `vials_expired` are recomputed — all of it inside the single `version` advance the lot already books on this run, since §2.4 scopes the counter to the request rather than to the hop. The two halves of the visit are ordered: the accrual is computed and persisted *before* the lot's vials are expired, so on the very run that expires a lot the accrual still counts the vials this run is about to move out of `IN_STOCK`, and it is those pre-expiry figures that `carrying_cost_accrued_cents_total` sums. `EXPIRED` and `RECALLED` are both terminal lot statuses. The sweep answers with a summary rather than a resource: `lots_swept` counts the lots visited, `lots_expired` counts the lots that moved to `EXPIRED` on this run, `vials_expired` counts the vials that moved to `EXPIRED` on this run, `carrying_cost_accrued_cents_total` sums `carrying_cost_accrued_cents` across the lots swept, and `swept_at` reports the logical-clock instant of the sweep.

### 3.6. Booking an Injection Encounter: Product Selection, Vial Reservation and Safety Gates

Booking an encounter is the busiest operation in the system, and it is a `RetinaSpecialist` action. The specialist supplies four things plus two optional ones: the `treatment_plan_id` being treated, the `imaging_study_id` of the study that drove the treatment decision, the `encounter_datetime` at which the injection is (or was) given as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, and the `expected_treatment_plan_version` guard. The two optional inputs belong to the biosimilar override discussed below: `medical_necessity_override`, a boolean defaulting to false, and `override_reason`, its justification. The acting specialist's identifier is stamped as `clinician_id`, and `patient_id` and `eye` are copied from the plan; none of the three may be supplied by the client. A new encounter is born in `DRAFT`.

The plan must be treatable, meaning its status is `LOADING`, `MAINTENANCE` or `INTENSIFIED`. A plan in `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `DISCONTINUED` cannot receive an injection, and the rejection carries its own dedicated code: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_TREATABLE` (HTTP 409). The acting clinician must also be fit to inject — `status` `ACTIVE` and `injection_privilege` true — and a suspended or unprivileged clinician is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409).

The imaging study is validated next. It must belong to the plan's patient and its `eye` must equal the plan's `eye`, or the request is `ERR_IMAGING_EYE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). It must also be fresh relative to the injection: the difference between `encounter_datetime` and the study's `study_datetime` must be at least zero and no more than fourteen days, where that fourteen-day limit is an exact instant difference of 336 hours rather than a truncated whole-day count, so an encounter dated fourteen days and one second after its own study is already stale, and an encounter dated *before* its own imaging study is just as invalid as one dated more than two weeks after it. Either failure is `ERR_IMAGING_STUDY_STALE` (HTTP 422).

**Choosing the product actually injected.** Before any check that depends on the drug, the server resolves which product will really go into the eye, writing `effective_drug_id` and, when a swap occurred, `substituted_from_drug_id` (null otherwise). The candidate pool is built first: every drug whose `reference_drug_id` names the plan's product, whose own `status` is `ACTIVE`, and for which a coverage policy exists on this patient's payer with `coverage_tier` of `PREFERRED`. Candidates are ranked by that policy's `drug_allowed_cents_per_billing_unit` ascending — cheapest contracted allowable first — with the candidate drug's own `created_at` ascending breaking any tie; the drug's identifier is never used as a tie-breaker. The single best candidate, or nothing when the pool is empty, is then fed through the decision cascade, and exactly one of its five arms fires, tested in this order.

1. When `medical_necessity_override` is true, the plan's product is kept: `effective_drug_id` is the plan's `drug_id` and `substituted_from_drug_id` is null. A clinician's documented medical-necessity claim overrides even a mandatory payer preference — but `override_reason` must then be present and non-empty, or the request is rejected with `ERR_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); non-empty here means non-empty once surrounding whitespace is trimmed, so a string of blanks fails exactly as an empty one does. `override_reason` carries no declared length bound of its own at §2.8 step 5, and both it and `medical_necessity_override` are stored on the encounter exactly as supplied whichever arm fires — `override_reason` remaining null when it was omitted, even on an encounter whose override flag is false.
2. Otherwise, when the plan's product is itself a biosimilar (its `is_biosimilar` is true) or the candidate pool came back empty, the plan's product is kept and `substituted_from_drug_id` stays null. Biosimilars are not substituted for other biosimilars.
3. Otherwise, when the payer's `biosimilar_preference` is `MANDATORY`, the chosen candidate becomes `effective_drug_id` and the displaced plan product is recorded in `substituted_from_drug_id`.
4. Otherwise, when the payer's `biosimilar_preference` is `PREFERRED` **and** the coverage policy governing the plan's own product carries `coverage_tier` `NON_PREFERRED`, the same swap happens — the payer prefers biosimilars and has tiered the reference product down, so the candidate wins.
5. Otherwise the plan's product is kept with `substituted_from_drug_id` null. In particular, a payer whose `biosimilar_preference` is `PREFERRED` while the plan product's own tier is `PREFERRED` triggers no swap, and a payer whose preference is `NONE` never triggers one.

**Safety and capacity gates.** The gap since the plan's previous injection is checked against the *effective* product's floor: when the plan already has a non-`VOIDED` encounter, the whole days between that encounter's `encounter_datetime` and the requested one — truncated downward — must be at least the effective drug's `min_interval_days`, with the boundary inclusive so a gap exactly equal to the minimum is allowed. The reference encounter for this gate is drawn from every encounter of the plan whose status is not `VOIDED` — a still-`DRAFT` booking that never delivered a dose included — and is the one carrying the greatest `encounter_datetime`, with the greatest `created_at` breaking a tie; it is chosen without regard to the requested instant, so a booking back-dated ahead of that reference produces a negative whole-day count and fails this gate. That derivation is independent of the plan's stored `last_encounter_id`, which is what the two lineage fields described below are taken from, so the two may name different encounters. A shorter gap is `ERR_INJECTION_INTERVAL_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Separately, the acting clinician's daily volume is capped. The server takes a twenty-four-hour window that is **closed at both ends** and measured from the requested `encounter_datetime` rather than from the logical clock: it runs from that instant moved back twenty-four hours through that instant itself. Within it the server counts every non-`VOIDED` encounter belonging to the acting clinician across all patients and all plans, and requires that count to be strictly fewer than the clinician's `max_injections_per_day`. An existing encounter sitting exactly twenty-four hours before the requested instant is *inside* the window and consumes capacity; one at twenty-four hours and one second before it is outside. Exceeding the cap is `ERR_CLINICIAN_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

**Coverage and authorization.** With the effective product known, the coverage policy for the patient's payer paired with that product must exist — its absence is `ERR_PAYER_POLICY_MISSING` (HTTP 409) — and its `coverage_tier` must not be `EXCLUDED`, an exclusion being `ERR_DRUG_EXCLUDED_BY_PAYER` (HTTP 422). When that policy's `pa_required` is true, the plan must hold at least one authorization whose status is `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED`; holding none is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (HTTP 409). When `pa_required` is false, no authorization is consulted and the encounter's `prior_authorization_id` stays null.

Selecting *which* authorization covers the encounter is deliberate. Among the plan's authorizations whose status is `APPROVED` or `EXHAUSTED`, the server first looks for those whose closed coverage window — `effective_date` through `expiration_date`, both endpoints included — contains the logical clock, and ranks those by `effective_date` descending with `created_at` descending breaking ties, taking the first: the most recently effective covering authorization wins. If no authorization's window contains the clock, the server instead takes the one with the greatest `expiration_date`, with `created_at` descending breaking a tie, flips that authorization to `EXPIRED`, persists the flip, and rejects the encounter with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). A successfully selected authorization must then survive two more checks: its `drug_id` must equal the encounter's `effective_drug_id` — a mandatory biosimilar substitution routinely invalidates an authorization written for the reference product, and the mismatch is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_DRUG_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) — and its `injections_used` must be strictly below its `approved_injections`, a full authorization being `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). On success the authorization's identifier is written to `prior_authorization_id`, its `injections_used` is bumped, and it flips `APPROVED → EXHAUSTED` when that bump reaches `approved_injections`.

**Reserving the vial.** Last of all, and only once every preceding gate has passed, the server picks the physical vial. The eligible pool is every vial whose own status is `IN_STOCK`, whose lot's status is `AVAILABLE`, whose lot's `drug_id` is the encounter's `effective_drug_id`, and whose lot's `expiration_date` is strictly after the logical clock — a lot expiring at exactly the clock instant is excluded. The pool is ranked first-expiring-first-out, by the lot's `expiration_date` ascending; ties are broken by the lot's `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` **descending**, burning the dearest capital first; and any remaining tie is broken by the vial's `created_at` ascending, never by the vial's identifier. The winning vial's identifier is written to `vial_id`, the vial moves `IN_STOCK → RESERVED` with `reserved_for_encounter_id` naming this encounter and `reserved_at` set to the logical clock, and the lot's `vials_on_hand` and `vials_reserved` are recomputed. An empty pool is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VIAL` (HTTP 409); no vial is ever reserved by a request that goes on to fail.

Two lineage fields close out the creation. `previous_encounter_id` is set to whatever the plan's `last_encounter_id` was at the moment of creation, and is null for a plan's first encounter — this is the self-reference that lets the practice walk an eye's injection history. `interval_days_from_previous` records the whole days between that previous encounter's `encounter_datetime` and this one's, truncated toward zero and signed — a booking dated earlier than the encounter the pointer names records a negative count rather than being rejected — and is likewise null for a first encounter. Only once both lineage fields have been captured from the pre-creation pointer does the plan's `last_encounter_id` move: it is re-derived under the §3.3 invariant over the plan's non-`VOIDED` encounters, the new `DRAFT` encounter now among them, so a booking dated later than every surviving encounter takes the pointer while a back-dated one leaves it where it stood. Nothing else on the plan's schedule moves at creation — `current_interval_days`, `next_due_date`, `injections_administered` and `consecutive_quiescent_visits` are untouched until the encounter is documented. The plan's `version` advances by 1. While the encounter remains in `DRAFT`, `administered_dose_ml`, `wasted_ml`, `administered_billing_units`, `wasted_billing_units`, `total_billed_billing_units`, `modifiers`, `injection_site_prep_confirmed`, `post_injection_iop_mmhg`, `attested_by_clinician_id` and `void_reason` are all null. Encounters may be listed filtered on `treatment_plan_id`, `patient_id`, `clinician_id`, `imaging_study_id`, `vial_id`, `prior_authorization_id`, `previous_encounter_id`, `effective_drug_id`, `substituted_from_drug_id`, `attested_by_clinician_id`, `status` and `eye`.

### 3.7. Documenting the Injection, Attesting Wastage, and Voiding

Documentation is what turns a booking into a clinical fact, and it moves the encounter `DRAFT → DOCUMENTED`. Only the injecting clinician may do it: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the encounter's `clinician_id`, and a different specialist attempting it is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), as is any attempt to document an encounter that is not in `DRAFT` — documenting twice is impossible. The request carries `administered_dose_ml`, the volume actually injected; `injection_site_prep_confirmed`, the aseptic-prep attestation, which must be true or the request is `ERR_PREP_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); and `post_injection_iop_mmhg`, the intraocular pressure measured after the injection, a required integer accepted only from 0 through 80 inclusive so that a reading outside that range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at §2.8 step 5. All three fields are required. `administered_dose_ml` by contrast is declared with neither a numeric bound nor a decimal-place limit at step 5 — a fourth decimal digit is accepted rather than rejected, and a zero or negative volume passes step 5 to be caught by the physical-possibility check below, so such a dose reports `ERR_DOSE_EXCEEDS_VIAL_FILL` rather than a malformed request. The reserved vial's lot must still be `AVAILABLE` at this moment — a lot quarantined or recalled in the window between reservation and documentation blocks the injection with `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). And the dose must be physically possible: strictly greater than zero and no greater than the effective drug's `vial_fill_ml`, either violation being `ERR_DOSE_EXCEEDS_VIAL_FILL` (HTTP 422).

**Splitting the vial into billing units.** The single most error-prone arithmetic in this system happens here, and its rounding order is pinned. Wastage volume comes first: for a `SINGLE_DOSE` product, `wasted_ml` is the effective drug's `vial_fill_ml` less `administered_dose_ml`, rounded half up to three decimal places; for a `MULTI_DOSE_COMPOUNDED` product, `wasted_ml` is exactly 0, because compounded multi-dose stock produces no billable discard. Each volume is then converted to milligrams by multiplying by the drug's `mg_per_ml` — the administered volume giving the administered milligrams, the wasted volume giving the wasted milligrams. Each of those two milligram figures is then divided by the drug's `billing_unit_mg` and rounded half up to a whole number **independently**, producing `administered_billing_units` and `wasted_billing_units`. Only after both lines have been rounded are they added together to give `total_billed_billing_units`.

That order is not interchangeable with summing the milligrams and rounding once, and the difference is real money. Consider a product supplied at 0.27 ml per vial with a concentration of 40 mg per ml and a billing unit of 1 mg, from which 0.06 ml is injected. The administered milligrams are 2.4, which rounds to 2 units; the wasted volume is 0.21 ml, or 8.4 mg, which rounds to 8 units; the total billed is therefore 10 units. Summing first would give 10.8 mg and round to 11 units — that answer is wrong and is rejected.

**Assembling the modifiers.** The encounter's `modifiers` array is built in a pinned order. Position zero is always laterality: `RT` when the encounter's `eye` is `OD`, `LT` when it is `OS`. For a `SINGLE_DOSE` effective product a wastage modifier is appended second — `JW` when `wasted_billing_units` is greater than zero, and `JZ` when it is exactly zero, `JZ` being the attestation that nothing was discarded. A `MULTI_DOSE_COMPOUNDED` product carries neither `JW` nor `JZ`, so its `modifiers` array holds the laterality modifier alone.

Documentation's side effects reach three entities. The encounter itself stores the three supplied inputs — `administered_dose_ml` as the volume given, `injection_site_prep_confirmed` (necessarily `true`, since no other value survives the gate above) and `post_injection_iop_mmhg` — gains `wasted_ml`, `administered_billing_units`, `wasted_billing_units`, `total_billed_billing_units` and `modifiers`, and moves to `DOCUMENTED`. The reserved vial moves `RESERVED → ADMINISTERED` and has `administered_at` stamped with the encounter's `encounter_datetime` — deliberately the clinical instant, not the logical clock, so that the trailing-burn-rate window of §3.5 reflects when injections actually happened. Its `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `reserved_at` are deliberately **not** cleared on this hop: only the `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` compensating edge of §3.5 clears them, so an administered vial goes on naming the encounter that consumed it. The vial's lot has `vials_reserved` and `vials_administered` recomputed. And the plan is advanced through the full treat-and-extend recomputation of §3.3: `injections_administered`, `consecutive_quiescent_visits`, `current_interval_days`, `next_due_date`, `last_encounter_id`, possibly `status`, and `version`.

**Second-clinician wastage attestation.** Because a discarded portion of a high-cost biologic is billable revenue, it must be witnessed. Attestation moves the encounter `DOCUMENTED → ATTESTED` and carries a single input, `attested_wasted_ml`, a required number that must be at least 0 — a negative volume is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at §2.8 step 5 rather than a wastage mismatch. The attester must be a different clinician from the injector: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the encounter's `clinician_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The attester must additionally be in `ACTIVE` status; a suspended clinician cannot witness, and is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). That eligibility test is the step-14 check of §2.8 and therefore runs *after* the encounter's own state check of step 13, so a suspended attester aimed at an encounter that is not `DOCUMENTED` reports `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` rather than the eligibility failure — while the duties conflict of step 9 still outranks both. And the witnessed figure must agree with the computed one: `attested_wasted_ml` must equal the encounter's `wasted_ml` to three decimal places, a disagreement being `ERR_ATTESTED_WASTAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). On success the acting clinician's identifier is written to `attested_by_clinician_id`. Attesting anything that is not `DOCUMENTED` — a `DRAFT` encounter, or one already attested — is `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The encounter's remaining two forward moves are made by the system. `ATTESTED → BILLED` happens when a claim is created for the encounter, and nothing on the encounter changes beyond its status and `updated_at`. `BILLED → CLOSED` happens during remittance posting, once *both* of the claim's line dimensions have reached terminal values, and again nothing else on the encounter changes. `CLOSED` and `VOIDED` are the encounter's two terminal statuses.

**Voiding, and why a used vial never goes back on the shelf.** A specialist may void an encounter that is in `DRAFT` or `DOCUMENTED`, supplying a `void_reason` — a required string of at least one character, an empty one being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at §2.8 step 5. Those two are the *only* source states the void accepts: an `ATTESTED` encounter has already been witnessed and can no more be voided than a `BILLED`, a `CLOSED` or an already-`VOIDED` one, and every such attempt is `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The two source states behave differently in exactly one respect, and it is the respect that matters most.

Voiding from `DRAFT` releases the reservation. The vial moves `RESERVED → IN_STOCK`, both `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `reserved_at` are cleared to null, and the lot's counters are recomputed. That release acts only on a vial that is still `RESERVED`: where another path has already moved it out of that state — a recall having quarantined it while deliberately leaving its reservation pointer in place — the release is a silent no-op and the vial stays exactly where that path left it, pointer included. Because the encounter never consumed a dose, the plan's dose counters were never bumped and need no adjustment, and neither `current_interval_days` nor `next_due_date` is recomputed; the plan's `last_encounter_id` is nevertheless re-derived over its surviving non-`VOIDED` encounters, and the plan's `updated_at` and `version` move only when that re-derivation actually changes the pointer — voiding a `DRAFT` encounter that was not the plan's newest therefore leaves the plan's row entirely alone. The authorization's `injections_used` is recomputed either way — as the count of that authorization's non-`VOIDED` encounters — and an `EXHAUSTED` authorization that regains capacity flips back to `APPROVED`, but only while its own coverage window is still open: the revival additionally requires the logical clock to be at or before the authorization's `expiration_date`, so freed capacity on an authorization whose window has already lapsed leaves it sitting in `EXHAUSTED`. The authorization's `updated_at` and `version` move only when that recomputation actually changes its `injections_used` or its status. This is also the path taken by cascades: a plan suspension voids the plan's `DRAFT` encounters with `void_reason` recorded as `PLAN_SUSPENDED`, a plan discontinuation does the same but records `PLAN_DISCONTINUED` instead, and a lot recall voids the `DRAFT` encounters holding vials from the recalled lot with `void_reason` recorded as `LOT_RECALLED` — with the one difference that the recall has already moved those vials out of `RESERVED` and into `QUARANTINED` before the encounters are voided, so on that path no reservation is released and the vials' reservation fields are left as they stood (§3.9).

Voiding from `DOCUMENTED` is permitted only while no claim exists for the encounter; once billed, the void is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Here the vial has already been injected, and pretending otherwise would break the conservation invariant of §2.9, so the vial is **not** returned to stock: it moves `ADMINISTERED → QUARANTINED`, preserving the lot's total while removing the vial from usable inventory, and the lot's `vials_administered` and `vials_quarantined` are recomputed. That quarantine leaves `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `reserved_at` still naming the voided encounter, exactly as documentation left them, so §3.5's lot release — which returns to stock only those quarantined vials whose reservation pointer is null — can never put this vial back on the shelf. The plan is then unwound: `injections_administered` is decremented, and `last_encounter_id`, `current_interval_days`, `consecutive_quiescent_visits` and `next_due_date` are all re-derived from the plan's surviving non-`VOIDED` encounters taken in ascending `encounter_datetime` order with ascending `created_at` breaking ties — that is, the schedule ends up where it would have been had the voided injection never been documented. The re-derivation is a replay from the plan's activation baseline: it starts at the `current_interval_days` of 28 and the `next_due_date` that activation wrote, and a quiescent run of 0, then walks each surviving counted encounter through the same branch arithmetic of §3.3, taking `next_due_date` from the last of them. Because the baseline is activation's and not the latest resumption's, voiding the only documented encounter of a plan returns `current_interval_days` to 28 and `next_due_date` to exactly the instant activation computed, rather than leaving either null. A plan whose status carries no due date at all — `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `DISCONTINUED` — still ends the replay with `next_due_date` null. The plan's own `status` is left where it stood. Finally the authorization's `injections_used` is recomputed with the same `EXHAUSTED → APPROVED` revival. The voided encounter keeps every figure it had computed, including its `modifiers` and its billing units; the record is annulled, not erased.

### 3.8. Revenue Cycle: Claims, Assistance Coordination, Remittance and Margin

Billing is where the buy-and-bill wager is settled, and every step of it belongs to the `BillingSpecialist`. A claim is created from a single input, `injection_encounter_id`, and the encounter must be `ATTESTED` — an unattested encounter cannot be billed, and the attempt is `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). At most one claim may exist per encounter; a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (HTTP 409) — though because creating the first claim carries the encounter `ATTESTED → BILLED`, and the encounter-state check of §2.8 step 13 is evaluated ahead of the duplicate check of step 37, a second create aimed at the same encounter reports `ERR_ENCOUNTER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) in practice. The claim copies `patient_id` from the encounter and `payer_id` from that patient as the payer stood at claim creation, so a later payer change on the patient record does not retroactively reprice a claim already cut. It copies `hcpcs_code` from the encounter's effective drug, `modifiers` from the encounter's `modifiers` array, and `billed_billing_units` from the encounter's `total_billed_billing_units`, and it sets `administration_cpt_code` to the constant `67028`, the intravitreal-injection administration code. Both status axes are born `PENDING`.

**The three charge lines.** Pricing reads the coverage policy for the claim's payer paired with the encounter's effective drug. `drug_billed_cents` is the encounter's `administered_billing_units` multiplied by the policy's `drug_allowed_cents_per_billing_unit`. `wastage_billed_cents` is the encounter's `wasted_billing_units` multiplied by that same rate — the discard is *always* billed, whether or not this payer reimburses it. `admin_billed_cents` is the policy's `admin_allowed_cents`. From those, the expected allowables at creation are: `drug_allowed_cents` equal to `drug_billed_cents`; `wastage_allowed_cents` equal to `wastage_billed_cents` when the policy's `wastage_reimbursed` is true and exactly 0 when it is false; and `admin_allowed_cents` equal to `admin_billed_cents`. Each of the three `_paid_cents` fields — `drug_paid_cents`, `wastage_paid_cents` and `admin_paid_cents` — is 0 until a remittance posts.

**The patient-responsibility waterfall.** The waterfall runs twice over a claim's life: a provisional pass at creation and a final pass at each remittance posting. Its evaluation order is normative, each value being read exactly once and never fed back into an earlier step.

1. `total_allowed_cents` is `drug_allowed_cents` plus `wastage_allowed_cents` plus `admin_allowed_cents`.
2. `coinsurance_cents` is `total_allowed_cents` multiplied by the payer's `coinsurance_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents.
3. The enrollment is selected. The eligible pool is every assistance enrollment whose `patient_id` is the claim's patient, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose closed benefit window `effective_date` through `expiration_date` contains the logical clock, and which is not the forbidden pairing of a `MANUFACTURER_COPAY_CARD` `program_type` with a payer whose `federal_program` is true — federal beneficiaries may not accept manufacturer copay support. The pool is ranked by remaining benefit, that is `annual_benefit_cents` less `benefit_used_cents`, descending; then by `per_encounter_cap_cents` descending; then by `created_at` ascending, never by the enrollment's identifier. The first entry becomes the claim's `assistance_enrollment_id`, and an empty pool leaves that field null.
4. The per-encounter cap is keyed on the coinsurance computed in step 2, not on the allowed total. With no enrollment the cap is 0. When `coinsurance_cents` is greater than or equal to 50000 cents, the high tier applies and the cap is the enrollment's `per_encounter_cap_cents` in full. Below that threshold the cap is the enrollment's `per_encounter_cap_cents` multiplied by 0.5 and rounded half up to whole cents — small balances draw only half the per-encounter allowance, reserving the programme's money for the punishing ones.
5. `foundation_applied_cents` is the smallest of three quantities: `coinsurance_cents`, the per-encounter cap from step 4, and the remaining benefit, which is the enrollment's `annual_benefit_cents` less its `benefit_used_cents` and is 0 when no enrollment was selected. That remaining benefit is read *exclusive* of this claim's own earlier contribution, which the final pass reverses out before re-reading it.
6. The pre-relief patient balance is `coinsurance_cents` less `foundation_applied_cents`.
7. Small-balance relief and the payer shortfall are then settled. When that pre-relief balance is less than or equal to 1000 cents, the whole of it becomes a small-balance write-off and `patient_responsibility_cents` becomes 0; above that threshold the write-off is 0 and `patient_responsibility_cents` is the full pre-relief balance — the practice does not chase a ten-dollar bill. The payer shortfall is `total_allowed_cents` less the sum of `drug_paid_cents`, `wastage_paid_cents` and `admin_paid_cents`, less `coinsurance_cents`, floored at 0 so a payer who overpays never produces a negative shortfall. `practice_writeoff_cents` is the small-balance write-off plus that payer shortfall.
8. The ledger is then posted: the selected enrollment's `benefit_used_cents` increases by `foundation_applied_cents`, and the enrollment flips `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED` when its `benefit_used_cents` reaches or passes its `annual_benefit_cents`, after which no further foundation dollars are applied. The enrollment row's own `updated_at` and `version` move on this posting only when `foundation_applied_cents` is nonzero, so a pass that selects an enrollment yet applies nothing to it — a claim whose coinsurance is 0, or one facing an enrollment with no remaining benefit — records the selection on the claim while leaving the enrollment's version exactly where it stood.

On the provisional pass the allowed amounts are the policy-derived expectations and the payer is assumed to pay exactly its share, so the paid total is treated as `total_allowed_cents` less `coinsurance_cents` and the payer shortfall is therefore 0. On the final pass the allowed and paid amounts come from the remittance posting instead, and a re-run first reverses this claim's previous `foundation_applied_cents` out of the enrollment's `benefit_used_cents` before re-applying the freshly computed figure. That reversal is aimed at whichever enrollment the claim's own `assistance_enrollment_id` recorded on the previous pass, and it fires only when that previous `foundation_applied_cents` was nonzero; the selection of step 3 then runs afresh over the eligible pool and may land on a different enrollment altogether, in which case the reversal has credited one enrollment while the new application debits another. That reversal is what makes the `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` revival possible: when the re-run lowers `benefit_used_cents` back below `annual_benefit_cents`, the enrollment returns to `ACTIVE`. One further lazy edge fires at claim creation: an enrollment in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` whose `expiration_date` is earlier than the logical clock is flipped to `EXPIRED` at that moment, and the claim's waterfall then proceeds with `foundation_applied_cents` of 0.

**Provisional margin.** Also at creation, `standard_drug_cost_cents` is written as the encounter's `administered_billing_units` plus its `wasted_billing_units`, multiplied by the effective drug's `asp_cents_per_billing_unit` — the published-benchmark cost of the whole vial's worth of billed units. `provisional_margin_cents` is then the sum of `drug_allowed_cents`, `wastage_allowed_cents` and `admin_allowed_cents`, less `standard_drug_cost_cents`. Both `actual_acquisition_cost_cents` and `realized_margin_cents` remain null at this stage, and `denial_code` and `appeal_basis` are null until a denial or an appeal writes them.

**Submission.** Submitting the claim moves *both* axes `PENDING → SUBMITTED` in a single call; the administration line has no independent side effect of its own, and the two dimensions leave `PENDING` together. Submitting a claim whose lines have already left `PENDING` is `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

**Remittance.** After submission the two axes advance independently, and remittance posting is the only place either moves. The request carries any of `drug_line_outcome`, `drug_allowed_cents`, `drug_paid_cents`, `wastage_allowed_cents`, `wastage_paid_cents`, `admin_line_outcome`, `admin_allowed_cents`, `admin_paid_cents` and `denial_code`, and at least one of the two outcome fields must be supplied — a posting that names neither line is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every field on the request is optional in its own right, and each of the six `_cents` figures must be an integer of at least 0 when it is supplied. The conditional requirements the outcomes impose below — `drug_allowed_cents` and `drug_paid_cents` on a `PAID` drug line, `admin_allowed_cents` and `admin_paid_cents` on a `PAID` administration line, and `denial_code` on a `DENIED` line of either dimension — are themselves §2.8 step 5 failures carrying `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and because step 5 precedes step 6 they are reported before the path identifier is resolved: a posting that both names a nonexistent claim and omits a figure its own outcome requires reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. Remitting a line that is still `PENDING` is `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

On the drug axis, a `drug_line_outcome` of `PAID` is accepted from `SUBMITTED` or from `APPEALED`, requires `drug_allowed_cents` and `drug_paid_cents`, stores those along with `wastage_allowed_cents` and `wastage_paid_cents` — the remitted allowables replacing the expectations written at creation, and an omitted wastage figure being written as 0 rather than left standing at its creation-time expectation, so a payer that pays the drug line without mentioning the discard drives both `wastage_allowed_cents` and `wastage_paid_cents` to 0 — and re-runs the waterfall on those remitted figures. Reaching `PAID` from `APPEALED` is the compensating edge of the money-conservation invariant: the re-run unwinds the shortfall previously parked in `practice_writeoff_cents`. A `drug_line_outcome` of `DENIED` is likewise accepted from `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`, requires `denial_code` — one of `NOT_COVERED`, `AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`, `WASTAGE_NOT_PAYABLE` or `DUPLICATE_CLAIM` — sets `drug_paid_cents` and `wastage_paid_cents` to 0, stores the code, and re-runs the waterfall, which pushes the resulting payer shortfall into `practice_writeoff_cents`. A `drug_line_outcome` of `WRITTEN_OFF` is accepted only from `DENIED` or `APPEALED`; it leaves `drug_paid_cents` at 0 and keeps the shortfall in `practice_writeoff_cents`, and it writes nothing else — `wastage_paid_cents`, both drug allowables and any `denial_code` already stored stand exactly as the denial left them, and the waterfall re-runs over those unchanged figures. `PAID` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are the drug axis's two terminal values.

On the administration axis, an `admin_line_outcome` of `PAID` is accepted only from `SUBMITTED`, requires `admin_allowed_cents` and `admin_paid_cents`, and stores them; an `admin_line_outcome` of `DENIED`, also only from `SUBMITTED`, sets `admin_paid_cents` to 0 and stores `denial_code`. That write list is exhaustive: the denial branch ignores any `admin_allowed_cents` the posting happens to carry, so the administration allowable written at claim creation still stands and still feeds `total_allowed_cents` when the waterfall re-runs — in deliberate contrast to the drug axis's `PAID` branch, where the remitted allowables replace the expectations written at creation. The administration line has **no appeal edge at all**: `DENIED` is terminal on that axis even while the drug axis is still `APPEALED` and may yet be paid. That asymmetry between the two dimensions is deliberate.

**Appealing the drug line.** An appeal carries `appeal_basis` and moves the drug axis `DENIED → APPEALED`, leaving `admin_line_status` untouched. It must be filed inside the payer's appeal window: the logical clock must be no later than the claim's `updated_at` plus the payer's `appeal_window_days` days, with the boundary inclusive, and a late filing is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). Appealing a drug line that is not `DENIED` is `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

**Realized margin and closure.** Once — and only once — the drug axis has reached `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` *and* the administration axis has reached `PAID` or `DENIED`, the remittance posting writes the true economics. `actual_acquisition_cost_cents` is set to the `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` of the lot behind the encounter's administered vial: buy-and-bill charges the entire vial to cost even though only a fraction of it entered the eye. `realized_margin_cents` is then the sum of `drug_paid_cents`, `wastage_paid_cents`, `admin_paid_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents` and `foundation_applied_cents`, less `actual_acquisition_cost_cents`. `practice_writeoff_cents` is deliberately excluded from that sum, because a write-off is money the practice will never collect. The realized figure diverges from the provisional one even when every payer amount lands exactly on its expectation, because the two are costed on different bases — published ASP against the dearest-first lot actually dispensed — and comparing them is the whole point of the exercise. The same posting moves the encounter `BILLED → CLOSED`. Claims may be listed filtered on `injection_encounter_id`, `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `assistance_enrollment_id`, `drug_line_status` and `admin_line_status`, and are readable by every role except `Patient`.

**Assistance enrollments.** An enrollment is created by the `BillingSpecialist` with `patient_id`, `program_type` — one of `MANUFACTURER_COPAY_CARD`, `INDEPENDENT_FOUNDATION` or `FREE_DRUG_PAP` — `annual_benefit_cents`, `per_encounter_cap_cents`, `effective_date` and `expiration_date`, where all six are required, the two money figures must each be at least 0, and the two dates are the first and last instants the benefit covers. No ordering check couples those two dates, so an `expiration_date` earlier than its own `effective_date` is accepted at creation and simply yields a window that no logical clock can ever fall inside. It is born `PENDING` with `benefit_used_cents` of 0 and `termination_reason` null. A `program_type` of `MANUFACTURER_COPAY_CARD` is refused outright for a patient whose payer carries `federal_program` true, with `ERR_COPAY_CARD_FEDERAL_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Approval moves `PENDING → ACTIVE` and changes nothing else, but requires that the patient have no other enrollment already in `ACTIVE`; a second one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT` (HTTP 409). Termination is available from `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, stores `termination_reason`, and is terminal; benefit already applied to paid claims is never clawed back. `EXPIRED` is the other terminal value. Approving anything that is not `PENDING`, or terminating something already `TERMINATED` or `EXPIRED`, is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Enrollments may be listed filtered on `patient_id`, `program_type` and `status`.

### 3.9. Adverse-Event Surveillance, Lot Clusters and Recall Management

**Filing a report.** A `RetinaSpecialist` files an adverse event against an encounter, supplying `injection_encounter_id`, `event_type` — one of `ENDOPHTHALMITIS`, `IOP_SPIKE`, `RETINAL_DETACHMENT` or `INTRAOCULAR_INFLAMMATION` — `onset_datetime` as the ISO 8601 UTC instant symptoms began, `severity` as `MILD`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, and optionally `culture_result`, which defaults to `PENDING` and may otherwise be `POSITIVE` or `NEGATIVE`. The named encounter is accepted whatever its own status: no state gate guards this create, so a report may be filed against a booking still in `DRAFT`, or against one a cascade has already `VOIDED`, exactly as against a `CLOSED` one. The server stamps `reported_by_clinician_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, copies `patient_id` from the encounter, and resolves `source_drug_lot_id` by following the vial that the encounter's `vial_id` names through to that vial's lot — whatever custody state the vial now stands in, so a reservation that was never injected still yields a source lot — the join that lets surveillance reason about product rather than about patients. `days_post_injection` is written as the whole days from the encounter's `encounter_datetime` to `onset_datetime`, truncated toward zero and carrying its sign, so an `onset_datetime` earlier than the injection records a negative count rather than being rejected or floored at 0. The event is born `REPORTED`, with `adjudicated_by_clinician_id` and `adjudication_note` null.

**Reportability.** The same calculation runs at filing and again at adjudication. An event is `reportable` when any one of three conditions holds: its `event_type` is `ENDOPHTHALMITIS` and its `days_post_injection` is no more than thirty — a ceiling with no matching floor, so a negative day count satisfies it too; or its `severity` is `SEVERE`; or its `culture_result` is `POSITIVE`. When reportable, `fda_report_due_date` is `onset_datetime` plus fifteen days for a `SEVERE` event and plus thirty days for any other severity — the deadline runs from symptom onset, not from the filing instant, so a late-filed severe event can arrive with its clock already largely spent. When not reportable, `fda_report_due_date` is null and `reportable` is false. Because adjudication recomputes both fields, a culture that turns `POSITIVE` between filing and adjudication converts a non-reportable event into a reportable one with a deadline still anchored on the original onset.

**Investigation and the divergent adjudication branches.** A `ComplianceOfficer` opens the investigation, moving `REPORTED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and changing nothing but `status` and `updated_at`. Adjudication is then a `RetinaSpecialist` action carrying `outcome` and `adjudication_note`, permitted only from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; anything else is `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The adjudicator may not be the clinician who gave the injection: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the source encounter's `clinician_id` is refused with `ERR_ADJUDICATOR_IS_INJECTOR` (HTTP 403). Both branches set `adjudicated_by_clinician_id` and `adjudication_note` and recompute `reportable` and `fda_report_due_date`. Beyond that they diverge completely, and the divergence is the point.

An `outcome` of `UNRELATED` moves the event to `ADJUDICATED_UNRELATED` and fires **nothing else**: the treatment plan is left exactly as it was, no vial is released, no authorization is recomputed, and the lot's `related_endophthalmitis_count` is not touched.

An `outcome` of `RELATED` moves the event to `ADJUDICATED_RELATED` and fires the system's deepest cascade, four hops of it. Hop two: because the event became `ADJUDICATED_RELATED`, the source encounter's treatment plan moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` recorded as `ADVERSE_EVENT` and `next_due_date` cleared to null. That hop is conditioned on the plan's current status. It fires only for a plan that is treatable — `LOADING`, `MAINTENANCE` or `INTENSIFIED`. A plan already sitting in `SUSPENDED` is *not* re-suspended: its `suspension_reason`, its `next_due_date` and its `version` are left exactly as they stood, and the cascade drops straight into hop three over that plan's `DRAFT` encounters. A plan in `DRAFT` or `DISCONTINUED` receives no plan hop at all, so the adjudication touches only the event itself and, conditionally, the lot. Hop three: because that plan became `SUSPENDED`, every vial reserved for one of the plan's `DRAFT` encounters returns to `IN_STOCK` and those encounters move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` recorded as `PLAN_SUSPENDED`. Hop four: because those encounters became `VOIDED`, the plan's authorizations have `injections_used` recomputed and any that stood `EXHAUSTED` returns to `APPROVED`. A fifth, conditional hop fires only when the event's `event_type` is `ENDOPHTHALMITIS` and the event carries a non-null `source_drug_lot_id`: the source lot's `related_endophthalmitis_count` is *recomputed* rather than incremented — it is the number of adverse events naming that lot as their `source_drug_lot_id` whose `event_type` is `ENDOPHTHALMITIS` and whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED_RELATED`, and the lot's `updated_at` and `version` move only when that recomputation changes the stored figure — and if the recomputed count reaches two **and** the lot is still `AVAILABLE`, the lot moves `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` recorded as `ENDOPHTHALMITIS_CLUSTER`, which in turn moves all of that lot's `IN_STOCK` vials to `QUARANTINED` and has the lot's vial counters recomputed. A lot already `QUARANTINED`, `RECALLED` or `EXPIRED` takes the recomputed count and nothing further; `RESERVED` vials are left alone on this path exactly as a plain quarantine leaves them. Two related endophthalmitis cases traced to one lot are treated as a contamination signal and the remaining stock is frozen without any human deciding to freeze it. Adverse events may be listed filtered on `injection_encounter_id`, `patient_id`, `reported_by_clinician_id`, `adjudicated_by_clinician_id`, `source_drug_lot_id`, `event_type`, `severity` and `status`.

**Initiating a recall.** A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a recall with `drug_lot_id`, `recall_class` — `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III` — and a free-text `reason`. At most one non-`CLOSED` recall may exist per lot; a second is `ERR_RECALL_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — but the lot-state check below is the earlier rule under §2.8 (step 13 preceding step 40), so a repeat attempt on a lot that the first recall already moved to `RECALLED` reports `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` instead. The recall is born `INITIATED`, `initiated_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `notification_deadline_date` is computed as the logical clock plus the class notice period: three days for `CLASS_I`, ten for `CLASS_II`, thirty for `CLASS_III`. Initiation also drives the lot itself. The lot must be in `AVAILABLE` or `QUARANTINED` — a lot already `RECALLED` or `EXPIRED` cannot be recalled again, and the attempt is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — and from either of those two source states the effect is identical: the lot moves to `RECALLED`, which is terminal; both its `IN_STOCK` and its `RESERVED` vials move to `QUARANTINED`, and a vial quarantined out of `RESERVED` keeps its `reserved_for_encounter_id` and `reserved_at` exactly as they stood — the recall does not clear them, which is precisely what §3.5 relies on to hold such a vial back when the lot is later released; any `DRAFT` encounter holding one of those reserved vials is `VOIDED` with `void_reason` recorded as `LOT_RECALLED` — the vial is already `QUARANTINED` by then, so that void releases nothing and clears nothing — and for each plan that lost a `DRAFT` encounter this way, `injections_used` is recomputed on the authorization behind it with the usual `EXHAUSTED → APPROVED` revival and the plan's `last_encounter_id` is re-derived under the §3.3 invariant, the plan's `version` advancing only when that pointer actually moves; the notification rows described below are generated; and the lot's `version` advances by 1.

**Generating the notification set.** The exposed population is every injection encounter whose administered vial belongs to the recalled lot and whose `status` is `DOCUMENTED`, `ATTESTED`, `BILLED` or `CLOSED`. `DRAFT` and `VOIDED` encounters generate no notification even when they held a vial from the lot, because no drug reached the patient. The exposed encounters are taken in ascending `encounter_datetime` order, with ascending `created_at` breaking ties and the encounter's identifier never used as a tie-breaker, and one notification is created for each, in that order. Each notification is born `PENDING` with `lot_recall_id` naming the recall, `patient_id` and `injection_encounter_id` copied from the exposing encounter, `channel` copied from that patient's `notification_channel` as it stood at generation time, and `sent_at`, `acknowledged_at` and `attempt_note` all null. The recall's `notifications_required` is set to the size of that exposed set, while `notifications_acknowledged`, `notifications_unreachable` and `vials_returned_count` all start at 0, `credit_expected_cents` starts at 0, and `credit_received_cents`, `carrier_reference`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closure_note` all start null.

**The five-step recall ladder.** The recall's steps must be walked in order, and any attempt to take one out of turn is `ERR_RECALL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); each step also belongs to a different role, so no single person can walk the whole ladder. Every free-text input named on this ladder and at initiation — the recall's own `reason`, the return shipment's `carrier_reference`, the closure's `closure_note` and a notification's `attempt_note` — is a required string of at least one character, and `credit_received_cents` is a required integer of at least 0; a violation of any of them is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at §2.8 step 5, reported ahead of the recall's own state check.

The `ComplianceOfficer` sends notifications, moving the recall `INITIATED → PATIENTS_NOTIFIED` and every `PENDING` notification of that recall to `SENT` with `sent_at` set to the logical clock. The `PharmacyTech` then ships the return with a `carrier_reference`, moving the recall `PATIENTS_NOTIFIED → RETURN_SHIPPED`; the lot must have at least one `QUARANTINED` vial or the step is refused with `ERR_NO_QUARANTINED_VIALS` (HTTP 409). Shipping moves every `QUARANTINED` vial of the lot to `RETURNED`, recomputes `vials_returned_count`, and **freezes** `credit_expected_cents` at `vials_returned_count` multiplied by the lot's `unit_acquisition_cost_cents` — frozen at this instant, so nothing that happens later re-prices the expected credit. The `BillingSpecialist` then reconciles the manufacturer credit with `credit_received_cents`, moving the recall `RETURN_SHIPPED → CREDIT_RECONCILED`; the figure may not exceed `credit_expected_cents`, and a larger one is `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED` (HTTP 422). A short credit is accepted, and the difference is simply a loss the practice absorbs.

Closure is a `ComplianceOfficer` action carrying `closure_note`, moving the recall `CREDIT_RECONCILED → CLOSED`, which is terminal. Two conditions guard it. Every notification must be resolved: `notifications_acknowledged` plus `notifications_unreachable` must equal `notifications_required` exactly, and any shortfall is `ERR_PATIENT_NOTIFICATIONS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409) — a recall cannot be closed while a single exposed patient is still merely `SENT`. And the closer must not be the initiator: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to `initiated_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_RECALL_CLOSER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403). On success `closure_note` is stored and `closed_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting identity. Recalls may be listed filtered on `drug_lot_id`, `recall_class` and `status`.

**Resolving a notification.** A notification has exactly two terminal outcomes, both reachable only from `SENT`. The patient acknowledges: this is the sole endpoint the `Patient` role may write, `acknowledged_at` is set to the logical clock, and the parent recall's `notifications_acknowledged` rises by one. Only the owning patient may do it — an acting `Patient` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the notification's `patient_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_NOTIFICATION_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Alternatively the `ComplianceOfficer` marks the patient unreachable, supplying an `attempt_note` that records the outreach attempted; the notification moves to `UNREACHABLE` and the parent recall's `notifications_unreachable` rises by one. Neither rise is an increment, though: either resolution recomputes *both* of the parent recall's tallies from that recall's notification rows, and advances the recall's own `updated_at` and `version` by 1 even though the response body carries the notification rather than the recall. Acknowledging a notification that is still `PENDING`, or acting on one that has already reached `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `UNREACHABLE`, is `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Notifications may be read and listed by every role including `Patient`, filtered on `lot_recall_id`, `patient_id`, `injection_encounter_id` and `status`. For an acting `Patient` the collection listing is narrowed to that patient's own rows whatever filters accompany it: sending no `patient_id` still returns only their own notifications, while sending a `patient_id` that names anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_NOTIFICATION_OWNER` (HTTP 403) rather than answered with an empty page — the same 403 that a single-notification read of another patient's row draws. Every other authorised role sees the collection unnarrowed.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint is mounted directly at the API root, and every one requires the `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers described in §2.1. Endpoints that create a new resource — a payer, a drug, a coverage policy, a patient, a clinician, an imaging study, a treatment plan, a drug lot, a prior authorization, an injection encounter, a claim, an assistance enrollment, an adverse event or a lot recall — return HTTP 201 on success, with the created resource in the `data` field. Every other successful call returns HTTP 200: all retrievals and listings, every state-transition action, the inventory sweep, the lot exposure computation, and the logical-clock setter. State-transition actions return the full updated resource in `data`, so a caller never needs a follow-up read to observe the counters a cascade has just rewritten. Listing endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` in addition to the filters named below, and their `meta` block carries the four pagination fields of §2.3. A path parameter written as `{id}` is the resource's own `id`. Where an endpoint is listed for several roles, any one of them may call it; a known role that is not listed receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
