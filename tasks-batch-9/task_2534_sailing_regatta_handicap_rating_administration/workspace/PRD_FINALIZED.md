# Sailing Regatta Handicap Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A yacht club racing committee runs a handicap regatta on paperwork that has to be exactly right before a single boat leaves the dock. A boat's measurement file becomes a rating certificate; the certificate's rating decides which division the boat sails in; the division decides whether there is a fleet at all; the fleet decides whether a race can start; and the finishing times, once corrected by those same ratings, decide a series result that a protest hearing can legitimately overturn days later. Today this chain lives in spreadsheets, printed notices pinned to a clubhouse board, and the memory of whichever volunteer happens to be on the committee boat. When a measurer discovers an irregularity in a hull measurement after entries have closed, nobody can say with confidence which entries are now invalid, which divisions have dropped below their fleet minimum, or which races must not be started.

This document specifies the Sailing Regatta & Handicap Rating Administration API, the system of record for the whole chain. It covers measurement filing and certificate issuance under two competing rating rules, division construction by rating band and hull family with fleet minimums, publication and amendment of the notice of race and the sailing instructions, crew registration and equipment inspection, race committee volunteer staffing with mark boat logistics, start sequences with individual and general recall procedures including the black flag escalation, corrected-time scoring with two series scoring systems and three discard policies, and protest hearings with jury composition, evidence, penalties, redress and a single permitted rehearing.

Two properties dominate the design. The first is that every consequence of an administrative act is realised inside the request that caused it: suspending a certificate does not enqueue work for a nightly job, it invalidates the affected entries, re-derives the affected division counts, and blocks the affected race starts before the response is written. The second is that arithmetic is reproducible to the digit — two officials computing a corrected time or a series total from the same data must reach the same number, so rounding points and evaluation order are part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Every route described here is mounted under the `/regatta-ops` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows them. Where this section fixes a name, a format, a bound or an ordering, that fixture is contractual.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting person or operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. Both must be present, and `X-User-Role` must spell one of exactly six declared roles — `ClubAdmin`, `RegattaChair`, `Measurer`, `RaceOfficer`, `JuryChair`, `Competitor`. A request missing either header, or naming a role outside that set of six, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is not listed against the endpoint it calls is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Section 4 is the authoritative role list per endpoint.

`ClubAdmin` is the club office operator: it maintains the member roster and member credentials and drives the logical clock in test environments, and it holds no racing authority whatsoever. When `X-User-Role` is `ClubAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server neither resolves it to a `ClubMember` row nor compares it to any entity's `id`.

The other five roles are member-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `RegattaChair`, `Measurer`, `RaceOfficer`, `JuryChair` or `Competitor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ClubMember`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `ClubMember` compare the resource's member-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. A member-backed `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing `ClubMember` row is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR`; the sole exception is the member-enrolment endpoint itself, which is reached before any member exists and is therefore exempt from that check. Concretely:

- A `RegattaChair` publishes documents, shapes divisions, admits entries, forms panels and staffs the committee. Its identity is recorded where a transition captures an actor, and it is compared to `chair_member_id` only when it also acts as a jury chair.
- A `Measurer` files measurement data and issues, suspends and reinstates certificates. Its `X-User-ID` is written into `measured_by_member_id` on every certificate it creates, and the issue action additionally requires that `X-User-ID` equal the `measured_by_member_id` already stored on the certificate being issued. A `Measurer` whose `ClubMember` row has a null `measurer_license_id` is refused every certificate operation with `ERR_MEASURER_NOT_LICENSED`.
- A `RaceOfficer` inspects equipment, runs starts, recalls, abandons and scores races and records results. Its `X-User-ID` is stamped into `recorded_by_member_id` on every recall signal it makes.
- A `JuryChair` schedules and holds hearings, decides protests, reopens a decision and rules protests invalid. On every protest action, `X-User-ID` must equal the `chair_member_id` of the `JuryPanel` bound to that protest — or, when scheduling a hearing, of the panel named in the payload — and on a recusal it must equal the `chair_member_id` of the panel being changed. Any other case is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ACTING_PARTY`.
- A `Competitor` registers boats, submits entries, maintains the crew manifest, withdraws, files protests and submits evidence, and acts only on its own boats and the entries it helms. Its `X-User-ID` must equal `owner_member_id` of the boat being registered, `helm_member_id` of the entry being acted on, or `helm_member_id` of the entry named as `protestor_entry_id` when filing a protest. Any other case is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ACTING_PARTY`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Unless a subsection states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains after the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and the Logical Race Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. The exception is the recall signal record: a `RecallEvent` is append-only and immutable, carries only `id` and `created_at`, and has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. There is no update endpoint and no delete endpoint for recall signals. Every other instant that the server itself stamps or computes rather than echoing back from the client — `results_closed_utc` on a race, `filed_utc` on a protest, the `recorded_utc` of an evidence element, the `seated_utc` of a panel seat, the `current_utc` the clock capability answers with, and the `coverage_window_start_utc` and `coverage_window_end_utc` of the staffing read — is rendered in that same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. An instant supplied by the client, on the other hand, is stored and returned exactly as the client wrote it, without renormalisation.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Every deadline, validity window, coverage window and expiry rule in this document is evaluated against that clock. Before the first clock call the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host machine's real time. A `ClubAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, which touches no business row and answers with the resulting `current_utc`.

### 2.5. Rating Arithmetic and Numeric Precision

All rounding in this system is half-up: a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero. The rounding points are fixed and load-bearing, because rounding at a different step yields a different published result:

- The intermediate speed index `raw_speed_index` on a certificate is rounded to 4 decimal places before the rating rule branch is taken. Rounding after the branch instead is forbidden and shifts the published rating by as much as a tenth of a second per mile.
- A time-on-distance `rating_value` and every `band_key_spm` are rounded to 1 decimal place. A time-on-time `rating_value` is rounded to 4 decimal places.
- Corrected times, that is `corrected_seconds`, are rounded to 1 decimal place.
- Race points, the values in `points`, are rounded to 1 decimal place, and a tie group's average is rounded once at group level rather than per member.
- The `points_pool_total` of a race, and the `series_points_gross` and `series_points_net` of an entry, are rounded to 1 decimal place. Series totals sum the already-rounded per-race `points` values; summing unrounded per-race values and rounding only the total is forbidden and can differ by a tenth of a point for every three fractional races.
- `crew_weight_kg` is rounded to 1 decimal place.
- Duty minutes are whole integers throughout; both ends of every duty window fall on a whole minute, so no truncation question arises.

Band comparisons use the 1-decimal `band_key_spm` value with no tolerance whatsoever — a key exactly equal to a band's exclusive upper bound is outside that band.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Boats and Regattas

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`, because both are legitimately repeatable operations whose second execution silently displaces the first.

Creating a rating certificate — `POST /rating-certificates` — requires the non-optional field `expected_boat_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Boat` named by `boat_id`. If that value does not equal the boat's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOAT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Every certificate create, issue, suspend and reinstate increments `Boat.version` by 1, so a measurer working from a stale picture of the boat's measurement history is told to re-read the boat rather than stacking a second draft on top of a certificate they never saw. A second certificate create against the same boat is perfectly valid behaviour when the client's view is current.

Publishing a race document — `POST /race-documents` — requires the non-optional field `expected_regatta_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Regatta` named by `regatta_id`. If that value does not equal the regatta's current `version`, the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REGATTA_VERSION` and HTTP 412. An amendment legitimately supersedes the prior revision, so a second publication is valid behaviour on a current view; a stale version merely proves that the publisher has not seen the revision that landed in between, which would otherwise renumber revisions and point `amends_document_id` at a row that is already superseded.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, entry actions, division actions, race actions, duty actions, jury actions and protest actions accept no expected-version input, and their concurrency behaviour is governed purely by the state preconditions described in section 3.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once. The server therefore evaluates its checks in one strict, global order and answers with the **first** failing check — never with a later one, and never with a collection of them. The list below is that order, from first evaluated to last. Where a check applies only to certain endpoints, the applicable set is stated exhaustively; a check with no stated restriction applies to every endpoint.

1. Both authentication headers are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. For a member-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `ClubMember` row, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR`. This check applies to every endpoint except member enrolment.
3. The acting role is listed against the endpoint in section 4, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. When the acting role is `Measurer`, the acting member's `measurer_license_id` is non-null, else `ERR_MEASURER_NOT_LICENSED`. This check applies to certificate creation and to every certificate action — issue, suspend and reinstate.
5. Acting-party identity as described in section 2.1, else `ERR_NOT_ACTING_PARTY`. This check applies to exactly twelve operations: boat registration, entry submission, crew addition, crew removal, entry withdrawal, protest filing, evidence filing, hearing scheduling, holding a hearing, deciding a protest, reopening a protest, invalidating a protest, and jury recusal.
6. The body parses, every required input field of the endpoint is present and non-null, no unknown field is present, every enum-valued field carries a declared member, and every numeric field satisfies its declared range, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. On a collection read, `limit` and `offset` are within their declared bounds, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
8. Every `id` in the path resolves, checked left to right, and then every foreign key in the body resolves, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, else `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
9. On certificate creation, `expected_boat_version` equals the boat's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_BOAT_VERSION`.
10. On document publication, `expected_regatta_version` equals the regatta's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_REGATTA_VERSION`.
11. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, on the dimension that endpoint advances, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. This check applies to every action endpoint — that is, every operation that names a verb on a single resource.
12. The regatta is in the phase the operation requires, else `ERR_REGATTA_PHASE_INVALID`. Division creation requires `NOR_PUBLISHED` or `SI_PUBLISHED`; entry submission requires that the regatta is no longer `DRAFT`, the too-late cases being handled by the next check; starting a sequence, recording a result and scoring a race all require `RACING`.
13. Entries may not be submitted once the regatta has reached `RACING` or `SCORED`, else `ERR_ENTRIES_CLOSED`. This check applies to entry submission only.
14. The race's `start_blocked_reason` is null, else `ERR_RACE_START_BLOCKED`. This check applies to starting a sequence only.
15. The newest published sailing-instructions revision of the regatta has an `effective_utc` at or before the current clock, else `ERR_SAILING_INSTRUCTIONS_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. This check applies to starting a sequence only.
16. The clock has reached the protest's `hearing_utc`, else `ERR_HEARING_NOT_DUE`. This check applies to holding a hearing only.
17. No entry named by the request is `WITHDRAWN`, else `ERR_ENTRY_WITHDRAWN`. This check applies to four operations: protest filing, evidence filing, result recording and recall signalling. Because it outranks the protest-window deadline, a protest filed against a withdrawn entry after the window has closed is answered with `ERR_ENTRY_WITHDRAWN`, not with `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
18. Every member on the entry's `crew_manifest` has a non-null `safety_course_expiry_utc` that is at or after the regatta's `series_start_utc`, else `ERR_CREW_SAFETY_COURSE_EXPIRED`. This check applies to crew addition and to entry acceptance. Because it outranks the band check, an entry whose crew safety course has lapsed and whose pinned band no longer matches its division is answered with `ERR_CREW_SAFETY_COURSE_EXPIRED`, not with `ERR_DIVISION_BAND_MISMATCH`.
19. The entry's `inspection_status` is `PASSED`, else `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED`. This check applies to entry acceptance only.
20. The race committee is fully on station for the race, else `ERR_STAFFING_INCOMPLETE`. This check applies to starting a sequence only.
21. Jury segregation of duties holds, else `ERR_JURY_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. This check applies to four operations: panel formation, seating a judge, confirming a duty assignment and scheduling a hearing.
22. A duty assignment names `mark_boat_id` when and only when it must, and the named boat belongs to the same regatta, else `ERR_MARK_BOAT_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID`. This check applies to duty creation only.
23. Cross-parent coherence holds: `race_id`, `entry_id`, `division_id`, `certificate_id` and `jury_panel_id` all resolve to rows belonging to the same regatta — and a certificate additionally belongs to the boat named by the entry — else `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`. This check applies to six operations: entry submission, result recording, protest filing, hearing scheduling, duty creation and recall signalling.
24. `amends_document_id`, when present, names a `PUBLISHED` document of the same regatta and the same `doc_type`, and it is absent exactly when no published document of that type exists, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`. This check applies to document publication only.
25. The certificate pinned by the entry is `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED`. This check applies to division assignment and to entry acceptance.
26. The regatta's `series_start_utc` falls inside the certificate's inclusive validity window, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`. This check applies to entry submission and to entry acceptance.
27. `registered_crew_count` equals `declared_crew_count` and exactly one manifest element has `position` `HELM` with a `member_id` equal to `helm_member_id`, else `ERR_CREW_MANIFEST_INCOMPLETE`. This check applies to entry acceptance only.
28. The pinned `band_key_spm` still falls inside the assigned division's band and the boat's `boat_type` still matches its `boat_type_filter`, else `ERR_DIVISION_BAND_MISMATCH`. This check applies to entry acceptance only.
29. The division selector finds at least one eligible division, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DIVISION`. This check applies to division assignment only.
30. The division's `assigned_entry_count` has reached its effective floor, else `ERR_FLEET_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. This check applies to division confirmation only.
31. The merge selector finds at least one absorbing candidate, else `ERR_NO_MERGE_TARGET`. This check applies to division merging only.
32. No uniqueness rule is violated, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. This check applies to eight creations: boats, club members, divisions, races, mark boats, race results, panel seats and crew manifest elements.
33. The new race's prestart occupancy window is clear of every other non-abandoned race of the regatta, else `ERR_START_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. This check applies to race scheduling only.
34. The new duty window does not overlap another non-released window of the same volunteer, else `ERR_MEMBER_DUTY_OVERLAP`. This check applies to duty creation only.
35. The named mark boat still has room under its concurrent-assignment limit, else `ERR_MARK_BOAT_OVERBOOKED`. This check applies to duty creation only.
36. The protested race has at least one recorded result, so its `results_closed_utc` is non-null, else `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. This check applies to protest filing only.
37. The protest is filed inside the protest time limit, else `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. This check applies to protest filing only.
38. `protestor_entry_id` differs from `protestee_entry_id`, and `incident_utc` lies within the race's sailing interval, else `ERR_PROTEST_PARTIES_INVALID`. This check applies to protest filing only.
39. Jury qualification and quorum hold, else `ERR_JURY_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. This check applies to panel formation and to hearing scheduling.
40. The protest carries at least one piece of evidence, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`. This check applies to holding a hearing only.
41. The protest has not been reopened before, else `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. This check applies to reopening a protest only.
42. The decision payload is coherent, else `ERR_DECISION_PAYLOAD_INVALID`. This check applies to deciding a protest only.
43. The redress average has at least one source result, else `ERR_REDRESS_NOT_COMPUTABLE`. This check applies to deciding a protest only.
44. The result payload is coherent, else `ERR_RESULT_PAYLOAD_INVALID`. This check applies to result recording only.
45. The scored or recalled entry's `registration_status` is `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_ACCEPTED`. This check applies to result recording and to recall signalling.
46. The recall payload is coherent, else `ERR_RECALL_PAYLOAD_INVALID`. This check applies to recall signalling only.
47. An amendment's `effective_utc` is strictly later than that of the document it amends, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_LATER`. This check applies to document publication only.
48. Measurement inputs are inside their declared ranges and `valid_until` is strictly after `valid_from`, else `ERR_MEASUREMENT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. This check applies to certificate creation only.
49. Every division of the regatta holding at least one accepted entry is `CONFIRMED`, else `ERR_DIVISION_NOT_CONFIRMED`. This check applies to scoring a race only.
50. `actual_start_utc` is at or after the opening of the prestart window, else `ERR_START_TIME_INVALID`. This check applies to recording the start only.
51. `hearing_utc` is strictly later than the clock, else `ERR_HEARING_TIME_INVALID`. This check applies to hearing scheduling only.

The exact substance of each check — what "coherent", "eligible" or "on station" means — is given in section 3 alongside the workflow it guards. What this subsection fixes is the order.

### 2.8. Reference Resolution and Cross-Regatta Coherence

Resources in this system are peers: no collection is nested inside another collection's URL, and every parent-to-child traversal happens through a filtered list read rather than through a nested path. The only sub-path segments that exist are action verbs on a single resource and two computed reads.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right. Where a path carries two identifiers — as the crew-removal operation does, naming an entry and then a `member_id` — the entry is resolved first, and only if it exists is the manifest element sought. Body foreign keys are resolved after every path identifier, in the order in which the endpoint declares its inputs. A failure at any step yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Resolution alone is not coherence. A request may name rows that all exist but belong to different events, and that is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`: a result whose `race_id` and `entry_id` sit in different regattas, a protest whose two parties do not both sail the protested race's regatta, a hearing bound to a panel formed for another regatta, a duty assignment whose `race_id` or `mark_boat_id` belongs elsewhere, a recall naming an entry from another event. The same code covers a certificate that does not belong to the boat named by the entry.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

Every cascade in this system completes inside the request that triggers it. There is no eventual consistency, no background reconciliation and no partially applied cascade: by the time the response is emitted, every derived counter, every dependent state and every series total is already correct. Where a cascade is described in section 3 as a sequence of hops, each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the triggering call, which matters because a hop whose precondition is unmet simply stops the chain at that point without failing the request.

Derived counters are never incremented blindly; they are re-derived from the rows they count. `assigned_entry_count` on a division is the number of entries pointing at that division whose `registration_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. `accepted_entry_count` is the number pointing at it whose `registration_status` is exactly `ACCEPTED`. `confirmed_division_count` on a regatta is the number of its divisions in status `CONFIRMED`. `final_race_count` is the number of its races whose `scoring_status` is `FINAL` and whose `start_status` is not `ABANDONED`. `nor_revision` and `si_revision` hold the highest revision among the regatta's documents of type `NOR` and of type `SI` respectively. `registered_crew_count` is the length of `crew_manifest`, `evidence_count` the length of `evidence_log`, `seated_member_count` the number of `seats` elements whose `recused` flag is false, `general_recall_count` the number of general recall signals recorded against the race, and `rescore_count` the number of times a jury outcome has forced the race to be scored again.

A mutation refreshes the `updated_at` of every row it changes and increments that row's `version` by 1. That includes rows changed as a downstream consequence rather than as the request's direct target: when a certificate suspension pushes entries to `RATING_INVALID` and pulls a division back to `PROVISIONAL`, those entry and division rows are themselves mutated rows.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The measurer who signed a boat's certificate, the officer who runs its start, and the jury that judges its incidents are three distinct capacities, and the system refuses to let one person occupy two of them where the outcome could be tainted. A member who is seated and not recused on a `SEATED` panel of a regatta may not be the helm of any entry of that regatta, may not appear on any `crew_manifest` of that regatta, and may not hold a `CONFIRMED` duty assignment with `position` `PRO` for that regatta. Additionally, a panel chair may not be the `measured_by_member_id` of the certificate pinned by either entry named in a protest that panel is asked to hear. Every one of these conflicts is reported as `ERR_JURY_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`, and the check runs in all four directions: when a panel is formed, when a judge is seated, when a duty assignment is confirmed, and when a hearing is scheduled.

### 2.11. Filtered Collections and Reverse Reachability

Several rows in this system are created by cascades rather than by a client call, and are therefore never returned by any creation response: the recall events appended by a signal, the `DNC` results minted at scoring time, the certificates a supersede pushed to `SUPERSEDED`, the entries a suspension pushed to `RATING_INVALID`, the protests a recusal pushed back to `FILED`. Each collection read therefore exposes every one of its foreign keys and status dimensions as an optional filter, and that is the channel through which such rows are found.

The member collection filters on `judge_certification_level`. Boats filter on `owner_member_id` and `boat_type`. Certificates filter on `boat_id`, `measured_by_member_id`, `supersedes_certificate_id`, `status` and `rule_code`. Regattas filter on `status`. Documents filter on `regatta_id`, `amends_document_id`, `doc_type` and `status`. Divisions filter on `regatta_id`, `merged_into_division_id`, `status` and `boat_type_filter`. Entries filter on `regatta_id`, `boat_id`, `certificate_id`, `helm_member_id`, `division_id`, `registration_status` and `inspection_status`. Mark boats filter on `regatta_id`. Duty assignments filter on `regatta_id`, `race_id`, `member_id`, `mark_boat_id`, `position` and `status`. Races filter on `regatta_id`, `start_status`, `scoring_status` and `course_code`. Results filter on `race_id`, `entry_id`, `finish_status` and `voided`. Recall events filter on `race_id`, `entry_id`, `recorded_by_member_id` and `recall_type`. Panels filter on `regatta_id`, `chair_member_id` and `status`. Protests filter on `regatta_id`, `race_id`, `protestor_entry_id`, `protestee_entry_id`, `jury_panel_id`, `penalty_target_entry_id` and `status`. All of these are optional, all combine conjunctively, and all coexist with `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.12. Conserved Quantities

Two identities must hold after every request, and they are the sharpest available statement of what the compensating actions in section 3 are required to undo.

The duty-minutes identity: summed over every club member, `assigned_duty_minutes` equals the summed `duty_minutes` of every duty assignment whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`. Creating an assignment and then releasing it must leave both sides exactly at their pre-creation values — no half decrement, no residue on the mark boat's `committed_minutes` — and confirming the assignment in between changes neither side, because a `PROPOSED` row already counted.

The series-points identity, evaluated per regatta: summed over the regatta's `ACCEPTED` entries, `series_points_gross` equals the summed `points_pool_total` of the regatta's races whose `scoring_status` is `FINAL` and whose `start_status` is not `ABANDONED`. Scoring a race adds exactly one term to each side. Abandoning a race that was `FINAL` removes exactly one term from each side: every entry's `series_points_gross` drops by precisely the points that race had awarded it, the race's `points_pool_total` becomes 0, and the race leaves the counted set. Reopening a protest restores both sides to their pre-decision values. Both sides are compared exactly, at 1 decimal place.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Club Roster and the Boat Register

Everything begins with people. A `ClubAdmin` enrols a club member with a `full_name` and an `email`; the address must contain an at sign and is unique across all members, compared without regard to letter case, so a second enrolment on the same address is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. Three credential fields are optional at enrolment and default to nothing: `measurer_license_id` defaults to null, `judge_certification_level` defaults to `NONE`, and `safety_course_expiry_utc` defaults to null. The certification ladder runs `NONE`, `CLUB`, `REGIONAL`, `NATIONAL`, `INTERNATIONAL`, and only the top three rungs qualify a member to chair a jury. A member's duty ledger, `assigned_duty_minutes`, is initialised to 0. Those same three credential fields are the only mutable ones: a `ClubAdmin` may revise a licence number, a certification level or a safety-course expiry at any time, and each such revision advances the member's `version`.

Boats are registered by their owner or by the club office. A registration carries a `sail_number` unique across every boat in the register — again compared case-insensitively, between 3 and 12 characters, and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` on collision — a `boat_name`, an `owner_member_id` naming the member of record who may submit entries for that boat, a `boat_type` of `MONOHULL`, `MULTIHULL` or `KEELBOAT_SPORT`, and a declared `loa_meters` greater than 0 and at most 40. Length overall is documentation only: it is stored, it is returned on reads, and it never enters any rating or scoring formula. A newly registered boat's `version` is 1, which is the value the first certificate creation must echo.

### 3.2. Measurement Data, Rating Values and the Certificate Cycle

A licensed `Measurer` files a measurement by naming the `boat_id`, choosing the rating rule in `rule_code` — either `TOD_PHRF`, which mints a seconds-per-mile allowance, or `TOT_ORC`, which mints a time-correction factor — and supplying four measured quantities: `sail_area_m2` greater than 0 and at most 400, `hull_length_m` greater than 0 and at most 40, `displacement_kg` greater than 0 and at most 100000, and `draft_m` greater than 0 and at most 6. The validity window is declared with `valid_from` and `valid_until`, and `valid_until` must fall strictly after `valid_from`. Any measured quantity outside its range, or a window whose end does not strictly follow its start, is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The creation also carries `expected_boat_version`, as described in section 2.6. The filed row is minted in status `DRAFT`, its `measured_by_member_id` is set from the acting measurer's identity, `suspension_reason_code` and `supersedes_certificate_id` both start null, and the boat's `version` advances by one.

The rating is computed once, at creation, and the measurement inputs are immutable afterwards. It is built in two steps, and the boundary between them is where the rounding happens. **Step one** produces the intermediate speed index. Take the square root of `sail_area_m2` and divide it by the cube root of `displacement_kg`; that quotient is the base figure. Divide `hull_length_m` by 8.0 and confine the result to the closed range 1.00 to 1.60 — a quotient below 1.00 is treated as 1.00 and one above 1.60 as 1.60 — giving the length factor. A boat whose `draft_m` is strictly greater than 2.20 metres carries the deep-draft penalty of 0.03, so its draft multiplier is 0.97; a draft of exactly 2.20 metres carries no penalty and a multiplier of 1.00. Hull family supplies the last multiplier: 1.00 for a `MONOHULL`, 0.82 for a `MULTIHULL` and 0.94 for a `KEELBOAT_SPORT`, read from the referenced boat's `boat_type`. Multiply the base figure by the length factor, then by the draft multiplier, then by the hull-family multiplier, and round the product half-up to four decimal places. That stored value is `raw_speed_index`, and it is the rounded value — not the unrounded product — that step two consumes.

**Step two** branches on the chosen rule. Under `TOD_PHRF`, multiply `raw_speed_index` by 380.0, subtract that product from 420.0, round half-up to one decimal place, and then confine the result to the closed range from −30.0 to 300.0; the outcome is `rating_value`, expressed in seconds per nautical mile, and `band_key_spm` is set equal to it. Under `TOT_ORC`, multiply `raw_speed_index` by 0.7000, add 0.4000, round half-up to four decimal places, and then confine the result to the closed range 0.5000 to 1.6000; the outcome is `rating_value`, a dimensionless factor. Because divisions are banded on a single scale, a time-on-time certificate additionally gets a seconds-per-mile projection: subtract 0.4000 from `rating_value`, multiply the difference by 542.8571, subtract that product from 420.0 and round half-up to one decimal place, and store the result as `band_key_spm`. `band_key_spm` is always on the seconds-per-mile scale whatever the rule, and it is the sole key used for division band matching.

Issuing is what makes a certificate usable, and only the measurer who filed it may issue it: the acting identity must equal the row's `measured_by_member_id`, and the logical clock must lie inside the inclusive window from `valid_from` to `valid_until`. The certificate then moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`. That first hop causes a second: if the same boat already held an `ISSUED` certificate, that older row moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED` and the newly issued row records the older row's identifier in its own `supersedes_certificate_id`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — a certificate never returns from it. Crucially, superseding never re-points any entry: an entry that pinned the older certificate stays pinned to it, which is exactly how a boat can go on racing the series under a rating that has since been replaced. The boat's `version` advances by one.

When a measurement irregularity comes to light, the measurer suspends the certificate, supplying a `suspension_reason_code` that must be present and non-blank. The certificate moves `ISSUED → SUSPENDED`, and a four-hop transitive cascade runs, each hop caused by the one before it rather than by the suspension itself. Hop one records the new status and the reason code. Hop two moves every entry that pinned this certificate and whose `registration_status` is `ACCEPTED` to `RATING_INVALID`; an entry still in `SUBMITTED` is untouched, because no edge leads from `SUBMITTED` into `RATING_INVALID`. Hop three has every division holding one of those entries re-derive `assigned_entry_count` and `accepted_entry_count`, and any division in status `CONFIRMED` whose refreshed `assigned_entry_count` has fallen below its effective floor reverts `CONFIRMED → PROVISIONAL`. Hop four sets `start_blocked_reason` to `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM` on every race of that regatta whose `start_status` is still `PENDING`. The boat's `version` advances by one. A division's effective floor is the greater of its own `min_entries` and the regatta's `fleet_minimum`, a rule that recurs throughout this document.

Reinstatement closes the cycle `ISSUED → SUSPENDED → ISSUED`. The measurer reinstates a `SUSPENDED` certificate provided the logical clock is still inside the inclusive validity window; hop one restores status `ISSUED` and clears `suspension_reason_code` back to null. Hop two returns every entry pinning this certificate whose `registration_status` is `RATING_INVALID` to `SUBMITTED` — deliberately not to `ACCEPTED`, so the chair must admit each one again — and hop three has the divisions of those entries re-derive both counts. Because the entries land in `SUBMITTED`, `accepted_entry_count` does not recover here, and a division that regains enough assigned entries is **not** auto-confirmed: the chair must confirm it a second time. `start_blocked_reason` is cleared on every `PENDING` race of the regatta whose divisions all meet their floors. The boat's `version` advances by one once more.

### 3.3. Opening an Event: Notices, Sailing Instructions and Amendments

A regatta is opened by the club office or by the `RegattaChair` and starts life in status `DRAFT`. It is declared with a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters as printed on the notice of race, a `host_club_name` identifying the organising authority, and the policy choices that will govern every race and every result of the series: `scoring_system`, either `LOW_POINT` or `BONUS_POINT`; `discard_policy`, one of `NONE`, `ONE_PER_FIVE` or `ONE_PER_FOUR`, defaulting to `NONE`; `handicap_basis`, either `PER_CERTIFICATE` or `UNIFIED_TOD`, defaulting to `PER_CERTIFICATE`, which selects the corrected-time variant for every result of the event; and `safety_category`, one of `CAT_4`, `CAT_3` or `CAT_2`, where the two offshore categories additionally demand a safety boat on station before any start. A `planned_race_count` between 1 and 20 states how many races the series is scheduled to sail. Three thresholds carry defaults: `fleet_minimum`, the event-wide floor on assigned entries per division, defaults to 3 and must lie between 2 and 20; `protest_time_limit_minutes` defaults to 90 minutes — an hour and a half after a race's results close — and must lie between a quarter of an hour and eight hours, that is between 15 and 480 minutes; and `jury_quorum_required`, the number of seated, non-recused members including the chair that a panel needs, defaults to 3 and must lie between 2 and 7. Finally `series_start_utc` fixes the first scheduled warning signal of the series, and it is the single instant against which certificate validity and crew safety-course validity are tested. On creation, `nor_revision`, `si_revision`, `confirmed_division_count` and `final_race_count` are all 0 and `version` is 1.

Publishing is what actually opens the event. A `RegattaChair` publishes a document for a `regatta_id` with a `doc_type` of `NOR` for the notice of race or `SI` for the sailing instructions, a `body_summary` describing the published text, an `effective_utc` from which the revision governs, and `expected_regatta_version`. The `revision` is never supplied by the client: an original publication takes revision 1, and an amendment takes one more than the revision it amends. The document's own `status` starts at `PUBLISHED`. Publishing the first `NOR` moves the regatta `DRAFT → NOR_PUBLISHED` and sets `nor_revision` to 1; it is refused if a notice of race already exists for that regatta. Publishing the first `SI` requires the regatta to be in `NOR_PUBLISHED` with `nor_revision` of at least 1, and moves it `NOR_PUBLISHED → SI_PUBLISHED` with `si_revision` set to 1. Each of these publications advances the regatta's `version` by one. Divisions and entries may only be created from `NOR_PUBLISHED` onwards.

Amendments work through `amends_document_id`, which names the currently `PUBLISHED` revision being superseded. That field must be present exactly when a published document of the same type already exists for the regatta, and absent exactly when none does; it must name a document of the same `regatta_id` and the same `doc_type`, and that document must still be `PUBLISHED` rather than already superseded. A violation of any of those conditions is `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`. An amendment's `effective_utc` must additionally fall strictly later than the `effective_utc` of the document it amends; otherwise `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_LATER`. When the amendment lands, the amended row moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`, the new row takes the amended row's revision plus one, and the regatta's `nor_revision` or `si_revision` is set to the new revision. An amendment published with an `effective_utc` in the future is an entirely legitimate act — and it is precisely the situation that stops a start sequence, as section 3.7 explains.

Once the sailing instructions are out, the chair opens racing, which moves the regatta `SI_PUBLISHED → RACING`. That is permitted only when `si_revision` is at least 1, when `confirmed_division_count` is at least 1, when no division of the regatta is simultaneously `PROVISIONAL` and holding at least one assigned entry, and when at least one jury panel of the regatta has reached status `SEATED`. Opening racing freezes entry submission and advances the regatta's `version`.

The last transition is not requested by anyone. At the end of a successful race scoring, the regatta moves `RACING → SCORED` — a terminal state — if and only if two aggregate conditions hold: `final_race_count` has, after that scoring run, become equal to `planned_race_count`; and no protest of the regatta is still open, meaning none is in `FILED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `HEARD` or `REOPENED`. This is a bubble-up: the parent advances because its children reached a required aggregate state. An outstanding protest keeps the regatta in `RACING` even when every planned race is already `FINAL`, and the regatta then bubbles up later, at the moment that last protest is decided or invalidated. Each such transition advances the regatta's `version`.

### 3.4. Divisions: Rating Bands, Fleet Minimums and Mergers

A division is a rating band with a hull-family restriction. The chair creates one against a `regatta_id` with a `code` of 1 to 8 characters — the division letter or class code as printed in the sailing instructions, unique within the regatta and otherwise refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` — a `boat_type_filter` of `MONOHULL`, `MULTIHULL`, `KEELBOAT_SPORT` or `ANY`, and the two band edges `rating_band_low` and `rating_band_high`. The band is asymmetric and deliberately so: `rating_band_low` is an **inclusive** lower bound and `rating_band_high` an **exclusive** upper bound, and the high edge must be strictly greater than the low edge. An optional `min_entries`, defaulting to 3 and constrained to between 2 and 20, sets the division's own fleet floor. A division opens in status `PROVISIONAL` with `assigned_entry_count` and `accepted_entry_count` both 0 and `merged_into_division_id` null. Divisions may only be created while the regatta is in `NOR_PUBLISHED` or `SI_PUBLISHED`.

Confirmation is the chair's declaration that a real fleet exists. It is permitted only from `PROVISIONAL`, only while the regatta is in `NOR_PUBLISHED` or `SI_PUBLISHED`, and only when the division's `assigned_entry_count` has reached the greater of its own `min_entries` and the regatta's `fleet_minimum`; a division short of that floor is refused with `ERR_FLEET_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. On success the division moves `PROVISIONAL → CONFIRMED`, the regatta's `confirmed_division_count` is refreshed and its `version` advances, and — this is the reason a chair chases confirmations mid-event — `start_blocked_reason` is cleared back to null on every race of the regatta that was carrying `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM`, but only for those races where no other division of the same regatta remains `PROVISIONAL` while holding at least one assigned entry. One confirmed division does not unblock the start line while a second under-subscribed division is still open.

The reverse edge is never requested; it is a consequence. Whenever a cascade re-derives a `CONFIRMED` division's `assigned_entry_count` and finds it below the effective floor, the division moves `CONFIRMED → PROVISIONAL`, `start_blocked_reason` is set to `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM` on every race of the regatta whose `start_status` is `PENDING`, and the regatta's `confirmed_division_count` is refreshed. This revert edge is what makes the division lifecycle non-linear, and it is the third and fourth hops of the certificate-suspension cascade of section 3.2 as well as the tail of a withdrawal.

When a division simply never fills, the chair merges it away. Merging is permitted only from `PROVISIONAL` and only while the division's `assigned_entry_count` is still below the greater of its `min_entries` and the regatta's `fleet_minimum` — a division that already meets its floor is confirmed, not merged. The absorbing division is chosen by the server, never named by the client. Candidates are the other divisions of the same regatta whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `boat_type_filter` either equals the merging division's `boat_type_filter` or is `ANY`; the merging division itself is excluded, and a division already in `MERGED` is never a candidate. Among the candidates the server computes each band's midpoint — the sum of `rating_band_low` and `rating_band_high` divided by two — and prefers the candidate whose midpoint is closest to the merging division's own midpoint, measured as an absolute difference. Ties are broken by the lower `rating_band_low`, and any remaining tie by the earlier `created_at`; the identifier is never used as a tie-breaker. If no candidate exists at all, the merge is refused with `ERR_NO_MERGE_TARGET` and the division stays `PROVISIONAL`.

A successful merge runs four hops. Hop one moves the division `PROVISIONAL → MERGED`, a terminal state, and records the chosen absorber in `merged_into_division_id`. Hop two re-points every entry currently held by the merged division to the target division — only entries in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` bear on any count, but the pointer itself moves for every entry of the merged division. Hop three has both divisions re-derive `assigned_entry_count` and `accepted_entry_count`, and if the target is `PROVISIONAL` and its refreshed `assigned_entry_count` now reaches the greater of the target's own `min_entries` and the regatta's `fleet_minimum`, the target auto-transitions `PROVISIONAL → CONFIRMED` and the regatta's `confirmed_division_count` grows accordingly. Hop four clears `start_blocked_reason` to null on every race of the regatta that was carrying `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM`, once no division of the regatta remains `PROVISIONAL` while holding at least one assigned entry. The regatta's `version` advances.

### 3.5. Entries: Submission, Crew, Inspection, Assignment and Acceptance

An entry is submitted by the helm or on their behalf by the chair, naming the `regatta_id` being entered, the `boat_id` being entered, the `certificate_id` to be pinned, the `helm_member_id` of the person who will steer, and a `declared_crew_count` between 1 and 20 that includes the helm. The pinned certificate is the heart of the design: it is captured at submission time and **never re-pointed**, so a later supersede leaves the entry racing on the certificate it was submitted with. At submission the pinned certificate's validity window must cover the regatta's `series_start_utc`, both bounds inclusive, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`; and the certificate must belong to the boat named by the entry and the boat must belong to the same event context, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`. A submission arriving after the regatta has reached `RACING` or `SCORED` is refused with `ERR_ENTRIES_CLOSED`, and one arriving while the regatta is still `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_REGATTA_PHASE_INVALID`. A new entry carries two independent status dimensions: `registration_status` starts at `SUBMITTED` and `inspection_status` at `NOT_INSPECTED`. Its `crew_manifest` starts empty, with `registered_crew_count` 0, `crew_weight_kg` 0, `division_id` null, `equipment_deficiency_code` null, `series_points_gross` and `series_points_net` 0, `discarded_race_numbers` empty and `series_rank` null.

The crew manifest is built one person at a time. Registering a crew member supplies a `member_id` naming a club member, a `position` from `HELM`, `TACTICIAN`, `TRIM`, `BOW` and `FLOATER`, and a `weight_kg`. Each addition appends an element to `crew_manifest`, increments `registered_crew_count` by one, adds the declared `weight_kg` into `crew_weight_kg` — which is maintained rounded half-up to one decimal place — and advances the entry's `version`. A manifest holds at most one element with `position` `HELM`, and a given member appears at most once on a given entry; a repeat naming of the same member is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. Every addition also re-tests the whole manifest against the safety requirement: every member on the manifest must have a non-null `safety_course_expiry_utc` at or after the regatta's `series_start_utc`, and an expired or missing sea-survival course anywhere on the manifest is refused with `ERR_CREW_SAFETY_COURSE_EXPIRED`. A crew member may be taken off again, but only by the `Competitor` who helms the entry: removal names the member in the path, drops that manifest element, decrements `registered_crew_count` by one, subtracts that element's `weight_kg` from `crew_weight_kg`, and advances the entry's `version`.

Equipment inspection is the second dimension, and it is advanced only by a `RaceOfficer` recording an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL`. Inspection may be recorded while the entry's `registration_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RATING_INVALID`. A first pass moves `NOT_INSPECTED → PASSED` and requires `registered_crew_count` of at least 1; it also clears `equipment_deficiency_code` to null. A first failure moves `NOT_INSPECTED → FAILED` and requires an `equipment_deficiency_code` in the payload, which is stored. A boat that fixes its deficiency is re-inspected: from `FAILED → PASSED`, which again clears `equipment_deficiency_code` to null. A boat that breaks something after passing goes `PASSED → FAILED`, again requiring an `equipment_deficiency_code`, which is stored. The two dimensions of an entry advance without gating each other: an entry that is already `ACCEPTED` and then fails inspection **keeps** `registration_status` `ACCEPTED` and stays in its division's counts, because a later inspection failure never rewrites the paperwork dimension. Only one edge in the whole entry lifecycle reads across the two dimensions, and that is acceptance.

Division assignment is performed by the chair and computed by the server; the client never names a division. The selector reads the `band_key_spm` of the entry's pinned certificate as its key and the `boat_type` of the entry's boat, and the pinned certificate must be `ISSUED` at that moment or the assignment is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED`. Eligible divisions are those of the entry's own regatta whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED` — a `MERGED` division is never eligible — whose `boat_type_filter` either equals the boat's `boat_type` or is `ANY`, and whose band contains the key, meaning `rating_band_low` is less than or equal to the key and the key is strictly less than `rating_band_high`. Among the eligible divisions the narrowest band wins: candidates are ordered by band width, that is `rating_band_high` minus `rating_band_low`, ascending; ties by `rating_band_low` ascending; and any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending, never by identifier. The first candidate becomes the entry's `division_id`, both of that division's counts are refreshed, and the entry's `version` advances. When no division is eligible, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DIVISION` and `division_id` is left null.

Acceptance is the chair's admission of the entry to the fleet, and it moves `registration_status` from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`. Every one of the following must hold. The entry must have a `division_id`, and that division must be in `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED` rather than `MERGED`; an entry with no division or a merged one cannot be accepted and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The `inspection_status` must be `PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED`. Every member on the `crew_manifest` must hold a valid safety course as described above, otherwise `ERR_CREW_SAFETY_COURSE_EXPIRED`. The pinned certificate must be `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED`, and its inclusive validity window must still cover `series_start_utc`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`. The manifest must be complete: `registered_crew_count` equal to `declared_crew_count`, with exactly one element whose `position` is `HELM` and whose `member_id` equals the entry's `helm_member_id`, otherwise `ERR_CREW_MANIFEST_INCOMPLETE`. And the pinned `band_key_spm` must still fall inside the assigned division's band, low edge inclusive and high edge exclusive, with the boat's `boat_type` still matching that division's `boat_type_filter`, otherwise `ERR_DIVISION_BAND_MISMATCH`. On success the division's `assigned_entry_count` and `accepted_entry_count` are refreshed and the entry's `version` advances. Note the cross-aggregate guard here: an entry cannot advance unless a row belonging to an entirely different aggregate — the boat registry's rating certificate — is in `ISSUED` with a window covering the series start.

Withdrawal is available to the chair and to the helming `Competitor`, and it is terminal: `registration_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` from either `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. Withdrawing an entry that is already `ACCEPTED` is permitted only while no race of the regatta with `scoring_status` `FINAL` holds a non-voided result for that entry; once the entry's score is in the books, the entry stays in the series and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The entry keeps its `division_id` for audit, but the division re-derives both counts, which may pull it `CONFIRMED → PROVISIONAL` and set `start_blocked_reason` to `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM` on every `PENDING` race of the regatta. No result already recorded for the entry is rewritten or removed by a withdrawal, and whatever those results contributed to their race's `points_pool_total` stays there. The entry's `version` advances.

### 3.6. Staffing the Race Committee: Mark Boats and Duty Windows

No race can be run by nobody, and the volunteer side of the event is modelled as explicitly as the racing side. The chair first charters the boats: a mark boat is created against a `regatta_id` with a `call_sign` unique within that regatta — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` — and a `crew_capacity` between 1 and 12 people. Two scheduling knobs come with defaults. `concurrent_assignment_limit`, defaulting to 2 and constrained to between 1 and 6, caps how many non-released duties may overlap that boat at any instant. `turnaround_buffer_minutes`, defaulting to 20 minutes and constrained to between 0 and 120, is added to **both** ends of every window already committed on that boat before overlap is tested, which is why two windows that merely touch nose-to-tail still collide on a mark boat. A newly chartered boat's `committed_minutes` is 0.

Duties are then proposed. A duty assignment names the `regatta_id` being staffed, an optional `race_id`, the volunteer's `member_id`, an optional `mark_boat_id`, the `position` being filled — one of `PRO`, `TIMEKEEPER`, `SIGNALS`, `MARK_BOAT_DRIVER` or `SAFETY_BOAT` — and the duty window as `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. A null `race_id` means the duty covers the whole regatta and counts towards every race of it; a stated `race_id` narrows it to that race alone. The window is **half-open**: `window_start_utc` is included, `window_end_utc` is excluded, and the end must fall strictly after the start. Because the window is half-open, two back-to-back duties on the same volunteer that touch at exactly one instant do **not** collide; any genuine overlap of two non-released windows on the same `member_id` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_DUTY_OVERLAP`. On a mark boat the test is different: the requested window is compared against every non-released assignment of that boat after each of those existing windows has been widened by `turnaround_buffer_minutes` at both ends, and if the number of such buffer-widened windows intersecting the request has already reached the boat's `concurrent_assignment_limit`, the request is refused with `ERR_MARK_BOAT_OVERBOOKED`. `mark_boat_id` must be present when `position` is `MARK_BOAT_DRIVER` or `SAFETY_BOAT` and absent for every other position, and the boat named must be chartered for the same regatta; otherwise `ERR_MARK_BOAT_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID`.

A proposed duty already counts in both ledgers. Its `duty_minutes` is the number of whole minutes between `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`; that figure is added to the volunteer's `assigned_duty_minutes` and, when a mark boat is named, to that boat's `committed_minutes`. Both ledgers count assignments whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` and ignore those in `RELEASED`, which remain queryable. Creating a duty advances the volunteer's `version`, and the assignment itself opens in `PROPOSED`.

Confirmation puts the volunteer on station: `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED`. Neither ledger moves at confirmation, precisely because the `PROPOSED` row already counted — this is the property that makes the duty-minutes identity of section 2.12 hold across a create-confirm-release sequence with no drift. Confirmation of a duty whose `position` is `PRO` is blocked when that member currently occupies a non-recused seat on any `SEATED` jury panel of the same regatta, and the refusal is `ERR_JURY_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. Because only `CONFIRMED` rows count towards staffing, a confirmation re-derives `staffing_ready` and `staffed_position_codes` on every `PENDING` race whose coverage window this assignment now fully covers, and it advances the assignment's `version`.

Release is the compensating action and the terminal state. A duty may be released from `PROPOSED` or from `CONFIRMED`, carrying a `release_reason_code` that is stored on the row. Releasing a `CONFIRMED` duty is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` while any race of the regatta whose staffing depends on that assignment is in `SEQUENCE_STARTED` — you do not take the timekeeper off the boat between the warning signal and the start. Release subtracts `duty_minutes` from the volunteer's `assigned_duty_minutes` and, where a mark boat was named, the same figure from that boat's `committed_minutes`, leaving both ledgers exactly at their pre-creation values with no residue. It frees the window, so a later assignment on the same volunteer or the same mark boat no longer collides with it, re-derives `staffing_ready` and `staffed_position_codes` on every `PENDING` race the window had covered, and advances the volunteer's `version`.

Readiness itself is computed, not declared. For a given race, the coverage window runs from half an hour before its `scheduled_start_utc` to two hours after it — that is, from 30 minutes before to 120 minutes after. An assignment counts as on station only if its `status` is `CONFIRMED`, its `regatta_id` is the race's regatta, its `race_id` is either that race or null, its `window_start_utc` is at or before the start of the coverage window, and its `window_end_utc` is at or after the end of it. A window that merely overlaps part of the coverage window contributes nothing at all; a window flush with both edges counts, because both comparisons are inclusive. `PROPOSED` and `RELEASED` assignments are ignored entirely. Three positions are always required, one of each: `PRO`, `TIMEKEEPER` and `SIGNALS`. A race whose `course_code` is `WINDWARD_LEEWARD` or `TRIANGLE` additionally requires one `MARK_BOAT_DRIVER`, since those courses have marks to be laid and shifted; a `DISTANCE_COASTAL` course does not. A regatta whose `safety_category` is `CAT_3` or `CAT_2` additionally requires one `SAFETY_BOAT`; `CAT_4` does not. `staffing_ready` is true exactly when every required position has at least the required count on station, and `staffed_position_codes` holds the sorted, distinct positions actually on station across the whole window. When readiness fails, the request that needed it is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_INCOMPLETE` — including the degenerate case of no confirmed assignments whatsoever. A read-only staffing capability exposes the same computation for planning: it answers with the `race_id`, the boolean `staffing_ready`, the `staffed_position_codes`, the `missing_position_codes` still outstanding, and the coverage window as `coverage_window_start_utc` and `coverage_window_end_utc`.

### 3.7. Scheduling Races and Managing the Start Line

A race is scheduled by the chair or by a race officer against a `regatta_id`, carrying a `race_number` between 1 and 20 that is unique within the regatta — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` — a `course_code` of `WINDWARD_LEEWARD`, `TRIANGLE` or `DISTANCE_COASTAL`, a `course_distance_nm` greater than 0 and at most 300 nautical miles, and a `scheduled_start_utc` naming the warning-signal instant. The start line is a physical resource, so each race occupies it for a closed prestart window running from five minutes before `scheduled_start_utc` up to and including `scheduled_start_utc`. Two non-abandoned races of the same regatta may not have overlapping prestart windows, and consecutive races additionally need a three-minute gap between the end of one window and the start of the next; a schedule that violates either condition is refused with `ERR_START_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A new race opens with `start_status` `PENDING` and `scoring_status` `NOT_SCORED`, with `actual_start_utc` null, `general_recall_count` 0, `black_flag_active` false, `points_pool_total` 0, `results_closed_utc` null, `rescore_count` 0 and `abandon_reason_code` null. Two computed fields are seeded at creation: staffing readiness is evaluated immediately, filling `staffing_ready` and `staffed_position_codes`; and `start_blocked_reason` is set to `DIVISION_UNDER_MINIMUM` right away if some division of the regatta that already holds at least one assigned entry is still short of the greater of its `min_entries` and the regatta's `fleet_minimum`. Otherwise `start_blocked_reason` is null. That field is the only value it ever takes.

Starting the sequence advances `start_status` `PENDING → SEQUENCE_STARTED`. Four conditions gate it: the regatta must be in `RACING`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REGATTA_PHASE_INVALID`; `start_blocked_reason` must be null, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_RACE_START_BLOCKED`; the newest `PUBLISHED` sailing-instructions revision of the regatta must have an `effective_utc` at or before the current clock, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SAILING_INSTRUCTIONS_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — which is exactly what an amendment published with a future effective instant produces; and the committee must be fully on station, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_STAFFING_INCOMPLETE`. On success `staffing_ready` and `staffed_position_codes` are refreshed from the readiness computation.

The same operation is also the re-entry edge after a general recall, advancing `GENERAL_RECALLED → SEQUENCE_STARTED` under the same conditions — `start_blocked_reason` null, committee on station, sailing instructions in force, regatta racing. That closes the cycle `SEQUENCE_STARTED → GENERAL_RECALLED → SEQUENCE_STARTED`. What the re-start deliberately does **not** do is reset the recall counters: `general_recall_count` and `black_flag_active` are left exactly as they were, which is why the third attempt at a start runs under black flag.

Recalls are signalled by the race officer and recorded as immutable events. A recall may be signalled only while the race's `start_status` is `SEQUENCE_STARTED`, whatever the `recall_type`; a signal reaching a race in `PENDING`, `GENERAL_RECALLED`, `STARTED` or `ABANDONED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A signal carries a `recall_type` of `INDIVIDUAL` or `GENERAL`, an `entry_id` that is required for `INDIVIDUAL` and must be null for `GENERAL`, a `signal_utc` naming the instant the signal was displayed, and a `reason_code` such as `PREMATURE_START` or `UNIDENTIFIED_BOATS`. The `signal_utc` may not precede the opening of the prestart window, that is five minutes before `scheduled_start_utc`; a payload violating the type-and-entry pairing or the timing is refused with `ERR_RECALL_PAYLOAD_INVALID`. A named entry must belong to the same regatta as the race, or `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`; must not be `WITHDRAWN`, or `ERR_ENTRY_WITHDRAWN`; and must have `registration_status` `ACCEPTED`, or `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Each signal appends a recall record carrying its `race_id`, `recall_type`, `entry_id`, `signal_utc` and `reason_code`, with `recorded_by_member_id` set from the signalling officer's identity, `resulting_start_status` set to the race's `start_status` immediately after the event, and `black_flag_applied` set to whatever `black_flag_active` was at signal time.

A `GENERAL` recall sends the whole fleet back: `start_status` moves `SEQUENCE_STARTED → GENERAL_RECALLED`, `general_recall_count` grows by one, and once that cumulative count reaches 2, `black_flag_active` becomes true. The count is cumulative across the whole race and is never reset. An `INDIVIDUAL` recall penalises one identified boat and leaves the start standing, and its consequence depends entirely on the black flag. While `black_flag_active` is false, the race stays in `SEQUENCE_STARTED` and no result is minted at all. While `black_flag_active` is true, the same signal mints a result for the named entry with `finish_status` `OCS`, and that result is marked non-discardable — its `discardable` flag is false — which moves the race's `scoring_status` `NOT_SCORED → PROVISIONAL` and stamps `results_closed_utc` from the logical clock, opening the protest window as a side effect of a recall.

Recording the start moves `start_status` `SEQUENCE_STARTED → STARTED` and stores the supplied `actual_start_utc`, which may not precede the opening of the prestart window five minutes before `scheduled_start_utc`; an earlier instant is refused with `ERR_START_TIME_INVALID`.

Abandonment is the compensating action for the entire scoring chain, and it is terminal on the start dimension. It carries a `reason_code`, stored as `abandon_reason_code`. Abandoning a race that is still in `SEQUENCE_STARTED` simply moves it to `ABANDONED` and stores the reason; no result exists yet, so nothing is voided. Abandoning a race in `STARTED` runs three hops. Hop one sets `start_status` `ABANDONED`, stores `abandon_reason_code`, forces `scoring_status` back to `NOT_SCORED` — including from `FINAL`, which is the only way a scored race leaves `FINAL` — and sets `points_pool_total` to 0. Hop two rewrites every result of the race: `voided` becomes true, `points` becomes 0, `place` becomes null and `corrected_seconds` becomes null. Hop three re-runs the series standing for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the regatta, so each entry's `series_points_gross` falls by precisely the points this race had awarded it. Finally the regatta's `final_race_count` is refreshed, since an abandoned race never counts towards the planned total.

### 3.8. Recording Results and Correcting Time

Results are recorded one boat at a time by the race officer, naming the `race_id`, the `entry_id` being scored and a `finish_status`. On creation the permitted values are narrow: `FINISHED`, `DNF`, `DNS`, `RET` and `DNC`. The three remaining abbreviations are derived only — `OCS` is minted by a black-flag individual recall, and `DSQ` and `RDG` are minted by jury decisions — so a client supplying any of them is refused with `ERR_RESULT_PAYLOAD_INVALID`. The same code covers the elapsed-time pairing: `elapsed_seconds` is required when `finish_status` is `FINISHED` and must be absent for every other value, and when supplied it must be greater than 0 and at most 604800, that is at most seven days of elapsed time. An optional `penalty_percent` records a scoring penalty taken by the boat under the sailing instructions and defaults to 0; the only accepted values are 0, 20, 30 and 40. A result may be recorded only while the race is in `STARTED` and the regatta is in `RACING`, only for an entry whose `registration_status` is `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_ACCEPTED` — and never for a `WITHDRAWN` entry, which yields `ERR_ENTRY_WITHDRAWN`. One result exists per race and entry pair, so a second recording for the same pair is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`, and the race and the entry must belong to the same regatta or the recording is refused with `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`. A fresh result carries `voided` false and `discardable` true for every `finish_status` except `DSQ`.

The first result of a race moves its `scoring_status` `NOT_SCORED → PROVISIONAL` and stamps `results_closed_utc` from the logical clock; every subsequent result re-stamps it, so `results_closed_utc` always names the instant the most recent result of that race was created. That instant is what opens the protest window. What a result does **not** get at recording time is a place or a score: `place`, `points` and the race's `points_pool_total` stay unwritten until the race is scored.

Corrected time, on the other hand, is computed the moment a finisher is recorded, and again whenever the race's points are recomputed. It is written to `corrected_seconds` only for a result whose `finish_status` is `FINISHED`; for every other status `corrected_seconds` stays null. The computation reads the certificate **pinned by the entry** — not the boat's current certificate — the race's `course_distance_nm`, and the regatta's `handicap_basis`, and it comes in two variants whose results differ for the same boat and the same elapsed time.

The **time-on-distance** variant applies when the regatta's `handicap_basis` is `UNIFIED_TOD`, whatever the certificate's `rule_code`, and also when `handicap_basis` is `PER_CERTIFICATE` while the pinned certificate's `rule_code` is `TOD_PHRF`. Its allowance in seconds per mile is the certificate's `rating_value` when `rule_code` is `TOD_PHRF`, and the certificate's `band_key_spm` when `rule_code` is `TOT_ORC` — which is how a unified-scale regatta scores a time-on-time certificate on the distance scale. Multiply that allowance by `course_distance_nm`, subtract the product from `elapsed_seconds`, and round half-up to one decimal place. The handicap effect therefore grows with the length of the course and is entirely unaffected by how long the boat took.

The **time-on-time** variant applies in exactly one case: the regatta's `handicap_basis` is `PER_CERTIFICATE` and the pinned certificate's `rule_code` is `TOT_ORC`. Multiply `elapsed_seconds` by the certificate's `rating_value` and round half-up to one decimal place. Here the handicap effect grows with elapsed time and ignores `course_distance_nm` completely. For one and the same time-on-time certificate and one and the same elapsed time the two variants return different corrected times, and on a long light-wind race they can invert the finishing order — which is why `handicap_basis` is fixed for the whole event at regatta creation rather than chosen per race.

### 3.9. Scoring a Race and Rolling the Series Forward

Closing scoring is a single act by the race officer with a long tail. It requires the race to be in `scoring_status` `PROVISIONAL` with `start_status` `STARTED`, the regatta to be in `RACING`, and every division of the regatta holding at least one accepted entry to be in status `CONFIRMED`; a still-provisional fleet is refused with `ERR_DIVISION_NOT_CONFIRMED`. Four hops follow. Hop one mints a result with `finish_status` `DNC` for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the regatta that has no result in this race — the did-not-compete rows nobody enters by hand. Hop two computes corrected times and then places and points, division by division, writing `corrected_seconds`, `place`, `points` and the race's `points_pool_total`. Hop three re-runs the series standing for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the regatta, writing `series_points_gross`, `series_points_net`, `discarded_race_numbers` and `series_rank`. Hop four refreshes the regatta's `final_race_count` and may bubble the regatta `RACING → SCORED` as described in section 3.3. The race's own `scoring_status` moves `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`.

Places and points are computed per division, over the divisions of the race's regatta whose `accepted_entry_count` is at least 1. Two things are read from the division: the division itself supplies the fleet size used in every non-finisher formula, namely its `accepted_entry_count` at scoring time; and the candidate results are those results of this race that are not voided and whose entry sits in that division. Entries whose `registration_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `RATING_INVALID` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded both from the fleet size and from the candidate set, and any rows they may hold keep `points` 0.

The finishers among the candidates are ordered by `corrected_seconds` ascending; equal corrected times are broken by `elapsed_seconds` ascending, and any remaining tie by the entry's `created_at` ascending — never by identifier. That ordering fills slots 1, 2, 3 and so on. Each slot has a base value that depends on the regatta's `scoring_system`. Under `LOW_POINT`, the base value of a slot is simply the slot number, so consecutive slots always differ by exactly 1.0. Under `BONUS_POINT`, the first six slots are worth 0.0, 3.0, 5.7, 8.0, 10.0 and 11.7 respectively, and every slot from the seventh onwards is worth its slot number plus 6.0.

Ties are then resolved as groups. A tie group is a maximal run of finishers whose `corrected_seconds` are equal — and since corrected times are already one-decimal values, equality is exact. Every member of a group takes the same `place`, namely the first slot the group occupies. Every member also takes the same group score: the mean of the base values of all the slots the group occupies, rounded half-up to one decimal place. That rounding happens once, at group level, and not per member.

A finisher that took a scoring penalty is scored worse but never worse than a non-finisher. When `penalty_percent` is greater than 0 and the `finish_status` is `FINISHED`, take the group score and add the fleet size multiplied by `penalty_percent` divided by 100; then, if that sum exceeds the fleet size plus 1, use the fleet size plus 1 instead; then round half-up to one decimal place. When `penalty_percent` is 0, the boat's `points` are simply the group score.

Non-finishers keep `place` null and score by formula. A result whose `finish_status` is `DNF`, `DNS`, `RET`, `OCS` or `DSQ` scores the fleet size plus 1. A result whose `finish_status` is `DNC` scores the fleet size plus 2. A result whose `finish_status` is `RDG` takes the score computed by the redress rule of section 3.10 and is not recomputed here. Once every division is scored, the race's `points_pool_total` becomes the sum of `points` over every non-voided result of the race across all divisions, rounded half-up to one decimal place.

The series standing is then derived per `ACCEPTED` entry, inside its division. The counted races are the races of the entry's regatta whose `scoring_status` is `FINAL` and whose `start_status` is not `ABANDONED`, taken in ascending `race_number` order; races in `PROVISIONAL` or `NOT_SCORED` contribute nothing, and a voided result is skipped even inside a counted race. `series_points_gross` is the sum of the entry's `points` across those counted races, rounded half-up to one decimal place — and the summands are the per-race values **already** rounded to one decimal by the race scoring step. Summing unrounded per-race values and rounding only the total is forbidden; under `BONUS_POINT` in particular, a three-boat tie group averaging 3.0, 5.7 and 8.0 produces a repeating decimal, and the two orders of operation diverge by as much as a tenth of a point every three fractional races.

Discards depend on the regatta's `discard_policy` and on the number of counted races. Under `NONE` nothing is ever discarded. Under `ONE_PER_FIVE` one result is discarded once at least 5 races have been counted, and never more than one. Under `ONE_PER_FOUR` the entitlement is the number of counted races divided by 4 and rounded down, capped at 2 — so 4 to 7 counted races give one discard, 8 or more give two, and fewer than 4 give none. The discard pool for an entry is its results in the counted races that are not voided and whose `discardable` flag is true; the entitlement is spent on the highest-scoring members of that pool, and when two pool members carry equal `points`, the one from the **higher** `race_number` is discarded first, never the one with the lower identifier. `series_points_net` is `series_points_gross` minus the summed `points` of the discarded results, rounded half-up to one decimal place, and `discarded_race_numbers` holds the `race_number` values of the discarded results in ascending order.

Ranking runs within the division over its `ACCEPTED` entries. The primary key is `series_points_net` ascending. The first tie-break counts firsts: the entry with more results whose `place` is 1 ranks higher; if those are equal, the entry with more results whose `place` is 2 ranks higher, and so on upward through the places until the fleet size is exhausted. The second tie-break is the score in the last race: whichever entry took lower `points` in the counted race with the highest `race_number` ranks higher. The third and final tie-break is the earlier `created_at` of the entry. The result is written to `series_rank` as a 1-based rank, and `series_rank` is null for every entry of a division while that division's regatta has no counted races at all.

The same computation is available as a read for a single entry, and it answers with the `entry_id`, the `division_id`, the `gross_points` and `net_points`, the `discarded_race_numbers`, the number of counted races as `races_counted`, the division `rank`, and a `tie_break_trace` explaining which tie-break decided the rank.

### 3.10. Juries, Protests, Penalties and Redress

A jury panel is formed by the chair of the organising authority against a `regatta_id`, naming a `chair_member_id`. The nominated chair must hold a `judge_certification_level` of `REGIONAL`, `NATIONAL` or `INTERNATIONAL`; a `NONE` or `CLUB` nomination is refused with `ERR_JURY_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, the same code that covers an under-quorum panel later. A panel is created with the chair alone: its `seats` array holds one element whose `member_id` is the chair, whose `seat_role` is `CHAIR`, whose `recused` flag is false and whose `seated_utc` records when the seat was taken, giving `seated_member_count` of 1 and status `FORMING`.

Quorum is reached by seating additional judges, one `member_id` at a time. Each seating appends a `seats` element with `seat_role` `MEMBER`, `recused` false and its own `seated_utc`, increments `seated_member_count`, and advances the panel's `version`; a member already holding a seat on that panel is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. Once `seated_member_count` reaches the regatta's `jury_quorum_required`, the panel bubbles up `FORMING → SEATED`. Both panel formation and each seating enforce the segregation-of-duties rule of section 2.10 and are refused with `ERR_JURY_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` when the member concerned helms an entry of that regatta, appears on any `crew_manifest` of that regatta, or holds a `CONFIRMED` duty assignment with `position` `PRO` for it.

Recusal is the reverse, and it is the one panel operation reserved to the `JuryChair` of that panel. Naming a seated `member_id`, hop one marks that `seats` element's `recused` flag true and decrements `seated_member_count`. Hop two, caused by the first, moves the panel `SEATED → FORMING` when the refreshed count has fallen below the regatta's `jury_quorum_required`. Hop three, caused by the second, reverts every protest bound to this panel whose status is `HEARING_SCHEDULED` back to `FILED`, clearing that protest's `jury_panel_id` and `hearing_utc` to null. The panel's `version` advances. A panel that stays at or above quorum after the recusal keeps its `SEATED` status and its bound hearings.

A protest is lodged by a competitor or by a race officer, naming the `race_id` in whose incident it arose, the `protestor_entry_id` lodging it, the `protestee_entry_id` it is directed against, the `rule_cited` alleged to have been broken, and the `incident_utc` of the alleged incident. The server copies `regatta_id` from the protested race and stamps `filed_utc` from the logical clock. Filing is possible only once the race's results have opened — its `results_closed_utc` must be non-null, otherwise `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — and only within the time limit: the clock at filing must be at or before `results_closed_utc` plus the regatta's `protest_time_limit_minutes`, the exact limit instant still counting as inside, otherwise `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The two parties must be different entries and `incident_utc` must lie between the race's `actual_start_utc` and its `results_closed_utc`, both bounds inclusive; a violation is `ERR_PROTEST_PARTIES_INVALID`. Neither party may be `WITHDRAWN`, and that check outranks the window deadline as section 2.7 records. A new protest opens in status `FILED` with an empty `evidence_log`, `evidence_count` 0, `reopen_count` 0, `decision` `NONE`, and `jury_panel_id`, `hearing_utc`, `penalty_target_entry_id`, `redress_race_number` and `invalid_reason_code` all null. While it remains open it holds the regatta back from `SCORED`.

Evidence is filed by a competitor or by the jury chair. Each filing supplies a `kind` of `WRITTEN_STATEMENT`, `WITNESS`, `GPS_TRACK` or `PHOTO`, a `summary`, and the `submitted_by_entry_id` of the entry submitting it; the server appends an element to `evidence_log` with `recorded_utc` taken from the logical clock, increments `evidence_count` by one, and advances the protest's `version`. The log is append-only.

Scheduling a hearing binds a panel and fixes a time, moving the protest `FILED → HEARING_SCHEDULED`. The payload names a `jury_panel_id` and a `hearing_utc`. The named panel must be in status `SEATED` with `seated_member_count` at least the regatta's `jury_quorum_required`, otherwise `ERR_JURY_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; the acting identity must be that panel's `chair_member_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_ACTING_PARTY`; no non-recused seat of the panel may belong to the helm of either the protestor's or the protestee's entry and the chair may not be the `measured_by_member_id` of the certificate pinned by the protestee's entry, otherwise `ERR_JURY_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`; the panel must serve the same regatta as the protest, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_REGATTA_REFERENCE`; and `hearing_utc` must be strictly later than the current clock, otherwise `ERR_HEARING_TIME_INVALID`. The same operation is also the re-entry edge after a reopening, moving `REOPENED → HEARING_SCHEDULED` under a seated panel and a future hearing time.

Holding the hearing moves `HEARING_SCHEDULED → HEARD`. It requires `evidence_count` of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`; it requires the clock to have reached `hearing_utc`, otherwise `ERR_HEARING_NOT_DUE`; and it must be performed by the chair of the bound panel.

The decision moves `HEARD → DECIDED` and carries a `decision` of `PENALTY_DSQ`, `PENALTY_SCORING_20`, `DISMISSED` or `REDRESS_GRANTED`, an optional `penalty_target_entry_id` and an optional `redress_race_number`. Payload coherence is strict, and a violation is `ERR_DECISION_PAYLOAD_INVALID`: `penalty_target_entry_id` must be present and must name either the protestor's or the protestee's entry for every decision except `DISMISSED`, and `redress_race_number` must be present exactly when the decision is `REDRESS_GRANTED` and absent otherwise, naming a race of the regatta that is `FINAL` and not abandoned. The protested race itself must already have reached `scoring_status` `FINAL`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and only the chair of the bound panel may decide.

A decision then runs four hops. Hop one stores the status, the `decision`, the `penalty_target_entry_id` and, for a redress, the `redress_race_number`. Hop two rewrites results according to the decision, and each branch is different. For `PENALTY_DSQ`, the target entry's result in the protested race is rewritten to `finish_status` `DSQ` with `elapsed_seconds` null, `corrected_seconds` null and `discardable` **false** — a disqualification cannot be thrown away as a discard. For `PENALTY_SCORING_20`, that same result keeps its existing `finish_status` and instead takes `penalty_percent` 20, so it is re-scored through the penalty formula of section 3.9. For `REDRESS_GRANTED`, the target entry's result in the race named by `redress_race_number` is rewritten to `finish_status` `RDG` with an averaged score, as described below. For `DISMISSED`, no result is rewritten at all. Hop three re-runs the race points and places for the affected race and increments that race's `rescore_count` by one; the race's `scoring_status` stays `FINAL` throughout, which is exactly why `rescore_count` exists as the observable of a re-score. Hop four re-runs the series standing for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the affected division, so a non-discardable `DSQ` can push a different race out of an entry's `discarded_race_numbers` and reshuffle `series_rank`. Once no protest of the regatta is open, the regatta may bubble up `RACING → SCORED`. The protest's `version` advances.

The redress score looks circular and is deliberately defined as a fixed evaluation order rather than an iteration. First, ensure the points of every counted race other than the redress race are current. Second, gather the target entry's source results: its non-voided results in counted races other than the redress race whose `finish_status` is one of `FINISHED`, `DNF`, `DNS`, `RET` or `OCS`. Results with `finish_status` `DSQ` or `DNC`, and any other `RDG` result, are excluded from the average. Third, if that source set is empty, the whole decision is refused with `ERR_REDRESS_NOT_COMPUTABLE`; otherwise the redress score is the arithmetic mean of the `points` of the source results, rounded half-up to one decimal place. Fourth, the target entry's result in the redress race becomes `finish_status` `RDG` with `points` equal to that redress score, `place` null, `corrected_seconds` null and `discardable` true. Fifth, the race points and places are re-run for the redress race, leaving the other rows of that race unchanged, and then the series standing is re-run for every `ACCEPTED` entry of the division. Sixth — and this is the load-bearing part — the redress score is **not** recomputed after that re-run, even though the discard set and the ranks it feeds may have moved. The value is defined by the third step alone.

Reopening is the compensating action for a decision, and it is available once and only once per protest: a protest whose `reopen_count` is already 1 is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. Performed by the chair of the bound panel from `DECIDED`, hop one moves the status to `REOPENED`, sets `reopen_count` to 1, returns `decision` to `NONE` and clears `hearing_utc` to null. Hop two restores the result the reversed decision had rewritten to the `finish_status` it carried before that decision, with `discardable` true and `penalty_percent` 0. Hop three re-runs the race points and places and then the series standing, restoring every entry's `series_points_gross` to its pre-decision value with no drift. The protest's `version` advances, and from `REOPENED` the only ways onwards are a fresh hearing or invalidation.

Invalidation is the terminal procedural exit. From `FILED` or from `REOPENED`, the jury chair rules the protest `INVALID`, supplying a `reason_code` that is stored as `invalid_reason_code`. Nothing in the scoring chain is touched, the protest's `version` advances, and because the protest is no longer open the regatta may bubble up `RACING → SCORED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is rooted at the `/regatta-ops` prefix. An operation that brings a brand-new resource of its own into existence answers with HTTP 201: enrolling a club member, registering a boat, filing measurement data, opening a regatta, publishing a document revision, creating a division, submitting an entry, chartering a mark boat, proposing a duty, scheduling a race, signalling a recall, recording a result, forming a jury panel and lodging a protest. Every other successful operation answers with HTTP 200 — the clock setter, every read, every state action on an existing resource, and the three operations that append a sub-record to a resource and return that resource: registering a crew member on an entry, seating a judge on a panel and filing evidence on a protest. Removing a crew member from an entry manifest also answers with HTTP 200. The request and response payloads are not repeated here — the inputs each operation accepts, the fields it writes and the cascades it triggers are specified in section 3, and the rejection order is specified in section 2.7.
