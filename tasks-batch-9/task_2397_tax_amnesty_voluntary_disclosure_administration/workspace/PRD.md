# Tax Amnesty & Voluntary Disclosure Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state department of revenue periodically opens a **tax amnesty program**: a bounded window during which delinquent and unregistered taxpayers may come forward, file the returns they never filed, pay the tax they owe, and have some or all of the statutory penalty and interest forgiven. The administrative burden is considerable. Program designers must publish which tax types and which historical periods are eligible and on what waiver terms. Screening staff must exclude the taxpayers the program was never meant to reward — those already under field or desk audit, and above all those under criminal investigation. Sophisticated non-filers will not surface at all unless their counsel can negotiate terms anonymously first, which means the department must run a mediated bargaining process against a client whose identity it does not yet know. And when the window shuts, the department must turn around and pursue everyone who stayed silent, at a deliberately harsher rate.

This API is the system of record for that whole arc. It governs program design and the eligible-period terms attached to it; anonymous pre-clearance and the round-by-round negotiation between a departmental disclosure officer and a taxpayer's representative; the drafting and dual-signature execution of a **voluntary disclosure agreement** with a limited lookback period and a prospective compliance commitment; return filing with statutory accrual and program waiver resolution; lump-sum and installment payment with an exact allocation waterfall; program closure, non-participant identification and revenue realization against forecast; post-program enforcement escalation; and — the sharpest edge of the design — breach of an executed agreement, which claws back every cent of waived penalty and interest, and rescission of that breach, which must put the ledger back precisely as it was.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money is exact**: every monetary amount is an integer number of cents, rounding is explicit and happens at a named point, and the waiver ledger obeys a stated identity at all times. Second, **compensating actions are exact**: reversal of a payment and rescission of a breach are not approximate rollbacks but precise inverses of the cascades they undo. Implementers should treat these two commitments as the acceptance criteria that matter most.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every resource in this system is mounted beneath the `/amnesty` prefix, with the sole exception of the test-clock capability described in §2.4. This section states the rules that hold everywhere, regardless of which resource a request addresses.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two identity headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five recognized role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The five roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `DisclosureOfficer`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `Representative`, and `Taxpayer`. When the headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorized for the operation being attempted, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the work as follows. A `ProgramAdministrator` designs programs and their eligible tax type terms, opens, closes and reconciles the window, ages installment schedules, curates the taxpayer and representative registries, and reverses cash. A `DisclosureOfficer` screens pre-clearance requests, posts the state's negotiating offers, drafts, executes and amends agreements, assesses filed returns, and rules on proposed payment plans. A `ComplianceAuditor` owns the audit and investigation records, certifies prospective compliance, declares and rescinds breach, defaults payment plans, and drives post-program enforcement. A `Representative` mediates anonymous pre-clearance and may file returns on behalf of the clients it has disclosed. A `Taxpayer` proposes payment plans and remits payments on its own agreements, and may also draft and file its own returns.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters. When `X-User-Role` is `Representative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Representative`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Representative` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Taxpayer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Taxpayer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Taxpayer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. For these two entity-backed roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `DisclosureOfficer`, or `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition writes it — as `opened_by_user_id` on an `EnforcementCase`, `last_offer_officer_user_id` on a `PreClearanceRequest`, `actor_user_id` on a `NegotiationRound`, `drafted_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` on a `VoluntaryDisclosureAgreement`, `assessed_by_user_id` on a `DisclosureReturn`, `created_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on an `EnforcementAction` — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

Ownership is enforced narrowly. A `Taxpayer` may only address agreements, returns, payment plans and payments whose `taxpayer_id` is its own `X-User-ID`. A `Representative` may only address pre-clearances whose `representative_id` is its own `X-User-ID`, and returns whose agreement's `pre_clearance_id` belongs to that representative. Violations produce `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Note that a `Taxpayer` proposing a plan records itself in `proposed_by_user_id`.

Two operations are additionally governed by **segregation of duties**. The officer whose `X-User-ID` equals an agreement's `drafted_by_user_id` may not execute that agreement, and the auditor whose `X-User-ID` equals an enforcement action's `created_by_user_id` may not issue that action. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the window is applied and `has_more` states whether rows remain beyond it.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row. The one exception is the `NegotiationRound`: a negotiation round is an append-only record of what was proposed and by whom, is never modified after creation, and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Every temporal rule in this document reads a single **logical clock** rather than wall time, and a test-driving capability lets an administrator set it. That capability takes a `to_iso8601` instant, returns the new clock value as `current_utc`, and modifies no business entity. Before any such call the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Throughout this document, "the current instant" means the value of that logical clock at the moment the request is processed. Where a rule compares a date rather than an instant, the date used is the calendar date of that instant in UTC.

Calendar dates supplied by clients — period bounds, due dates, lookback bounds — are strings in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Timestamps are ISO 8601. A malformed date or timestamp, a missing required field, a wrong type, an enum value outside its declared set, a monetary field that is not a positive integer number of cents where positivity is declared, or a percentage outside the inclusive range 0.00 through 1.00, all produce `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost identifier that fails to resolve decides the error. A request naming a tax type term under a program that does not exist fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` before the term is ever looked up. The full family of 404 conditions is `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_TYPE_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAXPAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after path identifiers, in the order the fields are declared for that operation, and an unresolvable one produces the corresponding 404 from the same family. This applies to `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances`, `POST /amnesty/agreements`, `POST /amnesty/enforcement-cases`, `POST /amnesty/enforcement-actions`, `POST /amnesty/payment-plans`, `POST /amnesty/payments` and `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/disclose-identity`.

The entity graph is deliberately flat: peers are joined by explicit foreign keys carried in request bodies, and the handful of nested path segments that exist — a program's tax type terms, a pre-clearance's negotiation rounds, an agreement's returns, plans and payments, a plan's installments — exist only as reverse lookups over those foreign keys, plus the two creation paths that take their parent from the path (`POST /amnesty/programs/{id}/tax-type-terms` sets `program_id`, and `POST /amnesty/agreements/{id}/returns` sets `agreement_id`).

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule would reject a request, the rules are evaluated in strictly ascending precedence order and the **first** violation decides the response. Implementers must honour this ordering, because it is the difference between a request surfacing the reason a caller cares about and being masked by an incidental one. The order is:

1. Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must be present — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` must be one of `ProgramAdministrator`, `DisclosureOfficer`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `Representative`, `Taxpayer` — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The role must be authorized for the endpoint per the table in §4 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. For the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` must be an existing `Taxpayer` `id` when the role is `Taxpayer`, or an existing `Representative` `id` when the role is `Representative` — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint reachable by `Taxpayer` or `Representative`.
5. Path identifiers resolve left to right; the leftmost missing one decides which 404 is returned, drawn from `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_TYPE_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAXPAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
6. Body foreign keys resolve in declared field order, yielding `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAXPAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances`, `POST /amnesty/agreements`, `POST /amnesty/enforcement-cases`, `POST /amnesty/enforcement-actions`, `POST /amnesty/payment-plans`, `POST /amnesty/payments` and `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/disclose-identity`.
7. Ownership, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to `POST /amnesty/agreements/{id}/returns`, `POST /amnesty/returns/{id}/file`, `POST /amnesty/payment-plans`, `POST /amnesty/payments`, `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/counter`, `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/disclose-identity` and `POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/accept`.
8. Body schema, types, enum membership, date and timestamp form, positive integer cents and percentage bounds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint with a request body.
9. `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
10. `expected_agreement_version` must equal the addressed agreement's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /amnesty/payment-plans` and `POST /amnesty/agreements/{id}/amend`.

### 2.7. Monetary and Percentage Precision

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere, and no monetary value is ever stored or returned as a decimal string. Wherever a monetary quantity is derived by multiplying by a rate, the product is converted to an integer number of cents by **rounding half up**, and that rounding happens exactly once, at the point named in the rule that produces the value. Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, a return's statutory penalty and its statutory interest are rounded independently of one another, and a return's waived penalty and waived interest likewise. Second, agreement-level and program-level totals are always the sum of already-rounded per-row values — never the rounding of an unrounded sum.

Percentages behave differently. Waiver fractions, haircut fractions, tier rates and multipliers are fractions in the inclusive range 0.00 through 1.00 (except `post_program_penalty_multiplier`, which lies in the inclusive range 1.00 through 3.00), they carry up to four decimal places, and they are never rounded to cents at the point they are resolved — only the money they are applied to is. Every threshold comparison written as "at least" in this document is inclusive at the boundary, and every one written as "strictly before" or "more than" excludes it.

One rate is rounded before it is compared rather than after: the program's realization percentage is rounded half up to **four** decimal places first, and its forecast band is then derived from the rounded value. A raw ratio of 0.89999974 therefore becomes 0.9000 and reports the healthiest band.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations require the client to declare which version of a parent record it was looking at, and are refused if that declaration is stale. This is the only concurrency mechanism in the system; no other endpoint takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

Proposing a payment plan (`POST /amnesty/payment-plans`) requires `expected_agreement_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `VoluntaryDisclosureAgreement`. If it does not equal that agreement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newer proposal legitimately supersedes the prior `PROPOSED` plan, so a writer holding a stale view of the agreement — its balance, or the existence of a proposal it never saw — must be refused rather than allowed to silently supersede.

Amending an agreement (`POST /amnesty/agreements/{id}/amend`) likewise requires `expected_agreement_version` as a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed agreement, and a mismatch with the current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The amendment supersedes the prior agreement and copies its scope forward, so drafting from a stale view would carry forward lookback bounds or liability figures that no longer hold.

Posting a state offer (`POST /amnesty/pre-clearances/{id}/offer-terms`) requires `expected_pre_clearance_version` as a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `PreClearanceRequest`. A mismatch with the current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRE_CLEARANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new offer supersedes the standing one, so an officer writing from a stale view of the negotiation — an unseen counter, an unseen identity disclosure — must be refused.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Maintenance

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. When they do, the mutation is immediate and complete before the response is returned: there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation. Each row the server touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, including rows the client did not address and rows whose only change was to a derived field. An implementer who increments the version of the addressed row but not of the collaterally revised rows will not satisfy the contract.

The derived counters and totals named on programs, taxpayers and agreements are not free-standing state that clients may set; they are recomputed from their definitions whenever a contributing fact changes. On an `AmnestyProgram` these are `collected_cents` (the sum of `amount_cents` over payments whose `status` is `APPLIED` across all agreements of the program), `waived_total_cents` (the sum of `waived_amount_cents` over agreements of the program whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `FULFILLED` or `SUPERSEDED`), `reinstated_total_cents` (the sum of `reinstated_amount_cents` over agreements of the program whose `status` is `BREACHED`), `participant_count` (the number of distinct `taxpayer_id` values over agreements of the program whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `FULFILLED`, `BREACHED` or `SUPERSEDED`) and `nonparticipant_count` (the number of distinct taxpayers whose `nonparticipant_flag` was set true by this program's closing cascade). On a `Taxpayer` these are `open_case_count` (the number of that taxpayer's enforcement cases whose `status` is `OPEN`), `open_enforcement_action_count` (the number of that taxpayer's enforcement actions whose `status` is `ISSUED`) and `nonfiler_period_count` (the number of returns with `is_nonfiler_period` true and `status` `FILED`, `ASSESSED`, `SETTLED` or `REINSTATED` under agreements of that taxpayer).

A design invariant governs the waiver ledger and must hold at every instant at which the agreement's `status` is not `BREACHED`: summed across the agreement's qualifying returns, gross penalty plus gross interest equals the agreement's `waived_amount_cents` plus the summed payable penalty and payable interest. Nothing is created or destroyed by a waiver — it is only moved between the waived and payable columns. Breach moves the whole waived column back to payable; rescission moves it back again.

### 2.10. Interval Conventions and Enumerations

Two kinds of period interval appear in this domain and they are deliberately **not** the same. A `ProgramTaxTypeTerm` publishes a **half-open** eligible window: `eligible_period_start` is inclusive and `eligible_period_end` is exclusive. Two terms of the same program and tax type whose windows touch at a shared endpoint — one term's `eligible_period_end` equal to the next term's `eligible_period_start` — do **not** overlap and may coexist. A `DisclosureReturn` declares a **closed** period: both `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` are inclusive. Two returns of the same agreement and tax type whose periods merely touch — a new `period_start_date` equal to an existing `period_end_date` — **do** overlap and the second is refused; adjacency, where the new `period_start_date` is the day after an existing `period_end_date`, does not overlap and is accepted.

The enumerations used throughout are fixed. Tax types, wherever they appear, are `SALES_USE`, `CORPORATE_INCOME`, `PERSONAL_INCOME`, `WITHHOLDING` and `EXCISE_FUEL`. A taxpayer's `taxpayer_type` is `INDIVIDUAL` or `BUSINESS`. A case's `case_type` is `FIELD_AUDIT`, `DESK_AUDIT`, `CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION` or `COLLECTION`. A plan's `plan_type` is `LUMP_SUM` or `INSTALLMENT`. An enforcement action's `action_type` is `NOTICE`, `ASSESSMENT` or `LIEN_REFERRAL`, and its `origin` is `MANUAL` or `BREACH_CASCADE`. A negotiation round's `proposed_by` is `STATE` or `REPRESENTATIVE`. An agreement's `liability_variant` is `AMNESTY_STATUTORY` or `VDA_NEGOTIATED`. A pre-clearance's `screening_result` is `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE_SELF_REPORTED_AUDIT`, `INELIGIBLE_CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION` or `INELIGIBLE_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS`, and is null before the screen has run. A taxpayer's `escalation_tier` is `T0`, `T1` or `T2`. The state enumerations of each lifecycle are given with their workflows in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing a Program and Publishing Its Eligible Terms

A `ProgramAdministrator` opens the story by designing an **amnesty program**. The design payload gives the program a `name`, fixes the participation window with `window_open_at` and `window_close_at`, sets `lookback_months` as the maximum lookback depth in whole months measured back from `window_open_at` (an integer from 1 through 120 inclusive), publishes the default waiver terms as `base_penalty_waiver_pct` and `base_interest_waiver_pct` (each a fraction from 0.00 through 1.00 inclusive), declares the `post_program_penalty_multiplier` that will punish non-participants after the window (a number from 1.00 through 3.00 inclusive), and states the `revenue_forecast_cents` the program is expected to collect (a positive integer). Two further inputs are optional and carry defaults: `prospective_compliance_months`, the commitment length copied onto each agreement, defaults to 36 and must lie between 12 and 120 inclusive; and `installment_max_count`, the ceiling on how many installments any plan under this program may schedule, defaults to 12 and must lie between 1 and 24 inclusive. A window whose `window_open_at` is not strictly earlier than its `window_close_at` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_WINDOW`. On creation the program's `status` is `DRAFT`, `closed_at` and `final_realization_pct` are null, and `collected_cents`, `waived_total_cents`, `reinstated_total_cents`, `participant_count` and `nonparticipant_count` all read 0.

While the program remains a `DRAFT`, the administrator attaches one **tax type term** per eligible tax type and eligible span. A term declares its `tax_type`, the half-open span `eligible_period_start` through `eligible_period_end`, and the `min_liability_cents` below which a reported liability for that tax type will not be accepted (a non-negative integer). It may override the program's defaults with a `penalty_waiver_pct` and an `interest_waiver_pct`; when either override is null the corresponding program-level base applies instead. It may also set `requires_prior_nonfiler`, which defaults to false and, when true, narrows that tax type to periods for which no return was ever filed. Each term records the owning `program_id` taken from the path. A term whose window overlaps an existing term of the same program and the same tax type is refused with `ERR_TAX_TYPE_TERM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; because the windows are half-open, terms that merely touch at an endpoint are fine.

Opening the window advances the program `DRAFT → OPEN`. Three preconditions guard it: the program must currently be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_DRAFT`), at least one tax type term must exist for the program — an empty set is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TAX_TYPE_TERMS` — and the current instant must be strictly before `window_close_at`, since opening a window that has already elapsed is meaningless and yields `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The only effects are that `status` becomes `OPEN` and the program's `version` advances by one. The program's later transitions, `OPEN → CLOSED` and `CLOSED → RECONCILED`, are described in §3.8; `RECONCILED` is terminal.

### 3.2. The Registries: Taxpayers, Representatives and Enforcement Records

Before anyone can participate, the administrator populates two registries. A `Taxpayer` is registered with a `legal_name`, a `taxpayer_type`, and `tin_last4` — exactly four digits of the taxpayer identification number. The optional `is_registered` flag, which defaults to true, records whether the taxpayer already holds a state tax account; a false value marks the classic unregistered non-filer. Every newly registered taxpayer starts with an `escalation_tier` of `T0`, a `compliance_behavior_score` of 100, a `nonparticipant_flag` of false, and `open_case_count`, `open_enforcement_action_count` and `nonfiler_period_count` all at 0. A `Representative` is registered with a `firm_name` and a `bar_number` — its state bar or CPA licence number — and begins with `status` `ACTIVE`. An administrator may later suspend a representative, moving `status` to `SUSPENDED`, which blocks every further negotiation act by that firm with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SUSPENDED`.

The `ComplianceAuditor` owns the enforcement side of the registry. Opening an **enforcement case** records the `taxpayer_id` under examination, the `case_type`, the `tax_type` under examination, and `opened_at`, the calendar date the case was opened. The case is created with `status` `OPEN`, `closed_at` null, and `opened_by_user_id` set from the acting auditor's `X-User-ID`; creating it immediately recomputes that taxpayer's `open_case_count`, `escalation_tier` and `compliance_behavior_score`. An auditor may later resolve a case in one of two ways, and the two are not interchangeable. Closing it moves the case `OPEN → CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` with the current instant, and recomputes the taxpayer's `open_case_count`, `escalation_tier` and `compliance_behavior_score` — a closed case no longer blocks program eligibility and no longer carries the open-case penalty. Referring it moves the case `OPEN → REFERRED`, leaves `closed_at` null, and recomputes the same three taxpayer fields, but a referred case carries the heavier referred-case penalty in place of the open-case penalty. Both `CLOSED` and `REFERRED` are terminal; attempting either on a case that is not `OPEN` yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_OPEN`.

The taxpayer's **compliance behavior score** is the department's summary judgement of that taxpayer's history, and it exists in two variants computed from identical facts. The `HISTORICAL` variant is the one persisted in `compliance_behavior_score`. It starts from a base of 100 and then subtracts 20 points for each enforcement case of that taxpayer whose status is `OPEN`, subtracts 30 points for each case whose status is `REFERRED`, subtracts 5 points for each period counted in `nonfiler_period_count`, subtracts a flat 25 points if `nonparticipant_flag` is true, subtracts 8 points for each action counted in `open_enforcement_action_count`, and finally adds 15 points for each agreement of that taxpayer whose status is `FULFILLED`. The result is clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100. Every arithmetic step is integer arithmetic.

The `PROGRAM_ADJUSTED` variant asks a different question: how would this taxpayer look if we credited the program for what it surfaced? It begins from the `HISTORICAL` value and then forgives the non-filer penalty on the periods the program brought in — for each return whose `is_nonfiler_period` is true, whose agreement belongs to the program in question with a status of `EXECUTED`, `FULFILLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and whose `period_start_date` falls on or after that agreement's `lookback_start_date`, 5 points are added back. If the taxpayer holds at least one such agreement, a further 18 points of participation credit are added. The result is clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100 exactly as the historical variant is. The persisted field always holds the `HISTORICAL` value; the adjusted value is reported, never stored. A per-taxpayer behavior report exposes both as `historical_score` and `program_adjusted_score`, alongside the component breakdown that produced them: `taxpayer_id`, `open_case_count`, `referred_case_count`, `nonfiler_period_count`, `forgiven_nonfiler_period_count`, `open_enforcement_action_count`, `nonparticipant_flag` and `escalation_tier`.

A taxpayer's `escalation_tier` is resolved from just two facts, evaluated in this order: the tier is `T2` when `nonparticipant_flag` is true and `open_case_count` is at least 1; otherwise it is `T1` when `nonparticipant_flag` is true; otherwise it is `T0`. It is re-resolved whenever an enforcement case is closed or referred, and whenever the program's closing cascade flips a non-participant flag.

### 3.3. Anonymous Pre-Clearance and Mediated Negotiation

The most delicate workflow in the system is the one that lets a taxpayer bargain before revealing who it is. A `Representative` submits a **pre-clearance request** naming the `program_id` under which clearance is sought, the `tax_type` the unnamed client wishes to disclose, an `anonymous_alias` to use while the client stays anonymous, an `estimated_liability_cents` figure (a positive integer estimate of the unpaid tax), a `self_reported_under_audit` declaration, and `earliest_unfiled_period_start`, the earliest unfiled period start the client is willing to admit. The server sets `representative_id` from the submitting representative's `X-User-ID`, leaves `disclosed_taxpayer_id` null, sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, and initialises `round_index` and `counter_count` to 0 with `screening_result`, `rejection_reason`, `current_offer_lookback_months`, `current_offer_penalty_waiver_pct`, `current_offer_interest_waiver_pct` and `last_offer_officer_user_id` all null. Submission requires the program to be `OPEN` and the window to be live, and a suspended representative may not submit at all.

Beginning review is the anonymous eligibility screen, and it forks on the representative's own declaration. When `self_reported_under_audit` is false the request advances `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` with `screening_result` set to `ELIGIBLE`. When `self_reported_under_audit` is true the same operation instead takes the automatic branch `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, setting `screening_result` to `INELIGIBLE_SELF_REPORTED_AUDIT` and copying that same enum name into `rejection_reason` as a string — no officer discretion is involved, a self-declared audit subject is simply excluded. Both branches require the request to be `SUBMITTED` (otherwise `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_SUBMITTED`) and the program to be `OPEN`, and both advance the request's `version` by one.

Negotiation then proceeds as an alternating exchange recorded in an append-only history of **negotiation rounds**. A `DisclosureOfficer` posts the state's position, moving the request `UNDER_REVIEW → TERMS_OFFERED` on the first offer or cycling it `COUNTERED → TERMS_OFFERED` on any later one. The offer payload carries `expected_pre_clearance_version` for the staleness check of §2.8, plus `offered_lookback_months`, `offered_penalty_waiver_pct` and `offered_interest_waiver_pct`, and an optional `offer_note`. The first offer additionally requires `offered_lookback_months` to be no greater than the program's `lookback_months`; a deeper offer is refused with `ERR_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDS_PROGRAM_LIMIT`. Posting an offer appends a round whose `proposed_by` is `STATE`, whose `round_index` is one greater than the request's current `round_index`, whose `lookback_months`, `penalty_waiver_pct` and `interest_waiver_pct` are the offered figures, whose `note` holds the supplied note, and whose `actor_user_id` is the officer's `X-User-ID`. The request's own `round_index` advances by one, its three `current_offer_*` mirrors are refreshed from this round's terms, `last_offer_officer_user_id` records the posting officer, `status` becomes `TERMS_OFFERED`, and `version` advances by one. A request that is neither `UNDER_REVIEW` nor `COUNTERED` cannot receive an offer and is refused with `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`.

The representative may push back, moving the request `TERMS_OFFERED → COUNTERED`. A counter carries `counter_lookback_months`, `counter_penalty_waiver_pct` and `counter_interest_waiver_pct`, plus an optional `counter_note`, and appends a round whose `proposed_by` is `REPRESENTATIVE` with `round_index` one greater than the current value; the request's `round_index` and `counter_count` each advance by one, `status` becomes `COUNTERED`, and `version` advances by one. Countering requires a standing offer — a request not in `TERMS_OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_NOT_OFFERED` — requires the program to be `OPEN`, and requires the acting representative's own `status` to be `ACTIVE`, so suspending a firm mid-negotiation freezes it. Crucially, the loop is bounded: a representative may post at most **three** counters on a single request, and the fourth is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Because the state's `current_offer_*` mirrors always reflect the newest `STATE` round, a counter does not erase the standing offer — it only stops the request from being accepted until the officer responds. The cycle `TERMS_OFFERED → COUNTERED → TERMS_OFFERED` may therefore turn at most three times.

Identity disclosure is the moment the anonymity ends and the screen is re-run against real records. Disclosing carries a `taxpayer_id` and requires the request to be in `TERMS_OFFERED`, requires the referenced taxpayer to exist, and requires that `disclosed_taxpayer_id` is still null — a second disclosure is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_ALREADY_DISCLOSED`. When the named taxpayer carries no blocking record, the request stays in `TERMS_OFFERED` — the transition is a self-loop — with `disclosed_taxpayer_id` set from the payload and `screening_result` set to `ELIGIBLE`. When a blocking record exists, the same operation takes the automatic rejection branch `TERMS_OFFERED → REJECTED`: `disclosed_taxpayer_id` is still recorded, `screening_result` becomes `INELIGIBLE_CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION` if the taxpayer has an enforcement case whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `case_type` is `CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION`, or `INELIGIBLE_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` if instead the taxpayer has an enforcement case whose `status` is `OPEN`, whose `case_type` is `FIELD_AUDIT` or `DESK_AUDIT`, and whose `tax_type` equals the request's own `tax_type`; `rejection_reason` is set to the same enum name as a string and `status` becomes `REJECTED`. The criminal test is evaluated **before** the audit test, so a taxpayer matching both is reported as under criminal investigation. Either branch advances the request's `version` by one.

Acceptance closes the negotiation. Accepting moves the request `TERMS_OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and requires four things: a standing offer, a non-null `disclosed_taxpayer_id` — accepting while still anonymous is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_DISCLOSED` — an `ACTIVE` representative, and an `OPEN` program. An officer may also close the file at any negotiable point, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, `TERMS_OFFERED → REJECTED` or `COUNTERED → REJECTED` and recording the supplied `rejection_reason` verbatim; a request already in a terminal state is refused with `ERR_PRE_CLEARANCE_TERMINAL`. The terminal states of a pre-clearance are `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED`, and `EXPIRED` is produced only by the program's closing cascade described in §3.8. Every negotiation round remains readable afterwards by its owning `pre_clearance_id`, and a round may be filtered by `proposed_by`.

### 3.4. Drafting, Executing and Amending the Agreement

A `DisclosureOfficer` drafts a **voluntary disclosure agreement** naming the `program_id` whose terms will govern it, the `taxpayer_id` disclosing, the `tax_types` in scope (a non-empty array drawn from the tax type enumeration), and the inclusive bounds `lookback_start_date` and `lookback_end_date` of the limited lookback period. Two inputs are optional. `pre_clearance_id` is supplied for a negotiated voluntary disclosure and omitted for a walk-in amnesty filing; `prospective_compliance_months` overrides the commitment length, and when omitted it is copied from the program's `prospective_compliance_months`. The server sets `drafted_by_user_id` from the acting officer, sets `status` to `DRAFTED`, leaves `amends_agreement_id`, `executed_by_user_id`, `executed_at`, `compliance_due_at` and `breach_reason` null, sets `payment_status` to `UNPAID`, and zeroes `gross_liability_cents`, `haircut_pct`, `waived_amount_cents`, `reinstated_amount_cents`, `total_due_cents`, `paid_total_cents`, `unapplied_credit_cents` and `down_payment_applied_cents`.

The single most consequential thing drafting decides is the `liability_variant`, and it is decided by whether `pre_clearance_id` was supplied. An agreement with no `pre_clearance_id` is `AMNESTY_STATUTORY`; an agreement with one is `VDA_NEGOTIATED`. As §3.5 shows, that choice moves the accrual cutoff for every return the agreement will ever carry, and it also decides which waiver percentages govern.

Drafting is gated hard. The program must be `OPEN` and the window must still be live. The lookback bounds must respect the program's limitation: `lookback_start_date` may not precede the calendar date of `window_open_at` shifted back by `lookback_months` whole months, and `lookback_end_date` may not precede `lookback_start_date`; a violation of either is `ERR_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDS_PROGRAM_LIMIT`. Every member of `tax_types` must have a published term on the program, otherwise `ERR_TAX_TYPE_NOT_IN_PROGRAM`. When a `pre_clearance_id` is supplied, that request's `status` must be `ACCEPTED` (`ERR_PRECLEARANCE_NOT_ACCEPTED`) and its `disclosed_taxpayer_id` must equal the agreement's `taxpayer_id` (`ERR_PRECLEARANCE_TAXPAYER_MISMATCH`) — the department will not let a representative's accepted terms be transplanted onto a different taxpayer. And the two eligibility exclusions bite here as well as at execution: a taxpayer with an `OPEN` `CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION` case is excluded outright with `ERR_TAXPAYER_UNDER_CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION`, and a taxpayer with an `OPEN` `FIELD_AUDIT` or `DESK_AUDIT` case whose `tax_type` is a member of the agreement's `tax_types` is excluded for that tax type with `ERR_TAXPAYER_UNDER_AUDIT_FOR_TAX_TYPE`.

Execution is the department's signature and it advances the agreement `DRAFTED → EXECUTED`. It requires the agreement to be `DRAFTED` (otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFTED`), the program to be `OPEN` with the current instant strictly before `window_close_at`, the acting officer's `X-User-ID` to differ from `drafted_by_user_id` — the drafter may not sign their own draft — and the taxpayer to have no `OPEN` `CRIMINAL_INVESTIGATION` case, since a case opened between drafting and execution must stop the signature. Executing sets `status` to `EXECUTED`, records `executed_by_user_id` and stamps `executed_at` with the current instant, computes `compliance_due_at` as `executed_at` advanced by `prospective_compliance_months` whole months, recomputes the program's `participant_count`, and advances the agreement's `version` by one. Because the default commitment runs three years, an agreement executed under the default cannot be certified until three years of the logical clock have passed.

Amendment supersedes an executed agreement with a fresh draft. It advances the addressed agreement `EXECUTED → SUPERSEDED` and simultaneously creates a **new** agreement in `DRAFTED` whose `amends_agreement_id` points back at the superseded one, carrying the same `program_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `pre_clearance_id` and `tax_types`, taking `lookback_start_date` and `lookback_end_date` from the amendment payload, taking `prospective_compliance_months` from the payload when supplied, and recording the amending officer in `drafted_by_user_id`. The payload also carries `expected_agreement_version` for the staleness check of §2.8. Amendment requires the program to be `OPEN` with the window live, requires the addressed agreement to be `EXECUTED` (otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`), and re-applies the lookback limitation to the revised bounds. The program's `participant_count` is recomputed, though because it counts distinct taxpayers an amendment never changes it. The agreement's `version` advances by one.

Certification is the auditor's judgement that the prospective commitment was honoured, advancing `EXECUTED → FULFILLED`. It requires the agreement to be `EXECUTED`, requires at least one return to exist on the agreement — an agreement with none is refused with `ERR_NO_DISCLOSURE_RETURNS` — requires every return that is not `VOIDED` to be `SETTLED`, otherwise `ERR_RETURNS_NOT_SETTLED`, and requires the current instant to be at or after `compliance_due_at`, otherwise `ERR_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Those three checks are evaluated in that order. Fulfilment recomputes the taxpayer's `compliance_behavior_score`, which now earns the fulfilled-agreement credit, and advances the agreement's `version` by one. The agreement's terminal states are `FULFILLED`, `LAPSED` and `SUPERSEDED`; `LAPSED` is produced only by the closing cascade of §3.8, and the `EXECUTED → BREACHED → EXECUTED` cycle is the subject of §3.7.

### 3.5. Disclosure Returns: Statutory Accrual and Waiver Resolution

A **disclosure return** is one tax type for one period. It may be drafted by the disclosing `Taxpayer` or by the `Representative` that mediated the disclosure, and it may be drafted while the owning agreement is either `DRAFTED` or `EXECUTED`. The draft payload names the `tax_type`, the inclusive period bounds `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, the `original_due_date` the filing missed, and the `reported_tax_cents` for the period (a positive integer). An optional `is_nonfiler_period` flag, false by default, marks a period for which no return was ever filed. The return records the owning `agreement_id` from the path, enters with `status` `DRAFT`, and holds `filed_at`, `accrual_end_date`, `penalty_rate_applied` and `assessed_by_user_id` null with `days_late`, `gross_penalty_cents`, `gross_interest_cents`, `waived_penalty_cents`, `waived_interest_cents`, `payable_penalty_cents`, `payable_interest_cents` and `balance_due_cents` all at 0.

Drafting a return runs a dense gauntlet of period rules. The `tax_type` must be a member of the agreement's `tax_types`, otherwise `ERR_TAX_TYPE_NOT_IN_AGREEMENT`. The period must lie inside the agreement's inclusive lookback window and inside the half-open eligible window of the matching term, per the conventions of §2.10, failing with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_LOOKBACK` and `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_ELIGIBLE_WINDOW` respectively. It must not overlap another non-`VOIDED` return of the same agreement and tax type, failing with `ERR_RETURN_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. When the matching term sets `requires_prior_nonfiler`, only a return with `is_nonfiler_period` true is accepted, otherwise `ERR_NONFILER_PERIOD_REQUIRED`. And `reported_tax_cents` must be at least the term's `min_liability_cents`, otherwise `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_LIABILITY`.

Filing is where the money is computed, and it advances the return `DRAFT → FILED`. Filing requires the return to be `DRAFT`, the agreement to be `EXECUTED`, the program to be `OPEN` and the current instant strictly before `window_close_at`. It stamps `filed_at` with the current instant and then runs the **statutory liability** computation in two variants selected by the agreement's `liability_variant`.

The variants differ in exactly one operand: the accrual cutoff. Under `AMNESTY_STATUTORY` the return's `accrual_end_date` is the calendar date of the program's `window_close_at` — a walk-in accrues all the way to the end of the program window no matter how early it files, because nothing the walk-in does stops the statutory clock. Under `VDA_NEGOTIATED` the return's `accrual_end_date` is the calendar date of the agreement's `executed_at` — executing the negotiated agreement freezes the clock, so whenever execution happened before the window closes, an otherwise identical return carries a strictly smaller liability than the walk-in variant would. Everything downstream of the cutoff is shared. `days_late` is the number of whole days from `original_due_date` to `accrual_end_date`, floored at 0 so a cutoff earlier than the due date yields zero rather than a negative count. The base penalty rate is then selected by tier: 5 percent when `days_late` is at most 90; 15 percent when `days_late` is more than 90 and at most 365; and 25 percent when `days_late` is more than 365. A non-filer period adds a further 5 percentage points on top of the tier rate, and the sum is stored in `penalty_rate_applied`. Finally, `gross_penalty_cents` is `reported_tax_cents` multiplied by `penalty_rate_applied`, rounded half up to whole cents; and `gross_interest_cents` is `reported_tax_cents` multiplied by a daily interest rate of 0.00025 and by `days_late`, rounded half up to whole cents. The two components are rounded independently and each is rounded exactly once.

Filing then re-resolves the **effective waiver** for the whole agreement, and this is the part implementers most often get wrong, because it revises rows the client did not touch. Resolution has four steps and the order is load-bearing. Step one picks the base waiver pair by precedence: when the agreement's `liability_variant` is `VDA_NEGOTIATED`, the pair is the linked pre-clearance's `current_offer_penalty_waiver_pct` and `current_offer_interest_waiver_pct`, because the negotiated offer overrides everything the program published; otherwise, when the term matching the return's `tax_type` carries a non-null `penalty_waiver_pct` and `interest_waiver_pct` override, that pair is used; otherwise the program's `base_penalty_waiver_pct` and `base_interest_waiver_pct` apply. Step two aggregates the agreement **before any waiver is applied**: `gross_liability_cents` is the sum, over the agreement's returns whose `status` is `FILED`, `ASSESSED` or `SETTLED`, of `reported_tax_cents` plus `gross_penalty_cents` plus `gross_interest_cents`. Step three selects the high-liability **haircut** from that aggregate: `haircut_pct` is 0.25 when `gross_liability_cents` is at least 250,000 dollars, otherwise 0.10 when `gross_liability_cents` is at least 50,000 dollars, otherwise 0.00 — both comparisons inclusive at the boundary. Step four applies the haircut asymmetrically: `effective_penalty_waiver_pct` is the base penalty fraction minus `haircut_pct`, and `effective_interest_waiver_pct` is the base interest fraction minus **half** of `haircut_pct`, each floored at 0.00 so a haircut larger than the base waiver produces no waiver rather than a negative one. The haircut is always derived from pre-waiver gross figures, never from post-waiver ones.

Because step two aggregates across every qualifying return, filing the Nth return can push the agreement across a haircut threshold and therefore **revise the already-stored waiver figures of returns one through N minus one**. That revision is the **waiver rollup**, and it runs per return: `waived_penalty_cents` is `gross_penalty_cents` multiplied by `effective_penalty_waiver_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; `waived_interest_cents` is `gross_interest_cents` multiplied by `effective_interest_waiver_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; `payable_penalty_cents` is `gross_penalty_cents` less `waived_penalty_cents`; `payable_interest_cents` is `gross_interest_cents` less `waived_interest_cents`; and `balance_due_cents` is `reported_tax_cents` plus `payable_penalty_cents` plus `payable_interest_cents`. The agreement's `waived_amount_cents` is then the sum, over its qualifying returns, of `waived_penalty_cents` plus `waived_interest_cents` — a sum of already-rounded values, never a rounding of a raw sum — and its `total_due_cents` is the sum of `balance_due_cents` over returns whose `status` is `FILED`, `ASSESSED`, `SETTLED` or `REINSTATED`. The program's `waived_total_cents` is recomputed in turn. Every sibling return whose figures were revised has its own `version` advanced by one, as do the filed return and the agreement.

One further side effect fires only conditionally: when the filed return's `is_nonfiler_period` is true, the taxpayer's `nonfiler_period_count` and `compliance_behavior_score` are both recomputed, since a newly surfaced unfiled period costs the taxpayer 5 points of historical score.

Assessment is the department's acknowledgement of a filed return, advancing `FILED → ASSESSED`. It requires the return to be `FILED` and the agreement to be `EXECUTED`, records `assessed_by_user_id` from the acting officer, and advances the return's `version` by one — no money moves. The return's remaining transitions are driven by cash and by breach: `ASSESSED → SETTLED` when a payment covers the agreement in full, `SETTLED → REINSTATED` when breach claws the waiver back, `REINSTATED → FILED` or `REINSTATED → SETTLED` when the breach is rescinded, and `DRAFT → VOIDED` when the program closes over an unexecuted agreement. `VOIDED` is the return's only terminal state.

### 3.6. Payment Plans, Installment Schedules and Cash Application

A `Taxpayer` proposes a **payment plan** against its own executed agreement, naming the `agreement_id`, the `plan_type`, the `installment_count`, the `down_payment_cents` it commits to, and `expected_agreement_version` for the staleness check of §2.8. The plan is created in `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` set from the proposer, `scheduled_total_cents` at 0, and `approved_at`, `rejection_reason` and `default_reason` null; any other plan of the same agreement still sitting in `PROPOSED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` at that moment, and the agreement's `version` advances by one. Two constraints are checked at proposal time. The `installment_count` must lie between 1 and the program's `installment_max_count` inclusive, and a `LUMP_SUM` plan must declare exactly 1, either failing yielding `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLMENT_COUNT`. And the commitment must be serious: `down_payment_cents` must be at least ten percent of the agreement's `total_due_cents`, that minimum itself being rounded half up to whole cents, otherwise `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

Approval by a `DisclosureOfficer` advances the plan `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and is the point at which the schedule comes into existence. Approval requires the plan to be `PROPOSED`, the agreement to be `EXECUTED`, and the program to be `OPEN` with the current instant strictly before `window_close_at` — a plan cannot be blessed after the window shuts. It also requires the down payment to have actually arrived: the agreement's `paid_total_cents` must be at least the plan's `down_payment_cents`, otherwise `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`. The **schedule generation** then computes the schedulable amount as the agreement's `total_due_cents` less the plan's `down_payment_cents`, and divides it by `installment_count` using integer floor division to obtain a base amount. Every installment except the last is set to that base amount; the last one is set to the schedulable amount less the base amount multiplied by one fewer than `installment_count`, so the final installment absorbs the entire division remainder and the schedule sums exactly to the schedulable amount. Each installment records its `payment_plan_id`, a 1-based `seq_index`, an `amount_cents`, a `paid_cents` starting at 0, a `status` of `SCHEDULED`, and a `due_date` computed as the calendar date of `approved_at` advanced by 30 days multiplied by that installment's `seq_index` — so the first installment falls due 30 days after approval, not on the approval date itself.

Two guards are evaluated **before any installment row is written**, so a rejected approval leaves no partial schedule behind. The horizon guard requires the last installment's `due_date` to fall no later than the calendar date of `window_close_at` plus one full year of 365 days, otherwise `ERR_INSTALLMENT_HORIZON_EXCEEDED`. The floor guard requires the smallest generated installment amount to be at least 2,500 cents, otherwise `ERR_INSTALLMENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Once both pass, the plan's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `approved_at` is stamped with the current instant, `scheduled_total_cents` is set to the sum of the generated `amount_cents`, the down payment is moved out of float and into commitment — the agreement's `unapplied_credit_cents` decreases by `down_payment_cents` and its `down_payment_applied_cents` increases by the same amount — the agreement's `payment_status` is recomputed, and both the plan's and the agreement's `version` advance by one.

Rejection is the divergent sibling of approval and its side-effect set is deliberately much thinner. Rejecting advances the plan `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and records the supplied `rejection_reason`; **no** installment rows are generated, the agreement's `unapplied_credit_cents` and `down_payment_applied_cents` are left untouched, its `payment_status` is not recomputed, and only the plan's own `version` advances by one. A plan that is not `PROPOSED` cannot be approved or rejected and yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PROPOSED`.

Cash arrives as **payments** remitted by the `Taxpayer` against an `agreement_id`, carrying an `amount_cents` (a positive integer) and an optional `payment_reference`. The payment is created with `status` `APPLIED`, `received_at` stamped with the current instant, and `reversal_reason` null. A payment ordinarily may not exceed the agreement's outstanding amount — its `amount_cents` may not be greater than `total_due_cents` less `paid_total_cents`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` — but that ceiling is deliberately not enforced while the agreement has no return in `FILED`, `ASSESSED`, `SETTLED` or `REINSTATED` status, because a pre-filing down payment has nothing to be measured against and lands wholly in unapplied credit.

Application follows an exact **waterfall**. The server walks the installments of the agreement's `ACTIVE` plan in ascending `seq_index` order, skipping any whose `status` is `PAID`, `VOIDED` or `DEFAULTED`. For each one it takes the smaller of the cash still remaining and that installment's `amount_cents` less its `paid_cents`; when that take is greater than zero it adds the take to `paid_cents`, appends an entry recording the `installment_id` and the `amount_cents` taken to the payment's `allocations` array, and reduces the remaining cash by the take; an installment whose `paid_cents` has reached its `amount_cents` transitions `SCHEDULED → PAID`; and the walk stops as soon as no cash remains. Earlier installments therefore fill completely before later ones receive anything. Whatever cash is left when the walk ends is recorded as the payment's `unapplied_delta_cents` and added to the agreement's `unapplied_credit_cents`; when the agreement has no `ACTIVE` plan at all, the whole payment becomes unapplied credit, which is precisely what a pre-approval down payment is. The agreement's `paid_total_cents` is then recomputed as the sum of `amount_cents` over its payments whose `status` is `APPLIED`, and the program's `collected_cents` is recomputed in turn.

The agreement's `payment_status` — a second, independent state dimension that never touches the agreement's own `status` — is then derived by testing four conditions in this order: `PAID_IN_FULL` when `paid_total_cents` is at least `total_due_cents`; otherwise `DELINQUENT` when any plan of the agreement has `status` `DEFAULTED`; otherwise `PARTIAL` when `paid_total_cents` is greater than 0; otherwise `UNPAID`. Two further cascades ride on full payment: every return in `ASSESSED` status advances `ASSESSED → SETTLED` once the agreement's `paid_total_cents` is at least its `total_due_cents` (a `FILED` return is never settled directly — it must be assessed first, per the return lifecycle in §3.5), and a plan every one of whose installments has reached `PAID` advances `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`. Each touched row's `version` advances by one, and the agreement's does too.

Reversal is the exact inverse, and only a `ProgramAdministrator` may perform it. Reversing advances the payment `APPLIED → REVERSED` and records the supplied `reversal_reason`. It requires the payment to be `APPLIED`, requires the agreement not to be `FULFILLED`, and requires that the reversal not undermine a committed down payment: the agreement's `paid_total_cents` less the payment's `amount_cents` must remain at least its `down_payment_applied_cents`, otherwise `ERR_REVERSAL_BREAKS_DOWN_PAYMENT`. Every entry in the payment's `allocations` array is then unwound — each named installment's `paid_cents` is reduced by the amount that entry recorded, and any installment that had reached `PAID` returns to `SCHEDULED`. The agreement's `unapplied_credit_cents` is reduced by the payment's `unapplied_delta_cents`, its `paid_total_cents` is reduced by the payment's `amount_cents`, and its `payment_status` is recomputed. The program's `collected_cents` is recomputed. Any return that had been `SETTLED` but whose agreement is no longer fully paid falls back — to `ASSESSED` when that return's `assessed_by_user_id` is not null, and to `FILED` when it is null. Both the payment's and the agreement's `version` advance by one.

Schedules age on the administrator's motion through a program-scoped sweep, which reports how much it moved as `marked_late_count`, `defaulted_installment_count` and `defaulted_plan_count`. The sweep runs in three steps. Step one advances every `SCHEDULED` installment whose `due_date` falls strictly before the current calendar date to `LATE`. Step two takes every `LATE` installment whose `due_date` plus a 30-day grace period still falls strictly before the current calendar date, advances it `LATE → DEFAULTED`, and advances its owning `ACTIVE` plan `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED` with `default_reason` set to the literal string `SWEEP_GRACE_EXPIRED`. Step three advances the remaining installments of a plan defaulted in step two — those still in `SCHEDULED` or `LATE` — to `DEFAULTED`. Agreements whose plan defaulted have `payment_status` set to `DELINQUENT`, and every touched installment, plan and agreement has its `version` advanced by one. An auditor may also default an `ACTIVE` plan directly, supplying a `default_reason`: that advances the plan `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED`, advances each of its installments in `SCHEDULED` or `LATE` to `DEFAULTED`, and sets the agreement's `payment_status` to `DELINQUENT` while leaving the agreement's own `status` untouched, advancing both the plan's and the agreement's `version` by one. A plan that is not `ACTIVE` cannot be defaulted and yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. A plan's terminal states are `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `COMPLETED` and `DEFAULTED`; an installment's are `PAID`, `DEFAULTED` and `VOIDED`.

### 3.7. Breach Remedies and Rescission

Breach is the program's enforcement teeth, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. A `ComplianceAuditor` declares breach against an `EXECUTED` agreement, supplying a `breach_reason`, and the declaration is only admissible if there is something to claw back: either the agreement's `waived_amount_cents` is greater than zero, or at least one of its returns is in `FILED`, `ASSESSED` or `SETTLED` status. An agreement with nothing to reinstate is refused with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REINSTATE`. Declaring advances the agreement `EXECUTED → BREACHED`, records the `breach_reason`, and then fires five hops in order.

The first hop reinstates the money: `reinstated_amount_cents` is set to the sum, over the agreement's returns in `FILED`, `ASSESSED` or `SETTLED` status, of `waived_penalty_cents` plus `waived_interest_cents`, and `waived_amount_cents` is zeroed. The second hop, caused by the first, rewrites each of those returns: each advances to `REINSTATED` with `payable_penalty_cents` set to its `gross_penalty_cents`, `payable_interest_cents` set to its `gross_interest_cents`, `waived_penalty_cents` and `waived_interest_cents` both zeroed, and `balance_due_cents` recomputed from the reported tax and the now-unwaived components; the agreement's `total_due_cents` is recomputed from the new figures. The third hop, caused by the second, is conditional: **when** the agreement has a plan in `ACTIVE` status **and** the recomputed `total_due_cents` now exceeds `down_payment_applied_cents` plus that plan's `scheduled_total_cents` — that is, when the reinstated liability no longer fits the schedule the taxpayer agreed to — the plan advances `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED` with `default_reason` set to the literal string `BREACH_CASCADE`, each of its installments in `SCHEDULED` or `LATE` status advances to `VOIDED` with its `paid_cents` preserved untouched, and the agreement's `payment_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`. The fourth hop, caused by the third, escalates: an `EnforcementAction` is created directly in `ISSUED` with `origin` set to `BREACH_CASCADE`, `action_type` set to `ASSESSMENT`, `base_tax_cents` set to the agreement's `reinstated_amount_cents`, `issued_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM`, and `assessed_penalty_cents` computed by the post-program penalty rules of §3.9 from a taxpayer snapshot taken **before** the fifth hop runs. The fifth hop, caused by the fourth, recomputes the taxpayer's `open_enforcement_action_count` and `compliance_behavior_score`, which now absorb the issued-action penalty. Finally the program's `reinstated_total_cents` and `waived_total_cents` are recomputed and the agreement's `version` advances by one.

Rescission exists because breach declarations are sometimes wrong, and it is specified as an **exact compensation** of the cascade above rather than an approximate rollback. An auditor rescinds a `BREACHED` agreement with a `rescind_reason`; the program must be `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, so a fully reconciled program can no longer be disturbed. Rescinding advances the agreement `BREACHED → EXECUTED` and nulls `breach_reason`, then fires four compensating hops. The first restores every return in `REINSTATED` status: its `waived_penalty_cents` and `waived_interest_cents` are recomputed by the waiver rollup of §3.5, its `payable_penalty_cents`, `payable_interest_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are recomputed from those, and its `status` becomes `SETTLED` when the agreement's `paid_total_cents` is at least its `total_due_cents` and `FILED` otherwise. The second sets `reinstated_amount_cents` back to 0 and recomputes `waived_amount_cents` and `total_due_cents`. The third restores the schedule: every plan of the agreement whose `status` is `DEFAULTED` **and** whose `default_reason` is exactly `BREACH_CASCADE` returns `DEFAULTED → ACTIVE` with `default_reason` nulled, each of its `VOIDED` installments returns to `SCHEDULED` with its `paid_cents` preserved, and the agreement's `payment_status` is recomputed. A plan defaulted for any other reason — an auditor's own motion, or the sweep's `SWEEP_GRACE_EXPIRED` — stays defaulted, because rescission compensates only what breach caused. The fourth abates the escalation: every enforcement action of this agreement whose `origin` is `BREACH_CASCADE` and whose `status` is `ISSUED` advances `ISSUED → ABATED` with `abatement_reason` set to the literal string `BREACH_RESCINDED`, and the taxpayer's `open_enforcement_action_count` and `compliance_behavior_score` are recomputed. The program's `reinstated_total_cents` and `waived_total_cents` are recomputed, and the agreement's `version` advances by one. When the dust settles, the waiver ledger identity of §2.9 must hold again, and the agreement may cycle back into breach later if the taxpayer defaults again. Rescinding an agreement that is not `BREACHED` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_BREACHED`.

### 3.8. Closing the Window, Reconciliation and Revenue Realization

Closing is the administrator's act of shutting the program, and it may only happen once the window has genuinely elapsed: the program must be `OPEN` and the current instant must be at or after `window_close_at` — inclusive at the exact instant — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. Closing advances the program `OPEN → CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` with the current instant, and runs two independent chains of cascade. Closing is the **only** producer of an expired pre-clearance, a lapsed agreement, and a raised non-participant flag.

The primary chain has four hops. Hop one expires the unfinished negotiations: every pre-clearance of this program whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `TERMS_OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` advances to `EXPIRED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, punishes the ones that had already named their client: for each request that just became `EXPIRED` and whose `disclosed_taxpayer_id` is not null, that taxpayer's `nonparticipant_flag` is set true and the program's `nonparticipant_count` is recomputed. A request that expired while still anonymous flags nobody — the department never learned who to pursue. Hop three, caused by hop two, re-resolves the `escalation_tier` of each taxpayer whose flag just flipped, by the tier rules of §3.2, so a flagged taxpayer with at least one `OPEN` case lands in `T2` and one with none lands in `T1`. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes each such taxpayer's `compliance_behavior_score` under the `HISTORICAL` variant, which now carries the 25-point non-participant penalty.

The secondary chain has two hops and runs over agreements that never got signed. Hop one advances every agreement of this program whose `status` is `DRAFTED` to `LAPSED`. Hop two, caused by that transition, advances every return of a lapsed agreement whose `status` is `DRAFT` to `VOIDED`. Every pre-clearance, taxpayer, agreement and return touched by either chain has its `version` advanced by one, as does the program itself.

Reconciliation freezes the accounting and advances the program `CLOSED → RECONCILED`, its terminal state. It requires the program to be `CLOSED` (otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_CLOSED`) and requires that no agreement of the program remains in `DRAFTED` status — after a correct close there should be none, and the check is a guard against reconciling over unfinished business. Reconciling sets `final_realization_pct` to the realization percentage computed at that instant, frozen there permanently, and advances the program's `version` by one. Before reconciliation `final_realization_pct` is null.

**Revenue realization** is reported live for any program and is what reconciliation freezes. Collections are the sum of `amount_cents` over every payment whose `status` is `APPLIED` across the program's agreements, reported as `collected_cents` alongside the program's own `revenue_forecast_cents`. The `realization_pct` is collections divided by forecast, rounded half up to four decimal places. The `forecast_status` band is then derived from that **rounded** value: `ON_TRACK` when `realization_pct` is at least 0.9000, otherwise `WATCH` when it is at least 0.7000, otherwise `SHORTFALL`. Because the rounding precedes the comparison, a raw ratio of 0.89999974 rounds to 0.9000 and reports `ON_TRACK`. The same report carries `waived_total_cents`, `reinstated_total_cents`, `participant_count` and `nonparticipant_count`, plus the compliance behavior analysis: `avg_historical_score` is the mean of the `HISTORICAL` scores of the program's participant taxpayers and `avg_program_adjusted_score` is the mean of their `PROGRAM_ADJUSTED` scores, each rounded half up to a whole integer. The gap between the two averages is the program's own argument for its existence.

### 3.9. Post-Program Enforcement Escalation

Once a program is closed, the department turns on everyone who stayed silent. A `ComplianceAuditor` drafts an **enforcement action** naming the `taxpayer_id` being escalated against, the `program_id` whose post-program regime applies, the `action_type`, and the `base_tax_cents` the penalty will be computed on (a positive integer). An optional `agreement_id` is supplied when the action arose from a breached agreement and omitted for a pure non-participant escalation. The action is created with `status` `DRAFT`, `origin` `MANUAL`, `created_by_user_id` set from the drafting auditor, `issued_by_user_id`, `issued_at` and `abatement_reason` null.

Drafting immediately snapshots the taxpayer's posture and computes the penalty, so that later changes to the taxpayer's record cannot retroactively move an assessment. Three snapshot fields are recorded. `escalation_tier_applied` captures the taxpayer's `escalation_tier` at that instant. `multiplier_applied` is the program's `post_program_penalty_multiplier` when the taxpayer's `nonparticipant_flag` is true, and 1.00 otherwise — the escalation is reserved for people who could have come forward and did not. `behavior_adjustment_applied` is 0.05 when the snapshotted `compliance_behavior_score` is strictly below 60, and 0.00 otherwise.

The **assessed penalty** then composes an additive rate, a multiplicative escalation, an upper cap and a lower floor, and the order in which they compose matters. The tier rate is 0.10 for tier `T0`, 0.20 for tier `T1` and 0.35 for tier `T2`. A raw amount is computed as `base_tax_cents` multiplied by the sum of the tier rate and `behavior_adjustment_applied`, then multiplied by `multiplier_applied`, and rounded half up to whole cents. A cap amount is computed as `base_tax_cents` multiplied by 0.50 and rounded half up to whole cents. The `assessed_penalty_cents` is then the greater of a floor of 10,000 cents and the lesser of the raw amount and the cap amount — the cap is applied first and the floor last, on already-rounded integers, so on a small base the floor legitimately beats the cap and the assessed penalty may exceed half the base tax.

Issuance advances the action `DRAFT → ISSUED`. It requires the action to be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_ACTION_NOT_DRAFT`), requires the governing program to be `CLOSED` or `RECONCILED` — post-program escalation is strictly post-program, and an attempt while the program is still open or still a draft is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_CLOSED` — and requires the issuing auditor's `X-User-ID` to differ from the action's `created_by_user_id`. Issuing records `issued_by_user_id` and stamps `issued_at` with the current instant, then recomputes the taxpayer's `open_enforcement_action_count` and `compliance_behavior_score`, which now absorbs the 8-point issued-action penalty. Abatement advances the action `ISSUED → ABATED`, its terminal state, records the supplied `abatement_reason`, and recomputes the same two taxpayer fields with the issued-action penalty removed. An action created by the breach cascade of §3.7 skips the draft stage entirely — it appears already `ISSUED`, signed by `SYSTEM`, and is abated by the rescission cascade with the reason `BREACH_RESCINDED` rather than by an auditor's hand. Every issuance or abatement advances the action's `version` by one.
