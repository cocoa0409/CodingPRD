# Security Awareness & Phishing Simulation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise security awareness programs fail in two directions at once. They fail upward, when a compliance team can produce a spreadsheet of completion percentages but cannot say whether anybody actually became harder to phish; and they fail downward, when a simulation program becomes so aggressive or so tone-deaf that employees stop trusting internal mail altogether and the reporting channel dries up. The API described here exists to make both failure modes measurable and preventable inside one system: it owns the training curriculum and its deadlines, the escalation and access consequences that give those deadlines teeth, the design and governance of phishing simulation campaigns, the per-employee interaction record that comes back from the mail gateway, and the analytics that correlate the program's reporting culture against real incident volume.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that **reporting is a first-class metric, not a consolation prize** — a person who clicks a simulated lure and then reports it is counted as a clicker *and* as a reporter, so the program-level click rate and report rate are independent series that may legitimately sum to more than one. The second is that **realism is bounded by decency**: pretexts may exploit urgency, authority, curiosity and reward, but a simulation may never impersonate a layoff notice, a medical result, a compensation bonus, a charity appeal or a family emergency, and the system refuses to approve any template or campaign that does.

The service is mounted under the `/awareness` path prefix. It is a compliance-audit system first and a workflow orchestrator second: every consequential mutation is traceable, every derived number is reproducible from the stored rows and the server's logical clock, and every compensating action (an extension, a void, an offboarding, a cancellation) is required to restore the ledgers it unwinds to exactly the value they held before the action it compensates.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the service. Where §3 describes what the business does, this section describes the shape of every request and response, the identity model, the arithmetic conventions, and the cross-cutting invariants that no workflow is allowed to break.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Six roles exist, and their spelling is exact: `ProgramAdmin`, `CampaignAuthor`, `CampaignApprover`, `Manager`, `Learner`, and `SecurityAnalyst`. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those six strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header that is present but consists only of whitespace counts as empty; otherwise surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both header values before the role is matched against the six names and before `X-User-ID` is used in any ownership comparison. When both headers are well formed but the declared role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the addressed endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide into two identity families, and the distinction governs every ownership check in the system.

`Manager` and `Learner` are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Manager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee` row; ownership checks compare that value against the foreign-key field of the resource being touched — specifically against the `manager_id` of the employee who owns the assignment being extended, and against the `manager_id` recorded on the escalation notice being acknowledged. When `X-User-Role` is `Learner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Employee`, and ownership checks compare it to the `employee_id` of the assignment being started, submitted or retaken.

`ProgramAdmin`, `CampaignAuthor`, `CampaignApprover` and `SecurityAnalyst` are **opaque**. For each of these four, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is recorded where a workflow demands attribution — a campaign's `author_user_id` is the `X-User-ID` of the `CampaignAuthor` who created it, and `reviewer_user_id` is the `X-User-ID` of the `CampaignApprover` who most recently approved or rejected it — but those two fields hold opaque operator identifiers and are never `Employee` identifiers.

Three ownership failures are distinguished by their own error identifiers. A `Learner` acting on an assignment that is not theirs is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `Manager` who is neither the assignment employee's `manager_id` (when granting an extension) nor the notice's `manager_id` (when acknowledging) is refused with `ERR_NOT_DIRECT_REPORT_MANAGER` (HTTP 403). A `CampaignAuthor` whose `X-User-ID` is not the campaign's `author_user_id`, on submit-for-review, on scheduling, or on cancellation, is refused with `ERR_NOT_CAMPAIGN_AUTHOR` (HTTP 403). Ownership is checked only on those mutating actions: read and list endpoints apply role authorization alone, so a `Manager` or `Learner` whose role appears in an endpoint's authorized-role list may read any employee, any assignment or any escalation notice that endpoint exposes, not merely the ones they own, and the list endpoints impose no implicit ownership filter.

Segregation of duties is structural rather than advisory. A `CampaignApprover` may never approve or reject a campaign whose `author_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). Likewise, the `Learner` who completes a module is never the `ProgramAdmin` who assigned or voided it, and the `ProgramAdmin` who runs sweeps and applies consequences is never the `Manager` who grants relief from them.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from real wall-clock time, at the moment the response envelope is built — so every response produced without an intervening clock move carries the same timestamp, and the response of the call that moves the clock already reports the new instant. Error envelopes stamp it the same way.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent from the `meta` of every single-object response and of every error response.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a `limit` or `offset` whose text is not an integer literal. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is legal and yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still between the calls that move it, many rows legitimately share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by ascending creation order, so rows minted at the same clock instant come back in the order they were created. The one documented exception is the per-delivery interaction log, which is ordered by `sequence_index` ascending. List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows match the filters before paging is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Every filter named on a list endpoint is optional, filters combine with AND, and query parameters the endpoint does not name are ignored. A filter whose value is drawn from a closed vocabulary — a state, a department, an assignment reason, an outcome class, a window type — is validated on the way in, and a value outside that vocabulary is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than silently matching nothing. Filters that name an identifier are the opposite: they are matched, never resolved, so an `employee_id`, `manager_id`, `module_id`, `assignment_id`, `campaign_id` or similar value that matches no row simply matches no rows and returns an empty page rather than a not-found error.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Every other instant the service emits — `updated_at`, `published_at`, `due_at`, `assigned_at`, `sent_at`, `applied_at`, `delivered_at`, `swept_at` and the rest — is rendered in exactly that same shape, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, always at UTC and always with three fractional digits. Wherever this document calls a scalar field empty, unset or cleared — an unstamped instant, an unrecorded reason code, an absent identifier, an uncollected note — the field is still present in the response body, carrying JSON `null` rather than an empty string and never simply omitted; an array field called empty is emitted as an empty array. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical clock on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Whenever the narrative in §3 says that an operation "bumps" or "advances" a row's `version`, it means that single increment together with a refreshed `updated_at`.

Three record types are deliberately append-only and therefore carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: `InteractionEvent`, `IncidentRecord`, and `CommunicationWindow`. Each of these exposes only `id` and `created_at` among the common properties, and none of them has an update endpoint. An interaction event, once appended, is immutable — later events on the same delivery are new rows, never edits of an existing one.

Version increments cascade. Because most of this domain's interesting state lives in derived fields on `Employee`, a single write against an assignment, a delivery, a consequence or a campaign routinely bumps `Employee.version` as well as the version of the row directly addressed. Read-only recomputation is the exception: reads that refresh a derived field (an employee's tenure band and risk scores, an assignment's overdue-day count and next reminder instant, a campaign's four rates, an intervention's live tier and cadence) never bump `version` and never refresh `updated_at`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this service is evaluated against a server-held logical UTC clock, never against real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. State lives in memory only: every boot starts from a completely empty registry with the clock at that instant, nothing is seeded, and nothing survives a restart. Deadlines, extension windows, reminder instants, overdue-day counts, rolling repeat-clicker windows, quiet periods, blackout comparisons and event-ordering checks are all resolved against this clock.

An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock: it accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and responds with exactly two fields — `current_utc`, holding the instant just installed, and `previous_utc`, holding the instant the clock held immediately before the move — so setting the clock to where it already stands echoes the two as equal. Moving the clock mutates no entity and implies no sweep — time passing is not, by itself, an event; the compliance sweep must be run explicitly for overdue marking and escalation to occur.

Day arithmetic throughout the system adds whole days to an instant without truncating to midnight, so an assignment created at 09:00Z with a fourteen-day window falls due at 09:00Z fourteen days later. Where a count of elapsed days is needed, the elapsed interval in seconds is divided by 86,400 and floored — never rounded — so a deadline missed by twenty-three hours and fifty-nine minutes still yields an overdue-day count of zero. Calendar hire dates are supplied as `YYYY-MM-DD` and are always interpreted at UTC midnight; that pattern is the only accepted form for a calendar date, and an instant supplied where a date is expected is malformed. Every instant a client supplies — `to_iso8601`, `scheduled_send_at`, `occurred_at`, the effectiveness-report period bounds, the incident-list bounds — must carry a date, a time of day down to whole seconds, and a zone designator that is either `Z` or a numeric UTC offset such as `+02:00`; fractional seconds are optional and up to nine digits are accepted; a value carrying an offset is normalised to UTC before any comparison is made. Anything else — a bare calendar date among them — is rejected as malformed.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Two precision regimes coexist and must not be confused. **Rates and correlations** — a campaign's `click_rate`, `report_rate`, `credential_rate` and `resilience_ratio`, every segment rate on a scorecard, every per-month report rate in the effectiveness report, and the effectiveness correlation itself — are carried to 4 decimal places, rounded half up. **Risk scores** — `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` — are carried to 2 decimal places, rounded half up, and clamped to the closed interval from 0.00 to 100.00.

Rounding happens exactly once, at the end, and the order is pinned: a rate is computed by dividing raw integer counts and only then rounded. A program-level rate is never the average of its segment rates. If seven evaluable deliveries produce three clicks, split as two clicks out of three deliveries in one department and one click out of four in another, the program-level click rate is 0.4286 — the result of dividing three by seven — and not 0.4584, which is what averaging the two rounded segment rates would produce.

Where a denominator can be zero, the result is `null` rather than zero: a campaign with no evaluable deliveries reports `null` for all four rates, and a scorecard segment with no evaluable deliveries reports `null` for each of its rates rather than 0.0000.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Reservation-Bearing Writes

Three write operations can silently destroy work that the client never observed, because each of them legitimately supersedes an existing row. All three are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency field that the client must supply as a mandatory, non-optional part of the request body. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

Creating a curriculum assignment through `POST /awareness/curriculum-assignments` requires `expected_employee_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Employee`. If that value does not equal the employee row's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard matters because a create for an (`employee_id`, `module_id`) pair that already has a non-terminal assignment supersedes that assignment, voiding it — so without the guard, a client acting on a stale view would discard an assignment it had never seen.

Applying an access consequence to an employee likewise requires `expected_employee_version`, matched against the same employee row's current `version`, and a mismatch is likewise rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the reason is arithmetic as well as referential: a prior active consequence of the same type is superseded by the new one, and the derived severity rung depends on the posture the client believed the employee held, so a stale view produces the wrong rung.

Scheduling or rescheduling a campaign requires `expected_campaign_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PhishingCampaign`. A mismatch with the campaign's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a fresh schedule releases every delivery the previous reservation held, a client acting on a stale campaign view would destroy a reservation set another operator had just built.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Validation

Path parameters are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. Each resource family has its own not-found identifier: `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND` cover employees, training modules, curriculum assignments, campaigns, phishing templates and simulation deliveries respectively, all at HTTP 404. Path identifiers for the remaining families — escalation notices, access consequences, repeat-clicker interventions, incident records and communication windows — resolve to the generic `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved with the same family-specific identifiers: an unknown `employee_id`, `manager_id` or `root_cause_employee_id` yields `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `module_id` yields `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `template_id` yields `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`. Body-key resolution of this kind is performed on employee registration, on assignment creation, on campaign creation, on incident recording, and on coaching assignment. Two routing-level failures sit outside the families: a request whose path matches no route the service declares is refused with the generic `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request that names a method the matched path does not support is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carried on HTTP 405. A trailing slash on any path is tolerated and resolves to the same route.

Because many operations are guarded by several checks at once, the evaluation order is fixed for the whole service. It runs as follows, and the first check to fail produces the response:

1. Header authentication — both headers present and the role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorization against the endpoint's authorized-role list (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
3. Request well-formedness (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`): required body fields present, enum members recognised, arrays free of duplicate entries, integers inside their declared ranges, `score_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, `extension_days` at least 1, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0, `end_at` strictly after `start_at`, and `period_end` strictly after `period_start`. Non-ISO-8601 instants and wrong JSON types fall here too, and a JSON boolean supplied where an integer is expected is one of those wrong types. Body keys an endpoint does not name are ignored rather than rejected. A request that carries no body at all is read as an empty JSON object, so each of its required fields is reported missing by this same error; a body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, is likewise rejected here on HTTP 400.
4. Existence of every path-templated resource, left to right.
5. Existence of every body-supplied foreign key.
6. Uniqueness: `email` on `Employee` and `code` on `TrainingModule` must be unique across all rows, producing `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_MODULE_CODE` (HTTP 409). Email uniqueness is case-insensitive and holds regardless of the other row's `employment_status`; the comparison also ignores whitespace surrounding either value, while the address itself is stored and echoed exactly as the client supplied it. Module `code` uniqueness, by contrast, compares the two strings exactly, so two codes differing only in letter case or in surrounding whitespace may coexist.
7. Learner ownership of an assignment (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`).
8. Manager ownership of a direct report or of a notice (`ERR_NOT_DIRECT_REPORT_MANAGER`).
9. Campaign-author ownership (`ERR_NOT_CAMPAIGN_AUTHOR`).
10. Optimistic-concurrency comparison of `expected_employee_version` or `expected_campaign_version` against the named parent (`ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION`).
11. State legality: the addressed resource must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. The identifier depends on the family — `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_INTERACTABLE`, `ERR_INTERVENTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MODULE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_CONSEQUENCE_ALREADY_LIFTED` — and all of them are HTTP 409. This check applies to every action endpoint.
12. Employment liveness: the target employee's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED` (HTTP 409). This applies to assignment creation, to starting an assignment, to offboarding an already-offboarded employee, and to applying an access consequence.

Everything after step 12 is domain-specific and is described where the workflow lives, but the relative order continues to matter and is stated here once, in ascending order of evaluation: harmful-pretext screening; segregation of duties; template usability; campaign-tier-versus-template-tier; module publication status; coaching-module type; role eligibility; credential-capture and prior-click requirements; interaction event ordering; schedule-in-the-future; blackout conflict; quiet-period conflict; empty cohort; communication-policy acknowledgement; extension budget; extension window; report-period sanity; and launch-before-schedule. The pointed consequence of this ordering is that **safety outranks everything domain-specific**: a harmful-theme campaign is refused no matter who asks and no matter what state its template is in, so `ERR_HARMFUL_PRETEXT_THEME` surfaces ahead of both `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` and `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_APPROVED`.

### 2.9. Conserved Ledgers and Compensating Actions

Two integer fields on `Employee` are ledgers rather than statistics, and the system holds them exact rather than eventually consistent.

`reserved_simulation_slots` counts that employee's simulation deliveries currently in state `QUEUED`. The invariant is global: the sum of `reserved_simulation_slots` across all employees always equals the number of `SimulationDelivery` rows in state `QUEUED`, both before and after every operation. Scheduling a cohort increments the field once per employee reserved; releasing a reservation — through cancellation, through a reschedule that supersedes the prior set, through delivery at launch, or through offboarding — decrements it once per delivery released.

`active_suspension_count` counts that employee's access consequences that are simultaneously in state `ACTIVE` and of type `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION`. Every path out of `ACTIVE`, whether an earned lift or a compensating void, recomputes it, and the compensating actions of the escalation cascade (voiding an assignment, granting an extension, offboarding the employee) must restore it to the value it held before escalation created the consequence.

Compensation is asymmetric on purpose. A consequence that is genuinely discharged moves to `LIFTED`; a consequence that should never have existed moves to `VOIDED`. Both are terminal, both stamp `lifted_at`, and both record a `lift_reason_code` — but only the second class represents the system admitting that a cascade fired in error.

### 2.10. Realism Boundaries and Communication Policy

Simulation realism is a program goal, not a licence. A template's `theme_tags` array, which defaults to empty, records the psychological levers its pretext pulls, drawn from `URGENCY`, `AUTHORITY`, `CURIOSITY`, `REWARD`, `BONUS_COMPENSATION`, `LAYOFF_NOTICE`, `MEDICAL_RESULT`, `CHARITY_APPEAL` and `FAMILY_EMERGENCY`, with duplicate entries rejected as a malformed request. The first four are always permitted. The last five are the prohibited themes, and they are prohibited in exactly that declaration order: `BONUS_COMPENSATION`, `LAYOFF_NOTICE`, `MEDICAL_RESULT`, `CHARITY_APPEAL`, `FAMILY_EMERGENCY`.

The harmful-theme screen computes `harmful_flag_hits` as the list of prohibited themes that the template's `theme_tags` actually contains, ordered as the prohibited list is declared above rather than in the order the author happened to type them. The screen passes only when `harmful_flag_hits` is empty. It is deliberately **advisory at creation and blocking at approval**: creating a template with prohibited tags succeeds and records the hits, while approving that template for use, or approving any campaign that references it, is refused with `ERR_HARMFUL_PRETEXT_THEME` (HTTP 422).

The other half of the realism boundary is disclosure. Employees are told in advance that simulations happen and are debriefed afterwards, and the system enforces both ends of that promise through two gates on the campaign lifecycle: the author must have set `communication_policy_ack` to true before a campaign can enter review, and a non-empty `debrief_note` must be supplied when a running campaign is closed. Either omission is refused with `ERR_COMMUNICATION_POLICY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (HTTP 422). A third mechanism protects the organisation's own quiet periods: a `CommunicationWindow` of `window_type` `SIMULATION_BLACKOUT` or `ANNOUNCEMENT_MORATORIUM`, carrying a `start_at`, an `end_at` strictly after it, a free-text `reason` that must be a string with at least one non-whitespace character, and an optional `scope_department` (where the absence of a department means every department), declares an interval in which simulation traffic is restricted. A `ProgramAdmin` declares such a window and it is thereafter immutable; declared windows are listable with filters on `window_type` and `scope_department` alongside `limit` and `offset`. Nothing forbids two windows from overlapping, and of the two types only `SIMULATION_BLACKOUT` is consulted by the scheduling gate of §3.8 — an `ANNOUNCEMENT_MORATORIUM` window is recorded and readable but blocks no operation the service offers.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Employee Registry and Three Independent State Axes

Registering someone into the awareness program requires a work `email`, a `full_name`, an organizational `department`, a job `role_code`, and a calendar `hire_date`; an optional `manager_id` names the `Employee` who will receive escalation notices about them. That identifier need only resolve to an existing `Employee` row: neither the named person's `role_code` nor their `employment_status` is examined, so anyone already registered may be named as a manager. A `hire_date` later than the current logical clock is likewise accepted rather than refused. Departments are drawn from `ENGINEERING`, `FINANCE`, `SALES`, `HR`, `IT` and `EXECUTIVE`, and the same six values are used for campaign targeting, for scorecard segmentation, for incident attribution and for scoping communication windows. Role codes are drawn from `IC`, `ENGINEER`, `FINANCE_APPROVER`, `IT_ADMIN`, `EXECUTIVE` and `MANAGER`, and they drive eligibility for role-specific curriculum. The `email` value is unique across every row in the registry regardless of employment state, and it must be non-empty; beyond that non-emptiness no address-format validation is applied, so any non-blank string is a legal address. Leaving `manager_id` unset is meaningful rather than merely tidy: an employee with no manager never receives an escalation notice, which — as §3.4 explains — silently suppresses the access consequences that would otherwise follow.

A newly registered employee starts with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE`, `compliance_state` of `COMPLIANT`, `access_posture` of `NONE`, `active_suspension_count` of 0, `repeat_clicker_tier` of 0, `consecutive_clean_simulations` of 0, `reserved_simulation_slots` of 0, and both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` at 0.00. The `tenure_band` is computed immediately.

**Tenure band.** The number of whole days since hire is the interval from UTC midnight on the `hire_date` to the current logical clock, expressed in seconds, divided by 86,400 and floored. Fewer than 90 such days makes the `tenure_band` `NEW_HIRE` — and because that count is a plain floored difference rather than a clamped one, it goes negative for a `hire_date` that lies in the future, so an employee hired in the future is `NEW_HIRE` as well; from 90 up to but not including 365 days it is `EARLY`; from 365 up to but not including 1,095 days — that is, up to but not including the third anniversary — it is `ESTABLISHED`; and 1,095 days or more makes it `VETERAN`. Because the band is a function of the clock, it is recomputed on every read that surfaces it and again at cohort resolution when a campaign reserves its audience.

An employee carries three state axes that move independently of one another, and conflating them is the single most common way to get this domain wrong.

**Axis one, employment.** `employment_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `OFFBOARDED`, moves only forward, and moves only when a `ProgramAdmin` offboards the person. Offboarding an employee who is already `OFFBOARDED` is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`. The offboarding cascade is broad and must run in full: every curriculum assignment of that employee whose state is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED` becomes `VOIDED` with a `void_reason_code` of `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`; every escalation notice belonging to one of those assignments whose state is still `SENT` becomes `EXPIRED`; every access consequence of that employee whose state is `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED` with `lift_reason_code` of `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED` and `lifted_at` set to the current logical clock; every simulation delivery of that employee still in state `QUEUED` becomes `EXCLUDED` with `excluded_reason` of `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`, decrementing `reserved_simulation_slots` once for each; and the derived fields are then recomputed, which drives `access_posture` to `NONE`, `active_suspension_count` to 0, `compliance_state` to `COMPLIANT`, and both risk scores to their new values. The employee's `version` is bumped once for the operation, and every child row the cascade rewrites — each voided assignment, each expired notice, each voided consequence, each excluded delivery — has its own `version` bumped by one as well.

**Axis two, training compliance.** `compliance_state` is `COMPLIANT`, `AT_RISK` or `DELINQUENT`, and unlike employment it moves in both directions. It is recomputed as a side effect of every curriculum-assignment state change, of every compliance sweep, and of offboarding. The rule reads the employee's complete set of assignments, regardless of module or reason: if any of them is in state `ESCALATED`, the employee is `DELINQUENT`; otherwise, if any is in state `OVERDUE`, the employee is `AT_RISK`; otherwise the employee is `COMPLIANT`. Assignments in `COMPLETED`, `VOIDED`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `FAILED_ATTEMPT` contribute nothing at all, and `ESCALATED` dominates `OVERDUE`. Concretely: moving from `COMPLIANT` to `AT_RISK` requires at least one assignment to be `OVERDUE` while none is `ESCALATED`; moving from `AT_RISK` to `DELINQUENT` requires at least one assignment to be `ESCALATED`; and returning from `DELINQUENT` to `COMPLIANT` requires that no assignment of that employee remains in either `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`. Each of those three moves bumps the employee's `version` by one.

**Axis three, access posture.** `access_posture` is `NONE`, `WARNED`, `ELEVATED_REVIEW` or `SUSPENDED`, and it is a pure function of the employee's currently active access consequences: it is `NONE` when none is active, `WARNED` when the highest active severity rank is 1, `ELEVATED_REVIEW` when it is 2, and `SUSPENDED` when it is 3. Each of those four postures is reached by a system recomputation triggered whenever a consequence enters or leaves the `ACTIVE` state, and each recomputation that changes the posture bumps `Employee.version` by one. Reaching `SUSPENDED` also sets `active_suspension_count` to the number of that employee's active suspension-type consequences, and dropping to `NONE` — which requires that no consequence of the employee remains `ACTIVE` — sets `active_suspension_count` back to 0. Discharging the topmost consequence while a lower one is still active does not return the employee to `NONE`; it moves the posture down to the rank of whatever remains active.

The independence of axes two and three is deliberate and load-bearing. Completing the last escalated module returns `compliance_state` to `COMPLIANT` inside that same request, but `access_posture` only moves when the underlying access-consequence rows themselves leave `ACTIVE`. An employee may therefore be `COMPLIANT` and `SUSPENDED` at the same instant.

Two further capabilities round out the registry. A `ProgramAdmin` may change an employee's `role_code`, which alters eligibility for role-specific modules on *future* assignments only — assignments already created are untouched — and bumps `Employee.version` by one. Reading a single employee surfaces both risk-score variants and all three axes, and refreshes `tenure_band`, `repeat_clicker_tier`, `consecutive_clean_simulations`, `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` without bumping `version`. Listing employees is the reverse-lookup channel for the self-referential manager link and accepts filters on `manager_id`, `department`, `compliance_state`, `access_posture` and `employment_status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Curriculum Modules and the Creation of Assignments

A training module is authored by a `ProgramAdmin` with a unique short `code`, a human-readable `title`, and a `module_type` chosen from `NEW_HIRE`, `ANNUAL`, `ROLE_SPECIFIC` and `REMEDIAL`. Several optional knobs shape how the module behaves once assigned. `applies_to_role_codes` lists the role codes eligible for it, where an empty array means every role is eligible — but a `ROLE_SPECIFIC` module with an empty list is a contradiction, and while it may be created it may not be published. `criticality` is `STANDARD` by default and may be `HIGH`, which halves the base deadline window as §3.2's due-date rule describes. `passing_score_pct` defaults to 80, must itself lie between 0 and 100 inclusive, and is the inclusive minimum score that turns a submitted attempt into a completion. `reminder_offsets_days` defaults to the ordered list 7, 3, 1 and −2, and each entry positions one reminder relative to the effective deadline, so the negative entry is a reminder that fires two days *after* the deadline has passed; any array of integers is accepted in its place, negative and repeated entries included, and an explicitly empty array means no reminder is ever due for assignments of that module. `escalate_after_reminders` defaults to 3 and is recorded on the module as informational cadence guidance for program owners — escalation itself is driven entirely by the ladder in §3.4, not by this number. `max_extension_days` defaults to 10 and is the cumulative cap on all extension days ever granted against assignments of this module; it must be at least 0, and `escalate_after_reminders` must be at least 1.

Modules move through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and `RETIRED`. A new module is `DRAFT` and cannot be assigned. Publishing it requires that its `status` is still `DRAFT` and that it is not a `ROLE_SPECIFIC` module with an empty `applies_to_role_codes`; on success the `status` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the module's `version` is bumped. Retiring requires that the `status` is `PUBLISHED`; it stamps `retired_at` and bumps `version`. Publishing anything that is not `DRAFT`, or retiring anything that is not `PUBLISHED`, is refused with `ERR_MODULE_STATE_INVALID`. Retirement does not touch existing assignments — they survive untouched in whatever state they hold, but they become unstartable, and any attempt to start or retake one is refused with `ERR_MODULE_RETIRED`.

Assigning a module to an employee is a `ProgramAdmin` action that takes `employee_id`, `module_id`, an `assignment_reason` drawn from `NEW_HIRE`, `ANNUAL`, `ROLE_CHANGE` and `REMEDIAL`, and the mandatory `expected_employee_version` described in §2.7. Four domain gates apply beyond the generic ones. The referenced module's `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or the create is refused with `ERR_MODULE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The target employee's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`. The module's `applies_to_role_codes` must either be empty or contain the employee's current `role_code`, or the create is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_MODULE`. And the supplied `expected_employee_version` must match, or the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION`.

**The due-date rule.** A new assignment stamps `assigned_at` with the current logical clock and derives its deadline from the reason it was raised. The base window is 14 days for a `NEW_HIRE` reason, 30 days for `ANNUAL`, 21 days for `ROLE_CHANGE`, and 7 days for `REMEDIAL`. If the referenced module's `criticality` is `HIGH`, the window becomes the greater of 3 days and the base window halved with any fraction discarded — so a `HIGH`-criticality annual module is due in 15 days, a `HIGH`-criticality remedial module in 3 rather than the 3.5 that halving alone would give, and a `HIGH`-criticality role-change module in 10 rather than 10.5. Otherwise the window is the base window unchanged. The `due_at` instant is `assigned_at` advanced by that many whole days, and `effective_due_at` is `due_at` advanced by a further `granted_extension_days` days. `granted_extension_days` starts at 0 and only a manager's extension ever changes it. The sweep and every overdue calculation read `effective_due_at` and nothing else.

**Supersession.** Assigning a module to an employee who already holds a non-terminal assignment for that same module is legitimate and is treated as a replacement rather than a duplicate. The older row becomes `VOIDED`, its `void_reason_code` is set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ASSIGNMENT`, and its `superseded_by_assignment_id` is set to the `id` of the newly created row; the full void compensation of §3.5 runs against it, expiring its `SENT` escalation notices and voiding its `ACTIVE` access consequences — those consequences record a `lift_reason_code` of `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ASSIGNMENT` rather than the `ASSIGNMENT_VOIDED` an explicit void writes. The employee's `compliance_state` and both risk scores are then recomputed and `Employee.version` is bumped by one. This is precisely the scenario the optimistic-concurrency guard exists to protect: without it, a second create issued from a stale view would quietly discard an assignment the client had never observed. That single `Employee.version` bump is not peculiar to supersession — every successful create bumps it by exactly one whether or not a prior assignment was replaced, so a client issuing two creates in a row must re-read the employee between them to learn the `expected_employee_version` the second create needs.

A newly created assignment starts in state `ASSIGNED` with `attempt_count` at 0, `last_score_pct` unset, `completed_at` unset, `reminders_sent` at 0, `last_reminder_at` unset, `escalation_level` at 0, `days_overdue` at 0, and `next_reminder_due_at` computed from the cadence rule in §3.4. Reading a single assignment surfaces `effective_due_at`, `days_overdue` and `next_reminder_due_at`, recomputing the latter two on read without bumping `version`. Listing assignments is the reverse-lookup channel for the employee link, the module link and the supersession link, and accepts filters on `employee_id`, `module_id`, `state`, `assignment_reason` and `superseded_by_assignment_id`, with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. The Learner's Path Through an Assignment

Only the owning `Learner` may drive an assignment forward, and every one of the three learner actions compares `X-User-ID` against the assignment's `employee_id`, refusing a mismatch with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`. All three also require the referenced module's `status` to still be `PUBLISHED`; a module retired since the assignment was raised makes the assignment unstartable and yields `ERR_MODULE_RETIRED`.

Starting an assignment moves it into `IN_PROGRESS` and is legal from three different states, which is what makes this lifecycle cyclic rather than linear. From `ASSIGNED` it is the ordinary first step. Starting additionally requires that the learner's `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, and that requirement is not peculiar to the `ASSIGNED` source: all three source states are checked alike, immediately after the state-legality check and before the module-still-published check, with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED` the refusal. From `OVERDUE` it is a re-entry: `OVERDUE → IN_PROGRESS` puts a late learner back into the pipeline without clearing `escalation_level`, so the escalation record of the lateness survives the resumption. From `ESCALATED` it is likewise a re-entry: `ESCALATED → IN_PROGRESS` leaves both `escalation_level` and any `ACTIVE` access consequence in place, and they survive until the assignment actually reaches `COMPLETED`. Each of the three starts bumps the assignment's `version` by one and changes nothing else.

Submitting an attempt carries a single `score_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive and is legal only from `IN_PROGRESS`. The submission always sets `last_score_pct` to the supplied score and increments `attempt_count` by one. Which state it lands in is decided by comparison against the module's `passing_score_pct`, and the boundary is inclusive: a score at least equal to `passing_score_pct` passes, a score strictly below it fails.

A failing submission moves `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED_ATTEMPT` and does nothing else at all — no notices expire, no consequences lift, no employee-level field changes, and only the assignment's own `version` is bumped. From `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, retaking returns the assignment to `IN_PROGRESS` and again bumps only the assignment's `version`. That cycle — `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED_ATTEMPT → IN_PROGRESS` — may repeat without bound, and `attempt_count` is the only field that witnesses how many times it has happened.

A passing submission moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and triggers the widest release cascade in the training half of the system. In addition to `last_score_pct` and `attempt_count`, it stamps `completed_at` with the current logical clock and clears `next_reminder_due_at` to nothing, since a finished assignment is never reminded again. Every escalation notice belonging to this assignment whose state is `SENT` becomes `EXPIRED`; notices already `ACKNOWLEDGED` are left alone, because the compensation cascade only ever touches notices still in `SENT`. Every access consequence whose `source_assignment_id` is this assignment and whose state is `ACTIVE` becomes `LIFTED`, with `lifted_at` stamped and `lift_reason_code` recorded as `TRAINING_COMPLETED` — this is the earned exit, not a compensated one, because the obligation was genuinely discharged. The employee's `access_posture` and `active_suspension_count` are then recomputed from whatever consequences remain `ACTIVE`, and `compliance_state` is recomputed as well. If this assignment happens to be the coaching assignment of a repeat-clicker intervention sitting in `COACHING_ASSIGNED`, that intervention bubbles up to `COACHING_COMPLETED` in the same request. Finally both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` are recomputed, and the operation bumps the assignment's `version` by one and the employee's `version` by one.

`COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal states of an assignment. Requesting any transition the state machine does not declare — submitting from `ASSIGNED`, retaking from `IN_PROGRESS`, or any action at all against a `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` row — is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Compliance Sweeps: Reminder Cadence, Overdue Marking and the Four-Hop Escalation Chain

Deadlines do not enforce themselves. A `ProgramAdmin` runs a compliance sweep, which evaluates the whole population against the current logical clock in four ordered steps and reports what it did in six fields, whose semantics are exact. `assignments_scanned` is the total number of curriculum-assignment rows the registry holds, taken once before any step runs and counting every row regardless of state — rows already `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, and rows belonging to offboarded employees, are all inside it even though no step can act on them. `reminders_sent_count` is the number of assignments that received a reminder on this run, which is also the number of reminders sent, because a sweep sends at most one per assignment. `overdue_count` is the number of assignments this run moved into `OVERDUE`. `escalations_created_count` is the number of `EscalationNotice` rows this run created, so a rung reached by a learner who has no `manager_id` contributes nothing to it even though `escalation_level` still advanced. `consequences_applied_count` is the number of `AccessConsequence` rows this run created — one for each newly reached rung of 2 or higher that actually produced a notice, so a three-rung walk that fires notices for rungs 1, 2 and 3 reports 3 escalations and 2 consequences. `swept_at` is the logical-clock instant at which the sweep ran. That six-field report, and not any of the notice or consequence rows the sweep may have minted along the way, is the whole of the response body, which is why a sweep answers on HTTP 200 rather than 201. Steps A through D run in that order within a single sweep, which is why an assignment can become overdue and be escalated by the very same run.

**Step A — the reminder cadence.** A module's `reminder_offsets_days` list is consumed in the order the author wrote it; that order is authoritative and is never sorted. The instant at which the next unsent reminder falls due is the assignment's `effective_due_at` moved *backwards* by the offset sitting at the position given by `reminders_sent`, counting positions from zero — so with the default list of 7, 3, 1 and −2, the first reminder is due seven days before the effective deadline, the second three days before, the third one day before, and the fourth two days *after* it, because subtracting a negative offset moves the instant forward. Once `reminders_sent` has consumed every offset in the list, `next_reminder_due_at` becomes nothing at all and no further reminder is ever due. When the sweep finds an assignment whose `next_reminder_due_at` is set and is at or before the current clock — the boundary is inclusive, so a sweep run at exactly that instant does send the reminder — it increments `reminders_sent` by one, stamps `last_reminder_at` with the clock, and recomputes `next_reminder_due_at` from the new count; that bumps the assignment's `version` by one and leaves the employee's `version` untouched, because no employee-level field moves when a reminder goes out. A single sweep sends **at most one reminder per assignment**, and it never skips ahead: an assignment whose first three reminder instants have all already passed emits exactly one reminder on this sweep, so three separate sweeps are required to drive `reminders_sent` to 3. The step considers only assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order; assignments in `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` carry no next reminder instant and are skipped.

**Step B — overdue marking.** Any assignment in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `FAILED_ATTEMPT` whose `effective_due_at` is *strictly* earlier than the current clock moves to `OVERDUE`. All three source states behave identically here: the transition recomputes the employee's `compliance_state` — which moves `COMPLIANT → AT_RISK` when no sibling assignment of that employee is `ESCALATED` — recomputes both risk scores, and bumps the assignment's `version` by one and the employee's `version` by one. An assignment whose effective deadline is exactly equal to the clock is not yet overdue.

**Step C — the escalation ladder.** For each assignment now in `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, in ascending `created_at` order, the sweep first computes `days_overdue` as the interval from `effective_due_at` to the clock in seconds, divided by 86,400, floored, and floored again at 0 so it never goes negative. That same expression is exactly what an assignment's `days_overdue` field reports on every read, evaluated against the clock at read time and never conditioned on the assignment's state: a `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` assignment whose effective deadline has since passed still reports a positive count, and any assignment whose effective deadline is still ahead reports 0. The target rung follows from that count: 14 or more days overdue targets rung 3, at least 7 but fewer than 14 targets rung 2, at least 1 but fewer than 7 targets rung 1, and anything less targets rung 0. Because `days_overdue` is floored, it remains 0 for the first twenty-three hours, fifty-nine minutes and fifty-nine seconds after the effective deadline. `escalation_level` only ever moves forward: it becomes the greater of its current value and the target rung. Crucially, the sweep does **not** advance one rung at a time — it walks every rung from one above the current `escalation_level` up to and including the target rung, in ascending order, firing the full cascade for each. A sweep run twenty days after an untouched deadline therefore produces notices for rungs 1, 2 and 3 in that order in a single run. Moving `OVERDUE → ESCALATED` requires the target rung to be at least 2, which in turn requires `days_overdue` to be at least 7.

The cascade fired for each newly reached rung is a four-hop causal chain, and every hop must be honoured:

- **Hop 1** sets `escalation_level` to the rung reached.
- **Hop 2** creates an `EscalationNotice` for that rung — but only when the employee has a `manager_id`. The notice records `assignment_id` pointing at the overdue assignment, `manager_id` copied from the learner's own `manager_id` at the moment of creation, `level` set to the rung (a value from 1 to 3), `state` of `SENT`, and `sent_at` stamped with the current clock; `acknowledged_at` and `action_plan_note` are both empty.
- **Hop 3** applies an access consequence — but **only for notices whose `level` is 2 or higher**. A rung-1 escalation is notify-only: it produces a notice and nothing more, leaving `access_posture` at whatever it already was. When hop 3 does fire, it creates an `AccessConsequence` whose `source_assignment_id` is this assignment and whose `state` is `ACTIVE`, with its severity derived by the ladder in §3.6 — the rung reached is what that ladder takes as its triggering rung. The row's `reason_code` is the literal `ESCALATION_LEVEL_` followed by the rung, so a rung-2 cascade records `ESCALATION_LEVEL_2` and a rung-3 cascade records `ESCALATION_LEVEL_3`.
- **Hop 4** is the recomputation that the new `ACTIVE` consequence triggers: the employee's `access_posture` and `active_suspension_count` are recalculated from the full set of that employee's active consequences.

The chain is fragile at hop 2 by design. When `manager_id` is empty, hop 2 produces nothing, so hops 3 and 4 never fire — `escalation_level` still advances and the assignment still reaches `ESCALATED`, but `access_posture` stays `NONE` and no consequence is ever created. Reaching a rung of 2 or higher also drives the employee's `compliance_state` to `DELINQUENT`, and the step bumps both the assignment's `version` and the employee's `version` by exactly one — one bump each per assignment the step acted on, however many rungs the walk covered and however many consequences it created on the way, and an assignment whose target rung is no higher than its current `escalation_level` is left entirely alone and bumps nothing.

**Step D** closes the sweep by recomputing every affected employee's derived fields, including `compliance_state` and both risk-score variants.

### 3.5. Manager Response, Deadline Relief and Voiding

An escalation notice arrives addressed to one person: the `Manager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the notice's `manager_id`. Acknowledging it is legal only while the notice is in `SENT`, requires a non-empty `action_plan_note` describing the remediation the manager intends, and moves `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED` while stamping `acknowledged_at` with the logical clock and storing the note. Acknowledgement bumps the notice's `version` by one and — this is the point worth stating twice — **does not lift any access consequence**. Acknowledging that a report is late is not the same as the report becoming un-late. Anyone other than the addressed manager is refused with `ERR_NOT_DIRECT_REPORT_MANAGER`, and acknowledging a notice that is already `ACKNOWLEDGED` or already `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`. The alternative exit from `SENT` is `SENT → EXPIRED`, driven by the system rather than by any operator: it fires when the underlying assignment reaches `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, when an extension resets `escalation_level`, or when the employee is offboarded, and it too bumps the notice's `version`. An `ACKNOWLEDGED` notice is never re-expired. Notices may be read individually or listed with filters on `assignment_id`, `manager_id`, `state` and `level`, alongside `limit` and `offset`; the list is the reverse-lookup channel from an assignment or a manager to the notices raised against them.

Where acknowledgement records intent, granting an extension changes facts. The learner's own manager — again compared by `X-User-ID` against the employee's `manager_id`, with `ERR_NOT_DIRECT_REPORT_MANAGER` otherwise — supplies `extension_days`, which must be at least 1. Two independent budget-and-window checks then apply, in this order. First the budget: the assignment's existing `granted_extension_days` plus the requested `extension_days` must not exceed the module's `max_extension_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Then the window, which has two clauses joined by "and": the assignment's `escalation_level` must be strictly below 3, because an assignment that has already reached the suspension rung can no longer be extended at all; and the grant must actually move the deadline into the future, meaning the current clock must be at or before `due_at` advanced by the sum of `granted_extension_days` and the new `extension_days`. Failing either clause is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).

Extensions are legal from three states and the target state is decided by `attempt_count`, not by the source state. From `OVERDUE` and from `ESCALATED`, an assignment on which the learner has never submitted anything — `attempt_count` of exactly 0 — returns all the way to `ASSIGNED`, giving `OVERDUE → ASSIGNED` and `ESCALATED → ASSIGNED`; an assignment with `attempt_count` of 1 or more instead lands in `IN_PROGRESS`, giving `OVERDUE → IN_PROGRESS` and `ESCALATED → IN_PROGRESS`. The third legal source is `ASSIGNED` itself, where `attempt_count` is 0 and the deadline has not yet passed: this is a self-loop, `ASSIGNED → ASSIGNED`, which changes fields but not state. A pre-deadline grant is explicitly legal, which is why the generic state check does not reject an extension merely because the assignment is not yet overdue. Extending from `IN_PROGRESS` or `FAILED_ATTEMPT` is not declared and is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Whichever rung it is granted from, the extension performs one identical compensation set. It adds `extension_days` to `granted_extension_days` and recomputes both `effective_due_at` and `next_reminder_due_at` from the new value. It resets `escalation_level` to 0. Every escalation notice of this assignment still in `SENT` becomes `EXPIRED`. Every access consequence whose `source_assignment_id` is this assignment and whose state is `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED`, with `lift_reason_code` recorded as `DEADLINE_EXTENDED` — a compensated exit, because the consequence should never have been applied to a learner who was entitled to more time. The employee's `access_posture`, `active_suspension_count`, `compliance_state` and both risk scores are then recomputed, and the operation bumps the assignment's `version` by one and the employee's `version` by one. On the `ASSIGNED → ASSIGNED` self-loop there are by construction no `SENT` notices and no `ACTIVE` consequences to unwind, so that part of the cascade finds nothing to do — but the field updates and the two version bumps still happen.

Voiding is the `ProgramAdmin`'s counterpart instrument and the true compensating action of the escalation cascade. It takes a `void_reason_code` and is legal from any of the five non-terminal states — `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, `OVERDUE` and `ESCALATED` — with `ESCALATED` being the rung that actually has notices and consequences to unwind. The assignment becomes `VOIDED`, the supplied `void_reason_code` is stored, and `next_reminder_due_at` is cleared to nothing. Every `SENT` escalation notice of the assignment becomes `EXPIRED`. Every `ACTIVE` access consequence whose `source_assignment_id` is this assignment becomes `VOIDED` with `lift_reason_code` of `ASSIGNMENT_VOIDED`. The employee's `access_posture`, `active_suspension_count`, `compliance_state` and both risk scores are recomputed, and both the assignment's `version` and the employee's `version` are bumped by one. The obligation on the void action is exact: `active_suspension_count` must come back to the value it held before escalation created the consequence in the first place.

### 3.6. The Access-Consequence Severity Ladder

Access consequences are the teeth behind training deadlines. Each row records the `employee_id` whose access is constrained, an optional `source_assignment_id` naming the delinquent assignment that caused it — left empty when a `ProgramAdmin` applies one directly — a `consequence_type` of `WARNING`, `MFA_REVIEW` or `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION`, an integer `severity_rank`, a `state` of `ACTIVE`, `LIFTED` or `VOIDED`, a `reason_code` explaining why it was applied, an `applied_at` instant, and, on exit, a `lifted_at` instant and a `lift_reason_code`. The rank mapping is fixed: `WARNING` ranks 1, `MFA_REVIEW` ranks 2, and `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION` ranks 3.

A `ProgramAdmin` may apply a consequence directly to an employee, supplying a `reason_code`, the mandatory `expected_employee_version`, and optionally an explicit `consequence_type`. The target employee's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`. Omitting `consequence_type` hands the choice of rung to the severity ladder, which is the same ladder hop 3 of the escalation cascade uses.

That ladder has an apparent circularity — the severity of the new row depends on the employee's posture, and the posture depends on the set of active rows including the new one — and it is resolved by a **pinned evaluation order of five steps** that must be followed exactly:

1. Compute the employee's pre-existing rank as the highest `severity_rank` among that employee's access consequences whose state is `ACTIVE`, or 0 when there are none. The row about to be created is deliberately **not** part of this set.
2. Choose the new rank. If the request supplied a `consequence_type`, the new rank is simply that type's rank. Otherwise the new rank is derived: take the rung that triggered this application — the `level` of the escalation notice when the cascade fired it, and 1 when there is no such notice — then take the greater of the pre-existing rank plus one and that triggering rung, then take the lesser of that result and 3. The composition is evaluated left to right in exactly that order: the "greater of" first, then the cap at 3.
3. Create the `AccessConsequence` with `consequence_type` set to the type matching the new rank, `severity_rank` set to the new rank, `state` of `ACTIVE`, and `applied_at` stamped with the current logical clock. Then, every prior consequence of that same employee with the **same** `consequence_type` and state `ACTIVE` becomes `LIFTED`, with `lift_reason_code` of `SUPERSEDED` and `lifted_at` stamped.
4. Recompute the employee's post-application rank as the highest `severity_rank` among that employee's `ACTIVE` rows, now including the row just created, and set `access_posture` from it: rank 0 gives `NONE`, rank 1 gives `WARNED`, rank 2 gives `ELEVATED_REVIEW`, and rank 3 gives `SUSPENDED`.
5. Set `active_suspension_count` to the number of that employee's rows that are simultaneously `ACTIVE` and of type `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION`.

Only rows in state `ACTIVE` participate in steps 1, 4 and 5; `LIFTED` and `VOIDED` rows are excluded from all three. Because step 1 excludes the row being created, the derived ladder climbs one rung per application: an employee already at `WARNED` who receives a second derived consequence gets `MFA_REVIEW`, a third gets `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION`, and a fourth stays at `SYSTEM_ACCESS_SUSPENSION` because of the cap — and that fourth row supersedes the third, lifting it with `SUPERSEDED`. Applying a consequence bumps the employee's `version` by one.

Discharging a consequence is a `ProgramAdmin` action taking a `lift_reason_code`. It is legal only from `ACTIVE`; attempting it against a row already `LIFTED` or already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_CONSEQUENCE_ALREADY_LIFTED`. On success the row becomes `LIFTED`, `lifted_at` is stamped with the clock, the employee's `access_posture` is recomputed as the posture matching the highest `severity_rank` among the rows that remain `ACTIVE`, `active_suspension_count` is recomputed, and both the consequence's `version` and the employee's `version` are bumped by one. The `lift_reason_code` vocabulary is worth reading as a map of how a consequence can end: `MANUAL_LIFT` for a direct discharge, `SUPERSEDED` when a same-type row replaced it, and `TRAINING_COMPLETED` when the learner finished the module — those three all land in `LIFTED`; while `ASSIGNMENT_VOIDED`, `DEADLINE_EXTENDED`, `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED` and `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ASSIGNMENT` — the last written when a fresh assignment supersedes the one that sourced the consequence — are the four compensated exits, all landing in `VOIDED`. That vocabulary describes the codes the system itself writes; it is not enforced against the client. The `lift_reason_code` a `ProgramAdmin` supplies on a direct discharge is required only to be a non-empty string and is then stored and echoed verbatim, and the same holds for the `reason_code` supplied when a consequence is applied directly and for the `void_reason_code` supplied when an assignment is voided. Consequences may be read individually or listed with filters on `employee_id`, `source_assignment_id`, `state` and `consequence_type`, plus `limit` and `offset`, which is how the link from an employee or a delinquent assignment back to its consequences is traversed.

### 3.7. Pretext Templates and Campaign Governance

A pretext template is the reusable creative asset behind a simulation. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `CampaignAuthor` may author one, supplying a `name`, a `pretext_category` drawn from `PACKAGE_DELIVERY`, `IT_PASSWORD_RESET`, `SHARED_DOCUMENT`, `INVOICE_APPROVAL`, `HR_BENEFITS_ENROLLMENT` and `EXEC_URGENT_REQUEST`, and a `difficulty_tier` drawn from `TIER_1_BASIC`, `TIER_2_TARGETED` and `TIER_3_SPEAR` — the highest campaign tier this template is permitted to back. Optionally the author supplies `theme_tags` and sets `credential_capture_enabled`, which defaults to false and declares whether the landing page simulates the capture of credentials. A new template's `review_state` is `DRAFT`, its `blocked_reason` is empty, and its `harmful_flag_hits` is computed at creation time by the screen of §2.10 — recorded, not rejected, so that the safety gate bites at approval time where a human decision is being made.

Approving a template for use is a `CampaignApprover` action. It requires the `review_state` to be `DRAFT` and requires `harmful_flag_hits` to be empty; on success the template moves `DRAFT → APPROVED_FOR_USE` and its `version` is bumped. A non-empty `harmful_flag_hits` produces `ERR_HARMFUL_PRETEXT_THEME`, while a template that is not in `DRAFT` produces `ERR_TEMPLATE_STATE_INVALID`. Blocking is the `ProgramAdmin`'s veto and is legal from either `DRAFT` or `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, giving `DRAFT → BLOCKED` and `APPROVED_FOR_USE → BLOCKED`; both variants record the supplied `blocked_reason` — a mandatory body field that must be a string with at least one non-whitespace character, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and whose text is then stored and echoed verbatim — and bump `version`, and both check only that the template is not already `BLOCKED`, since `BLOCKED` is terminal. Blocking is forward-looking only: it does not retroact onto campaigns that are already `APPROVED`, it merely prevents future approvals.

A campaign is created by a `CampaignAuthor` with a `name`, the `template_id` of the pretext it will send, and its own base `difficulty_tier` from the same three-value tier vocabulary. Three optional targeting inputs define the audience: `target_departments` lists the departments to include, where an empty array means every department; `target_tenure_bands` lists the tenure bands to include, where an empty array means every band; and `target_min_repeat_tier`, an integer from 0 to 3 defaulting to 0, sets the minimum `repeat_clicker_tier` an employee must carry to be included, evaluated at the moment the campaign is scheduled rather than at creation. A fourth optional input, `communication_policy_ack`, defaults to false and is the author's attestation that the pre-announcement and post-campaign debrief policy will be honoured. Creation stamps `author_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, leaves `reviewer_user_id` empty, and puts the campaign in `DRAFT` with `rejection_reason`, `scheduled_send_at`, `launched_at`, `closed_at`, `cancelled_at` and `debrief_note` all empty, `target_count` and `evaluable_count` both 0, and all four scorecard figures `null` — there being no delivery yet to divide by. One referential check applies at creation: the rank of the campaign's `difficulty_tier` may not exceed the rank of its template's `difficulty_tier`, where `TIER_1_BASIC` ranks 1, `TIER_2_TARGETED` ranks 2 and `TIER_3_SPEAR` ranks 3. A `TIER_3_SPEAR` campaign on a `TIER_2_TARGETED` template is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_TIER_EXCEEDS_TEMPLATE` (HTTP 422).

Submitting a campaign for review is reserved to its own author and is legal from two states: `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` for a first submission and `REJECTED → IN_REVIEW` for a resubmission after feedback. Both require that `communication_policy_ack` is true, refusing otherwise with `ERR_COMMUNICATION_POLICY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, and both bump the campaign's `version`. The resubmission path additionally clears `rejection_reason` back to empty. The `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED → IN_REVIEW` cycle may repeat without bound.

Review itself forks into two branches whose checks are deliberately asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the interesting part.

**Approval** moves `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`. Three checks guard it, evaluated in this order: first, the referenced template's `harmful_flag_hits` must be empty, or the request is refused with `ERR_HARMFUL_PRETEXT_THEME`; second, the acting approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the campaign's `author_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`; third, the template's `review_state` must be exactly `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409) — which is what a `DRAFT` or `BLOCKED` template yields. Because safety is checked before authorship, a harmful-theme campaign is refused with the safety error even when the person asking is also its author. On success the campaign's `reviewer_user_id` is set to the approving `X-User-ID` and its `version` is bumped; no rejection field is touched and the campaign does not return to the author.

**Rejection** moves `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED` and requires a `rejection_reason`, which must likewise be a string with at least one non-whitespace character — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the campaign is even resolved — and which is stored verbatim. It enforces segregation of duties in exactly the same way — an approver may not reject their own campaign either, and the refusal is again `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` — but it runs **no template checks whatsoever**. A campaign built on a harmful-theme template can therefore always be rejected and can never be approved, which is precisely the behaviour a safety reviewer needs. On success `reviewer_user_id` is set to the rejecting `X-User-ID`, `rejection_reason` is stored, and `version` is bumped.

Cancellation is the author's own withdrawal and lands the campaign in the terminal `CANCELLED` state, stamping `cancelled_at` with the logical clock and bumping `version`. It is legal from `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REJECTED` and `APPROVED`, where nothing has been reserved yet and there is nothing to release, and it is also legal from `SCHEDULED`, where the reservation release described in §3.8 applies. Requesting any campaign transition the lifecycle does not declare — launching from `APPROVED`, closing from `SCHEDULED`, cancelling something already `CLOSED` — is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. Campaigns may be read individually, which recomputes the four scorecard rates on read without bumping `version`, or listed with filters on `template_id`, `state` and `difficulty_tier` plus `limit` and `offset`; the list is the reverse-lookup channel from a template to the campaigns that use it.

### 3.8. Reserving and Delivering a Simulation Cohort

Scheduling is where an approved design becomes a concrete reservation against real people. The author supplies the `scheduled_send_at` instant and the mandatory `expected_campaign_version` of §2.7, and the operation is legal from two states: `APPROVED → SCHEDULED` for a first reservation, and the self-loop `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` for a reschedule. Four temporal and cohort gates apply, and they are evaluated in this order after the version comparison.

First, `scheduled_send_at` must be strictly later than the current logical clock; an instant equal to or earlier than the clock is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422).

Second, the send instant must fall outside every applicable communication blackout. A `CommunicationWindow` whose `window_type` is `SIMULATION_BLACKOUT` and whose `scope_department` is either empty (meaning it covers every department) or names a department present among the resolved cohort's departments defines a **closed** interval: the schedule is refused when `scheduled_send_at` is at or after the window's `start_at` and at or before its `end_at`. Both endpoints are therefore blocking, and the refusal is `ERR_COMMUNICATION_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Third, no cohort member may still be inside their personal quiet period. Every non-excluded simulation delivery an employee holds defines a **half-open** quiet interval: it begins at a reference instant — the delivery's `delivered_at` when that is set, and otherwise the `scheduled_send_at` of the campaign that delivery belongs to — and it ends at `quiet_period_until`, which is that reference instant advanced by 5 days. The schedule is refused when `scheduled_send_at` falls at or after the reference instant and strictly before `quiet_period_until` for any cohort member, with `ERR_SIMULATION_QUIET_PERIOD_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the upper bound is exclusive, a send positioned exactly at `quiet_period_until` is allowed. On a reschedule, the campaign's own currently-`QUEUED` deliveries are ignored when this check runs, so a campaign does not conflict with itself.

Fourth, the resolved cohort must contain at least one evaluable member, or the schedule is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_TARGET_COHORT` (HTTP 422).

**Cohort resolution.** The candidate set is every employee whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` — offboarded people are never candidates — filtered by three ANDed axes, on each of which an empty array means no constraint at all: the employee's `department` must appear in `target_departments` unless that array is empty; the employee's freshly computed `tenure_band` must appear in `target_tenure_bands` unless that array is empty; and the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier` must be greater than or equal to `target_min_repeat_tier`. The resulting cohort is ordered by ascending `created_at`, and that ordering is the creation order of the delivery rows — never identifier order. The cohort then splits: members whose `access_posture` is `SUSPENDED` receive a delivery in state `EXCLUDED` with `excluded_reason` of `ACCESS_SUSPENDED`, and everybody else receives a delivery in state `QUEUED`. An excluded-at-birth row is otherwise shaped exactly like a queued one — it carries the same `queued_at` stamp, the same `base_difficulty_tier` and the same per-employee `effective_difficulty_tier` — but because it reserves nothing, creating it bumps no `Employee.version` at all. Every delivery, queued or excluded at birth, additionally freezes the member's `tenure_band` as computed at that instant; that frozen band is retained for the scorecard segmentation of §3.9 and is not itself one of the delivery's response fields. The campaign's `target_count` is the size of the whole cohort including the suspended members, while `evaluable_count` is the number of members who are not suspended — and it is `evaluable_count` reaching zero, not `target_count`, that triggers the empty-cohort refusal.

Each `QUEUED` delivery records `campaign_id`, `employee_id`, a `queued_at` instant stamped with the current clock, a `base_difficulty_tier` copied from the campaign's `difficulty_tier`, and an `effective_difficulty_tier` computed per employee and then frozen — it is never recomputed after the reservation is made. Creating the reservation increments each reserved employee's `reserved_simulation_slots` by one and bumps that `Employee.version` by one, sets `scheduled_send_at`, `target_count` and `evaluable_count` on the campaign, and bumps the campaign's `version` by one.

**Per-employee difficulty escalation.** The effective tier for one cohort member is computed from exactly four inputs, all read at schedule time: the campaign's `difficulty_tier`, and the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier`, `consecutive_clean_simulations` and `tenure_band`. Start from the base rank of the campaign's tier, where `TIER_1_BASIC` is 1, `TIER_2_TARGETED` is 2 and `TIER_3_SPEAR` is 3. Streak relief is 1 when the employee's `consecutive_clean_simulations` is 3 or more, and 0 otherwise. The raw rank is the greater of the base rank and the quantity formed by adding the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier` to the base rank and then subtracting the streak relief — so relief can never pull a member below the campaign's own base tier. The raw rank is then capped at 3. Finally, if the employee's `tenure_band` is `NEW_HIRE`, the rank is capped again at 2, because a brand-new hire is never spear-phished; for every other band the previous value stands. Rank 1 gives `TIER_1_BASIC`, rank 2 gives `TIER_2_TARGETED` and rank 3 gives `TIER_3_SPEAR`. The composition is evaluated in exactly that order — greater-of first, then the cap at 3, then the new-hire cap. The three-way interaction is non-linear on purpose: a `repeat_clicker_tier` of 2 against a `TIER_2_TARGETED` campaign yields `TIER_3_SPEAR` for a `VETERAN` but only `TIER_2_TARGETED` for a `NEW_HIRE`, and a clean streak of 3 pulls that `VETERAN` back down to `TIER_2_TARGETED`.

**Rescheduling.** The `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` self-loop re-evaluates the same blackout, quiet-period and non-empty-cohort preconditions against the *new* `scheduled_send_at`. It then releases the prior reservation set: every delivery of this campaign in state `QUEUED` becomes `EXCLUDED` with `excluded_reason` of `CAMPAIGN_RESCHEDULED`, decrementing that employee's `reserved_simulation_slots` by one for each. The cohort is then resolved afresh and new `QUEUED` deliveries are created, incrementing `reserved_simulation_slots` again — the superseded rows are kept as excluded history rather than deleted, so an employee reserved twice ends up holding two delivery rows for the one campaign, and their `Employee.version` is bumped twice by the single reschedule, once by the release and once by the fresh reservation. `scheduled_send_at` is overwritten, `target_count` and `evaluable_count` are recomputed, and the campaign's `version` is bumped. Because a new reservation set legitimately supersedes the old one, this is exactly the write that the stale-campaign-version guard protects.

**Cancelling a scheduled campaign** performs the release half without the re-reservation half: `cancelled_at` is stamped, every `QUEUED` delivery becomes `EXCLUDED` with `excluded_reason` of `CAMPAIGN_CANCELLED`, each affected employee's `reserved_simulation_slots` is decremented by one and their `version` bumped, `evaluable_count` falls to 0 while `target_count` keeps whatever the last reservation set it to, and the campaign's `version` is bumped. The reservation-ledger invariant of §2.9 must hold before and after.

**Launching.** A `ProgramAdmin` launches a `SCHEDULED` campaign, moving `SCHEDULED → RUNNING`, but only once the logical clock has reached `scheduled_send_at`; a launch attempted while the clock is still strictly earlier is refused with `ERR_LAUNCH_BEFORE_SCHEDULE` (HTTP 409). On success `launched_at` is stamped with the clock, and every `QUEUED` delivery of the campaign is resolved according to its employee: a delivery whose employee is still `ACTIVE` moves `QUEUED → DELIVERED`, stamping `delivered_at` with the clock, setting `quiet_period_until` to `delivered_at` advanced by 5 days, and setting `outcome_class` to `PENDING`; a delivery whose employee has since become `OFFBOARDED` instead moves `QUEUED → EXCLUDED` with `excluded_reason` of `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED`. Either way the reservation is released, so each affected employee's `reserved_simulation_slots` is decremented by one and their `version` bumped by one. The campaign's `evaluable_count` is recomputed and its `version` bumped.

Deliveries are readable individually and listable with filters on `campaign_id`, `employee_id`, `state` and `outcome_class` plus `limit` and `offset`, which is the reverse-lookup channel from a campaign or an employee to the deliveries that concern them. Either way a delivery serializes, alongside the common `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`, the stored fields `campaign_id`, `employee_id`, `base_difficulty_tier`, `effective_difficulty_tier`, `state`, `queued_at`, `delivered_at`, `first_click_at`, `first_report_at`, `credential_submitted_at` and `excluded_reason`, plus exactly three derived fields: `outcome_class`, `time_to_report_minutes` and `quiet_period_until`. All three are recomputed on every read of any delivery, excluded rows included, so a row that was excluded before it was ever delivered still reports a `quiet_period_until` five days after its campaign's `scheduled_send_at`. The `excluded_reason` vocabulary is closed and consists of `CAMPAIGN_CANCELLED`, `CAMPAIGN_RESCHEDULED`, `EMPLOYEE_OFFBOARDED` and `ACCESS_SUSPENDED`; a quiet-period conflict never appears here, because a conflicting schedule is rejected outright rather than producing an excluded delivery.

### 3.9. Interaction Capture, Outcome Classification and the Campaign Scorecard

Interactions arrive from the mail-gateway integration, which a `ProgramAdmin` drives on the gateway's behalf. Each append supplies an `event_type` from `OPEN`, `CLICK`, `REPORT` and `CREDENTIAL_SUBMIT`, an `occurred_at` instant, and a `source` from `MAIL_GATEWAY`, `REPORT_BUTTON` and `LANDING_PAGE`. The resulting `InteractionEvent` records a `delivery_id` pointing at the delivery it belongs to, a `sequence_index` assigned as one more than the number of events already on that delivery so that the first event is index 1, and a `superseding_effect` describing which delivery-state transition the event caused, written as the source state, the two characters `->` and the target state with no spaces anywhere in between, so that `DELIVERED->REPORTED`, `DELIVERED->CLICKED`, `REPORTED->CLICKED` and `CLICKED->CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED` are the only four transition strings the service ever emits — or the literal `NONE` whenever the delivery's state did not move. Events are immutable once appended, and the per-delivery log is read in ascending `sequence_index` order with `limit` and `offset` for paging; that log is the reverse-lookup channel from a delivery to its events.

Two guards apply to every append. The owning campaign must be in state `RUNNING`. And the event may be neither backdated before its delivery nor postdated past the clock: `occurred_at` must be at or after the delivery's `delivered_at` and at or before the current logical clock, with any violation refused as `ERR_EVENT_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 422). As for which deliveries accept events at all: `DELIVERED`, `REPORTED` and `CLICKED` are all interactable, while a delivery that is still `QUEUED` or that has reached one of the terminal states `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`, `NO_INTERACTION` or `EXCLUDED` refuses the append with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_INTERACTABLE` (HTTP 409). Of those two state checks the delivery's own is evaluated first, so an append against a still-`QUEUED` delivery of a campaign that has not reached `RUNNING` returns `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_INTERACTABLE` rather than `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. An `OPEN` event is recorded for the audit trail but drives no state transition at all, so its `superseding_effect` is `NONE` and the delivery row itself is left completely untouched — no field changes and its `version` is not bumped.

The remaining three event types drive a small state machine whose asymmetry encodes the program's central measurement decision.

A `REPORT` on a `DELIVERED` delivery moves `DELIVERED → REPORTED`, setting `first_report_at` to the event's `occurred_at`, computing `time_to_report_minutes`, and setting `outcome_class` to `REPORTED_ONLY`; the delivery's `version` is bumped and nothing on the employee changes. `time_to_report_minutes` is the interval from `delivered_at` to `first_report_at` in seconds, divided by 60 and floored, and it stays empty for as long as `first_report_at` is empty.

A `CLICK` on a `DELIVERED` delivery moves `DELIVERED → CLICKED`, setting `first_click_at` to the event's `occurred_at` and `outcome_class` to `CLICKED_NO_REPORT`. This is a failure, so it recomputes the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier`, resets `consecutive_clean_simulations` to 0, opens a repeat-clicker intervention when the recomputed tier warrants one, recomputes `risk_score_provisional`, and bumps both the delivery's `version` and the employee's `version` by one.

A `CLICK` that arrives *after* a report moves `REPORTED → CLICKED`, with the identical field updates except that `outcome_class` becomes `CLICKED_THEN_REPORTED`, and with the identical repeat-clicker recomputation and intervention cascade. A reporter who later clicks is a clicker: any click dominates the delivery's state.

A `REPORT` that arrives *after* a click is the self-loop `CLICKED → CLICKED`. It sets `first_report_at` to the event's `occurred_at` if that field is still empty, computes `time_to_report_minutes`, and sets `outcome_class` to `CLICKED_THEN_REPORTED`; because the delivery's state does not move, the event's `superseding_effect` is `NONE`. The delivery's `version` is bumped. This delivery now counts in the click numerator **and** in the report numerator, which is exactly why the two program-level rates are independent.

Repeating an event the delivery has already absorbed is inert but still logged. A `CLICK` against a delivery already in `CLICKED`, and a `REPORT` against a delivery already in `REPORTED`, are each appended as a normal immutable event with the next `sequence_index` and a `superseding_effect` of `NONE`, and change nothing whatsoever: no stored instant is overwritten, the delivery's `version` is not bumped, and neither the repeat-clicker recomputation nor the intervention cascade runs. The one inert case that does still bump the delivery's `version` is a further `REPORT` against a delivery already in `CLICKED` whose `first_report_at` is already set, because the `CLICKED → CLICKED` self-loop above touches the row unconditionally.

A `CREDENTIAL_SUBMIT` moves `CLICKED → CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`, the delivery's worst terminal outcome. Two additional gates apply. The template behind the campaign must have `credential_capture_enabled` set to true, and there must be a prior `CLICK` on this delivery. The two failures are distinct: a `CREDENTIAL_SUBMIT` against a `DELIVERED` or `REPORTED` delivery has no prior click and is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_WITHOUT_CLICK` (HTTP 422), while a `CREDENTIAL_SUBMIT` against a `CLICKED` delivery whose template has capture disabled is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_CAPTURE_DISABLED` (HTTP 422); when both conditions are violated at once, the capture clause is evaluated first and its error is the one returned. The event's `occurred_at` must additionally be at or after `first_click_at` as well as at or before the clock. On success `credential_submitted_at` is set to `occurred_at` and `outcome_class` becomes `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`; the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier` is recomputed — with a delivery counting as at most one failure no matter how many failure events it carries — the intervention cascade is re-evaluated at the new tier, `risk_score_provisional` is recomputed, and both the delivery's `version` and the employee's `version` are bumped.

**Closing a campaign** is a `ProgramAdmin` action legal only from `RUNNING`, and it requires a non-empty `debrief_note` in the request; an empty or missing note is refused with `ERR_COMMUNICATION_POLICY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, and a note that is present but consists only of whitespace counts as empty for that purpose. A `debrief_note` that is not a JSON string at all is a different failure and is caught earlier, with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` returned before the campaign's state is even examined; an accepted note is stored exactly as supplied, untrimmed. On success `closed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the `debrief_note` is stored, and every delivery still in `DELIVERED` moves `DELIVERED → NO_INTERACTION` with `outcome_class` of `NO_INTERACTION`, each such transition incrementing that employee's `consecutive_clean_simulations` by one and bumping that delivery's `version` by one. A delivery that ended in `REPORTED` also earns a `consecutive_clean_simulations` increment at close, but it keeps the state `REPORTED` and its own `version` is left alone, since the close rewrites no field on it — `REPORTED` is quasi-terminal rather than one of the three listed terminal states. Employee versions are bumped by cohort membership rather than by transition: every employee holding at least one non-excluded delivery of this campaign has their `version` bumped by exactly one, however many of their deliveries the close moved and even when none of them moved, while an employee whose only delivery here was excluded is not bumped at all. The close then freezes `click_rate`, `report_rate`, `credential_rate` and `resilience_ratio` on the campaign, recomputes `consecutive_clean_simulations`, `repeat_clicker_tier`, `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` for every cohort member, and bumps the campaign's `version`. Before the close these four rates are recomputed on every read of the campaign; the close is the only moment at which they are frozen.

**Classifying one delivery.** The `outcome_class` of a delivery is decided by the first matching clause of an ordered test whose only inputs are the delivery's own `state` and its three stored instants `credential_submitted_at`, `first_click_at` and `first_report_at`; the interaction log is never re-read to classify, so the class is reproducible from the delivery row alone. If the delivery's state is `EXCLUDED`, its class is `EXCLUDED`. Otherwise, if `credential_submitted_at` is set, the class is `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`. Otherwise, if both `first_click_at` and `first_report_at` are set, the class is `CLICKED_THEN_REPORTED`. Otherwise, if only `first_click_at` is set, the class is `CLICKED_NO_REPORT`. Otherwise, if only `first_report_at` is set, the class is `REPORTED_ONLY`. Otherwise, if the state is `DELIVERED`, the class is `PENDING`. In every remaining case the class is `NO_INTERACTION`.

**Computing the rates.** The evaluable set is every delivery of the campaign whose state is not `EXCLUDED`; excluded deliveries sit outside every numerator *and* outside the denominator. Let the denominator be the size of that evaluable set. The click numerator counts evaluable deliveries whose `first_click_at` is set, which therefore includes both `CLICKED_THEN_REPORTED` and `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED` deliveries. The report numerator counts evaluable deliveries whose `first_report_at` is set, which likewise includes `CLICKED_THEN_REPORTED`. The credential numerator counts evaluable deliveries whose `credential_submitted_at` is set. Deliveries still classified `PENDING` remain inside the denominator, so the report rate of a running campaign genuinely rises as reports arrive. When the denominator is zero, all four figures are `null`. Otherwise `click_rate`, `report_rate` and `credential_rate` are each their numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and `resilience_ratio` is the report numerator divided by the greater of 1 and the click numerator, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The double counting of a click-then-report delivery is intentional: `click_rate` and `report_rate` are independent primary metrics and their sum may exceed 1.0000, because reporting culture is worth measuring even among the people who clicked.

**Segments.** A dedicated computed read returns the program-level figures alongside per-segment breakdowns and accepts an optional `segment_by` selector whose vocabulary is exactly `DEPARTMENT` and `TENURE_BAND`. That value is matched after trimming surrounding whitespace and upper-casing, so `department` and ` Tenure_Band ` both resolve; anything else, the empty string included, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Supplying it restricts the breakdown to that one axis; omitting it emits both, all of the department segments first and then all of the tenure-band segments, in one flat `segments` array. Its response carries the `campaign_id`, the `evaluable_count`, an `excluded_count` of deliveries outside the evaluable set, the four program-level figures `click_rate`, `report_rate`, `credential_rate` and `resilience_ratio`, and that `segments` array — never any pagination fields, since the array is not paged. Segments group deliveries by the employee's `department` and by the employee's `tenure_band` as frozen at delivery creation. Group discovery walks every delivery of the campaign in ascending `created_at` order, excluded rows included, and the segments come back in the order their keys were first met on that walk; the rates within a group, however, are computed over that group's evaluable rows alone, so a group all of whose deliveries are excluded still appears, carrying an `evaluable_count` of 0 and `null` for each rate. Each segment row spells its identity twice over: `segment_type` and `segment_by` both hold the axis name (`DEPARTMENT` or `TENURE_BAND`), `segment_key` and `segment_value` both hold the group's own value, and the row additionally repeats that value under `department` on a department segment or under `tenure_band` on a tenure-band segment. Alongside those it carries the group's `evaluable_count`, `click_rate`, `report_rate`, `credential_rate` and `resilience_ratio`. Each segment's rates are computed from that segment's own raw counts and rounded once, and a segment with no evaluable deliveries reports `null` for each of its rates rather than 0.0000. The program-level figures are never the average of the segment figures — the rule is sum-then-round, not round-then-average.

### 3.10. Repeat Clickers, Targeted Coaching and Risk Measurement

**The repeat-clicker tier.** An employee's `repeat_clicker_tier` is a rolling-window count of failures, recomputed whenever an interaction event drives one of that employee's deliveries into `CLICKED` or `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`, whenever a campaign closes, and whenever an intervention closes. The window opens exactly 180 days before the current logical clock, and its lower bound is inclusive. Inside that window, a failure delivery is one of this employee's own deliveries whose state is either `CLICKED` or `CREDENTIAL_SUBMITTED`, whose campaign is in state `RUNNING` or `CLOSED`, and whose `delivered_at` is at or after the window's opening instant. Each such delivery is counted exactly once, however many failure events it carries. No failures gives a tier of 0; exactly one failure gives 1; two or three failures give 2; four or more give 3. Deliveries in `EXCLUDED`, `QUEUED`, `DELIVERED`, `REPORTED` or `NO_INTERACTION` are never failures, and deliveries belonging to campaigns in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CANCELLED` are outside the input set entirely. That banded figure is then reduced by the employee's accumulated decay count — the number of intervention closes that have ever applied a tier decay to this employee — and the difference is floored at 0. The decay count is cumulative and permanent rather than a single one-off adjustment: two decayed closes subtract 2 from every later evaluation of the tier, three subtract 3, and nothing restores what has been subtracted — not a fresh failure, not the 180-day window rolling forward past the old failures, not the opening or closing of any later intervention. An employee with four in-window failures and two decays therefore carries a tier of 1, and an employee with one in-window failure and one decay carries 0.

**The clean streak.** `consecutive_clean_simulations` counts how many of that employee's non-excluded deliveries, taken in ascending `delivered_at` order with ties broken by ascending creation order, ended in `REPORTED` or `NO_INTERACTION` after the most recent failure delivery. Three refinements pin that walk. A delivery carrying no `delivered_at` at all — anything still `QUEUED` — is outside the walk entirely. A delivery still sitting in `DELIVERED` is passed over without either counting or breaking the run, so an in-flight simulation neither helps nor hurts. And a `REPORTED` delivery counts only once its own campaign has reached `CLOSED`, so a report inside a still-`RUNNING` campaign is worth nothing yet, while a `NO_INTERACTION` delivery counts unconditionally — it can only have been produced by a close in the first place. A failure delivery zeroes the running count at the position it occupies in that ordering, so a click leaves the streak at 0 unless one of that employee's counted clean deliveries sorts after it; closing a campaign is the moment that turns that campaign's clean deliveries into counted ones. A streak of 3 is the threshold that both grants relief in the difficulty-escalation rule of §3.8 and permits a tier decay at intervention close.

**Opening an intervention.** When a failure recomputation leaves `repeat_clicker_tier` at 2 or higher and that employee has no intervention already in `OPEN`, `ESCALATED_TO_MANAGER`, `COACHING_ASSIGNED` or `COACHING_COMPLETED`, the system opens a `RepeatClickerIntervention`. It records the `employee_id` of the repeat clicker, the `triggering_delivery_id` of the delivery whose failure pushed the tier up, a `tier_at_open` holding the tier at that instant (a value of 2 or 3), an `opened_at` instant stamped with the logical clock, an empty `coaching_assignment_id`, an empty `resolution_code`, an empty `closed_at`, and `tier_decay_applied` set to false. It normally opens in state `OPEN` — but a tier of exactly 3 at the moment of opening puts it straight into `ESCALATED_TO_MANAGER` instead. An intervention already `OPEN` when a further failure pushes the tier to 3 moves `OPEN → ESCALATED_TO_MANAGER` by the same system recomputation, bumping its `version`. Reading an intervention refreshes two derived fields without bumping `version`: `current_tier`, which is the employee's live `repeat_clicker_tier`, and `simulation_frequency_days`, the targeted re-simulation cadence — 14 days when `current_tier` is 3, 30 days when it is 2, and 45 days otherwise. Higher-risk people are simulated more often, which is the whole point of tracking the tier.

**Assigning coaching.** A `ProgramAdmin` assigns targeted remedial coaching by supplying a `module_id`. The intervention must be in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED_TO_MANAGER`, and both source states behave identically: `OPEN → COACHING_ASSIGNED` and `ESCALATED_TO_MANAGER → COACHING_ASSIGNED` share one side-effect set. The named module's `module_type` must be exactly `REMEDIAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COACHING_MODULE_TYPE_INVALID` (HTTP 422), and its `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MODULE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). On success a curriculum assignment is created for that employee with an `assignment_reason` of `REMEDIAL` — which, per §3.2, gives it a 7-day base window before any criticality halving — and its identifier is stored on the intervention as `coaching_assignment_id`. That create is raised by the system on the administrator's behalf rather than through the assignment endpoint of §3.2, so it takes no `expected_employee_version` and applies neither the role-eligibility gate nor the employment-liveness gate: a remedial module whose `applies_to_role_codes` excludes the employee's `role_code` is assigned anyway, and so is a module whose employee has since been offboarded — offboarding does not close interventions, so that path stays reachable. It does run the supersession cascade of §3.2 in full, so a prior non-terminal assignment of the same employee-and-module pair is voided with `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ASSIGNMENT`, its `SENT` notices expire and its `ACTIVE` consequences are voided. The intervention's `version` and the employee's `version` are each bumped by one, while the new assignment is born at `version` 1. Attempting to assign coaching to an intervention that already has coaching is refused with `ERR_INTERVENTION_STATE_INVALID`, as is closing an intervention whose state is anything other than `COACHING_COMPLETED`.

**Completing and closing.** The child assignment drives the parent: when the coaching assignment reaches `COMPLETED`, the intervention moves `COACHING_ASSIGNED → COACHING_COMPLETED` by system action, bumping its `version`. A `ProgramAdmin` then closes it with a `resolution_code` — a mandatory field required only to be a string with at least one non-whitespace character, refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise and stored verbatim when accepted — moving `COACHING_COMPLETED → CLOSED_RESOLVED`, stamping `closed_at` with the logical clock and storing the code. The close is also the only place a tier ever decays, and the decay is conditional: only when the employee's `consecutive_clean_simulations` is 3 or more does the close add one unit to that employee's accumulated decay count — the count that the tier rule of this section subtracts from the banded figure before flooring at 0 — and set `tier_decay_applied` to true. That unit is added once per qualifying close and is never given back, so a second qualifying close, on a later intervention, subtracts a second unit from the tier for good. Coaching alone does not earn the decay; a demonstrated clean streak does. Both risk scores are then recomputed, and the intervention's `version` and the employee's `version` are each bumped by one. Interventions are readable individually or listable with filters on `employee_id`, `triggering_delivery_id`, `coaching_assignment_id` and `state`, plus `limit` and `offset`.

**Risk scoring, in two variants.** Both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_settled` follow one formula over different input sets, and both are recomputed on every read of an employee as well as after every delivery state change, assignment state change, compliance sweep and intervention close. The penalty side sums three weighted counts: 18 points for each in-scope delivery whose `credential_submitted_at` is set, 10 points for each in-scope delivery whose `first_click_at` is set but whose `credential_submitted_at` is not, and 8 points for each of that employee's curriculum assignments currently in state `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`. That penalty subtotal is then multiplied by a tier surcharge equal to 1 plus one tenth of the employee's `repeat_clicker_tier`, so a tier of 3 inflates the penalties by thirty percent. The credit side sums 6 points for each in-scope delivery whose `first_report_at` is set, a flat 5 points of relief when the employee's `tenure_band` is `NEW_HIRE` and nothing otherwise, and 2 points for each settled clean delivery. The raw figure is the multiplied penalty minus the total credits; the stored score is that raw figure rounded half up to 2 decimal places and then clamped into the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00 — the rounding happens once, before the clamp. The counts of overdue-or-escalated assignments and the value of `repeat_clicker_tier` are identical in both variants.

The variants differ in exactly one respect, the in-scope delivery set. For `risk_score_provisional`, the in-flight view used for operational triage, in-scope deliveries are those whose campaign is in state `RUNNING` or `CLOSED`, and the count of settled clean deliveries is forced to zero — a clean simulation earns no credit until its campaign has closed. For `risk_score_settled`, the audit view that feeds the effectiveness report, in-scope deliveries are only those whose campaign is in state `CLOSED`, and the count of settled clean deliveries is the number of those in-scope deliveries whose state is `NO_INTERACTION`, each earning its 2 points. Identical stored data therefore yields two different numbers: an employee who ignored one closed campaign and clicked in a still-running one scores 11.00 provisional against 0.00 settled.

**Real incidents.** A `SecurityAnalyst` records real security incidents, supplying an `incident_type` from `CREDENTIAL_PHISH`, `BEC`, `MALWARE_DELIVERY` and `DATA_LOSS`, a `severity` from `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` and `CRITICAL`, the `department` the incident is attributed to, the `occurred_at` instant, a short `summary` that must be a string carrying at least one non-whitespace character, and optionally a `root_cause_employee_id` naming the employee who was the initial access vector — an identifier that need only resolve to some `Employee` row, since neither that person's `employment_status` nor their `role_code` is examined. The computed `phish_related` flag is true exactly when the `incident_type` is `CREDENTIAL_PHISH` or `BEC`; malware-delivery and data-loss incidents are excluded from the correlation series entirely. Incident records are immutable and carry no `version` and no update capability. They may be read individually or listed with filters on `department`, `incident_type`, `root_cause_employee_id`, `occurred_from` and `occurred_to`, plus `limit` and `offset`; `occurred_from` and `occurred_to` are both inclusive bounds on `occurred_at`, so an incident stamped exactly at either bound is inside the page.

**Program effectiveness.** The final computed read asks whether a strengthening reporting culture actually coincides with fewer real phishing incidents. It takes a `period_start`, a `period_end` that must be strictly later, a `variant` of either `CONCURRENT` or `LAGGED`, and an optional `department` filter. Being a `GET`, it reads those four inputs from the query string — but it also accepts each of them as a key of a JSON request body, for callers that would rather not put instants in a URL, and when a name appears in both places the query-string value is the one used. The requested span may cover at most 24 calendar months — the count is of the month buckets described below rather than of the literal interval, so a span that would produce a twenty-fifth bucket is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID` (HTTP 422), whereas a `period_end` that is not after `period_start` is caught by the earlier well-formedness check and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

The period is divided into UTC calendar-month buckets intersecting the half-open interval from `period_start` up to but not including `period_end`, ordered ascending. Bucketing snaps to whole calendar months rather than to the requested bounds: the first bucket opens at midnight on the first day of the month containing `period_start` — which may therefore precede `period_start` itself — each subsequent bucket opens on the first day of the following month, and the walk stops with the last bucket whose opening instant is strictly earlier than `period_end`, so the final bucket may extend past `period_end`. Each bucket spans the half-open interval from its own opening instant up to but not including the next bucket's, and it is measured over that whole calendar month rather than clipped back to the requested bounds. Each bucket appears in the response as an object carrying `month`, the bucket's `YYYY-MM` label; `bucket_start` and `bucket_end`, its two boundary instants; `report_rate`; and `incident_count`. For each bucket, the delivery set is every simulation delivery whose campaign is in state `CLOSED`, whose campaign's `closed_at` falls inside that bucket, and whose own state is not `EXCLUDED`, restricted to the named department when the `department` filter is supplied. That bucket's report rate is the number of those deliveries whose `first_report_at` is set divided by the total number of them, rounded half up to 4 decimal places — and it is `null` when the bucket holds no such deliveries at all. That bucket's incident count is the number of incident records whose `phish_related` flag is true and whose `occurred_at` falls inside the bucket, again restricted to the named department when supplied.

Pairs are then formed according to the `variant`. Under `CONCURRENT`, each bucket's report rate is paired with that same bucket's own incident count, so every bucket in the period can contribute a pair. Under `LAGGED`, each bucket's report rate is paired with the *following* month's incident count — the lag is exactly 1 month — so the final bucket of the period contributes its report rate to nothing and is dropped; the pair count is consequently one lower and the correlation differs for identical data. In either variant, a bucket whose report rate is `null` is dropped after pairing, and its partner is dropped with it.

If fewer than 2 pairs survive, or if either of the two paired series has zero variance, the `correlation` is `null` and a `correlation_reason_code` of `INSUFFICIENT_PAIRS` or `ZERO_VARIANCE` respectively explains why. Otherwise the `correlation` is the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient of the surviving report-rate series against the surviving incident-count series, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The order of rounding is pinned: each bucket's report rate is rounded to 4 decimal places *before* it enters the correlation, and the correlation itself is then rounded to 4 decimal places. The response echoes the `variant`, lists the per-month `buckets` with their report rates and incident counts, and reports `pair_count`, the `mean_report_rate` as the arithmetic mean of the non-null bucket report rates rounded half up to 4 decimal places, `total_incidents` as the sum of the phish-related incident counts across the period's buckets, and the `correlation` with its `correlation_reason_code`. Both `mean_report_rate` and `total_incidents` are taken over every bucket of the period rather than over the surviving pairs, so under `LAGGED` the dropped final bucket still contributes its report rate to the mean and its incidents to the total; `mean_report_rate` is `null` when no bucket has a non-null rate, and `correlation_reason_code` is `null` whenever a correlation was in fact computed.
