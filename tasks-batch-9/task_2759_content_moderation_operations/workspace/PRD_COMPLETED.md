# Content Moderation Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A platform that removes, age-gates or warns on user content at scale lives or dies by the defensibility of each individual decision. Every enforcement action taken by this system must be traceable to a written policy as it stood at the moment the content was flagged, to a human or automated actor who is accountable for it, and to a reversal path that restores the author's position exactly if the decision turns out to be wrong. The Content Moderation Operations API is the operational backbone for that promise. It carries the policy taxonomy and its versioned enforcement rules, ingests classifier signals and routes them either to automatic enforcement or to a human queue, manages a multilingual reviewer workforce with wellness protections and specialty certifications, samples decided cases for quality assurance, adjudicates appeals through independent reviewers, and produces the periodic transparency figures that regulators and the public read.

Three recurring failures motivate the design. First, policies change: a category's thresholds are rewritten while thousands of cases are mid-flight, and a naive system silently re-evaluates old work against new rules. This API therefore **pins** the governing policy version onto each case at the instant the case is created; thresholds, review deadlines and appeal windows are read from that pinned row forever, never from whatever version happens to be active later. Second, reviewer quality is measured from two different evidence bases — internal audits and appeal outcomes — that can disagree, so the system computes both measurements, keeps both on the reviewer's record, and applies a documented rule for which one gates a demotion. Third, enforcement must be reversible without drift: when an appeal succeeds, the author's penalty ledger and their appeal-credit balance must both return to the value they would have held had the decision never been taken, and reviewer workload and psychological-exposure budgets must balance to zero when a case leaves a reviewer's hands undecided.

Every route described in this document is mounted under the `/moderation` prefix. The system is fully deterministic and does not read wall-clock time: all timestamps, deadlines, shift-window comparisons and reporting periods are evaluated against a server-held logical UTC clock that operators advance explicitly. Given the same sequence of requests, the system must produce the same stored figures every time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every endpoint: identity and authorization, the response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, the shared shape of persisted resources, numeric precision, propagation of cascading side effects, the order in which failures are reported, optimistic concurrency, and the conservation invariants that the whole design rests on.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent — an empty header value counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, spelled with exactly the casing shown, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well formed but the declared role is not permitted to perform the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The six roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `TrustSafetyAdmin`, `PolicyManager`, `Reviewer`, `QAAuditor`, `LegalCounsel` and `AccountHolder`.

`TrustSafetyAdmin` is the operations owner of the pipeline: it registers accounts, ingests content, creates queues, onboards reviewers, drives assignment and workforce wellness, submits classifier signals, opens and computes transparency reports, and advances the logical clock. `PolicyManager` owns the taxonomy and its versioned enforcement rules — drafting, back-testing, revising and activating policy versions, and granting reviewer certifications. `Reviewer` is a frontline or senior human moderator who decides and escalates the cases assigned to them, resolves the appeals they are the independent reviewer for, and may dispute a quality-assurance verdict on their own decision. `QAAuditor` is a reviewer whose workforce class is quality assurance: it draws and audits samples of decided cases, routes appeals to independent reviewers, and may assign, escalate or return escalated cases. `LegalCounsel` handles legal removal requests and government demands: it opens jurisdiction-bearing cases, decides those cases without holding any frontline certification, and may co-sign the publication of a transparency report. `AccountHolder` is the platform user whose content was enforced against; it may file one appeal per decided case on its own content and read its own appeals.

Three of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those the value of `X-User-ID` is meaningful as a foreign key. When `X-User-Role` is `Reviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reviewer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Reviewer` compare the resource's reviewer reference to `X-User-ID` — deciding or escalating a case requires `X-User-ID` to equal that case's `assigned_reviewer_id`, disputing an audit verdict requires it to equal the case's `decided_by_reviewer_id`, and resolving an appeal requires it to equal that appeal's `independent_reviewer_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `QAAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Reviewer` row, and the acting reviewer's `role_class` must be `QA` for audit sampling; the auditor's identity is recorded as `auditor_reviewer_id` on the sample it draws. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountHolder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; an appeal may only be filed for content whose author account matches `X-User-ID`, and an `AccountHolder` may only read appeals whose `account_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are operator identities not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `TrustSafetyAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PolicyManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, but the server does record it: the manager who drafts a policy version is stored as `authored_by_user_id` on that version, the manager who activates it is stored as `activated_by_user_id`, and the two must differ. When `X-User-Role` is `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server may record for audit but never resolves to an entity row; a legal decision leaves `decided_by_reviewer_id` null precisely because no reviewer entity is behind it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the decimal digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Its wording is advisory only; the contract is carried entirely by `error_id` and the HTTP status code.
- The same envelope is also used for failures that fall outside the domain catalogue, and there the HTTP status is not the one §5 tabulates for the identifier. A request to a path that matches no route answers HTTP 404, a request whose method is not allowed on an otherwise existing path answers HTTP 405, and an unexpected server fault answers HTTP 500 — each carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A trailing slash on any documented path is tolerated and resolves to the same route.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query-string parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many records come back; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter says how many records to skip before the first returned record; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Values outside these bounds are malformed input and are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a `limit` or `offset` whose text is not a plain base-10 integer literal.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, collections are ordered by the record's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order — oldest first, newest last. Because the logical clock stands still unless an operator moves it, many records legitimately share one `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the server persisted the records — earliest insertion first — and never by `id`. This ordering also governs the signal listing scoped to a single content item.

List responses report `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of records that match the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the returned page — precisely when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned is still less than `total_count`. A narrowing filter whose value is not a recognised member of that field's enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; identifier-valued and free-text filters are matched by exact equality, so a value that matches nothing yields an empty page rather than a not-found error, and query parameters a listing does not define are ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Temporal Semantics

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. A single server-held logical UTC clock stamps `posted_at` on ingested content, `filed_at` on appeals, every `decided_at`, `audited_at`, `disputed_at`, `resolved_at`, `activated_at`, `backtest_completed_at`, `computed_at`, `published_at` and `reinstated_at`, and it is the instant against which every deadline — `sla_due_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, the wellness window and each reporting period — is compared. Before any operator moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server never falls back to real time, not even on its very first request.

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Every instant accepted as input anywhere in this API — `to_iso8601`, `period_start`, `period_end` — is read in a tolerant ISO 8601 form: the date and time parts may be joined by `T` or by a single space, the seconds component and any fractional part are both optional, and a trailing `Z` or a `±HH:MM` (or `±HHMM`) offset is optional as well. A value carrying an offset is normalised to UTC before use; a value carrying none is read as already being UTC. A fractional part finer than milliseconds is truncated, not rounded, when the instant is rendered back out. A string that does not parse this way is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The clock is monotonic: the supplied instant must be at or after the clock's current value, and an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to the value it already holds is permitted. Advancing the clock modifies no entity: no deadline is re-evaluated, no state changes, and no counter moves as a side effect of the clock alone. Deadline effects surface only when a subsequent request is processed — an appeal filed after the deadline fails at filing time, and a case's `sla_breached` flag is evaluated exactly once, at the moment its decision is recorded.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Ten of the eleven persisted resource kinds — accounts, content items, policy categories, policy versions, reviewers, review queues, moderation cases, appeals, audit samples and transparency reports — carry the same four system-managed fields:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavored formatting; it is a bare UUID.
- **`created_at`** (string): the logical-clock instant at which the record was first persisted, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.
- **`updated_at`** (string): the logical-clock instant of the record's most recent modification, in the same format. Any mutation — including a mutation the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade from some other resource's transition — refreshes `updated_at`.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by one on every mutation of that record, including server-initiated cascades. A recomputation of a derived field such as an account's `strike_points`, a queue's `open_case_count` or a reviewer's `accuracy_score_blended` is a mutation of that record and therefore advances both `updated_at` and `version` — and it does so even when the recomputation lands on exactly the value the field already held.

Detection signals are the exception. A signal is immutable once written: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and it has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field. Its computed fields — `effective_confidence`, `disposition`, `resulting_case_id` and `pinned_policy_version_label` — are all written during the single request that creates it and never change afterwards.

All timestamps rendered anywhere in a response body use the ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. Fields documented as nullable are present in the response body with a JSON null until they acquire a value.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

The system carries two families of numbers, and they round differently.

Confidence values, thresholds, divergence rates and accuracy or overturn rates are decimal fractions in the inclusive range 0.0000 to 1.0000 and are stored and returned with **four decimal places**, rounded **half up** — a value whose fifth decimal digit is 5 or greater rounds away from zero. Every such figure is emitted as a JSON number rather than a string, so JSON's own numeric rendering suppresses trailing zeros: a stored `0.5000` appears in the response body as `0.5`, a stored `1.0000` as `1.0`, and a stored `0.0000` as `0.0`. This applies to `classifier_confidence`, `effective_confidence`, `auto_action_threshold`, `review_floor`, `backtest_divergence_rate`, `accuracy_score_audit_only`, `accuracy_score_blended`, `appeal_overturn_rate`, `legal_grant_rate`, and each per-category `rate` inside `category_rate_breakdown`. Where a formula names an evaluation order, rounding happens exactly at the point the order specifies and no earlier: rounding an intermediate value and then continuing the arithmetic is a different, incorrect answer.

Strike points, exposure points, review deadlines in minutes and every volume count are integers. Where a formula produces a fractional intermediate that must become an integer — the strike points charged by a decision, or the minute allowance of a review deadline — the conversion is a **ceiling** to the next whole number, applied once, after the entire product or quotient has been evaluated. Comparisons against integer caps and budgets are exact integer comparisons.

Boundary inclusivity is never approximate anywhere in this document. Where a rule says "at or above", the boundary value satisfies the rule; where it says "strictly below" or "strictly greater", the boundary value does not.

### 2.7. Policy Pinning and Cascading Propagation

The single most important consistency rule in the system is that a moderation case reads its governing rules from the policy version **pinned onto it at creation**, held in its `policy_version_id`. The confidence thresholds that routed it, the base review allowance in minutes that produced its deadline, and the appeal window that produced its `appeal_deadline_at` are all read from that pinned row. When a newer version of the same category is later activated, the pinned row moves to `SUPERSEDED` but the case keeps pointing at it; the only thing the activation may change on an existing case is the boolean `requires_recertified_review`, which redirects the certification check the deciding reviewer must satisfy. Cases opened after the activation pin the newly activated version instead, because they read the category's `active_version_id` as it stands at their own creation instant.

Several operations mutate more than one resource. Where this document describes such an operation, the cascade is ordered and every step is mandatory: a partial cascade is a defect, not a lenient implementation. Cascades never chain further than the steps written down — an activation, for example, decertifies reviewers and flags cases but does not itself move any reviewer's `status`, and only the accuracy gate described in §3.8 and the two wellness operations described in §3.6 ever move a reviewer's `status`. Compound parent states are derived, never set by a client: a policy category's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` because one of its versions reached `ACTIVE`, and an account's `standing` is recomputed from its `strike_points` every time that ledger is recomputed, in both the punitive and the restorative direction.

When a request fails any validation, nothing is persisted. This is explicit for the case-creation path: if no review queue can serve the pinned category and the content's language, the whole request is rejected and not even the detection signal row survives.

### 2.8. Reference Resolution and the Order of Rejections

A request can fail for several reasons at once, and the reported `error_id` is not arbitrary — it is the first failure in the fixed evaluation order below. Every implementation must report exactly the same error for the same request, so this ordering is part of the contract.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` a recognised role name, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role is permitted on the requested operation, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. The same code also covers the role-scoped restrictions: opening a case with `origin` `LEGAL_REQUEST` is reserved to `LegalCounsel` and `origin` `MANUAL_REPORT` to `TrustSafetyAdmin`; a `LegalCounsel` decision is only permitted on a case whose `escalation_reason` is `LEGAL_REQUEST`; and drawing an audit sample requires the acting reviewer's `role_class` to be `QA`.
3. **Request well-formedness.** Every required input present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges respected, arrays that must be non-empty non-empty, confidence and threshold values inside 0.0000 to 1.0000, `break_minutes` positive, and `correct_outcome` supplied whenever an audit verdict is a disagreement. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field present with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: a required field so supplied counts as missing, and an optional one falls back to its documented default. A request body that parses to something other than a JSON object, or that is not parseable JSON at all, is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a wholly absent or empty body is read as an empty object — so an operation with no required inputs succeeds without a body. Booleans are never accepted where an integer or a number is required.
4. **Path identifiers**, resolved strictly left to right, each with its own not-found code: `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUEUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETECTION_SIGNAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. A nested path fails on the leftmost unresolvable segment before the server looks at any segment to its right.
5. **Body-referenced identifiers and natural keys**, resolved in the order the operation's input list declares them: `account_id`, then `content_item_id`, then `policy_category_id`, then `policy_category_code`, then `policy_version_id`, then `case_id`, then `target_reviewer_id`, then each member of `exclude_reviewer_ids`. An unknown `policy_category_code` is `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown reviewer named in `target_reviewer_id` or listed in `exclude_reviewer_ids` is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Ownership.** A reviewer acting on a case must be its `assigned_reviewer_id` when deciding or escalating and its `decided_by_reviewer_id` when disputing an audit verdict, else `ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`; the reviewer resolving an appeal must be that appeal's `independent_reviewer_id`, else `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER`.
7. **Appellant ownership.** An `AccountHolder` may only file an appeal on content authored by their own account and may only read appeals whose `account_id` equals `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_OWNED`. This applies to appeal filing and to both appeal read paths — fetching one appeal and listing appeals.
8. **Segregation of duties**, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on exactly three operations: activating a policy version (the activator's `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`), drawing an audit sample (the auditor may not be the case's `decided_by_reviewer_id`), and routing an appeal to an independent reviewer (who may not be the case's `decided_by_reviewer_id`).
9. **Lifecycle state**, evaluated in this inner order: `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED`; `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNED`; `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`; `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`; `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_BACKTESTED`; `ERR_CASE_NOT_DECIDED`; `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`; `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_AUDITABLE` and `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_DISPUTABLE`; `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`; `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`; `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ON_BREAK`; `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_LIVE`; `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY_VERSION`; `ERR_ACCOUNT_BANNED`; `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`.
10. **Business rules**, evaluated in this inner order: `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`; `ERR_REVIEWER_LANGUAGE_MISMATCH`; `ERR_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED`; `ERR_REVIEWER_OFF_SHIFT`; `ERR_REVIEWER_AT_CAPACITY` together with `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_ESCALATION`; `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER`; `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_QUEUE`; `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`; `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`; `ERR_APPEAL_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED`; `ERR_WELLNESS_BREAK_OVERLAP`; `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID`; `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; `ERR_REPORT_NOT_RESTATED`; `ERR_OPEN_APPEALS_IN_PERIOD`; `ERR_BACKTEST_DIVERGENCE_EXCEEDED`; `ERR_THRESHOLD_ORDER_INVALID`; `ERR_CHILD_SEVERITY_EXCEEDS_PARENT`; `ERR_QUEUE_SPECIALTY_MISMATCH`; then the uniqueness and clock rules `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_HANDLE` and `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. The jurisdiction requirement on a legal-request case, `ERR_LEGAL_CASE_REQUIRES_JURISDICTION`, is evaluated in this tier, before queue selection is attempted.
11. **Optimistic concurrency, always last.** `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_REVIEWER_VERSION` are reported only when every check above has passed, so a request that is both stale and otherwise invalid reports the other failure.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations are guarded by an explicit version expectation, and on both the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

Case assignment requires `expected_case_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ModerationCase`. If that number does not equal the case's current `version`, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because reassignment is a legitimate product operation: a second assignment against an already `ASSIGNED` case deliberately supersedes the prior one and would otherwise succeed on its own merits, so a client acting on a stale view of the case can only be detected by version and never by state.

Granting a reviewer certification requires `expected_reviewer_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Reviewer`. A mismatch with the reviewer's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEWER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A grant legitimately adds to `certified_policy_version_ids` and may clear a pending-retraining obligation, so without the guard a second grant carrying an older observed version would be accepted while acting on a reviewer profile that a policy activation cascade has already rewritten.

No other operation accepts or requires a version field. Deciding, escalating or returning a case, resolving an appeal, auditing or disputing a sample, and every policy-version or report transition are all unversioned.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities

Three quantities in this system must balance exactly, and every reversible action is specified in terms of restoring them.

The **account strike ledger** is never adjusted by a delta. An account's `strike_points` is always the full sum of `strike_points_applied` over every `DECIDED`, non-overturned case raised against content authored by that account, with no time window; the empty set sums to 0. `lifetime_removed_count` is re-derived in the very same step and by the very same discipline: it is always the full count of that author's `DECIDED`, non-overturned cases whose `decision_outcome` is `REMOVE`, never an increment or a decrement, so wherever this document says that count rises by one or falls by one it is describing the observable effect of that recount rather than a separate delta. Both a new decision and an overturned appeal trigger the same full recomputation, which is what guarantees that a reversal lands on precisely the value the account would have held had the decision never been recorded. `standing` is then recomputed from the fresh `strike_points` immediately, in the same request.

**Appeal credits** are a finite quota. Filing an appeal spends one credit from the appellant's `appeal_credits_remaining`; an appeal that ends `UPHELD` does not return it, and only an appeal that ends `OVERTURNED` refunds exactly one credit. The quota is therefore a budget for wrong decisions, and an account that files three losing appeals is out of credits.

The **reviewer exposure budget** protects moderator wellbeing. A reviewer's `exposure_points_today` equals the sum of the exposure charge of every case currently `ASSIGNED` to them plus the charge of every case they have decided since their most recent wellness break began. A case that leaves a reviewer *without* being decided — through reassignment to somebody else, through escalation, through a wellness break or through a suspension cascade — refunds its charge in full; a case the reviewer actually decided never refunds, because the reviewer was in fact exposed to the material. Both a wellness break and the suspension arm of the accuracy gate reset `exposure_points_today` to exactly 0, discarding even the retained decided-case exposure, and set `open_case_count` to 0.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Authors, Their Content and Their Standing

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` brings a platform author under trust-and-safety supervision by registering an **Account**. Registration supplies an `external_handle` of between 1 and 64 characters, which must be unique across all accounts under exact, case-sensitive whole-string comparison — nothing is trimmed or case-folded first, so `alice` and `Alice` are two distinct handles — a collision being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HANDLE`, and a `region_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the registration region, a two-character value that is not two uppercase letters being malformed input rather than a domain failure. The account's `tier` may be supplied as one of `STANDARD`, `HIGH_PROFILE` or `GOVERNMENT`, and defaults to `STANDARD` when omitted; the tier expresses enforcement sensitivity and, as §3.3 explains, both dampens classifier confidence and forces human review. An admin may also seed `appeal_credits_remaining`, which must not be negative; when the field is omitted the account opens with three credits. Everything else about a fresh account is computed and starts at its floor: `strike_points` at 0, `lifetime_removed_count` at 0 and `standing` at `GOOD_STANDING`.

`standing` is a posture, not an input. It is recomputed from `strike_points` immediately after every ledger recomputation, using inclusive breakpoints: an account with 10 or more points is `BANNED`, an account with 6 through 9 points is `SUSPENDED`, an account with 3 through 5 points is `LIMITED`, and an account with 2 or fewer points is `GOOD_STANDING`. Exactly 6 points is `SUSPENDED`; exactly 5 points is `LIMITED`. Because the ledger is recomputed in both directions, an overturned appeal that lowers the point total moves the posture back down the same scale — from `BANNED` to `SUSPENDED`, `LIMITED` or `GOOD_STANDING` as the fresh total dictates. A `BANNED` account is a hard block on ingestion: attempting to ingest new content for an author whose `standing` is `BANNED` fails with `ERR_ACCOUNT_BANNED` (HTTP 403), and that block lifts by itself as soon as a reversal moves the posture off `BANNED`. `BANNED` is the only posture that gates anything: `LIMITED` and `SUSPENDED` are reporting postures alone, so content is still ingested normally for an author sitting in either of them.

Content ingestion is likewise an admin operation. A **ContentItem** is ingested with the authoring `account_id`, a `language` from `EN`, `ES`, `DE`, `AR` or `HI`, a `media_kind` of `TEXT`, `IMAGE` or `VIDEO`, and an optional `graphic_weight` between 0 and 10 inclusive that defaults to 0. The server stamps `posted_at` from the logical clock. The `language` decides which specialty queue can accept work on the item; `media_kind` and `graphic_weight` together decide how much of a reviewer's wellness budget a case on this item consumes.

A content item advances along **two independent status dimensions**, and confusing them is the classic implementation error. `visibility_status` is what the public sees and takes the values `LIVE`, `AGE_GATED`, `REMOVED` and `REINSTATED`, starting at `LIVE`. `review_status` is where the item sits in the moderation pipeline and takes the values `CLEAR`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACTIONED` and `APPEALED`, starting at `CLEAR`. The two evolve on separate axes: a `WARN` decision moves `review_status` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ACTIONED` while `visibility_status` stays `LIVE`, and an overturned appeal on that same `WARN` case returns `review_status` to `CLEAR` while `visibility_status` is still `LIVE` and no reinstatement occurs. Only the `REMOVE` and `AGE_GATE` outcomes touch the visibility dimension, moving it from `LIVE` to `REMOVED` or `AGE_GATED` respectively, and only a reversal of such a decision moves it on to `REINSTATED` while stamping `reinstated_at`; that field stays null for an item that was never reinstated. Alongside these, `open_case_count` on the item tracks how many cases raised on it are still in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `ASSIGNED` or `ESCALATED`, rising by one as each case opens and falling by one as each case is decided.

Content items can be listed and narrowed by `account_id`, by `visibility_status` or by `review_status`, and any one item can be fetched on its own to read both dimensions at once. The classifier signals raised against a single item are also readable as their own collection, ordered oldest first by `created_at`; that collection carries signals of every `disposition`, `DISCARDED` ones included, defines no narrowing filter of its own beyond `limit` and `offset`, and resolves the content item before anything else, so an unresolvable id in the path is `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.2. Policy Taxonomy and Versioned Enforcement Rules

A `PolicyManager` builds the taxonomy from **PolicyCategory** nodes. Each category carries a `code` — a stable taxonomy key that classifiers cite, written in uppercase letters and underscores, beginning with a letter and running from 3 to 40 characters in total — and a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters. The `code` must be unique; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE`. A category may hang beneath another by supplying `parent_category_id`; omitting it produces a taxonomy root, and the field is null there. Each node declares a `severity_tier` of `T1`, `T2`, `T3` or `T4`, where `T1` is the most severe and `T4` the least, and a `specialty` of `VIOLENCE`, `CSAM_ADJACENT`, `HATE`, `SPAM`, `MISINFO` or `SELF_HARM` naming the reviewer training required to work the category. Severity may only soften as the tree deepens: a child's tier must be equal to or less severe than its parent's, so a child of a `T2` parent may be `T2`, `T3` or `T4` but never `T1`, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_CHILD_SEVERITY_EXCEEDS_PARENT` (HTTP 422). A newly created category opens with `status` `DRAFT` and a null `active_version_id`; both are computed, and the category's `status` reaches `ACTIVE` only by bubble-up when one of its own versions reaches `ACTIVE`, at which point `active_version_id` points at that version. Categories can be listed and narrowed by `parent_category_id`, by `status` or by `specialty`, which is how a client walks the self-referential tree one level at a time.

Enforcement rules live in **PolicyVersion** rows beneath a category, referenced through `policy_category_id`. A draft is written with an operator-facing `version_label` of 1 to 16 characters (for example `v3`), the two routing thresholds `auto_action_threshold` and `review_floor`, and the `enforcement_action` — one of `REMOVE`, `AGE_GATE` or `WARN` — that automatic enforcement will apply. The floor may not sit above the ceiling: `review_floor` must be less than or equal to `auto_action_threshold`, and a draft that inverts them is rejected with `ERR_THRESHOLD_ORDER_INVALID` (HTTP 422), both when the draft is written and again after any revision. Three further inputs are optional and default: `appeal_window_hours`, between 1 and 8760, defaults to 72 and sets how long after a decision an appeal may be filed; `review_sla_minutes`, between 5 and 10080, defaults to 240 and is the base human-review allowance before congestion and severity adjust it; and the boolean `requires_recertification` defaults to true and decides whether activating this version will revoke the certifications reviewers hold on the version it supersedes. The drafting manager's `X-User-ID` is captured as `authored_by_user_id` for the later duty-separation check. A fresh draft has `status` `DRAFT`, a null `supersedes_version_id`, `backtest_sample_size` at 0, and null `backtest_divergence_rate`, `backtest_completed_at`, `activated_by_user_id` and `activated_at`. Nothing about a draft is unique: `version_label` is an operator-facing label the server never checks for collision, and a category may carry any number of concurrent `DRAFT` versions.

Before a draft may govern anything it must be **back-tested** against history, which advances it `DRAFT → BACKTESTING`; attempting a back-test on a version that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). The back-test replays the candidate's own thresholds over the decided cases that the category's *current* `active_version_id` governed. The sample is every `ModerationCase` whose pinned `policy_version_id` is that currently active version, whose status is `DECIDED`, and whose `detection_signal_id` is not null — legal-request and manual-report cases carry no classifier confidence and are excluded entirely — taken in ascending `created_at` order. For each sampled case the historical routing class is `AUTO_ACTION` when its `decision_source` was `AUTO` and `HUMAN_REVIEW` otherwise. The predicted class is derived from the case's detection signal's stored `effective_confidence`, replayed exactly as stored and never recomputed, against the candidate draft: below the candidate's `review_floor` the prediction is `DISCARDED`; otherwise, if the category's `severity_tier` is `T1` the prediction is `HUMAN_REVIEW`; otherwise, if the author account's `tier` is anything other than `STANDARD` — read as that account's tier stands **at replay time**, not as it stood when the historical decision was taken — the prediction is `HUMAN_REVIEW`; otherwise, if the confidence is at or above the candidate's `auto_action_threshold` the prediction is `AUTO_ACTION`; and in every remaining case the prediction is `HUMAN_REVIEW`. `backtest_sample_size` records how many cases were replayed, and `backtest_divergence_rate` is the count of cases whose predicted class differs from their historical class divided by that sample size, rounded half up to four decimal places. An empty sample — a category with no active version yet, or one with no decided classifier-origin cases — yields exactly `0.0000`, which passes the activation gate. `backtest_completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Neither the back-test nor the activation described below takes any input beyond the path identifier, and neither reads a request body at all, so whatever accompanies such a call — well-formed JSON or not — is ignored rather than rejected.

A manager who dislikes what the back-test shows may **revise** the draft, which moves it `BACKTESTING → DRAFT`; revising anything that is not `BACKTESTING` is likewise `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. A revision may overwrite any of `auto_action_threshold`, `review_floor`, `review_sla_minutes`, `appeal_window_hours` and `requires_recertification`, each of them optional and an omitted one simply keeping the value already stored; every other field of the version — `version_label` and `enforcement_action` among them — is not revisable, and such a key appearing in the body is ignored rather than rejected, so a revision carrying no fields at all is accepted and does nothing beyond the state change and the voiding below. The floor-below-ceiling rule is then re-checked against the **merged** pair — the supplied value where one was supplied and the stored value where it was not — so a revision that lowers only `auto_action_threshold` beneath the untouched stored `review_floor` is `ERR_THRESHOLD_ORDER_INVALID`. The revision voids the stale evidence: `backtest_divergence_rate` and `backtest_completed_at` return to null and `backtest_sample_size` returns to 0, so the draft must be back-tested again before it can be activated.

**Activation** moves the version `BACKTESTING → ACTIVE` and is the most consequential operation in the system. It is refused unless the version is `BACKTESTING` with a non-null `backtest_divergence_rate` (`ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_BACKTESTED`, HTTP 409); unless the activating manager's `X-User-ID` differs from `authored_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403); and unless the recorded divergence is at most one quarter — a `backtest_divergence_rate` strictly greater than `0.2500` is rejected with `ERR_BACKTEST_DIVERGENCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), while exactly `0.2500` activates. Those three guards are enumerated here, not ordered: §2.8 puts the duty-separation check of tier 8 ahead of the lifecycle check of tier 9 and the divergence ceiling of tier 10 behind both, so a manager attempting to activate their own draft that was never back-tested is told `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the server stamps `activated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `activated_at` from the logical clock, and records `supersedes_version_id` as the category's prior `active_version_id`, leaving it null when this is the category's first activation. Four cascades then run in order. First, the category's prior `ACTIVE` version transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. Second — and only when the newly activated version's `requires_recertification` is true — every reviewer whose `certified_policy_version_ids` contains the superseded version's id drops that entry and gains the **newly activated** version's id in `pending_retraining_version_ids` — appended with set semantics, so an id already pending is not duplicated — with both edits landing inside a single mutation of that reviewer, hence one `updated_at` refresh and one `version` step each. A reviewer holding no certification on the superseded id is not visited at all and their `version` does not move, and no reviewer's `status` is touched by an activation. Third, again only when `requires_recertification` is true, every `ASSIGNED` case held by one of those reviewers, and every `ASSIGNED` or `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` case whose pinned `policy_version_id` is the superseded id, has `requires_recertified_review` set to true; those cases stay assigned to whoever holds them, because the block bites at decision time rather than at activation time. A case that already carries the flag is left entirely untouched, so neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moves, and a case in `ESCALATED` or `DECIDED` is never flagged whatever version it pins. When `requires_recertification` is false, neither certifications nor case flags are touched at all. Fourth, the parent category's `active_version_id` is repointed to the newly activated version and its `status` becomes `ACTIVE` if it was still `DRAFT` — after which detection signals citing that category's `code` stop failing with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY_VERSION`.

Policy versions can be fetched individually, showing their back-test outcome and supersession chain, and listed with narrowing by `policy_category_id`, by `supersedes_version_id` — which is how a client walks the supersession chain — or by `status`.

### 3.3. Automated Detection, Queue Routing and Review Deadlines

Human work is organised into **ReviewQueue** rows, each created by a `TrustSafetyAdmin` with a queue `name` of 1 to 80 characters, the `policy_category_id` of the single category it serves, the `language` it serves (one of `EN`, `ES`, `DE`, `AR`, `HI`) and its `specialty`. The queue's `specialty` must be identical to the specialty declared on its category; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_QUEUE_SPECIALTY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The category need not have reached `ACTIVE` — a queue may be stood up against a `DRAFT` category — and no uniqueness applies to queues at all: several queues may serve the same category in the same language, which is precisely the situation the ranking below has to resolve. Queues have no lifecycle state of their own — they are always available — but they carry two live depth counters that both start at 0: `open_case_count`, the number of cases on the queue in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `ASSIGNED` or `ESCALATED`, and `unassigned_case_count`, the number sitting specifically in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`. Queues can be fetched individually to read those counters and listed with narrowing by `policy_category_id` or by `language`.

Whenever a case that needs human eyes is opened, the server picks its queue by constraint. The candidate set is every queue whose `policy_category_id` equals the case's category and whose `language` equals the content item's `language`. Candidates are ranked by `unassigned_case_count` ascending — the shallowest backlog wins — and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending, that is, the older queue wins; the queue's `id` is never used as a tie-breaker. If the candidate set is empty the entire request fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_QUEUE` (HTTP 422) and nothing is persisted, not even the detection signal that provoked it.

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` submits a **DetectionSignal** for a `content_item_id` under a `policy_category_code`, naming the model in `classifier_id` (1 to 40 characters) and its raw `classifier_confidence` as a value between 0.0000 and 1.0000. The natural key is resolved to a category row, and an unknown code fails with `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`. Content whose `visibility_status` is already `REMOVED` cannot be scanned again: such a submission is rejected with `ERR_CONTENT_NOT_LIVE` (HTTP 409). The resolved category must have `status` `ACTIVE` and a non-null `active_version_id`, or the submission fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 409). That active version is the one this signal's outcome will pin, and its `version_label` is echoed onto the signal as `pinned_policy_version_label` for audit whenever a case results. The signal row records the submitted `policy_category_code` itself and exposes no resolved category identifier.

The raw model score is not used directly. The server first computes `effective_confidence` in a pinned order: take the confidence multiplier for the author account's `tier` — 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 0.85 for `HIGH_PROFILE`, 0.80 for `GOVERNMENT` — and multiply the `classifier_confidence` by it; then add a repeat-offender bonus of 0.01 for each of the account's current `strike_points`, counting at most ten points, so the bonus is at most 0.10; then clamp the result so it can never exceed 1.0; and only then round half up to four decimal places, exactly once. The account's `strike_points` is read as it currently stands, that is, after every previously decided non-overturned case on that account. Clamping happens before rounding, never after.

`effective_confidence` and the thresholds of the pinned version then decide the signal's `disposition`, evaluated as an ordered chain of five branches. If the effective confidence is strictly below the pinned version's `review_floor`, the disposition is `DISCARDED`: the signal row is still persisted and readable afterwards, no case is created, `resulting_case_id` and `pinned_policy_version_label` stay null, and the content item is untouched. Queue selection is attempted only on the `HUMAN_REVIEW` branch, so a `DISCARDED` — or an `AUTO_ACTION` — submission succeeds even when no queue serves the category and language at all, and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_QUEUE` can only arise where human work was about to be queued. Otherwise, if the category's `severity_tier` is `T1`, the disposition is `HUMAN_REVIEW` with `escalation_tier` 1 and `escalation_reason` `IMMINENT_HARM` — the most severe tier is never auto-actioned, however confident the classifier. Otherwise, if the author account's `tier` is `HIGH_PROFILE` or `GOVERNMENT`, the disposition is again `HUMAN_REVIEW`, with `escalation_tier` 1 and `escalation_reason` `HIGH_PROFILE`. Otherwise, if the effective confidence is at or above the pinned version's `auto_action_threshold`, the disposition is `AUTO_ACTION`. In every remaining case the disposition is `HUMAN_REVIEW` with `escalation_tier` 0 and a null `escalation_reason`.

An `AUTO_ACTION` signal mints a **ModerationCase** that is born in `DECIDED`, with `origin` `CLASSIFIER` and `detection_signal_id` pointing back at the signal, and the signal records the new case in `resulting_case_id`. Such a case is never queued: `queue_id` and `assigned_reviewer_id` and `decided_by_reviewer_id` are all null, `decision_source` is `AUTO`, `decision_outcome` is copied from the pinned version's `enforcement_action`, and `decided_at` is the logical clock's current instant. Because no queue and no reviewer were involved, no review deadline applies — `sla_due_at` stays null — and `sla_breached` remains false. The server then charges `strike_points_applied` by the rule set out in §3.5, sets `appeal_deadline_at` to `decided_at` advanced by the pinned version's `appeal_window_hours`, applies the enforcement to the content — `visibility_status` to `REMOVED` for a `REMOVE` outcome, to `AGE_GATED` for `AGE_GATE`, and left as it is for `WARN`, with `review_status` set to `ACTIONED` in all three — and finally updates the author: `lifetime_removed_count` rises by one when the outcome was `REMOVE`, then `strike_points` is recomputed from the whole ledger, then `standing` is recomputed from that fresh total, in that order.

A `HUMAN_REVIEW` signal instead mints a case in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`. Its `policy_version_id` is pinned to the category's `active_version_id`; its `queue_id` comes from the queue selection above; the queue's `open_case_count` and `unassigned_case_count` each rise by one **before** the review deadline is computed; the content item's `review_status` becomes `UNDER_REVIEW` and its `open_case_count` rises by one; and the escalation posture computed by the routing chain — `escalation_tier` 1 with `IMMINENT_HARM` for a `T1` category, `escalation_tier` 1 with `HIGH_PROFILE` for a non-`STANDARD` account on a category that is not `T1`, and otherwise `escalation_tier` 0 with a null reason — is recorded on the case. Note that a mandatory-human-review case of this kind still enters `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`; being flagged at `escalation_tier` 1 does not put it into `ESCALATED`. Whatever its origin, a newly minted case opens with its remaining computed fields at their floors: `sla_breached`, `is_overturned` and `requires_recertified_review` are all false, `strike_points_applied`, `exposure_points_charged` and `reassignment_count` are all the integer 0 rather than null, and `assigned_reviewer_id`, `decided_by_reviewer_id`, `decision_outcome`, `decision_source`, `decided_at` and `appeal_deadline_at` are all null.

The review deadline, `sla_due_at`, is computed in a strictly pinned order, and the order matters because the queue counters the formula reads are the ones this very case has just changed. Step one increments the queue's `open_case_count` and `unassigned_case_count` for the new case. Step two reads the queue's `open_case_count` **after** that increment, so the count includes this case; reading the pre-increment count is a different and wrong answer. Step three converts that depth into a congestion multiplier: a queue holding 5 or fewer open cases multiplies by 1.0, a queue holding 6 through 15 multiplies by 1.5, and a queue holding 16 or more multiplies by 2.0. Step four takes the pinned version's `review_sla_minutes`, multiplies it by that congestion multiplier, divides by the severity divisor of the case's category — 4 for `T1`, 2 for `T2`, 1 for `T3` and 1 for `T4` — and takes the ceiling of the single resulting quotient to a whole number of minutes. Step five sets `sla_due_at` to the logical clock's current instant advanced by that many minutes.

Two exceptions ride on top of that procedure. A case that is **born** with an `escalation_tier` of 1 or more still performs step one — the queue's counters always account for it — but skips the congestion and severity arithmetic entirely: for a `T1` or non-`STANDARD`-account case its `sla_due_at` is simply the current instant plus 30 minutes, and for a case of `LEGAL_REQUEST` origin it is the current instant plus 1440 minutes, that is, twenty-four hours. And when an escalated case is later returned to its queue, only `unassigned_case_count` rises — `open_case_count` is left alone, because the case has been counted as open continuously since it was created, and incrementing it a second time would double-count one case — after which steps two through five run again against the queue's current `open_case_count`.

Signals are immutable and can be fetched individually to inspect their computed `effective_confidence`, `disposition`, `resulting_case_id` and `pinned_policy_version_label`.

### 3.4. The Reviewer Workforce: Certification, Eligibility and Assignment

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` onboards a **Reviewer** with a `display_name` of 1 to 80 characters, a non-empty `languages` array without duplicates drawn from `EN`, `ES`, `DE`, `AR` and `HI`, a non-empty `specialties` array without duplicates drawn from the same six specialties the taxonomy uses, and a shift definition: `utc_offset_minutes` between -720 and 840 giving the reviewer's fixed offset from UTC, an inclusive `shift_start_local_hour` between 0 and 23, and an exclusive `shift_end_local_hour` between 1 and 24. When `shift_end_local_hour` is less than or equal to `shift_start_local_hour` the window wraps midnight, and when the two are equal that wrapped window covers every hour of the day, so such a reviewer is never off shift. The reviewer's `role_class` is one of `FRONTLINE`, which takes ordinary cases, `SENIOR`, which may additionally take escalated cases and appeals, or `QA`, which performs audit sampling. Two wellness controls default when omitted: `max_concurrent_cases`, between 1 and 20, defaults to 3 and caps how many cases the reviewer may hold at once, and `daily_exposure_cap_points`, between 1 and 200, defaults to 20 and caps the exposure the reviewer may carry at once. Everything else on a new reviewer is computed: `status` is `ACTIVE`, `certified_policy_version_ids` and `pending_retraining_version_ids` are empty arrays, `open_case_count` and `exposure_points_today` are 0, the four quality counters `audited_count`, `agree_count`, `appealed_count` and `upheld_appeal_count` are 0, both `accuracy_score_audit_only` and `accuracy_score_blended` are null, `wellness_breaks_taken` is 0, and `wellness_break_start` and `wellness_break_end` are null. Because a fresh reviewer holds no certifications, they cannot yet decide anything.

Certification is a `PolicyManager` act. A grant names the `policy_version_id` being granted and carries `expected_reviewer_version` as described in §2.9; that expectation must be a non-negative integer, and a negative or non-integer value fails as malformed input at tier 3 rather than as a staleness mismatch. The version being granted must be in `BACKTESTING` or `ACTIVE`; a `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` version cannot be certified on and is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_VERSION_NOT_CERTIFIABLE` (HTTP 409). A successful grant appends the id to `certified_policy_version_ids` with set semantics — granting the same version twice leaves one entry and is not an error — removes that id from `pending_retraining_version_ids` if present, and increments the reviewer's `version`. A grant against a reviewer in `ACTIVE` leaves them `ACTIVE`. A grant against a reviewer in `PENDING_RETRAINING` moves them `PENDING_RETRAINING → ACTIVE` once `pending_retraining_version_ids` is empty; the grant is accepted when the granted id is in that pending list, when the reviewer holds no certification for that category yet, or when the pending list is already empty. That last case is not a loophole but the designed route back: a demotion by the accuracy gate adds nothing to the pending list, so a grant that merely re-affirms an existing certification is the only way for such a reviewer to return to duty. A grant against a reviewer in `SUSPENDED` likewise moves them `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` as a recalibration grant, adding the granted id to their certifications. A grant against a reviewer who is `ON_WELLNESS_BREAK` records the certification and clears any matching pending obligation but leaves that status exactly where it is; only return-to-duty ends a break.

Reviewers can be fetched individually — the response carries both accuracy variants, both exposure counters and both certification lists — and listed with narrowing by `status`, by served `language`, by `specialty` or by `role_class`.

Assignment is performed by a `TrustSafetyAdmin` or a `QAAuditor` and always carries `expected_case_version`, which must be a non-negative integer — a negative or non-integer value fails as malformed input at tier 3 rather than as a staleness mismatch. It drives three transitions: `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT → ASSIGNED` for a first assignment, `ASSIGNED → ASSIGNED` for a reassignment that legitimately supersedes the prior one, and `ESCALATED → ASSIGNED` when a senior reviewer picks up escalated work; a case that has already reached `DECIDED` can be assigned no longer, and that attempt is `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The caller may name a specific reviewer in `target_reviewer_id`, or omit it and let the server choose, in which case the optional `exclude_reviewer_ids` array names reviewers the server must not choose.

When the server chooses, it evaluates a seven-part eligibility filter and every part must hold. The reviewer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The certification target for the case — as defined in the paragraph below — must be present in the reviewer's `certified_policy_version_ids`. The queue's `language` must be one of the reviewer's `languages`, and the queue's `specialty` must be one of the reviewer's `specialties`; these two predicates are evaluated only when a queue is in play, so the appeal-routing reuse of this same selector described in §3.7 — which supplies no queue — skips both of them. The reviewer's `open_case_count` must be strictly less than their `max_concurrent_cases`. The reviewer's `exposure_points_today` plus this case's exposure charge must be less than or equal to their `daily_exposure_cap_points`, so an assignment that lands exactly on the cap is allowed. The logical clock, converted into the reviewer's local time, must fall inside their shift window. And the reviewer must be neither a member of `exclude_reviewer_ids` nor the case's current `assigned_reviewer_id`, which is what makes a reassignment always move the case to somebody new. One further predicate applies only when the case is `ESCALATED`: the reviewer's `role_class` must be `SENIOR` or `QA`. Local time is derived by adding the reviewer's `utc_offset_minutes` to the logical clock's UTC instant and taking the hour of the resulting local time as a number from 0 to 23; the reviewer is in shift when that hour is at or after `shift_start_local_hour` and strictly before `shift_end_local_hour`, or, for a window that wraps midnight, when the hour is at or after `shift_start_local_hour` or strictly before `shift_end_local_hour`. Surviving candidates are ranked by `open_case_count` ascending, then by `accuracy_score_blended` descending with a null score treated as `1.0000` so that an unmeasured reviewer ranks with the best, and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending; the reviewer's `id` is never a tie-breaker. If no reviewer satisfies every predicate the assignment fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER` (HTTP 409), the case keeps `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, and its `version` is **not** incremented.

The certification target is conditional on the case's `requires_recertified_review` flag, and this is the rule the whole recertification cascade exists to serve. While the flag is false, the reviewer must hold the case's **pinned** `policy_version_id`. Once the flag is true — because the pinned version was superseded by an activation that required recertification, or because the holding reviewer was demoted to `PENDING_RETRAINING` — the reviewer must instead hold the category's **current** `active_version_id`. Either way, a reviewer without the required certification is rejected with `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422). That check guards three operations: case assignment, case decision, and the routing of an appeal to an independent reviewer.

When `target_reviewer_id` names a specific reviewer, the candidate pool is that one reviewer and each predicate is checked individually so the caller learns exactly what disqualified them, in the order fixed by §2.8: a reviewer who is not `ACTIVE` yields `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409); a missing certification yields `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`; a reviewer who does not serve the queue's language or its specialty yields `ERR_REVIEWER_LANGUAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); an assignment whose charge would push `exposure_points_today` past `daily_exposure_cap_points` yields `ERR_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); a target whose local hour falls outside the half-open shift window yields `ERR_REVIEWER_OFF_SHIFT` (HTTP 422); a target whose `open_case_count` already equals `max_concurrent_cases` yields `ERR_REVIEWER_AT_CAPACITY` (HTTP 422); and a `FRONTLINE` target directed at an `ESCALATED` case yields `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_ESCALATION` (HTTP 422). Two predicates of the server-choice filter survive on this directed path but are reported differently: once all seven predicates above have passed, a named target who is a member of `exclude_reviewer_ids`, or who is already the case's current `assigned_reviewer_id`, is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER` (HTTP 409) rather than with a predicate-specific code. Naming a reviewer therefore does not exempt them from either exclusion; it only moves both tests to the very end, after every individually-diagnosed predicate has been cleared.

A successful assignment sets `assigned_reviewer_id`, raises that reviewer's `open_case_count` by one, records `exposure_points_charged` on the case as the content item's `graphic_weight` multiplied by its media multiplier — 1 for `TEXT`, 2 for `IMAGE`, 3 for `VIDEO` — and adds that charge to the reviewer's `exposure_points_today`. On a first assignment out of `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` the queue's `unassigned_case_count` falls by one while `open_case_count` stays where it is. On a reassignment the outgoing reviewer's `open_case_count` falls by one and their `exposure_points_today` is reduced by exactly the case's `exposure_points_charged`, the incoming reviewer's counters rise by the same amounts, and the case's `reassignment_count` — which counts assignments after the first — rises by one. An `ESCALATED → ASSIGNED` pick-up likewise raises the new holder's `open_case_count` and `exposure_points_today` and increments `reassignment_count`, closing the `ESCALATED → ASSIGNED → ESCALATED` cycle a case may travel more than once. Every successful assignment increments the case's `version`.

### 3.5. Working a Case: Hand-Opened Origins, Decisions, Escalation and Return

Not every case begins with a classifier. A case may also be opened by hand against a `content_item_id` under a `policy_category_code`, with an `origin` of either `LEGAL_REQUEST` or `MANUAL_REPORT`; the `origin` `CLASSIFIER` is reserved for cases minted by detection and cannot be requested — it is simply not a recognised member of this operation's `origin` enum, so asking for it is rejected at tier 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as an authorization failure. A `LEGAL_REQUEST` may only be opened by `LegalCounsel`, and a `MANUAL_REPORT` only by a `TrustSafetyAdmin`; the wrong role for an origin is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Both origins are subject to the same two gates as a detection signal: the content item's `visibility_status` may not be `REMOVED` (`ERR_CONTENT_NOT_LIVE`) and the resolved category must be `ACTIVE` with a non-null `active_version_id` (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY_VERSION`).

A legal case carries a `legal_jurisdiction` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the requesting jurisdiction and a `legal_reference` holding the government or court reference string; a `LEGAL_REQUEST` submitted without them is rejected with `ERR_LEGAL_CASE_REQUIRES_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 422). Both fields stay null on cases of any other origin: supplying either one alongside `origin` `MANUAL_REPORT` is not an error, and the value is still shape-checked at tier 3 — a `legal_jurisdiction` that is not exactly two uppercase letters is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` whatever the origin — but it is then discarded rather than stored. A legal case opens directly in `ESCALATED` with `escalation_reason` `LEGAL_REQUEST` and `escalation_tier` 2. Its `queue_id` is still selected by the queue rule so the work is attributed to the right specialty, and the queue's `open_case_count` rises by one — but its `unassigned_case_count` does **not**, because an `ESCALATED` case is not queue-assignable. Its `sla_due_at` is the current instant plus 1440 minutes. The content item's `review_status` becomes `UNDER_REVIEW` and its `open_case_count` rises by one.

A manual report opens in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` and behaves in every respect like a human-review case minted by detection: the category's `active_version_id` is pinned into `policy_version_id`, a queue is selected, both queue counters rise before the deadline is computed, the content item moves to `UNDER_REVIEW` with its `open_case_count` raised by one, and the escalation posture is set from the same rule — `escalation_tier` 1 with `IMMINENT_HARM` for a `T1` category, `escalation_tier` 1 with `HIGH_PROFILE` for a `HIGH_PROFILE` or `GOVERNMENT` account on a category that is not `T1`, and otherwise `escalation_tier` 0 with a null `escalation_reason` — with the corresponding 30-minute deadline in the two flagged cases. A manual case has no classifier behind it, so its `detection_signal_id` stays null.

**Recording a decision** advances the case `ASSIGNED → DECIDED` and is the reviewer's core act. The reviewer supplies an `outcome` of `REMOVE`, `AGE_GATE`, `WARN` or `NO_ACTION` and may attach a free-text `rationale`, which the server validates as a string but does not persist: like the optional free-text `reason` on return-to-queue and on a wellness break, it lands in no stored field and in no response body. Four conditions guard it, and the list that follows only enumerates them — it is not an evaluation order, because which of several competing failures is reported is fixed by §2.8, whose tier 6 ownership check therefore precedes every lifecycle-state check of its tier 9: the case must not already be `DECIDED` (`ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED`); it must be `ASSIGNED` when a `Reviewer` decides and `ESCALATED` when `LegalCounsel` decides on the legal path below, any other status yielding `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNED`; when the actor is a `Reviewer`, `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `assigned_reviewer_id` (`ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`), a check the legal arm does not carry because no reviewer entity stands behind a `LegalCounsel` decision; the reviewer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE`), which is what makes a demotion to `PENDING_RETRAINING` bite even though the case stayed assigned; and the conditional certification check of §3.4 must pass (`ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`). Those last two guards, exactly like the ownership check, are carried only on the `Reviewer` arm: the legal arm below looks up no reviewer row at all, so neither `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE` nor `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED` can ever arise from a `LegalCounsel` decision.

On success the case records `decision_source` `HUMAN`, `decided_by_reviewer_id` from `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the logical clock, and `sla_breached` as true exactly when `decided_at` is later than `sla_due_at` — a judgement made once, at this instant, and never revisited afterwards. `decision_outcome` holds the supplied outcome and `appeal_deadline_at` becomes `decided_at` advanced by the **pinned** version's `appeal_window_hours`. The deciding reviewer's `open_case_count` falls by one, but their `exposure_points_today` is deliberately **not** reduced: they saw the material, so the wellness charge stands. The case keeps both its `assigned_reviewer_id` and its `queue_id` after the decision; neither reference is cleared, which is what lets a decided case still be read back against the reviewer who held it. The queue's `open_case_count` falls by one. On the content item, `visibility_status` becomes `REMOVED` for a `REMOVE` outcome and `AGE_GATED` for `AGE_GATE`, and is left untouched for `WARN` and `NO_ACTION`; `review_status` becomes `ACTIONED` for `REMOVE`, `AGE_GATE` and `WARN` but `CLEAR` for `NO_ACTION`; and the item's `open_case_count` falls by one. Finally the author's ledger is updated in a fixed order: `lifetime_removed_count` rises by one when the outcome was `REMOVE`, then `strike_points` is recomputed in full, then `standing` is recomputed from the fresh total.

The penalty a decision carries, `strike_points_applied`, is computed once at decision time and frozen thereafter; on a case that has not yet been decided the field is the integer 0 rather than null, and it keeps its frozen value even after an overturn, because an overturned case is dropped from the ledger through `is_overturned` and not by rewriting this field. Take the base points of the case's pinned category severity — 8 points for `T1`, 4 for `T2`, 2 for `T3` and 1 for `T4`. Take the weight of the recorded outcome — 1.0 for `REMOVE`, 0.75 for `AGE_GATE`, 0.25 for `WARN` and 0.0 for `NO_ACTION`. Take the repeat multiplier, which is one plus a quarter-point step for each of the author's prior removals, counting at most four removals, so the multiplier runs from 1.0 for a first-time author up to 2.0 for an author with four or more removals. The author's `lifetime_removed_count` is read as it stood **immediately before** this decision's own increment, so a `REMOVE` decision never inflates its own multiplier. Multiply all three factors together, and only then take the ceiling of the single product to a whole number of points. Rounding each factor before multiplying produces a different integer and is wrong: base 2 points at weight 0.75 with a multiplier of 1.25 gives a product of 1.875, whose ceiling is 2 — not the 3 that per-factor rounding would yield. A `NO_ACTION` outcome always charges 0 points.

`LegalCounsel` decides its own cases along a separate arm, `ESCALATED → DECIDED`, permitted only when the case's `escalation_reason` is `LEGAL_REQUEST`; an attempt on any other escalated case is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. That restriction is evaluated at tier 2 against the case as it already stands, so it can only fire on a `case_id` that actually resolves; when the path names no case at all the request instead falls through to well-formedness and then to `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. On that arm `decision_source` is `LEGAL` and `decided_by_reviewer_id` stays null, because no reviewer entity is accountable and no reviewer's accuracy is affected. Everything else matches the human arm exactly: `decided_at` and `sla_breached` are stamped the same way, `strike_points_applied` and `appeal_deadline_at` are computed the same way, the content item's visibility and review dimensions move the same way, the queue's `open_case_count` and the item's `open_case_count` each fall by one, and the author's `lifetime_removed_count`, `strike_points` and `standing` cascade in the same order.

**Escalation** moves a case `ASSIGNED → ESCALATED` and may be triggered by the holding `Reviewer` or by a `QAAuditor`, who supplies an `escalation_reason` of `IMMINENT_HARM`, `LEGAL_REQUEST`, `HIGH_PROFILE` or `CALIBRATION`. The acting reviewer must be the case's `assigned_reviewer_id` (`ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`) and must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE`) — both conditions bind whichever of the two roles acts, so a `QAAuditor` may escalate only a case assigned to their own reviewer row and only while that row is `ACTIVE`; a case that is not `ASSIGNED` cannot be escalated and yields `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Escalation records the supplied reason, raises `escalation_tier` to 2, and releases the reviewer: `assigned_reviewer_id` becomes null, the prior holder's `open_case_count` falls by one and their `exposure_points_today` is reduced by exactly the case's `exposure_points_charged`, since the case is leaving undecided and both workload and exposure must be conserved. The case itself keeps its `exposure_points_charged` at the figure the assignment recorded — the refund is taken from the reviewer only, and the field is simply re-derived from the content item the next time the case is assigned. The queue's `unassigned_case_count` stays where it is — an escalated case is open but not assignable — and so does its `open_case_count`. The deadline is tightened to the current instant plus 30 minutes, and the congestion multiplier is **not** applied on this branch. The case's `version` increments.

**Returning a case to its queue** moves it `ESCALATED → PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` and is available to a `QAAuditor` or a `TrustSafetyAdmin`, who may record a `reason`. The case must be `ESCALATED`: one that has already reached `DECIDED` is rejected with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_DECIDED`, and any other non-`ESCALATED` status is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`. On return, `escalation_tier` drops to 1 — de-escalated but still flagged — while `escalation_reason` is retained as history rather than cleared. The queue's `unassigned_case_count` rises by one and its `open_case_count` is untouched, and `sla_due_at` is recomputed by the congestion-and-severity procedure of §3.3 against the queue's post-return `open_case_count`. The case's `version` increments.

Individual cases can be fetched to read their pinned version, deadline, escalation posture and decision record. The case collection is the reverse-reachability channel for work created as a side effect of detection, and can be narrowed by `content_item_id`, `detection_signal_id`, `policy_version_id`, `queue_id`, `assigned_reviewer_id`, `decided_by_reviewer_id`, `status` or `origin`.

### 3.6. Wellness Protection and Exposure Accounting

Moderation is harmful work, and the system treats reviewer exposure as a budgeted quantity. Every case charges `exposure_points_charged` equal to the content item's `graphic_weight` multiplied by the media multiplier of its `media_kind` — 1 for `TEXT`, 2 for `IMAGE`, 3 for `VIDEO` — so a graphic-weight-6 video costs 18 points against a default budget of 20 while the same weight in text costs 6. The charge is levied when a case is assigned and released only if the case leaves the reviewer's hands without a decision.

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` may place a reviewer on a protective break lasting `break_minutes` — any integer of 1 or more, with no upper bound — optionally noting a `reason`. The reviewer must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The requested break occupies the half-open window beginning at the current logical instant and ending `break_minutes` later, and it may not overlap the reviewer's retained window running from `wellness_break_start` up to but not including `wellness_break_end`; an overlap is rejected with `ERR_WELLNESS_BREAK_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because both windows are half-open, a request that starts exactly at the retained window's end instant does not overlap and is accepted. The comparison is only made when both retained instants are already present, so a reviewer's very first break can never collide, and each accepted break **overwrites** the retained pair, meaning only the most recent break is ever tested against. A successful request moves the reviewer `ACTIVE → ON_WELLNESS_BREAK`, sets `wellness_break_start` to the current instant and `wellness_break_end` to that instant plus `break_minutes`, and increments `wellness_breaks_taken` — all of it one single mutation of the reviewer row, so that row's `version` rises by exactly one however much the workload cascade below moves.

The break then cascades over the reviewer's live workload. Every case they hold in `ASSIGNED` transitions `ASSIGNED → PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` with `assigned_reviewer_id` cleared. Because those cases have returned, the reviewer's `open_case_count` is set to 0 and their `exposure_points_today` is set to 0 — the reset discards even the exposure retained from cases they had already decided — and each affected queue's `unassigned_case_count` rises by one per returned case. Two things are deliberately left alone: each affected queue's `open_case_count`, because those cases never stopped being open, and each returned case's `sla_due_at`, which is not recomputed; the deadline is only ever recomputed on the explicit return-to-queue path described in §3.5.

Ending a break moves the reviewer `ON_WELLNESS_BREAK → ACTIVE`; a reviewer who is not `ON_WELLNESS_BREAK` cannot be returned to duty and the attempt yields `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ON_BREAK` (HTTP 409). Return-to-duty takes no input beyond the path identifier and reads no request body at all, so whatever accompanies the call — well-formed JSON or not — is ignored rather than rejected, and the transition itself moves nothing but `status`: no case, queue or counter is disturbed on the way back in. Returning to duty does **not** clear `wellness_break_start` and `wellness_break_end`: those two instants are retained precisely so the next break request has something to be tested for overlap against.

### 3.7. Appeals, Reinstatement and Restoration

An enforcement decision is never the last word. The author, acting as `AccountHolder`, files an **Appeal** against a decided case by naming the `case_id` and stating their `grounds_text` in 1 to 500 characters. Five conditions are checked: `X-User-ID` must equal the author account of the appealed case's content item, or the filing is `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403); the case must be `DECIDED`, or `ERR_CASE_NOT_DECIDED` (HTTP 409); no appeal may already exist for that `case_id`, since each case admits at most one, or `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409); the logical clock must be at or before the case's `appeal_deadline_at`, or `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422) — filing exactly at the deadline instant is still in time; and the account's `appeal_credits_remaining` must be at least 1, or `ERR_APPEAL_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 402).

A filed appeal opens in `SUBMITTED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock and `account_id` copied from the appealed case's content item rather than taken from the request. The account's `appeal_credits_remaining` falls by one immediately. On the content item, `review_status` becomes `APPEALED` unconditionally — normally out of `ACTIONED`, but equally out of the `CLEAR` that a `NO_ACTION` decision left behind — while `visibility_status` is left exactly as the decision left it — a removed item stays `REMOVED` for the duration of the appeal. Nothing about the original decider moves yet: their `appealed_count` and both accuracy variants change only when the appeal reaches a terminal status. The appeal's `independent_reviewer_id`, `resolved_at`, `resolution_note` are null and `reinstated_content` is false at this point.

A `QAAuditor` routes a submitted appeal to an independent reviewer, moving it `SUBMITTED → INDEPENDENT_REVIEW`; an appeal that is not `SUBMITTED` cannot be routed and yields `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` (HTTP 409). The auditor may name a `target_reviewer_id` or omit it and let the server select by the ranked selector of §3.4 with the original decider excluded from the pool. Whoever is chosen must have a `role_class` of `SENIOR` or `QA`, must be `ACTIVE`, and must not be the case's `decided_by_reviewer_id` — routing an appeal back to the reviewer who decided it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and must hold the case's certification target in `certified_policy_version_ids` — the conditional target of §3.4, that is, the pinned `policy_version_id` while the case's `requires_recertified_review` is false and the category's current `active_version_id` once it is true — failing which the routing is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Because appeal review is off-queue, the selector runs with no queue and a zero exposure charge, so the language and specialty predicates of §3.4 are not applied here. A named `target_reviewer_id` is vetted against a shorter ladder than the one §3.4 applies to a directed case assignment, in this order: a target who is not `ACTIVE` is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE`, a missing certification is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and a `FRONTLINE` `role_class` is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_ESCALATION` (HTTP 422) — and that is all: a named target's shift window, their `open_case_count` against `max_concurrent_cases` and their exposure budget are never examined. When the auditor omits the target and the server selects instead, the full filter of §3.4 does run, so shift and capacity genuinely do constrain that pool, and an empty pool is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER`. Appeal review is off-queue work: setting `independent_reviewer_id` levies no exposure charge and does not touch that reviewer's `open_case_count`.

The independent reviewer then resolves the appeal by supplying a `resolution` of `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` with an optional `resolution_note`. Only that reviewer may do so — `X-User-ID` must equal `independent_reviewer_id`, else `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER` (HTTP 403) — and only from `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW`, else `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. Those two are the only conditions: the resolving reviewer's own `status` is never examined, so a reviewer whom the accuracy gate has since moved to `PENDING_RETRAINING` or `SUSPENDED` may still resolve the appeal they were routed. The two arms are deliberately asymmetric, and both are specified in full.

On the `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW → UPHELD` arm the original decision stands. `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the `resolution_note` is stored, and `reinstated_content` stays false. The content item returns to `review_status` `ACTIONED` with `visibility_status` unchanged, and the account's `appeal_credits_remaining` is **not** refunded — a losing appeal costs a credit. The deciding reviewer's `appealed_count` rises by one and their `upheld_appeal_count` rises by one; then `accuracy_score_blended` is recomputed; then the accuracy gate of §3.8 runs.

On the `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW → OVERTURNED` arm the decision is unwound in six ordered cascades. First, `resolved_at` is stamped and the case's `is_overturned` becomes true, which permanently excludes that case from the strike ledger and from restated transparency volumes. Second, following from that, the content item is restored: when its `visibility_status` was `REMOVED` or `AGE_GATED` it becomes `REINSTATED`, `reinstated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the appeal's `reinstated_content` becomes true; when the reversed decision was a `WARN` or a `NO_ACTION` the item's visibility was never disturbed, so no reinstatement occurs and `reinstated_content` stays false. Either way the item's `review_status` returns to `CLEAR`. Third, also following from the overturn, the author is made whole: `lifetime_removed_count` falls by one when the reversed `decision_outcome` was `REMOVE`, `strike_points` is recomputed in full over the account's remaining decided non-overturned cases, and `appeal_credits_remaining` rises by one as a refund. Fourth, following from that recomputation, `standing` is recomputed from the fresh point total by the same inclusive breakpoints, which is how an account walks back from `BANNED`. The credit refund, the ledger recount and the fresh `standing` are all one single mutation of the account row, so that row's `version` rises by exactly one across those two cascades together. Fifth, again following from the overturn, the original decider's `appealed_count` rises by one while `upheld_appeal_count` is left unchanged, both accuracy variants are recomputed, and the accuracy gate may transition that reviewer `ACTIVE → PENDING_RETRAINING` or `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. Sixth, following from whichever demotion the gate chose: on the `PENDING_RETRAINING` arm every case still held by that reviewer in `ASSIGNED` has `requires_recertified_review` set to true and stays assigned to them; on the `SUSPENDED` arm those cases instead transition `ASSIGNED → PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` with `assigned_reviewer_id` cleared, the reviewer's `open_case_count` and `exposure_points_today` are both set to 0, and each affected queue's `unassigned_case_count` rises by one per returned case.

Appeals can be fetched individually and listed with narrowing by `case_id`, `account_id`, `independent_reviewer_id` or `status`; an `AccountHolder` reading either way sees only appeals whose `account_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. On the list path that scoping is silent when the filter is omitted — the listing is simply narrowed to their own account — but an `AccountHolder` who explicitly passes an `account_id` naming somebody else is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_OWNED` rather than handed an empty page.

### 3.8. Quality Assurance, Calibration and the Accuracy Gate

Independently of appeals, decided work is sampled for quality. A `QAAuditor` draws an **AuditSample** by naming a `case_id` and a `sampling_reason` of `RANDOM`, `TARGETED_LOW_ACCURACY` or `POST_POLICY_CHANGE`. The case must be `DECIDED` (`ERR_CASE_NOT_DECIDED`), the acting reviewer's `role_class` must be `QA` (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`) — which also means a `QAAuditor` who has no reviewer row of their own at all cannot draw a sample and fails with that same `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — and `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `decided_by_reviewer_id` — nobody audits their own decision, and an attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A fresh sample opens in `SAMPLED` with `auditor_reviewer_id` taken from `X-User-ID` and `counts_toward_accuracy` set to true, but with `verdict`, `correct_outcome`, `audited_at`, `dispute_note` and `disputed_at` all null; no accuracy figure moves until a verdict exists. Nothing limits a case to a single sample: unlike an appeal, one decided case may be drawn over and over, by the same auditor or by different ones, and every draw mints an independent sample.

Recording a verdict moves the sample `SAMPLED → AUDITED`, and the same operation also closes the calibration loop by moving a contested sample `DISPUTED → AUDITED`, overwriting the earlier verdict with the calibrated one. Any other status is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_AUDITABLE` (HTTP 409). The auditor supplies a `verdict` of `AGREE`, `DISAGREE_MINOR` or `DISAGREE_MATERIAL`, and whenever the verdict is either kind of disagreement they must also supply the `correct_outcome` they consider right — one of `REMOVE`, `AGE_GATE`, `WARN` or `NO_ACTION` — the omission of which is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Any `QAAuditor` may record the verdict: this operation checks neither the sample's `auditor_reviewer_id` nor the actor's `role_class`, so the auditor who draws a sample and the one who scores it need not be the same person. A `correct_outcome` supplied alongside an `AGREE` verdict is accepted rather than rejected, but it is discarded — the stored `correct_outcome` is forced to null on every `AGREE`. `audited_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `counts_toward_accuracy` returns to true. On the `DISPUTED → AUDITED` re-audit the earlier `dispute_note` and `disputed_at` are retained rather than cleared, so the dispute stays readable as history beside the calibrated verdict. The deciding reviewer's `audited_count` and `agree_count` are then re-derived, both accuracy variants are recomputed, and the accuracy gate runs.

A reviewer who believes an audit verdict is wrong may dispute it, moving the sample `AUDITED → DISPUTED`; a sample in any other status yields `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_DISPUTABLE` (HTTP 409), and only the reviewer who decided the underlying case may dispute — `X-User-ID` must equal that case's `decided_by_reviewer_id`, else `ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`. The dispute stores the reviewer's `dispute_note` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — stamps `disputed_at`, and sets `counts_toward_accuracy` to false, which removes the sample from **both** the numerator and the denominator of the accuracy measurement until it is re-audited; the sample's `verdict`, `correct_outcome` and `audited_at` are all retained untouched. The reviewer's counters and both accuracy variants are re-derived without it, and the accuracy gate described below then runs on this path exactly as it does after an audit, so a dispute can itself demote the disputing reviewer. One consequence is deliberate: a reviewer already sitting in `PENDING_RETRAINING` whose recomputed score now clears the retraining floor is **not** automatically restored to `ACTIVE`; restoration only ever happens through a certification grant as described in §3.4.

Two accuracy measurements are maintained side by side over the same evidence, and both are stored on the reviewer. The audit denominator, `audited_count`, is the number of samples in status `AUDITED` with `counts_toward_accuracy` true on cases the reviewer decided, and `agree_count` is the subset of those whose `verdict` is `AGREE`. The appeal denominator, `appealed_count`, is the number of appeals in a terminal status — `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` — on cases the reviewer decided, and `upheld_appeal_count` is the subset in `UPHELD`. Cases whose `decision_source` is `AUTO` or `LEGAL` carry a null `decided_by_reviewer_id` and are attributed to nobody, so they never enter either measurement, and neither measurement is restricted to any time window. From those four counters: `accuracy_score_audit_only` is `agree_count` divided by `audited_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is null while `audited_count` is 0; `accuracy_score_blended` is the sum of `agree_count` and `upheld_appeal_count` divided by the sum of `audited_count` and `appealed_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is null while that combined denominator is 0. The two variants can diverge sharply on the same data, which is the point of keeping both: a reviewer with one agreeing audit and no terminal appeals scores `1.0000` on the audit-only variant, while the same audit plus a single `OVERTURNED` appeal scores `0.5000` on the blended variant. The audit-only variant is the figure quality assurance reports on for calibration, and the blended variant is the figure used for all workforce reporting.

After both variants are stored, the **accuracy gate** runs, and it chooses between them: the gate score is `accuracy_score_blended` whenever the reviewer has at least one terminal appeal, and `accuracy_score_audit_only` otherwise. The gate only acts once there is enough evidence — the sum of `audited_count` and `appealed_count` must be at least 2. Given that, a gate score strictly below 0.6000 transitions the reviewer `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, and a gate score at or above 0.6000 but strictly below 0.8500 transitions them `ACTIVE → PENDING_RETRAINING`. A gate score of exactly 0.8500 does not demote. The gate acts only on a reviewer who is `ACTIVE` at the moment it runs: a reviewer already sitting in `PENDING_RETRAINING`, `SUSPENDED` or `ON_WELLNESS_BREAK` is left exactly where they are however far the recomputed score falls. When the gate does demote, the status change shares the single `version` increment of the recomputation that provoked it rather than adding a second one. The two demotions differ in how they treat live work, and the difference is intentional. `PENDING_RETRAINING` keeps the reviewer's `ASSIGNED` cases assigned to them but sets `requires_recertified_review` to true on each, so the work waits for a retrained decider rather than returning to the pool; because the reviewer is no longer `ACTIVE`, any attempt by them to decide such a case fails with `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ACTIVE`. `SUSPENDED` instead returns the work: every case they hold in `ASSIGNED` transitions `ASSIGNED → PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, their `open_case_count` and `exposure_points_today` are set to 0, and each affected queue's `unassigned_case_count` rises by one per returned case — while, exactly as on the wellness path, each affected queue's `open_case_count` and each returned case's `sla_due_at` are left untouched.

Audit samples can be fetched individually, showing verdict and dispute history, and listed with narrowing by `case_id`, `auditor_reviewer_id`, `status` or `verdict`.

### 3.9. Transparency Reporting

A `TrustSafetyAdmin` opens a **TransparencyReport** over a closed reporting period bounded by `period_start` and `period_end`, both inclusive. `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, or the request is `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID` (HTTP 422), and the requested closed period may not intersect the closed period of any existing report — sharing even a single endpoint instant counts as an intersection — or the request is `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A new report opens in `DRAFT` with a null `variant`, null `computed_at` and `published_at`, every volume counter at 0, `appeal_overturn_rate` and `legal_grant_rate` at `0.0000`, and an empty `category_rate_breakdown`.

Computation moves the report `DRAFT → COMPUTED`, and it may be repeated: a `COMPUTED` report may be recomputed under the other `variant`, which overwrites every figure and switches the recorded `variant`. Only a published report is closed to computation, which is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). The caller supplies the `variant` — `PROVISIONAL` or `RESTATED` — and `computed_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

Every figure is derived from the same window: the cases whose `status` is `DECIDED` and whose `decided_at` falls inside the closed interval from `period_start` through `period_end`, with both endpoint instants included. The variant then chooses the counting base. Under `PROVISIONAL` the base is the whole window, so decisions later overturned still count. Under `RESTATED` the base is the window restricted to cases whose `is_overturned` is false. From that base, `enforcement_volume` is the number of cases in the base, and `removal_volume`, `age_gate_volume` and `warn_volume` are the counts of base cases whose `decision_outcome` is `REMOVE`, `AGE_GATE` and `WARN` respectively. `overturn_adjusted_volume` is the number of window cases whose `is_overturned` is false and is therefore identical under both variants; under `RESTATED` it necessarily equals `enforcement_volume`, and because overturned cases drop out of every volume, a `RESTATED` `enforcement_volume` is never larger than the `PROVISIONAL` figure over identical data.

Appeal figures are computed by rounding per category **before** averaging, and the order is pinned because averaging first and rounding once produces a different number. Appeals are attributed to a category through the pinned policy version of the case they contest. For each policy category with at least one counted appeal on a window case, taken in ascending order of the category's `code`, the per-category rate is the number of that category's appeals in `OVERTURNED` divided by the number of its appeals in a terminal status — `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` — rounded half up to four decimal places. `appeal_overturn_rate` is then the arithmetic mean of those already-rounded per-category rates, rounded half up to four decimal places again, or exactly `0.0000` when no category contributed a counted appeal at all. `category_rate_breakdown` lists one object per contributing category in the same ascending `code` order, each carrying `category_code`, `appealed` as that category's terminal appeal count, `overturned` as its overturned count, and `rate` as its already-rounded per-category rate. `appeal_count` is the number of appeals counted for the variant: under `RESTATED` only terminal appeals count, while under `PROVISIONAL` appeals still sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW` are additionally included in `appeal_count` — but never in any rate numerator or denominator, and never in the breakdown.

Two legal figures round out the report. `legal_request_count` is the number of window cases whose `origin` is `LEGAL_REQUEST`, and `legal_grant_rate` is the number of those whose `decision_outcome` is `REMOVE` divided by `legal_request_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, or exactly `0.0000` when the period contains no legal-origin decided case.

Publication moves the report `COMPUTED → PUBLISHED` and may be performed by a `TrustSafetyAdmin` or by `LegalCounsel`. Three conditions guard it, and the already-published test is evaluated ahead of all three, so a second publication attempt reports `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` and never `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`: the report must be `COMPUTED`, or the attempt is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED` (HTTP 409); its recorded `variant` must be `RESTATED`, because provisional figures are not publishable, or the attempt is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_RESTATED` (HTTP 422); and no appeal on a case decided inside the period may still be sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `INDEPENDENT_REVIEW`, or the attempt is `ERR_OPEN_APPEALS_IN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). On success `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock and every figure is frozen: a `PUBLISHED` report can neither be recomputed nor republished, and both attempts yield `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. Publication takes no input beyond the path identifier and reads no request body at all, so whatever accompanies the call — well-formed JSON or not — is ignored rather than rejected. A report can be fetched individually at any point to read its `variant`-dependent volumes and per-category breakdown.
