# Convenience-Store Foodservice Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Fresh food is the highest-margin and highest-risk category a convenience-store chain operates. A single site runs a made-to-order sandwich line, a beverage bar, a hot-food counter and a bakery case simultaneously, all sharing one touchscreen order funnel, one set of holding equipment and one hourly labor budget. The operational pain is not any single one of those workstreams — it is that they contradict each other. A par-baked pretzel that sat past its hold time is unsellable but still occupies a hot-case slot. A daypart menu flip retires half the case while orders are mid-production against it. A promotion that discounts sandwiches interacts with a catering service fee that must not itself be discounted, and both feed a loyalty accrual that has to be provisionally printed on a receipt before the tender has actually settled.

This document specifies the backend program that reconciles those workstreams into one consistent record. The service captures customized builds from a touchscreen, a mobile pre-order app or a catering channel; sequences the resulting work across four kitchen stations; plans and tracks par-baked production against physical holding-unit slots with hold clocks and discard logs; enforces food-safety temperature logging with a two-person corrective-action protocol; values every unit of waste on both a food-cost and a retail basis for the health-inspection binder; derives labor minutes from forecast transaction counts; and posts fuel-and-food combination promotions and loyalty points against member accounts.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **all time-dependent behaviour reads a logical clock rather than wall-clock time**, so hold expiry, pickup windows, fuel look-backs and temperature-log compliance are all reproducible. Second, **the money axis and the production axis of an order advance independently**: an order can be assembled before it is paid and paid before it is assembled, and the only coupling between the two is a small number of explicitly stated preconditions. Implementers should treat those two commitments as invariants, not as conveniences.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Devices and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the principal's role. If either header is absent or blank, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called — the authorization column of the endpoint table in §4 is the normative list — the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before any part of the request body is inspected.

The seven roles are `ChainAdmin`, `StoreManager`, `KitchenAssociate`, `ShiftLead`, `FoodSafetyLead`, `StoreTerminal` and `Customer`.

A `ChainAdmin` is a chain-office operator who owns store registration, the menu and modifier catalogue, promotion rules, customer-care point adjustments and the logical clock. A `StoreManager` is a site general manager who owns labor plans, daypart menu transitions, refunds and the operator release path. A `KitchenAssociate` works the make line and the bakery: baking, pulling and discarding production batches, recording probe readings and working kitchen tickets. A `ShiftLead` is the on-duty lead who reopens finished tickets for rework, voids and refunds orders and verifies corrective actions that do not involve a locked-out unit. A `FoodSafetyLead` is the food-safety authority — the only role permitted to clear a locked-out holding unit — and the owner of inspection readiness. A `StoreTerminal` is the in-store touchscreen and register device that captures, places, tenders, voids, releases and hands off orders. A `Customer` is a loyalty member ordering from the mobile app.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ChainAdmin`, `StoreManager`, `KitchenAssociate`, `ShiftLead`, `FoodSafetyLead` or `StoreTerminal`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator or device identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is nevertheless recorded wherever the scaffolded workflows demand an actor of record — as `logged_by_user_id` on a temperature log or a waste log, as `opened_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a corrective action, and as `started_by_user_id` and `completed_by_user_id` on a kitchen ticket — and those recorded values are compared against each other to enforce segregation of duties.

The `Customer` role is the single exception and is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `LoyaltyAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `LoyaltyAccount` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Customer` acting on an order whose `loyalty_account_id` differs from the caller's `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403). That ownership check guards seven operations: adding a line to an order, deleting a line from an order, placing an order, authorizing an order, settling an order, picking up an order, and reading a single order. An anonymous walk-in order — one created with no `loyalty_account_id` — therefore has no `Customer` owner at all and can only be driven by a `StoreTerminal`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list endpoints return their records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters, where an endpoint offers them, narrow the set before pagination is applied, so `total_count` reflects the filtered population rather than the whole table.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Daypart Authority

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. A single **logical clock** supplies the instant used by every timestamp, hold-time computation, pickup-window comparison, fuel look-back, temperature-log compliance evaluation and service-date derivation. Before any clock-setting call has been made, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ChainAdmin` advances it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response body reports the new value as `current_utc` and no domain entity is modified by the call. The clock is monotonic non-decreasing: a requested instant that is at or before the current clock value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422). Datetimes are serialized in ISO 8601 UTC form.

Dayparts are a separate matter and are frequently misread, so the rule is stated plainly here. A store's `current_daypart` is **operational state, not a derived read of the clock**. It begins at `BREAKFAST` when the store is registered and changes only through an explicit daypart-transition request issued by a `StoreManager`. Every menu-eligibility check, every promotion-eligibility check and every staffing-plan lookup compares against the store's `current_daypart`; none of them consults the clock's time-of-day directly. The clock's only role in this area is to decide whether a *requested* transition is due.

Four dayparts exist: `BREAKFAST`, `LUNCH`, `DINNER` and `OVERNIGHT`. Their windows are evaluated on the logical clock's UTC time-of-day as half-open intervals that include the start instant and exclude the end instant: `BREAKFAST` covers 04:00:00 up to but not including 11:00:00; `LUNCH` covers 11:00:00 up to but not including 16:00:00; `DINNER` covers 16:00:00 up to but not including 22:00:00; and `OVERNIGHT` covers 22:00:00 up to but not including midnight together with midnight up to but not including 04:00:00. A transition request names its destination as `to_daypart`. The request is accepted only when that destination both equals the daypart implied by the logical clock's current UTC time-of-day and differs from the store's present `current_daypart`; a request that targets the daypart the store already occupies, or that skips the successor in the cycle running `BREAKFAST` to `LUNCH` to `DINNER` to `OVERNIGHT` and back to `BREAKFAST`, is rejected with `ERR_DAYPART_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409).

Each accepted transition also increments the store's `daypart_epoch`, a monotonic counter that starts at 0 and is stamped onto every waste record booked thereafter. The epoch exists so that inspectors can tell which menu period a write-off belongs to even when several transitions occur on the same service date.

### 2.5. Money, Points and Arithmetic Precision

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer cents**. No floating-point money is ever persisted or returned; a price, a discount, a fee, a food cost and a retail value are all whole numbers of cents. Loyalty points are likewise whole integers.

Where a calculation cannot avoid a fractional intermediate value, the rounding method is **round half up on the absolute value, to whole cents or whole integers**, unless a specific rule below names a different method. Three rules deliberately name a different method and implementers must honour them exactly: loyalty point accrual **truncates toward zero** (fractional points are always dropped, never rounded up); the per-station labor terms of the staffing calculation **round up to the next whole hour** before being converted to minutes; and the promotion cap and pickup-window geometry rules use exact integer comparison with no rounding at all.

Basis points appear in several places — `discount_bp` on a promotion, `mto_share_bp` and `peak_multiplier_bp` on a staffing plan, and the internal tier multiplier of the loyalty program. A basis-point figure is always a fraction of ten thousand: 10000 basis points is exactly one times the base, 11500 basis points is one and fifteen hundredths of the base, and 800 basis points is eight hundredths of the base.

Gallon quantities are the one non-integer input the system accepts. Fuel volumes are recorded and summed at three decimal places.

### 2.6. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation. `Store`, `HoldingUnit`, `MenuItem`, `PromotionRule`, `LoyaltyAccount`, `Order`, `OrderLine`, `KitchenTicket`, `ProductionBatch`, `CorrectiveAction` and `StaffingPlan` all carry the full set.

Four record types are append-only evidence rather than mutable resources, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations. Those four are `ModifierOption`, `FuelTransaction`, `WasteLog` and `TemperatureLog`. A modifier option is a catalogue leaf that is never edited in place; a fuel transaction, a waste log row and a temperature reading are immutable facts in an audit trail.

A "mutation" for versioning purposes includes recomputation of a derived field. When an order's `subtotal_gross_cents` is recomputed because a line was added, when a store's `hot_case_slots_used` changes because a batch entered or left the case, or when a loyalty account's `points_balance` moves because points accrued or reversed, that is a mutation of the affected resource: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. A single request may therefore bump the version of several resources, and the requirements in §3 name each of them explicitly.

### 2.7. Request Validation Order

Validation is a fixed pipeline and the **first** failure wins; later checks are never reached and never reported. The order is:

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization against the endpoint's permitted roles — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request-body shape on write endpoints — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This covers a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, an unrecognized enum member, an integer outside its declared bound (a line quantity below 1, a capacity below 1, a share in basis points outside the range 0 through 10000 inclusive), a malformed ISO 8601 UTC datetime, a non-positive gallon or quantity figure, a missing additional quantity when topping off a batch that is already holding, a missing basis-point figure on a per-line-percentage promotion, and a missing flat-cents figure on a cents-off promotion.
4. Path identifier resolution, evaluated **left to right** — the store before the holding unit, the order before the line. The first identifier in the path that resolves to nothing produces its own 404: `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOYALTY_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KITCHEN_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFFING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROMOTION_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. A line identifier that exists but belongs to a different order is treated as unresolved and yields `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Foreign keys carried in the request body, resolved in the order the fields are declared in the endpoint's input list. This step applies to order creation, line creation, production-batch creation and corrective-action creation, and may yield `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODIFIER_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOYALTY_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TEMPERATURE_LOG_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Code uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409) — on store registration, menu-item creation, modifier-option creation, promotion-rule creation, loyalty enrollment and holding-unit creation.
7. Order ownership for the `Customer` role — `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`.
8. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — on corrective-action verification and kitchen-ticket reopen.
9. The locked-out escalation rule — `ERR_LOCKOUT_REQUIRES_SAFETY_LEAD` — on corrective-action verification.
10. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — on staffing-plan creation and batch discard.
11. State-transition legality on every state-transition endpoint — `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ORDER_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_REWORKABLE`, `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACTION_ALREADY_VERIFIED` or `ERR_UNIT_NOT_IMPAIRED` (all HTTP 409).
12. The locked-plan guard on staffing-plan creation — `ERR_PLAN_LOCKED`.
13. Onward, the domain rules in exactly this order — this is the tie-breaking sequence whenever a single request would fail more than one of them: `ERR_ORDER_EMPTY`, then `ERR_DAYPART_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`, then `ERR_ORDER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, then `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, then `ERR_BATCH_HOLD_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_TEMP_READING_OUT_OF_SENSOR_RANGE`, then `ERR_MODIFIER_NOT_FOR_ITEM`, then `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_IN_DAYPART`, then `ERR_ITEM_NOT_CATERING_ELIGIBLE`, then `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PAR_BAKED`, then `ERR_MODIFIER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_SLOT_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`, then `ERR_UNIT_SLOT_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_RESERVED_DISCARD_REASON`, then `ERR_DISCARD_QTY_EXCEEDS_REMAINING`, then `ERR_DAYPART_NOT_DUE`, then `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`, then `ERR_POINTS_BALANCE_NEGATIVE`, then the pickup-window family in the order `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_PREORDER_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_ELAPSED`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_FULL`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, and finally `ERR_HOT_CASE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BATCH_QTY`, then `ERR_ORDER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`.

The practical consequence engineers must internalize: a request that is both unauthorized and malformed reports the authorization failure, and a stale version on a batch discard is reported *before* the batch's own state is examined, so discarding from a sold-out batch with a stale version yields the stale-version error rather than the invalid-state error.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write endpoints participate in optimistic concurrency control, and both guard against a client writing on top of a parent resource it has not observed. No other endpoint accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere is not part of the contract.

`POST /stores/{id}/staffing-plans` requires a non-optional `expected_store_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Store`. If that value does not equal the store's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This guard exists because plan creation legitimately supersedes any existing draft or published plan for the same store, service date and daypart combination: without the check, a second sequential create carrying the version the client read before the first create would silently overwrite a plan the client never saw. The stale-version comparison makes that rejection deterministic.

`POST /production-batches/{id}/discard` requires a non-optional `expected_batch_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductionBatch`. If that value does not equal the batch's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Partial write-offs are repeatable by design — a second discard of the same quantity against a batch that still holds units is a legitimate operation that no state precondition rejects — so the stale-version comparison is the only deterministic guard against writing off units the client never saw, whether those units were moved by a top-off bake, by a reservation or by an earlier write-off.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Many writes in this system move more than one row. The requirements in §3 enumerate each cascade individually, but three structural patterns are worth stating up front so that no cascade is treated as optional.

**Order production cascades run to completion inside one request.** When an order enters the assembled state it is immediately evaluated for the ready gate, and if the gate holds it advances to ready in the same request. Advancing to ready converts par-baked reservations into sales, and that conversion can in turn exhaust a batch, driving the batch to a sold-out state, which in turn decrements the store's `hot_case_slots_used` and increments the store's `version`. A single kitchen-ticket completion can therefore mutate the ticket, the order, one or more batches and the store.

**Safety and daypart events sweep the case.** An out-of-band probe reading and an accepted daypart transition each trigger a sweep over the affected holding batches, writing off unreserved remaining units, appending waste records, accumulating the store's waste cost and freeing hot-case slots. Sweeps have deterministic ordering, stated in §3.

**Compensating pairs must not drift.** Placement reserves stock and accrues provisional points; void returns that stock and reverses those points. Settlement posts final points; refund reverses them. The reversal side of each pair is specified in §3 field by field, and two invariants below hold after every one of these operations.

### 2.10. Ledger Invariants

Two equalities are asserted after every mutation that touches them, and an implementation that lets either drift is incorrect regardless of which individual endpoint appears to behave.

For each production batch, the cumulative baked quantity always equals the sum of the units still available on the case, the units reserved to placed orders, the units converted to sales and the units written off. Separately, a batch's reserved quantity always equals the sum of the reserved quantities of the order lines pointing at that batch. These two equalities are asserted after every batch mutation: initial bake, top-off bake, pull, reservation, release of a reservation, sale conversion, operator discard and system sweep. The placement-and-void pair is the compensating pair most likely to break them.

For each loyalty account, the spendable balance always equals the lifetime points earned minus the lifetime points redeemed minus the total points reversed. This equality is asserted after every loyalty mutation: placement accrual, settlement delta, refund reversal, void reversal and customer-care adjustment. The settle-and-refund pair and the place-and-void pair are the compensating pairs most likely to break it.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two workflows in this system require a second pair of hands, and both compare the acting `X-User-ID` against an actor recorded on the prior step rather than against a role.

Verifying a corrective action requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `logged_by_user_id` recorded on the temperature reading being remediated: the associate who took a failing probe reading may not sign off on its own remediation. Reopening a finished kitchen ticket requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `completed_by_user_id` recorded on that ticket: the associate who declared a build finished may not be the actor who sends it back for rework. Both violations report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chain Configuration: Sites, Holding Equipment and the Menu Catalogue

Nothing can be ordered, baked or measured until the chain office has described the physical world. A `ChainAdmin` registers a store site by supplying a `store_code`, a display `name`, a `hot_case_slot_capacity` and a `pickup_slot_capacity`, and optionally an `is_fuel_site` flag that defaults to true and determines whether fuel-and-food combination promotions can fire at that site. The `store_code` must be unique across the chain and between 1 and 16 characters long; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Both capacity figures must be at least 1. A freshly registered store opens in the `BREAKFAST` daypart with a `daypart_epoch` of 0, a `hot_case_slots_used` count of 0, a `waste_cost_cents_mtd` of 0 and a `rework_waste_cents_mtd` of 0. The `hot_case_slot_capacity` is the ceiling on how many production batches the site may simultaneously hold in either the baking or the holding phase, and `hot_case_slots_used` is the running count of exactly those batches. The `pickup_slot_capacity` is the ceiling on how many *other* in-flight pre-order or catering orders may have a pickup window overlapping a new one.

Holding equipment is added beneath a store. Each **holding unit** is described by a `unit_code` that must be unique within its store — a collision again reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — a `unit_type` of `HOT_CASE`, `COLD_CASE` or `BAKERY_CASE`, a safe holding band expressed as `min_temp_f` and `max_temp_f` in degrees Fahrenheit, and a `slot_count` of at least 1 giving the number of independently schedulable pan slots in the cabinet. The `max_temp_f` must be strictly greater than the `min_temp_f`. The unit's own `store_id` records the site it lives in. A new unit opens in the `OPERATIONAL` posture with a `consecutive_out_of_range_count` of 0 and a `last_temp_log_at` of null, since no probe reading has yet been taken against it. Both a `ChainAdmin` and a `StoreManager` may add units; the list of a store's units may be filtered by `status`.

The menu catalogue is chain-wide rather than store-scoped. A menu item is created with a chain-unique `sku` (a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), a display `name`, a `station` of `SANDWICH`, `BEVERAGE`, `HOT_FOOD` or `BAKERY` naming the kitchen station that produces it, and a `fulfillment_mode` of either `MADE_TO_ORDER` or `PAR_BAKED_CASE`. That mode is the most consequential field in the catalogue: a `MADE_TO_ORDER` item generates kitchen work when its order is committed, whereas a `PAR_BAKED_CASE` item is instead reserved out of stock already sitting in a holding unit. Pricing is given by `base_price_cents`, the retail price of one unit before any modifiers, which must be zero or greater, and `unit_food_cost_cents`, the ingredient cost of one unit, which must be zero or greater and must not exceed the `base_price_cents`. The item also declares `valid_dayparts`, a non-empty subset of the four daypart names, restricting when it may be ordered.

Four further menu-item fields carry defaults and are frequently left out of the creation payload. The `prep_seconds` field is the hands-on make time for one unit at its station and defaults to 0; it is 0 for par-baked items, which are never made to order. The `bake_minutes` field is the oven time for one production batch of the item and defaults to 0; it is 0 for made-to-order items, which are never batched. The `hold_minutes` field is the maximum safe hot-case hold duration once a batch of the item has been pulled into the case and defaults to 0. The `included_premium_count` field is the number of premium modifiers included with each unit before a surcharge applies and defaults to 0. Finally, `is_catering_eligible` defaults to false and gates whether the item may appear on a catering-channel order at all.

Build customization hangs off a menu item as a set of **modifier options**. Each option belongs to its parent through `menu_item_id`, carries a `code` that must be unique within that menu item — collisions report `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — and a `modifier_class` naming the build slot it occupies, one of `BREAD`, `PROTEIN`, `CHEESE`, `TOPPING`, `PREP` or `SIZE`. Its `price_delta_cents`, which must be zero or greater, is the per-unit amount added to the line's unit price for each unit of the option taken. The `is_premium` flag defaults to false and marks the option as one that counts against the parent item's included premium allowance. The `max_qty` field defaults to 1, must be at least 1, and caps how many units of that option a single order line may take. A menu item's option list may be filtered by `modifier_class`. Modifier options are catalogue leaves: they are created and read but never edited, which is why they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

### 3.2. Loyalty Membership, Fuel Capture and Promotion Rules

A loyalty member is enrolled by a `ChainAdmin` or by a `StoreTerminal` at the register, with a chain-unique `member_code` — a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — and an `email` for contact. Enrollment sets all four point counters to 0 and the `tier` to `BASIC`. The four counters are deliberately separate: `points_balance` is what the member can spend, `lifetime_points_earned` is the cumulative total ever accrued and never decreases, `lifetime_points_redeemed` accumulates points removed by negative customer-care adjustments, and `points_reversed_total` accumulates points clawed back when an order is voided or refunded.

Tier is derived, not set. It is recomputed from `lifetime_points_earned` alone after every mutation of that counter: the tier is `ELITE` when lifetime earnings are at least 2000 points, `PLUS` when they are at least 500 but below 2000, and `BASIC` below 500. Both boundaries are inclusive on the lower side — exactly 500 lifetime points is `PLUS` and exactly 2000 is `ELITE`. Because only `lifetime_points_earned` feeds the derivation, reversals and redemptions never demote a member.

Customer-care adjustments are a `ChainAdmin` capability. The payload carries a signed `delta_points` figure and a free-text `reason`. A positive delta is treated as an earning event: it is added to `points_balance` and added to `lifetime_points_earned`. A negative delta is treated as a redemption: it is added to `points_balance` (thereby reducing it) and its magnitude is added to `lifetime_points_redeemed`. Either way the tier is re-derived afterwards and the account's `version` increments by one. A negative adjustment that would drive `points_balance` below zero is rejected with `ERR_POINTS_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 422); the same floor applies to a refund reversal that would take the balance negative.

Fuel is captured against a member so that combination promotions have something to look at. A fuel sale records `gallons` — a positive figure carried at three decimal places — a positive `price_per_gallon_cents`, and the `occurred_at` instant at which the sale happened, which is what the look-back window reads. The system computes `fuel_spend_cents` as the gallons multiplied by the posted price per gallon, rounded half up to whole cents. A member's fuel sales are listable. Fuel transactions, like modifier options, are immutable evidence and carry no `updated_at` and no `version`; each one references its owner through `loyalty_account_id`.

The look-back that promotion eligibility consults sums a member's gallons over a **closed thirty-day window** ending at the logical clock. The window opens thirty days before the current logical-clock instant, and a fuel sale participates when its `occurred_at` is at or after that opening instant and at or before the clock itself — both bounds inclusive. The resulting gallon total is summed at three decimal places and compared against a promotion's requirement with an at-least comparison, so a member with exactly the required gallonage qualifies.

Promotion rules are chain-level configuration owned by a `ChainAdmin`. A rule carries a chain-unique `code` (collisions report `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), a `kind` of either `FUEL_FOOD_COMBO` or `DAYPART_BUNDLE`, and a `discount_kind` of either `PCT_PER_LINE` or `CENTS_OFF`. A `PCT_PER_LINE` rule discounts each eligible line individually and sums the results; a `CENTS_OFF` rule discounts the order once as a flat amount. Accordingly, `discount_bp` — a basis-point figure in the inclusive range 0 through 10000, defaulting to 0 — is required on a per-line-percentage rule, and `discount_cents` — zero or greater, defaulting to 0 — is required on a cents-off rule; a rule that omits the figure its discount kind needs is rejected as a malformed request. Every rule carries a mandatory `max_discount_cents` of zero or greater, which is a hard cap applied to whatever the rule computes.

Eligibility for a rule is narrowed by four more fields. The `min_food_subtotal_cents` figure, defaulting to 0, is the minimum gross food subtotal an order must reach, compared with an at-least comparison. The `requires_fuel_gallons` figure, a decimal defaulting to 0, is the minimum thirty-day gallon total on the order's loyalty account, again compared with an at-least comparison, and is only meaningful for a `FUEL_FOOD_COMBO` rule. The `target_station` field, defaulting to `ALL`, restricts which lines a per-line-percentage discount touches and takes one of `SANDWICH`, `BEVERAGE`, `HOT_FOOD`, `BAKERY` or `ALL`, where `ALL` means every line. The `valid_dayparts` array is a non-empty subset of the four daypart names and is compared against the store's `current_daypart`, never against the clock. Finally `stacking_rank`, an integer of at least 1, is the first tie-break when two rules produce the same discount, and a lower rank wins.

A rule opens in the `ACTIVE` status and only active rules ever enter the candidate pool. A `ChainAdmin` may suspend a rule, moving its `status` to `SUSPENDED`; from that moment the selector stops considering it, but orders already placed keep the `applied_promotion_id` and the discount they were given. The rule list may be filtered by `status` and by `kind`.

### 3.3. Forecast-Driven Labor Planning by Daypart

Order placement at a site is gated on labor having been planned for the daypart in question, so a `StoreManager` builds one plan per store, service date and daypart. The plan payload names the `service_date` as a calendar date in year-month-day form interpreted in UTC, the `daypart` the plan covers, a `forecast_txn_count` of zero or greater, an `mto_share_bp` figure in the inclusive range 0 through 10000 giving the basis points of forecast transactions expected to be made-to-order foodservice rather than grab-and-go, and the `expected_store_version` described in §2.8. The plan's own `store_id` records the site it staffs, and a new plan opens in `DRAFT`.

Two figures are computed at creation. The peak indicator `peak_multiplier_bp` is 11500 basis points when the `forecast_txn_count` is at least 400 transactions, and 10000 basis points otherwise — the threshold is inclusive, so a forecast of exactly 400 is a peak daypart. The `planned_labor_minutes` figure is then derived in a strictly ordered pipeline. First, the made-to-order transaction count is the forecast count multiplied by the made-to-order share in basis points, divided by ten thousand, and **rounded up to the next whole transaction**. Second, the grab-and-go transaction count is the forecast count minus that made-to-order count. Third, a raw minute figure is assembled from three independent terms: the made-to-order count divided by a throughput of 18 transactions per labor hour and rounded up to a whole hour, then converted to minutes by multiplying by 60; plus the grab-and-go count divided by a throughput of 45 transactions per labor hour and likewise rounded up to a whole hour and converted to minutes; plus a flat two hours of bakery preparation, that is 120 minutes, when and only when the plan's daypart is `BREAKFAST`. Fourth, the raw figure is multiplied by `peak_multiplier_bp` and divided by ten thousand, with the single result rounded half up to whole minutes. Fifth and last, a floor of three hours — 180 minutes — is applied to that result, so the plan's `planned_labor_minutes` is whichever is larger. The rounding-up of each station term happens *before* the two terms are summed, the half-up rounding happens once and only after the peak multiplier has been applied, and the three-hour floor is applied after the multiplier rather than before it. Only the plan's own inputs participate; no other plan contributes.

Creating a plan for a store, service date and daypart combination that already has one **supersedes** the incumbent. Any existing plan for that same combination whose status is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `version` incremented; the store's `version` is also incremented by the create. If the incumbent has already been locked, however, the new plan is refused outright with `ERR_PLAN_LOCKED` (HTTP 409) and nothing is superseded.

Publishing is what authorizes trade. A `StoreManager` publishes a plan, moving it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, after which orders may be placed at that store in that daypart on that service date. Publishing a plan that is not in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Locking moves a plan `PUBLISHED → LOCKED` so that it can no longer be superseded by a later plan for the same combination; locking a plan that is not in `PUBLISHED` reports the same `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Both transitions increment the plan's `version` by one. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. A store's plans may be listed and filtered by `daypart`, by `service_date` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Par-Baked Production: Slot Planning, Baking, Holding and Top-Off

A **production batch** is one pan of a par-baked item scheduled into one physical slot of one holding unit. Either a `StoreManager` or a `KitchenAssociate` plans a batch by naming the `store_id` producing it, the `menu_item_id` being produced, the `holding_unit_id` whose slot it will occupy, the `slot_index` within that unit, a `planned_qty` of at least 1 for the first bake, and the planned oven-in instant as `bake_start_at`. The referenced menu item must have a `fulfillment_mode` of `PAR_BAKED_CASE`; a made-to-order item cannot be batched, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PAR_BAKED` (HTTP 422). The `slot_index` must be at least 1 and at most the unit's `slot_count`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) is returned. The target unit's posture must be `OPERATIONAL`; planning into a unit that is `IMPAIRED` or `LOCKED_OUT` is rejected with `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (HTTP 409).

Slot scheduling is an interval problem and its geometry is pinned. Each batch occupies its slot over a half-open interval that begins at `bake_start_at` and ends at the computed `occupancy_end_at`, which is the `bake_start_at` advanced by the item's `bake_minutes`, then by the item's `hold_minutes`, and then by a mandatory sanitation window of 15 minutes. The buffer is folded into the end bound rather than checked separately. A new batch is refused with `ERR_UNIT_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when some other batch on the *same* holding unit and the *same* slot index, whose status is `PLANNED`, `BAKING` or `HOLDING`, satisfies both of these: the new batch's `bake_start_at` falls strictly before that batch's `occupancy_end_at`, and that batch's `bake_start_at` falls strictly before the new batch's `occupancy_end_at`. Because both comparisons are strict, intervals that merely touch are legal: a new batch whose `bake_start_at` equals an existing batch's `occupancy_end_at` is accepted, while a start one minute earlier is refused. Batches that have reached `SOLD_OUT` or `DISCARDED` free their slot immediately and are never conflict candidates.

A newly planned batch opens in `PLANNED` with every quantity counter at 0: `baked_qty`, `remaining_qty`, `reserved_qty`, `sold_qty` and `discarded_qty` are all zero, and `hold_started_at` and `hold_expires_at` are both null. The five counters mean, respectively, the cumulative units ever baked into the batch, the units available on the case right now, the units held for placed orders that have not yet gone ready, the units converted to sales when their order reached ready, and the units written off.

A `KitchenAssociate` ovens a batch in, driving it `PLANNED → BAKING`. Three preconditions apply: the batch must be in `PLANNED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); the holding unit must be `OPERATIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_OPERATIONAL`; and the store's `hot_case_slots_used` must be strictly below its `hot_case_slot_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_HOT_CASE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). On success the batch's `baked_qty` and `remaining_qty` are both set to the `planned_qty`, the store's `hot_case_slots_used` increases by one and the store's `version` increments by one, and the batch's own `version` increments by one.

Pulling the pan into the case drives the batch `BAKING → HOLDING` and starts the hold clock. The batch must be in `BAKING`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID`, and the unit must be `OPERATIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. The pull sets `hold_started_at` to the current logical-clock instant and `hold_expires_at` to that instant advanced by the item's `hold_minutes`; a batch at or past its `hold_expires_at` may no longer fulfil order lines. Only from this point is the batch visible to the stock selector described in §3.8. The batch's `version` increments by one.

The **top-off bake** is the cycle edge that makes this machine non-linear. An associate may add units to a case that is already holding, driving the batch `HOLDING → BAKING` a second time. The batch must be in `HOLDING`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID`; an `additional_qty` of at least 1 must be supplied, otherwise the request is malformed; and the unit must be `OPERATIONAL`. The top-off adds the `additional_qty` to both `baked_qty` and `remaining_qty`, and it clears the hold clock by setting `hold_started_at` and `hold_expires_at` back to null — the case clock restarts only on the next pull, not at the moment of the top-off. Critically, the store's `hot_case_slots_used` is *unchanged* by a top-off, because the batch already occupies its slot and never gave it up. The batch's `version` increments by one.

Batches may be read individually with their full quantity ledger, hold clock and `version`, and listed with filters on `store_id`, `menu_item_id`, `holding_unit_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Fresh-Food Waste: Operator Write-Offs and Daypart Menu Transitions

Every unit of food that leaves the case without being sold has to be valued twice, because operations and health inspectors ask different questions of the same event. The **food-cost valuation** of a write-off is the quantity multiplied by the item's `unit_food_cost_cents`; the **retail valuation** of the same write-off is the quantity multiplied by the item's `base_price_cents`. Since a menu item's ingredient cost never exceeds its retail price, the retail figure is strictly larger whenever the two differ. Each waste record carries both: `food_cost_cents` on the food-cost basis and `retail_value_cents` on the retail basis. Only the food-cost basis accumulates onto the store's `waste_cost_cents_mtd`; the retail basis is aggregated on demand for the inspection summary. For the purposes of this system the entire run is treated as a single month-to-date period, so no date filter is applied when accumulating.

An operator write-off is performed by a `KitchenAssociate` or a `ShiftLead` against a batch that is currently holding stock. The payload carries the `qty` to write off, a `reason`, and the `expected_batch_version` guard from §2.8. Four preconditions are evaluated: the batch must be in `HOLDING`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID` — this covers a batch that is still planned or baking as well as one already `DISCARDED` or `SOLD_OUT`; the supplied `expected_batch_version` must match the batch's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION`; the declared `reason` must be either `HOLD_EXPIRED` or `QUALITY_REJECT`, otherwise `ERR_RESERVED_DISCARD_REASON` (HTTP 422); and the `qty` must not exceed the batch's `remaining_qty`, otherwise `ERR_DISCARD_QTY_EXCEEDS_REMAINING` (HTTP 422). The reason vocabulary is deliberately partitioned by provenance: `HOLD_EXPIRED` and `QUALITY_REJECT` are operator-declared, while `UNIT_IMPAIRED` and `DAYPART_TRANSITION` are reserved for system cascades and may never be supplied by a caller. A write-off that references a batch whose `hold_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock with a reason other than `HOLD_EXPIRED` is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_HOLD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).

A successful write-off subtracts the `qty` from `remaining_qty` and adds it to `discarded_qty`, then appends a waste record carrying the `production_batch_id` of the batch, the `qty`, the `reason`, both valuations, a `logged_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and a `daypart_epoch` copied from the store's `daypart_epoch` at the moment the write-off was booked. The store's `waste_cost_cents_mtd` increases by the record's `food_cost_cents` and the store's `version` increments by one; the batch's `version` increments by one. Whether the batch survives depends on what is left: if after the write-off both `remaining_qty` and `reserved_qty` are zero, the batch goes `HOLDING → DISCARDED` and the store's `hot_case_slots_used` decreases by one; otherwise — including the case where units are still reserved to placed orders — the batch stays in `HOLDING` and the slot count is untouched. A batch's write-off history is listable as inspection-binder evidence and may be filtered by `reason`.

Note the asymmetry that gives a batch its two exhaustion outcomes. A batch driven to zero by write-offs ends as `DISCARDED`; a batch driven to zero because its last reserved units converted to sales ends as `SOLD_OUT`. Both are terminal, both free the slot, and the distinction is decided by whichever action removed the final unit.

A **daypart menu transition** is the second source of waste, and it is a sweep rather than a single write-off. When a `StoreManager` flips the store to its next daypart, the store's `current_daypart` becomes the requested `to_daypart`, the `daypart_epoch` increases by one and the store's `version` increments. The sweep then walks the store's batches in a deterministic order — ascending `bake_start_at`, and where those tie, ascending `created_at` — and for every batch of that store which is currently in `HOLDING` and has a `reserved_qty` of zero, writes it off entirely when either of two conditions holds: its `hold_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, or the menu item's `valid_dayparts` does not include the newly current daypart. Each swept batch has its `remaining_qty` added to its `discarded_qty` and then set to zero, goes `HOLDING → DISCARDED`, and appends a waste record whose `reason` is `DAYPART_TRANSITION`, whose `logged_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`, and whose `daypart_epoch` is the **new** epoch value rather than the one in effect before the flip. Each swept batch adds its food-cost valuation to the store's `waste_cost_cents_mtd`, decrements the store's `hot_case_slots_used` by one, increments the store's `version`, and increments its own `version` by one. Batches carrying reservations are left in `HOLDING` and are not swept.

### 3.6. Food Safety: Probe Readings, Impairment Sweeps, Corrective Actions and Inspection Readiness

Temperature discipline is the backbone of health-inspection readiness, and it is enforced by making a failing reading immediately expensive. A `KitchenAssociate`, `ShiftLead` or `FoodSafetyLead` records a probe reading against a holding unit by supplying a single `reading_f` figure. The reading must lie within the physical capability of the probe, that is between minus 40 and 250 degrees Fahrenheit inclusive; anything outside that span is rejected with `ERR_TEMP_READING_OUT_OF_SENSOR_RANGE` (HTTP 422) as a sensor fault rather than a food-safety event. An accepted reading is stored with its own `holding_unit_id`, a computed `in_range` flag, a `logged_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` and a `recorded_at` set to the current logical-clock instant. The `in_range` flag is true exactly when the `reading_f` is greater than or equal to the unit's `min_temp_f` and less than or equal to its `max_temp_f` — **both** band bounds are inclusive, so a reading exactly on either edge is compliant. The unit's `last_temp_log_at` is updated to the reading's `recorded_at` regardless of outcome. Readings are immutable evidence: they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, and a unit's readings are listed newest-first with an optional filter on `in_range`.

An out-of-band reading escalates the unit's posture. A unit in `OPERATIONAL` goes `OPERATIONAL → IMPAIRED`, and its `consecutive_out_of_range_count` increases by one, reaching 1. A unit already in `IMPAIRED` goes `IMPAIRED → LOCKED_OUT` when a further out-of-band reading arrives, with the counter increasing again to reach 2. `LOCKED_OUT` is the terminal posture of the escalation path; there is no further escalation above it. The counter is described as the number of consecutive failing readings since the last verified corrective action, and it returns to zero only when such a verification occurs — not merely because a subsequent reading happens to be in range. Every posture change increments the unit's `version` by one.

Both escalations run the same **impairment sweep** over the unit's stock. Every production batch in that unit whose status is `HOLDING` and whose `reserved_qty` is zero goes `HOLDING → DISCARDED`: its `remaining_qty` is added to its `discarded_qty` and then set to zero, and a waste record is appended with a `reason` of `UNIT_IMPAIRED`, a `qty` equal to the units written off, and a `logged_by_user_id` of the literal string `system`. Each swept batch adds its food-cost valuation to the store's `waste_cost_cents_mtd`, decrements the store's `hot_case_slots_used` by one, increments the store's `version`, and increments its own `version` by one. Batches that already have units reserved to placed orders are treated differently and the difference matters: those batches keep their `HOLDING` status and their slot, and only their unreserved `remaining_qty` is written off. While a unit sits in `IMPAIRED` or `LOCKED_OUT`, planning, baking and pulling batches in it are all blocked with `ERR_HOLDING_UNIT_NOT_OPERATIONAL` until a corrective action has been verified.

Remediation is a two-person protocol. A `KitchenAssociate`, `ShiftLead` or `FoodSafetyLead` opens a **corrective action** against the failing reading by supplying the `temperature_log_id` of that reading and an `action_taken` narrative for the inspection binder. The action is created in `OPEN` with `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and with `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at` both null. Actions may be read individually and listed with filters on `temperature_log_id` and `status`.

Verification is where the segregation of duties bites, and its four checks are evaluated in this order. First, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `logged_by_user_id` recorded on the underlying reading, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, if the unit is currently `LOCKED_OUT`, the acting `X-User-Role` must be `FoodSafetyLead`; a `ShiftLead` attempting to clear a locked-out unit is rejected with `ERR_LOCKOUT_REQUIRES_SAFETY_LEAD` (HTTP 403). Third, the action's own `status` must still be `OPEN`; a second verification of an already-verified action is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Fourth, the unit reached through the reading must currently be in `IMPAIRED` or `LOCKED_OUT`; verifying an action whose unit is already `OPERATIONAL` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_IMPAIRED` (HTTP 409). A `ShiftLead` may verify an action for an impaired unit; only a `FoodSafetyLead` may verify one for a locked-out unit.

A successful verification moves the action `OPEN → VERIFIED`, records `verified_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `verified_at` as the current logical-clock instant, returns the unit to service by driving it `IMPAIRED → OPERATIONAL` or `LOCKED_OUT → OPERATIONAL`, resets the unit's `consecutive_out_of_range_count` to zero, and increments both the action's `version` and the unit's `version` by one. `VERIFIED` is terminal for the action.

Inspection readiness is a pure computed read available to a `StoreManager`, a `FoodSafetyLead` or a `ChainAdmin`; nothing is persisted by requesting it. Seven figures are returned. The `temp_log_compliance_pct` figure is the share of the store's holding units that have at least one probe reading whose `recorded_at` falls at or after the instant four hours — 240 minutes — before the current logical clock; it is expressed as an integer percentage of one hundred times the compliant unit count divided by the total unit count, rounded half up to zero decimal places, and is defined to be 100 when the store has no holding units at all. The `units_impaired_count` figure counts the store's units currently in `IMPAIRED`, and `units_locked_out_count` counts those currently in `LOCKED_OUT`. The `open_corrective_actions_count` figure counts corrective actions still in `OPEN` whose underlying reading belongs to one of the store's units. The `food_cost_waste_cents_mtd` figure is the store's `waste_cost_cents_mtd` plus its `rework_waste_cents_mtd`, so that made-to-order rework appears alongside case waste in one operational number. The `retail_waste_exposure_cents` figure sums the `retail_value_cents` of every waste record belonging to the store's batches. Finally `readiness_status` is a three-valued verdict: it is `NOT_READY` when at least one unit is locked out or the compliance percentage is below 75; otherwise it is `AT_RISK` when at least one corrective action is open, or at least one unit is impaired, or the compliance percentage is below 90; otherwise it is `READY`.

### 3.7. Touchscreen Order Capture and Build Pricing

An order begins life as an empty draft opened either by a `StoreTerminal` at the register or touchscreen, or by a `Customer` in the mobile app. The creation payload names the `store_id` fulfilling the order, a `channel` of `TOUCHSCREEN`, `MOBILE_PREORDER` or `CATERING`, and optionally a `loyalty_account_id`. That last field is null for an anonymous walk-in touchscreen order, and an anonymous order accrues no points at any stage. Where the channel promises a collection time, the payload also carries `pickup_window_start`, the inclusive start of the promised window, and `pickup_window_end`, its exclusive end; all window arithmetic in this system treats a window as half-open, including its start instant and excluding its end instant.

Channel and window must agree at creation time. A `TOUCHSCREEN` order must carry neither bound; supplying either one is rejected with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). A `MOBILE_PREORDER` or `CATERING` order must carry both; omitting either is rejected with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Where both are present, the `pickup_window_end` must be strictly after the `pickup_window_start`, and a window that is not strictly ordered is rejected with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A new order opens with a `production_status` of `DRAFT` on the kitchen axis and a `settlement_status` of `UNPAID` on the money axis. Its money counters `subtotal_gross_cents`, `promo_discount_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `total_cents` all start at 0, as do `points_provisional` and `rework_count`. The `points_final` field is null until settlement computes it, and `daypart_at_placement`, `applied_promotion_id`, `expected_ready_at`, `placed_at`, `ready_at` and `tender_type` are all null until the workflow stages that populate them. Orders may be read individually — the representation exposes both status axes, the money ledger and the promised ready time — and listed with filters on `store_id`, `loyalty_account_id`, `applied_promotion_id`, `production_status`, `settlement_status` and `channel`.

Builds are added to a draft as **order lines**. Each line names the `menu_item_id` being built, a `qty` of at least 1, and an optional `modifiers` array whose entries are objects pairing a `modifier_option_id` with a `qty`; when the array is omitted it defaults to empty and the build is the plain item. The owning `order_id` is taken from the request path rather than the body. Lines may only be added to, or removed from, an order whose `production_status` is still `DRAFT`; attempting either on a committed order is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). Removing a line addresses it by its `line_id` in the path and returns no body; a `line_id` that resolves to nothing, or that resolves to a line belonging to a different order, reports `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.

Four line-level rules guard the build. Every referenced modifier option must belong to the line's own `menu_item_id`; borrowing an option from a different item is rejected with `ERR_MODIFIER_NOT_FOR_ITEM` (HTTP 422). The menu item's `valid_dayparts` must include the store's `current_daypart`, checked when the line is added and re-checked when the order is committed; a violation reports `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_IN_DAYPART` (HTTP 422). On a `CATERING` order every line's menu item must be flagged `is_catering_eligible`, checked both at line-add time and again at commit time, with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_CATERING_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422) on failure. And each modifier entry's `qty` must be at least 1 and must not exceed that option's `max_qty`, otherwise `ERR_MODIFIER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Line pricing is computed at line creation and re-verified at commit, and its per-unit-versus-per-line reading is pinned. First, count the **premium modifier units** on the line: the sum of the `qty` figures of those modifier entries whose option carries `is_premium` — this counts modifier *units*, not distinct options, so taking two units of one premium option contributes two. Second, the line's `unit_price_cents` is the menu item's `base_price_cents` plus, for every modifier entry on the line, that option's `price_delta_cents` multiplied by the entry's `qty`. Third, the line's `premium_surcharge_cents` is 75 cents multiplied by the amount, never less than zero, by which the premium modifier unit count exceeds the menu item's `included_premium_count`. Fourth, the line's `line_gross_cents` is the sum of `unit_price_cents` and `premium_surcharge_cents`, that whole sum then multiplied by the line's `qty`. The surcharge is therefore a **per-unit** amount multiplied by quantity alongside the unit price, not a flat per-line amount added once; for any quantity above one the two readings give different totals and the per-unit reading is the correct one. Every term is an integer number of cents, so no rounding arises. Only this line's own modifier array participates.

At line creation the line's `station` is copied from the menu item's `station`, its `line_discount_cents` and `line_net_cents` remain 0 until the order is committed, its `reserved_qty` is 0 and its `fulfillment_batch_id` is null. Adding a line, and equally removing one, recomputes the order's `subtotal_gross_cents` as the sum of the `line_gross_cents` of all its remaining lines and increments the order's `version` by one. Individual lines may be read with their full modifier pricing breakdown, and lines may be listed with filters on `order_id`, `menu_item_id` and `fulfillment_batch_id`.

### 3.8. Committing an Order: Eligibility Gates, Promotion Selection, Totals, Stock Reservation and Ticket Sequencing

Committing a draft is the single heaviest operation in the system: it drives the order `DRAFT → PLACED` and, in one request, prices the order, chooses its promotion, reserves par-baked stock and cuts kitchen tickets. It is available to a `StoreTerminal` and to a `Customer` acting on their own order.

**The eligibility gates.** The gates are presented below in the order that reads most naturally as a business narrative; where a single commit would fail more than one of them, the reported failure is decided by the precedence sequence in §2.7, not by the order of presentation here. The order's `production_status` must still be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`. The order must have at least one line, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_EMPTY` (HTTP 422). Exactly one staffing plan must exist for the order's store whose `daypart` equals the store's `current_daypart`, whose `service_date` equals the calendar date of the current logical clock in UTC, and whose `status` is either `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED`; absent such a plan the commit is refused with `ERR_DAYPART_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). Every line's menu item must still list the store's `current_daypart` in its `valid_dayparts`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_IN_DAYPART`. The channel-and-window agreement described in §3.7 is re-checked: a `TOUCHSCREEN` order must still carry no window bounds (`ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_ALLOWED`), and a `MOBILE_PREORDER` or `CATERING` order must still carry both (`ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_REQUIRED`), with the end strictly after the start (`ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_INVALID`) and with the `pickup_window_end` strictly after the current logical clock, since a window that ends exactly now has already elapsed — that last failure reports `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_ELAPSED` (HTTP 409).

Lead time is then checked against the clock at commit. A `MOBILE_PREORDER` order requires that its `pickup_window_start` be at least 15 minutes after the current logical clock; a `CATERING` order requires at least twelve hours — 720 minutes — of lead. Either shortfall reports `ERR_PREORDER_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Next, window capacity: the system counts the *other* orders of the same store, excluding this one, whose `channel` is `MOBILE_PREORDER` or `CATERING` and whose `production_status` is one of `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `ASSEMBLED` or `READY`, and whose window overlaps this one in the sense that this order's `pickup_window_start` falls strictly before that order's `pickup_window_end` and that order's `pickup_window_start` falls strictly before this order's `pickup_window_end`. Draft, picked-up and voided orders are excluded, and touchscreen orders never occupy a window at all. If that count is already at or above the store's `pickup_slot_capacity`, the commit is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_FULL` (HTTP 409); the commit proceeds only when the count is strictly below the capacity. Note that this geometry is a **half-open aggregate count of concurrent windows**, deliberately unlike the exclusive single-occupant slot geometry of §3.4: windows that merely touch, where one ends exactly as the other starts, do not overlap.

Catering orders face two further gates: every line's menu item must be `is_catering_eligible` (`ERR_ITEM_NOT_CATERING_ELIGIBLE`), and the order's `subtotal_gross_cents` must reach at least 5000 cents, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). Last of the gates, every `PAR_BAKED_CASE` line must be coverable by an available batch; if the stock selector finds none, the commit is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BATCH_QTY` (HTTP 422), and a line pointed at a batch whose hold has already expired reports `ERR_BATCH_HOLD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).

**Stock selection is first-expiring-first-out.** For each par-baked line, taken in ascending line `created_at` order so that two lines competing for the same scarce batch are served oldest-line-first, the candidate batches are those whose `store_id` matches the order's store, whose `menu_item_id` matches the line's item, whose status is `HOLDING`, whose `hold_expires_at` is **strictly** after the current logical clock — a batch whose hold expires exactly now is expired and excluded — and whose `remaining_qty` is at least the line's `qty`, since a line is never split across two batches. Among the candidates the batch with the earliest `hold_expires_at` wins, and where two share that instant the one with the earlier `created_at` wins. Datetime comparison is at millisecond resolution.

**Pricing pipeline.** Once the gates pass, `placed_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant and `daypart_at_placement` snapshots the store's `current_daypart`. The order's `subtotal_gross_cents` is recomputed as the sum of the `line_gross_cents` of all its lines. The evaluation order that follows is pinned, because the catering fee depends on the discounted subtotal while the discount depends on the *undiscounted* subtotal: step one is the gross subtotal just computed; step two runs promotion eligibility against that gross subtotal; step three computes the discount; step four derives the discounted food figure as the gross subtotal minus the discount; step five computes the service fee from that discounted food figure; and step six computes `total_cents` as the gross subtotal minus the discount plus the service fee. The service fee is never part of promotion eligibility, and the fee never charges itself.

**Promotion selection picks exactly one rule; promotions never stack.** The candidate pool is every promotion rule whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — suspended rules are excluded outright — whose `valid_dayparts` contains the store's `current_daypart`, and whose `min_food_subtotal_cents` is at most the order's gross `subtotal_gross_cents`. A rule whose `kind` is `FUEL_FOOD_COMBO` carries three extra requirements: the store must be flagged `is_fuel_site`, the order must have a non-null `loyalty_account_id`, and that member's thirty-day gallon total from §3.2 must be at least the rule's `requires_fuel_gallons`. A `DAYPART_BUNDLE` rule has no fuel requirement. Candidates are then ranked by the discount each would actually produce, cap included: the rule producing the **largest** discount wins; where two produce the same discount the one with the **lower** `stacking_rank` wins; and where those also tie the one with the earlier `created_at` wins. Candidate discounts are evaluated in whole cents before ranking. If the candidate pool is empty, the order's `applied_promotion_id` stays null and its `promo_discount_cents` stays 0 — this is not an error and the commit proceeds normally.

**Discount computation and the cap.** Where the winning rule's `discount_kind` is `PCT_PER_LINE`, the eligible lines are those whose `station` matches the rule's `target_station`, or all lines when the `target_station` is `ALL`. Walking those eligible lines in ascending `created_at` order, each line's `line_discount_cents` is that line's `line_gross_cents` multiplied by the rule's `discount_bp` and divided by ten thousand, **rounded half up per line**, and the raw discount is the sum of those already-rounded per-line figures. The two-step order is pinned: round each line first, then sum. Rounding once over the summed un-rounded amounts produces a different total — two eligible lines of 1010 cents each at 1500 basis points give 152 plus 152, that is 304 cents, under the pinned order, whereas rounding the combined 2020 cents once gives 303; 304 is correct. Where the winning rule's `discount_kind` is `CENTS_OFF`, the raw discount is the smaller of the rule's `discount_cents` and the order's `subtotal_gross_cents`. In both cases the order's `promo_discount_cents` is the smaller of the raw discount and the rule's `max_discount_cents`. When the cap bites under a per-line-percentage rule, the per-line figures are scaled back down so that they still sum exactly to `promo_discount_cents`: cents are removed from the **last** eligible line in descending `created_at` order until the sum agrees. The identity that the per-line discounts sum to the order's discount always holds. Each line's `line_net_cents` is finally its `line_gross_cents` minus its `line_discount_cents`.

**Service fee.** The order's `service_fee_cents` is 0 for a `TOUCHSCREEN` or `MOBILE_PREORDER` order. For a `CATERING` order it is whichever is larger: a floor of 500 cents, or the discounted food figure multiplied by 800 basis points and divided by ten thousand — that is, eight percent of the discounted food total — rounded half up to whole cents. That single rounding is the only rounding in the totals pipeline; every other step is integer arithmetic.

**Provisional loyalty accrual.** When the order carries a `loyalty_account_id`, `points_provisional` is credited at commit so that the touchscreen can print an earned-points line on the receipt. Its basis is the order's `total_cents`, which **includes** any catering `service_fee_cents`, and its multiplier is read from the member's `tier` as it stands *before* this order's own accrual: 10000 basis points at `BASIC`, 12500 at `PLUS`, 15000 at `ELITE`. The points figure is the basis in cents multiplied by that basis-point multiplier, divided by one million, with any fractional remainder **discarded** rather than rounded — fractional points are always dropped. At `BASIC` that works out to one point per whole dollar of order total. The computed figure is added to the account's `points_balance` and to its `lifetime_points_earned`, the account's `tier` is re-derived, and the account's `version` increments by one. An anonymous order accrues zero in this variant and leaves no account untouched.

**Stock reservation.** For each par-baked line the selected batch is recorded on the line as its `fulfillment_batch_id`, the line's `reserved_qty` is set to the line's `qty`, and on the batch the same quantity is subtracted from `remaining_qty` and added to `reserved_qty`, with the batch's `version` incrementing by one. The batch's status is not changed by a reservation.

**Kitchen tickets and station sequencing.** One kitchen ticket is cut per **distinct station** among the order's `MADE_TO_ORDER` lines; par-baked lines never generate tickets. Each ticket records the `order_id` it serves, a `store_id` denormalized from the order so that station queues can be read per site, and its `station`, and opens in `QUEUED` with `started_by_user_id` and `completed_by_user_id` null and a `rework_count` of 0. Stations are processed in the fixed sequence `SANDWICH`, `BEVERAGE`, `HOT_FOOD`, `BAKERY`; tickets cut earlier in the same commit are already inserted when the next station's ticket is computed, though because tickets of different stations never share a queue this never changes an outcome.

A ticket's `prep_seconds` is the sum, over the order's `MADE_TO_ORDER` lines routed to that ticket's station, of the menu item's `prep_seconds` multiplied by the line's `qty`, plus 15 seconds of modifier handling multiplied by the line's total modifier unit count and again by the line's `qty`. A line's modifier unit count is the sum of the `qty` figures across its modifier entries. Par-baked lines contribute nothing to any ticket's `prep_seconds`.

The queue a new ticket joins consists of the tickets of the same `store_id` and the same `station` whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, ordered by ascending `created_at` and then by ascending `sequence_position`; tickets already `DONE` or `VOIDED` are excluded, so completing or voiding work genuinely shortens the lead time seen by the next commit. The new ticket's `sequence_position` is one more than the number of tickets already in that queue, counted **before** the new ticket is inserted. Its `lead_seconds` is the sum of the `prep_seconds` of exactly those tickets ahead of it. Its `promised_ready_at` is the current logical-clock instant advanced by its `lead_seconds` and then by its own `prep_seconds`, expressed in whole seconds and serialized with no fractional part. The order's `expected_ready_at` is the latest `promised_ready_at` among the tickets this commit created, or, when the commit created no tickets at all, the order's `placed_at`.

**Immediate cascades at commit.** The commit increments the order's `version` by one. When every line of the order is par-baked and therefore **zero** tickets were created, there is no kitchen work to wait for and the order advances `PLACED → ASSEMBLED` inside the same request. Entering `ASSEMBLED` immediately evaluates the ready gate described in §3.9, and where that gate holds the order advances `ASSEMBLED → READY` in the same request too, with all the reservation-to-sale consequences that entails.

### 3.9. The Make Line, Fulfilment Hand-Off, and the Independent Money Axis

The kitchen works from queues. A store's make-line queue is read per station and returned ordered by `sequence_position` and then by `created_at`, with optional filters on `station` and `status`. The tickets cut for one order can be listed with the same two filters, and tickets across the chain can be listed with filters on `order_id`, `order_line_id`, `station` and `status`. Individual tickets are readable by a `KitchenAssociate`, a `ShiftLead`, a `StoreManager` or a `StoreTerminal`.

**Claiming a ticket.** A `KitchenAssociate` claims a queued ticket, driving it `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS`. Three preconditions apply. The ticket's `status` must be `QUEUED`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — the same code covers completing a ticket that is not in progress, reopening a ticket that is not done, and any trigger at all against a `VOIDED` ticket. The owning order's `settlement_status` must be `AUTHORIZED`; kitchen work is deliberately blocked until tender has been authorized, and an attempt before then reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 409). And the owning order's `production_status` must be `PLACED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The claim records `started_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the ticket's `version` by one; where the order was still `PLACED`, it also advances the order `PLACED → IN_PRODUCTION` and increments the order's `version` by one.

**Completing a ticket.** A `KitchenAssociate` finishes a ticket in progress, driving it `IN_PROGRESS → DONE` and recording `completed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`; the ticket's `version` increments by one. `DONE` is *not* terminal, because rework can send the ticket back. When after this completion no ticket of the order remains in `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, the order advances `IN_PRODUCTION → ASSEMBLED` and its `version` increments; entering `ASSEMBLED` then evaluates the ready gate, and the resulting chain — assembly to ready, ready to reservation conversion, conversion to batch exhaustion, exhaustion to a freed hot-case slot — all lands inside this one request.

**The ready gate.** An order entering `ASSEMBLED` advances immediately to `READY` when either the `channel` is `TOUCHSCREEN`, or the current logical clock has reached the order's `pickup_window_start`. A pre-order or catering order that is assembled ahead of its window therefore *stays* in `ASSEMBLED`, which is why the operator release path below exists. On entering `READY` the order's `ready_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant and the order's `version` increments by one. For every line carrying a `fulfillment_batch_id`, the line's `reserved_qty` is subtracted from the batch's `reserved_qty` and added to the batch's `sold_qty`, the line's `reserved_qty` is then set to 0, and the batch's `version` increments by one. For every batch touched this way, if both its `remaining_qty` and its `reserved_qty` are now zero the batch advances `HOLDING → SOLD_OUT` — sale-driven exhaustion, as opposed to the write-off-driven `DISCARDED` outcome of §3.5 — which decrements the store's `hot_case_slots_used` by one and increments the store's `version` by one.

**The operator release path.** A `StoreTerminal` or a `StoreManager` may explicitly release an assembled pre-order or catering order once its window has opened, driving it `ASSEMBLED → READY`. The order's `production_status` must be `ASSEMBLED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`, and the current logical clock must have reached the order's `pickup_window_start`, otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Its side effects are identical, field for field, to the automatic transition into `READY` described above: `ready_at`, the reservation-to-sale conversion on every line with a `fulfillment_batch_id`, the possible batch exhaustion to `SOLD_OUT` with its slot release and store version bump, and the order's own version increment.

**Hand-off.** A `StoreTerminal` or the owning `Customer` hands a ready order to the customer, driving it `READY → PICKED_UP`, which is terminal on the production axis. The order's `production_status` must be `READY`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`, and for a `MOBILE_PREORDER` or `CATERING` order the current logical clock must have reached the `pickup_window_start`; a hand-off before the window opens, or after the `pickup_window_end` has passed, reports `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The reservation-to-sale conversion already happened when the order entered `READY`, so hand-off only advances the status and increments the order's `version` by one.

**Rework: the ticket cycle edge.** A `ShiftLead`, and only a `ShiftLead`, may send a finished ticket back to the make line with a `reason`, driving it `DONE → IN_PROGRESS`. Three preconditions apply, and the third is the segregation-of-duties rule from §2.11: the ticket's `status` must be `DONE`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`; the owning order's `production_status` must be `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ASSEMBLED`, so an order already picked up or voided is not reworkable and reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_REWORKABLE` (HTTP 409); and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the ticket's recorded `completed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A reopen increases the ticket's own `rework_count` by one and the order's `rework_count` by one, increments the order's `version`, and increments the ticket's `version`. Where the order had already reached `ASSEMBLED`, the reopen walks it back `ASSEMBLED → IN_PRODUCTION`. Rework also has a cost: the store's `rework_waste_cents_mtd` increases by the sum, over the reopened ticket's lines, of the menu item's `unit_food_cost_cents` multiplied by the line's `qty`, and the store's `version` increments by one. This is the one waste figure booked on the food-cost basis that produces **no** waste-log row, because nothing came out of a holding case; it nonetheless surfaces in the inspection summary of §3.6 alongside case waste.

**The money axis moves independently.** The settlement axis begins at `UNPAID`, and the only thing it ever reads on the production axis is enough to exclude a voided order. A `StoreTerminal` or the owning `Customer` authorizes tender, driving `UNPAID → AUTHORIZED`, by supplying a `tender_type` of `CARD`, `CASH` or `APP_WALLET`. The order's `settlement_status` must be `UNPAID`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and its `production_status` must be one of `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `ASSEMBLED` or `READY`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`. Authorization records the `tender_type`, increments the order's `version` by one, moves no points, and unblocks ticket claiming for every ticket of the order.

**Settlement and the final accrual.** A `StoreTerminal` or the owning `Customer` settles an authorized order, driving `AUTHORIZED → SETTLED`. The `settlement_status` must be `AUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and the `production_status` must not be `VOIDED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`. Where the order carries a `loyalty_account_id`, settlement computes `points_final` on a different basis from the provisional accrual, and the three differences are all deliberate. Its basis is the order's `subtotal_gross_cents` minus its `promo_discount_cents` and therefore **excludes** any catering `service_fee_cents`, because fees never earn points. Its tier multiplier is re-read from the member's `tier` **after** the provisional accrual has already lifted it, so an order that crosses a tier boundary earns at the higher multiplier. And when the promotion applied to the order has a `kind` of `FUEL_FOOD_COMBO`, a flat bonus of 50 points is added on top. As with the provisional variant, the base figure is the basis in cents multiplied by the tier multiplier in basis points, divided by one million, with the fractional remainder discarded rather than rounded; the fuel bonus is then added to that truncated figure. The settlement delta is `points_final` minus `points_provisional` and may be positive or negative. That delta is added to the account's `points_balance` and to its `lifetime_points_earned`, the `tier` is re-derived, and the account's `version` increments by one. Where the order carries no `loyalty_account_id`, `points_final` is set to 0 and no account is touched. Either way the order's `version` increments by one.

**Refund.** A `StoreManager` or a `ShiftLead` refunds a settled order, driving `SETTLED → REFUNDED`, which is terminal on the money axis, and supplying a `reason`. The `settlement_status` must be `SETTLED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` — this also rejects a second refund. The amount refunded is the order's `total_cents`. Where the order carries a `loyalty_account_id`, the account's `points_balance` is reduced by `points_final` and its `points_reversed_total` is increased by `points_final`, and the account's `version` increments by one; `lifetime_points_earned` and the derived `tier` deliberately stay exactly where settlement left them, because lifetime earnings are historical. The order's `version` increments by one.

**Void, and the compensating reversal.** A `StoreTerminal` or a `ShiftLead` voids an order before hand-off with a `reason`, driving `production_status` to `VOIDED` from `DRAFT`, `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ASSEMBLED`. `VOIDED` is terminal on the production axis. Two preconditions apply: the `production_status` must be one of those four, so an order already `READY` or `PICKED_UP` is **not** voidable — its reservations have already converted to sales — and an attempt reports `ERR_ORDER_PRODUCTION_STATE_INVALID`; and the `settlement_status` must not be `SETTLED`, because a settled order must be refunded rather than voided, with the attempt reporting `ERR_ORDER_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Voiding a draft is trivial: no reservations, tickets or points exist yet, so only the `production_status` and the order's `version` change. Voiding an order from `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `ASSEMBLED` runs an identical, larger set of reversals in every one of those three cases. Every kitchen ticket of the order whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS` goes to `VOIDED` and leaves its station queue, with each ticket's `version` incrementing by one; tickets already `DONE` are left untouched. For every line carrying a `fulfillment_batch_id` and a `reserved_qty` above zero, that reserved quantity is added back to the batch's `remaining_qty` and subtracted from the batch's `reserved_qty`, the line's `reserved_qty` is set to 0, and the batch's `version` increments by one — the batch's status is unchanged, because the released units simply return to the case. Where `points_provisional` is above zero, the account's `points_balance` is reduced by `points_provisional` and its `points_reversed_total` increased by the same figure, with the account's `version` incrementing by one; `lifetime_points_earned` and `tier` are historical and do not move. Where the `settlement_status` was `AUTHORIZED`, it moves to `RELEASED` on the money axis, which releases the authorization hold and is terminal there; no points move on that axis, since the production-axis void has already reversed the provisional accrual. Finally the order's `version` increments by one.
