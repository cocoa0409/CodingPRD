# Nonprofit Merger & Affiliation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Nonprofit boards rarely restructure quickly, and almost never cleanly. A hospice looking to fold its bereavement program into a larger behavioral-health agency must first argue the strategic rationale to its own directors, then open a confidential conversation with a partner it may not be able to name publicly, then survive a diligence process that reaches into restricted gifts, litigation reserves and workforce obligations, then win a supermajority at two separate board tables, then persuade a state attorney general that charitable assets are not being diverted — and only then begin the unglamorous work of merging payroll systems and deciding which programs survive. Each of those steps produces documents that the next step depends on, and each of them can be undone. The single most common failure mode in this domain is not a missed deadline; it is a decision recorded against a version of the deal that has since changed underneath it.

This document specifies a system of record for that process. It tracks organizations and their programs, the restructuring transaction between two of them, the letters of intent that open and close exclusivity windows, the diligence findings that can throw a signed deal back to the negotiating table, the restricted funds that need donor consent or a court order before they can move, the board resolutions that allocate seats on the combined board, the attorney-general filings that gate closing, and the integration tasks that must finish before consummation. Every derived figure — reserve months, alignment scores, filing fees, seat allocations, restricted corpus — is computed by the server from stored inputs, never accepted from a client, so that two parties reading the same deal read the same numbers.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **compensating actions are first-class**: a certified board resolution can be rescinded, an executed restricted-fund transfer can be unwound, and a consummated-but-not-yet-closed deal can be abandoned, each restoring the quantities it consumed exactly. Second, **segregation of duties is enforced mechanically rather than by policy memo**: the diligence lead who raises a finding cannot clear it, the secretary who records minutes cannot certify them, and the counsel who files with the attorney general cannot record the regulator's answer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the caller is claiming. Header names are matched case-insensitively, and surrounding whitespace on either header *value* is stripped before that value is used, so a header that is present but empty or whitespace-only counts as absent. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the target endpoint as listed in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `StrategyOfficer`, `BoardSecretary`, `DiligenceLead`, `CharitableTrustCounsel`, `StateCharitiesRegulator` and `IntegrationManager`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the registry operator: it onboards organizations, programs and restricted funds, runs the periodic regulatory sweep, and drives the logical clock. It is never a party to a deal and never votes.

A `StrategyOfficer` is the acting officer of a party organization. It opens exploratory conversations, negotiates letters of intent, selects the legal structure, and consummates or abandons the transaction.

A `BoardSecretary` is the board secretary function of a party organization. It records the board vote and certifies the resulting minutes and fiduciary memorandum.

A `DiligenceLead` is an independent reviewer across the five diligence workstreams. It raises findings, escalates them, and may clear or waive only findings raised by a different user.

A `CharitableTrustCounsel` handles restricted gifts and endowments: the donor-consent branch, the judicial-modification branch, transfer execution and unwinding, and attorney-general filings.

A `StateCharitiesRegulator` represents the state attorney general's charities bureau. It reads filings and records their decisions, and holds no write access to anything else, so it can never manufacture the transaction it reviews.

An `IntegrationManager` is the post-close integration lead across staffing, systems, branding and program continuity. It plans and runs integration tasks and records program continuity decisions.

**Identity semantics.** `StrategyOfficer` and `BoardSecretary` are entity-backed roles: when `X-User-Role` is `StrategyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Organization`, and when `X-User-Role` is `BoardSecretary`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Organization`. For both roles, ownership checks on any resource that references an `Organization` compare the resource's organization reference to `X-User-ID` — concretely, a deal's `initiator_org_id` and `target_org_id`, and for a financial restatement the organization named in the path. The remaining five roles — `PlatformAdmin`, `DiligenceLead`, `CharitableTrustCounsel`, `StateCharitiesRegulator` and `IntegrationManager` — carry `identity: opaque`: their `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `raised_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a diligence finding, where the state transitions write it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Where an entity-backed actor is permitted at all, being *a* party is not always sufficient. Deal-party ownership is checked as its own rule and, when violated, always answers `ERR_NOT_DEAL_PARTY` (HTTP 403). The precise expectation per operation is stated in §2.7 and reinforced in the workflow narratives of §3.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` — concretely the literal `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states a different order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because every `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, many rows legitimately share one, so rows that tie on the ordering key are ordered by ascending insertion sequence — the order in which the server created them — which makes every list order total and deterministic; that same insertion-sequence tie-break runs last on the two endpoints that order by `sequence_no` or by `round_no` and then `created_at`. The `meta` block of a list response carries all four pagination fields — `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` set to true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp. Clients never supply `id` values for resources they create; a client-supplied `id` is ignored. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation and equal to `created_at` on a row that has not yet been mutated, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on each mutation of that row. `Organization`, `Program`, `Restructuring`, `LetterOfIntent`, `DiligenceRequest`, `RestrictedFund`, `BoardApproval`, `AGFiling` and `IntegrationTask` all carry both. The one exception is `AlignmentAssessment`, which is an immutable snapshot: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. There is no endpoint that mutates an assessment, and a later financial restatement or program continuity decision never retroactively edits one — it only changes what a *subsequent* assessment would compute.

All time-derived values in this system read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The server's startup default clock value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any clock-setting call the logical clock reads that default and must not fall back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced, both rendered in the canonical three-fraction-digit form above however the input was spelled. That one input is liberal about spelling: it is accepted with or without a trailing `Z`, with no fractional digits or with between one and nine of them, and with a numeric `±HH:MM` offset that is normalised to UTC before the comparison below. The clock is monotonic — a requested value earlier than the value currently held is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock does not itself apply any time-driven transition; the regulatory sweep described in §3.9 is what applies them. Every field the workflows populate from "the current moment" — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `submitted_at`, `executed_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `decision_recorded_at`, `petition_filed_at`, `consent_signed_at`, and every sweep comparison — reads the logical clock.

### 2.5. Deal-Version Pinning on Create-with-Supersede Writes

Two write operations in this system legitimately supersede an earlier record: issuing a new letter of intent for a fresh negotiation round, and recording a new board vote for a party that has already voted. Because a repeat of either operation is *valid*, a state precondition alone cannot tell a deliberate second attempt apart from one made against a stale reading of the deal. Both are therefore pinned to the parent `Restructuring` row's `version`.

Issuing a letter of intent requires a non-optional `expected_restructuring_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Restructuring`. If that value does not equal the deal's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESTRUCTURING_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so a client cannot displace a negotiating position, a seat pool, a round counter or a restricted-asset posture change it has not seen.

Recording a board approval likewise requires a non-optional `expected_restructuring_version` matching the deal's current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESTRUCTURING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A re-vote by the same party returns that party's seat allocation to the pool and supersedes its earlier resolution, so the write must be pinned to the deal version the board actually deliberated over.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, none of the letter-of-intent actions, none of the diligence actions, none of the restricted-fund actions, none of the certify or rescind actions, and none of the integration-task actions accept an expected-version input.

**Version arithmetic is per request, not per side effect.** When a single request both writes a child row and drives an automatic transition of its parent deal, `Restructuring.version` increases by exactly one, never by two. A request refused by any validation — including the two version checks above — increments nothing and mutates nothing.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Precision

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; no monetary field is ever fractional. Where a division produces cents, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. There is one deliberate exception, called out again in §3.6: the endowment spending-rate slice is truncated toward zero rather than rounded.

Ratios and scores are decimal numbers with fixed widths. `program_overlap_index` and both vote ratios (`approval_ratio` and `required_ratio`) carry four decimal places; `reserve_months`, the three component point figures (`mission_alignment_points`, `program_overlap_points`, `sustainability_points`) and `composite_score` carry two decimal places. Every one of these is rounded half up at the stated width. That width fixes the rounding precision, not the rendered spelling: each such figure is serialised as a JSON number and never as a quoted string, and insignificant trailing zeros are not preserved — a `required_ratio` of `0.6000` is rendered `0.6`, a `program_overlap_index` of `1.0000` is rendered `1.0`, `program_overlap_points` of `30.00` is rendered `30.0`, and a `composite_score` of `70.00` is rendered `70.0`. Seat counts and day counts are integers with no rounding and no fractional allocation.

Rounding order is itself part of the contract wherever the specification pins it. §3.2 pins the order for combined monthly expense, and §3.7 pins the order for the attorney-general fee tier; implementations that reorder those steps will produce off-by-one-cent and off-by-one-tier results.

### 2.7. Request Admission Order

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the **lowest-numbered** rule in the ladder below. Evaluation is strictly in ascending order and short-circuits on the first violation, so a later, more specific rule can never mask an earlier, more general one.

1. Both authentication headers present, with `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The supplied role is authorized for the target endpoint per §4 — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. The body and query string parse; required fields are present and non-null; types match; enum values are members of their declared set; integers fall inside their declared ranges; dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates and timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC; `limit` is between 1 and 100 and `offset` is at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A request body may be omitted entirely, or sent empty, wherever every field it would carry is optional: an absent or empty body is read as the empty JSON object, and the request's `Content-Type` is never inspected. A body that is present but is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON that is not an object, is rejected at this position. An optional field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated as absent so its documented default applies, whereas a required field supplied as `null` is rejected. A query parameter supplied as an empty string is likewise treated as absent, so it neither filters nor overrides a default. Body fields and query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Every body field that names another resource — `restructuring_id`, `org_id`, `initiator_org_id`, `target_org_id` and `depends_on_task_id` — must additionally be a non-blank string at this position, so an empty or whitespace-only reference is a malformed request answered here rather than a not-found refusal further down the ladder.
4. Every `Organization` referenced by a path segment or by a body reference field — `org_id`, `initiator_org_id`, `target_org_id` — exists, otherwise `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards exactly the six operations that name an organization directly: the single-organization read, the organization's program list, the financial restatement, program registration, restructuring creation and restricted-fund registration. No write elsewhere takes an organization reference in its body: a restricted-fund transfer and a program continuity decision each derive the counterparty organization from the deal rather than accepting it, so neither can answer this code.
5. The `Restructuring` named by a path segment or by the `restructuring_id` body field exists, otherwise `ERR_RESTRUCTURING_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards every deal-scoped path and every create that references a deal: alignment assessments, letters of intent, diligence findings, board approvals, attorney-general filings, integration tasks, and restricted-fund transfer.
6. The `Program` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards the program continuity decision.
7. The `LetterOfIntent` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_LETTER_OF_INTENT_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards all four letter actions: counter, revise, execute and withdraw.
8. The `DiligenceRequest` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_DILIGENCE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards all four finding actions: respond, clear, escalate and waive.
9. The `RestrictedFund` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_RESTRICTED_FUND_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards the single-fund read and all six fund actions: seek-consent, record-consent, file-petition, record-court-decision, transfer and unwind.
10. The `BoardApproval` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_BOARD_APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards certify and rescind.
11. The `AGFiling` named by a path segment exists — otherwise `ERR_AG_FILING_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards the regulator decision.
12. The `IntegrationTask` named by a path segment exists, and a supplied `depends_on_task_id` resolves to an existing task — otherwise `ERR_INTEGRATION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards the single-task read, task creation, start and complete.
13. Deal-party ownership for entity-backed roles, otherwise `ERR_NOT_DEAL_PARTY`. For `StrategyOfficer` and `BoardSecretary`, `X-User-ID` must equal `initiator_org_id` or `target_org_id` of the deal being acted on, and for a financial restatement it must equal the organization named in the path. Where a workflow names one specific party, the other party is refused with this same error: countering and executing a letter require the target organization; revising and withdrawing a letter, selecting the structure, and consummating require the initiator organization; recording a board approval and its certify and rescind actions require the organization resolved from `approving_party`. Abandonment accepts either party. Responding to a diligence finding checks only two-party membership, since either side may answer. This rule guards structure selection, consummation, abandonment, letter issuance and all four letter actions, board approval recording and both of its actions, the financial restatement, the program continuity decision, and the diligence response.
14. Segregation of duties, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On clearing and on waiving a finding, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `raised_by_user_id`. On certifying a board approval, the supplied `certifying_secretary_ref` must differ from the approval's stored `recording_secretary_ref` — a `BoardSecretary`'s `X-User-ID` is the party organization's `id` and therefore cannot by itself distinguish two staff members. This rule guards exactly three operations: clear, waive and certify.
15. `expected_restructuring_version` equals the deal's current `version` per §2.5 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_RESTRUCTURING_VERSION`. This rule guards letter-of-intent issuance and board-approval recording.
16. `Restructuring.status` permits the requested operation — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. This rule guards structure selection, consummation, abandonment, alignment-assessment creation, letter issuance, diligence-finding creation, diligence-finding escalation, board-approval recording, board-approval rescission, attorney-general filing creation, integration-task creation, integration-task start, restricted-fund transfer, restricted-fund unwind, and the program continuity decision. It does not guard letter execution, any of the other three finding actions, board-approval certification, or integration-task completion.
17. `LetterOfIntent.status` permits the operation, and `exclusivity_status` is `ACTIVE` where counter, revise or execute is requested — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LETTER_STATE`. This rule guards counter, revise, execute and withdraw.
18. `RestrictedFund.transfer_status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_FUND_STATE`. This rule guards seek-consent, record-consent, file-petition, record-court-decision, transfer and unwind. On transfer this rule covers only the two already-final dispositions `TRANSFERRED` and `RETAINED`; the pre-authority states `HELD`, `CONSENT_SOUGHT`, `CONSENT_DENIED` and `PETITION_FILED` are refused instead at rule 30, which names the missing authority rather than the row's status.
19. `BoardApproval.status` permits the operation — certify requires `RECORDED`, rescind requires `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`.
20. `AGFiling.status` is `SUBMITTED` when a regulator decision is recorded — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`.
21. `IntegrationTask.status` permits the operation — start requires `PLANNED`, complete requires `IN_PROGRESS` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`.
22. `DiligenceRequest.status` permits the operation — respond requires `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, clear requires `RESPONDED`, escalate requires `OPEN` or `RESPONDED`, waive requires `OPEN`, `RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED`, and a row already in the terminal `CLEARED` or `WAIVED` status is refused by all four actions — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DILIGENCE_STATE`.
23. `initiator_org_id` and `target_org_id` name different organizations — otherwise `ERR_SELF_RESTRUCTURING`.
24. Neither party's `affiliation_role` is `DISSOLVED`, `DIVESTED` or `SUBSIDIARY` — otherwise `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
25. The supplied `ein` is not already registered to another organization — otherwise `ERR_EIN_ALREADY_REGISTERED`.
26. On transfer, the fund's `org_id` is one of the two party organizations of the supplied `restructuring_id` — otherwise `ERR_FUND_NOT_DEAL_PARTY_ASSET`. The recipient is not supplied on the request: it is derived as whichever of the two parties does not hold the fund, so this one membership test is the whole of the rule.
27. On a continuity decision, the program's `org_id` is one of the two party organizations of the deal, `transferred_to_org_id`, where it is set, is the other party, and the program's `continuity_status` is still `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_DEAL_PARTY_ASSET`. All three conditions answer that one code from this single ladder position, so a program that has already received a disposition is refused here rather than with a state error.
28. A supplied `depends_on_task_id` belongs to the same deal as the task being created and is not the task itself — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_IN_SAME_DEAL`.
29. A fund whose `restriction_conflict` is true may not be transferred on `transfer_basis` `DONOR_CONSENT` — otherwise `ERR_JUDICIAL_ORDER_REQUIRED`.
30. A fund may only be transferred once it holds an authority, and a petition may only be filed straight from `HELD` in the unreachable-donor case — otherwise `ERR_DONOR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. This rule guards transfer and file-petition.
31. `historic_dollar_value_cents` does not exceed `principal_cents` plus `accumulated_appreciation_cents` — otherwise `ERR_HDV_EXCEEDS_FUND_VALUE`.
32. The deal's `ag_review_required` is true when an attorney-general filing is created — otherwise `ERR_AG_REVIEW_NOT_REQUIRED`.
33. The filing's `state_code` is a party organization's state of incorporation and is one of the attorney-general review states — otherwise `ERR_AG_STATE_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
34. `filing_type` equals the required type derived in §3.7 — otherwise `ERR_AG_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
35. Where `ag_review_required` is true, an attorney-general clearance is required — otherwise `ERR_AG_APPROVAL_MISSING`. This rule guards two operations, with deliberately different conditions: starting an integration task requires only that some filing of the deal stand in `APPROVED` or `DEEMED_APPROVED`, whereas consummation requires that *and* that no filing of the deal stand in `OBJECTED`.
36. `conflict_disclosures_count` is at least `recusals`, and the `fiduciary_memo_ref` supplied at certification is a non-blank reference — otherwise `ERR_CONFLICT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`. Within this one ladder position the disclosure comparison speaks before the memo check, and the memo's blankness is deliberately answered here rather than at rule 3.
37. `votes_for` plus `votes_against` plus `abstentions` does not exceed `seats_total` minus `recusals` — otherwise `ERR_VOTE_TALLY_EXCEEDS_SEATS`.
38. Attendance meets quorum — otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`.
39. `votes_for` plus `votes_against` is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTES`.
40. The requested exclusivity window does not overlap an `ACTIVE` window of another deal involving either party — otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`.
41. The cooling-off period after a released or expired window with a *different* counterparty has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_COOLING_OFF`.
42. The requested crew window does not overlap another non-cancelled task sharing the same `crew_code` — otherwise `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
43. Where `depends_on_task_id` is set, the predecessor is `COMPLETE` before the dependent task may start — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_COMPLETE`.
44. The `structure_type` on the request equals the executed letter's `proposed_structure_type` — otherwise `ERR_STRUCTURE_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
45. `board_seat_split_initiator` is at least 1 and at most `combined_board_seats` minus 1 — otherwise `ERR_SEAT_SPLIT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. This rule guards both letter issuance and letter revision.
46. `restricted_asset_posture` is `NOT_ASSESSED` or `CLEARED` at consummation — otherwise `ERR_RESTRICTED_ASSETS_UNRESOLVED`.
47. Every integration task of the deal is `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED` at consummation — otherwise `ERR_INTEGRATION_INCOMPLETE`.
48. The latest diligence-adjusted assessment exists and is rated `STRONG` or `MODERATE` — otherwise `ERR_ALIGNMENT_RATING_INSUFFICIENT`.
49. That same assessment's `reserve_months` meets the reserve floor — otherwise `ERR_RESERVE_BELOW_FLOOR`.
50. The derived `transferred_charitable_asset_value_cents` is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_NO_CHARITABLE_ASSETS_TRANSFERRED`. The `legal_costs_cents` lower bound of 0 is not part of this rule: a negative figure violates a declared integer range and is therefore refused far earlier, at rule 3, with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
51. A requested logical clock value is at least the value currently held — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`.

The substantive content of rules 23 through 51 — what each threshold means and which business event each guards — lives in the workflow narratives of §3. This ladder fixes only the order in which they speak.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Where they do, the specification names every hop, and an implementation that applies only the first hop is incomplete. Two cascades run deep enough to deserve names of their own: escalating a critical diligence finding reaches five hops, described in §3.4, and executing a restricted-fund transfer reaches four, described in §3.6. Abandoning a deal fans out across four child collections, described in §3.9.

Alongside the cascades, two quantities are **conserved** and may be checked at any instant. The first is the deal's seat ledger: the sum of `reserved_seat_pool` and the `allocated_seats` of every certified board approval of that deal always equals `combined_board_seats`, from the moment a letter is executed onward. Because that identity holds continuously, a compliant implementation can never observe a reserved pool too small to satisfy a certification, and no client-facing error is defined for that situation. The second is the party-pair restricted corpus: the sum of `restricted_corpus_cents` across a deal's two party organizations is unchanged by executing a restricted-fund transfer and by unwinding one, because a transfer only moves value from the holder's retained side to the recipient's received side of the same total.

Whenever a cascade touches a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, subject to the per-request arithmetic of §2.5. Re-deriving a computed figure is not by itself such a touch: §3.1 and §3.6 name the recomputations that rewrite a derived field in place while leaving the holding row's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they were.

### 2.9. Path Resolution for Nested Reads

The deal-scoped list endpoints are the only nested paths in this API; every write addresses a top-level collection and names its parent through a body field instead. Where a path does nest, its segments are validated left to right: the parent deal's existence is confirmed before any filter on the child collection is applied, so a request for the letters of a nonexistent deal answers `ERR_RESTRUCTURING_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty page. The same left-to-right discipline applies to the organization's program list, which resolves the organization first and answers `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND` when it is absent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Registry: Organizations and Programs

Before any transaction exists there must be organizations to transact. A `PlatformAdmin` registers a nonprofit by supplying its registered charitable `legal_name` (between 1 and 160 characters, not blank), its `ein` (formatted as two digits, a hyphen, then seven digits, and unique across the whole registry — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_EIN_ALREADY_REGISTERED`), its `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters, which is what later determines attorney-general jurisdiction), its `mission_code` (two uppercase letters, a hyphen, then one or more uppercase letters or underscores — the two characters *before* the hyphen are the organization's **mission family**), its total `annual_expense_cents`, and its `unrestricted_net_assets_cents`. The two financial figures must each be at least 0.

Registration derives one figure immediately and initialises four others. `monthly_expense_cents` is the organization's annual operating expense divided by twelve and rounded half up to a whole cent — a per-organization figure that is deliberately computed and stored *before* any deal ever sums it. `restricted_corpus_cents` and `governance_seats` both start at 0, `affiliation_role` starts at `INDEPENDENT`, and `parent_org_id` starts as null.

An organization's structural role after a transaction closes is recorded in `affiliation_role`, which takes one of `INDEPENDENT`, `SURVIVOR`, `DISSOLVED`, `DIVESTED`, `PARENT`, `SUBSIDIARY`, `MSA_PROVIDER` or `MSA_CLIENT`. Only a consummated deal writes any value other than `INDEPENDENT`, and only a consummated parent-subsidiary transaction sets `parent_org_id` on the absorbed organization. Because the registry supports reverse lookup along that self-reference, the organization list can be filtered by `parent_org_id`, by `affiliation_role` and by `state_code`.

Audited financials get restated more often than outsiders expect. A `PlatformAdmin` or the organization's own `StrategyOfficer` may restate a single organization's figures by supplying a revised `annual_expense_cents`, a revised `unrestricted_net_assets_cents` and a `restatement_reason`. The restatement recomputes `monthly_expense_cents` from the new annual figure by the same divide-by-twelve, round-half-up step, and bumps the organization's `version`. The `restatement_reason` is required and must not be blank, but it is audit narrative only: it is not stored on the organization and appears nowhere in the response. It does **not** reach backwards into any existing alignment assessment; snapshots taken before the restatement keep the numbers they were computed with.

`restricted_corpus_cents` on an organization is not an input and is never accepted from a client. It is recomputed on *every* restricted-fund mutation — registration, consent solicitation, consent recording, petition filing, court-decision recording, transfer and unwind — for every organization the fund touches, and it is the sum of three disjoint contributions. First, for every fund whose holder of record is this organization and whose `transfer_status` is anything other than `TRANSFERRED`, add that fund's `principal_cents` plus its `accumulated_appreciation_cents`. Second, for every fund whose holder of record is this organization and whose `transfer_status` is `TRANSFERRED`, add only that fund's `retained_amount_cents`. Third, for every fund anywhere in the registry whose recipient is this organization and whose `transfer_status` is `TRANSFERRED`, add that fund's `transferred_amount_cents`. The three sums are exact integer cents with no rounding at any step, and no time window or ordering enters into them. Recomputation writes the figure in place and is not by itself a mutation of the organization row: where the recomputed total equals the value already stored, that organization's `updated_at` and `version` both stand still. The two operations that actually move value between two parties — an executed restricted-fund transfer and its unwind — are the exception, stamping each of the two party organizations as mutated whether or not its own figure moved.

Programs are the operating units that a restructuring actually rearranges. A program is registered against one organization by supplying that organization's identifier as `org_id`, a `name` between 1 and 160 characters, a `service_category` drawn from `YOUTH_MENTORING`, `FOOD_SECURITY`, `HOUSING`, `WORKFORCE_TRAINING`, `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH` and `ARTS_EDUCATION`, a `county_code` in the same two-letters-hyphen-uppercase-word shape as a mission code, an `annual_expense_cents` of at least 0, and a `clients_served` count of at least 0 representing annual unduplicated clients. A new program's `continuity_status` is `ACTIVE` and its `transferred_to_org_id` is null. `continuity_status` moves through `ACTIVE`, `PLANNED_TRANSFER`, `PLANNED_SUNSET`, `TRANSFERRED` and `SUNSET`, and the program list of one organization can be filtered by `service_category` and by `continuity_status`. That list is not confined to the programs the organization registered: it admits every program whose `org_id` is this organization **or** whose `transferred_to_org_id` is, so a program a consummated transfer moved here appears on both organizations' lists at once.

### 3.2. Opening the Conversation and Building the Strategic Rationale

A `StrategyOfficer` opens a confidential exploratory conversation by naming the two organizations involved: its own as `initiator_org_id` and the prospective partner as `target_org_id`. Creation is not one of the operations guarded by ladder rule 13, so the server does not require the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `initiator_org_id`; both organizations need only exist. It may optionally set a `confidentiality_tier` of `STANDARD`, `RESTRICTED` or `BOARD_ONLY`; when the field is omitted the tier defaults to `STANDARD`. The two organizations must be different — naming the same organization twice is refused with `ERR_SELF_RESTRUCTURING` — and neither may already be `DISSOLVED`, `DIVESTED` or `SUBSIDIARY`, since an organization that has been dissolved, divested or absorbed cannot restructure again; those cases answer `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Concurrent exploratory conversations with *different* partners are permitted at this stage; what constrains them is the exclusivity machinery of §3.3.

A newly opened deal is `EXPLORATORY`, with `round_no` at 1, `combined_board_seats` and `reserved_seat_pool` at 0, `unrestricted_transfer_value_cents` at 0, `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` at 0, `restricted_asset_posture` at `NOT_ASSESSED`, `structure_type` null, `abandonment_reason` null and `version` at 1. Its `status` will move through `EXPLORATORY`, `RATIONALE_BUILT`, `LOI_NEGOTIATION`, `DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL`, `REGULATORY_REVIEW` and `INTEGRATION` before reaching one of the two terminal states `CONSUMMATED` and `ABANDONED`. One field is fixed at creation and never recomputed: `ag_review_required` is set to true when either party's `state_code` is one of the five attorney-general review states — California, New York, Massachusetts, Illinois and Washington, written `CA`, `NY`, `MA`, `IL` and `WA` — and false otherwise. Deals may be listed filtered by `initiator_org_id`, by `target_org_id`, by `status` and by `structure_type`.

The strategic rationale itself is expressed as an **alignment assessment** — an immutable scored snapshot created by a `StrategyOfficer` against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, in one of two `variant` values: `SCREENING`, the pre-diligence rationale, or `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED`, the post-diligence snapshot that later gates structure selection. Snapshots may only be taken while the deal is `EXPLORATORY`, `RATIONALE_BUILT`, `LOI_NEGOTIATION`, `DILIGENCE` or `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`; any other deal status answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. Each new snapshot receives a `sequence_no` equal to one plus the count of prior snapshots of the *same* deal and the *same* variant, which is what makes "the latest diligence-adjusted assessment" a well-defined selection. The very first successful `SCREENING` snapshot of a deal also advances the deal `EXPLORATORY → RATIONALE_BUILT` and bumps its `version` by one; the transition fires only from `EXPLORATORY`, so subsequent snapshots leave the deal status alone.

Computing a snapshot begins with the financial picture. The two parties' already-rounded monthly figures are added to give `combined_monthly_expense_cents`: each organization's annual expense is divided by twelve and rounded half up to whole cents **first**, and only then are the two rounded figures summed. Adding the two annual expenses together and dividing the total by twelve once is a different order that can land one cent lower, and one cent is enough to move `reserve_months` across a tier boundary and change `composite_score` by ten points; the per-organization-then-sum order is the only sanctioned one. Next, `open_diligence_exposure_cents` is determined: it is always 0 for a `SCREENING` snapshot, while for a `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` snapshot it is the sum of `exposure_cents` over the deal's diligence findings whose `status` is `OPEN`, `RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED` — findings already `CLEARED` or `WAIVED` contribute nothing. Then `available_unrestricted_cents` is the two parties' `unrestricted_net_assets_cents` added together, less that open exposure, floored at 0 so it is never negative. Finally `reserve_months` is `available_unrestricted_cents` divided by `combined_monthly_expense_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places — and exactly `0.00` when the combined monthly expense is 0, which is the only division-by-zero case in the whole calculation.

The score itself is the sum of three components, each rounded half up to two decimal places, whose total `composite_score` lies between `0.00` and `100.00`. The **mission component** starts from a three-way comparison of the parties' mission codes: identical codes earn `40.00` points, different codes that nevertheless share a mission family (the two characters preceding the hyphen) earn `28.00`, and unrelated codes earn `12.00`. For a `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` snapshot only, if the deal has any diligence finding whose `workstream` is `RESTRICTED_FUNDS`, whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is currently `ESCALATED`, ten points are deducted from that base and the result is floored at `0.00`; the resulting figure is `mission_alignment_points`. A `SCREENING` snapshot never applies that deduction.

The **overlap component** peaks in the middle rather than at the extremes, because a partner with no program overlap offers no consolidation savings while a partner with total overlap offers no new reach. The variant decides which programs are admitted: `SCREENING` admits every program of both parties regardless of `continuity_status`, whereas `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` admits only programs whose `continuity_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PLANNED_TRANSFER`. `included_program_count` is the total number of admitted programs across both parties. `overlap_pair_count` is the number of ordered pairs — one admitted initiator program and one admitted target program — that share both the same `service_category` and the same `county_code`. Let the denominator be the smaller of the two parties' admitted program counts. When that denominator is 0, `program_overlap_index` is `0.0000`; otherwise it is `overlap_pair_count` divided by that denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places and then capped at `1.0000`. `program_overlap_points` is then thirty points multiplied by the quantity one minus twice the absolute difference between `program_overlap_index` and `0.5`, rounded half up to two decimal places: an index of exactly `0.5` yields the full `30.00`, and an index of `0.0000` or `1.0000` yields `0.00`.

The **sustainability component** is a four-tier step function on `reserve_months`: at least six months earns `30.00` points, at least three but under six earns `20.00`, at least one and a half but under three earns `10.00`, and anything below one and a half months of reserve earns `0.00`. `sustainability_points` records the result. `composite_score` is `mission_alignment_points` plus `program_overlap_points` plus `sustainability_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `composite_rating` bands it: at least `80.00` is `STRONG`, at least `60.00` but under 80 is `MODERATE`, at least `40.00` but under 60 is `WEAK`, and anything below `40.00` is `NOT_VIABLE`.

Because the two variants differ in program admission, in exposure netting and in the restricted-funds deduction, the same stored data legitimately produces two different `composite_score` values and can produce two different `composite_rating` values. That is the point: the screening snapshot argues the case for talking, and the diligence-adjusted snapshot argues the case for signing. A deal's snapshots may be listed in ascending `sequence_no` order and filtered by `variant`.

### 3.3. Letters of Intent, Exclusivity Windows and the Negotiation Cycle

Once a rationale exists, the initiator's `StrategyOfficer` issues the round's **letter of intent**. Issuance is permitted only while the deal is `RATIONALE_BUILT` or `LOI_NEGOTIATION` — any other deal status answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` — and the request must carry the deal's identifier as `restructuring_id`, the deal-version pin `expected_restructuring_version` described in §2.5, a `proposed_structure_type` drawn from `MERGER`, `ASSET_ACQUISITION`, `PARENT_SUBSIDIARY` and `MANAGEMENT_SERVICES_AGREEMENT`, a `combined_board_seats` count between 3 and 51 inclusive, a `board_seat_split_initiator` naming how many of those seats the initiator would hold, an `exclusivity_days` length between 1 and 365 inclusive, and an `exclusivity_start_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The seat split must leave both sides represented: it must be at least 1 and at most `combined_board_seats` minus 1, and a split outside that range is refused with `ERR_SEAT_SPLIT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The start date is supplied by the issuing party and may fall later than the calendar date the logical clock currently reads, but it may not fall earlier than that date; an earlier date is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A newly issued letter lands with `status` `ISSUED`, `exclusivity_status` `ACTIVE`, `counter_round_count` at 0, `countered_by_party` null, `executed_at` null and `withdrawal_reason` null, and it copies the deal's current `round_no` into its own `round_no` so that later rounds are distinguishable. The server derives the closed exclusivity window from the two supplied inputs: `exclusivity_end_date` is `exclusivity_start_date` advanced by `exclusivity_days` minus one calendar day, so a one-day window starts and ends on the same date and both endpoints are inside the window. `expires_at` renders that end date at `00:00:00.000Z`. The first letter of a deal also advances it `RATIONALE_BUILT → LOI_NEGOTIATION`, and issuing a letter for a new round supersedes any letter of a **lower** `round_no` still sitting in `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED`: those letters move to `SUPERSEDED` with `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED`. Whether or not any of that fan-out happens, the deal's `version` rises by exactly one for the request.

Exclusivity is where concurrent courtships get policed, and two independent rules apply, both evaluated on issuance. The first forbids overlap: the requested window from `exclusivity_start_date` through `exclusivity_end_date`, inclusive at both ends, must not overlap the `ACTIVE` exclusivity window of any *other* deal in which either party organization appears as initiator or as target. Because both endpoints are inclusive, two windows that share even a single calendar day conflict, and the offending request is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`. The second imposes a cooling-off period after a courtship ends: where the most recent `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` window of a party involved a **different** counterparty than the one now proposed, the new `exclusivity_start_date` must fall strictly more than thirty calendar days after that window's `exclusivity_end_date`, and a start date inside those thirty days answers `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_COOLING_OFF`. "Most recent" is decided across every `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` letter of every deal in which either party appears, by the latest `exclusivity_end_date` with the later-created letter breaking a tie, and only that one window is examined: once a more recent window exists, an older different-counterparty window imposes nothing. A repeat window with the *same* counterparty is exempt from the cooling-off period entirely — parties are allowed to resume talks with each other immediately, just not to shop the deal.

Every letter of intent carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the most common implementation error in this area. `status` is the negotiation dimension, moving through `ISSUED`, `COUNTERED`, `EXECUTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `LAPSED` and `SUPERSEDED`, of which the last four are terminal. `exclusivity_status` is the confidentiality dimension, taking `ACTIVE`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED`. They evolve without regard for one another: a sweep can flip `exclusivity_status` from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` while `status` remains `EXECUTED`, and a counter can flip `status` from `ISSUED` to `COUNTERED` while `exclusivity_status` remains `ACTIVE`. Letters of one deal may be listed filtered by `status` and by `exclusivity_status`, ordered by ascending `round_no` and then `created_at`.

Countering is the target organization's move, and only the target's — the non-issuing party is the one that objects. Countering an `ISSUED` letter with a `counter_note` — required and not blank, but, like a restatement reason, recorded nowhere on the letter and absent from the response — advances it `ISSUED → COUNTERED`, sets `countered_by_party` to `TARGET`, increases `counter_round_count` by one, and pointedly leaves `exclusivity_status` untouched at `ACTIVE`. The letter's own `version` rises by one; the deal row is not touched and stays in `LOI_NEGOTIATION`. A counter attempted by the initiator answers `ERR_NOT_DEAL_PARTY`; a counter attempted against a letter that is not `ISSUED`, or whose window is no longer `ACTIVE`, answers `ERR_INVALID_LETTER_STATE`.

Revision closes the loop. The initiator organization revises a `COUNTERED` letter by supplying a new `board_seat_split_initiator`, which overwrites the stored value and returns the letter `COUNTERED → ISSUED`. The revised split is held to the same range as the original — at least 1 and at most `combined_board_seats` minus 1, else `ERR_SEAT_SPLIT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Revision deliberately preserves the negotiation history and the confidentiality clock: `counter_round_count`, `exclusivity_start_date`, `exclusivity_end_date` and `exclusivity_status` are all left exactly as they were, and only the letter's `version` moves. The `ISSUED → COUNTERED → ISSUED` cycle may repeat as many times as the parties can stand, with the deal sitting in `LOI_NEGOTIATION` throughout.

Execution is a countersignature, so it too belongs to the target organization. Executing an `ISSUED` letter, optionally with an `executed_note` (which must not be blank where it is supplied, and which is likewise recorded nowhere on the letter), requires that the letter's `exclusivity_status` still be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LETTER_STATE` — and that the logical clock still read strictly earlier than the letter's `expires_at`. On success the letter moves `ISSUED → EXECUTED`, records `executed_at` from the logical clock, and keeps `exclusivity_status` at `ACTIVE` for the remaining life of the deal. In the same request the deal advances `LOI_NEGOTIATION → DILIGENCE`, copies the letter's `combined_board_seats` into its own `combined_board_seats`, and seeds `reserved_seat_pool` to that same number — the moment from which the seat-ledger identity of §2.8 begins to hold. Both the letter's `version` and the deal's `version` rise by one. That deal-side half of the transition is conditional on the deal actually standing in `LOI_NEGOTIATION`: execution is not one of the operations guarded by ladder rule 16, so a second `ISSUED` letter of the same round may still be executed after the deal has moved past `LOI_NEGOTIATION`, and in that case the letter alone changes — the deal's status, its seat figures and its `version` are all left untouched.

Withdrawal belongs to the initiator, works from either `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED`, and requires a `withdrawal_reason`. The letter moves to `WITHDRAWN` and its `exclusivity_status` is set to `RELEASED`, freeing the parties from the window and starting the cooling-off clock of the previous paragraph but one. Withdrawal touches only the letter row and its `version`.

Two further letter transitions are driven by time rather than by a party, and both are applied by the sweep of §3.9. A letter still in `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` whose `expires_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves to `LAPSED` with `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED`. Separately, and independently, a letter whose `exclusivity_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `exclusivity_end_date` now falls strictly before the calendar date of the logical clock has its `exclusivity_status` set to `EXPIRED` with `status` left completely untouched — this is the dimension-B-only transition that proves the two dimensions are genuinely separate.

Finally, an executed letter can be taken away from the parties by events elsewhere in the deal. An `EXECUTED` letter moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED` when a critical diligence finding is escalated (§3.4) or when the deal is abandoned (§3.9), and that supersession in turn drives the certified board approvals of the same deal into supersession as well.

### 3.4. Due Diligence: Findings, Clearance and the Escalation Cascade

With a letter executed, the deal sits in `DILIGENCE` and the reviewers go to work. A `DiligenceLead` raises a finding against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, choosing a `workstream` from `FINANCE`, `CONTRACTS`, `RESTRICTED_FUNDS`, `LITIGATION` and `WORKFORCE`, a `severity` from `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` and `CRITICAL`, a one-line `summary` of between 1 and 400 characters, and an `exposure_cents` figure of at least 0 quantifying the financial exposure the finding represents (0 where the finding carries no measurable exposure). The new finding lands with `status` `OPEN`, with `response_ref`, `escalation_reason`, `waiver_justification` and `cleared_by_user_id` all null, and with `raised_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the comparand for every later segregation-of-duties check on this row.

Findings are not confined to the diligence phase. Raising one is permitted while the deal is `DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL` or `REGULATORY_REVIEW`; any other deal status answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. Post-signing findings are a real occurrence in this domain and are the entry point of the deepest cascade in the system. Raising a finding while the deal is `DILIGENCE_CLOSED` cycles the deal straight back `DILIGENCE_CLOSED → DILIGENCE` and bumps its `version`; raised from any of the three later statuses, the deal's status is left alone until and unless the finding is escalated. A newly raised finding also changes what every *later* diligence-adjusted snapshot computes, because its `exposure_cents` now counts toward open exposure. A deal's findings may be listed filtered by `workstream`, by `severity` and by `status`.

Responding is the counterparty's answer and may come from either side of the deal — a `StrategyOfficer` of either party or a `DiligenceLead`. A response supplies a `response_ref` pointing at the document produced, and may optionally carry a revised `exposure_cents`, which overwrites the stored figure when present. Responding moves the finding `OPEN → RESPONDED`, and it also works from `ESCALATED`, which makes `RESPONDED → ESCALATED → RESPONDED` a genuine cycle. Responding deliberately does **not** mutate the deal row at all: neither the deal's `status` nor its `version` changes, because an answer on its own never closes diligence. Only the finding's own `version` rises.

Clearance is the independence control. A `DiligenceLead` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the finding's `raised_by_user_id` clears a `RESPONDED` finding with a `clearance_note` — required and not blank, and, like a counter note, recorded nowhere on the finding; the same lead who raised it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Clearance moves the finding `RESPONDED → CLEARED`, records `cleared_by_user_id` as the acting user, and removes this row's `exposure_cents` from what later diligence-adjusted snapshots treat as open exposure. It then re-evaluates the deal's closure condition.

Waiving is the risk-acceptance route to the same terminal shelf, and carries the same independence control. A `DiligenceLead` other than the one who raised the finding waives it from `OPEN`, `RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED` by supplying a `waiver_justification` recording the board-level acceptance of unresolved risk; the raiser attempting it is again refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the lead who raised a finding may not be the one who accepts it unresolved. The finding moves to `WAIVED`, its exposure likewise drops out of later open-exposure sums, and the deal's closure condition is re-evaluated.

**Closing diligence** is an aggregate condition rather than an explicit action. Whenever any finding of a deal in `DILIGENCE` is mutated, the deal is re-examined: if it has at least one finding and *every* one of them is either `CLEARED` or `WAIVED`, the deal advances `DILIGENCE → DILIGENCE_CLOSED` and its `version` rises by one. A deal with no findings at all never satisfies that condition, so diligence cannot be closed by never looking.

**Escalation is the depth-five cascade origin**, and it is the one place where a single request rewrites five different kinds of row. A `DiligenceLead` escalates a finding from `OPEN` or `RESPONDED` with an `escalation_reason`, and escalation is only available while the deal is `DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL` or `REGULATORY_REVIEW`. Hop one always happens: the finding moves to `ESCALATED` and stores the reason, and its `version` rises by one. Whether the remaining four hops happen depends entirely on the finding's `severity`. If `severity` is anything other than `CRITICAL` the cascade stops right there and no other row in the system is touched — the deal is left exactly as it was. If `severity` is `CRITICAL`, hop two reverts the deal to `LOI_NEGOTIATION` from whichever of those five statuses it held, increases `round_no` by one, and bumps the deal's `version` by one. Hop three follows from that revert: every letter of intent of the deal standing in `EXECUTED` — ordinarily exactly one — becomes `SUPERSEDED` and has its `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED`. Hop four follows from the letter's supersession: every board approval of the deal in `CERTIFIED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Hop five follows from those supersessions: each superseded approval returns its `allocated_seats` to the deal's `reserved_seat_pool`, sets its own `allocated_seats` to 0, and resets `governance_seats` to 0 on the corresponding party organization. The deal's `structure_type` is deliberately *retained* across the revert, which is what forces the next round's letter to propose the same structure.

Two ordering notes matter on the finding actions. First, all four actions share the same status guard, and a row already sitting in the terminal `CLEARED` or `WAIVED` is refused by every one of them with `ERR_INVALID_DILIGENCE_STATE`. Second, on clear and on waive the status guard is evaluated *after* the segregation-of-duties check, per the ladder in §2.7: a raiser attempting to clear a finding that is not `RESPONDED` sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, not the state error, because the independence rule speaks first.

### 3.5. Committing to a Structure and the Board Approval Ledger

Once diligence has closed, the initiator's `StrategyOfficer` commits the transaction to one of the four legal structures by supplying a `structure_type` and the `unrestricted_transfer_value_cents` the transaction will move — the unrestricted charitable asset value, in integer cents, that will later be recited to the attorney general. Commitment is available only while the deal is `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, and only to the initiator; the target attempting it answers `ERR_NOT_DEAL_PARTY`.

Three substantive gates guard the commitment, and they speak in this order. First, the chosen `structure_type` must equal the `proposed_structure_type` of the deal's `EXECUTED` letter of intent with the highest `round_no`; a mismatch answers `ERR_STRUCTURE_TYPE_MISMATCH`, which is what makes a critical escalation genuinely expensive — the deal's `structure_type` survives the revert, so the replacement letter has to propose the same structure. Second, the deal's latest `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` alignment assessment (the one with the highest `sequence_no` for that variant) must exist and must be rated `STRONG` or `MODERATE`; a `WEAK` or `NOT_VIABLE` rating, or no diligence-adjusted snapshot at all, answers `ERR_ALIGNMENT_RATING_INSUFFICIENT`, because such a rating cannot support a fiduciary finding that the transaction advances the mission. Third, that same snapshot's `reserve_months` must be at least `3.00` months of combined operating reserve; anything strictly below that floor answers `ERR_RESERVE_BELOW_FLOOR`. On success the deal records both request fields and advances `DILIGENCE_CLOSED → STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, with its `version` rising by one.

Each organization's board then votes separately. A `BoardSecretary` records one board's resolution against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, pinned with `expected_restructuring_version` per §2.5, naming which side of the table it speaks for through `approving_party` — `INITIATOR`, which resolves to the deal's `initiator_org_id`, or `TARGET`, which resolves to its `target_org_id`. The acting secretary's `X-User-ID` must equal the organization that `approving_party` resolves to, and the other party's secretary is refused with `ERR_NOT_DEAL_PARTY`. Recording is permitted only while the deal is `STRUCTURE_SELECTED` or `BOARD_APPROVAL`.

The tally itself is supplied as `seats_total` (the total seated directors on that board, between 1 and 51 inclusive), `recusals` (directors stepping aside for conflict of interest, at least 0 and at most `seats_total`, defaulting to 0 when omitted), `votes_for` and `votes_against` (each at least 0), `abstentions` (directors present but abstaining, at least 0, defaulting to 0), `conflict_disclosures_count` (written conflict-of-interest disclosures filed with the fiduciary record, at least 0, defaulting to 0), and `recording_secretary_ref` (the staff reference of the secretary who took the minutes, between 1 and 64 characters and not blank).

From those inputs the server derives the whole outcome and accepts none of it from the client. `eligible_seats` is `seats_total` less `recusals`. `quorum_required` is `eligible_seats` divided by two and rounded **up** to the next whole seat. Attendance is `votes_for` plus `votes_against` plus `abstentions`. `votes_cast` is `votes_for` plus `votes_against` — abstentions are present for quorum but are excluded from the denominator of the vote. Four fiduciary checks then run, and by the ladder of §2.7 they speak in exactly this sequence: `conflict_disclosures_count` must be at least `recusals`, since every recused seat must be backed by a disclosed conflict in the fiduciary record, else `ERR_CONFLICT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`; attendance must not exceed `eligible_seats`, else `ERR_VOTE_TALLY_EXCEEDS_SEATS`; attendance must be at least `quorum_required`, else `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; and `votes_cast` must be greater than zero, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTES`, because an all-abstain board records no decision and leaves the approval ratio with no denominator.

The supermajority threshold is keyed on the deal's committed structure, which is why a board vote cannot be recorded before a structure is selected. `required_ratio` is `0.6667` for a `MERGER` and for an `ASSET_ACQUISITION`, `0.6000` for a `PARENT_SUBSIDIARY`, and `0.5000` for a `MANAGEMENT_SERVICES_AGREEMENT`. `approval_ratio` is `votes_for` divided by `votes_cast`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is `0.0000` when `votes_cast` is zero. `decision` is `APPROVED` when `votes_cast` is greater than zero and `approval_ratio` is greater than or equal to `required_ratio`, and `REJECTED` otherwise — the comparison is inclusive, so a merger carried eight votes to four yields `0.6667` and passes. The resolution's initial `status` is conditional on that outcome: `RECORDED` when `decision` is `APPROVED`, and directly the terminal `REJECTED` when `decision` is `REJECTED`. A fresh resolution starts with `fiduciary_memo_ref`, `certifying_secretary_ref`, `pinned_composite_score`, `pinned_assessment_sequence_no` and `rescission_reason` all null, and `allocated_seats` at 0. Recording the deal's first resolution advances it `STRUCTURE_SELECTED → BOARD_APPROVAL`, and it does so whether that resolution's `decision` came out `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` — the transition is keyed on the deal standing in `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, never on the outcome of the vote, so a board that votes the transaction down still moves the deal into `BOARD_APPROVAL`; recording a *later* resolution for the same `approving_party` supersedes that party's prior resolution in `RECORDED` or `CERTIFIED`, returning its `allocated_seats` to `reserved_seat_pool`, zeroing its own `allocated_seats` and resetting that organization's `governance_seats` to 0. Either way the deal's `version` rises by exactly one for the request. A deal's resolutions may be listed filtered by `approving_party` and by `status`; the full status set is `RECORDED`, `CERTIFIED`, `REJECTED`, `RESCINDED` and `SUPERSEDED`.

**Certification is the two-signature control.** A `BoardSecretary` of the same party certifies a `RECORDED` resolution by supplying a `fiduciary_memo_ref` — the reference to the fiduciary-duty memorandum being filed, required, of at least one character with no upper length bound, and required to be non-blank — and a `certifying_secretary_ref` naming the secretary attesting to the minutes, between 1 and 64 characters and not blank like the recording reference it is compared against. Unlike every other blankness check in this system, the memo reference's is not enforced at rule 3: an empty string fails the one-character minimum and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas a value that is non-empty but consists only of whitespace is admitted by rule 3 and refused instead at ladder position 36 with `ERR_CONFLICT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` — and only after the segregation-of-duties check, the approval-status check and the disclosure-count re-check have each passed, so a whitespace-only memo sent by the recording secretary answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and one sent against an already-`CERTIFIED` resolution answers `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`. The `certifying_secretary_ref` must differ from the resolution's stored `recording_secretary_ref`, and an equal pair is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because a secretary's `X-User-ID` is the party organization's own identifier, it cannot by itself distinguish two staff members of that organization, which is precisely why the control runs on the two staff references instead. Certification re-checks that `conflict_disclosures_count` is at least `recusals`, answering `ERR_CONFLICT_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` if it is not, and it refuses a resolution that is not in `RECORDED` with `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`.

Certification does four things at once. It moves the resolution `RECORDED → CERTIFIED` and stores both supplied references. It pins the evidentiary basis of the fiduciary finding by copying `composite_score` and `sequence_no` from the deal's latest `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` alignment assessment into `pinned_composite_score` and `pinned_assessment_sequence_no`; a diligence-adjusted snapshot taken *after* certification therefore makes the certified resolution demonstrably stale, which is exactly the audit signal the pin exists to produce. It draws the party's seats from the reserved pool, described in the next paragraph. And it re-evaluates the deal's own progression: once **both** `INITIATOR` and `TARGET` hold a `CERTIFIED` resolution, the deal advances `BOARD_APPROVAL → REGULATORY_REVIEW`. Where the deal's `ag_review_required` is false, the deal does not rest there — it continues `REGULATORY_REVIEW → INTEGRATION` inside the same request, so a deal with no attorney-general exposure is never observable sitting in `REGULATORY_REVIEW`. The resolution's `version`, the deal's `version` and the certifying organization's `version` each rise by one.

**Seat allocation** draws on the letter of intent rather than on the resolution. The governing letter is the one of this deal whose `status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` with the highest `round_no`; where two such letters tie on `round_no`, the one with the later `created_at` governs, and a further tie on `created_at` is broken by insertion sequence, the row the server created last winning. If `approving_party` is `INITIATOR`, the seats due are that letter's `board_seat_split_initiator`; if it is `TARGET`, the seats due are the deal's `combined_board_seats` less that same `board_seat_split_initiator`. The seats due are written to the resolution's `allocated_seats`, subtracted from the deal's `reserved_seat_pool`, and written to `governance_seats` on the approving party's organization. Allocation is in whole seats with no rounding and no fractional split. The seat-ledger identity of §2.8 holds after every certification, every rescission and every supersession.

**Rescission is the compensating action for certification.** A `BoardSecretary` of the same party rescinds a `CERTIFIED` resolution with a `rescission_reason`; a resolution in any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`, and rescission is unavailable once the deal has reached `CONSUMMATED` or `ABANDONED`, which answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. The resolution moves `CERTIFIED → RESCINDED`, returns its `allocated_seats` to `reserved_seat_pool`, zeroes its own `allocated_seats`, and resets `governance_seats` to 0 on that party. Because the both-parties-certified condition no longer holds, a deal sitting in `REGULATORY_REVIEW` reverts `REGULATORY_REVIEW → BOARD_APPROVAL`. That revert is conditional on the deal actually standing in `REGULATORY_REVIEW` and nothing more: a deal that skipped straight to `INTEGRATION` because its `ag_review_required` is false, or that has since been released there by a regulator's approval, keeps the status it holds while the seats come back. The resolution's `version`, the deal's `version` and the organization's `version` each rise by one, the deal's and the organization's whether or not the status revert fired and whether or not the seats moved.

### 3.6. Restricted Gifts: Donor Consent, Judicial Modification and Transfer

Restricted gifts are the reason nonprofit mergers stall. A donor who gave for one purpose in one place does not automatically consent to that money following the program into a merged entity, and where the restriction actively conflicts with the receiving organization's charitable purposes, no amount of donor goodwill can cure it — only a court can. This system models both routes and keeps them distinguishable forever after.

A `PlatformAdmin` or a `CharitableTrustCounsel` registers a restricted gift held by one organization, supplying that organization's identifier as `org_id`, a donor-facing `fund_name` between 1 and 160 characters, a `fund_type` of `TEMP_RESTRICTED`, `PERM_ENDOWMENT` or `CONDITIONAL_PLEDGE`, a `principal_cents` of at least 1, an `accumulated_appreciation_cents` of at least 0 representing net appreciation above principal (defaulting to 0), a `historic_dollar_value_cents` of at least 0 representing the floor protected by state endowment law, a donor-imposed `restriction_purpose_code` in the same two-letters-hyphen-uppercase-word shape as a mission code, a `restriction_conflict` flag (defaulting to false) that is true when the donor restriction conflicts with the receiving organization's charitable purposes so that donor consent alone cannot cure it, and a `donor_of_record_reachable` flag (defaulting to true) that is true when a living donor of record can be solicited. The historic dollar value may not exceed `principal_cents` plus `accumulated_appreciation_cents`; a larger value is refused with `ERR_HDV_EXCEEDS_FUND_VALUE`, because the judicial spending-rate slice would otherwise be computed against value the fund does not hold. A newly registered fund starts with `transfer_status` `HELD`, with `transferable_amount_cents`, `transferred_amount_cents`, `retained_amount_cents` and `unwind_count` all at 0, with `consent_method`, `consent_decision`, `consent_signed_at`, `petition_basis`, `petition_filed_at`, `court_docket_no`, `court_decision`, `modified_purpose_code`, `transfer_basis`, `transferred_to_org_id` and `transfer_restructuring_id` all null, and it immediately recomputes the holder organization's `restricted_corpus_cents` per §3.1. Funds may be listed filtered by `org_id`, by `transferred_to_org_id`, by `transfer_restructuring_id`, by `transfer_status` and by `fund_type`. Each of those five is an exact match against the value the field holds at read time and carries no history of its own: because an unwind clears `transferred_to_org_id` and `transfer_restructuring_id` back to null, a fund whose transfer has been unwound stops matching a recipient or authorising-deal filter altogether, and a fund that has never moved matches neither.

`transfer_status` runs `HELD`, `CONSENT_SOUGHT`, `CONSENT_GRANTED`, `CONSENT_DENIED`, `PETITION_FILED`, `MODIFIED`, `TRANSFERRED` and `RETAINED`, of which `RETAINED` is terminal. The shape is a fork-and-join: the machine forks at the donor's answer, the two branches carry genuinely different side-effect sets, and they rejoin at `TRANSFERRED`.

The consent branch opens when a `CharitableTrustCounsel` solicits the donor, supplying the `consent_method` used — `WRITTEN` or `ELECTRONIC`. Solicitation requires the fund to be `HELD` and requires `donor_of_record_reachable` to be true; an unreachable donor of record is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FUND_STATE`, which is the signal to go to court instead. A conflicting restriction does **not** block the solicitation itself — the donor is entitled to be asked, and may consent or refuse — it blocks only the later consent-based transfer. On success the fund moves `HELD → CONSENT_SOUGHT`, and because a fund's disposition feeds every deal its holder is a party to, the deal-level `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and `restricted_asset_posture` are recomputed on every restructuring in which that organization appears as initiator or as target.

Recording the donor's answer is the fork. The counsel supplies a `decision` of `GRANTED` or `DENIED` against a fund in `CONSENT_SOUGHT`, and `consent_signed_at` is stamped from the logical clock regardless of which way the answer went; any client-supplied signing timestamp is ignored. On `GRANTED` the fund moves `CONSENT_SOUGHT → CONSENT_GRANTED`, `consent_decision` is set to `GRANTED`, and `transferable_amount_cents` is fixed at the full fund value — `principal_cents` plus `accumulated_appreciation_cents`, with `historic_dollar_value_cents` ignored entirely, because a consenting donor can release their own appreciation. On `DENIED` the fund moves `CONSENT_SOUGHT → CONSENT_DENIED`, `consent_decision` is set to `DENIED`, `transferable_amount_cents` stays at 0, and the deal-level posture is recomputed to `BLOCKED` on every deal in which the holder is a party — a denied fund does not merely leave the count unresolved, it actively blocks. Both branches recompute `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and the posture and raise the fund's `version` by one; the divergence between them is the point.

The judicial branch is entered from `CONSENT_DENIED`, and from `HELD` in exactly one circumstance. A `CharitableTrustCounsel` files a modification petition by supplying a `petition_basis` of `IMPRACTICABLE`, `WASTEFUL`, `UNLAWFUL` or `DONOR_UNREACHABLE`, together with the `court_docket_no` the court assigned. Filing straight from `HELD` is permitted only where `donor_of_record_reachable` is false *and* the pleaded `petition_basis` is `DONOR_UNREACHABLE`; a reachable donor must be asked before a court is, and a violation of either condition answers `ERR_DONOR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. On success the fund moves to `PETITION_FILED`, records the basis and docket number, and stamps `petition_filed_at` from the logical clock. The count and posture are recomputed, and a posture that had been `BLOCKED` clears back to `RESTRICTED_PENDING` once no fund of either party remains in `CONSENT_DENIED`.

Recording the court's answer is the join. The counsel supplies a `decision` of `GRANTED`, `GRANTED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `DENIED` against a fund in `PETITION_FILED`, optionally with a `modified_purpose_code`, in the same two-letters-hyphen-uppercase-word shape as a restriction purpose code, naming the purpose the court substituted. On `GRANTED` or `GRANTED_WITH_CONDITIONS` the fund moves `PETITION_FILED → MODIFIED`, stores the outcome in `court_decision` and the substituted purpose in `modified_purpose_code` where one was supplied — omitted, that field simply stays null and the modification stands on the original purpose — and fixes `transferable_amount_cents` by the judicial rule below. On `DENIED` the fund moves `PETITION_FILED → RETAINED`, which is terminal: `court_decision` is `DENIED`, `transferable_amount_cents` is 0, `retained_amount_cents` is set to the whole fund value since nothing is going anywhere, and a `modified_purpose_code` sent alongside a denial is discarded rather than stored. Either way the count and posture are recomputed and the fund's `version` rises; a `RETAINED` fund counts as resolved and no longer blocks.

**The two release rules differ, and the difference is deliberate.** Under a `DONOR_CONSENT` basis, `transferable_amount_cents` is the entire fund value: `principal_cents` plus `accumulated_appreciation_cents`. Under a `JUDICIAL_MODIFICATION` basis, the answer depends on `fund_type`. For a `PERM_ENDOWMENT` — the one class whose judicial release is capped — the releasable amount is the excess of the fund value over `historic_dollar_value_cents`, floored at 0 so a fund below its historic dollar value contributes nothing from this term, plus a capped slice equal to seven percent of `historic_dollar_value_cents`, that slice being truncated toward zero rather than rounded half up. For a `TEMP_RESTRICTED` or `CONDITIONAL_PLEDGE` fund the judicial rule releases the entire fund value, the same as consent would. The consequence for a permanent endowment with identical stored data is that the judicial route releases strictly less than the consent route. In both cases `retained_amount_cents` is the fund value less `transferable_amount_cents`, and every figure is exact integer cents. The value is frozen at the instant the branch resolves, so a later restatement of the fund's inputs never silently changes what an executed transfer moved.

**Executing the transfer is the depth-four cascade origin.** A `CharitableTrustCounsel` executes it against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, declaring which authority it relies on through `transfer_basis` — `DONOR_CONSENT` or `JUDICIAL_MODIFICATION`. The declared basis must match the branch the fund actually travelled: `DONOR_CONSENT` requires the fund to be in `CONSENT_GRANTED`, and `JUDICIAL_MODIFICATION` requires it to be in `MODIFIED`. A fund whose `restriction_conflict` is true may not move on a `DONOR_CONSENT` basis at all and answers `ERR_JUDICIAL_ORDER_REQUIRED`; a conflicting restriction can only be released by a judicial modification, so such a fund must travel the petition branch and arrive as `MODIFIED`. A fund still sitting in `HELD`, `CONSENT_SOUGHT`, `CONSENT_DENIED` or `PETITION_FILED` holds neither donor consent nor a judicial order and answers `ERR_DONOR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`, while a fund already `TRANSFERRED` or judicially `RETAINED` answers `ERR_INVALID_FUND_STATE` instead — the first names the missing authority, the second the exhausted row. The fund's `org_id` must be one of the deal's two party organizations and the counterparty must be the other, else `ERR_FUND_NOT_DEAL_PARTY_ASSET`; and the deal must be in `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL`, `REGULATORY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRATION`, else `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`.

The four hops run as follows. Hop one moves the fund to `TRANSFERRED` and writes `transfer_basis`, `transfer_restructuring_id` naming the authorising deal, `transferred_to_org_id` set to the counterparty organization, `transferred_amount_cents` equal to the previously fixed `transferable_amount_cents`, and `retained_amount_cents` equal to the fund value less what moved. Hop two follows because value has moved: `restricted_corpus_cents` is recomputed on **both** party organizations by the three-part sum of §3.1, and each mutated organization's `version` rises by one — their pair total is unchanged, which is the conservation property of §2.8. Hop three follows because the party corpus figures changed: the deal recomputes `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and `restricted_asset_posture`, and the posture flips `RESTRICTED_PENDING → CLEARED` at the moment the unresolved count reaches zero; the same recomputation then runs over every other restructuring in which either party organization appears. Hop four follows because the posture became `CLEARED`: every integration task of that deal whose `workstream` is `PROGRAM_CONTINUITY` and whose `status` is `HELD` moves `HELD → PLANNED` with `held_reason` cleared to null. The fund's `version` and the deal's `version` each rise by one.

**The deal-level posture** is derived, never supplied, and recomputed on every restricted-fund mutation that touches a party. The relevant set — the deal's *party funds* — is every fund whose `org_id` is one of the two party organizations **or** whose `transferred_to_org_id` is one of them. `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` is the number of those funds whose `transfer_status` is neither `TRANSFERRED` nor `RETAINED`. `restricted_asset_posture` is then decided by taking the first matching branch of four, in this order: if the party-fund set is empty the posture is `NOT_ASSESSED`; otherwise if any party fund is in `CONSENT_DENIED` the posture is `BLOCKED`; otherwise if the unresolved count is greater than zero the posture is `RESTRICTED_PENDING`; otherwise the posture is `CLEARED`. Order matters — a deal holding one denied fund and three unresolved ones is `BLOCKED`, not `RESTRICTED_PENDING`. The recomputation is registry-wide and status-blind: every restructuring in which either touched organization appears as initiator or as target is re-derived, deals already `CONSUMMATED` or `ABANDONED` included. It also rewrites those two derived fields in place without counting as a mutation of the deal row, so among the restricted-fund operations only an executed transfer and an unwind refresh a deal's `updated_at` and raise its `version`; registration, consent solicitation, consent recording, petition filing and court-decision recording leave both standing even where the posture they recompute genuinely changes.

**Unwinding is the compensating action for a transfer**, and it conserves the pair corpus exactly. A `CharitableTrustCounsel` unwinds a `TRANSFERRED` fund with an `unwind_reason` — required and not blank, and recorded nowhere on the fund — provided the deal named by `transfer_restructuring_id` has not reached `CONSUMMATED`; a consummated deal answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. The return state is conditional on the authority that was used: a fund transferred on `DONOR_CONSENT` returns `TRANSFERRED → CONSENT_GRANTED`, and one transferred on `JUDICIAL_MODIFICATION` returns `TRANSFERRED → MODIFIED`. The unwind clears `transferred_to_org_id`, `transfer_restructuring_id` and `transfer_basis` to null, sets `transferred_amount_cents` and `retained_amount_cents` both to 0, and increases `unwind_count` by one. It deliberately leaves `transferable_amount_cents` at its pre-transfer value, so a re-transfer releases the identical amount rather than recomputing it against possibly restated inputs. Both parties' `restricted_corpus_cents` are recomputed and land back on their exact pre-transfer values; the deal's `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and `restricted_asset_posture` are recomputed, reverting `CLEARED → RESTRICTED_PENDING`, and so are those of every other restructuring in which either party appears; and every integration task of that deal whose `workstream` is `PROGRAM_CONTINUITY` and whose `status` is `PLANNED` returns `PLANNED → HELD` with `held_reason` set to `RESTRICTED_ASSETS_UNRESOLVED`. The fund's `version` and the deal's `version` each rise by one.

### 3.7. State Attorney General Notice and Approval

Charitable assets cannot simply be handed from one board to another. In five states — those whose two-letter `state_code` is `CA`, `NY`, `MA`, `IL` or `WA` — the attorney general's charities bureau must be told, and above a threshold must affirmatively approve. Whether a given deal owes that duty at all was decided when the deal was opened: `ag_review_required` is true when either party is incorporated in one of those five states. Attempting a filing on a deal whose `ag_review_required` is false is refused with `ERR_AG_REVIEW_NOT_REQUIRED`. That refusal, rather than a deal-status refusal, is the one that must speak, which is why filing is admitted while the deal is either `REGULATORY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRATION`: a deal with no attorney-general exposure never rests in `REGULATORY_REVIEW`, so refusing it on deal status would make the substantive rule unreachable. A late filing on a deal that genuinely does owe review is admitted for the same reason, and an objection recorded on it will block closing. Any other deal status answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. Several filings may coexist for one deal — a separate notice per party state, for instance — and a deal's filings may be listed filtered by `status` and by `filing_type`.

A `CharitableTrustCounsel` submits a filing against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, naming the `state_code` whose attorney general receives it, the `filing_type` — `NOTICE` for below-threshold transfers or `APPROVAL_PETITION` where prior approval is required — and the `legal_costs_cents`, being the transaction legal costs excluding the attorney-general filing fee itself, which must be at least 0. Everything else is computed server-side. The `state_code` on the filing must be the state of incorporation of one of the two party organizations **and** must be one of the five review states; a state that fails either test answers `ERR_AG_STATE_NOT_APPLICABLE`. A `state_code` that is not exactly two uppercase letters never reaches that test at all: it violates the field's declared shape and is refused at rule 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. In the amounts that follow, dollar figures are given for readability and every stored figure is integer cents at one hundred cents to the dollar.

The recited asset value comes first. `transferred_charitable_asset_value_cents` is the deal's `unrestricted_transfer_value_cents` — the figure fixed at structure selection — plus the sum of `transferred_amount_cents` over every restricted fund whose `transfer_restructuring_id` is this deal and whose `transfer_status` is `TRANSFERRED`. Funds in any other transfer status contribute nothing. A filing must recite *some* transferred charitable asset value: where that derived total comes out at zero, the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_CHARITABLE_ASSETS_TRANSFERRED` — but not before the filing-type check described two paragraphs below, which sits earlier in the ladder and therefore speaks first where both are violated, so a zero-value filing whose `filing_type` is also wrong answers `ERR_AG_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

The filing fee is tiered, and the tier depends on a total that the fee itself is part of. That circularity is broken by pinning the evaluation order. Step one computes `provisional_consideration_cents`, the fee-**exclusive** provisional total: `transferred_charitable_asset_value_cents` plus `legal_costs_cents`. Step two selects the tier by reading *only* that provisional total: below one million dollars the `ag_filing_fee_cents` is two hundred fifty dollars; at one million dollars or above but below ten million dollars it is one thousand five hundred dollars; at ten million dollars or above it is five thousand dollars. Both tier comparisons are strictly less-than against their ceilings, so a provisional total landing exactly on a ceiling falls into the *higher* tier. Step three folds the fee in: `closing_costs_cents` is `legal_costs_cents` plus `ag_filing_fee_cents`, and `total_consideration_cents` is `transferred_charitable_asset_value_cents` plus `closing_costs_cents`.

The filing type is validated, not chosen. Two conditions each independently force the heavier route: a recited asset value of five million dollars or more, or the presence of at least one contributing transferred fund whose `fund_type` is `PERM_ENDOWMENT`. If either holds, the required type is `APPROVAL_PETITION`; otherwise it is `NOTICE`. A `filing_type` that does not equal the required type — a notice where a petition is owed, or a petition where only a notice is owed — is refused with `ERR_AG_FILING_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

The statutory review period follows from the type and from two surcharges. `judicial_basis_present` is true when at least one contributing transferred fund carries `transfer_basis` `JUDICIAL_MODIFICATION`, and false otherwise. `statutory_waiting_days` starts at forty-five calendar days for an `APPROVAL_PETITION` and at twenty calendar days for a `NOTICE`. If the deal's `structure_type` is `ASSET_ACQUISITION` **and** the recited asset value is five million dollars or more, fifteen further days are added. If `judicial_basis_present` is true, ten further days are added. Both surcharges can apply to the same filing, in which case both are added. Filings are created already in `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, `decision_recorded_at` and `objection_reason` null, and `review_deadline_at` set to `submitted_at` advanced by `statutory_waiting_days` calendar days — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, like every other instant in the system. Submitting a filing writes the filing row and nothing else: the deal's status, `updated_at` and `version` are all left where they were, which is why several filings can accumulate against one deal without ever disturbing the version pin of §2.5.

Only the `StateCharitiesRegulator` may answer, never the counsel who filed: the bureau records its own `decision` of `APPROVED` or `OBJECTED` against a filing still in `SUBMITTED`, and a filing in any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_FILING_STATE`. An `OBJECTED` decision must carry a non-blank `objection_reason`; omitting it answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either way `decision_recorded_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the filing's `version` rises by one. An approval moves the filing `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and releases the deal `REGULATORY_REVIEW → INTEGRATION`, raising the deal's `version` by one as well — but that deal-side half is conditional on the deal actually standing in `REGULATORY_REVIEW`, so an approval recorded against the late filing this section admits, on a deal already sitting in `INTEGRATION`, changes the filing alone and leaves the deal's status, `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they were. An `objection_reason` sent alongside an `APPROVED` decision is discarded rather than stored, just as a `modified_purpose_code` sent alongside a court denial is. An objection moves the filing `SUBMITTED → OBJECTED`, records the reason, and deliberately leaves the deal sitting in `REGULATORY_REVIEW` so that a fresh filing is required; the deal row is not touched.

A filing can also be approved by silence. Where the logical clock has reached or passed a `SUBMITTED` filing's `review_deadline_at` — the comparison is inclusive at the exact instant — the sweep of §3.9 moves it `SUBMITTED → DEEMED_APPROVED`, stamps `decision_recorded_at`, and releases the deal `REGULATORY_REVIEW → INTEGRATION` exactly as an express approval would.

### 3.8. Integration Planning, Program Continuity and Closing

Integration planning does not wait for the regulator. As soon as the structure is fixed, an `IntegrationManager` may plan work: task creation is permitted while the deal is `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL`, `REGULATORY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRATION`, and any other deal status answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. A task is planned against a deal named by `restructuring_id`, in a `workstream` of `STAFFING`, `SYSTEMS`, `BRANDING` or `PROGRAM_CONTINUITY`, with a `title` between 1 and 160 characters and not blank, a `crew_code` matching the literal prefix `CREW-` followed by between two and eight uppercase letters or digits, and a window given as `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. It may optionally name a predecessor through `depends_on_task_id`, which defaults to null.

The crew window is **half-open**: `planned_start_date` is inside the window and `planned_end_date` is outside it, and the end date must fall strictly after the start date; an end date equal to or earlier than the start date is a malformed request answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at ladder position 3, before the deal named by `restructuring_id` or any supplied predecessor has been resolved at all. An integration crew can occupy exactly one window at a time, so a requested window that overlaps the window of any other task sharing the same `crew_code` whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the interval is half-open, adjacency is not overlap — one task ending on the very day the next begins is allowed. A supplied `depends_on_task_id` must belong to the same deal as the task being created and must not be the task itself, else `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_IN_SAME_DEAL`; a predecessor identifier that resolves to nothing at all answers `ERR_INTEGRATION_TASK_NOT_FOUND` earlier in the ladder.

A new task's initial `status` is conditional on the restricted-asset picture. Where the `workstream` is `PROGRAM_CONTINUITY` **and** the deal's `restricted_asset_posture` is `RESTRICTED_PENDING` or `BLOCKED`, the task is created directly in `HELD` with `held_reason` set to `RESTRICTED_ASSETS_UNRESOLVED` — moving programs around before their restricted funding has been resolved is exactly the thing donors sue over. Every other combination of workstream and posture creates the task in `PLANNED` with `held_reason` null. `started_at` and `completed_at` both start null. Planning a task writes the task row and nothing else: the parent deal's `updated_at` and `version` do not move, whichever initial status the task lands in. As §3.6 describes, a held program-continuity task is released `HELD → PLANNED` when a transfer drives the posture to `CLEARED`, and a planned one is pulled back `PLANNED → HELD` when an unwind reverts the posture to `RESTRICTED_PENDING`. Tasks may be listed filtered by `restructuring_id`, by `depends_on_task_id`, by `crew_code`, by `workstream` and by `status`.

Starting a task, optionally with a `started_note`, is guarded by four conditions that speak in ladder order. First the deal must have reached `INTEGRATION`, else `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` — planning may run early, but execution may not. Then the task itself must be `PLANNED` — a `HELD`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED` task answers `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE` — and because the deal rule sits earlier in the ladder, a task in the wrong status on a deal that has not yet reached `INTEGRATION` answers the deal error and not the task one. Then, where the deal's `ag_review_required` is true, some filing of that deal must stand in `APPROVED` or `DEEMED_APPROVED`, else `ERR_AG_APPROVAL_MISSING`; a filing standing in `OBJECTED` elsewhere on the deal does not by itself block a start, which is the one respect in which this gate is looser than the consummation gate below. And where `depends_on_task_id` is set, that predecessor task must already be `COMPLETE`, else `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_COMPLETE`. On success the task moves `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at` from the logical clock. Completing an `IN_PROGRESS` task, optionally with a `completion_note`, moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE` and stamps `completed_at`; completion carries no deal-status gate of its own and never consults the deal at all. Each of those raises the task's `version` by one and touches nothing else. Both notes must be non-blank where they are supplied, and, like a counter note, neither is recorded on the task or appears anywhere in the response.

Program continuity is decided by the same `IntegrationManager`, one program at a time, against a deal named by `restructuring_id` with a `disposition` of `TRANSFER` or `SUNSET`. The program must currently be `ACTIVE` and its `org_id` must be one of the deal's two party organizations — either failure answers `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_DEAL_PARTY_ASSET`, so a program already sitting in `PLANNED_TRANSFER`, `PLANNED_SUNSET`, `TRANSFERRED` or `SUNSET` is refused with that code rather than with a state error — and the deal must be in `DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL`, `REGULATORY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRATION`. A `TRANSFER` disposition moves the program `ACTIVE → PLANNED_TRANSFER`; a `SUNSET` disposition moves it `ACTIVE → PLANNED_SUNSET`. The two dispositions differ in one consequential respect that reaches back into §3.2: a `PLANNED_TRANSFER` program remains admitted by both assessment variants, whereas a `PLANNED_SUNSET` program is dropped from the `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` inclusion set of every *later* assessment while remaining in the `SCREENING` set. Sunsetting a program therefore changes the overlap arithmetic of subsequent diligence-adjusted snapshots but never rewrites one already taken. Either decision raises the program's `version` by one.

**Consummation is the close.** The initiator's `StrategyOfficer` closes the transaction by supplying a `closing_memo_ref` — required and not blank, but, like a restatement reason, recorded nowhere on the deal and absent from the response — and the deal must be in `INTEGRATION`. Three substantive gates apply, and they speak in this order. Where `ag_review_required` is true, the deal must hold a filing in `APPROVED` or `DEEMED_APPROVED` and must hold **no** filing in `OBJECTED`, else `ERR_AG_APPROVAL_MISSING`. The deal's `restricted_asset_posture` must be `NOT_ASSESSED` — meaning the transaction touches no restricted funds at all — or `CLEARED`; no party fund may remain in `HELD`, `CONSENT_SOUGHT`, `CONSENT_DENIED`, `CONSENT_GRANTED`, `PETITION_FILED` or `MODIFIED`, and a violation answers `ERR_RESTRICTED_ASSETS_UNRESOLVED`. And every integration task of the deal must be `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED`; a task still `PLANNED`, `HELD` or `IN_PROGRESS` blocks the close with `ERR_INTEGRATION_INCOMPLETE`.

Closing rewrites the shape of both organizations. The deal moves `INTEGRATION → CONSUMMATED`, and `affiliation_role` is written on both parties according to the committed `structure_type`: a `MERGER` makes the initiator `SURVIVOR` and the target `DISSOLVED`; an `ASSET_ACQUISITION` makes the initiator `SURVIVOR` and the target `DIVESTED`; a `PARENT_SUBSIDIARY` makes the initiator `PARENT` and the target `SUBSIDIARY`, and additionally sets the target's `parent_org_id` to the initiator organization; a `MANAGEMENT_SERVICES_AGREEMENT` makes the initiator `MSA_PROVIDER` and the target `MSA_CLIENT`. Every program of either party sitting in `PLANNED_TRANSFER` moves `PLANNED_TRANSFER → TRANSFERRED` with `transferred_to_org_id` set to whichever party now holds the surviving, parent or provider role — that is, the party whose `affiliation_role` is `SURVIVOR`, `PARENT` or `MSA_PROVIDER`. Every program of either party sitting in `PLANNED_SUNSET` moves `PLANNED_SUNSET → SUNSET`. The deal's `version` rises by one, and so does the `version` of each mutated organization and each mutated program.

### 3.9. Time-Driven Sweeps, Abandonment and Reconciliation

Nothing in this system happens because a clock ticked on its own. Time-driven transitions are applied only when a `PlatformAdmin` runs the **regulatory sweep**, an operator checkpoint that evaluates every deadline against the logical clock's current value and applies whatever has come due. The sweep does three things in one pass. It deems unanswered attorney-general filings approved: every filing in `SUBMITTED` whose `review_deadline_at` the clock has reached or passed becomes `DEEMED_APPROVED` and releases its deal `REGULATORY_REVIEW → INTEGRATION`. It lapses stale letters: every letter in `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed becomes `LAPSED` with `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED`. And it expires elapsed confidentiality windows: every letter whose `exclusivity_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `exclusivity_end_date` now falls strictly before the calendar date of the clock has `exclusivity_status` set to `EXPIRED` with its negotiation `status` left completely untouched. The three passes run in that order over the whole of each collection, and on the letters the order is observable: a letter the lapse pass has just moved to `LAPSED` is already `RELEASED`, so the expiry pass no longer sees it. A letter still in `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` when its window elapses is therefore reported as lapsed and never as expired, the two letter lists are always disjoint within one sweep, and `EXPIRED` is in practice reached only by a letter already `EXECUTED`, whose confidentiality clock keeps running after the countersignature.

The sweep's response reports `swept_at` — the logical clock value it ran at — together with three lists of identifiers: `deemed_approved_filing_ids`, `lapsed_letter_of_intent_ids` and `expired_exclusivity_letter_of_intent_ids`. Each list carries the rows it affected in ascending insertion sequence — the order in which the server created them. The sweep is idempotent with respect to the clock: running it a second time without moving the clock returns three empty lists and mutates nothing.

**Abandonment** is available to either party's `StrategyOfficer` at any point before the deal reaches a terminal state — from `EXPLORATORY`, `RATIONALE_BUILT`, `LOI_NEGOTIATION`, `DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_CLOSED`, `STRUCTURE_SELECTED`, `BOARD_APPROVAL`, `REGULATORY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRATION` alike — and a deal already `CONSUMMATED` or `ABANDONED` answers `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`. The acting officer supplies an `abandonment_reason`, which is stored on the deal alongside the new `ABANDONED` status. Abandonment then dismantles the deal's live records across four child collections. Every letter of intent of the deal in `ISSUED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `exclusivity_status` set to `RELEASED`, freeing both parties from the exclusivity window. Every board approval of the deal in `RECORDED` or `CERTIFIED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, returning its `allocated_seats` to `reserved_seat_pool`, zeroing its own `allocated_seats` and resetting `governance_seats` to 0 on that party's organization. Every integration task of the deal in `PLANNED`, `HELD` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `CANCELLED`. And every restricted fund whose `transfer_restructuring_id` is this deal and whose `transfer_status` is `TRANSFERRED` is unwound exactly as a deliberate unwind would unwind it — conditional return state, restored pair corpus, `unwind_count` increased, `transferable_amount_cents` left intact, and the deal's own `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and `restricted_asset_posture` recomputed, so the abandoned deal's posture reverts away from `CLEARED`. Because the task fan-out above has already cancelled every live task, the program-continuity hold-back that a deliberate unwind performs finds nothing to pull back. The deal's `version` rises by one.

Finally, because the two conserved quantities of §2.8 are the fastest way to tell a correct implementation from a nearly-correct one, each deal exposes a **reconciliation dossier** as a single computed read. It reports `restructuring_id`, the deal's own `deal_status`, `structure_type` and `round_no`; the two scoring figures side by side as `screening_composite_score` and `diligence_adjusted_composite_score`, each taken from the latest snapshot of that variant — the one holding the highest `sequence_no` for it — and reported as `0.0` where the deal holds no snapshot of that variant at all, together with their `composite_delta`, which is the diligence-adjusted figure *less* the screening figure and therefore runs negative where diligence lowered the score; the financial position as `reserve_months` and `combined_monthly_expense_cents`, both copied from that same latest `DILIGENCE_ADJUSTED` snapshot where one exists and otherwise from the latest `SCREENING` snapshot, and reported as `0.0` and `0` respectively where the deal has taken no snapshot of either variant; the restricted-asset picture as `restricted_asset_posture`, `unresolved_restricted_fund_count` and `pair_restricted_corpus_cents` (the sum of `restricted_corpus_cents` across the two party organizations); and the seat ledger as `reserved_seat_pool`, `certified_allocated_seats_total` (the sum of `allocated_seats` over the deal's `CERTIFIED` resolutions), `seat_ledger_total` (those two added together) and `combined_board_seats`. Reading the dossier mutates nothing.
