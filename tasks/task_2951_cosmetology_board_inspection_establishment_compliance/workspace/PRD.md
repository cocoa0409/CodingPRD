# Cosmetology Board Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state cosmetology board carries an unusual regulatory burden: it licenses both places and people, and the two licences are only meaningful when they are physically joined at a chair. A salon holds a facility permit that caps how many workstations it may operate and obliges it to meet sanitation, disinfection and equipment standards. A cosmetologist, barber, nail technician, esthetician or instructor holds an individual licence that must be visibly posted at the station where they actually work. Neither licence, on its own, tells an inspector whether the service being delivered in front of them is lawful. The Cosmetology Board Compliance API exists to make that joint answerable — continuously, and from records rather than recollection.

The system this document specifies is mounted under `/cosmetology` and covers the full regulatory arc the board actually walks. Candidates accumulate clock hours at accredited schools or supervised apprenticeship hours under a sponsoring instructor, become exam-eligible, and are issued licences that expire on a fixed term. Establishments register, build out workstations, post practitioner licences and open booth-rental or employment agreements with the practitioners who occupy those stations. Field inspectors schedule routine and complaint-driven visits, record violation findings against a published rule schedule, and — where they observe an imminent hazard to the public — exercise immediate closure authority on the spot, without waiting for anyone's review. Board staff finalize each inspection, which converts findings into demerit points, drives the establishment's licensing status and posture, and opens the door to a citation with a statutory fine. Cited parties have hearing rights, and adjudicators may affirm, amend or dismiss. Separately, suspected infection clusters must be reported to the public-health authority inside a statutory window, investigated, and — when substantiated — converted into sanction points against the practitioner implicated.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule below. First, **every conserved quantity is a ledger, not a counter**: fines in cents, establishment demerit points and practitioner sanction points all exist only as the signed sum of immutable ledger rows, so a reversal is a compensating entry rather than an edit, and the audit trail is the balance. Second, **enforcement is derived, not asserted**: an establishment's gating posture, a workstation's posting compliance, a candidate's exam eligibility and a citation's outstanding balance are all recomputed from primary records on every write that could move them, so an operator can never leave the system in a state where the summary disagrees with the evidence. The remainder of this document specifies the contract precisely (Section 2), the regulatory workflows in narrative form (Section 3), and the endpoint and error surfaces (Sections 4 and 5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: who may act, what responses look like, how lists page, how derived quantities are computed and rounded, and in what order rules are evaluated when several of them are unhappy at once.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The board recognises exactly seven roles: `BoardAdmin`, `Inspector`, `HearingOfficer`, `EstablishmentOwner`, `Practitioner`, `SchoolRegistrar` and `HealthAuthorityLiaison`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those seven strings is treated as an unauthenticated caller rather than an unauthorized one, and likewise yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the role is recognised, it must appear on the target endpoint's authorized-role list; if it does not, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary operator string but the primary key of a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `EstablishmentOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Establishment`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Establishment` compare the resource's establishment foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Practitioner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Practitioner`, and ownership checks compare the resource's practitioner foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolRegistrar`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`, and that registrar may reach only `Practitioner` rows and `TrainingRecord` rows whose `school_id` equals `X-User-ID`. For all three, an `X-User-ID` that is syntactically well-formed but names no row of the backing entity is an authorization failure — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) — never a 404, so a caller cannot use the authentication surface to probe for the existence of rows.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise opaque; the server may record an inspector's identity where a workflow writes it — an inspection's `inspector_user_id` is exactly such a field — but it never resolves it against a table. When `X-User-Role` is `HearingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque adjudicator identifier, recorded on a hearing as `presiding_officer_user_id` and compared against `inspector_user_id` for the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10, but never resolved against a table. When `X-User-Role` is `HealthAuthorityLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque public-health operator identifier, recorded nowhere and compared to no `id`.

Role capabilities follow the board's own separation of function. A `BoardAdmin` alone owns the reference registries, all practitioner licensing actions, board review and finalization of inspections, hazard clearance, citation issuance, hearing scheduling and the logical clock. An `Inspector` schedules and conducts inspections, records findings, declares imminent hazards and submits for review, but may never finalize a review, issue a citation or adjudicate one. A `HearingOfficer` records hearing decisions and nothing else. An `EstablishmentOwner` acts for their own salon only: workstations, postings, rentals, sanitation logs, contests, payments and incident reports scoped to the establishment named by their `X-User-ID`. A `Practitioner` may terminate their own rental, log their own sanitation procedures, and contest or pay a citation naming them. A `SchoolRegistrar` enrols candidates and files clock-hour records for candidates of their own school. A `HealthAuthorityLiaison` receives, notifies and closes infection investigations, with read-only visibility into establishments, sanitation logs and inspections.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination arguments. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or either value supplied as something other than an integer is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a list is explicitly given another order below, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The board's reading habits mean most enforcement lists are chronologically reversed, so the following orders are fixed per list: establishments by `created_at` descending then `id` ascending; practitioners by `created_at` descending then `id` ascending; training records by `performed_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending; workstations by `station_number` ascending then `created_at` ascending; the sanitation logs of one workstation by `performed_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; booth rentals by `term_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; inspections by `window_start_at` descending then `created_at` descending; violation findings by `created_at` ascending, which is deliberately the same order the scoring calculation walks them in; citations by `issued_at` descending then `created_at` descending; the hearings of one citation by `round_index` ascending; infection incidents by `reported_at` descending then `created_at` descending; and ledger entries by `occurred_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending, which is the replay order that proves the compensating sums balance.

Each list also accepts optional equality filters, and any filter left out simply widens the result set. Establishments filter on `license_status`, `county_code`, `compliance_posture` and `establishment_type`. Practitioners filter on `school_id`, `license_status`, `license_type` and `exam_eligible`. Training records filter on `practitioner_id`, `school_id`, `sponsor_practitioner_id`, `credit_type` and `discipline`. Workstations filter on `establishment_id`, `posted_practitioner_id`, `station_type` and `posting_status`. A workstation's sanitation logs filter on `log_type`. Booth rentals filter on `workstation_id`, `practitioner_id`, `status` and `rental_type`. Inspections filter on `establishment_id`, `reinspection_of_id`, `status`, `inspection_type` and `hazard_state`. Violation findings filter on `inspection_id`, `violation_code_id`, `attributed_practitioner_id`, `workstation_id` and `declared_hazard`. Citations filter on `inspection_id`, `subject_practitioner_id`, `subject_type` and `status`. Infection incidents filter on `establishment_id`, `suspected_practitioner_id` and `status`. Ledger entries filter on `establishment_id`, `citation_id`, `practitioner_id`, `inspection_id`, `ledger_type` and `entry_reason`. Unknown fields anywhere in a query string or a request body are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix and is never accepted as client input. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. Four record types are immutable by design and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from `TrainingRecord`, `ViolationCode`, `SanitationLog` and `LedgerEntry`. A training record is never amended once filed, a violation code is published reference data, a sanitation log is a contemporaneous procedure record, and a ledger entry is an accounting fact; corrections to any of them take the form of new rows, never edits.

Because compliance is entirely deadline-driven, the server does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a **logical clock** whose value every deadline, window, expiry, currency check and overlap rule consults, and whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `BoardAdmin` moves it by supplying `to_iso8601`; the clock may only move forward, so a `to_iso8601` less than or equal to the current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE` (HTTP 422). Advancing the clock is not a passive act: it runs the three time-driven sweeps described in §3.9, and every entity those sweeps mutate has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed like any other write.

Nested resources are addressed through their parent, and path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. A request naming a workstation beneath an establishment, or a finding beneath an inspection, fails on the leftmost identifier that names no row, and the error names the entity that was missing: `ERR_ESTABLISHMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKSTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_CODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFECTION_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are validated the same way, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and produce the same 404 family.

### 2.5. The Compliance Ledger

Three quantities in this system are conserved: money owed on citations, demerit points accumulated by establishments, and sanction points accumulated by practitioners. None of the three is stored as a mutable counter. Each exists only as the signed sum of immutable `LedgerEntry` rows, and each entry declares which quantity it moves through `ledger_type`, one of `FINE_CENTS`, `DEMERIT_POINTS` or `SANCTION_POINTS`. Exactly one of the two magnitude fields is populated per row: `delta_cents`, a signed whole-cent movement, is non-null precisely when `ledger_type` is `FINE_CENTS` and null otherwise, while `delta_points`, a signed movement carried to two decimal places, is non-null precisely when `ledger_type` is `DEMERIT_POINTS` or `SANCTION_POINTS` and null otherwise. Each row records `occurred_at`, the logical-clock instant at which it was posted, which together with `created_at` ascending gives the deterministic chronological replay order.

Each entry also declares `entry_reason`, naming the transition that posted it, drawn from `CITATION_ASSESSMENT`, `CITATION_PAYMENT`, `CITATION_LATE_PENALTY`, `CITATION_AMENDMENT_ADJUSTMENT`, `CITATION_DISMISSAL_REVERSAL`, `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_ASSESSMENT`, `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_REVERSAL`, `INFECTION_SANCTION_ASSESSMENT` and `REINSTATEMENT_SANCTION_REVERSAL`. The subject links are polymorphic and populated by quantity rather than by convenience: `citation_id` is set on every `FINE_CENTS` entry and additionally on an `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_REVERSAL` entry posted by a dismissal, and is null otherwise; `establishment_id` is set on every `DEMERIT_POINTS` entry and null otherwise; `practitioner_id` is set on every `SANCTION_POINTS` entry and null otherwise; and `inspection_id` is set on `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_ASSESSMENT` and `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_REVERSAL` entries and null otherwise.

The three balances follow mechanically. A citation's `outstanding_fine_cents` is the sum of `delta_cents` over `FINE_CENTS` entries carrying that citation's `id` in `citation_id`. An establishment's `demerit_balance` is the sum of `delta_points` over `DEMERIT_POINTS` entries carrying that establishment's `id` in `establishment_id`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A practitioner's `sanction_points` is the sum of `delta_points` over `SANCTION_POINTS` entries carrying that practitioner's `id` in `practitioner_id`, likewise rounded half up to two decimal places. These sums are signed running totals: a reversal may legitimately bring a balance back to exactly 0.00, and the sum must never be clamped to zero. A negative balance is a defect in the entries that produced it, not something the reader should mask.

### 2.6. Establishment Posture: The Derived Gate Over Everything

An establishment's licensing lifecycle lives in `license_status`, which takes one of `PENDING_INSPECTION`, `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD` or `REVOKED`. A newly registered facility starts at `PENDING_INSPECTION`, and `REVOKED` is terminal. Three of those states — `PENDING_INSPECTION`, `ACTIVE` and `PROBATION` — are **operable**: the establishment may still be built out and worked. The other three — `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD` and `REVOKED` — are not, and any request against such an establishment that would add capacity or personnel (adding a workstation, changing a licence posting, opening a booth rental) or that would schedule a new inspection is rejected with `ERR_ESTABLISHMENT_NOT_OPERABLE` (HTTP 409).

Sitting on top of the licence status is a second, purely derived dimension: `compliance_posture`, one of `CLEAR`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, defaulting to `CLEAR` at registration. It is recomputed on every write that appends a `DEMERIT_POINTS` or `FINE_CENTS` ledger entry or changes `license_status` — that is, on inspection finalization, on a hearing decision, on a citation payment, on an infection-incident resolution and on every advance of the logical clock. The posture is resolved in strict priority order, and the boundaries are inclusive at the threshold. The posture is `FROZEN` when `license_status` is `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD` or `REVOKED`, or when `demerit_balance` has reached 100.00 points or more. Otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` when `demerit_balance` has reached 50.00 points or more, or when `open_critical_citation_count` is at least 1. Otherwise it is `WATCH` when `demerit_balance` has reached 20.00 points or more. Otherwise it is `CLEAR`. To make the inclusivity concrete: a balance of 49.99 yields `WATCH`, 50.00 yields `RESTRICTED`, 99.99 remains `RESTRICTED`, and 100.00 yields `FROZEN`. Note the consequence that an establishment which is still operable on paper can nevertheless be frozen purely on accumulated demerits; a `FROZEN` establishment may not add workstations, change postings or open booth rentals, and such attempts are rejected with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409).

The same recomputation refreshes the rest of the establishment's derived summary. `station_count` is the number of `Workstation` rows whose `establishment_id` names this establishment, with no status filter, because workstations are never deleted; it must never exceed the facility permit's `station_capacity`. `outstanding_fine_total_cents` is the sum of `outstanding_fine_cents` over every citation reachable through this establishment's inspections whose `status` is neither `DISMISSED` nor `SETTLED`. `delinquent_fine_total_cents` is that same sum restricted to citations whose `status` is `DELINQUENT`. `open_critical_citation_count` counts those same non-dismissed, non-settled citations whose `max_finding_severity` is `CRITICAL` or `IMMINENT_HAZARD`. Dismissed and settled citations drop out of all three money-and-count aggregates, but the reversal ledger rows they produced remain in `demerit_balance` forever. `last_finalized_inspection_at` holds the `finalized_at` of the most recently finalized inspection of this establishment, choosing by `finalized_at` descending and then `created_at` descending, and is null while the establishment has never had one finalized.

One further derived flag closes the sanitation loop at the establishment level. `foot_spa_biofilm_current` is true when every `Workstation` of the establishment whose `station_type` is `FOOT_SPA` has at least one sanitation log of type `FOOT_SPA_WEEKLY_BIOFILM` whose `performed_at` falls on or after the logical clock minus seven days. An establishment with no `FOOT_SPA` stations at all satisfies this vacuously and the flag is true, which is why a newly registered facility reports it as true.

### 2.7. Precision and Rounding Discipline

All point quantities — `base_points`, per-finding point values, inspection scores, `demerit_balance`, `sanction_points`, credited hours and hour requirements — are carried to two decimal places and rounded **half up**. All monetary quantities are whole cents: `fine_cents_base`, `assessed_fine_cents`, `administrative_fee_cents`, `total_due_cents`, `outstanding_fine_cents`, `amended_fine_cents`, `monthly_rent_cents`, `amount_cents` and the establishment-level fine totals are all integers, and any intermediate product that lands between two cents is rounded half up to the nearer cent. Counts such as `station_count`, `finding_count`, `citation_count`, `observed_count`, `case_count`, `hearing_round_count`, `round_index`, `implement_batch_count` and `open_critical_citation_count` are plain integers.

Where a calculation aggregates over groups, the **order of rounding is part of the specification and is not interchangeable with any equivalent-looking alternative**. Two calculations depend on this. Inspection scoring rounds each finding's point value to two decimal places first, then sums the rounded values, then rounds the sum again to two decimal places; summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and yields different results whenever repeat multipliers are in play. Apprentice credited hours on the apprentice track round each ISO week's credited figure to two decimal places first, then sum, then round the total again. Sections 3.5 and 3.2 give the two calculations in full.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Chained Writes

Two creation operations sit downstream of a parent whose state a second actor can legitimately have moved in the meantime, and both are protected by an optimistic-concurrency token that the client must supply from its last read of the parent.

Creating a booth rental — `POST /cosmetology/booth-rentals` — requires the non-optional field `expected_workstation_version`, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent `Workstation`. If it does not equal the workstation's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORKSTATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists precisely because a chain of successive rentals on one station is a legitimate product operation — adjacent terms are permitted by the half-open overlap geometry of §3.3 — so a second create cannot be treated as an error on its own. The stale-version check is what forces the caller to re-read the station's current occupancy chain, and its current posting, before booking on top of it.

Creating a citation — `POST /cosmetology/citations` — requires the non-optional field `expected_inspection_version`, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent `Inspection`. If it does not equal the inspection's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). One finalized inspection may legitimately yield several citations — one against the establishment plus one per attributed practitioner — so a repeat create is valid behaviour; the guard forces the caller to re-read `citation_count` and `final_adjusted_score` before assessing another fine off the same visit, since the finalize cascade and every earlier citation on that inspection both advanced the version.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where an endpoint is not named in this section, the client sends no `version` and the server performs no version comparison.

### 2.9. Order of Rule Evaluation

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following evaluation order. Implementations must evaluate in this sequence rather than in whatever order is convenient, because the observable error identifies which rule fired first.

1. **Header presence**, then **role recognition** — both `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permitted on the endpoint** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Entity-backed identity resolution**: for any endpoint whose authorized roles include `EstablishmentOwner`, `Practitioner` or `SchoolRegistrar`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must resolve to a row of the backing entity — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. **Path existence**, left to right — the matching `ERR_..._NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Body foreign-key existence**, in the endpoint's declared input order — the matching `ERR_..._NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Ownership scope** for entity-backed roles — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This runs after existence, so a caller cannot probe for row existence through the authorization surface, and before any state check, so a caller outside their scope never learns the target's state.
7. **Request shape**: required fields present, fields the endpoint does not declare ignored rather than rejected, enum fields carrying a declared member, quantities non-negative where the field demands it, every field whose name ends in `_at` parsing as an ISO 8601 UTC instant and every field whose name ends in `_on` parsing as an ISO 8601 calendar date — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. **Pagination range** on list endpoints — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
9. **Conditional field pairing** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The pairings are: an inspection's `window_end_at` must be strictly after its `window_start_at`; a booth rental's `term_end_at`, when supplied, must be strictly after `term_start_at`; `complaint_reference` is required when `inspection_type` is `COMPLAINT_DRIVEN` and forbidden otherwise; `reinspection_of_id` is required when `inspection_type` is `REINSPECTION` and forbidden otherwise; `subject_practitioner_id` is required when `subject_type` is `PRACTITIONER` and forbidden when it is `ESTABLISHMENT`; `school_id` is required when `credit_type` is `SCHOOL_CLOCK_HOUR` and must be null when it is `APPRENTICE_HOUR`, while `sponsor_practitioner_id` is required when `credit_type` is `APPRENTICE_HOUR` and must be null when it is `SCHOOL_CLOCK_HOUR`; `amended_fine_cents` is required when `decision` is `AMENDED` and forbidden otherwise; and `implement_batch_count` is required when `log_type` is `IMPLEMENT_DISINFECTION` and must be null for every foot-spa log type.
10. **Clock monotonicity** — `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE`.
11. **State preconditions**, in this internal order: establishment operability (`ERR_ESTABLISHMENT_NOT_OPERABLE`); referenced practitioner licence active (`ERR_PRACTITIONER_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`); then the entity-specific status gates — `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PENDING_REVIEW`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_HAZARD_ALREADY_DECLARED`, `ERR_HAZARD_NOT_DECLARED`, `ERR_CITATION_NOT_CONTESTABLE`, `ERR_CITATION_NOT_CONTESTED`, `ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_CITATION_ALREADY_SETTLED`, `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_NOT_PROPOSED`, `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_NOTIFIABLE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_NOTIFIED` and `ERR_LICENSE_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED`, in that sequence.
12. **Optimistic-concurrency tokens** — `ERR_STALE_WORKSTATION_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION`. Both therefore fire only once the target's own state gates have passed.
13. **Safety and regulatory rules**, in this internal order: hazard-finding eligibility (`ERR_HAZARD_FINDING_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); frozen posture (`ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN`); segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); hazard-clearance reinspection (`ERR_HAZARD_CLEARANCE_REINSPECTION_REQUIRED`); contact-time minimum (`ERR_DISINFECTANT_CONTACT_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`); log-type-to-station-type match (`ERR_SANITATION_LOG_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`); posting compliance (`ERR_WORKSTATION_POSTING_NONCOMPLIANT`).
14. **Temporal geometry**: booth-rental overlap (`ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_OVERLAP`); inspection-window overlap (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`); inspection cooldown buffer (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_COOLDOWN`); foot-spa cycle gap (`ERR_FOOT_SPA_CYCLE_GAP_VIOLATION`); contest window (`ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_EXPIRED`).
15. **Referential business rules**: practitioner-to-establishment linkage (`ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_LINKED_TO_ESTABLISHMENT`); workstation belongs to the inspection's establishment (`ERR_WORKSTATION_NOT_IN_ESTABLISHMENT`); reinspection parent eligibility (`ERR_REINSPECTION_PARENT_REQUIRED`); sponsor and school eligibility (`ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); citation subject present in findings (`ERR_CITATION_SUBJECT_NOT_IN_FINDINGS`).
16. **Capacity and quota rules**: duplicate station number (`ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_NUMBER`), then station capacity (`ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), then hearing rounds exhausted (`ERR_HEARING_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`), then exam eligibility (`ERR_EXAM_ELIGIBILITY_REQUIRED`), then training-hour composition (`ERR_TRAINING_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`).
17. **Fiscal rules**: payment amount (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`), then the delinquency block on expansion (`ERR_OUTSTANDING_FINES_BLOCK_EXPANSION`).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright because they are easy to get backwards. Because the frozen-posture check at step 13 precedes the delinquency block at step 17, an establishment that is simultaneously `FROZEN` and carrying delinquent fines reports `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN`, not `ERR_OUTSTANDING_FINES_BLOCK_EXPANSION`. And because the duplicate-station-number check precedes the capacity check, an attempt to add a station that both duplicates an existing `station_number` and would exceed `station_capacity` reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_NUMBER`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Cascade Consistency

The board's central integrity rule is that the same person may never both inspect and adjudicate. An inspection records the field officer as `inspector_user_id`. When a hearing is scheduled on a citation derived from that inspection, the supplied `presiding_officer_user_id` must differ from that `inspector_user_id`; and when a decision is recorded, the acting `X-User-ID` must likewise differ from the `inspector_user_id` of the inspection behind the citation. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). This is enforced at both the scheduling and the deciding step, so substituting a different officer at decision time does not launder the conflict.

Cascades in this system are immediate and complete within the responding request. When a write changes a parent, every dependent value the specification names as derived from that parent is recomputed before the response is emitted — there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation. Each entity a cascade actually mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1, and an entity a cascade merely reads is left untouched. Where a cascade has ordered hops, the ordering is part of the contract because intermediate values are snapshotted mid-cascade; Section 3.7 gives the five-hop finalize cascade, whose second hop pins a value that the third hop then invalidates.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Registries and Establishment Registration

Before any inspection can be scored, the board must publish the rule schedule the scoring reads. A `BoardAdmin` publishes each entry in the violation schedule with a `code` — the board rule citation string, for example `VC-DIS-210` — which must be non-empty and unique across the whole schedule. Each entry declares a `category` naming the regulatory family it belongs to, drawn from `SANITATION`, `DISINFECTION`, `IMPLEMENT_HANDLING`, `FOOT_SPA`, `LICENSING_POSTING`, `FACILITY`, `EQUIPMENT` and `RECORDKEEPING`; a `severity` band of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, `CRITICAL` or `IMMINENT_HAZARD`; a strictly positive `base_points` value carried to two decimal places, which is the per-unit demerit weight before the severity multiplier, the observed count and the repeat factor are applied; and a `fine_cents_base`, the statutory base fine in whole cents that one finding of this code contributes, which may be zero but never negative. Schedule entries are reference data and are never amended.

Registering a licensed cosmetology school is likewise a board-staff act. A school carries a `name`, a `county_code` giving the board district of the campus as exactly three uppercase letters, and a `required_clock_hours` figure — strictly positive, two decimal places — that every candidate of that school must accumulate before becoming exam-eligible. Two further attributes govern what the school may back. Its `accreditation_status` is one of `ACCREDITED`, `PROBATIONARY` or `WITHDRAWN`, defaulting to `ACCREDITED`, and its `apprenticeship_program_approved` flag, defaulting to false, records whether the board has approved it to sponsor apprenticeship hours.

An establishment registers with a `legal_name` — non-empty, up to 200 characters — an `establishment_type` of `SALON`, `BARBERSHOP`, `NAIL_SALON`, `ESTHETICS_SPA` or `SCHOOL_CLINIC`, which is what determines the equipment the facility is expected to carry, a `county_code` of exactly three uppercase letters used for inspection routing, and a `station_capacity` of at least 1 giving the maximum number of workstations the facility permit allows. Registration is available to board staff and to an owner acting for themselves. A freshly registered facility opens at `license_status` of `PENDING_INSPECTION` and `compliance_posture` of `CLEAR`, with `station_count` at 0, `demerit_balance` at 0.00, `open_critical_citation_count` at 0, both `outstanding_fine_total_cents` and `delinquent_fine_total_cents` at 0, `foot_spa_biofilm_current` true because it has no foot spas yet, and `last_finalized_inspection_at` null. It may be built out immediately, but it cannot reach `ACTIVE` until an inspection has been finalized — a facility permit is a promise to be inspected, not a certificate of compliance.

### 3.2. The School-to-Licence Pipeline

A candidate is enrolled with a `full_name` of up to 200 characters and a `license_type` naming the discipline of the licence or candidacy: `COSMETOLOGIST`, `BARBER`, `NAIL_TECHNICIAN`, `ESTHETICIAN`, `INSTRUCTOR` or `APPRENTICE`. Enrolment is available to board staff and to the registrar of the school the candidate is joining. A `school_id` may be supplied to bind the candidate to a licensed school, or omitted entirely for a pure apprenticeship candidate who never enrolled anywhere. Every new candidate opens at `license_status` of `STUDENT` with `credited_hours_school`, `credited_hours_apprentice`, `credited_hours_total` and `sanction_points` all at 0.00, `exam_eligible` false, `license_expires_on` null and `suspension_cause_points` null. The candidate's `required_clock_hours_effective` is resolved at once: it is the `required_clock_hours` of the school named by `school_id` when that field is non-null, and otherwise the standalone apprenticeship requirement of 1600.00 hours.

Hours reach a candidate as immutable training records, filed one at a time by board staff or by the candidate's own school registrar. Each record names the `practitioner_id` of the candidate earning the hours, a `credit_type` of either `SCHOOL_CLOCK_HOUR` or `APPRENTICE_HOUR` selecting which hour ledger it feeds, a strictly positive `hours` figure of at most 24 carried to two decimal places, a `performed_on` UTC calendar date, and a `discipline` naming the curriculum area the hours are booked against: `THEORY`, `PRACTICAL`, `SANITATION`, `DISINFECTION` or `BUSINESS_LAW`. A school-clock record names the backing `school_id`; an apprentice record instead names the supervising instructor as `sponsor_practitioner_id`. Eligibility of those two references is checked before the record is accepted: a `school_id` on a school-clock record must name a school whose `accreditation_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`; a `sponsor_practitioner_id` on an apprentice record must name a practitioner other than the subject, whose `license_type` is one of `COSMETOLOGIST`, `BARBER`, `NAIL_TECHNICIAN`, `ESTHETICIAN` or `INSTRUCTOR`, and whose `license_status` is `ACTIVE`; and where the subject's own `license_type` is `APPRENTICE`, an apprentice record additionally requires that the sponsoring practitioner's school, when they have one, or otherwise the subject's own school, carries `apprenticeship_program_approved` set to true. Any of those failures is reported as `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

Filing a record recomputes the candidate's whole credited-hour ledger from scratch, walking every training record they hold in `performed_on` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Records are append-only and are never voided, so there is no status filter on the walk. The **school ledger** is computed by grouping the school-clock records by their `performed_on` UTC date, taking for each such date the lesser of the total hours claimed on that date and the daily cap of eight hours, adding those capped daily figures together, and rounding the result half up to two decimal places into `credited_hours_school`. The daily cap is what stops a candidate from booking a sixteen-hour Saturday.

The **apprentice ledger** is where the same rows produce two different answers, and which answer applies is selected by the candidate's own `license_type`. For a candidate whose `license_type` is `APPRENTICE`, the apprentice-hour records are pooled by the ISO-8601 week containing `performed_on` — the week that runs from Monday at `00:00:00.000Z` through Sunday at `23:59:59.999Z` — and for each such week the total hours claimed in that week are capped at 40 hours, the capped figure is multiplied by the apprenticeship credit ratio of 0.60, and that weekly product is rounded half up to two decimal places. The rounded weekly figures are then added together and the total is rounded half up to two decimal places again into `credited_hours_apprentice`; rounding per week before summing is the rule, not an optimisation. For a candidate whose `license_type` is anything other than `APPRENTICE` — a licensed or candidate practitioner logging supervised hours — the identical set of apprentice-hour records is instead pooled by `performed_on` UTC date, each date's total capped at the same eight-hour daily cap, credited at a ratio of 1.00, summed across dates, and rounded half up once to two decimal places. The consequence is deliberate: the same rows credit differently depending on who filed them, and a candidate's track cannot be changed retroactively without changing the ledger.

`credited_hours_total` is the sum of `credited_hours_school` and `credited_hours_apprentice`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `required_clock_hours_effective` is re-resolved on the same pass, and `exam_eligible` becomes true exactly when `credited_hours_total` has reached `required_clock_hours_effective` or more — the comparison is inclusive, so hitting the requirement to the hundredth of an hour qualifies. Filing a training record increments the subject practitioner's `version`.

Licensing itself moves through board actions, each supplying an `action`, an optional `effective_at` that defaults to the current logical clock, and an optional `reason`. The legality table is exact, and any action attempted from a status it does not permit is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 409): `GRANT_EXAM_ELIGIBILITY` only from `STUDENT`; `ISSUE_LICENSE` only from `EXAM_ELIGIBLE`; `PLACE_ON_PROBATION` only from `ACTIVE`; `SUSPEND` only from `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; `REINSTATE` only from `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `LAPSED`; `REVOKE` only from `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. Nothing but `ISSUE_LICENSE` is legal from `EXAM_ELIGIBLE`, and nothing at all is legal from `REVOKED`, which is terminal.

Granting exam eligibility moves the candidate `STUDENT → EXAM_ELIGIBLE` after re-running the credited-hour ledger, and it carries two gates of its own. The candidate's `credited_hours_total` must have reached `required_clock_hours_effective`, and a shortfall is reported as `ERR_EXAM_ELIGIBILITY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). Beyond raw hours, the candidate must hold at least one training record whose `discipline` is `SANITATION` and at least one whose `discipline` is `DISINFECTION`, and a school-affiliated candidate's school must carry `accreditation_status` of exactly `ACCREDITED` at the moment of the action — a school that has slipped to `PROBATIONARY` or `WITHDRAWN` between the filing of the hours and the granting of eligibility blocks the grant. Either composition failure is reported as `ERR_TRAINING_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Issuing the licence moves `EXAM_ELIGIBLE → ACTIVE`, requires `exam_eligible` to be true (otherwise `ERR_EXAM_ELIGIBILITY_REQUIRED` again), and stamps `license_expires_on` as the UTC calendar date reached by adding the licence term of 730 days to `effective_at`.

`PLACE_ON_PROBATION` moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` and does nothing else. `REINSTATE` closes the cycle from `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `LAPSED` back to `ACTIVE`, and what it does beyond that depends on where it came from. When the practitioner carries a non-null `suspension_cause_points` — the snapshot taken when the suspension fired — reinstatement appends a compensating sanction-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `REINSTATEMENT_SANCTION_REVERSAL` and `delta_points` equal to the negative of that snapshot, then sets `suspension_cause_points` back to null. Because the reversal is sized to the snapshot rather than to the last assessment, the practitioner's `SANCTION_POINTS` rows sum to exactly 0.00 afterwards and `sanction_points` becomes 0.00: the whole sanction history that caused the suspension is wiped, not merely the final increment. A `REINSTATE` from `PROBATION` or `LAPSED`, where `suspension_cause_points` is null, posts no reversal entry at all and leaves `sanction_points` untouched. A `REINSTATE` that lifts a lapse additionally re-stamps `license_expires_on` as the UTC date 730 days after `effective_at`, and recomputes the posting verdict on every workstation whose `posted_practitioner_id` names this practitioner.

Suspension and revocation are the destructive actions. `SUSPEND` moves the licence from `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` to `SUSPENDED`, snapshots `suspension_cause_points` from the current `sanction_points`, and then cascades into the establishments where that practitioner works: every booth rental of theirs that is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` set to the current logical clock, and every rental of theirs that is still `PROPOSED` moves `PROPOSED → VOIDED`. For each such rental whose `practitioner_id` equals the `posted_practitioner_id` of its workstation, that posting is cleared to null, the workstation's `posting_status` is recomputed to `MISSING_POSTING`, and the workstation's `version` is incremented. `REVOKE` drives the licence to the terminal `REVOKED` state and runs exactly the same booth-rental and posting cascade. Both increment the practitioner's `version`. Wherever an operation demands a currently valid licence — posting a practitioner at a station, or activating their rental — a practitioner in `STUDENT`, `EXAM_ELIGIBLE`, `SUSPENDED`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_PRACTITIONER_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), and so is one merely on `PROBATION`; probation is tolerated only for terminating an existing rental and for being named as the attributed practitioner on a finding.

### 3.3. Facility Build-Out, Licence Posting and Booth Rental Relationships

Adding a workstation to an establishment is the board's unit of capacity, and it is the most heavily gated write in the system because it is where an establishment grows. The request supplies a `station_number`, an integer of at least 1 labelling the station inside the facility, and a `station_type` naming its equipment class: `STYLING_CHAIR`, `NAIL_TABLE`, `FOOT_SPA` or `FACIAL_ROOM`. A station opens with `posted_practitioner_id` null, `posting_status` at `MISSING_POSTING` and `last_biofilm_cycle_at` null, and the write refreshes the parent establishment's `station_count` and `foot_spa_biofilm_current` and increments its `version`. Numbers must be unique within the establishment — two stations of the same establishment may never share a `station_number`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_NUMBER` (HTTP 409) — but numbering need not be contiguous, so a facility may run stations 1, 2 and 7. Capacity is enforced inclusively against the facility permit: the addition that brings `station_count` to exactly `station_capacity` succeeds, and the next one is rejected with `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Before either of those, the establishment must be operable and must not be `FROZEN`; after them, the establishment's `delinquent_fine_total_cents` must be exactly 0, and an establishment carrying delinquent fines is refused expansion with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_FINES_BLOCK_EXPANSION` (HTTP 402). Unpaid fines that have not yet gone delinquent do not block anything.

Posting a licence at a station is what makes the station lawful to work. The request names a `posted_practitioner_id`, and the practitioner must already be linked to this establishment through a booth rental in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` status on one of its workstations; a practitioner with no such link is refused with `ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_LINKED_TO_ESTABLISHMENT` (HTTP 422). The practitioner's own licence must be `ACTIVE`, and the establishment must be operable and not `FROZEN`. Posting recomputes the station's `posting_status` and increments the station's `version`.

That verdict is derived and never set directly. When `posted_practitioner_id` is null the verdict is `MISSING_POSTING`. Otherwise the posting is **valid** — and the verdict is `COMPLIANT` — when three conditions all hold: the posted practitioner's `license_status` is `ACTIVE`; their `license_expires_on` falls on or after the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock, so a licence expiring today is still valid; and there exists a booth rental on this very workstation, for this very practitioner, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `term_start_at` is at or before the current clock, and whose `term_end_at` is either null or strictly after the current clock. If any of the three fails, the verdict is `STALE_POSTING`. Rentals in `PROPOSED`, `TERMINATED` or `VOIDED` status never make a posting `COMPLIANT`, which produces the deliberate two-step choreography of this domain: a rental is proposed first, the licence is posted second, and only then can the rental be activated — at which point the station finally reads `COMPLIANT`.

A booth rental records the commercial relationship between a station and the person who occupies it. The request names the `workstation_id` of the station being occupied and the `practitioner_id` of the occupant, a `rental_type` of either `EMPLOYEE` or `BOOTH_RENTER_INDEPENDENT_CONTRACTOR` — the latter being the classification that carries the independent licence-posting obligation the board cares about — a `term_start_at` instant that is the inclusive start of occupancy, an optional `term_end_at` that is the exclusive end and whose absence means open-ended, and a `monthly_rent_cents` figure of at least 0, since zero rent is entirely legal for an `EMPLOYEE` row. The rental opens at `status` of `PROPOSED` with `terminated_at` null, and its creation increments the parent workstation's `version`. Creating a rental is an expansion of personnel, so it inherits the same operability, `FROZEN`-posture and delinquency gates as adding a workstation, and it additionally requires the stale-version token described in §2.8.

Occupancy geometry on a station is **half-open**, spanning `term_start_at` inclusive to `term_end_at` exclusive, with a null end treated as extending forever. No two rentals in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` status on the same workstation may overlap, and an overlap of a single millisecond is rejected with `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). There is no buffer between terms: two rentals that merely touch, where the new term's `term_start_at` is exactly the previous term's `term_end_at`, are accepted, which is how a station hands over cleanly from one renter to the next at midnight. Rentals in `TERMINATED` or `VOIDED` status are ignored by the check entirely, and where a terminated rental is considered at all its effective end is its `terminated_at`.

Activating a proposed rental moves it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`. The rental must currently be `PROPOSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_NOT_PROPOSED` (HTTP 409). The occupant's `license_status` must be exactly `ACTIVE` — `PROBATION` is explicitly not sufficient to activate a rental — and the station's `posted_practitioner_id` must already name that same practitioner, a mismatch being reported as `ERR_WORKSTATION_POSTING_NONCOMPLIANT` (HTTP 409). The establishment must be in `PENDING_INSPECTION`, `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, and its `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`. Activation recomputes the parent station's `posting_status`, which typically moves `STALE_POSTING → COMPLIANT` once the term covers the current clock, and increments both the station's `version` and the rental's `version`.

Terminating an active rental is available to board staff, to the establishment, and to the occupying practitioner themselves. An optional `terminated_at` may be supplied and otherwise defaults to the current logical clock, and an optional `reason` may accompany it. The rental must currently be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_RENTAL_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Termination moves the rental `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, writes `terminated_at`, and truncates the term by setting `term_end_at` to that same instant. Where the station's `posted_practitioner_id` equals the departing rental's `practitioner_id`, that posting is cleared to null so the station's `posting_status` falls to `MISSING_POSTING`, and the station's `version` is incremented; a station whose posting names somebody else is left alone. The rental's own `version` is incremented. Rentals may also reach a terminal state without anyone asking: a rental that is `PROPOSED` when its establishment is placed on probation or revoked, or when its practitioner is suspended or revoked, moves `PROPOSED → VOIDED` with `terminated_at` stamped at the current clock and the same posting-clearing cascade, and voided rentals are never resurrected when the establishment later recovers.

### 3.4. Sanitation, Disinfection and Foot-Spa Protocol Verification

Disinfection is the part of this domain where the board's rules are least negotiable, and every executed protocol is filed as an immutable sanitation log against the station where it happened. Board staff, the establishment, and the practitioner performing the work may all file one. Each log names the `workstation_id` whose implements or foot spa were processed, a `log_type` naming the protocol executed, a `performed_at` instant that is the inclusive start of the disinfection cycle, a `contact_minutes` figure that is the strictly positive wet-contact duration carried to two decimal places, and the `disinfectant_epa_reg_number` — the non-empty EPA registration number of the hospital-grade disinfectant used, which is what lets an inspector verify that the chemistry itself was approved. Where the protocol is implement disinfection, the log also names an `implement_batch_count` giving the number of implements in the batch; that field is required for implement disinfection and must be null for every foot-spa protocol. The system computes `occupancy_end_at` as `performed_at` advanced by `contact_minutes` minutes, which is the exclusive end of the cycle's occupancy of the station.

Four protocols exist, and each carries a minimum wet-contact time that the log must meet or exceed; the comparison is inclusive, so exactly the minimum is compliant. `IMPLEMENT_DISINFECTION`, `FOOT_SPA_AFTER_CLIENT` and `FOOT_SPA_END_OF_DAY` each require at least 10 minutes of contact. `FOOT_SPA_WEEKLY_BIOFILM` — the weekly biofilm-removal cycle, which is the one procedure the board treats as a deep clean rather than a turnover — requires at least six hours of contact, that is, 360 minutes. A shortfall is rejected with `ERR_DISINFECTANT_CONTACT_TIME_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

The protocol must also match the equipment. `FOOT_SPA_AFTER_CLIENT`, `FOOT_SPA_END_OF_DAY` and `FOOT_SPA_WEEKLY_BIOFILM` are valid only on a station whose `station_type` is `FOOT_SPA`, while `IMPLEMENT_DISINFECTION` is valid only on a `STYLING_CHAIR`, a `NAIL_TABLE` or a `FACIAL_ROOM` station. A mismatch is reported as `ERR_SANITATION_LOG_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Beyond that, the station's `posting_status` must be `COMPLIANT` at the moment of filing; a station reading `MISSING_POSTING` or `STALE_POSTING` may not have sanitation work logged against it at all, because the board cannot attribute the procedure to a verified licensee, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_WORKSTATION_POSTING_NONCOMPLIANT` (HTTP 409).

Foot-spa cycles additionally have to be physically possible. Each log occupies the station from its `performed_at` up to its `occupancy_end_at`, and the next cycle on the same station may not begin until at least five minutes after the previous cycle's `occupancy_end_at` — the gap is inclusive, so a new `performed_at` exactly five minutes after the previous `occupancy_end_at` is accepted, while anything earlier, including an outright overlap, is rejected with `ERR_FOOT_SPA_CYCLE_GAP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Consider a spa whose after-client cycle starts at 09:00 with 10 minutes of contact: it occupies the basin until 09:10, and the earliest lawful next cycle begins at 09:15.

Filing a log has two upward consequences. When the `log_type` is `FOOT_SPA_WEEKLY_BIOFILM`, the station's `last_biofilm_cycle_at` is refreshed to the `performed_at` of the most recent such log at that station — choosing by `performed_at` descending and then `created_at` descending, and remaining null while the station has never had one — and the station's `version` is incremented. Every log then refreshes the establishment's `foot_spa_biofilm_current` flag and increments the establishment's `version`. That flag matters far outside this subsection: an immediate closure cannot be lifted while any foot spa in the facility is outside its seven-day biofilm currency window.

### 3.5. Inspections: Scheduling, Conduct and Violation Scoring

An inspection is booked against a window rather than an instant, because a field officer's arrival time is not knowable in advance. Board staff and inspectors may both schedule one, supplying the `establishment_id` of the facility under inspection, an `inspection_type` of `ROUTINE`, `COMPLAINT_DRIVEN` or `REINSPECTION`, a `window_start_at` that is the inclusive start of the scheduled window, a `window_end_at` that is its inclusive end and must be strictly after the start, and the `inspector_user_id` of the officer credited with the visit. A complaint-driven visit must also carry the external `complaint_reference` docket string, which is forbidden on the other two types. A reinspection must carry `reinspection_of_id`, the self-referential edge back to the visit it follows, which is forbidden on the other two types. A new inspection opens at `status` of `SCHEDULED` and `hazard_state` of `NONE`, with `finding_count` and `citation_count` at 0, `started_at`, `submitted_at`, `finalized_at` and `demerit_balance_before_finalize` all null, and both score fields and both outcome fields — `provisional_adjusted_score`, `final_adjusted_score`, `provisional_outcome` and `final_outcome` — null.

Non-operable establishments are not entirely closed to the board, and the distinction is deliberate. A `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD` or `REVOKED` establishment rejects a new `ROUTINE` inspection with `ERR_ESTABLISHMENT_NOT_OPERABLE` (HTTP 409), but a `COMPLAINT_DRIVEN` visit or a `REINSPECTION` remains schedulable — otherwise a closure could never be investigated or lifted.

Two temporal rules protect the establishment from being inspected into the ground, and their interval geometries differ on purpose. First, no two inspections in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW` status on the same establishment may overlap, and here the window is a **closed** interval running from `window_start_at` through `window_end_at` inclusive, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint *do* collide — the opposite of the booth-rental rule in §3.3. A collision is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), and it applies to every `inspection_type`, reinspections included. Second, a `ROUTINE` or `COMPLAINT_DRIVEN` window must additionally clear a symmetric cooldown buffer of 24 hours around every non-terminal window on the same establishment: the requested `window_start_at` must fall at or after the other window's `window_end_at` advanced by 24 hours, or else the requested `window_end_at` must fall at or before the other window's `window_start_at` pulled back by 24 hours. Failing that, the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_COOLDOWN` (HTTP 409). A `REINSPECTION` is exempt from the buffer while remaining subject to the strict closed-interval overlap check, which is precisely what lets the board book a reinspection for the morning after the visit it follows.

The parent named by `reinspection_of_id` is not free-form either. It must be an inspection of the **same** establishment, its `status` must be `FINALIZED`, and its `final_outcome` must be one of `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, `FAIL` or `IMMEDIATE_CLOSURE`. A passing inspection has nothing to reinspect, and a parent on another establishment is nonsense; either case, along with a missing parent on a reinspection, is reported as `ERR_REINSPECTION_PARENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

Opening the visit on site moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at` from the current logical clock; attempting it on an inspection that is not `SCHEDULED` yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409). While the visit is open — and only while it is open, any attempt outside `IN_PROGRESS` being refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409) — the inspector records findings one at a time. Each finding names the `violation_code_id` of the rule violated and an `observed_count` of at least 1 giving the number of distinct occurrences observed, which multiplies the per-unit points. The inspector sets `corrected_on_site` to true when they witnessed the correction before leaving, and false otherwise, which is its default. A finding may be attributed to an individual through `attributed_practitioner_id`, or left unattributed — null — when the violation belongs to the establishment itself rather than to any person. It may be tied to a station through `workstation_id`, or left null when it is not station-specific. A free-text `note` may accompany it. On creation, the finding's `severity` is copied from the referenced violation code's `severity` and frozen there for good, `declared_hazard` is set false, and `repeat_factor_provisional`, `repeat_factor_final`, `provisional_points` and `final_points` all stay null until the corresponding scoring pass runs. The write refreshes the inspection's `finding_count` and increments its `version`. Both optional references are checked: an `attributed_practitioner_id` must name a practitioner linked to this establishment through a `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` booth rental on one of its workstations, failing which the finding is refused with `ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_LINKED_TO_ESTABLISHMENT` (HTTP 422), and a supplied `workstation_id` must name a workstation belonging to the inspection's own establishment, failing which the refusal is `ERR_WORKSTATION_NOT_IN_ESTABLISHMENT` (HTTP 422).

Submitting the visit for board review moves it `IN_PROGRESS → PENDING_REVIEW`, stamps `submitted_at` from the current logical clock, and runs the **provisional** pass of the violation-scoring calculation. An inspection with no findings at all submits perfectly well, and its `provisional_adjusted_score` is 0.00 rather than null.

Violation scoring is one calculation evaluated twice, under two variants that differ in exactly two inputs. The **cutoff instant** is the inspection's `submitted_at` for the provisional variant and its `finalized_at` for the final variant. The **on-site-correction credit** is 0.50 for the provisional variant — the inspector's self-reported correction is granted in full at the point of submission — and 0.25 for the final variant, because board review halves an unverified credit. Everything else is identical, and the pass walks the inspection's findings in `created_at` ascending order.

For each finding, the pass first resolves its **repeat count**: the number of findings on *other* inspections that cite the very same `violation_code_id`, that belong to inspections of the same establishment, whose inspection's `status` is `FINALIZED`, and whose inspection's `finalized_at` falls at or after the cutoff instant pulled back by 730 days — two years of history — and strictly before the cutoff instant itself. Findings recorded on inspections still in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_REVIEW` never count, and neither do the other findings of the inspection being scored. The **repeat factor** follows from that count: 1.0 when the count is zero, 1.5 when the count is exactly one, and 2.0 when the count is two or more. Next the pass resolves the finding's **credit**, which is the variant's on-site-correction credit when `corrected_on_site` is true *and* the finding's `severity` is `MINOR` or `MAJOR`, and 0.00 in every other case — a `CRITICAL` or `IMMINENT_HAZARD` finding earns no credit for having been mopped up in front of the inspector. The finding's point value is then the referenced code's `base_points`, multiplied by the severity weight for the finding's own `severity` — 1.0 for `MINOR`, 2.5 for `MAJOR`, 5.0 for `CRITICAL` and 8.0 for `IMMINENT_HAZARD` — multiplied by `observed_count`, multiplied by the repeat factor, and finally multiplied by one minus the credit, with the whole product **rounded half up to two decimal places at this point**. That rounded per-finding value is written to `provisional_points` on the provisional pass and to `final_points` on the final pass, alongside `repeat_factor_provisional` and `repeat_factor_final` respectively.

The inspection's adjusted score is then the sum of those already-rounded per-finding values, with the sum itself rounded half up to two decimal places a second time. Rounding each finding before summing, and then rounding the sum, is the specified order; adding the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is not the rule and will disagree in the cents whenever repeat factors are in play. The score lands in `provisional_adjusted_score` on submission and in `final_adjusted_score` on finalization.

The outcome band is derived from the same variant's score, and it is evaluated in strict order. The outcome is `IMMEDIATE_CLOSURE` when the inspection's `hazard_state` is `HAZARD_DECLARED`, regardless of the score. Otherwise it is `FAIL` when the score is strictly greater than 40.00, or when any finding on the inspection carries a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, or when any carries `IMMINENT_HAZARD` — a single critical observation fails a visit no matter how low the arithmetic comes out. Otherwise it is `CONDITIONAL_PASS` when the score is strictly greater than 10.00. Otherwise it is `PASS`. The band is written to `provisional_outcome` on submission and to `final_outcome` on finalization. Because the final variant halves the on-site credit and re-resolves repeat counts against every inspection finalized up to the finalize instant, an unchanged set of findings scores strictly higher on the final pass than on the provisional pass whenever any `MINOR` or `MAJOR` finding was marked `corrected_on_site` — an establishment that reads `CONDITIONAL_PASS` at submission can therefore read `FAIL` after review, and that is intended rather than a defect.

### 3.6. Immediate Closure Authority and Hazard Clearance

An inspector who walks into an autoclave that has never been run, or a foot spa visibly colonised, cannot wait for board review. The board therefore grants closure authority to the field, and models it as a **second, independent state dimension** on the inspection. `hazard_state` takes `NONE`, `HAZARD_DECLARED` or `HAZARD_CLEARED`, starts at `NONE`, and evolves entirely separately from `status`: declaring a hazard never changes `status`, and the inspection continues through submission and finalization exactly as it would otherwise.

Declaring a hazard requires the inspection to be `IN_PROGRESS`, so it is an act performed on site, and it requires `hazard_state` to be `NONE` — a hazard may be declared at most once per inspection, and a second attempt on an inspection already at `HAZARD_DECLARED` or `HAZARD_CLEARED` is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_ALREADY_DECLARED` (HTTP 409). The declaration names the offending finding through `hazard_violation_finding_id` and records the inspector's `narrative` of what they saw. The named finding must belong to this very inspection and its `severity` must be exactly `IMMINENT_HAZARD`; a `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` finding cannot support immediate closure, and neither can a finding lifted from another visit, so either case is rejected with `ERR_HAZARD_FINDING_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

A successful declaration does four things at once. The named finding's `declared_hazard` flag becomes true. The inspection moves `NONE → HAZARD_DECLARED` on its hazard dimension and its `version` is incremented. The establishment's `license_status` moves immediately to `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD` — from `PENDING_INSPECTION`, from `ACTIVE` or from `PROBATION`, whichever it held — and its `compliance_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`, with the establishment's `version` incremented. Closure does not wait for finalize: the doors are shut the moment the finding is declared. And because `hazard_state` is `HAZARD_DECLARED` from then on, both the provisional and the final outcome bands of that inspection resolve to `IMMEDIATE_CLOSURE` regardless of the arithmetic score.

Lifting a closure is a board-staff act and is deliberately harder than imposing one. The inspection must currently be at `HAZARD_DECLARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_NOT_DECLARED` (HTTP 409), and the establishment must currently hold `license_status` of `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD`. The request names a `clearing_reinspection_id`, and that reinspection must satisfy every one of the following: its `inspection_type` is `REINSPECTION`; its `reinspection_of_id` is the hazard-declaring inspection itself; its `establishment_id` is the same establishment; its `status` is `FINALIZED`; its own `hazard_state` is `NONE`, so a reinspection that itself found a hazard cannot lift anything; and its `final_adjusted_score` is at most 10.00. A clearing reinspection that is not yet `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409), since that gate is evaluated among the state preconditions; every other defect in the nominated reinspection, including a `final_adjusted_score` above the 10.00 ceiling, is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_CLEARANCE_REINSPECTION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). One further condition sits alongside them: the establishment's `foot_spa_biofilm_current` must be true, so a facility with a foot spa outside its seven-day biofilm window cannot be reopened even on a clean reinspection score, and that failure is likewise reported as `ERR_HAZARD_CLEARANCE_REINSPECTION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

When clearance succeeds, the inspection moves `HAZARD_DECLARED → HAZARD_CLEARED`, which is terminal on that dimension, and its `version` is incremented. The establishment moves `CLOSED_IMMINENT_HAZARD → ACTIVE`, its `compliance_posture` is recomputed from its current `demerit_balance` and licence status, and its `version` is incremented. Note what does *not* happen: booth rentals voided or terminated during the closure are not resurrected, and the demerits the hazard inspection assessed are not reversed. Reopening restores the licence, not the history.

### 3.7. Board Review, the Finalize Cascade, and Citation Issuance

Finalizing an inspection is reserved to board staff and is the single most consequential write in the system, because it is where a field observation becomes a regulatory fact. The inspection must be in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and an attempt from any other status is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PENDING_REVIEW` (HTTP 409). What follows is an ordered five-hop cascade, and the order is contractual because hop two pins a value that hop three then invalidates.

**Hop one** moves the inspection `PENDING_REVIEW → FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` from the current logical clock, then runs the final variant of the scoring calculation of §3.5 — writing `repeat_factor_final` and `final_points` on every finding, then `final_adjusted_score` and `final_outcome` on the inspection. **Hop two** snapshots `demerit_balance_before_finalize` from the establishment's `demerit_balance` *before anything in this cascade posts to the ledger*, and then appends a demerit-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_ASSESSMENT` and `delta_points` equal to the positive `final_adjusted_score`, carrying the establishment's and the inspection's identifiers. **Hop three** recomputes the establishment's `demerit_balance` from the demerit ledger, so the snapshot from hop two is now historic — which is exactly the point, as §3.7's fine tiering reads the snapshot rather than the live balance and an establishment is therefore never tiered up by the very inspection being cited.

**Hop four** re-derives the establishment's `license_status` from the new balance and the outcome, in this order. An establishment sitting at `PENDING_INSPECTION` moves `PENDING_INSPECTION → ACTIVE` when `final_outcome` is `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS`; a `FAIL` or an `IMMEDIATE_CLOSURE` leaves it at `PENDING_INSPECTION`, so a facility that has never passed anything stays unlicensed. Then, if the recomputed `demerit_balance` has reached 200.00 points or more, an establishment in `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` moves to the terminal `REVOKED`. Otherwise, if the recomputed balance has reached 100.00 points or more while remaining below 200.00, an establishment in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. Because hop four evaluates the pass-driven promotion before the demerit-driven demotions, a facility can be promoted to `ACTIVE` and then placed on `PROBATION` inside one finalize.

**Hop five** propagates the licence move into personnel. A move to `PROBATION` voids every booth rental of the establishment that is still `PROPOSED`, moving each `PROPOSED → VOIDED`. A move to `REVOKED` is harsher: every rental of the establishment that is `PROPOSED` moves to `VOIDED` and every rental that is `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED`. For each rental so ended whose `practitioner_id` equals the `posted_practitioner_id` of its workstation, that posting is cleared to null, the workstation's `posting_status` falls to `MISSING_POSTING`, and the workstation's `version` is incremented — a cascade three levels deep from a single board decision. Finally the establishment's `compliance_posture`, `open_critical_citation_count`, `outstanding_fine_total_cents` and `delinquent_fine_total_cents` are recomputed, `last_finalized_inspection_at` is set to the inspection's `finalized_at`, and the establishment's `version` and the inspection's `version` are each incremented. A move to `REVOKED` or a hazard closure also drives the posture to `FROZEN`, since a non-operable licence status freezes the posture by definition.

Once an inspection is `FINALIZED`, board staff may issue citations against it. A citation names the `inspection_id` of the finalized visit it derives from, a `subject_type` of either `ESTABLISHMENT` or `PRACTITIONER` declaring who is being cited, the `subject_practitioner_id` of the individual when the subject is a practitioner, and the stale-version token of §2.8. An inspection that is not `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409). The subject must be evidenced by the findings: when `subject_type` is `PRACTITIONER`, the supplied `subject_practitioner_id` must equal the `attributed_practitioner_id` of at least one finding on that inspection, and when `subject_type` is `ESTABLISHMENT`, the inspection must carry at least one finding at all. Either failure is reported as `ERR_CITATION_SUBJECT_NOT_IN_FINDINGS` (HTTP 422). A new citation is minted with a unique, human-readable `citation_number`, opens at `status` of `ISSUED` with `hearing_round_count` at 0, and stamps `issued_at` from the current logical clock.

The fine is then computed, and every input is pinned. The **base** is the sum, over every finding of the citation's inspection walked in `created_at` ascending order with no status filter, of the referenced violation code's `fine_cents_base`. The **surcharge** punishes severity beyond a tolerance: the excess is the greater of zero and the inspection's `final_adjusted_score` minus 30.00 points, and the surcharge is 400 cents — four dollars — per excess point, rounded half up to whole cents. The **demerit tier multiplier** is selected from the snapshot `demerit_balance_before_finalize`, not from the live balance: it is 1.00 when the snapshot is below 20.00 points, 1.25 when the snapshot is at least 20.00 but below 50.00, 1.60 when the snapshot is at least 50.00 but below 200.00, and 2.00 when the snapshot is 200.00 or more. That value is frozen onto the citation as `demerit_tier_multiplier` at creation. The **subject factor** is 1.00 when `subject_type` is `ESTABLISHMENT` and 0.50 when it is `PRACTITIONER`, halving an individual's exposure relative to the business.

The raw amount is the base multiplied by the demerit tier multiplier and then by the subject factor, that product rounded half up to whole cents, with the surcharge added afterwards. `assessed_fine_cents` is that raw amount clamped into the statutory band: never below the floor of 5000 cents and never above the ceiling of 250000 cents. The ceiling clamp is inclusive at the boundary, so a raw amount of exactly 250000 cents passes through untouched while 250001 cents is clamped to 250000. Alongside the fine, `max_finding_severity` is written as the highest `severity` among the inspection's findings under the total order where `MINOR` is lowest, then `MAJOR`, then `CRITICAL`, then `IMMINENT_HAZARD` highest, and is null when the inspection has no findings.

The administrative fee looks circular — the fee scales with what is owed, and what is owed includes the fee — so the evaluation order is pinned and `total_due_cents` must never re-enter the fee lookup. First `assessed_fine_cents` is settled as above. Second, the fee percentage is keyed on `assessed_fine_cents` **alone**: 5 percent when the assessed fine is at most 25000 cents, 8 percent when it is above 25000 and at most 100000 cents, and 12 percent when it is above 100000 cents. Third, `administrative_fee_cents` is the assessed fine multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, `total_due_cents` is `assessed_fine_cents` plus `administrative_fee_cents`. Fifth, a fine-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `CITATION_ASSESSMENT` and `delta_cents` equal to the positive `total_due_cents` is appended against the citation. Sixth, `outstanding_fine_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `delta_cents` over that citation's fine-ledger rows — which, at this instant, equals `total_due_cents`. The citation's two deadlines are then stamped from `issued_at`: `due_at` at 30 days later, which is the boundary the delinquency sweep tests, and `contest_deadline_at` at 20 days later, which is the boundary the hearing right tests. The contest window is deliberately shorter than the payment term, so the right to contest closes while the citation is still `ISSUED` and well before delinquency can move it out of the contestable set.

Issuing a citation also refreshes the establishment's `outstanding_fine_total_cents`, `open_critical_citation_count` and `compliance_posture` and increments its `version`, and refreshes the inspection's `citation_count` and increments its `version` — which is why a second citation off the same visit needs a freshly read version token.

### 3.8. Hearing Rights, Adjudication and Settlement

A cited party has a right to be heard, and the citation lifecycle is deliberately cyclic to accommodate it. Contesting is open to board staff, to the establishment and to the practitioner named, and the request records the `grounds` on which the citation is disputed. A citation may be contested only while its `status` is `ISSUED` or `AMENDED`: `CONTESTED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `AFFIRMED`, `DELINQUENT`, `DISMISSED` and `SETTLED` are all past the contest stage, and an attempt from any of them is refused with `ERR_CITATION_NOT_CONTESTABLE` (HTTP 409). The current logical clock must also be at or before `contest_deadline_at`; the boundary is inclusive, so a contest filed at exactly the deadline instant is accepted, while anything later is refused with `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Contesting is deliberately *not* gated on `hearing_round_count`, so a citation amended in the second round may still be contested and the round cap surfaces later, at scheduling time. A successful contest moves the citation `ISSUED → CONTESTED`, or `AMENDED → CONTESTED` which is the arc that closes the adjudication cycle, and increments its `version`.

Scheduling an adjudication round is board-staff work. The request names the `presiding_officer_user_id` who will hear the matter and a `scheduled_at` instant for which the hearing is calendared, which must be at or after the current logical clock — a hearing cannot be booked into the past, and one that is is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The citation must currently be `CONTESTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CITATION_NOT_CONTESTED` (HTTP 409). The citation's `hearing_round_count` must be strictly below the cap of two rounds; a citation that has already had both is refused with `ERR_HEARING_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409), and its last decision stands for good. The segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10 applies here: the supplied `presiding_officer_user_id` must differ from the `inspector_user_id` of the inspection behind the citation, and a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A successful scheduling creates a hearing at `status` of `SCHEDULED` with `decision`, `amended_fine_cents`, `rationale` and `decided_at` all null and `round_index` set to the citation's `hearing_round_count` plus one — 1 for the first hearing and 2 for the second — then moves the citation `CONTESTED → HEARING_SCHEDULED`, increments `hearing_round_count` by one, and increments the citation's `version`.

Recording the decision is open to the presiding officer and to board staff. The hearing must still be `SCHEDULED`, and a second decision on the same hearing is refused with `ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409); the citation must be at `HEARING_SCHEDULED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must not be the `inspector_user_id` of the inspection behind the citation, so substituting a conflicted decider at the last moment is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Every decision supplies a `decision` of `AFFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `DISMISSED` and a written `rationale`, moves the hearing `SCHEDULED → DECIDED`, and stamps `decided_at` from the current logical clock.

An `AFFIRMED` decision moves the citation `HEARING_SCHEDULED → AFFIRMED` and changes nothing financial: `assessed_fine_cents`, `administrative_fee_cents`, `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_fine_cents` are all left exactly as they were and no ledger entry is posted. The citation's `version` is incremented.

An `AMENDED` decision reduces the fine. It requires `amended_fine_cents`, which must be at least the statutory floor of 5000 cents and at most the citation's current `assessed_fine_cents` — an amendment may lower a fine but never raise one, and a value outside that band is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The supplied figure is recorded on the hearing as `amended_fine_cents` and replaces the citation's `assessed_fine_cents`, after which `administrative_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` are re-derived by steps two through four of the fee computation in §3.7 — meaning the fee percentage is re-keyed on the **new** assessed fine, so an amendment that drops the fine from 120000 to 90000 cents also drops the fee band from 12 percent to 8 percent. A fine-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `CITATION_AMENDMENT_ADJUSTMENT` is appended, its `delta_cents` being the new `total_due_cents` minus the old `total_due_cents` — a negative movement — and `outstanding_fine_cents` is recomputed from the ledger. The citation moves to `AMENDED`, and its `contest_deadline_at` is re-based to the hearing's `decided_at` advanced by 20 days, which is what reopens the contest right for a second round. Demerit points are **not** adjusted by an amendment: only a dismissal reverses demerits.

A `DISMISSED` decision unwinds the assessment entirely, and it cascades. The citation moves `HEARING_SCHEDULED → DISMISSED`, which is terminal. A fine-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `CITATION_DISMISSAL_REVERSAL` is appended whose `delta_cents` is the negative of the citation's `outstanding_fine_cents` immediately before the dismissal, so the citation's fine-ledger rows sum to exactly 0 and `outstanding_fine_cents` returns to 0. A demerit-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `INSPECTION_DEMERIT_REVERSAL` is then appended, carrying the establishment's, the inspection's and the citation's identifiers, with `delta_points` equal to the negative of the inspection's `final_adjusted_score`; where an inspection carries several citations, that reversal is posted once, on the dismissal that leaves no citation of that inspection in a status other than `DISMISSED`. The establishment's `demerit_balance`, `outstanding_fine_total_cents`, `delinquent_fine_total_cents`, `open_critical_citation_count` and `compliance_posture` are all recomputed, and the establishment may move `PROBATION → ACTIVE` when the reduced `demerit_balance` has fallen below 100.00 points and the establishment holds no citation in `DELINQUENT` status. This closes the probation cycle; note that booth rentals voided when probation was imposed are not resurrected. The establishment's `version` is incremented, as are the hearing's and the citation's.

Payment is recorded against the citation's outstanding balance rather than against the fine, and the created ledger entry is what the caller gets back. Board staff, the establishment and the named practitioner may all pay. The request supplies `amount_cents` and may supply a `payer_reference`. A citation whose `status` is already `SETTLED` or `DISMISSED` accepts no further payment and is refused with `ERR_CITATION_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409). The `amount_cents` must be strictly positive and must not exceed the citation's current `outstanding_fine_cents`: overpayment is refused rather than credited, with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422). A valid payment appends a fine-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `CITATION_PAYMENT` and `delta_cents` equal to the negative of `amount_cents`, then recomputes `outstanding_fine_cents`. When the balance reaches exactly 0 the citation moves to `SETTLED` — from `ISSUED`, `AFFIRMED`, `AMENDED` or `DELINQUENT`, whichever it held — and a partial payment leaves the `status` exactly where it was while still reducing the balance. Where the payment settles the last `DELINQUENT` citation of an establishment that is itself `SUSPENDED`, and its `demerit_balance` is below 100.00 points, the establishment moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Every payment refreshes the establishment's `outstanding_fine_total_cents`, `delinquent_fine_total_cents`, `open_critical_citation_count` and `compliance_posture` and increments its `version`, and increments the citation's `version`.

### 3.9. Infection Investigations and the Clock-Driven Sweeps

An infection cluster traced to a salon is a public-health matter with a statutory reporting clock, and the board's obligation begins the moment a report is opened. Board staff, inspectors, the establishment itself and the public-health liaison may all open one, naming the `establishment_id` of the facility where exposure is suspected, a `case_count` of at least 1 giving the number of client infection cases in the cluster, an `onset_date` recording the UTC calendar date of the earliest symptom onset, a non-empty `narrative` describing the cluster, and optionally the `suspected_practitioner_id` of the individual implicated — left null when no individual is. The incident opens at `status` of `REPORTED` with `notified_at`, `health_authority_reference` and `findings_summary` all null, and `reported_at` stamped from the current logical clock.

The notification deadline is derived at once, and the severity of the cluster is what compresses it. A cluster of two or more cases must be reported within 24 hours of `reported_at`; a single-case cluster has 72 hours — three full days. The threshold is inclusive, so a `case_count` of 1 gets the 72-hour window and a `case_count` of 2 gets the 24-hour one. `notification_deadline_at` is `reported_at` advanced by that number of whole hours, with millisecond precision preserved.

Filing the mandated report is available to board staff and to the public-health liaison. The request carries the `health_authority_reference` returned by the authority and may supply an explicit `notified_at`, which otherwise defaults to the current logical clock. The incident must be at `REPORTED` or `ESCALATED_OVERDUE` — an already-notified or resolved incident cannot be notified again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_NOTIFIABLE` (HTTP 409). Both source states move to `NOTIFIED`, both write `notified_at` and `health_authority_reference`, and both increment the incident's `version`; reporting late is still reporting, and it lifts the escalation.

Closing an investigation requires the authority to have been informed first: the incident must be at `NOTIFIED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_NOTIFIED` (HTTP 409). The closer supplies a `resolution` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` together with a `findings_summary` recording the investigator's closing summary, which is written to the incident in both branches, and the incident's `version` is incremented in both branches.

An `UNSUBSTANTIATED` resolution moves the incident `NOTIFIED → RESOLVED_UNSUBSTANTIATED` and stops there: no ledger entry is posted and the suspected practitioner's `sanction_points` is unchanged. A `SUBSTANTIATED` resolution moves the incident `NOTIFIED → RESOLVED_SUBSTANTIATED` and, where `suspected_practitioner_id` is non-null, appends a sanction-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `INFECTION_SANCTION_ASSESSMENT` and `delta_points` of positive 10.00 against that practitioner, then recomputes their `sanction_points`. If the recomputed `sanction_points` has reached 15.00 points or more and the practitioner's `license_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, the licence is suspended automatically: `license_status` moves to `SUSPENDED`, `suspension_cause_points` is snapshotted from the current `sanction_points`, and the suspension carries the same personnel cascade as a manual suspension — every `ACTIVE` booth rental of that practitioner moves to `TERMINATED`, every `PROPOSED` one moves to `VOIDED`, each affected workstation whose `posted_practitioner_id` was that practitioner has the posting cleared to null so `posting_status` becomes `MISSING_POSTING`, and each affected workstation and rental has its `version` incremented. The threshold is inclusive at the boundary: 14.99 accumulated points leave the licence untouched, and 15.00 suspends. Two substantiated incidents therefore do not suspend anybody, but a third does. One thing an infection resolution never does is touch the establishment: it posts sanction points only, never demerit points, so the establishment's `demerit_balance` and `compliance_posture` are entirely unaffected by this workflow.

Advancing the logical clock is a board-staff capability that exists so the board can drive deadlines deterministically, and it runs three sweeps in the same request. The **delinquency sweep** examines every citation whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `AFFIRMED` or `AMENDED` — a `CONTESTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` citation is under adjudication and is exempt — whose `due_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock value, and whose `outstanding_fine_cents` is greater than zero. The strictness matters: a citation whose `due_at` equals the new clock value exactly is not yet delinquent. Each such citation moves to `DELINQUENT`, and a fine-ledger entry with `entry_reason` of `CITATION_LATE_PENALTY` is appended whose `delta_cents` is the citation's `outstanding_fine_cents` multiplied by the late-penalty rate of 10 percent, rounded half up to whole cents, after which `outstanding_fine_cents` is recomputed. Where the newly delinquent citation's `max_finding_severity` is `CRITICAL` or `IMMINENT_HAZARD`, the consequence reaches the licence: an establishment in `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` moves to `SUSPENDED`, its `compliance_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`, every booth rental on its workstations that is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` set to the new clock value, and the establishment's `version` is incremented.

The **infection sweep** moves every incident whose `status` is `REPORTED` and whose `notification_deadline_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock value to `ESCALATED_OVERDUE`; an incident whose deadline equals the new clock value exactly is still on time. The **licence-expiry sweep** moves every practitioner whose `license_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `license_expires_on` is strictly earlier than the UTC calendar date of the new clock value to `LAPSED`. A lapse is gentler than a suspension: the practitioner's `ACTIVE` booth rentals keep their `ACTIVE` status, but every workstation whose posting names them recomputes `posting_status` to `STALE_POSTING`, so the station stops being lawful to work and stops accepting sanitation logs until the licence is reinstated. After the three sweeps run, every affected citation's `outstanding_fine_cents`, every affected establishment's aggregate summary — `demerit_balance`, `outstanding_fine_total_cents`, `delinquent_fine_total_cents`, `open_critical_citation_count`, `compliance_posture` and `foot_spa_biofilm_current` — and every affected workstation's `posting_status` are recomputed, and the `version` of each mutated entity is incremented by one. Note that `foot_spa_biofilm_current` can flip to false purely from the passage of time, with no write against the establishment at all, because the seven-day biofilm currency window is measured against the clock.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the `/cosmetology` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint. All requests must carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1. Endpoints that create a new persistent resource respond with HTTP 201; endpoints that mutate an existing resource, including every sub-resource action endpoint, respond with HTTP 200; every read responds with HTTP 200. Each write returns the resource it created or modified in the `data` field, except for `POST /cosmetology/citations/{id}/payments`, which returns the immutable ledger entry it appended. Path parameters are resolved left to right, and a missing parent is reported before the child is looked at, as described in §2.4.
