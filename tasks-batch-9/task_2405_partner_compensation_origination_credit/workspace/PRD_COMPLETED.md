# Partner Compensation & Origination Credit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every year a law firm's compensation committee has to convert a year of professional activity into a single number per partner, and it has to do so under three different compensation philosophies at once. Some partners are paid on a contractual lockstep rung. Some are paid out of a formula that scores their credited collections, their hours, their firm-building work and the profitability of the matters they carry. Some are paid out of a discretionary pool that the committee itself sizes by ballot. The inputs to all three systems overlap, the origination credit that drives the largest formula weight is routinely contested between partners, and the money has to be paid out in monthly draws long before the year's collections are known. Today this work is done in spreadsheets that nobody can audit, in which a single re-keyed origination percentage silently redistributes several hundred thousand dollars.

This document specifies the Partner Compensation & Origination Credit API, the system of record for that process. It owns the compensation cycle as a staged workflow — intake of origination claims, self-assessment, peer review, committee deliberation, publication of awards, monthly draws, and a year-end true-up that reconciles what was paid against what was finally earned. It owns the origination credit ledger, in which each matter's credit is apportioned across four credit roles — the originating attorney, the billing attorney, the responsible attorney and the working attorneys — under half-open validity windows so that credit can legitimately change hands mid-year without rewriting history. And it owns the governance around all of it: which partner may review whose assessment, which arbiter may decide which dispute, who may read a ballot, and when a published award may still be reconsidered.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as binding rather than aspirational. The first is that every derived figure is reconciled immediately: posting a collection against a matter propagates through the credited-collection split, the partner-level credit index, the formula score and the cycle-level score total before the response is returned, so a read never shows a stale roll-up. The second is that every cash movement is an append to an immutable ledger. Money is never edited; a mistaken draw is reversed by a compensating entry, and a reconsidered cycle is unwound by reversing every draw it paid, so that the firm's undistributed cash is a conserved quantity that an auditor can recompute from the ledger alone. The entire service is mounted under `/comp`, with the single exception of the test-only logical clock described below.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the service: how a caller identifies itself, how responses are shaped, how lists are paged, how numbers are rounded, how concurrent writers are serialized, and in what order competing rejections are decided.

### 2.1. Actors, Authentication Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including the test-only logical-clock request — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined.

The service recognises exactly five role names, spelled exactly as follows: `CycleAdmin`, `Partner`, `CommitteeMember`, `CreditArbiter` and `AuditObserver`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those five, or that is one of them but is not authorised for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Three of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the header value names a persisted partner:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Partner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Partner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Partner` compare that resource's partner-valued field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Partner` row, and that row must additionally carry `is_committee_member` set to true.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CreditArbiter`, `X-User-ID` is again the `id` of the acting `Partner` row, and that row must additionally carry `is_credit_arbiter` set to true.

For all three of those roles the identifier must resolve to a `Partner` row whose `status` is anything other than `DEPARTED`, and the role-specific flag must be set; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_A_PARTNER` (HTTP 403). This check applies on every endpoint whose authorised roles include `Partner`, `CommitteeMember` or `CreditArbiter`.

The remaining two roles are **opaque**:

- When `X-User-Role` is `CycleAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record or log it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check applies to it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AuditObserver`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque identifier not tied to any persisted entity. `AuditObserver` holds no write operation of any kind; it is a read-only oversight role for a firm auditor or outside counsel, and it may never read a committee ballot under any cycle state.

Roles are not hierarchical and are never combined. A partner who sits on the committee and is also a designated arbiter has three distinct role headers available, and each request is evaluated under exactly the one supplied — holding `CommitteeMember` does not confer the plain `Partner` role's claim-filing and self-assessment operations, so such a partner must issue those requests under `X-User-Role` of `Partner` while keeping the same `X-User-ID`. Conversely, segregation of duties is enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on the role: the same partner identifier cannot occupy a ballot's subject seat and cast that ballot, cannot review its own self-assessment, and cannot adjudicate its own claim, no matter which role header is presented.

Ownership is a separate gate from role admission. A partner acting on a self-assessment must be that assessment's `partner_id`; on a peer review, its `reviewer_partner_id`; on a credit claim submission, its `claimant_partner_id`; on a ballot, its `committee_member_id`. A partner creating a contribution record may only create one whose `partner_id` is their own. Any of these mismatches yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List responses are paged through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0. Both must parse as integers, and a `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 or a negative `offset` is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Every list response repeats `limit` and `offset` in the envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the filter before paging, and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific list states otherwise, rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three lists depart from that default: the cycle list is ordered by `fiscal_year` descending and then `created_at` ascending; the matter list is ordered by `opened_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; and the contribution-record list is ordered by `service_period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, each list accepts its own set of optional equality filters, and supplying an unrecognised enum value or an unparseable value for any of them is a malformed request, while a query parameter that the list does not recognise at all is ignored rather than rejected:

- Cycles filter on `state` and `fiscal_year`.
- Partners filter on `compensation_model`, `status`, `practice_group` and `is_committee_member`.
- Matters filter on `client_key`, `billing_attorney_id`, `responsible_attorney_id`, `prior_responsible_attorney_id`, `state` and `matter_type`.
- Credit allocations, whether listed for one partner or for one matter, filter on `credit_role` and `state`.
- Contribution records filter on `partner_id`, `cycle_id`, `matter_id`, `kind`, `category` and `verified`.
- Credit claims filter on `cycle_id`, `matter_id`, `claimant_partner_id`, `disputing_partner_id`, `resolved_prior_originator_id` and `state`.
- Self-assessments filter on `cycle_id`, `partner_id` and `state`.
- Peer reviews filter on `self_assessment_id`, `reviewer_partner_id` and `state`.
- Ballots filter on `cycle_id`, `subject_partner_id`, `committee_member_id` and `state`.
- Awards filter on `cycle_id`, `partner_id`, `award_state` and `payment_state`.
- The cash ledger of one award filters on `kind` and `reversed`.
- Credit transitions filter on `departing_partner_id`, `successor_partner_id`, `matter_id` and `state`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits and read from the logical clock of section 2.5 rather than from the host's wall clock — only the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` comes from the host clock. Wherever an endpoint path contains an identifier segment it is written `{id}` and resolves against the `id` of the resource named by the preceding path segment.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed from that same logical clock on every change to the resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. This holds for compensation cycles, partners, matters, credit allocations, contribution records, credit claims, self-assessments, peer reviews, committee ballots, compensation awards and credit transitions. A single request advances a given row's `version` by at most 1 however many times it touches that row, so a direct write followed by the cascaded recomputation of the same row counts as one mutation, and a row the request itself created stays at `version` 1 even though the cascade immediately derives values onto it.

The one exception is the cash ledger. A draw-payment row is append-only and immutable: it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else of that family — it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and there is no endpoint that edits one. The only way the ledger changes is by appending further rows, which is how draw reversals, true-ups, clawbacks and capital withholdings are expressed.

Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation. When posting a collection against a matter changes a partner's formula score, that partner's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if the partner had been edited directly. This is what makes the stale-version guards of section 2.8 bite on cascades and not only on direct writes.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal comparison in this service reads a logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. Claim and assessment deadlines, the half-open credit-validity windows described in section 2.9, non-billable service periods, departure effective dates, and the stamping of `verified_at`, `submitted_at`, `accepted_at` and `decided_at` all resolve against it.

On service startup the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and that value holds for every request until a `CycleAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the test-only clock endpoint; that endpoint answers 200 OK with a `data` object holding the single field `current_utc` carrying the instant now in force, and a `to_iso8601` that is missing or does not parse as an instant is a malformed request. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and does not retroactively alter any timestamp already stored on a resource. Where a rule speaks of "the current date" it means the calendar date of the logical clock's current instant; where it speaks of "the current instant" it means the full instant.

### 2.6. The Anchor Cycle

Most of a partner's evaluation figures are cycle-scoped, yet the partner resource is not nested under a cycle. The scoping is resolved by a single rule: the **anchor cycle** is the compensation cycle whose `state` is anything other than `CLOSED` and whose `fiscal_year` is the greatest. Because `fiscal_year` is unique across cycles the comparison can never tie; if two rows nevertheless compared equal, the one with the earlier `created_at` would win.

The following partner fields are evaluated against the anchor cycle and against no other: `verified_billable_hours`, `non_billable_points`, `credited_collection_provisional_cents`, `credited_collection_final_cents`, `normalized_credit_index_provisional`, `normalized_credit_index_final`, `blended_realization_pct`, `weighted_profitability_points`, `formula_score_provisional`, `formula_score_final`, `committee_points` and `ytd_draw_paid_cents`.

When no anchor cycle exists — because no cycle has been created yet, or because every cycle has reached `CLOSED` — each of those fields evaluates to zero at its declared precision, which is 0 for the integer fields, 0.00 for the two-decimal fields and 0.0000 for the four-decimal fields, even though historic data still exists behind them. Creating a cycle for a later fiscal year immediately makes it the anchor and re-bases every one of those fields.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Monetary Representation

All money in this service is carried as an integer number of cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in any response body.

The single rounding method used throughout is **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. Where a rule states that rounding happens once at the end of a computation, intermediate steps carry full precision and the rounding is applied exactly once, at the point stated — never step by step.

The declared precisions are as follows. Credit percentages, recorded hours, non-billable points, committee points and per-ballot proposed points are carried to 2 decimal places. Realization, both profitability indices, blended realization, weighted profitability points, formula scores, the applied haircut fraction, the discretionary-pool fraction, the draw fraction and a peer review's composite score are carried to 4 decimal places. The firm normalization factor and the normalized credit indices are carried to 6 decimal places. Monetary values appear in responses as JSON numbers, never as strings. Those precisions bind inbound payloads too, but leniently: a numeric field supplied with more decimal places than its declared precision is rounded half up to that precision on the way in rather than rejected, its declared range having been checked against the value exactly as supplied.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Additive and Capacity-Consuming Writes

Six write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control. Each of them declares a non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of a *parent* resource. If the supplied value does not equal that parent's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is changed. Endpoints not named below take no version field at all.

- Posting billing, collection and cost deltas against a matter requires `expected_matter_version` matching the target matter's current `version`. Financial deltas are additive, so re-posting the same body is legitimate product behaviour on its own terms; the version check is what distinguishes an intended second posting from a re-send made against an out-of-date view of the matter ledger.
- Adjudicating an origination credit claim requires `expected_matter_version` matching the claim's matter's current `version`. Adjudication writes originating credit rows against the matter and consumes part of its 100.00 percent capacity, so an arbiter whose view of the matter has been superseded is working from a stale picture of the remaining capacity and is rejected rather than allowed to oversubscribe it.
- Casting, abstaining or recusing on a committee ballot requires `expected_cycle_version` matching the ballot's cycle's current `version`. Every accepted ballot decision advances the cycle's `version`, so a committee member holding a superseded view of the deliberation point pool fails deterministically.
- Contributing capital to a partner requires `expected_partner_version` matching that partner's current `version`. Contributions are additive, so a second contribution of the same amount legitimately succeeds under the product semantics; the version check is the only thing separating an intended second contribution from a re-send against an out-of-date capital account balance.
- Effectuating a credit transition requires `expected_matter_version` matching the transition's matter's current `version`. Effectuation rewrites the matter's originating allocations and possibly its responsible attorney, and several transitions can legitimately land on the same matter, so an administrator acting on a superseded matter version is rejected rather than allowed to overwrite an allocation layout it never observed.
- Voiding a credit transition requires `expected_matter_version` matching the transition's matter's current `version`. A void restores the departing partner's allocation and the matter's prior responsible attorney; restoring that snapshot onto a matter it no longer describes is refused.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascades and the Half-Open Credit Window

Consistency in this service is immediate, not eventual. Every write that changes an input to a derived figure recomputes that figure, and every figure derived from *it*, before the response is produced. The recomputation chains are long by design: a matter's financial deltas change its realization and profitability, which change the credited collections on every active allocation of that matter, which change each credited partner's credited-collection totals and credit index, which change each partner's formula score, which changes the cycle's total formula score, which changes the provisional target award of every award that a reconsideration reopen has returned to its pre-publication state — that is, an award in award state `PROPOSED` whose `reconsideration_count` is greater than 0. An award created in `PROPOSED` and never yet published is not touched by that last link and keeps its `target_award_cents` of 0 until the publication edge assigns it money. Each link in such a chain is a mutation of the resource it touches, refreshing that resource's `updated_at` and incrementing its `version`. Conversely, a rejected request changes nothing: every field named by a failed operation keeps the value it had before the call.

Credit is time-sliced rather than overwritten. Every credit allocation carries a half-open validity interval running from `credit_from` inclusive to `credit_through` exclusive, where a null `credit_through` means the interval is still open-ended. Two intervals that merely share an endpoint — one ending on the date the next begins — do not overlap, and this is what lets origination credit move from one partner to another mid-year without either interval being deleted.

Two conserved quantities are worth calling out because the compensating operations in section 3 exist to preserve them. On a matter, `origination_pct_assigned` — the total originating percentage in force — must return to its prior value when a credit transition is voided. On a cycle, `undistributed_cash_cents` must return to the full distributable pool when a published cycle is reopened and its draws are reversed.

### 2.10. Reference Resolution Order

Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. The resulting 404 carries the code belonging to that identifier's own resource type: `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BALLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_FOUND`. A request for a credit allocation under a matter that does not exist therefore reports the missing matter, not the missing allocation.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after the path and are checked in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields, again returning the not-found code of the first unresolvable reference. This applies to matter creation, contribution-record creation, claim creation, self-assessment creation, peer-review assignment and credit-transition effectuation, and the codes reachable this way are `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATTER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.11. The Validation Precedence Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is determined by the rule that sits highest on the following ladder. The ladder is total and it is the same on every endpoint; a check lower down is never allowed to mask a check higher up.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, on every endpoint under `/comp` and on the logical-clock endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. **Role admission.** A recognised role name that is authorised for this endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403.
3. **Actor resolution.** For endpoints open to `Partner`, `CommitteeMember` or `CreditArbiter`, the acting identifier resolves to a non-departed partner carrying the flag the role requires — `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_A_PARTNER`, 403.
4. **Payload schema.** On every endpoint that takes a body or query parameters: required fields present, declared types honoured, enum values inside their declared sets, numeric values inside their declared ranges, dates well formed, `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` not negative. Every free-text field carries a minimum length of 1, so a string supplied as the empty string fails here whether the field was required or optional. Unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422.
5. **Path identifiers**, resolved left to right as described in section 2.10 — the relevant 404.
6. **Body foreign keys**, resolved in declared field order as described in section 2.10 — the relevant 404.
7. **Ownership.** On self-assessment submission, peer-review decision, claim submission, ballot decision and contribution-record creation — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403.
8. **Ballot confidentiality.** On the two ballot reads — `ERR_BALLOT_CONFIDENTIAL`, 403.
9. **State of the acted-on row.** On every action endpoint, the row must currently be in a state from which the requested transition exists. Depending on the resource this surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_DRAFT`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DISPUTABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICABLE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_MUTABLE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_MATTER_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_DRAWABLE`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_TRUE_UP_READY`, `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_PENDING` or `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_VOIDABLE`, each 409.
10. **State of related entities.** On ballot decisions, credit-transition effectuation, credit-transition voiding, contribution-record creation and draw runs, the related rows must permit the operation: `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_DEPARTING`, `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_MATTER_NOT_OPEN` or `ERR_AWARD_NOT_DRAWABLE`, each 409.
11. **Stale parent version.** On the six guarded writes of section 2.8 — `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, 412.
12. **Deadline and stage windows.** On claim submission, claim dispute, assessment submission, assessment review decision and peer-review decision — `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` or `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`, each 409.
13. **Segregation of duties.** On ballot decisions, peer-review assignment, peer-review decisions, assessment review decisions, claim adjudication and claim dispute — `ERR_SELF_VOTE_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_ARBITER_CONFLICT` or `ERR_DISPUTER_IS_CLAIMANT`, each 403.
14. **Claim basis against client history.** On claim submission — `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_NEW`, 409.
15. **Interval, uniqueness and cap constraints.** On cycle creation, claim creation, claim adjudication, self-assessment creation, peer-review assignment, contribution-record creation, ballot decisions and draw runs — `ERR_CREDIT_INTERVAL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`, `ERR_DRAW_PERIOD_ALREADY_PAID`, `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, `ERR_POINTS_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSESSMENT` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_ASSIGNMENT`, each 409.
16. **Aggregate completeness gates on cycle advancement** — `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CLAIMS`, `ERR_ASSESSMENTS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_PEER_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE` or `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, each 409.
17. **Cash and capital closure on cycle advancement** — `ERR_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_REOPENABLE` or `ERR_AWARDS_NOT_SETTLED`, each 409.

Three collisions are common enough in practice to be worth stating as worked cases. A cycle sitting in `AWARDS_PUBLISHED` with unsettled awards that is asked to advance straight to `CLOSED` violates both the missing transition edge (rung 9) and the settlement gate (rung 17); the response is `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_TRANSITION`. A committee member who casts their own ballot with proposed points above the per-ballot cap violates both segregation of duties (rung 13) and the cap constraint (rung 15); the response is `ERR_SELF_VOTE_FORBIDDEN`. A claim asserting a brand-new client for a client that already has a sibling matter, submitted after the cycle's claim deadline has passed, violates both the window (rung 12) and the client-history rule (rung 14); the response is `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

### 2.12. Confidentiality of Committee Deliberation

Committee ballots are the most sensitive records in the system and their read rules are correspondingly narrow. Only `CycleAdmin` and `CommitteeMember` may read a ballot at all; the three other roles are simply not authorised for the ballot reads and are turned away at rung 2 of the ladder, which is why an `AuditObserver` can never see a ballot no matter what state its cycle is in.

Within that admission, a `CommitteeMember` may read only ballots whose `committee_member_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and when listing ballots must scope the list to their own `committee_member_id`. Once a cycle has closed, its `confidentiality_locked` flag becomes true and even that narrow window shuts: from then on only `CycleAdmin` may read any ballot of that cycle. On the ballot list that lock bites in two different ways for a `CommitteeMember` — naming a closed cycle in the `cycle_id` filter is refused outright, while listing without that filter simply omits the closed cycles' ballots from the page instead of failing. A read that breaches either restriction is rejected with `ERR_BALLOT_CONFIDENTIAL` (HTTP 403).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Partner Roster: Admission, Standing and Departure

A `CycleAdmin` admits an equity partner by supplying a `full_name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `practice_group` drawn from `LITIGATION`, `CORPORATE`, `TAX`, `IP` and `REGULATORY`, a `compensation_model` of `LOCKSTEP`, `FORMULA` or `DISCRETIONARY`, and an `admission_year` between 1950 and 2100 inclusive. Four optional flags shape how the partner participates: `lockstep_step` (an integer rung from 0 through 12, defaulting to 0), `is_committee_member` and `is_credit_arbiter` (both defaulting to false), and `evaluation_required` (defaulting to true, and set to false for the committee-only senior partners who vote but are not themselves scored this year). Two optional capital figures complete the record: `opening_capital_cents`, the balance carried into the cycle, and `target_capital_cents`, the balance the partnership agreement requires — both are integers of at least 0 and both default to 0. Admission returns 201 Created.

The lockstep rung and the compensation model are cross-checked at admission: a partner whose `compensation_model` is `LOCKSTEP` must carry a `lockstep_step` of at least 1, and a partner on either other model must carry a `lockstep_step` of exactly 0. A violation of either half of that rule is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Newly admitted partners begin with `status` of `ACTIVE` and `departure_effective_on` null, and their `capital_account_balance_cents` is seeded to exactly the `opening_capital_cents` supplied. Thereafter the capital balance is the sum of three things: that opening figure, every voluntary capital contribution recorded for the partner, and every ledger row of kind `CAPITAL_WITHHOLDING` posted against one of the partner's awards. A `LOCKSTEP` partner admitted with `evaluation_required` true immediately joins the firm's contractual lockstep obligation, which is the first bucket of the pool waterfall described in section 3.8.

Two counters on the partner record are worth reading as invariants rather than as stored data. `responsible_matter_count` is the number of matters currently in `OPEN` whose `responsible_attorney_id` is this partner — it rises when a matter opens under them, falls when that matter closes or is written off, and moves in both directions when a departure reassigns responsibility. `ytd_draw_paid_cents` is the signed sum of the `amount_cents` of every cash-ledger row of kind `SCHEDULED_DRAW` or `DRAW_REVERSAL` across all of the partner's awards in the anchor cycle, which is why reversing a draw pulls it back down rather than leaving a gross-of-reversals total behind.

Departure begins when a `CycleAdmin` records notice, moving the partner from `ACTIVE` to `NOTICE_GIVEN` and stamping the supplied `departure_effective_on`. Notice may only be given for a partner whose `status` is currently `ACTIVE`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_DEPARTING`; the effective date must fall on or after the logical clock's current date, and an earlier date is rejected as a malformed request. The interesting part of notice is what it stages. For every matter in state `OPEN` on which the departing partner holds a credit allocation whose `credit_role` is `ORIGINATING` and whose `state` is `ACTIVE`, the system creates one credit transition in `PROPOSED`, processing the matters in order of `opened_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Each staged transition records `departing_partner_id`, `matter_id`, and both `transferred_pct` and `prior_credit_pct` set to that allocation's current `credit_pct`; `successor_partner_id` and `effective_on` stay null while the transition remains `PROPOSED`. Recording notice increments the partner's `version`.

Effectuating a staged transition drives it from `PROPOSED` to `EFFECTIVE` and is the operation that actually moves credit. The administrator names a `successor_partner_id`, an `effective_on` date and the `expected_matter_version` required by section 2.8. A transition that is not currently `PROPOSED` is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_PENDING`; a departing partner whose `status` is not `NOTICE_GIVEN` yields `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_DEPARTING`; a successor whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`, or who is the departing partner themselves, yields `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; and an `effective_on` that is not strictly later than the departing originating allocation's `credit_from` — or, when that partner no longer holds an `ACTIVE` originating allocation on the matter, not strictly later than the matter's `opened_on` — is rejected as a malformed request, since a transfer cannot take effect before the credit it transfers began. That one date comparison is deliberately deferred: it is made only after the three state checks just listed and after the stale-version guard, so a request carrying both a superseded `expected_matter_version` and an impossible `effective_on` reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` rather than the malformed-request code that rung 4 of the ladder would otherwise dictate. On success the transition records its `successor_partner_id` and `effective_on` and moves to `EFFECTIVE`. The departing partner's `ORIGINATING` allocation moves from `ACTIVE` to `TRANSFERRED` with its `credit_through` set to `effective_on`, and a fresh `ORIGINATING` allocation opens for the successor at `credit_pct` equal to `transferred_pct`, with `credit_from` equal to `effective_on`, `credit_through` null and `source` of `TRANSITION`. In the same operation the matter's `prior_responsible_attorney_id` is set to its current `responsible_attorney_id` so that the arrangement can be undone exactly; and when the departing partner was in fact the responsible attorney, `responsible_attorney_id` becomes the successor, the departing partner's `RESPONSIBLE` allocation is superseded, and a `RESPONSIBLE` allocation opens for the successor at `credit_pct` of 100.00 with `credit_from` equal to `effective_on`. The matter's `origination_pct_assigned` is then recomputed and its `version` increments, which in turn recomputes `credited_collection_provisional_cents` and `credited_collection_final_cents` on every `ACTIVE` allocation of the matter, then both partners' `credited_collection_provisional_cents`, `credited_collection_final_cents` and `responsible_matter_count`, then their `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final`, then the cycle's `total_formula_score`, and finally `target_award_cents` on every award that a reconsideration reopen has returned to award state `PROPOSED` — one whose `reconsideration_count` is greater than 0 — a never-yet-published award sitting in `PROPOSED` being left at its `target_award_cents` of 0. The transition's own `version` increments. Should the resulting originating intervals on the matter overlap to more than 100.00 percent, the effectuation is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_INTERVAL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`.

Voiding a transition is the compensating action, and it has two distinct shapes depending on where it starts. Both require a `void_reason` — absent, the request is malformed — and both require the `expected_matter_version`; a transition already in `VOIDED` cannot be voided again and yields `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_VOIDABLE`. Voiding from `PROPOSED` is deliberately inert on the credit ledger: it records `void_reason`, moves the transition to `VOIDED`, touches no allocation at all because none was ever moved, and increments both the matter's `version` and the transition's own `version`. Voiding from `EFFECTIVE` reverses everything effectuation did. The successor's `ORIGINATING` allocation created by the transition is superseded, and where one was opened the successor's `RESPONSIBLE` allocation is superseded too — in both cases the row moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `credit_through` set to `effective_on`. The departing partner's `TRANSFERRED` originating row and superseded responsible row are restored to `ACTIVE` with `credit_pct` set back to `prior_credit_pct` and `credit_through` cleared to null. The matter's `responsible_attorney_id` is restored from `prior_responsible_attorney_id`, that snapshot is then cleared, `origination_pct_assigned` is recomputed back to exactly its pre-effectuation value, and the matter's `version` increments; the credited-collection chain then re-runs down through both partners' totals, their `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final`, and the cycle's `total_formula_score`. The transition records `void_reason`, moves to `VOIDED` and increments its `version`.

A partner completes the edge `NOTICE_GIVEN → DEPARTED` and reaches that terminal status not by an explicit call but as a consequence of effectuating or voiding a transition: each of those two operations, and only those two, re-tests the departing partner, and once no credit transition naming them as departing partner remains in `PROPOSED` — that is, once every one has reached `EFFECTIVE` or `VOIDED` — their `status` becomes `DEPARTED` and their `version` increments. Because the test fires nowhere else, a partner whose notice staged no transition at all is never promoted and simply stays in `NOTICE_GIVEN`. A departed partner is dropped from the cycle's `total_formula_score` and from the lockstep obligation bucket of the pool waterfall, although their own stored `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final` remain readable on the partner record.

Credit allocations therefore have a small lifecycle of their own, driven entirely by these system events rather than by any direct write. An allocation moves from `ACTIVE` to `TRANSFERRED` when the transition covering it is effectuated, taking `credit_through` from the transition's `effective_on`; it moves back from `TRANSFERRED` to `ACTIVE` when that transition is voided, restoring `credit_pct` from `prior_credit_pct` and clearing `credit_through` to null; and it moves from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` when its matter is written off or when a void unwinds the successor row that created it, taking `credit_through` from the logical clock's current date. Each of those moves increments the allocation's `version`.

### 3.2. Matters, Client History and Matter-Level Economics

A matter is opened by a `CycleAdmin` with a `client_key` of 1 to 64 characters — compared case-sensitively, and shared verbatim by every matter belonging to the same client — a human-readable `client_display_name`, a `matter_type` of `NEW_CLIENT`, `EXPANDED_MATTER`, `CROSS_SELL` or `EXISTING`, a `practice_group` drawn from the same five groups partners belong to, an `opened_on` calendar date, and the two attorney assignments `billing_attorney_id` and `responsible_attorney_id`. The matter begins in state `OPEN`, its `prior_responsible_attorney_id` and `written_off_reason` both null, and creation returns 201 Created.

Opening a matter seeds two of the four credit roles immediately and leaves the other two to be earned. A `BILLING` allocation is created for the billing attorney and a `RESPONSIBLE` allocation for the responsible attorney, each at `credit_pct` of 100.00, each with `source` of `DEFAULT_BILLING`, `credit_from` equal to the matter's `opened_on`, `credit_through` null and `state` of `ACTIVE`. No `ORIGINATING` allocation and no `WORKING` allocation is created here: origination credit arrives only through claim adjudication, and working credit only through hour verification. Creation also increments `responsible_matter_count` on the responsible attorney, and it computes `is_first_matter_for_client`, which is true when no other matter shares this `client_key` at an earlier position under an ordering of `opened_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending — in practice, whether any other non-written-off matter for the same client already exists. The flag is evaluated once, at creation, and is never revisited when sibling matters are later opened, closed or written off. That flag is the gate on a claim asserting a genuinely new client.

The matter's money arrives as signed deltas rather than as absolute figures. A posting supplies `billed_delta_cents`, `collected_delta_cents` and `direct_cost_delta_cents` together with the `expected_matter_version` required by section 2.8, and each delta is added to the running `billed_cents`, `collected_cents` and `direct_cost_cents` respectively. A posting that would drive any of those three accumulators below zero is rejected as a malformed request, and a posting against a matter that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_MATTER_NOT_OPEN`.

Each accepted posting immediately re-derives the matter's economics. **Realization** is the collected fraction of what was billed: `realization_pct` is `collected_cents` divided by `billed_cents`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and it is 0.0000 whenever `billed_cents` is 0 rather than being a division error. **Profitability** is computed twice, under two variants that differ only in whether attorney overhead is charged against the engagement, and both results are stored on every matter so that downstream scoring can pick one without recomputing. Under the contribution-margin variant, `profitability_index_contribution` is `collected_cents` less `direct_cost_cents`, divided by `collected_cents`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Under the fully-loaded variant, the same numerator is reduced further by an overhead charge of 9,000 cents — 90 dollars — for each of the matter's `verified_billable_hours`, giving `profitability_index_fully_loaded` as `collected_cents` less `direct_cost_cents` less that overhead amount, divided by `collected_cents`, again rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Both indices are 0.0000 whenever `collected_cents` is 0, even when direct costs have been posted; both may legitimately be negative and neither is floored at zero; and when a matter has no verified billable hours the two variants are numerically identical. Only the fully-loaded variant feeds partner scoring. The matter's own `verified_billable_hours` is the sum, to 2 decimal places, of the `hours` on every verified billable contribution record pointing at this matter.

Re-deriving those three figures is the head of the longest cascade in the service: the credited collections on every `ACTIVE` allocation of the matter are recomputed, which recomputes each credited partner's `credited_collection_provisional_cents`, `credited_collection_final_cents`, `normalized_credit_index_provisional`, `normalized_credit_index_final`, `blended_realization_pct`, `weighted_profitability_points`, `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final`, and finally the anchor cycle's `total_formula_score`.

A matter's lifecycle is driven by a `target_state`, and each of its three edges changes which calculation variant is allowed to see the matter. Closing a matter moves it `OPEN → CLOSED`; it recomputes `realization_pct` and both profitability indices, brings the matter into the final collected-basis input set — which recomputes `credited_collection_final_cents` on every `ACTIVE` allocation of the matter and from there each credited partner's `credited_collection_final_cents` and `formula_score_final` — decrements `responsible_matter_count` on the responsible attorney, and increments the matter's `version`. Reopening a matter moves it `CLOSED → OPEN`; it removes the matter from the final collected-basis input set and recomputes `credited_collection_final_cents` on every `ACTIVE` allocation, increments `responsible_matter_count` on the responsible attorney again, and increments the matter's `version`. Because reopening rewrites a basis that year-end settlement has already consumed, it is additionally refused with `ERR_AWARD_NOT_DRAWABLE` when any award of the anchor cycle has already reached payment state `TRUED_UP` or `SETTLED`. Writing a matter off moves it `OPEN → WRITTEN_OFF`, requires a `written_off_reason` — absent, the request is malformed — and is the harshest of the three: `collected_cents` is left as it stands but the matter is excluded from both the provisional accrual basis and the final collected basis, every credit allocation on the matter is superseded with `state` set to `SUPERSEDED` and `credit_through` set to the logical clock's current date, that supersession recomputes `credited_collection_provisional_cents` and `credited_collection_final_cents` for every affected partner, `responsible_matter_count` on the responsible attorney decrements because the counter admits only `OPEN` matters, and the matter's `version` increments. All three edges require the matter to be in the state the edge starts from and otherwise report `ERR_MATTER_NOT_OPEN`, and `WRITTEN_OFF` is terminal.

### 3.3. Contribution Records, Verification and Non-Billable Weighting

Time enters the system as a contribution record, which either books client-billable hours against a matter or books a non-billable firm contribution against no matter at all. The creator supplies `partner_id`, `cycle_id`, a `kind` of `BILLABLE` or `NON_BILLABLE`, a `category`, an `hours` figure of at least 0.00 carried to 2 decimal places, and the two ends of a closed service interval, `service_period_start` and `service_period_end`, the latter of which must not fall before the former. `matter_id` is required when `kind` is `BILLABLE` and must be omitted entirely when `kind` is `NON_BILLABLE`; `standard_rate_cents` must be greater than 0 when `kind` is `BILLABLE` and must be either 0 or omitted otherwise, defaulting to 0. The `category` is one of `CLIENT_WORK`, `FIRM_MANAGEMENT`, `RECRUITING`, `PRO_BONO` or `TRAINING`, and `CLIENT_WORK` is used if and only if the `kind` is `BILLABLE`. Any of those conditional-field rules broken makes the request malformed. Creation returns 201 Created, sets `standard_value_cents` to `hours` multiplied by `standard_rate_cents` rounded half up for billable rows and to 0 for non-billable rows, and leaves `verified` false with `verified_at` null.

Both a `CycleAdmin` and a `Partner` may file a record, but a partner may only file one whose `partner_id` is their own — otherwise the ownership rule rejects the request. A billable record additionally requires the referenced matter to be in state `OPEN`, and a record pointing at a closed or written-off matter is refused with `ERR_MATTER_NOT_OPEN`.

Non-billable records are held apart in time to stop the same firm-building week being booked twice under different guises. For any single pairing of `partner_id` and `category` — taken across every one of that partner's non-billable records in that category, whatever cycle each belongs to and whether or not it has been verified — two non-billable closed service windows must be separated by more than one calendar day: a window that overlaps an existing one, merely touches it end to start, or leaves a gap of only one day, is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The separation is counted in clear days, so the new window is admitted only when its `service_period_start` falls at least three calendar days after the existing window's `service_period_end`, or its `service_period_end` falls at least three calendar days before the existing window's `service_period_start`; the earliest legal start against an existing window ending on a given date is therefore the third day after that date.

Nothing a partner records counts until a `CycleAdmin` verifies it. Verification sets `verified` to true and `verified_at` to the logical clock's current instant; a record whose `verified` is already true cannot be verified twice and the second attempt yields `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_VERIFIED` with HTTP 409. Verification is what admits the record to every input set that scoring reads, and its cascade differs by kind. A verified billable record raises the partner's `verified_billable_hours` and the matter's `verified_billable_hours`, and it upserts the `WORKING` credit allocation for that pairing of matter and partner — `source` of `WORKING_DERIVED`, `credit_from` equal to the matter's `opened_on`, `credit_through` null — after which `credit_pct` is recomputed on *every* working allocation of that matter as 100 multiplied by that partner's verified billable hours on the matter, divided by the matter's total `verified_billable_hours`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. When that matter total is 0 the recomputation is skipped rather than dividing by zero and every working allocation keeps the `credit_pct` it already carried, so a row the verification has just created stands at 0.00. The matter's changed hour total then re-derives `profitability_index_fully_loaded`, which re-derives each credited partner's `weighted_profitability_points`, which re-derives `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final` and finally the cycle's `total_formula_score`. A verified non-billable record instead re-derives the partner's `non_billable_points`, and from there the same score chain.

Non-billable hours are not counted at face value; they are weighted, capped, discounted and finally scaled by the partner's compensation model, and the whole computation collapses into a single rounding at the very end. The steps run in this pinned order.

First, hours are gathered per category across the partner's verified non-billable records in the anchor cycle and each category total is capped: firm management is capped at 300 hours, recruiting at 150, pro bono at 200 and training at 100. Hours above a cap are silently discarded rather than spilling into another category.

Second, a weighted base is formed from the *capped* hours by multiplying each category's capped hours by its weight — 1.50 for firm management, 1.00 for recruiting, 1.25 for pro bono and 0.75 for training — and summing the four products. The sum of the four capped hour figures is retained as the capped total.

Third, a diminishing return is applied when the capped total is strictly greater than 400 hours. The hours in excess of 400 are multiplied by the base's average weight — the weighted base divided by the capped total — and by one half, and that product is subtracted from the base. Note that the average weight is deliberately taken from the capped weighted base rather than from raw hours, so raising an uncapped category changes the discount that lands on every other category; and note that a capped total of exactly 400 hours attracts no discount at all, because the comparison is strict. Because the largest single cap is 300 hours, no one category can ever reach the threshold by itself.

Fourth, a pro bono floor is enforced on the whole base rather than on the pro bono component alone: if the partner's capped pro bono hours are strictly below 50, the entire base is multiplied by 0.90. Exactly 50 capped pro bono hours attracts no penalty.

Fifth, the base is multiplied by the partner's compensation-model multiplier — 0.60 for `LOCKSTEP`, 1.00 for `FORMULA` and 1.20 for `DISCRETIONARY` — and only then is the result rounded half up to 2 decimal places and stored as `non_billable_points`. Steps one through four carry full precision; rounding after each step instead of once at the end drifts the result by as much as 0.04 points for a partner active in all four categories. A partner with no verified non-billable record at all has a base of 0, breaches the pro bono floor, and ends at 0.00 points whatever their model.

### 3.4. Origination Credit: Claims, Disputes and Adjudication

Origination credit is the largest single weight in the formula score and is consequently the most contested figure in the firm, so it is awarded through an adversarial process rather than by assertion. A `Partner` files a claim against a `cycle_id` and a `matter_id`, asserting a `claim_basis` of `NEW_CLIENT`, `EXPANDED_MATTER` or `CROSS_SELL`, a `requested_pct` between 0.01 and 100.00 carried to 2 decimal places, and a written `narrative` justifying it. The claimant is never taken from the payload: `claimant_partner_id` is set from `X-User-ID`. The claim is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` and `remand_count` both 0, and a partner may hold at most one claim per pairing of cycle, matter and claimant — a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`. Creation returns 201 Created.

Submitting a claim advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and closes the door on quiet editing. A claim that is not in `DRAFT` yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_DRAFT`. The claim's cycle must be in stage `OPEN_INTAKE` and the logical clock's current date must fall on or before that cycle's `claim_deadline_on`; either failure is `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Only once the window has been cleared is the asserted basis tested against the client's actual matter history, and that test has three arms, all of which report `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_NEW`: a basis of `NEW_CLIENT` requires that no *other* matter with the same `client_key` and a state other than `WRITTEN_OFF` exists; a basis of `EXPANDED_MATTER` or `CROSS_SELL` requires that at least one such sibling matter *does* exist; and a `CROSS_SELL` basis additionally requires the claimed matter's `practice_group` to differ from the `practice_group` of the client's earliest sibling matter, since selling more of the same practice into an existing client is an expansion rather than a cross-sell. A submitted claim is what blocks its cycle from leaving `OPEN_INTAKE`.

Any other partner may challenge a submitted claim, driving it `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` by supplying the `contested_pct` they claim for themselves and written `dispute_grounds`. The challenge records `disputing_partner_id` from `X-User-ID`. A claim that is not in `SUBMITTED` yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DISPUTABLE`; a partner attempting to dispute their own claim is refused with `ERR_DISPUTER_IS_CLAIMANT`; and the cycle must still be in `OPEN_INTAKE`, failing which the window error applies.

Adjudication belongs exclusively to a `CreditArbiter`, and it is the only operation in the service with four distinct outcomes selected by a `decision` field spelled `ALLOCATE`, `SPLIT`, `REJECT` or `REMAND`. `approved_pct` is required on `ALLOCATE` and `SPLIT`, `counterparty_pct` on `SPLIT` alone, both between 0.00 and 100.00 at 2 decimal places and both ignored on the branches that do not consume them, while `remand_reason` is optional even on `REMAND` — a remand supplied without one records null there. Whatever the decision, the arbiter must supply the `expected_matter_version` required by section 2.8, and no arbiter may decide a claim in which they are the claimant, the disputer, or the claimed matter's billing or responsible attorney — such a request is refused with `ERR_ARBITER_CONFLICT`. A claim that is in neither `SUBMITTED` nor `DISPUTED` is not adjudicable at all, and a `SPLIT` or `REMAND` aimed at a claim that is not `DISPUTED` is likewise not adjudicable; both report `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICABLE`.

An **allocate** decision drives the claim to `ALLOCATED` from either `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`. The arbiter supplies the `approved_pct` awarded to the claimant side, and the sharing convention below decides how much of it the claimant actually keeps and who receives the complement; `resolved_prior_originator_id` is written with the counterparty the convention selected, or left null when the convention gave the claimant the whole amount. The originating allocations the convention dictates are created — the claimant's row with `source` of `CLAIM_ALLOCATED` and the prior originator's row, where one exists, with `source` of `SHARING_CONVENTION`, both taking `credit_from` from the matter's `opened_on` with `credit_through` null — while the `BILLING` and `RESPONSIBLE` rows seeded at matter creation are left entirely untouched. The claim's `counterparty_pct` is *not* written on this branch; it remains null, and the complement is expressed only as the prior originator's allocation percentage. Should the resulting originating interval sum on the matter exceed 100.00 percent, the whole decision is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_INTERVAL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. On success the matter's `origination_pct_assigned` is recomputed and its `version` increments, which recomputes `credited_collection_provisional_cents` and `credited_collection_final_cents` on every `ACTIVE` allocation of the matter and then on every credited partner, which recomputes `formula_score_provisional`, `formula_score_final` and the cycle's `total_formula_score`, and finally `target_award_cents` on every award that a reconsideration reopen has returned to award state `PROPOSED` — one whose `reconsideration_count` is greater than 0 — a never-yet-published award sitting in `PROPOSED` being left at its `target_award_cents` of 0. The claim's own `version` increments.

A **split** decision is available only from `DISPUTED` and drives the claim to `ALLOCATED` while bypassing the sharing convention completely. The arbiter supplies both `approved_pct` for the claimant and `counterparty_pct` for the disputer, and both are written verbatim onto the claim. Their sum must not exceed 100.00 and the resulting originating interval sum on the matter must not exceed 100.00 either; a breach of either is `ERR_CREDIT_INTERVAL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. One originating allocation is created for `claimant_partner_id` at `approved_pct` with `source` of `CLAIM_ALLOCATED` and one for `disputing_partner_id` at `counterparty_pct` with `source` of `SHARING_CONVENTION`. Note the deliberate divergence from the allocate branch: a split never consults the sharing convention and never writes `resolved_prior_originator_id`, which stays null. The same cascade follows — `origination_pct_assigned` and the matter's `version`, then credited collections on every active allocation of the matter and on both partners, then `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final` and the cycle's `total_formula_score` — and the claim's `version` increments.

A **reject** decision drives the claim to the terminal `REJECTED` from either `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`. It sets `approved_pct` to 0.00 and creates no allocation for the claimant. It does, however, install a fallback so that no matter is left with unassigned origination credit: when the matter holds no credit allocation whose `credit_role` is `ORIGINATING` and whose `state` is `ACTIVE`, one is created for the matter's `billing_attorney_id` at `credit_pct` of 100.00 with `source` of `DEFAULT_BILLING`. The matter's `version` increments and so does the claim's.

A **remand** decision is the negotiation loop, available only from `DISPUTED` and returning the claim to `DRAFT` so the parties can try again. It is bounded: a claim whose `remand_count` has already reached 2 may not be remanded a third time, and the attempt reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICABLE`. On a permitted remand the dispute is unwound — `disputing_partner_id`, `contested_pct` and `dispute_grounds` are all cleared — the arbiter's `remand_reason` is recorded, `revision_count` and `remand_count` each increment by 1, and the claim's `state` returns to `DRAFT`. No credit allocation is created on this branch; the matter's `version` increments and so does the claim's. The full loop is therefore `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → DRAFT`, traversable at most twice before the claim must be allocated, split or rejected.

The sharing convention that an allocate decision applies asks a single question: does someone already hold origination credit for this client, and if so how much of the new award do they keep? The claimant's share of the arbiter's `approved_pct` depends on the asserted basis — a genuinely new client is not shared at all and the claimant keeps 100.00 percent of it, an expanded matter for an existing client is shared 60/40 in the claimant's favour, and a cross-sell into a different practice group is shared 50/50. Concretely, the claimant's percentage is `approved_pct` multiplied by that share fraction (100.00, 60.00 or 50.00 percent respectively) and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the counterparty's percentage is obtained by subtracting the claimant's percentage from `approved_pct` and rounding half up to 2 decimal places, so that the two shares always re-sum to `approved_pct` exactly. An `approved_pct` of 51.00 therefore yields 25.50 to each side under a cross-sell, and 30.60 to the claimant against 20.40 to the counterparty under an expanded matter.

Selecting *who* the counterparty is runs over the client's other matters. The candidate set is every matter sharing the claimed matter's `client_key`, excluding the claimed matter itself and excluding any matter in `WRITTEN_OFF`; from that set the earliest matter is chosen by `opened_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. If the set is empty there is nobody to share with: `resolved_prior_originator_id` is null and the claimant takes the full `approved_pct` with a counterparty share of 0.00 — which is what happens to an expanded-matter claim whose sibling matters have all been written off. If an earliest sibling exists, the convention looks at that sibling's credit allocations whose `credit_role` is `ORIGINATING` and whose `state` is `ACTIVE` and picks the holder of the largest `credit_pct`, breaking ties in favour of the earlier `created_at`; that holder's `partner_id` becomes the resolved prior originator. If the earliest sibling has no active originating allocation at all, the fallback is that sibling's `billing_attorney_id`. And in the case where the resolved prior originator turns out to be the claimant themselves, no counterparty allocation is created and the claimant keeps the full `approved_pct`.

The 100.00 percent ceiling on origination credit is an interval-overlap constraint rather than a simple sum. Only allocations whose `credit_role` is `ORIGINATING` and whose `state` is `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFERRED` participate — a `SUPERSEDED` row is ignored entirely — and their `credit_pct` values are summed across every set of half-open validity windows that overlap in time, the sum being evaluated at each participating window's `credit_from` date, which is where any overlapping set first reaches its maximum; if any such overlapping set would exceed 100.00 on a matter, the write that would create it is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_INTERVAL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. Two windows that merely share an endpoint do not overlap, which is precisely why a transferred allocation ending on the successor's start date coexists with it. The matter's `origination_pct_assigned` reports the total `credit_pct` currently in force across originating allocations in `ACTIVE`, and it is the conserved figure that an effectuate-then-void pair must leave unchanged.

### 3.5. Credited Collections and the Formula-Model Score

A matter's credited collection basis is split first across the four credit roles and then, within each role, across that role's active allocations. The role weights are fixed for the firm: origination takes 0.40 of the basis, billing 0.20, responsibility 0.15 and working 0.25. Only allocations in `state` of `ACTIVE` participate; `TRANSFERRED` and `SUPERSEDED` rows contribute nothing and receive nothing.

The split proceeds in two rounding layers and the layering is load-bearing. In the first layer, each of the four role pools is computed as the basis multiplied by that role's weight and rounded half up to whole cents. Then any of the billing, responsibility and working roles that has no active allocation folds its entire pool into the origination pool and is left at 0 — origination itself never folds. Finally the first layer's rounding residual, the basis less the sum of the four pools, is added to the origination pool, so the four pools sum to the basis exactly. In the second layer, each role's pool is distributed across that role's active allocations taken in `created_at` ascending order: each allocation's share is the pool multiplied by that allocation's `credit_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents, and the pool's own residual — the pool less the sum of those shares — is handed to the single allocation with the largest `credit_pct`, ties broken in favour of the earlier `created_at`. A role with no rows, or with a pool of 0, is skipped. The two layers round independently and on purpose: collapsing them into one multiplication of basis, weight and percentage produces a different total whenever both layers round the same way, and the per-allocation results then fail to re-sum to the basis. Where credit percentages inside a role sum to less than 100.00, the shortfall stays in that role's residual and is therefore handed to the largest-percentage row of the same role. A matter with no active allocation of any role leaves its whole basis unallocated and every credited figure at 0; a matter with only an originating allocation sees origination receive the entire basis through the fold-in rule.

The results are stored twice on each allocation, once per basis. `credited_collection_provisional_cents` is the row's share of the accrual basis and `credited_collection_final_cents` its share of the collected basis; a partner's own `credited_collection_provisional_cents` and `credited_collection_final_cents` are the sums of those figures over their `ACTIVE` allocations.

The formula-model score is then computed in a pinned eight-step order, because the normalization of step four depends on a firm-wide total that itself depends on every partner's step-three result — reorder the steps and every partner's score changes.

1. Run the credited-collection split above on every in-scope matter using the variant's basis.
2. Sum, for the partner, the credited amounts on their active allocations over in-scope matters. Call this the partner's credited collections.
3. Sum those partner figures across every in-scope partner to obtain the firm total.
4. Compute the normalization factor as the firm normalization target of 1,000,000,000 cents — ten million dollars — divided by the firm total, rounded half up to 6 decimal places. When the firm total is 0 the factor is exactly 1.000000. Because the factor is rounded before it is consumed, it is a pinned constant for the whole firm within one evaluation.
5. Compute the partner's normalized credit index as their credited collections multiplied by that normalization factor and divided by the same 1,000,000,000-cent target, rounded half up to 6 decimal places. This is stored as `normalized_credit_index_provisional` or `normalized_credit_index_final` according to the variant.
6. Compute `blended_realization_pct` as the sum, over the partner's in-scope active allocations, of each allocation's credited amount multiplied by its matter's `realization_pct`, divided by the partner's credited collections, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; it is 0.0000 when the partner's credited collections are 0.
7. Select the realization tier multiplier from that blended figure: 1.10 when it is at least 0.9500, otherwise 1.00 when it is at least 0.8500, otherwise 0.90 when it is at least 0.7500, and otherwise 0.75. Exactly 0.9500 therefore selects the top tier and exactly 0.8500 selects the second, never the first.
8. Compute `weighted_profitability_points` as the sum, over the same allocations, of each credited amount multiplied by its matter's `profitability_index_fully_loaded`, divided by the partner's credited collections, rounded half up to 4 decimal places and 0.0000 when those collections are 0. Form the hours component as the partner's `verified_billable_hours` divided by 1,800 — the firm's notional full billable year. Then form the unrounded weighted sum of four terms: 0.45 multiplied by the normalized credit index multiplied by 100; plus 0.20 multiplied by the hours component multiplied by 100; plus 0.15 multiplied by `non_billable_points` divided by 100; plus 0.20 multiplied by `weighted_profitability_points` multiplied by 100. Multiply that unrounded sum by the tier multiplier selected in step 7 and round the product half up to 4 decimal places. That is the formula score. The tier multiplier is applied to the unrounded sum and the rounding happens once, afterwards; rounding the weighted sum first and multiplying second can move the fourth decimal place.

The two variants differ in which basis is used and which matters are in scope, and both are maintained at all times so that a partner always carries a provisional and a final score. Under the **provisional accrual** variant, each matter's basis is its `billed_cents` multiplied by its `realization_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the matter scope is every matter in `OPEN` or `CLOSED` — work in progress is accrued at its historic realization rate. The result is stored as `formula_score_provisional` and is the figure consumed while the cycle sits in `OPEN_INTAKE`, `SELF_ASSESSMENT`, `PEER_REVIEW`, `DELIBERATION` or `AWARDS_PUBLISHED`. Under the **final collected** variant, each matter's basis is simply its `collected_cents` and the matter scope is matters in `CLOSED` only, so an open matter contributes nothing however much has been collected on it. That result is stored as `formula_score_final` and is the figure consumed from `TRUE_UP_OPEN` onward. A single matter moving from `OPEN` to `CLOSED` therefore changes the final scope without touching the provisional scope, and the two variants diverge asymmetrically.

Only partners whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` and whose `compensation_model` is `FORMULA` contribute to the cycle's `total_formula_score`, which is the sum of those partners' `formula_score_provisional` rounded half up to 4 decimal places; the provisional variant is the one that feeds this total whatever stage the cycle has reached, so the final award variant of section 3.8 divides a partner's `formula_score_final` by that same provisional-basis total. The total is carried on the anchor cycle alone: every other cycle, including one that has reached `CLOSED` and thereby ceased to be the anchor, reports `total_formula_score` of 0.0000 and `total_committee_points` of 0.00. A departed partner is dropped from that total while keeping their own stored scores. When the firm total is 0 every normalized credit index is 0.000000 and the score reduces to its hours and non-billable components under the bottom tier multiplier of 0.75. A partner with no credited collections but with hours has a blended realization of 0.0000, which also selects the bottom tier. A negative fully-loaded profitability index pulls the score down and is not floored.

### 3.6. Self-Assessment and Peer Review

Each partner inside the evaluation population writes their own account of the year. A `Partner` opens a self-assessment against a `cycle_id` with a `narrative`, a `claimed_origination_pct_total` between 0.00 and 10000.00 to 2 decimal places representing the origination percentage they believe they hold across all matters, and a `claimed_non_billable_hours` figure of at least 0.00 to 2 decimal places. As with claims, `partner_id` comes from `X-User-ID` and never from the body, exactly one assessment may exist per pairing of cycle and partner — a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSESSMENT` — and the row is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, `returned_reason`, `submitted_at` and `accepted_at` all null, and its peer-review roll-up at zero. Creation returns 201 Created.

Submitting drives the assessment `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock's current instant. Only the owning partner may submit, and only a `DRAFT` row may be submitted — otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_MUTABLE`. The cycle must be in stage `SELF_ASSESSMENT` and the logical clock's current date must fall on or before that cycle's `assessment_deadline_on`; either failure reports `ERR_ASSESSMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Reviewers are assigned by a `CycleAdmin`, who names a `self_assessment_id` and a `reviewer_partner_id`. The review is created in `ASSIGNED` with `technical_score`, `collaboration_score`, `client_service_score`, `composite_score`, `comment` and `recusal_reason` all null, and returns 201 Created. One assignment may exist per pairing of assessment and reviewer, a duplicate being `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_ASSIGNMENT`, and a partner may not be assigned to review their own assessment — that attempt is `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`.

The assigned reviewer then decides their review by supplying an `outcome` spelled `SCORE` or `RECUSE`, and the two outcomes are deliberately asymmetric in what they require and what they leave behind. A **score** outcome moves the review `ASSIGNED → SCORED`. It requires all three of `technical_score`, `collaboration_score` and `client_service_score` to be present integers between 1 and 5 inclusive — any missing or out-of-range and the request is malformed — accepts an optional confidential `comment`, and computes `composite_score` as the technical score weighted 0.50 plus the collaboration score weighted 0.25 plus the client-service score weighted 0.25, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The reviewer must be the review's own `reviewer_partner_id`, the review must currently be `ASSIGNED` (otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`), the reviewer must not be the assessment's own partner (otherwise `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`), and the assessment's cycle must be in `SELF_ASSESSMENT` or `PEER_REVIEW` (otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`). Scoring then re-derives the assessment's `scored_review_count`, the number of its reviews in `SCORED`, and its `mean_peer_score`, the mean `composite_score` over those scored reviews to 4 decimal places, which is 0.0000 when no review has been scored. A **recuse** outcome moves the review to the terminal `RECUSED`; it requires a `recusal_reason` — absent, the request is malformed — and requires the same `ASSIGNED` starting state and the same reviewer ownership, but it is *not* gated on the cycle stage window. Recusal leaves `scored_review_count` untouched, which is exactly how a late recusal can strand an assessment below the two scored reviews the firm requires.

A `CommitteeMember` disposes of a submitted assessment with a `decision` spelled `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`, and here too the two branches diverge sharply. Both require the assessment to be in `SUBMITTED`, failing which `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`; both forbid a partner from deciding their own assessment, failing which `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`; and both require the assessment's cycle to be in `SELF_ASSESSMENT` or `PEER_REVIEW`, failing which `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An **accept** decision drives the assessment `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at` with the logical clock's current instant, but only if the assessment already carries at least two reviews in `SCORED`; below that, acceptance is refused with `ERR_PEER_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE`. Acceptance preserves every peer-review score. A **return** decision drives the assessment back `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`, requires a `reason` — absent, the request is malformed — records it as `returned_reason`, and increments `revision_count` by 1. Returning is expensive by design: every peer review on the assessment that had reached `SCORED` is reset to `ASSIGNED` with `technical_score`, `collaboration_score`, `client_service_score`, `composite_score` and `comment` all cleared, and that reset re-derives `scored_review_count` and `mean_peer_score` back down. A returned assessment must therefore be re-submitted *and* re-scored from scratch before it can be accepted, which is the whole difference between the two branches.

### 3.7. Committee Deliberation and Discretionary Points

Ballots are not created by anyone; they are materialised by the cycle when it enters `DELIBERATION`. One ballot is created in `PENDING` for every pairing of a subject partner — one whose `compensation_model` is `DISCRETIONARY`, whose `evaluation_required` is true and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` — with a committee member, being any partner whose `is_committee_member` is true and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. The pairings are materialised subject by subject in the subjects' `created_at` order and, within one subject, in the committee members' `created_at` order, which is the `created_at` ordering the trimmed mean below and the ballot list both inherit. The pairing where the subject and the committee member are the same partner is created too, deliberately, and its only lawful exit is described below. Each ballot records `cycle_id`, `subject_partner_id` and `committee_member_id`, with `points_proposed`, `rationale`, `recusal_reason` and `decided_at` all null while it remains `PENDING`.

A `CommitteeMember` disposes of one of their own ballots by supplying an `action` spelled `CAST`, `ABSTAIN` or `RECUSE` together with the `expected_cycle_version` required by section 2.8, and the three actions share a common gate. In every case the ballot must be in `PENDING`, failing which `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_DECIDED`; the acting member must be the ballot's own `committee_member_id`, failing which the ownership rule applies; and the ballot's cycle must be in stage `DELIBERATION`, failing which `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_TRANSITION`. Every one of the three actions stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock's current instant, increments the ballot's `version`, and increments the cycle's `version` — which is what makes the next holder of a superseded cycle version fail deterministically.

A **cast** action moves the ballot `PENDING → CAST` and is the only action that carries weight. It records `points_proposed`, a figure between 0.00 and 100000.00 at the schema layer, together with a confidential `rationale`. Two further conditions apply to a cast and to no other action: the committee member may not be the subject, and casting on one's own ballot is refused with `ERR_SELF_VOTE_FORBIDDEN`; and the subject partner's self-assessment for that cycle must have reached `ACCEPTED`, failing which `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Separately from the schema range, a `points_proposed` above the per-ballot ceiling of 1000.00 points is a business-constraint breach reported as `ERR_POINTS_CAP_EXCEEDED` rather than as a malformed payload — and when a committee member casts their own ballot with points above that ceiling, the self-vote refusal wins, as section 2.11 sets out. A successful cast re-derives the subject's `committee_points` through the trimmed mean below and from there the cycle's `total_committee_points`.

An **abstain** action moves the ballot `PENDING → ABSTAINED`, and a **recuse** action moves it `PENDING → RECUSED`; the latter additionally requires a `recusal_reason`, absent which the request is malformed, and the reason is recorded in that same field on either branch. Neither action records `points_proposed` or `rationale`, and neither contributes anything to the subject's points or to quorum. The self-ballot that deliberation creates for a committee member who is also a discretionary subject can leave `PENDING` only through recusal or abstention, never through a cast.

A discretionary partner's `committee_points` are the **trimmed mean** of the points proposed on their cast ballots in the anchor cycle, and only ballots in `CAST` are counted — `PENDING`, `ABSTAINED` and `RECUSED` ballots enter neither the mean nor the quorum test. The cast ballots are ordered by `points_proposed` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. With no cast ballot at all the result is 0.00 and the partner's discretionary award is nothing. With three or more cast ballots the single lowest and single highest proposals are discarded and the mean of the remainder is taken, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — so exactly three casts leave only the middle value standing, and no single outlier ballot can carry a partner. With one or two cast ballots there is no trimming at all and the plain mean of them is taken, rounded the same way; the trimming threshold is three. Ties at either extreme are broken by the ascending `created_at` in the ordering, so which of two identical proposals is discarded is deterministic and the result is unaffected in any case. The cycle's `total_committee_points` is the sum of `committee_points` across the discretionary partners whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and like `total_formula_score` it is carried on the anchor cycle alone.

### 3.8. Cycle Staging, the Pool Waterfall and Award Publication

A `CycleAdmin` opens a compensation cycle for a `fiscal_year` between 2000 and 2100, which must be unique across all cycles — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE`. The cycle carries the year's cash parameters: `distributable_pool_cents`, the total the firm will distribute, and `lockstep_unit_cents`, the cash value of one lockstep rung, both integers of at least 0; `discretionary_pool_pct`, the fraction of the distributable pool reserved for discretionary partners, between 0.0000 and 1.0000 at 4 decimal places and defaulting to 0.20; and `draw_pct`, the fraction of a target award paid out as monthly draws before true-up, on the same scale and defaulting to 0.80. It also carries the two deadlines `claim_deadline_on` and `assessment_deadline_on`, each an inclusive calendar date. The cycle is created in `OPEN_INTAKE` with `lockstep_reserved_cents`, `discretionary_pool_cents`, `formula_pool_cents` and `unsettled_award_count` all at 0, `total_formula_score` at 0.0000 and `total_committee_points` at 0.00, `haircut_applied_pct` at 0.0000, `reopen_count` at 0 and `confidentiality_locked` false; `undistributed_cash_cents` is the one cash field that does not start at 0, standing at the full `distributable_pool_cents` because nothing has yet been paid out of it. Creation returns 201 Created. If its `fiscal_year` is the greatest among non-closed cycles it immediately becomes the anchor cycle of section 2.6 and re-bases every cycle-scoped partner figure.

Every stage change is requested by naming a `target_state`, and a `target_state` not reachable from the cycle's current stage is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_TRANSITION`. Each edge carries its own gate and its own cascade, and each increments the cycle's `version`.

`OPEN_INTAKE → SELF_ASSESSMENT` is gated on origination disputes being finished: no credit claim of the cycle may still be in `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`, and one that is reports `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CLAIMS`. Claims left sitting in `DRAFT` are allowed to lapse — only in-flight claims block the stage change.

`SELF_ASSESSMENT → PEER_REVIEW` is gated on the evaluation population having spoken: every partner whose `evaluation_required` is true and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` must have exactly one self-assessment for this cycle whose `state` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`, failing which `ERR_ASSESSMENTS_INCOMPLETE`.

`PEER_REVIEW → DELIBERATION` carries two gates and the heaviest cascade in the service. Every self-assessment of the cycle must still be `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — one that has been returned to `DRAFT` again reports `ERR_ASSESSMENTS_INCOMPLETE` — and every self-assessment must carry at least two reviews in `SCORED`, failing which `ERR_PEER_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE`. Acceptance itself is deliberately *not* gated here: the committee may enter deliberation with an assessment still merely `SUBMITTED`; what it cannot then do is cast points on that partner, which the ballot's own acceptance precondition blocks. On success the edge recomputes `realization_pct`, `profitability_index_contribution` and `profitability_index_fully_loaded` on every matter; recomputes `credited_collection_provisional_cents` and `credited_collection_final_cents` on every `ACTIVE` allocation; recomputes every partner's `non_billable_points`; recomputes every partner's `formula_score_provisional` and `formula_score_final` along with the cycle's `total_formula_score`; creates the `PENDING` ballots described in section 3.7; and creates one compensation award for every partner whose `evaluation_required` is true and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`, in award state `PROPOSED` and payment state `PENDING`, with `target_award_cents` of 0 and `basis_model` snapshotted from that partner's `compensation_model` at that moment. Those awards are materialised in the partners' `created_at` order, and each also starts with `draw_schedule_monthly_cents`, `draw_paid_cents`, `capital_shortfall_cents` and `reconsideration_count` at 0 and with `final_award_cents`, `true_up_delta_cents` and `capital_call_cents` all null until a true-up writes them.

`DELIBERATION → AWARDS_PUBLISHED` is where money is assigned. Two gates apply. Every subject partner that has ballots in this cycle must have at least two of them in `CAST`, failing which `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; and the pool waterfall must close without residual deficit, failing which `ERR_POOL_INSUFFICIENT` and the entire advance is rejected with all four pool fields left at their pre-call values.

The **pool waterfall** fills the three compensation pools out of the distributable cash in a fixed order. The lockstep obligation comes first because it is contractual: it is the sum, over partners whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` and whose `evaluation_required` is true and who are on the lockstep model, of each partner's `lockstep_step` multiplied by the cycle's `lockstep_unit_cents`. The discretionary reservation comes second and is taken off the top of the whole pool: `distributable_pool_cents` multiplied by `discretionary_pool_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The residual is `distributable_pool_cents` less the lockstep obligation less that discretionary reservation. When the residual is zero or positive, `lockstep_reserved_cents` and `discretionary_pool_cents` take those two gross figures unchanged, `formula_pool_cents` takes the residual, and `haircut_applied_pct` is 0.0000. When the residual is negative, the shortfall is the residual's magnitude and the buckets are cut in order, each down to a floor of 70 percent of its gross — that is, each may give up at most 30 percent of itself. The lockstep bucket is cut first: its cutting ceiling is the lockstep gross multiplied by 0.30 and rounded half up to whole cents, the actual cut is the lesser of the shortfall and that ceiling, `haircut_applied_pct` records the cut divided by the lockstep gross rounded half up to 4 decimal places (and stays 0.0000 when the lockstep gross is 0), `lockstep_reserved_cents` becomes the gross less the cut, and the shortfall is reduced by the cut. The discretionary bucket is cut second on exactly the same pattern: its ceiling is the discretionary gross multiplied by 0.30 and rounded half up, the cut is the lesser of the remaining shortfall and that ceiling, `discretionary_pool_cents` becomes the discretionary gross less that cut, and the shortfall is reduced again. If any shortfall survives both cuts the waterfall fails with `ERR_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`; otherwise `formula_pool_cents` is 0. A residual of exactly zero leaves `formula_pool_cents` at 0 with no haircut, and every formula partner's award is then nothing. With no lockstep partner at all the lockstep gross is 0, `haircut_applied_pct` stays 0.0000 even in deficit, and the whole shortfall falls on the discretionary bucket. A shortfall exactly equal to the lockstep ceiling is absorbed entirely by lockstep, leaving discretionary untouched and `haircut_applied_pct` at exactly 0.3000. And a `discretionary_pool_pct` of 0.0000 gives a discretionary gross of 0 and therefore a discretionary cutting ceiling of 0.

Turning a pool share into cash depends on which of the three models the award's `basis_model` snapshotted. A lockstep award is the partner's `lockstep_step` multiplied by the cycle's `lockstep_unit_cents` multiplied by one minus `haircut_applied_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — so a full 0.3000 haircut pays exactly 70 percent of the contractual figure. A formula award is `formula_pool_cents` multiplied by the partner's score and divided by the cycle's `total_formula_score`, rounded half up to whole cents, and is 0 when that total is 0 (in which case the formula pool stays entirely undistributed). A discretionary award is `discretionary_pool_cents` multiplied by the partner's `committee_points` and divided by the cycle's `total_committee_points`, rounded half up to whole cents, and is 0 when that total is 0. Only awards in award state `PROPOSED` or `PUBLISHED` are recomputed; a `FINALIZED` award is frozen.

That computation runs under two variants. The **provisional** variant runs on the publication edge, consumes `formula_score_provisional` and the pre-true-up `committee_points`, and writes `target_award_cents`. The **final** variant runs at true-up, consumes `formula_score_final` — which rests on the collected basis and on closed matters only — and writes `final_award_cents`; for a partner whose matters are still open the final figure normally lands below the target, and the gap becomes the true-up delta. On the publication edge the cycle also sets each award's `draw_schedule_monthly_cents` to `target_award_cents` multiplied by `draw_pct` divided by 12, rounded half up to whole cents; the monthly figure is rounded independently of the award, so twelve monthly draws need not sum to the award multiplied by `draw_pct`. A `draw_pct` of 0.0000 gives a monthly draw of 0 and pushes the whole award into the true-up. Publication then moves every award of the cycle from award state `PROPOSED` to `PUBLISHED`, recomputes `undistributed_cash_cents` and `unsettled_award_count`, and advances the cycle.

`AWARDS_PUBLISHED → DELIBERATION` is the reconsideration reopen, and it is bounded twice over: the cycle's `reopen_count` must still be below 1, and no award of the cycle may have reached payment state `TRUED_UP` or `SETTLED`; either failure is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_REOPENABLE`. A permitted reopen unwinds the cash it paid. For every award of the cycle, one ledger row of kind `DRAW_REVERSAL` is appended for each un-reversed scheduled draw, copying that draw's `period_year` and `period_month` and carrying `amount_cents` equal to the negation of that draw's `amount_cents`, the draws being processed in order of `period_year` ascending, then `period_month` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; each reversed scheduled draw has its `reversed` flag set to true, and a reversed draw no longer occupies its period bucket. Those appends drive each award's `draw_paid_cents` back to 0 and its payment state back to `PENDING`, which drives each partner's `ytd_draw_paid_cents` back to its pre-draw value and the cycle's `undistributed_cash_cents` back to the full `distributable_pool_cents` — the conserved quantity the whole reversal exists to protect. Every award moves from award state `PUBLISHED` back to `PROPOSED` with its `reconsideration_count` incremented by 1, and the cycle's `reopen_count` increments by 1 as it returns to `DELIBERATION`.

`AWARDS_PUBLISHED → TRUE_UP_OPEN` requires every award of the cycle to be in award state `PUBLISHED`, failing which the transition is not available and `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_TRANSITION` applies. It recomputes `formula_score_final` and `credited_collection_final_cents` for every partner in scope, moving the cycle onto the collected basis, and advances the stage.

`TRUE_UP_OPEN → CLOSED` requires the cycle's `unsettled_award_count` — the number of its awards whose payment state is anything other than `SETTLED` — to be exactly 0, failing which `ERR_AWARDS_NOT_SETTLED`. Closing moves every award of the cycle from award state `PUBLISHED` to the terminal `FINALIZED`, sets `confidentiality_locked` to true, and puts the cycle in its terminal `CLOSED` stage — at which point it is no longer eligible to be the anchor cycle, and only a `CycleAdmin` may read its ballots.

### 3.9. Draws, True-Up, Capital Settlement and Cycle Closure

An award carries two state dimensions that evolve independently of one another. Its `award_state` tracks determination and runs `PROPOSED → PUBLISHED → FINALIZED`, with the reopen edge `PUBLISHED → PROPOSED` available while the payment side has not yet been trued up or settled; the move to `PUBLISHED` sets `target_award_cents` and `draw_schedule_monthly_cents`, the reopen edge increments `reconsideration_count` and resets the payment dimension to `PENDING`, and the move to `FINALIZED` requires the payment dimension to be `SETTLED` already. Its `payment_state` tracks cash and runs `PENDING → DRAWING → TRUED_UP → SETTLED`, with a direct `DRAWING → SETTLED` shortcut described below. The combinations are genuinely orthogonal: an award may be published and pending, published and drawing, proposed and pending after a reopen, or published and settled.

Paying a monthly draw is a `CycleAdmin` act that names a `period_year` and a `period_month` and moves the award from `PENDING` to `DRAWING`, or keeps it in `DRAWING` when a further period is paid. `period_year` must lie between 2000 and 2100 and `period_month` between 1 and 12, or the request is malformed. Four conditions then guard it: the award's `payment_state` must still be `PENDING` or `DRAWING`, so an award already trued up or settled is not drawable; its `award_state` must be `PUBLISHED`; and the award's cycle must be in `AWARDS_PUBLISHED` or `TRUE_UP_OPEN` — any of those three failing is `ERR_AWARD_NOT_DRAWABLE`, tested in that order — and no un-reversed ledger row of kind `SCHEDULED_DRAW` may already exist on this award for that pairing of year and month, a collision being `ERR_DRAW_PERIOD_ALREADY_PAID`. The draw appends one ledger row of kind `SCHEDULED_DRAW` whose `amount_cents` is the award's `draw_schedule_monthly_cents`, with `award_id` pointing back at the award, `reversed` false and a human-readable `memo` that the server composes itself rather than accepting from the caller. That append re-derives the award's `draw_paid_cents` — the signed sum of `amount_cents` over its rows of kind `SCHEDULED_DRAW` and `DRAW_REVERSAL` — and sets the payment dimension to `DRAWING`, which in turn re-derives the partner's `ytd_draw_paid_cents` and the cycle's `undistributed_cash_cents`, the latter being `distributable_pool_cents` less the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger row belonging to any award of the cycle. The award's `version` increments.

The ledger recognises five kinds of movement: `SCHEDULED_DRAW`, `DRAW_REVERSAL`, `TRUE_UP`, `CLAWBACK` and `CAPITAL_WITHHOLDING`. `amount_cents` is signed, and it is negative for reversals and clawbacks. `period_year` and `period_month` describe the draw period, with `period_month` running 1 through 12 for scheduled draws and their reversals and being 0 for every other kind, and `period_year` likewise 0 for non-period movements. Ledger rows are never edited, so a correction is always another row.

Trueing up an award is the year-end reconciliation and requires the award's payment dimension to be `DRAWING` and its cycle to be in `TRUE_UP_OPEN`; either failure is `ERR_AWARD_NOT_TRUE_UP_READY`. It first runs the final award variant of section 3.8 to set `final_award_cents`, then computes `true_up_delta_cents` as `final_award_cents` less `draw_paid_cents` — positive means money still owed to the partner, negative means money owed back to the firm. It computes `capital_call_cents` as the partner's `target_capital_cents` less their `capital_account_balance_cents`, floored at 0 so that an over-funded account never produces a negative call. The amount actually withheld is the lesser of that capital call and the delta floored at 0, so a negative delta withholds nothing.

The ledger rows are then appended in a pinned order, and the order is what makes the outcome well defined. If the withheld amount is greater than 0, a `CAPITAL_WITHHOLDING` row of that amount is appended *first* and applied to the partner's `capital_account_balance_cents`. Then, if the delta net of the withholding is greater than 0, a `TRUE_UP` row of that net amount is appended; otherwise, if the delta is negative, a `CLAWBACK` row carrying the negative delta is appended. Only after those appends is `capital_shortfall_cents` evaluated, again as `target_capital_cents` less the now-updated `capital_account_balance_cents` floored at 0 — and it is that figure which selects the payment dimension: `SETTLED` when the shortfall is 0, `TRUED_UP` otherwise. Appending and applying the withholding before evaluating the shortfall is essential; evaluate it first and a partner whose delta fully funds their capital call would be stranded in `TRUED_UP` instead of reaching `SETTLED` inside the same operation, which is exactly the direct `DRAWING → SETTLED` path. Every figure here is already integer cents, so no rounding is needed anywhere in the settlement. A delta of exactly 0 appends neither a true-up nor a clawback row, and cannot append a capital withholding either since the withheld amount is the lesser of the call and zero. A negative delta with an outstanding capital call withholds nothing, appends a clawback and lands in `TRUED_UP`, leaving the shortfall to a later contribution. A capital call larger than a positive delta withholds the whole delta, appends no true-up row and lands in `TRUED_UP`. A partner whose target capital is already met has a call of 0, a shortfall of 0 and reaches `SETTLED` inside the true-up. And where draws already paid exceed the final award, the clawback is what drives the cycle's `undistributed_cash_cents` back up. Trueing up also re-derives the cycle's `unsettled_award_count` and `undistributed_cash_cents`, and increments the award's `version`.

An award left in `TRUED_UP` waits for capital. A `CycleAdmin` credits a partner's capital account by supplying an `amount_cents` integer of at least 0 together with the `expected_partner_version` required by section 2.8; the contribution increases `capital_account_balance_cents` by that amount, which re-derives `capital_shortfall_cents` on every trued-up award of that partner and moves any award whose shortfall reaches 0 from `TRUED_UP` to `SETTLED` with its `version` incremented. That settlement re-derives the cycle's `unsettled_award_count`, which is the single gate standing between `TRUE_UP_OPEN` and `CLOSED` — so capital collection is, quite literally, the last thing that closes a compensation year.
