# Trade Finance Letter Of Credit Issuance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A documentary credit is the bank's own irrevocable undertaking to pay an exporter against documents, and every one of them is written against a limit somebody in the bank has already promised to somebody else. The operational problem this service exists to solve is not the paperwork — it is the bookkeeping underneath the paperwork. When a trade finance officer issues a credit, the bank simultaneously commits an amount of the applicant's facility, prices a commission it will earn over the credit's validity, screens three counterparties against sanctions lists, and starts a clock that will eventually decide whether the bank still has the right to refuse discrepant documents. Today those five things live in five different places, are reconciled by spreadsheet, and disagree with each other as often as they agree.

This document specifies a single service that owns the whole instrument lifecycle: applicant onboarding and facility limits, collateral posting and release, credit drafting with its documentary requirements and incoterm alignment, credit approval and issuance, advising and confirming bank selection under country-risk and appetite constraints, amendment negotiation with beneficiary consent, document examination inside the five-banking-day period, discrepancy identification, waiver requests to the applicant, the single refusal notice, and finally payment, acceptance, negotiation or reimbursement with the charges that go with each. The service is deliberately conservative with money: exposure is committed at issuance rather than at drawing, every rounding step that could shave a cent chooses the direction that favours the bank's reserve, and no allocation is ever edited in place — it is released and re-cut, so the ledger is never observed in a half-written state.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **time is explicit**: the service reads a logical clock that only an operator can move, so that presentation windows, consent windows, examination deadlines and maturity dates are reproducible rather than wall-clock dependent. Second, **authority is split**: the officer who drafts an instrument is never the officer who approves it, and the officer who runs a sanctions screening is never the officer who releases it. Both commitments show up throughout the specification as concrete rejection rules rather than as aspirations.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Principals, Roles and Ownership

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity it acts in. If either header is missing or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else, so an unauthenticated request never learns whether the resource it addressed exists.

Eight roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `CreditOfficer`, `TradeFinanceOfficer`, `DocumentExaminer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ApplicantUser`, `BeneficiaryUser`, `CorrespondentBank` and `SystemOperator`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those eight strings, or is one of them but is not listed among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). There is no hierarchical override anywhere in this system: a `CreditOfficer` cannot do a `TradeFinanceOfficer`'s work and a `TradeFinanceOfficer` cannot do a `CreditOfficer`'s, regardless of seniority.

Roles fall into two identity families, and the difference determines what `X-User-ID` actually means.

Four roles carry **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `CreditOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it (for example as `onboarded_by_user_id` on an applicant record) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `TradeFinanceOfficer`, whose identifier is recorded as `drafted_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on credits and amendments and as `settled_by_user_id` on settlement claims; for `DocumentExaminer`, recorded as `raised_by_user_id`, `examined_by_user_id` and `requested_by_user_id`; and for `ComplianceOfficer`, recorded as `screened_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id`. For these four roles the only comparisons the server ever makes against `X-User-ID` are the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.10. `SystemOperator` is likewise opaque, and nothing at all is recorded from it.

Three roles are **entity-backed**, and for them `X-User-ID` is the primary key of the row the principal acts for. When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare the resource's `applicant_id` — reached directly on a credit, or through the presentation's credit on a waiver decision — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BeneficiaryUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Beneficiary`, and ownership checks compare the addressed credit's `beneficiary_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CorrespondentBank`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bank`; on a confirmation response the comparison is against the credit's `confirming_bank_id`, and on every presentation write it is against the presentation's `presenting_bank_id`. Any of these comparisons failing yields `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (HTTP 403).

A separate 403, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, is reserved for the four-eyes rules of §2.10 and is never used for a plain ownership mismatch.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

List responses report all four pagination fields in `meta`: the effective `limit`, the effective `offset`, the `total_count` of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless an endpoint's own description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints deviate deliberately and their ordering is part of their contract: the bank directory is ordered by `eligibility_score` descending, then by `country_risk_score` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; and the amendment list of one credit is ordered by `seq` ascending. Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, narrow the result set before pagination is applied and never change the ordering.

### 2.4. Instrument Records, Versions and Timestamps

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and clients never supply them — an `id` present in a request body is ignored.

Almost every record also carries three system-maintained fields. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations a record suffers as a downstream consequence of a write aimed at some other record. The rule is worth stating plainly because this domain is dense with cascades: if a credit's issuance drives a facility's exposure figures upward, the facility's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though nobody addressed the facility directly.

One entity is exempt. Presented documents are immutable evidence: a document row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, there is no endpoint that updates one and none that deletes one. A correction is made by presenting another document, not by editing an existing row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Deterministic Date Arithmetic

Nothing in this service reads the host wall clock. Every deadline, window boundary, maturity date and date-stamped field derives from a single logical clock that starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and moves only when a `SystemOperator` sets it. Advancing the clock mutates no entity — deadlines are re-evaluated only when a business write or the operational sweep runs — and an attempt to set the clock to an instant earlier than its current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422); the clock is monotonic by construction.

Date-typed fields (`facility_expiry_date`, `latest_shipment_date`, `expiry_date`, `issue_date`, `presentation_deadline_date`, `received_date`, `maturity_date`, `consent_window_start`, `consent_deadline_date`, `window_end_effective`, `confirmation_window_start`, `confirmation_window_end`) are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and are compared as dates. Instant-typed fields (`received_at`, `submitted_at`, `examination_deadline`, `refusal_notice_sent_at`, `requested_at`, `deadline_at`, `responded_at`, `resolved_at`, `released_at`, `settled_at`, `reviewed_at`) are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits and are compared as instants. Where a deadline is expressed as an instant derived from a date, it lands at `23:59:59.999Z` on that date.

Banking-day arithmetic appears in exactly one place — the examination period — and uses a fixed weekday rule with no holiday calendar: Monday through Friday are banking days, Saturday and Sunday are not.

### 2.6. Minor Units, Rounding and Ledger Conservation

All monetary amounts are integers in the minor unit of their currency, which this document calls cents; a field name ending in `_cents` is always an integer and never a decimal. Because every stored amount is already an exact whole number of cents, the specification's two-decimal-place presentation and its half-up rounding rule coincide: wherever a computation produces a fraction of a cent, that fraction is resolved by rounding half up — to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half rounded away from zero.

Two rounding disciplines run side by side and must not be confused. Exposure arithmetic — everything that decides how much of a facility a credit occupies — uses integer floor division throughout, never half-up rounding, so that the bank never under-reserves by a fraction. Charge arithmetic — commissions, confirmation charges, discount charges and document fees — uses half-up rounding, applied once per charge component and never to a running total. Where a charge is assessed per document, the rounding happens per document and the rounded values are then summed; summing exact fractions and rounding once at the end is a different and wrong answer, and the difference grows with the number of documents.

Percentages and basis points are integers. A basis-point figure is a rate in ten-thousandths, so a rate of 15 basis points applied to an amount means that amount multiplied by 15 and divided by 10,000.

The ledgers in this system are pure aggregations, never incrementally patched counters. A facility's occupied exposure is always the sum over its currently open allocation rows; a credit's drawable position is always derived from its own presentations. This is what makes the conservation property hold to the cent: releasing an allocation restores the facility to exactly the value it held before that allocation was opened, and voiding a drawing restores the credit to exactly its pre-submission drawable position.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Several resources are addressed through a parent: a facility under an applicant, a documentary requirement or an amendment under a credit, documents and discrepancies and waiver requests and settlement claims under a presentation. Path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right. The parent identifier is looked up first, and if it does not resolve the request fails there with that parent's own not-found error — the child identifier is not even examined. Only once the parent resolves is the child looked up, and a child that resolves but belongs to a different parent is treated as not found rather than as a permission problem.

Path identifiers are resolved before payload foreign keys. Within a payload, foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint. Each unresolvable reference maps to its own dedicated 404: `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND` for an applicant path id or an `applicant_id`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` for a facility path id or a `facility_id`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND` for a bank path id or any of `nominated_bank_id`, `advising_bank_id`, `confirming_bank_id` and `presenting_bank_id`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_NOT_FOUND` for a beneficiary path id or a `beneficiary_id`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_FOUND` for a credit path id or a `credit_id`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an amendment path id, `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_FOUND` for a presentation path id or a `presentation_id`, `ERR_WAIVER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for a waiver-request path id, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for a settlement-claim path id and `ERR_SCREENING_CHECK_NOT_FOUND` for a screening-check path id.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Credits and Facilities

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only two. Both are creates that the product rules deliberately allow to stack on or supersede a prior one, which means no state check and no business rule can catch a writer working from a stale view — a stale-version check is the only mechanism that can.

A `POST /credits/{id}/amendments` request must carry `expected_credit_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the `DocumentaryCredit` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the credit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this matters is structural: drafting a new amendment legitimately moves any earlier still-draft amendment on the same credit to `SUPERSEDED`, so a writer holding a stale view of the credit would silently discard an amendment it never saw. Note also that drafting an amendment is itself a write on the credit and therefore advances the credit's `version`, so a second amendment drafted from the same captured version is rejected.

A `POST /facilities/{id}/collateral-postings` request must carry `expected_facility_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the `CreditFacility` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the facility's current `version`, the posting is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Collateral postings stack additively, so a second sequential posting would always succeed on its own merits; the check earns its place because the facility's `version` also advances whenever an issuance cascade recomputes its occupied exposure, and that recomputation changes how much collateral credit a given posting will actually buy.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint may be made to fail on one.

### 2.9. The Order of Rejection

When one request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in strictly ascending priority bands, and the first band that produces a failure decides the response. Within a single band, checks are evaluated left to right in payload order, then in path order.

The bands, in order:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the role is one of the eight and is permitted on this endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403); entity-backed principals own the addressed row (`ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY`, 403); segregation of duties holds (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403).
3. **Shape, then existence** — required fields present, correctly typed, enum members declared, money and day-count integers non-negative except where a field explicitly permits a negative value, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); then every path identifier and payload foreign key resolves, path identifiers first and payload keys in declaration order, else the matching 404 from §2.7.
4. **State preconditions and concurrency** — the addressed row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, else the specific 409 for that instrument (`ERR_CREDIT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_PENDING_CONSENT`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_LODGED` or `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`); cross-entity state guards hold, which for issuance means the facility is `ACTIVE` and its posture is not `FROZEN` (`ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409), for settlement means the governing presented-documents screening is not still awaiting review (`ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED`, 409) and for amendment consent means the parent credit is still `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` (`ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED`, 409); and the optimistic-concurrency field of §2.8 matches, else `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (412). The cross-entity screening guard covers only the awaiting-review result — a blocked or escalated screening is deliberately excluded here and is adjudicated one band later.
5. **Compliance safety** — sanctions and dual-use prohibitions: `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403), `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409), `ERR_DUAL_USE_ESCALATED` (409).
6. **Temporal windows** — `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`, `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CONFIRMATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`.
7. **Structural constraints** — overlap, capacity, completeness and uniqueness: `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CONFIRMING_LINE_WINDOW_FULL`, `ERR_CONFIRMING_BANK_INELIGIBLE`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMING_BANK`, `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS`, `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET`.
8. **Referential coherence between parties and instrument** — `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED`, `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED`, `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN`, `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_CONSENT_REQUIRED`.
9. **Fiscal capacity, checked last of all** — `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CREDIT_DRAWABLE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CHARGES_EXCEED_PROCEEDS`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_POSTED`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_BLOCKED`.

The ordering of the last five bands is a deliberate compliance decision rather than a generic default: compliance safety is evaluated **before** temporal windows, which are evaluated **before** structural constraints, which are evaluated **before** referential coherence, which is evaluated **before** fiscal capacity. A sanctions or dual-use prohibition is an absolute legal bar, and the bank must refuse before it tells the presenter anything about which document was wrong, whether a bank was nominated, or whether the facility had headroom. Fiscal capacity comes last precisely because a facility breach is a curable commercial condition while a sanctions hit is not.

Two consequences are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. An issuance attempt on a credit that is still in `DRAFT` and whose committed allocation would also exceed the facility's headroom returns `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED` (409) — the state band outranks the fiscal band — and not `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). A settlement claim lodged on a presentation whose documents screened `BLOCKED`, sent with a claim form the credit's availability does not admit, returns `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and not `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422).

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three steps in this service are four-eyes controlled, and in each the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the identifier recorded against the step being reviewed. Credit approval requires an identifier different from the credit's `drafted_by_user_id`. Amendment issuance requires an identifier different from the amendment's own `drafted_by_user_id`. Screening review requires an identifier different from the check's `screened_by_user_id`. A violation of any of the three yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note the shape of the rule: it compares operator identifiers, not roles, so two different `TradeFinanceOfficer` principals satisfy it and one principal wearing the same role twice does not.

### 2.11. Window and Interval Conventions

This domain runs on windows, and the boundary conventions differ between them on purpose. Getting them wrong is the single most common source of off-by-one behaviour, so each is stated explicitly here and again in the workflow that owns it.

- The **presentation window** ends on `presentation_deadline_date`, and that date is **inclusive**: a submission on the deadline date itself is timely.
- The **confirmation window** runs from `confirmation_window_start` **inclusive** to `confirmation_window_end` **exclusive**. A request on the start date is inside the window; a request on the end date is outside it.
- The **examination period** ends at `examination_deadline`, an instant, and that instant is **inside** the period: an action stamped at exactly `23:59:59.999Z` on the final banking day is timely, and one millisecond later is not.
- The **waiver window** ends at `deadline_at`, an instant, **inclusive**.
- The **amendment consent window** is a closed interval running from `consent_window_start` to `consent_deadline_date`, both ends **inclusive**.
- **Confirming-line occupancy** is measured over half-open intervals and against a **peak concurrent** figure, not a lifetime total; equality with the appetite is admitted.
- **Amendment consent windows** on one credit are compared as closed intervals widened by a symmetric buffer on both ends, and touching endpoints do collide.

Capacity comparisons throughout this service admit exact equality: an allocation that consumes precisely the available headroom succeeds, a drawing that consumes precisely the drawable balance succeeds, and a confirming line whose peak occupancy exactly equals the bank's appetite is admitted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Parties, Facilities and Collateral

Nothing can be issued until three kinds of counterparty exist in the system. A `CreditOfficer` onboards the **applicant** — the importer who asks the bank to issue a credit — by supplying its registered `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), the `country_code` of incorporation as exactly two uppercase letters, a four-digit `industry_code`, the obligor grade `internal_risk_grade`, which is one of `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB`, `BB`, `B` or `CCC`, and the outcome of the onboarding file as `kyc_status`, which is one of `PENDING`, `CLEARED` or `REJECTED`. A `sanctions_flag` may be supplied and defaults to false when it is omitted. The server records the acting operator as `onboarded_by_user_id` and sets the applicant's `status` to `ACTIVE`; the field's other two declared values, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, are never produced at onboarding. Applicants are unique on the pair of `legal_name` and `country_code`, and a second onboarding of the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409).

Opening a **credit facility** for that applicant is also a `CreditOfficer` act. The facility is denominated in a `currency` given as exactly three uppercase letters, carries an approved `limit_cents` of at least 100,000 minor units, a `collateral_required_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and a `facility_expiry_date` — the last date, inclusive, on which the facility may support a new issuance. The new facility starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `collateral_posted_cents` and `allocated_cents` at zero, `available_cents` equal to the whole `limit_cents`, `suspended_reason` unset, and `utilisation_posture` `OPEN`. Its `restrict_threshold_cents` is derived once from the limit as ninety-five percent of `limit_cents` using integer floor division — that is, `limit_cents` multiplied by 95, divided by 100 with any remainder discarded. The facility's `applicant_id` is the applicant it was opened under.

The facility's occupied exposure is never patched incrementally. Whenever an allocation row is inserted, has its charges changed, or is released, `allocated_cents` is recomputed as the sum, over every allocation row on this facility whose `status` is `OPEN`, of that row's `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents`; released rows are excluded from the sum entirely and their `released_cents` is a historical record only. Rows are summed in `created_at` ascending order. From that figure, `available_cents` is `limit_cents` minus `allocated_cents` and may never go negative, and `utilisation_posture` is re-derived: it is `FROZEN` whenever the facility's `status` is `CLOSED`; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` whenever `allocated_cents` has reached or passed `restrict_threshold_cents`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. No rounding occurs anywhere in this recomputation, which is what allows the conservation property to hold exactly.

Crossing the restriction threshold has consequences beyond the posture field. When a recomputation leaves `allocated_cents` at or above `restrict_threshold_cents` on a facility that was `ACTIVE`, the facility moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH` and `utilisation_posture` set to `RESTRICTED`, its `version` increments, and every credit sitting on that facility in status `APPROVED` is moved to `ON_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each such credit's `version` incrementing by one. The reverse is symmetric: when a recomputation leaves `allocated_cents` strictly below `restrict_threshold_cents` on a facility that is `SUSPENDED` for reason `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`, the facility returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, `suspended_reason` is cleared to unset, `utilisation_posture` returns to `OPEN`, and every `ON_HOLD` credit on that facility returns to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order.

A `CreditOfficer` can also drive the facility's lifecycle by hand, supplying an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `CLOSE` together with an optional `reason`. A manual suspension takes an `ACTIVE` facility to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `MANUAL_SUSPENSION`; note the asymmetry with the automatic path — `utilisation_posture` is recomputed from the exposure figures as usual but is **not** forced to `RESTRICTED`. The same `APPROVED → ON_HOLD` cascade over sibling credits runs, in `created_at` ascending order. Reinstatement takes a `SUSPENDED` facility back to `ACTIVE`, clears `suspended_reason`, and returns every `ON_HOLD` credit on the facility to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order — but it is permitted only when the suspension was manual. A facility suspended for a threshold breach cannot be talked back into life: while `allocated_cents` remains at or above `restrict_threshold_cents` the request is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_STILL_RESTRICTED` (409), because a threshold breach is cleared only by releasing exposure. Closing takes an `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` facility to the terminal `CLOSED` state and sets `utilisation_posture` to `FROZEN`, and it requires that no allocation row on the facility is still `OPEN` — equivalently that `allocated_cents` is zero — failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_HAS_OPEN_ALLOCATION` (409).

**Collateral** moves the other lever. A `CreditOfficer` posts collateral by supplying an `amount_cents`, which is the one money field in this service that may legitimately be negative: a positive value posts collateral, a negative value releases it. An optional `posting_note` may accompany it, and the request must also carry `expected_facility_version` exactly as described in §2.8. The facility's `collateral_posted_cents` becomes its previous value plus the supplied `amount_cents`, postings being applied in arrival order, and the facility's `version` increments. Two guards protect a release. A release that would drive `collateral_posted_cents` below zero is rejected with `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_POSTED` (422). A release that would leave `collateral_posted_cents` below the total collateral credit already granted to the facility's `OPEN` allocations is rejected with `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_BLOCKED` (422), because that collateral is already doing work. Critically, a posting is forward-looking only: it never retroactively changes `committed_allocation_cents` on a credit that has already been issued. Collateral bought headroom for the credits issued after it, not before.

The **correspondent bank network** is registered by a `CreditOfficer` too. A bank carries a `bank_name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a `swift_bic` of either 8 or 11 uppercase alphanumeric characters which must be unique across all banks (a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, 409), a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `country_risk_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive where higher means riskier, an external `credit_rating` drawn from `AA`, `A`, `BBB` and `BB`, a `confirmation_appetite_cents` of at least zero representing the maximum peak concurrent confirmed exposure the bank will carry, and an optional `sanctions_flag` defaulting to false. A new bank starts with `status` `ACTIVE` — the only other permitted value is `SUSPENDED` — and `confirming_line_used_cents` at zero. Its `eligibility_score` is derived immediately: take 100 minus `country_risk_score`, multiply by 6, and add the rating points multiplied by 4, where the rating points are 100 for `AA`, 75 for `A`, 50 for `BBB` and 25 for `BB`. That score is the sort key of the bank directory, which orders by `eligibility_score` descending, then `country_risk_score` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, and which can be narrowed by `country_code`, `status`, `max_country_risk_score` and `credit_rating`.

A bank's risk posture is not frozen at registration. Either a `CreditOfficer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may revise its `status`, `sanctions_flag`, `country_risk_score`, `confirmation_appetite_cents` or `credit_rating`, and `eligibility_score` is re-derived from the new `country_risk_score` and `credit_rating` on the spot. Such a revision is forward-looking as well: it does **not** unwind confirmations the bank has already given. A bank that becomes ineligible simply stops being a candidate for new confirmation requests.

Finally, a `TradeFinanceOfficer` registers each **beneficiary** — the exporter in whose favour credits are issued — with a `beneficiary_name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a two-uppercase-letter `country_code`, a `nominated_bank_id` naming the beneficiary's own bank (which must resolve to a registered bank, else `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND`, 404), and an optional `sanctions_flag` defaulting to false. The beneficiary's `status` starts at `ACTIVE`; its only other value, `BLOCKED`, is reached solely through the document-screening cascade described in §3.8, and a blocked beneficiary is barred from being named on any new credit. The nominated bank matters later: it is always an acceptable presenting bank. Beneficiary and facility listings can be narrowed by `nominated_bank_id`, `country_code` and `status`, and the allocation listings by `status` and `credit_id`.

### 3.2. Drafting a Documentary Credit

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` drafts the credit. The draft names its four counterparties — `applicant_id`, `beneficiary_id`, `facility_id` and `advising_bank_id`, the last being the bank that will advise the credit to the beneficiary — and states the commercial terms: a `currency` of exactly three uppercase letters, an `amount_cents` of at least 100,000 minor units, an `available_by` drawn from `SIGHT_PAYMENT`, `ACCEPTANCE`, `NEGOTIATION` and `DEFERRED_PAYMENT`, an `incoterm` drawn from `EXW`, `FCA`, `FOB`, `CFR`, `CIF`, `CPT`, `CIP`, `DAP`, `DPU` and `DDP`, the `incoterm_place` that must accompany it (1 to 120 non-blank characters — the port of loading for `FOB`, `CFR` and `CIF`, the destination for `DAP` and `DDP`), a `latest_shipment_date`, an `expiry_date`, an `expiry_place` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters naming where documents must be presented, and a `presentation_period_days` between 1 and 30 inclusive. Four inputs are optional: `tolerance_pct`, the upward drawing tolerance, between 0 and 10 inclusive and defaulting to 0; `tenor_days`, between 0 and 360 inclusive and defaulting to 0; and the two shipment permissions `partial_shipment_allowed` and `transhipment_allowed`, both booleans defaulting to false.

Availability and tenor are paired, and the pairing is enforced at draft time. A credit available by `SIGHT_PAYMENT` must have `tenor_days` of exactly 0; a credit available by any of the three usance forms must have `tenor_days` of at least 1. Either violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).

The new draft is stamped with `status` `DRAFT` and `confirmation_status` `UNCONFIRMED` — the two lifecycle dimensions a credit carries, which evolve independently of each other for the whole life of the instrument. Its `amendment_seq` starts at 0, its `drafted_by_user_id` records the acting operator, and `issued_by_user_id`, `issue_date`, `confirming_bank_id` and `cancelled_reason` all start unset. The full enumeration of the primary dimension is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `ON_HOLD`, `ISSUED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, `UTILISED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `UTILISED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; the confirmation dimension enumerates `UNCONFIRMED`, `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` and `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, and has no terminal state at all. Any transition attempted against a credit already in a terminal state is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409).

Three figures are derived on the spot. The drawing ceiling `max_drawable_cents` is `amount_cents` multiplied by the quantity 100 plus `tolerance_pct`, divided by 100 with the remainder discarded — the credit's nominal amount grown by its upward tolerance. The `presentation_deadline_date` is the earlier of the `expiry_date` and the date obtained by adding `presentation_period_days` calendar days to `latest_shipment_date`; that resulting date is an inclusive upper bound. And a **provisional exposure estimate** is written to `provisional_allocation_cents`, which is important to understand for what it is not: it never debits the facility, no allocation row is created, and the facility's `allocated_cents` is untouched.

The provisional estimate is computed by the same five-step exposure rule the bank uses at issuance, but run with the tolerance and the tenor deliberately forced to zero, because a draft is not yet an undertaking. Concretely: the drawable ceiling for this run is `amount_cents` itself (a zero tolerance grows nothing); the usance risk add-on for this run is zero (a zero tenor buys no months); so the gross exposure for this run equals `amount_cents`. Against that gross figure the bank recognises collateral credit as the **smaller** of two caps — the first being ninety percent of the facility's `collateral_posted_cents`, computed by multiplying by 90 and dividing by 100 with the remainder discarded, which is what the applicant has actually posted after the advance-rate haircut; the second being the facility's `collateral_required_pct` applied to the gross figure the same way, which is what the facility's own collateral policy will recognise for an exposure of that size. The provisional figure is the gross exposure minus that collateral credit. Every division in this rule discards its remainder rather than rounding to nearest, in whichever direction that falls, so the bank never under-reserves.

Drafting a credit is also the first point at which the bank refuses business. If the named applicant, the named beneficiary or the named advising bank carries `sanctions_flag` set to true, the draft is refused outright with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403). An applicant whose `kyc_status` is anything other than `CLEARED` yields `ERR_APPLICANT_KYC_NOT_CLEARED` (422). An advising bank whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`, or which carries `sanctions_flag` true, yields `ERR_ADVISING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). A `latest_shipment_date` falling after the `expiry_date` is a logically impossible credit and yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY` (422); the same error also guards the later requirement that `expiry_date` be strictly after `issue_date`. The named facility must belong to the named applicant and be denominated in the credit's own `currency`, failing which the draft is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH` (422); and separately, a facility whose `facility_expiry_date` falls earlier than the credit's `expiry_date` yields `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED` (422), since the facility must outlive the instrument it supports.

**Documentary requirements** are drafted into the credit one at a time, and only while the credit is still `DRAFT` — an attempt on a credit in any other status is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_DRAFT` (409). Each requirement names a `doc_type` from `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, `BILL_OF_LADING`, `AIR_WAYBILL`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE`, `PACKING_LIST`, `CERTIFICATE_OF_ORIGIN`, `INSPECTION_CERTIFICATE` and `DRAFT_BILL_OF_EXCHANGE`, and each `doc_type` may appear at most once per credit. It states `originals_required` and `copies_required`, both integers between 0 and 5 inclusive, an `issuer_constraint` of up to 120 characters naming who must issue the document (for example "any insurance company" or "chamber of commerce"), and an optional `is_mandatory` flag that defaults to true; when a mandatory requirement's `doc_type` is absent from a presentation, that absence becomes a missing-document discrepancy. Requirements drafted before issuance carry `amended_in_seq` of 0, which records the amendment round at which the requirement was added or last altered. Adding requirements does not disturb `provisional_allocation_cents` — documentary terms are free.

Sending the draft to the credit officer moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, optionally accompanied by a `submission_note`, and re-derives `provisional_allocation_cents` by the same provisional rule against the facility's collateral posture as it stands at submission — a collateral posting made between drafting and submission therefore shows up here. The submission is also where the credit's paperwork is checked against its own delivery term. A credit must carry at least one mandatory `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` requirement and at least one transport requirement, meaning a requirement whose `doc_type` is `BILL_OF_LADING` or `AIR_WAYBILL`. On top of that, a credit whose `incoterm` is `CIF` or `CIP` must carry a mandatory `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` requirement, because under those terms the seller insures the goods; conversely, a credit whose `incoterm` is `FOB`, `FCA` or `EXW` must **not** carry an `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` requirement, because under those terms it is not the seller's insurance to produce. Every one of these failures reports `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH` (422). A returned draft may be resubmitted, so the `DRAFT → PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → DRAFT` loop can repeat without limit.

The credit register can be narrowed by `applicant_id`, `beneficiary_id`, `facility_id`, `advising_bank_id`, `confirming_bank_id`, `status` and `confirmation_status`; a credit's requirement list can be narrowed by `doc_type` and `is_mandatory`.

### 3.3. Approval, Issuance and the Facility Exposure Cascade

Approval is the `CreditOfficer`'s decision on whether the applicant's facility should carry this credit, and it is expressed as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN_TO_DRAFT`, optionally with an `approval_note`. Returning the credit takes it `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → DRAFT` and increments its `version`; that is the whole of that branch's effect, and it is what makes the drafting loop cyclic. Approving takes it `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and likewise increments `version`, but carries three guards beyond the state check: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the credit's `drafted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403); the applicant's `kyc_status` must be `CLEARED`, else `ERR_APPLICANT_KYC_NOT_CLEARED`; and the facility named by `facility_id` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Approval grants permission and nothing more — **no facility debit happens here**. The limit is consumed at issuance.

Issuance is the moment the bank binds itself, and it is the single densest operation in this service. A `TradeFinanceOfficer` triggers it, optionally with an `issue_note`, and the credit moves `APPROVED → ISSUED`. Six preconditions gate it. The credit must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED` (409) — and by the ordering of §2.9 that state failure is reported even when the credit would also have blown the facility limit. The facility must be `ACTIVE` and its `utilisation_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The facility's `facility_expiry_date` must be on or after the current clock date, else `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED` (422). The advising bank must have `status` `ACTIVE` and `sanctions_flag` false, else `ERR_ADVISING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). The committed allocation plus the issuance charges must fit inside the facility's `available_cents`, with exact equality admitted, else `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). And the screening of the credit's parties must not come back blocked, else `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403).

The **committed exposure** is the same five-step rule that produced the provisional estimate, now run in earnest: the tolerance and the tenor both count. Step one recomputes `max_drawable_cents` as `amount_cents` multiplied by the quantity 100 plus `tolerance_pct`, divided by 100 with the remainder discarded. Step two prices the usance risk add-on in whole months rounded **up**: for a credit available by `SIGHT_PAYMENT` the month count is zero, and otherwise it is `tenor_days` divided by 30 and rounded up to the next whole number; the add-on is then `max_drawable_cents` multiplied by 25, multiplied by that month count, divided by 10,000 with the remainder discarded — twenty-five basis points of the drawable ceiling for every whole or part month of usance. Step three sets `gross_exposure_cents` to `max_drawable_cents` plus that add-on. Step four computes the eligible collateral credit as the smaller of ninety percent of the facility's `collateral_posted_cents` (multiply by 90, divide by 100, discard the remainder) and the facility's `collateral_required_pct` applied to `gross_exposure_cents` the same way; the facility's `collateral_posted_cents` is read at the instant of this calculation, and postings accepted later never retroactively shrink an allocation already made. Step five sets `committed_allocation_cents` to `gross_exposure_cents` minus that collateral credit. For identical inputs this always returns a figure at least as large as the provisional estimate, and a strictly larger one whenever `tolerance_pct` or `tenor_days` is above zero. No other credit's figures enter the computation; the inputs are exactly this credit and the facility it names.

**Pricing** runs next, and its evaluation order is normative because the rule looks circular and is not. First, the validity of the credit in days is the number of days from `issue_date` to `expiry_date` counting **both ends inclusive** — that is, the day difference plus one. That day count is divided by 90 and rounded up to the next whole number to give the number of chargeable quarters, with a floor of one quarter. Second, the commission tier is selected from `gross_exposure_cents` **alone** and is recorded in `commission_bps_applied`: 15 basis points when `gross_exposure_cents` is at most 5,000,000 cents, 10 basis points when it is above that but at most 50,000,000 cents, and 7 basis points above that. The tier is emphatically **not** selected from `committed_allocation_cents`, not from `chargeable_utilisation_cents`, and not from any figure that already contains a charge. Third, `base_commission_cents` is the larger of a 25,000-cent floor and the result of multiplying `gross_exposure_cents` by `commission_bps_applied` and by the quarter count, dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up. Fourth, `confirmation_charge_cents` is zero whenever `confirming_bank_id` is unset or `confirmation_status` is anything other than `CONFIRMED`; the priced case belongs to §3.4. Fifth and last, `total_charge_cents` is `base_commission_cents` plus `confirmation_charge_cents`, and `chargeable_utilisation_cents` is `committed_allocation_cents` plus `total_charge_cents`. Those last two feed nothing above them.

Issuance also opens the credit's windows. `issue_date` is set to the clock date, `issued_by_user_id` to the acting operator. The `presentation_deadline_date` is re-derived as the earlier of `expiry_date` and `latest_shipment_date` advanced by `presentation_period_days` days, inclusive. The confirmation window is opened with `confirmation_window_start` equal to `issue_date`, inclusive, and `confirmation_window_end` equal to `expiry_date` advanced by `presentation_period_days` days — and that end date is **exclusive**, which is a different convention from the presentation deadline on purpose.

A screening of the credit's parties is created as part of issuance, with `scope` `CREDIT_PARTIES`, `credit_id` pointing at this credit, `presentation_id` unset, and `screened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal value `system` rather than any operator. Its `result` will be `CLEAR` or `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`; a blocked score aborts the entire issuance request with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and persists nothing at all — no screening row, no allocation, no status change. The scoring rule itself is described in §3.8.

What follows is a four-hop cascade, and each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than directly by the request. **Hop one** creates a facility allocation row: its `facility_id` and `credit_id` name the facility and the credit, its `basis` is `COMMITTED_ISSUANCE`, its `status` is `OPEN`, its `allocated_cents` is the credit's `committed_allocation_cents` and its `charge_cents` is the credit's `total_charge_cents`; its `released_cents` starts at 0 and its `released_at` is unset. **Hop two** is caused by hop one: the new open row re-drives the facility's exposure ledger, so the facility's `allocated_cents` rises by exactly the credit's `chargeable_utilisation_cents`, its `available_cents` falls by the same amount, and its `version` increments by one. **Hop three** is caused by hop two: if the facility's new `allocated_cents` has reached or passed `restrict_threshold_cents`, `utilisation_posture` flips `OPEN → RESTRICTED` and the facility's `status` flips `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`. **Hop four** is caused by hop three: the suspension itself moves every sibling credit on the same facility whose status is `APPROVED` — this credit excluded, since it is now `ISSUED` — to `ON_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each sibling's `version` incrementing by one. Finally the issued credit's own `version` increments.

`ON_HOLD` is a narrow state by design: it is reachable only from `APPROVED` and it returns only to `APPROVED`, so no memory of a prior state is ever needed. The return happens automatically whenever the facility's exposure falls back below its restriction threshold and the suspension reason was a threshold breach, or when a credit officer manually reinstates a manually suspended facility.

Cancellation is the compensating action for all of this, and it is available from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `ON_HOLD` and `ISSUED`, always landing on the terminal `CANCELLED`. A `reason` is required and is recorded as `cancelled_reason`. Cancelling an unissued credit needs nothing further — an unissued credit has created no beneficiary rights. Cancelling an `ISSUED` credit is different, because an irrevocable undertaking cannot be withdrawn unilaterally: the request must carry a present and non-blank `beneficiary_consent_reference`, else it is refused with `ERR_BENEFICIARY_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (422). It must also find the credit clean of drawings — every presentation on the credit must have `examination_status` `REFUSED` or a `settlement_status` among `VOID`, `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED` and `REIMBURSED`, and the credit's own `reserved_cents` and `drawn_settled_cents` must both be zero — failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_HAS_LIVE_PRESENTATION` (409).

When cancellation of an issued credit succeeds, the issuance cascade is run backwards. Every `OPEN` allocation row of the credit flips to `RELEASED` with `released_cents` set to that row's own `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents` and `released_at` set to the clock instant, and the row's `version` increments. That release is hop two's cause: the facility's `allocated_cents` falls by exactly `released_cents`, restoring the precise pre-issuance value, and the facility's `version` increments. Hop three follows if the facility's `allocated_cents` is now below `restrict_threshold_cents` and its `suspended_reason` is `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`: `status` returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `utilisation_posture` returns `RESTRICTED → OPEN`. Hop four follows from that: every `ON_HOLD` sibling credit on the facility returns to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order. Any live confirmation occupation is released as well, and the confirming bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` is re-derived. The cancelled credit's own `version` increments.

### 3.4. Confirmation and the Confirming-Line Window

Confirmation is the second, independent lifecycle dimension of a credit. It moves `confirmation_status` without ever touching `status`: a credit can be `ISSUED` and `UNCONFIRMED`, `ISSUED` and `CONFIRMED`, or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` and `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, and the two dimensions never interfere.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` asks a correspondent bank to add its confirmation. The request may name a `confirming_bank_id` explicitly, or omit it and let the selector policy choose, and may carry a `request_note`. The credit must be in status `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409), and its `confirmation_status` must be `UNCONFIRMED` — or `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, which is the retry edge. The clock date must fall inside the credit's half-open confirmation window, on or after `confirmation_window_start` and strictly before `confirmation_window_end`, else `ERR_CONFIRMATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).

Whichever bank ends up resolved must be eligible: its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, its `sanctions_flag` must be false, its `country_risk_score` must be at most 60, and it must not be domiciled in the beneficiary's own country — that is, its `country_code` must differ from the beneficiary's `country_code`, since a confirmation from within the exporter's own jurisdiction adds no country-risk cover. A named bank failing any of these is refused with `ERR_CONFIRMING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). Note one thing the rule does **not** require: the confirming bank need not differ from the advising bank. The bank that advises a credit is entirely free to confirm it too.

The bank's appetite is measured as **peak concurrent occupancy** of its confirming line, not as a lifetime total and not by any pairwise comparison. Each credit that names the bank as its `confirming_bank_id`, whose `confirmation_status` is `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `UTILISED`, occupies the line with a weight equal to its own `gross_exposure_cents` over the half-open interval from its `confirmation_window_start` up to but not including its `confirmation_window_end`. Cancelled credits, expired credits and credits whose confirmation was declined release their occupation immediately and do not appear. To measure the peak, form two events for each occupation — one at the window start adding that occupation's weight, one at the window end subtracting it — sort all events by date ascending and, where two events fall on the same date, by the signed weight ascending so that subtractions are applied before additions, then walk the events in order maintaining a running total and record the highest running total reached. That highest value is written to the bank's `confirming_line_used_cents`. When the measurement is an admission check for a new request, the candidate credit's own occupation is included in the events before the sweep runs, and the request is refused with `ERR_CONFIRMING_LINE_WINDOW_FULL` (409) only when the resulting peak is **strictly greater** than the bank's `confirmation_appetite_cents`; a peak exactly equal to the appetite is admitted. The reason subtractions sort before additions on a shared date is precisely that the intervals are half-open: an occupation ending on a given date frees the line before an occupation starting on that same date takes it.

When `confirming_bank_id` is omitted, the selector picks a bank. The candidate pool is every registered bank whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `sanctions_flag` is false, whose `country_risk_score` is at most 60, whose `country_code` differs from the beneficiary's `country_code`, which has not already returned a decline on this very credit, and whose peak concurrent occupancy including this candidate credit is at most its own `confirmation_appetite_cents`. Each surviving candidate is ranked by its `eligibility_score`: 100 minus its `country_risk_score`, multiplied by 6, plus its rating points multiplied by 4, where the rating points are 100 for `AA`, 75 for `A`, 50 for `BBB` and 25 for `BB`. The highest score wins. Ties are broken first by the lower `country_risk_score` and then by the earlier `created_at` — never by identifier. If the candidate pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMING_BANK` (422); there is no deferred queue and no retry scheduling, and the credit simply stays `UNCONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED` as it was.

A successful request sets the credit's `confirming_bank_id` to the named or selected bank, moves `confirmation_status` to `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, re-derives that bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` and increments the bank's `version` by one, and increments the credit's own `version` by one. The credit's `status` dimension is untouched.

The nominated bank answers for itself. Acting as `CorrespondentBank`, with its `X-User-ID` equal to the credit's `confirming_bank_id` — anything else is `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403) — it supplies a `decision` of `CONFIRM` or `DECLINE`, with a `declined_reason` that is required and must be non-blank on the decline branch (a missing or blank one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).

Confirming moves `confirmation_status` `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMED` and finally prices the confirmation charge. The rate is 20 basis points plus one further basis point for each point of the confirming bank's `country_risk_score`; `confirmation_charge_cents` is then `gross_exposure_cents` multiplied by that rate, multiplied by the same inclusive-both-ends quarter count used at issuance, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — computed from `gross_exposure_cents` and never from any running total. The charge is added into `total_charge_cents` and into `chargeable_utilisation_cents`. That triggers a cascade of its own: the credit's `OPEN` allocation row has its `charge_cents` raised by `confirmation_charge_cents`, which raises the facility's `allocated_cents` by the same amount, which may itself flip the facility's `utilisation_posture` to `RESTRICTED` and its `status` to `SUSPENDED`, which in turn moves that facility's `APPROVED` siblings to `ON_HOLD` in `created_at` ascending order. If the added charge would breach the facility's headroom, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). The credit's `version` increments.

Declining moves `confirmation_status` `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, clears `confirming_bank_id` back to unset, re-derives the declining bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` — its occupation is removed — and increments that bank's `version` by one, as well as the credit's. The recorded `declined_reason` is what keeps that bank out of the candidate pool for any later automatic selection on this credit. A fresh request may then be made against a different bank, closing the retry cycle `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMATION_DECLINED → CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, which carries exactly the same eligibility, country-risk and confirming-line preconditions and exactly the same side effects as the first request did.

### 3.5. Amendment Negotiation and Beneficiary Consent

An issued credit's terms are altered only through an Amendment, which is a two-stage instrument: it is drafted, then issued, and only on issuance does it either bind at once or open a consent window for the beneficiary.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` drafts an amendment against a credit by supplying a `change_set`, a `reason` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters, and an `expected_credit_version`. The `change_set` is a map from credit field name to new value carrying between 1 and 5 keys, and every key must be drawn from the amendable set: `amount_cents`, `tolerance_pct`, `expiry_date`, `latest_shipment_date`, `incoterm`, `incoterm_place`, `presentation_period_days`, `partial_shipment_allowed`, `transhipment_allowed`. Nothing else about a credit can be amended — not the beneficiary, not the facility, not `available_by`, not the nominated bank. Before the set is judged, every key whose new value equals the credit's current value is discarded as a no-op; if the surviving set is empty, or if any key names a field outside the amendable set, the draft is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET` (422). Drafting is permitted only while the credit's `status` is `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409); and the supplied `expected_credit_version` must equal the credit's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` (412).

A successful draft sets `status` to `DRAFT`, records `drafted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and assigns `seq` as one plus the number of Amendment rows that already exist for this credit. That counter is strictly monotone per credit and numbers are never reused, even when an earlier amendment was rejected, lapsed or superseded. `requires_beneficiary_consent` and `exposure_delta_cents` are computed at draft time as well. Because drafting is itself a write on the credit, the credit's own `version` increments — which is exactly why a second draft attempted from the same captured version is rejected. When a newer amendment is created for a credit while an earlier one is still `DRAFT`, the earlier one moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` as a system event, taking `resolved_at` at the clock instant and `window_end_effective` at the clock date, and incrementing its own `version`. A superseded amendment never occupied a consent window and is invisible to the overlap rule.

`requires_beneficiary_consent` follows a narrow product rule rather than a blanket one: a change that can only improve the beneficiary's position binds on issuance, and only a change that reduces the beneficiary's rights is held for consent. It is true when any one of the following holds of the surviving `change_set`: `amount_cents` is present and lower than the credit's current `amount_cents`; `tolerance_pct` is present and lower than the current `tolerance_pct`; `expiry_date` is present and earlier than the current `expiry_date`; `latest_shipment_date` is present and earlier than the current `latest_shipment_date`; `presentation_period_days` is present and lower than the current `presentation_period_days`; `incoterm` is present and different from the current `incoterm`; `incoterm_place` is present and different from the current `incoterm_place`; `partial_shipment_allowed` is present, newly false, and currently true; or `transhipment_allowed` is present, newly false, and currently true. Otherwise it is false. Note the asymmetry between the field families: a numeric or date field triggers consent only when it moves against the beneficiary, whereas any change at all to `incoterm` or `incoterm_place` triggers consent, and the two boolean permissions trigger consent only when a permission is withdrawn — granting one that was previously withheld does not. Because no-op keys are discarded first, a key restated at its current value never triggers consent.

`exposure_delta_cents` is the committed-issuance exposure of the credit as it would stand after applying the `change_set`, minus the credit's current `committed_allocation_cents`. It is positive for increases and negative for decreases.

A different `TradeFinanceOfficer` then issues the amendment, optionally with an `issue_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The amendment's `status` must be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_DRAFT` (409), and the credit must still be `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409). The prospective terms are re-validated: a `change_set` that would leave `latest_shipment_date` after `expiry_date` is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY` (422), and one that would break the incoterm-to-insurance-document pairing is refused with `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH` (422).

On both branches, and before the branch is taken, the amendment acquires its window: `consent_window_start` is set to the clock date, `consent_deadline_date` to the clock date plus ten days, and `window_end_effective` to `consent_deadline_date`. Every issued amendment owns an interval — a consent-free amendment is not exempt from the cooling-off buffer.

That interval must not collide with any other amendment interval on the same credit. The comparison is **pairwise and over closed intervals widened by a symmetric two-day cooling-off buffer**; one conflicting neighbour is enough, and there is no capacity to aggregate. Let the new interval run from the clock date to the clock date plus ten days, both ends inclusive. Then, over every other Amendment row of the same credit ordered by `created_at` ascending, skip any row whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` (it never occupied a window) and any row whose `consent_window_start` is unset (it is still a draft). For each remaining row, take its `consent_window_start` as the neighbour's start and its `window_end_effective` as the neighbour's end — the deadline date while the amendment is live, the resolution date once it is terminal. The intervals collide, and the issue is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), when the new start is on or before the neighbour's end plus two days **and** the new end is on or after the neighbour's start minus two days. Both comparisons admit equality, so touching endpoints do collide here — the opposite of the confirming-line sweep, where an interval ending on a date frees capacity for one starting on it. Terminal amendments keep occupying their window up to `window_end_effective` plus the buffer, which is what enforces a genuine cooling-off period between negotiation rounds.

The amendment fee is priced once, here at issuance, and is not recomputed if the beneficiary later rejects the amendment. When `exposure_delta_cents` is zero or negative, `amendment_fee_cents` is the flat 7,500 cents. When it is positive, `amendment_fee_cents` is 7,500 cents plus `exposure_delta_cents` multiplied by a recharge rate in basis points, multiplied by the remaining quarter count, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The remaining quarter count is the number of days from the clock date to the credit's `expiry_date` inclusive of both ends, divided by 90 and rounded up. The recharge rate is selected by the same three-tier ladder used at issuance — 15 basis points at or below 5,000,000 cents, 10 basis points at or below 50,000,000 cents, 7 basis points above — but keyed on the **new** `gross_exposure_cents` alone. That recharge rate is a purely local value used to price this one fee; it is never written back to the credit's `commission_bps_applied`, which keeps the tier chosen at issuance for the life of the credit.

Issuance then forks, and the two branches have genuinely divergent side-effect sets.

When `requires_beneficiary_consent` is false, the amendment traverses `DRAFT → ISSUED → ACCEPTED` inside the single issue call — a favourable amendment binds on issuance. `ISSUED` is never observable as a resting state on either branch. Because the window closed the instant it opened, `window_end_effective` is immediately pulled back from `consent_deadline_date` to the clock date. The `change_set` is applied to the credit and the full exposure and charge cascade described below runs. The credit's `status` stays `ISSUED` throughout; it never enters `AMENDMENT_PENDING` on this branch.

When `requires_beneficiary_consent` is true, the amendment traverses `DRAFT → ISSUED → PENDING_CONSENT`, `window_end_effective` stays at `consent_deadline_date`, the credit moves `ISSUED → AMENDMENT_PENDING`, and the `change_set` is **not** applied. No credit term, no exposure figure and no ledger changes yet; the consent-required branch defers every field change.

The beneficiary answers with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the credit's `beneficiary_id`, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). The amendment's `status` must be `PENDING_CONSENT`, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_PENDING_CONSENT` (409). The clock date must be on or before `consent_deadline_date` — the interval is closed, so a response landing exactly on the deadline date is in time — and a later response is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).

`ACCEPT` moves `PENDING_CONSENT → ACCEPTED`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant, and pulls `window_end_effective` back to the clock date, which is the point from which the cooling-off buffer for any subsequent amendment is measured. Then a four-hop cascade runs. Hop one applies the `change_set` to the credit, sets the credit's `amendment_seq` to this amendment's `seq`, and returns the credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED`. Exactly these credit fields are re-derived: `presentation_deadline_date` and the confirmation window bounds, `max_drawable_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents`, `committed_allocation_cents`, `chargeable_utilisation_cents` and `available_cents`. Exactly these are **not** re-priced: `commission_bps_applied`, `base_commission_cents`, `confirmation_charge_cents` and `total_charge_cents`. The commission is earned once at issuance and an amendment only adds `amendment_fee_cents` on top of it.

Hop two re-cuts the allocation rather than editing it in place. The credit's existing `OPEN` FacilityAllocation row flips to `RELEASED`, with `released_cents` set to that row's own `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents` and `released_at` set to the clock instant, and exactly one new row is opened whose `allocated_cents` is the credit's new `committed_allocation_cents` and whose `charge_cents` is the released row's `charge_cents` plus the amendment's `amendment_fee_cents`. The new row's `basis` is `AMENDED_INCREASE` when `exposure_delta_cents` is strictly positive and `AMENDED_DECREASE` otherwise — a zero delta takes the `AMENDED_DECREASE` label. Note the deliberate asymmetry this creates: the amendment fee occupies the facility through the allocation's `charge_cents`, but the credit's own `chargeable_utilisation_cents` remains `committed_allocation_cents` plus `total_charge_cents` under the pinned charge order and therefore excludes amendment fees altogether. The two figures are meant to differ after an amendment.

Hop three re-derives the facility's `allocated_cents`, `available_cents` and `utilisation_posture` from the new open-row set. Hop four is bidirectional: crossing `restrict_threshold_cents` upward suspends the facility and holds its `APPROVED` siblings, while falling back below the threshold reactivates the facility and returns its `ON_HOLD` siblings to `APPROVED`, in either case in `created_at` ascending order. A decreasing amendment is therefore a legitimate route out of `SUSPENDED`. If the accepted amendment's new exposure would breach the facility's headroom, the whole acceptance is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) and the amendment stays `PENDING_CONSENT` — it is not rejected, and the beneficiary may accept again once headroom is restored.

`REJECT` moves `PENDING_CONSENT → REJECTED`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant and `window_end_effective` to the clock date, returns the credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` on its original terms, and sets `amendment_fee_cents` to 0 — no fee is charged for a rejected amendment. No credit field, no FacilityAllocation row and no facility field changes at all.

If neither answer arrives, the amendment lapses. When the clock date is strictly past `consent_deadline_date`, the expiry sweep moves it `PENDING_CONSENT → EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant, sets `amendment_fee_cents` to 0, and — unlike the accept and reject branches — leaves `window_end_effective` at `consent_deadline_date` rather than pulling it back to the clock date, so a lapsed amendment continues to block the calendar for its full ten days plus buffer. The credit returns `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` with no field changes. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Presentation of Documents and the Examination Period

A Presentation is the file of documents tendered under a credit. It carries two independent status dimensions that evolve without disturbing each other: `examination_status`, which records where the documents stand against the credit's terms, and `settlement_status`, which records where the money stands. `examination_status` is one of `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `COMPLYING`, `DISCREPANT`, `WAIVER_REQUESTED`, `REFUSED` or `PRECLUDED`, starting at `RECEIVED`, with `COMPLYING`, `REFUSED` and `PRECLUDED` terminal. `settlement_status` is one of `UNSETTLED`, `CLAIM_LODGED`, `PAID`, `ACCEPTED`, `MATURED`, `NEGOTIATED`, `REIMBURSED` or `VOID`, starting at `UNSETTLED`, with `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED`, `REIMBURSED` and `VOID` terminal. Submission, examination and waiver handling move `examination_status` while `settlement_status` stays pinned at `UNSETTLED`; claiming and settling move `settlement_status` while `examination_status` stays pinned at `COMPLYING` or `PRECLUDED`. The one place the dimensions interact is refusal.

A `CorrespondentBank` opens a presentation file by naming the `credit_id`, the `presenting_bank_id` and optionally a `cover_letter_reference` of up to 60 characters. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `presenting_bank_id` — a bank may not present on another bank's behalf — else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). The credit must be `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409): documents may be presented while an amendment is out for consent. The presenting bank must be nominated, meaning `presenting_bank_id` equals the beneficiary's `nominated_bank_id`, or the credit's `advising_bank_id`, or the credit's `confirming_bank_id`; any other bank is refused with `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED` (422). The new file takes `examination_status` `RECEIVED`, `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED`, `received_at` at the clock instant, `received_date` as the clock date, `document_count` 0 and `drawn_amount_cents` 0. `examination_deadline` deliberately stays unset until submission: the five banking days run from the day the documents are actually presented as a complete set, not from the day the file was opened.

The same bank then adds documents one at a time, supplying `doc_type`, `originals_presented` and `copies_presented` (each 0 to 5 inclusive), `issue_date`, and optionally `shipment_date`, `invoice_amount_cents`, `goods_description` (up to 500 characters), `hs_code` (either empty or 6 to 10 digits), `consignee_country` (either empty or exactly two uppercase letters), `transit_country_codes` (an array of two-letter codes, empty by default) and `issuer_name` (up to 200 characters). `doc_type` is one of `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, `BILL_OF_LADING`, `AIR_WAYBILL`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE`, `PACKING_LIST`, `CERTIFICATE_OF_ORIGIN`, `INSPECTION_CERTIFICATE` or `DRAFT_BILL_OF_EXCHANGE`. `shipment_date` is the on-board or dispatch date and is expected for `BILL_OF_LADING` and `AIR_WAYBILL` and unset otherwise. `invoice_amount_cents` is required and must be strictly greater than zero when `doc_type` is `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); for every other document type it stays at its default of zero. Documents may be added only while `examination_status` is `RECEIVED`, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409). Each accepted document increments the presentation's `document_count`. Presented documents are immutable once recorded: there is no update path, no delete path, and — as noted in the record conventions — no `updated_at` and no `version` on them at all. A mis-tendered document is corrected by opening a fresh presentation, not by editing this one.

When the file is complete the presenting bank submits it, optionally with a `submission_note`. Submission requires `examination_status` to be `RECEIVED` and the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `presenting_bank_id`. The file must carry at least one document, and every mandatory `DocumentaryRequirement` of the credit must be answered by at least one document of the matching `doc_type` whose `originals_presented` is at least the requirement's `originals_required`; a file with no documents or an unanswered mandatory requirement is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (422). The clock date must be on or before the credit's `presentation_deadline_date`, that date itself included, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).

Submission freezes the money figures. `drawn_amount_cents` becomes the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over the file's `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` documents and is never re-summed afterwards, and `document_count` is likewise frozen. That frozen drawn amount must be less than or equal to the credit's `available_cents`; exact equality is admitted, and only a strict excess is refused, with `ERR_CREDIT_DRAWABLE_EXCEEDED` (422). `submitted_at` is set to the clock instant, `received_date` is set to the clock date at submission, and `maturity_date` becomes the clock date plus the credit's `tenor_days` when `available_by` is anything other than `SIGHT_PAYMENT`, and stays unset for a sight credit whose `tenor_days` is zero.

`examination_deadline` is then computed as **five banking days following the day of presentation**, at `23:59:59.999Z` on the fifth such day. Start from `received_date` and walk forward one calendar day at a time, counting a day only when it is Monday through Friday, until five have been counted; the day the walk lands on is the deadline date. The day of presentation itself is never counted, even when it is a banking day. There is no holiday calendar: Saturday and Sunday are the only non-banking days, so the deadline is reproducible from the date alone. The deadline instant is inside the period — a conclusion or refusal stamped at exactly `23:59:59.999Z` on the fifth banking day is timely, and one millisecond later is not.

Submission also creates a `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` ScreeningCheck over the file, scored as described in the screening section. A `BLOCKED` outcome aborts the submission with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and leaves the presentation at `RECEIVED`.

Finally, submission moves `examination_status` `RECEIVED → UNDER_EXAMINATION` and re-derives the credit's drawable ledger, which is a pure aggregation over that credit's presentations. The live set is every presentation of the credit whose `settlement_status` is `UNSETTLED`, `CLAIM_LODGED`, `ACCEPTED` or `MATURED` **and** whose `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `COMPLYING`, `DISCREPANT`, `WAIVER_REQUESTED` or `PRECLUDED`. The settled set is every presentation whose `settlement_status` is `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED` or `REIMBURSED`. The credit's `reserved_cents` is the sum of `drawn_amount_cents` over the live set, `drawn_settled_cents` is the sum over the settled set, and `available_cents` is `max_drawable_cents` minus `reserved_cents` minus `drawn_settled_cents`. A presentation still at `RECEIVED` has not frozen its drawn amount and belongs to neither set; a presentation whose `settlement_status` is `VOID` belongs to neither set either, which is precisely what makes refusal a clean compensating action. The credit's `version` increments on the submit, and so does the presentation's.

Presentation files can be listed with the filters `credit_id`, `presenting_bank_id`, `examination_status` and `settlement_status`; a file's documents can be listed filtered by `doc_type`.

### 3.7. Discrepancies, Waiver Requests, Refusal and Preclusion

A `DocumentExaminer` records each defect found on comparing the documents with the credit as a Discrepancy, supplying a `code`, a `severity` of `MATERIAL` or `MINOR`, and a `description` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters. The `code` is one of the fixed list `LATE_SHIPMENT`, `LATE_PRESENTATION`, `CREDIT_EXPIRED`, `MISSING_DOCUMENT`, `INSUFFICIENT_ORIGINALS`, `INVOICE_OVERDRAWN`, `GOODS_DESCRIPTION_MISMATCH`, `INCOTERM_MISMATCH`, `INSURANCE_UNDERVALUED` or `TRANSHIPMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`; a code drawn from neither the credit's own requirements nor that fixed list is refused with `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN` (422). A code already recorded on this presentation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409) — the pair of presentation and code is unique, so one defect is cited once. Discrepancies may be raised only while `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409), and only while the clock instant is at or before `examination_deadline`, else `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409). Each new row takes `status` `IDENTIFIED`, records `raised_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, sets the presentation's `examined_by_user_id` to the same value, and increments the presentation's `discrepancy_count`. A discrepancy's own dimension is small: `IDENTIFIED` is the initial state and `WAIVED` and `UPHELD` are both terminal, and neither is ever reached by direct call — only as a consequence of a waiver response, a waiver expiry, or a refusal.

`discrepancy_count` is a pure aggregation: the number of Discrepancy rows of the presentation whose `status` is not `WAIVED`. `UPHELD` rows therefore still count, which is why denying a waiver leaves the count exactly where it was while granting one lowers it.

The examiner then concludes the examination, optionally with a `conclusion_note`. The presentation must be `UNDER_EXAMINATION` and the clock instant must be at or before `examination_deadline`; past that instant the conclusion is refused with `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409) and the presentation must instead be swept to `PRECLUDED`. The conclusion is also barred while the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening for the presentation still sits at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, which returns `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409). The outcome is decided purely by the count: `discrepancy_count` equal to zero moves `UNDER_EXAMINATION → COMPLYING`, and a count of one or more moves `UNDER_EXAMINATION → DISCREPANT`. Either way `examined_by_user_id` records the concluding examiner. A second conclusion on an already-concluded presentation is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED` (409).

From `DISCREPANT` the examiner may ask the applicant to waive named defects, supplying `discrepancy_codes` — between 1 and 10 elements with no duplicates — and an optional `request_note`. Every element must match a Discrepancy of this presentation whose `status` is `IDENTIFIED` **and** whose `severity` is `MINOR`; a `MATERIAL` defect cannot be waived by the applicant at all, and citing one, or citing a code that is not an identified discrepancy of this presentation, is refused with `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN` (422). At most one waiver request per presentation may be live: a second attempt while one is `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS` (409). A request is likewise barred while the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening sits at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, with `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409).

The request takes `status` `PENDING`, `requested_at` at the clock instant and `requested_by_user_id` as the acting examiner, and moves the presentation `DISCREPANT → WAIVER_REQUESTED`. Its own `deadline_at` is the **earlier** of two instants: the date part of `requested_at` plus two days at `23:59:59.999Z`, and the presentation's `examination_deadline`. Requesting a waiver does **not** extend `examination_deadline` — the five banking days keep running underneath the waiver window, so a waiver raised late in the examination period gets a window shorter than two days, and a waiver raised on the last banking day gets a window ending at the same instant as the examination period itself. The cited discrepancies stay `IDENTIFIED` for the whole duration of the window; nothing moves them until an answer arrives.

The applicant answers with a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY` and an optional `applicant_note` of up to 500 characters. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `applicant_id` of the presentation's credit, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). The request must be `PENDING`, else `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PENDING` (409), and the clock instant must be at or before `deadline_at` — that instant included — else `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), in which case the request must instead be swept to `EXPIRED`. Either answer sets `responded_at` to the clock instant.

`GRANT` moves the request `PENDING → GRANTED` and every cited Discrepancy `IDENTIFIED → WAIVED`, which lowers `discrepancy_count`. Where the presentation lands then depends on whether the waiver was total: if `discrepancy_count` reaches zero the presentation moves `WAIVER_REQUESTED → COMPLYING` and may be settled, but if at least one `IDENTIFIED` discrepancy remains uncited and therefore unwaived, the presentation returns `WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT` instead. A partial grant is not a compliance event.

`DENY` moves the request `PENDING → DENIED`, moves every cited Discrepancy `IDENTIFIED → UPHELD`, leaves `discrepancy_count` unchanged because upheld rows still count, and returns the presentation `WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT`. This closes the cycle `DISCREPANT → WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT`, and a second waiver request naming the remaining codes is permitted so long as the examination period is still open. If no answer arrives at all, the expiry sweep moves the request `PENDING → EXPIRED`, moves the cited discrepancies `IDENTIFIED → UPHELD`, and returns the presentation to `DISCREPANT` exactly as a denial would. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal for a waiver request.

A discrepant presentation is finally refused by the examiner, who supplies a `disposal_instruction` of `HOLD_AT_DISPOSAL` or `RETURN_TO_PRESENTER` and an optional `refusal_note`. The presentation must be `DISCREPANT` with `discrepancy_count` of at least one, and the clock instant must be at or before `examination_deadline`, else `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409) — past that instant the bank is preclusion-barred and must honour, whatever the documents look like. No waiver request may still be `PENDING`; because a pending waiver leaves the presentation in `WAIVER_REQUESTED`, that situation returns the specific `ERR_WAIVER_RESPONSE_PENDING` (409) rather than the generic state error. Only one refusal notice may ever be sent per presentation, so a second attempt is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED` (409).

Refusal moves `examination_status` `DISCREPANT → REFUSED`, which is terminal, sets `refusal_notice_sent_at` to the clock instant, records the `disposal_instruction`, and moves every remaining `IDENTIFIED` Discrepancy to `UPHELD`. Then the three-hop compensating cascade runs. Hop one moves `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED → VOID`. Hop two follows from hop one: a void presentation leaves the drawable ledger's live set, so the credit's `reserved_cents` falls by exactly this presentation's `drawn_amount_cents`. Hop three follows from hop two: `available_cents` is re-derived as `max_drawable_cents` minus `reserved_cents` minus `drawn_settled_cents`, restoring precisely the value the credit had before the submission, and the credit's `version` increments. The facility is deliberately untouched: `allocated_cents` does not move, because facility exposure is committed at issuance and not at drawing, and the credit itself remains outstanding.

If the examination period runs out with the file still open, the expiry sweep precludes the presentation, and the bank loses its right to assert non-compliance. Three edges lead there, all system-driven and all requiring the clock instant to be strictly past `examination_deadline`. From `UNDER_EXAMINATION` the presentation moves straight to `PRECLUDED` and the drawing **stays** reserved — the bank must honour, so the money remains committed. From `DISCREPANT` it moves to `PRECLUDED` only while `refusal_notice_sent_at` is still unset, and every `IDENTIFIED` Discrepancy stays `IDENTIFIED`: on record, but no longer actionable. From `WAIVER_REQUESTED` it moves to `PRECLUDED` too, with the open waiver request moved `PENDING → EXPIRED` earlier in the same sweep, before the preclusion flip. A precluded presentation is settled exactly as a complying one is.

Discrepancies can be listed filtered by `status` and `severity`, and waiver requests filtered by `status`.

### 3.8. Sanctions and Dual-Use Screening

A ScreeningCheck is an immutable-by-convention scoring record. Every check carries the `credit_id` it belongs to — always set, including for document-scope checks, where it is taken from the presentation's credit — a `presentation_id` set only when the `scope` is `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` and unset for `CREDIT_PARTIES`, the `scope` itself, the four hit counters `country_hits`, `dual_use_hits`, `keyword_hits` and `transit_hits`, the resulting `risk_score`, and a `result` that is one of `PENDING`, `CLEAR`, `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, `BLOCKED`, `RELEASED` or `ESCALATED`, starting at `PENDING`. `screened_by_user_id` records the `ComplianceOfficer` who ran the check, or the literal string `system` when the check was created by the issuance cascade. `reviewed_by_user_id`, `review_note` and `reviewed_at` stay unset until a review happens.

Scoring uses four fixed reference lists. The sanctioned country codes are `IR`, `KP`, `SY` and `CU`. The high-risk transit codes are `AE`, `HK` and `TR`. The dual-use tariff prefixes are `8471`, `8542`, `9013` and `2811`, matched against the first four characters of a document's `hs_code`. The controlled keywords are `centrifuge`, `maraging` and `gyroscope`, matched as case-insensitive substrings of a document's lowercased `goods_description`.

For the `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` scope the inputs are every PresentedDocument row of the presentation, invoices and non-invoices alike. `country_hits` is the number of documents whose `consignee_country` is a sanctioned code **or** any element of whose `transit_country_codes` is a sanctioned code; a document matching in several ways at once counts exactly once. `dual_use_hits` is the number of documents whose `hs_code` begins with one of the dual-use prefixes. `keyword_hits` is the number of documents whose `goods_description` contains any controlled keyword. `transit_hits` is counted differently from the others: it is the number of document-and-transit-code **pairs** where the transit code is a high-risk transit code and is not also a sanctioned code, so one document routed through two high-risk countries contributes two transit hits, while a transit through a sanctioned country contributes to `country_hits` and never to `transit_hits`.

For the `CREDIT_PARTIES` scope the inputs are exactly three parties — the credit's applicant, its beneficiary and its advising bank. The confirming bank is not screened here; it is vetted separately at confirmation time through the confirming-bank eligibility precondition. `country_hits` is the number of those three parties whose `country_code` is a sanctioned code, plus one further hit for each of those three parties whose `sanctions_flag` is true. A party that is both domiciled in a sanctioned country and flagged therefore contributes two. `dual_use_hits`, `keyword_hits` and `transit_hits` are all zero on this scope.

`risk_score` is 100 per country hit, plus 60 per dual-use hit, plus 40 per keyword hit, plus 20 per transit hit, and the total is then capped at 200 — a score never exceeds 200 however many hits accumulate. The `result` follows from the score against two inclusive lower bounds: a score of 100 or more gives `BLOCKED`, a score of 40 or more but below 100 gives `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, and anything below 40 gives `CLEAR`. So exactly 40 reviews and exactly 100 blocks. Each of those three system transitions out of `PENDING` persists all four hit counters and the `risk_score`, and increments the check's `version`.

When a document-scope check scores `BLOCKED`, the beneficiary of the credit is moved `ACTIVE → BLOCKED`, which itself bars that beneficiary from being named on any new credit. When a `CREDIT_PARTIES` check would score `BLOCKED`, the behaviour is deliberately different: the issuance is aborted with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` and **no** ScreeningCheck row is persisted at all, so a blocked issuance leaves no screening history behind.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may re-run screening over a presentation's documents at any time, supplying a `scope` and an optional `screening_note`. Each run creates a new row — screening history is append-only, rows are never updated in place — and `screened_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The newest check for a given scope is the one every gate reads; older rows are history.

A check sitting at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` is adjudicated by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `decision` of `RELEASE`, `BLOCK` or `ESCALATE` and an optional `review_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `screened_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — the officer who ran a screening may not clear it. Only a check at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` is reviewable; any other `result` gives `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (409). All three branches set `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at` and `review_note` and increment the check's `version`, and the three side-effect sets then diverge deliberately. `RELEASE` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → RELEASED` and unblocks settlement. `BLOCK` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → BLOCKED`, freezes every live presentation and claim under the check, and — when the `scope` is `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` — cascades the beneficiary `ACTIVE → BLOCKED` just as an automatic block does. `ESCALATE` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → ESCALATED` and is the third branch of the fork: unlike `RELEASE` it does not unblock settlement, and unlike `BLOCK` it does **not** cascade to the beneficiary's `status` — the beneficiary stays `ACTIVE` and the presentation stays settleable in principle, barred only pending an external licence decision. `CLEAR`, `BLOCKED`, `RELEASED` and `ESCALATED` are all terminal; in particular there is no edge back from `ESCALATED` to `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, because the dual-use question has left the bank's hands.

The three non-clear results map onto three distinct settlement gate errors, and the mapping is exact. A newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` check at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` blocks settlement with `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED` (409). One at `ESCALATED` blocks settlement with `ERR_DUAL_USE_ESCALATED` (409). One at `BLOCKED` blocks settlement with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403). Settlement proceeds only when the newest check is `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`. `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409) is a different error with a different scope: it belongs to concluding an examination and to raising a waiver request, and is never returned by settlement.

Screening checks can be listed per credit filtered by `scope`, `result` and `presentation_id`, and per presentation filtered by `result`.

### 3.9. Settlement, Reimbursement and the Clock-Driven Sweep

A complying or precluded presentation is turned into money by a SettlementClaim. The presenting bank lodges it, naming a `claim_type` of `PAYMENT`, `ACCEPTANCE`, `NEGOTIATION`, `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` or `REIMBURSEMENT`, a `claimed_amount_cents` of at least 1, and an optional `claim_note`. A claim's own `status` is `LODGED`, `SETTLED` or `REJECTED`, starting at `LODGED`, and every fee and proceeds field — `variant_applied`, `commission_bps_applied`, `commission_cents`, `discount_cents`, `doc_examination_fee_cents`, `days_to_maturity` and `net_proceeds_cents` — stays at zero or unset until the claim is settled.

Lodging requires the presentation's `examination_status` to be `COMPLYING` or `PRECLUDED`; a precluded bank has lost the right to refuse and must honour, so it stands in the same position as a complying one. Anything else is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_COMPLYING` (409). The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the presentation's `presenting_bank_id`, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403), and that bank must still be a nominated bank of the credit — its `presenting_bank_id` must equal the credit's `advising_bank_id`, the credit's `confirming_bank_id` or the beneficiary's `nominated_bank_id` — else `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED` (422). At most one claim per presentation may be live, so a second attempt while one is `LODGED` is refused with `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS` (409). If the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening is `BLOCKED` the claim is refused with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403), and that safety check is evaluated **before** the claim-type coherence check, so a blocked presentation lodged with an inadmissible `claim_type` returns the sanctions error and not the mismatch error.

`claim_type` must match what the credit's `available_by` admits, else `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). The admission table is exact: `SIGHT_PAYMENT` admits `PAYMENT` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `NEGOTIATION` admits `NEGOTIATION` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `ACCEPTANCE` admits `ACCEPTANCE` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` admits `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` and `REIMBURSEMENT`. Every availability admits `REIMBURSEMENT`, and no availability admits any other form than its own. `claimed_amount_cents` must equal the presentation's frozen `drawn_amount_cents` exactly — no partial claim and no over-claim — else `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422).

Lodging sets the claim's `status` to `LODGED`, derives `variant_applied` from `claim_type` and the credit's `available_by`, and moves the presentation's `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED → CLAIM_LODGED`. A post-maturity reimbursement claim instead moves it `MATURED → CLAIM_LODGED`: the settlement dimension is cyclic through `CLAIM_LODGED` for usance credits, and this second visit is a fresh claim row with `claim_type` `REIMBURSEMENT`, checked for the same acting-principal identity and the same amount equality.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` then settles the claim, optionally with a `settlement_note`. The claim must be `LODGED`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_LODGED` (409), and the presentation must be at `CLAIM_LODGED`. The newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening must be `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`, with the three gate errors as described in the screening section. On the acceptance and deferred-payment forms the presentation's `maturity_date` must be set, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) — a sight-only presentation cannot be accepted at maturity.

The proceeds are then priced as a strict waterfall, in three costing steps and one deduction step. Step one is commission, taken as a single rounding on the whole drawn amount: `commission_bps_applied` is 12 basis points when the credit's `available_by` is `SIGHT_PAYMENT` and 20 basis points otherwise, and `commission_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` multiplied by `commission_bps_applied`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Step two is the per-document examination fee, and its rounding order is pinned: walk the presentation's PresentedDocument rows in `created_at` ascending order, and for each one round its own fee to whole cents **before** adding it to the running total. A `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` contributes its `invoice_amount_cents` multiplied by 5 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; every other document type contributes a flat 3,500 cents. Every row contributes, not only invoices. Summing exact per-document amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ from the correct answer by up to half the `document_count`. Step three is the discount charge, which applies only to the usance forms: for a sight honour, `days_to_maturity` is 0 and `discount_cents` is 0, while for the usance forms `days_to_maturity` is the whole number of days from the clock date to the presentation's `maturity_date` and `discount_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` multiplied by 450 basis points, multiplied by `days_to_maturity`, divided by the product of 10,000 and a 360-day year basis, rounded half up. Step four deducts the charges from the drawn amount in the fixed order commission, then discount, then document fees: `net_proceeds_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` minus `commission_cents` minus `discount_cents` minus `doc_examination_fee_cents`. The net may never go below zero, and a result that would be negative is refused with `ERR_CHARGES_EXCEED_PROCEEDS` (422). The drawn amount used throughout is the value frozen at submission, never a re-sum at settlement time.

Three variants use that arithmetic differently, and which one applied is recorded on the claim as `variant_applied`, whose value is `SIGHT_HONOUR`, `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT` or `ACCEPTANCE_AT_MATURITY`. `SIGHT_HONOUR` applies to a `PAYMENT` claim on a `SIGHT_PAYMENT` credit and to every `REIMBURSEMENT` claim: commission at 12 basis points and no discount, which for an identical presentation always yields a strictly larger net than the negotiation variant. `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT` applies to a `NEGOTIATION` claim: commission at 20 basis points and a discount charged for the whole run from the settlement date to `maturity_date` on the 360-day basis, so the same drawn amount and the same documents yield materially smaller proceeds. `ACCEPTANCE_AT_MATURITY` applies to `ACCEPTANCE` and `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` claims and uses arithmetic identical to `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT`, but with a different money movement.

Settling sets `status` `LODGED → SETTLED`, `settled_at` to the clock instant and `settled_by_user_id` to the acting officer, and writes `commission_cents`, `discount_cents`, `doc_examination_fee_cents`, `days_to_maturity` and `net_proceeds_cents`. Then the presentation's settlement dimension moves according to the variant. A sight honour on a `PAYMENT` claim moves `CLAIM_LODGED → PAID`, and the drawing moves out of the credit's `reserved_cents` into its `drawn_settled_cents` — both by exactly `drawn_amount_cents`, so `available_cents` is unchanged by settlement. A negotiation moves `CLAIM_LODGED → NEGOTIATED` with the same money movement. An acceptance or deferred payment moves `CLAIM_LODGED → ACCEPTED` and deliberately does **not** release `reserved_cents` and does not raise `drawn_settled_cents`: the bank's undertaking runs until maturity, so the money stays committed. Once the clock date reaches or passes `maturity_date`, the expiry sweep moves that presentation `ACCEPTED → MATURED`, the presenting bank lodges its `REIMBURSEMENT` claim, and settling that claim moves `CLAIM_LODGED → REIMBURSED` and finally moves the drawing from `reserved_cents` into `drawn_settled_cents`. The reimbursement leg is priced as a sight honour with `discount_cents` fixed at 0, because the discount was already taken at acceptance.

Settlement can also finish the credit. The credit moves `ISSUED → UTILISED` once its `available_cents` reaches zero — equivalently once `reserved_cents` is zero and `drawn_settled_cents` equals `max_drawable_cents` — and no presentation of the credit is still in `examination_status` `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `DISCREPANT` or `WAIVER_REQUESTED`. Both conditions must hold: a fully drawn credit with another presentation still under examination stays `ISSUED`.

Claims can be listed per presentation filtered by `status` and `claim_type`.

Time itself is operated explicitly. A `SystemOperator` advances the logical clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The service starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and this is the only way the clock ever moves. Setting the clock mutates no entity at all — deadlines are re-evaluated only when a sweep or an ordinary write runs — and an instant earlier than the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (422). The endpoint returns no resource body.

Every clock-driven transition is then applied by a single operational sweep, which a `SystemOperator` runs with an optional `dry_run` flag and which reports the counters `credits_expired`, `presentations_precluded`, `waiver_requests_expired`, `amendments_lapsed`, `allocations_released` and `facilities_reactivated`. When `dry_run` is true the counts are reported and nothing is mutated. The sweep runs five passes in a fixed order, and each pass processes its rows in `created_at` ascending order, which makes the whole sweep deterministic and idempotent — a second sweep at the same clock returns all zeros.

Pass one precludes presentations: every presentation whose `examination_deadline` has passed while its `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `DISCREPANT` or `WAIVER_REQUESTED` moves to `PRECLUDED`, and because the bank lost its right to refuse and must honour, the drawing stays reserved. Pass two expires waiver requests: every request past its `deadline_at` while `PENDING` moves to `EXPIRED` and its cited discrepancies move `IDENTIFIED → UPHELD`. Pass three lapses amendments: every amendment past its `consent_deadline_date` while `PENDING_CONSENT` moves to `EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT`, keeps `window_end_effective` at `consent_deadline_date`, and returns its credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` on the original terms. Pass four expires credits: every credit whose `presentation_deadline_date` is strictly before the clock date, whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and whose `reserved_cents` is zero moves to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal, releasing its `OPEN` FacilityAllocation and any confirmation occupation it held. Only `ISSUED` credits are scanned here, and that is sufficient precisely because pass three has already returned every credit whose consent window lapsed from `AMENDMENT_PENDING` to `ISSUED`, so the same sweep still catches it. Pass five re-derives facility ledgers: facilities that have fallen back below their `restrict_threshold_cents` return `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and their `ON_HOLD` credits return to `APPROVED`, in `created_at` ascending order.

The pass order matters and is normative. A presentation precluded in pass one keeps its drawing reserved, which is exactly what stops pass four from expiring its credit in the same sweep; an allocation released in pass four is what allows pass five to reactivate the facility in the same sweep.
