# SOC Report Readiness & Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Service organizations that sell into regulated markets live and die by their SOC report. Producing one is not a single event but a long, adversarial choreography: the service organization writes a system description and draws a boundary around it, an examining practitioner challenges that boundary, controls are mapped to trust services criteria, owners are named, evidence is collected on a calendar for months, samples are drawn and tested, exceptions are argued over, and only then does a report exist. Today that choreography is run on spreadsheets and shared drives, where the same control can be simultaneously "approved" in one tab and "unmapped" in another, where a carve-out decision made in month three silently invalidates a coverage claim made in month one, and where nobody can answer the question a customer actually asks — *what does your report cover, and what is still open?*

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is mounted under `/soc` and holds the complete readiness-and-examination record for an engagement: the system description and its boundary, the trust services criteria catalog and the control-to-criterion mapping graph, the evidence calendar and every artifact submitted against it, the readiness assessment and the remediation ledger it produces, the auditor's samples and the exceptions they surface, the subservice organizations relied upon and the presentation method chosen for each, and finally the downstream commercial life of the finished report — who received it, under what non-disclosure agreement, which bridge letters extend it, and how many inbound customer questionnaire items it deflects.

Two design commitments run through everything below. The first is **segregation of duties**: the operator who authors a description may not approve it, the operator who submits evidence may not review it, and the operator accountable for remediating a gap may not verify that remediation. The second is **posture-driven gating**: an engagement's accumulated remediation debt and unevaluated exception count are continuously distilled into a single posture value that either permits, restricts, or freezes the operations its child resources may perform. Neither commitment is advisory. Both are enforced on every request, and both are the reason this service exists rather than a folder of documents.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads and including the logical-clock utility — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the operator making the call, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven role names defined below, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401.

The seven roles are `ComplianceLead`, `ControlOwner`, `EvidenceReviewer`, `Auditor`, `AccountExecutive`, `SubserviceContact` and `PlatformAdmin`, spelled exactly as written.

- A `ComplianceLead` owns the readiness track. They open engagements, author system descriptions and their boundaries, register subservice organizations and set their presentation method, add controls, map controls to criteria and retire those mappings, assign control owners, generate the evidence calendar, run the readiness assessment and the calendar ops-run, plan and remediate gaps, draft and articulate complementary user entity controls, and approve, deny or revoke distribution requests.
- A `ControlOwner` is the operator accountable for one or more controls and submits the periodic evidence artifacts for them.
- An `EvidenceReviewer` independently accepts or rejects submitted artifacts.
- An `Auditor` is the examining practitioner: they return descriptions to draft or approve them, approve control design, draw and test samples, conclude and void them, evaluate exceptions, verify and reopen gaps, and advance the engagement onto the report track.
- An `AccountExecutive` owns the report's commercial afterlife: requesting and delivering distributions, drafting and issuing bridge letters, recording inbound customer questionnaires and deflecting them.
- A `SubserviceContact` acts on behalf of a subservice organization that is presented under the inclusive method.
- A `PlatformAdmin` curates the global trust services criteria catalog, may drive the calendar ops-run, and sets the server's logical clock. This role holds no business authority over any engagement's content.

Each endpoint in §4 declares the roles permitted to call it. **The role check runs before any path identifier is resolved**, so a role that is not permitted on an endpoint receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403) even when the identifier in the URL names no stored row at all. Several endpoints accept an `action`, `disposition` or `method` field and are shared between two roles that are each permitted only a subset of the possible values; where §3 names the acting role for a particular transition, a permitted-on-the-endpoint role that is not the named actor for the requested value is likewise rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters. For `ComplianceLead`, `ControlOwner`, `EvidenceReviewer`, `Auditor`, `AccountExecutive` and `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a rule in §3 says so — as `authored_by_user_id` on a system description, `owner_user_id` on a control, `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an evidence request, `drawn_by_user_id` on a sample, `evaluated_by_user_id` on an exception, `issued_by_user_id` on a bridge letter, `remediation_owner_user_id` on a gap — and compares it to previously recorded operator identifiers to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SubserviceContact`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `SubserviceOrganization` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a subservice organization compare that resource's `subservice_organization_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `SubserviceContact` whose `X-User-ID` does not name a stored `SubserviceOrganization`, or names one whose presentation method is not the inclusive one, is rejected with `ERR_SUBSERVICE_NOT_INCLUSIVE` (HTTP 403) on every endpoint whose role list includes `SubserviceContact`.

Two further identity rules recur throughout §3 and are named here so the reader knows which code to expect. An operator who is not the accountable owner of a control may not submit evidence against it: that rejection is `ERR_NOT_CONTROL_OWNER` (HTTP 403). And an operator who attempts to perform the independent half of a two-person step they themselves performed the first half of is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer of at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below says otherwise; exception rows belonging to a single sample are created in ascending `item_ordinal` order, so their `created_at` ordering and their ordinal ordering coincide. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row falls after the current window.

Filter parameters are documented per endpoint in §4 and behave as equality matches. When several filters are supplied they are combined conjunctively, and pagination is applied to the filtered set.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind — and a `created_at` timestamp. Timestamps everywhere in this API, in request bodies and responses alike, are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation. The single exception is the trust services criteria catalog: a `TrustCriterion` row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because catalog entries are never mutated after creation.

Some operations bump a *parent's* `version` in addition to their own row's. These cross-entity bumps are load-bearing — the stale-view protection in §2.5 reads exactly these numbers — so they are listed here in full. The engagement's `version` is incremented by one when a system description is authored against it, when an approval of a system description takes effect, when a boundary is locked, when any engagement phase transition is applied, when a readiness assessment is run, and when a calendar ops-run is executed. A control's `version` is incremented by one when a sample is drawn against it.

The server runs on a **logical clock**, not on wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it with `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and runs no sweep — the sweeping behaviour described in §3.7 happens only when the ops-run checkpoint is invoked. Every due-date comparison, expiry test, submission-window check and coverage-interval test in this specification is evaluated against this logical clock, and every field described below as "stamped at" some moment records the logical clock's value at that moment.

### 2.5. Stale-View Protection on Authoring and Sampling

Two write operations in this system act on a parent whose state may have moved underneath the client since it last read that parent, and both are therefore protected by an explicit version check.

Authoring a system description — `POST /soc/system-descriptions` — requires a non-optional `expected_engagement_version` field carrying the `version` of the target engagement as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the engagement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a newly approved description legitimately supersedes the engagement's prior approved narrative and repoints the engagement's approved-description pointer; an author whose view of the engagement predates that supersession is drafting against a boundary that no longer stands and must re-read before authoring.

Drawing an audit sample — `POST /soc/audit-samples` — requires a non-optional `expected_control_version` field carrying the `version` of the target control as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the control's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTROL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A fresh draw supersedes the prior non-voided sample on the same control, and the sample's size is derived from the control's population, frequency, automation and risk rating; a client holding a stale control view would size the sample against numbers the control no longer carries.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Absence of `expected_engagement_version` from a system-description create, or of `expected_control_version` from a sample draw, is a missing required field and is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422) under the shape check described in §2.7.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Interval Conventions

Unless a rule states otherwise, monetary-style and percentage quantities are carried to **two decimal places and rounded half up**. This covers `gap_debt_points`, the surcharge that feeds it, `readiness_score`, `criteria_coverage_pct_readiness`, `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation` and `projected_failures`. Two quantities are carried to **four decimal places, also rounded half up**: `deviation_rate` on a sample and `deflection_ratio` on a questionnaire. Counts — `criteria_in_scope_count`, `base_debt_points`, `sample_size`, `items_recorded` and their many siblings — are integers. Where a formula in §3 calls for rounding upward to the next whole number or truncating downward to the previous whole number, it says so explicitly, and those operations are not the same as half-up rounding.

Day arithmetic is exact: one day is 86400 seconds, and "N days after an instant" means that instant plus N times 86400 seconds.

Three different interval conventions coexist in this domain, and confusing them is the most common source of wrong answers. The observation period of an engagement and every evidence period on a control are **half-open**: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is outside it. Consequently one evidence period beginning at exactly the instant another ends does *not* overlap it. A bridge letter's coverage window is **closed**: both endpoints are inside the window, so two letters that merely touch at a single shared instant *do* overlap. A subservice organization's coverage window is closed as well, and additionally demands clear space between neighbours rather than mere non-overlap. Each of the three is restated where it is used.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

Many requests violate more than one rule at once, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated strictly in the following order; the first failure short-circuits the request and no later check runs.

1. **Headers present.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. **Role permitted on the endpoint**, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). This runs *before* any identifier lookup, so a forbidden role calling a nonexistent identifier sees the authorization failure, never a not-found.
4. **Subservice identity.** On any endpoint whose role list includes `SubserviceContact`, an acting `SubserviceContact` whose `X-User-ID` is not the `id` of a stored subservice organization presented under the inclusive method is rejected with `ERR_SUBSERVICE_NOT_INCLUSIVE` (403).
5. **Path identifiers resolve**, validated left to right along the URL. The first segment identifier that names no stored row decides the error; each entity has its own code, listed in §5.
6. **Body foreign keys resolve.** Every identifier supplied in the request body must name a stored row of the entity it references, else that entity's not-found code (404).
7. **Request shape.** Every required input field present, every value of the declared type, every enumerated value a declared member, every count field at least 0, and every ordering constraint between a period start and its end, or between a coverage start and an as-of instant, satisfied. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).
8. **Pagination parameters** valid on list endpoints, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422).
9. **`expected_engagement_version` matches**, else `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (412).
10. **`expected_control_version` matches**, else `ERR_STALE_CONTROL_VERSION` (412).
11. **Engagement phase.** The engagement's `status` is a member of the phase set the operation requires, else `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_PHASE_INVALID` (409).
12. **Engagement not frozen**, else `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN` (409). A frozen engagement rejects every gated child mutation *regardless of any later business rule the same request would also violate*.
13. **Engagement posture open** for report-track operations, where merely restricted is not sufficient, else `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED` (409).
14. **Target row state.** The row's own current state satisfies the requested transition's originating state and every precondition of that transition that does not reach into another entity, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
15. **Report delivered.** Where the operation demands a delivered report, else `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELIVERED` (409).
16. **Action defined.** The supplied `action`, `disposition` or `method` value is an outgoing edge from the row's current state, else `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` (409).
17. **Control ownership** on evidence submission, else `ERR_NOT_CONTROL_OWNER` (403). This runs *before* the submission-window check at step 25, so an off-owner submission made after the due date reports the ownership breach rather than the closed window.
18. **Segregation of duties**, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
19. **Boundary locked** before control design approval, else `ERR_BOUNDARY_NOT_LOCKED` (409).
20. **Complementary subservice controls articulated**, else `ERR_CSOC_NOT_ARTICULATED` (409).
21. **Control in scope**, else `ERR_CONTROL_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (409).
22. **Criterion in scope**, else `ERR_CRITERION_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (409).
23. **Boundary scope coherence**, else `ERR_BOUNDARY_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422); then **boundary immutability**, else `ERR_BOUNDARY_LOCKED` (409).
24. **Temporal occupation rules**, in this order: evidence period overlap (`ERR_EVIDENCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, 409), bridge window overlap (`ERR_BRIDGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409), subservice window buffer (`ERR_SUBSERVICE_WINDOW_BUFFER`, 409).
25. **Evidence submission window still open**, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
26. **Sample window inside the observation period**, else `ERR_SAMPLE_WINDOW_INVALID` (409).
27. **Uniqueness**, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409).
28. **Remaining business constraints**, in this order: evidence settled before sampling (`ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_SETTLED`), sample not already full (`ERR_SAMPLE_FULL`), sample complete before conclusion (`ERR_SAMPLE_INCOMPLETE`), calendar not already generated (`ERR_CALENDAR_ALREADY_GENERATED`) — all 409.
29. **Distribution ceiling**, else `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). This runs *after* the uniqueness check, so a duplicate recipient submitted while the engagement already sits at its ceiling reports the duplicate, not the ceiling.

### 2.8. Engagement Posture: the Cross-Cutting Gate

Every engagement carries `gap_posture`, a derived value that is not part of its `status` lifecycle but a second, independent dimension recomputed from the engagement's gap and exception children. Its three values are `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`, and it is recomputed immediately after every write that changes `gap_debt_points` or `open_exception_count` — that is, on every gap creation and every gap transition, when a sample concludes with exceptions or is voided, when an exception is evaluated as pervasive, when a calendar ops-run sweeps overdue evidence, and when an engagement is abandoned.

The posture is decided by a strict precedence in which the first matching clause wins and no later clause can soften the result. The engagement is `FROZEN` when any of the following holds: at least one gap of the engagement has severity `CRITICAL` and a status of either `OPEN` or `REOPENED`; or `gap_debt_points` is 26.00 or more; or `open_exception_count` is 3 or more. Failing all three, the engagement is `RESTRICTED` when `gap_debt_points` is 11.00 or more, or `open_exception_count` is 1 or more. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. The comparisons are inclusive at the boundary: a debt of exactly 11.00 is restricted and a debt of exactly 26.00 is frozen. Note also that a `CRITICAL` gap sitting in `PLANNED` does not trip the live-critical clause — only `OPEN` and `REOPENED` do — though its severity points still feed `gap_debt_points`, which typically trips the debt threshold anyway.

A frozen engagement rejects, with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN`, six operations: creating a criteria mapping, retiring a criteria mapping, submitting evidence, reviewing evidence, delivering a report distribution, and issuing a bridge letter. Four operations demand the strictly stronger condition that the posture be `OPEN` and reject a merely restricted engagement with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`: advancing the engagement to the reporting phase, issuing the report, delivering a report distribution, and issuing a bridge letter. The last two appear on both lists — a frozen engagement blocking them reports the frozen code, a restricted one the restricted code.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution and Denormalised Counters

Path identifiers are resolved left to right. Where a URL nests an action beneath a resource, the resource identifier is resolved first and its not-found code is the one reported; only once it resolves does the action's own logic run. Each entity has a dedicated not-found code, and the same code is used whether the identifier arrived in the URL or in a request body: engagements report `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, system descriptions `ERR_SYSTEM_DESCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, boundary components `ERR_BOUNDARY_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, criteria `ERR_TRUST_CRITERION_NOT_FOUND`, controls `ERR_CONTROL_NOT_FOUND`, mappings `ERR_CRITERIA_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND`, evidence requests `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, gaps `ERR_GAP_NOT_FOUND`, samples `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, exceptions `ERR_AUDIT_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, subservice organizations `ERR_SUBSERVICE_ORG_NOT_FOUND`, complementary user entity controls `ERR_CUEC_NOT_FOUND`, distributions `ERR_REPORT_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, bridge letters `ERR_BRIDGE_LETTER_NOT_FOUND` and questionnaires `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_FOUND`.

A large number of fields in this system are **denormalised counters and derived postures maintained by the server**, never accepted from a client. They are recomputed synchronously, inside the same request that changes their inputs, and are visible on the very next read. Where a cascade in §3 says a counter is recomputed, it means recomputed from the current rows — not incremented optimistically — unless the text says "increased by" or "decreased by". A single request may walk four levels of cascade; every level completes before the response is written.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening an Engagement and Authoring the System Description

A `ComplianceLead` opens an examination by registering the engagement. The payload names the service organization under examination as `client_name` (a required string of 1 to 200 characters) and declares `report_type`, which must be either `SOC_1_TYPE_2` or `SOC_2_TYPE_2` and which will later decide both which criteria are applicable and whether an addendum is added to sample sizes. The examination window is supplied as `observation_period_start` and `observation_period_end`, both required ISO 8601 UTC instants; the end must fall strictly after the start, and the window is half-open, so the start instant is inside the period and the end instant is not. The categories under examination arrive as `required_criteria_categories`, a required non-empty array whose members are drawn from `SECURITY`, `AVAILABILITY`, `CONFIDENTIALITY`, `PROCESSING_INTEGRITY` and `PRIVACY`; a duplicate member is rejected. Finally, `max_distribution_recipients` is optional, defaults to 5, and must be at least 1; it becomes the ceiling on live report recipients described in §3.8.

Upon registration the engagement begins in `SCOPING` with a posture of `OPEN` and a `version` of 1. The server computes `observation_period_days` immediately, as the number of whole days in the half-open window: the difference between `observation_period_end` and `observation_period_start` in seconds, divided by 86400 and truncated downward to a whole number. It also evaluates the current criteria catalog against the engagement, populating `criteria_in_scope_count`, both coverage percentages and `readiness_score` — all of which will be zero on a fresh engagement with no controls. The derived fields `boundary_locked` and `approved_system_description_id` start false and null respectively, `readiness_assessed_at` starts null, and `calendar_epoch` starts at 0. The seven-phase ladder an engagement walks is `SCOPING`, `READINESS`, `OBSERVATION`, `FIELDWORK`, `REPORTING` and finally `ISSUED`, with `ABANDONED` available as an early exit; `ISSUED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

The narrative half of scoping is the **system description**. A `ComplianceLead` authors one against the engagement, supplying `engagement_id`, a `version_label` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique among that engagement's descriptions, and a `boundary_narrative` of 1 to 5000 characters describing what the system is. The create also requires `expected_engagement_version` per §2.5. The new row begins in `DRAFT` with `review_round` at 0 and `boundary_locked` false, and the server records the calling operator's identity as `authored_by_user_id` — the value that later prevents that same operator from approving their own work. Its `supersedes_system_description_id` is null until approval, and its `in_scope_component_count` and `carved_out_component_count` counters begin at 0. Authoring bumps the engagement's `version`.

Descriptions move through `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `WITHDRAWN`, the last two being terminal. A `ComplianceLead` submits a draft for review, taking it `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`; this requires the description to have at least one in-scope boundary component and requires the engagement to still be in `SCOPING`. The examining `Auditor` may push it back, `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, which is permitted only while the description's own `boundary_locked` is still false and which increments `review_round` by one — the `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` pair is a genuine loop and a description may traverse it any number of times. Either party may abandon the narrative outright: a `ComplianceLead` withdraws a description from `DRAFT` or from `IN_REVIEW` to `WITHDRAWN`, with no cross-entity consequence. All four of these actions travel on the same request shape, carrying `action` (one of `SUBMIT_REVIEW`, `RETURN_TO_DRAFT`, `APPROVE`, `WITHDRAW`) and an optional free-text `reason`.

Approval — `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, performed by an `Auditor` — is where the interesting consequences live. Three preconditions guard it. First, segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the description's `authored_by_user_id`, and an author approving their own narrative is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, coherence between the boundary and the subservice presentation methods: every boundary component of this description whose `scope_status` is `CARVED_OUT` must point at a subservice organization whose `method` is `CARVE_OUT`, and a carved-out component pointing at an organization presented inclusively is rejected with `ERR_CSOC_NOT_ARTICULATED`. Third, the engagement must still be in `SCOPING`. On success the server stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock; moves the engagement's previously approved description — there is at most one — from `APPROVED` to `SUPERSEDED`, recording that row's identifier in the new row's `supersedes_system_description_id`; repoints the engagement's `approved_system_description_id` at the newly approved row; copies that row's `boundary_locked` onto the engagement's own `boundary_locked`; and increments the engagement's `version`. A description reaches `SUPERSEDED` only through this system-driven path, never by direct request.

Once a description is approved, a `ComplianceLead` **locks its boundary**. Locking sets `boundary_locked` to true on the description and on the engagement, and increments the engagement's `version`. Locking is one-way and is a hard precondition of control design approval and of leaving the scoping phase. From the moment a description's boundary is locked, no boundary component may be created against it and no existing component's scope may be changed; both attempts are rejected with `ERR_BOUNDARY_LOCKED`.

The engagement leaves `SCOPING` for `READINESS` on a `ComplianceLead`'s instruction, carrying `action` value `START_READINESS`. Three conditions must hold: the engagement's `approved_system_description_id` is non-null, its `boundary_locked` is true, and `criteria_in_scope_count` is at least 1. The transition recomputes both coverage percentages and the readiness score, and increments the engagement's `version`.

### 3.2. The Boundary and the Subservice Organizations Behind It

A boundary component is a single named piece of the system under examination, filed under one of five dimensions supplied as `component_type`: `INFRASTRUCTURE`, `SOFTWARE`, `PEOPLE`, `PROCEDURES` or `DATA`. A `ComplianceLead` adds one by naming its `system_description_id` and giving it a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, unique among that description's components; a repeat name is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Its `scope_status` is optional and defaults to `IN_SCOPE`, the other permitted values being `CARVED_OUT` and `EXCLUDED`. Adding or rescoping a component recomputes the owning description's `in_scope_component_count` and `carved_out_component_count`.

Scope carries an exclusivity rule that is checked both at creation and whenever a component is rescoped. A component that is `CARVED_OUT` must supply a `subservice_organization_id` and must leave `exclusion_rationale` null. A component that is `EXCLUDED` must supply an `exclusion_rationale` and must leave `subservice_organization_id` null. A component that is `IN_SCOPE` must leave both null. Any other combination is rejected with `ERR_BOUNDARY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Rescoping travels as a `scope_status` value together with whichever of the two companion fields the new scope demands.

**Subservice organizations** are the third parties whose controls the service organization relies upon. A `ComplianceLead` registers one against an `engagement_id` with a `name` of 1 to 200 characters that must be unique within the engagement, a `services_provided` narrative of 1 to 1000 characters, and a closed reliance window given as `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`, the latter on or after the former. New registrations begin under the **carve-out** presentation method: `method` is `CARVE_OUT`, `csoc_articulated` is false, `inclusive_control_count` is 0 and `carved_out_at` is null.

Switching between the two presentation methods is the single most consequential operation in scoping, and it is available to a `ComplianceLead` only while the engagement is in `SCOPING` or `READINESS`. The request carries `method` — `CARVE_OUT` or `INCLUSIVE` — and an optional `csoc_narrative`.

Moving `CARVE_OUT → INCLUSIVE` brings the organization's controls into the examined system. Before it is allowed, the new window must keep its distance from every *other* inclusive organization on the same engagement. For each such neighbour, take whichever of the two closed windows ends first and call it the earlier; the clear space between them is the difference between the later window's `coverage_start` and the earlier window's `coverage_end` in seconds, divided by 86400, truncated downward to a whole number, and then reduced by one. That clear space must be at least 1 whole day. Overlapping windows produce a negative clear space and adjacent windows — where the later start falls exactly one day after the earlier end — produce zero, so both are rejected with `ERR_SUBSERVICE_WINDOW_BUFFER`. On success `method` becomes `INCLUSIVE` while `carved_out_at` is left untouched, every control of that organization sitting in `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT` returns to `DESIGN_DRAFT` with `in_scope` set back to true, `inclusive_control_count` is recomputed, and both of the engagement's coverage percentages are recomputed.

Moving `INCLUSIVE → CARVE_OUT` runs the opposite cascade, four levels deep, and requires `csoc_narrative` to be non-null — the complementary subservice organization controls narrative that the report must carry when a subservice organization is carved out. The organization's `method` becomes `CARVE_OUT`, the supplied narrative is stamped onto `csoc_narrative`, `csoc_articulated` is set to true, and `carved_out_at` records the logical clock. That method change moves every control whose `subservice_organization_id` names this organization to `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT` with `in_scope` set to false. Those controls leaving scope drops their active criteria mappings out of both coverage views, which forces a recompute of `covered_criteria_count_readiness`, `covered_criteria_count_attestation`, `criteria_coverage_pct_readiness`, `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation` and `readiness_score`. The coverage recompute in turn synthesises one gap with `source` `COVERAGE_LOSS`, severity `HIGH`, a null `control_id` and a populated `trust_criterion_id`, for **every** in-scope criterion that was covered under the readiness view immediately before the call and is not covered immediately after it, created in ascending `criterion_code` order. And those gap creations in turn drive the recompute of `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`. A carve-out can therefore freeze an engagement in a single request.

A carved-out subservice organization must have its complementary controls articulated — `csoc_articulated` true — before the engagement may reach the reporting phase; an engagement carrying a carve-out organization that has not been articulated is rejected with `ERR_CSOC_NOT_ARTICULATED`. Because switching to carve-out is the only path that sets `csoc_articulated`, an organization that has never been anything but a carve-out since registration must be switched inclusive and back, or otherwise given its narrative through that same operation, before the report track opens.

The `SubserviceContact` role exists on the far side of this decision. Its `X-User-ID` is the subservice organization's own `id`, it may act only while that organization is presented inclusively, and its sole write capability is submitting evidence for controls whose `subservice_organization_id` equals its `X-User-ID`. A contact whose organization has just been switched to carve-out loses that access on the very next request.

### 3.3. The Criteria Catalog, the Control Set, and the Two Coverage Views

The trust services criteria catalog is global, shared across every engagement, and curated by a `PlatformAdmin`. A catalog entry carries a `criterion_code` of 1 to 20 uppercase characters that must be globally unique — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — a `category` drawn from the same five values an engagement may require, and a `title` of 1 to 200 characters. Two optional fields tune how the criterion behaves. `min_primary_controls` defaults to 1, must be at least 1, and states how many primary mappings a criterion needs before the attestation view will treat it as covered. `applies_to_report_types` defaults to an array containing only `SOC_2_TYPE_2`, must be non-empty, and may contain `SOC_1_TYPE_2`, `SOC_2_TYPE_2` or both; a criterion whose array omits an engagement's `report_type` is never in scope for that engagement. Catalog rows are immutable once written, which is why, as §2.4 notes, they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

**Which criteria are in scope** for a given engagement is the foundation of every coverage number in this system. A catalog criterion is in scope when its `category` is a member of the engagement's `required_criteria_categories` *and* its `applies_to_report_types` contains the engagement's `report_type`. The in-scope set is ordered by `criterion_code` ascending, and its size is `criteria_in_scope_count`.

A `ComplianceLead` populates the engagement's control set, an operation permitted only while the engagement is in `SCOPING` or `READINESS`. Each control names its `engagement_id`, a `control_ref` label of 1 to 40 characters unique within the engagement — which doubles as the deterministic sort key of the readiness assessment in §3.5 — and a `title` of 1 to 200 characters. The required `frequency` is one of `DAILY`, `WEEKLY`, `MONTHLY`, `QUARTERLY`, `ANNUAL` or `ADHOC` and drives both the evidence calendar and the sampling base. Three optional fields have defaults: `automation` defaults to `MANUAL` and may be `AUTOMATED`; `risk_rating` defaults to `MODERATE` and may be `LOW` or `HIGH`; `population_size` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A control performed by a third party names its `subservice_organization_id`; a control performed by the service organization itself leaves it null. The new control's `in_scope` is true when `subservice_organization_id` is null or when the named organization is presented inclusively, and false otherwise, and registering a control against an organization recomputes that organization's `inclusive_control_count`. Every counter on a fresh control — `primary_mapping_count`, `active_mapping_count`, `evidence_scheduled_count`, `evidence_accepted_count`, `evidence_missed_count`, `tested_item_count` and `open_gap_count` — starts at 0, and `owner_user_id` starts null. Each of those counters has a fixed meaning that the server maintains from the current rows: `active_mapping_count` is the number of the control's criteria mappings whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, counting both mapping types; `primary_mapping_count` is the number of those whose `mapping_type` is additionally `PRIMARY`; `evidence_scheduled_count`, `evidence_accepted_count` and `evidence_missed_count` are the numbers of the control's evidence requests sitting in `SCHEDULED`, `ACCEPTED` and `MISSED` respectively; `tested_item_count` is the sum of `items_recorded` across the control's samples that are not `VOIDED`; and `open_gap_count` is the number of gaps naming this control whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PLANNED` or `REOPENED`.

A control carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The design track, `design_status`, runs `DESIGN_DRAFT`, `DESIGN_MAPPED`, `DESIGN_APPROVED`, `DESIGN_DEFICIENT` and the terminal `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT`. The operating track, `operating_status`, runs `NOT_TESTED`, `IN_OBSERVATION`, `TESTED_EFFECTIVE`, `TESTED_EXCEPTION` and the terminal `TESTED_INEFFECTIVE`. The two advance on disjoint triggers and neither is a precondition of the other's forward edges, with a single exception noted in §3.4: leaving `NOT_TESTED` reads the design track.

**Mapping a control to a criterion** is how design coverage is claimed. A `ComplianceLead` supplies `control_id` and `trust_criterion_id`, plus an optional `mapping_type` that defaults to `PRIMARY` and may instead be `SECONDARY`, and an optional free-text `rationale`. Mapping is permitted only while the engagement is in `SCOPING`, `READINESS` or `OBSERVATION`, and never while the engagement is frozen. The referenced control must have `in_scope` true and a `design_status` other than `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT`, else `ERR_CONTROL_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; the referenced criterion must be in scope for the engagement, else `ERR_CRITERION_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; and the pair of `control_id` and `trust_criterion_id` must not already exist among that control's active mappings, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The new mapping's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `retired_at` is null. Creating it recomputes the control's `active_mapping_count` and `primary_mapping_count`; when `active_mapping_count` rises from 0 to 1 on an in-scope control, the control moves `DESIGN_DRAFT → DESIGN_MAPPED`. Both engagement coverage counts, both coverage percentages and `readiness_score` are recomputed.

Retiring a mapping is a soft delete: the row survives with `status` `RETIRED` and `retired_at` stamped from the logical clock, remains readable and listable, and stops participating in every coverage view. Retirement is subject to the same phase set — `SCOPING`, `READINESS` or `OBSERVATION` — and the same frozen-engagement prohibition as creation. It recomputes the control's two mapping counters, moves the control `DESIGN_MAPPED → DESIGN_DRAFT` when `active_mapping_count` falls to 0, and recomputes both coverage counts, both percentages and `readiness_score`.

An `Auditor` approves a control's design, moving it `DESIGN_MAPPED → DESIGN_APPROVED`, by sending `action` value `APPROVE_DESIGN` together with an optional `reason`. Five conditions gate it: the engagement's `boundary_locked` must be true, else `ERR_BOUNDARY_NOT_LOCKED`; the control's `primary_mapping_count` must be at least 1; its `owner_user_id` must be non-null; its `in_scope` must be true; and the engagement's `status` must be `SCOPING`, `READINESS` or `OBSERVATION`. Approval recomputes the engagement's attestation coverage percentage and its readiness score. The reverse edge, `DESIGN_APPROVED → DESIGN_DEFICIENT`, is system-driven only, raised when an exception on that control is evaluated as pervasive (§3.6); there is no client action value for it, and a deficient control stops qualifying under the attestation view, which is why that transition recomputes the attestation percentage. Recovery is the same `APPROVE_DESIGN` action, taking the control `DESIGN_DEFICIENT → DESIGN_APPROVED`, and it demands that every gap on this control whose `source` is `EXCEPTION_CASCADE` has reached `VERIFIED`, that `primary_mapping_count` is still at least 1, and that `owner_user_id` is still non-null. It too recomputes the attestation percentage. `DESIGN_APPROVED → DESIGN_DEFICIENT → DESIGN_APPROVED` is a genuine cycle and may be walked repeatedly.

Coverage is computed **twice over the same data, under two deliberately different definitions**, and the two numbers routinely disagree.

Under the **readiness view**, an in-scope criterion counts as covered when there exists at least one criteria mapping that is `ACTIVE`, that names this criterion, and whose control belongs to this engagement, has `in_scope` true, and has a `design_status` of either `DESIGN_MAPPED` or `DESIGN_APPROVED`. The mapping's `mapping_type` is not consulted at all, so a `SECONDARY` mapping counts here; the control's `operating_status` is not consulted; and the criterion's `min_primary_controls` is ignored. The number of in-scope criteria satisfying this is `covered_criteria_count_readiness`, and `criteria_coverage_pct_readiness` is 100 multiplied by that count, divided by `criteria_in_scope_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places — or exactly 0.00 when `criteria_in_scope_count` is 0.

Under the **attestation view**, an in-scope criterion counts as covered only when the number of criteria mappings that are `ACTIVE`, that have `mapping_type` `PRIMARY`, that name this criterion, and whose control belongs to this engagement with `in_scope` true, `design_status` exactly `DESIGN_APPROVED` and `operating_status` exactly `TESTED_EFFECTIVE`, is greater than or equal to that criterion's `min_primary_controls`. Retired mappings, out-of-scope controls, and controls sitting in `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT` or `DESIGN_DEFICIENT` are all excluded. The result is `covered_criteria_count_attestation`, and `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation` is 100 multiplied by that count, divided by `criteria_in_scope_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places — again exactly 0.00 when `criteria_in_scope_count` is 0. On identical data the attestation view can never exceed the readiness view and routinely reports strictly fewer covered criteria.

Both views are recomputed whenever a mapping is created or retired, whenever a control's design track moves to `DESIGN_APPROVED` or to `DESIGN_DEFICIENT`, whenever a control's operating track reaches `TESTED_EFFECTIVE`, on either subservice presentation-method switch, on the engagement's move into `READINESS` and on its move into `REPORTING`, and on demand by the readiness summary read.

The **readiness score** is a separate number and is *not* the readiness coverage percentage. Take the engagement's `required_criteria_categories`, deduplicate them, and sort them ascending as literal strings. For each category in that list, let the category total be the number of in-scope criteria whose `category` matches, and the category covered count be how many of those are covered under the readiness view. The category's raw percentage is 0 when its total is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the covered count and divided by the total. **Each category's raw percentage is rounded half up to two decimal places first**, and only then are the rounded per-category scores summed and divided by the number of categories, with that mean rounded half up to two decimal places to give `readiness_score`. The alternative order — pooling covered and total counts across all categories and rounding once at the end — is explicitly not used and produces different cents. A category with no in-scope criteria contributes a score of 0.00 while still counting toward the number of categories in the divisor.

### 3.4. Ownership, the Evidence Calendar, and the Observation Period

Before a control's design can be approved it needs an accountable operator. A `ComplianceLead` assigns one by supplying `owner_user_id`, the opaque operator identifier of the `ControlOwner` who will submit that control's evidence. Assignment sets the field and moves neither state dimension. From then on, only that identifier — or, where the control belongs to an inclusive subservice organization, that organization's `SubserviceContact` — may submit evidence against the control's evidence requests.

Evidence is scheduled either in bulk or one period at a time. **Bulk generation** builds the control's whole calendar for the observation period in one call by a `ComplianceLead`, and is permitted only while the engagement is in `SCOPING` or `READINESS`. It requires the control to be in scope and not carved out, else `ERR_CONTROL_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and it requires the control to carry no evidence request at all: a control that already has even one is rejected with `ERR_CALENDAR_ALREADY_GENERATED`. Pre-existing rows are never deleted or merged.

The generator works from the engagement's `observation_period_days` and the control's `frequency`. A control whose frequency is `ADHOC` receives exactly one period spanning the entire observation window, from `observation_period_start` to `observation_period_end`. Every other frequency has a period length in days — one day for `DAILY`, seven for `WEEKLY`, thirty for `MONTHLY`, ninety-one for `QUARTERLY` and three hundred sixty-five for `ANNUAL`. The number of periods generated is the observation span in days divided by that period length, truncated downward to a whole number, so **a trailing partial period is not scheduled**. The periods are laid end to end from the observation start: the period at zero-based ordinal `i` begins at the observation start plus `i` period-lengths of days and ends at the observation start plus `i` plus one period-lengths of days. Each generated row records that ordinal as `period_index`, its window as `period_start` and `period_end`, and a `due_date` of exactly five days after its `period_end`. Each period is half-open, so the period at ordinal `i` ends at precisely the instant the period at ordinal `i` plus one begins, and the two do not collide. All generated rows are created in `SCHEDULED`, and the control's `evidence_scheduled_count` is set to the number of rows created.

**Single-period scheduling** exists alongside the generator for the irregular cases. A `ComplianceLead` supplies `control_id`, a `period_index` of at least 0 that must be unique among that control's evidence requests, and a `period_start` with a `period_end` strictly after it. The half-open window must not intersect the half-open window of any existing evidence request on the same control whose `status` is anything other than `MISSED`; a collision is `ERR_EVIDENCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Touching endpoints — a new `period_start` exactly equal to an existing `period_end` — do not intersect and are accepted. The row is created in `SCHEDULED`, its `due_date` is set to five days after its `period_end`, and the control's `evidence_scheduled_count` rises by one. On a fresh row `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_at` are all null, `rejection_round` is 0, `artifact_ref` is null and `late` is false.

The engagement enters its observation window when a `ComplianceLead` sends `action` value `START_OBSERVATION`, taking it `READINESS → OBSERVATION`. Five conditions must hold: `readiness_assessed_at` is non-null, so the readiness assessment of §3.5 has been run at least once; the engagement's posture is anything but `FROZEN`; and every in-scope control of the engagement has `design_status` `DESIGN_APPROVED`, a non-null `owner_user_id`, and an `evidence_scheduled_count` of at least 1. The transition cascades down: every in-scope control of the engagement has its `operating_status` set to `IN_OBSERVATION`, while controls that have already moved past `NOT_TESTED` are left untouched. The engagement's `version` is incremented. The same `NOT_TESTED → IN_OBSERVATION` edge is also available one control at a time, driven by a `ComplianceLead` with `action` value `START_OBSERVATION` on the control itself, and in that form it requires the control's `design_status` to be `DESIGN_APPROVED`, the engagement's `status` to be `OBSERVATION`, and the control's `in_scope` to be true.

An evidence request walks `SCHEDULED`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `MISSED` and `WAIVED`, of which `ACCEPTED`, `MISSED` and `WAIVED` are terminal. **Submission** is performed by the control's owner (or its inclusive subservice contact) and carries the opaque pointer `artifact_ref`. It is permitted from `SCHEDULED` and from `REJECTED`, and four conditions guard it: the engagement is in `OBSERVATION`; the engagement's posture is not `FROZEN`; the acting `X-User-ID` equals the control's `owner_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_CONTROL_OWNER`; and the logical clock is at or before the request's `due_date`, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because the ownership check runs first, an outsider submitting after the deadline is told about the ownership breach and not about the window. A successful submit stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock and `submitted_by_user_id` from the header. On the `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED` edge it also decreases the control's `evidence_scheduled_count` by one, and it sets `late` to whether `submitted_at` falls strictly after `due_date` — which on this edge is always false, since a late submission cannot get past the window check at all. The `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` edge stamps the same two operator fields and does not touch `evidence_scheduled_count`, which the rejection already restored.

**Review** disposes of a submitted artifact and carries `action` — one of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT` or `WAIVE` — plus an optional `review_note`. Review is blocked while the engagement is frozen. `ACCEPT` and `REJECT` belong to the `EvidenceReviewer` role and both enforce a double segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the control's `owner_user_id` and must also differ from the request's `submitted_by_user_id`, with either collision reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` stamps `reviewed_at` and `reviewed_by_user_id` and increases the control's `evidence_accepted_count` by one. `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` increments `rejection_round` by one, stamps `reviewed_at` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, and increases the control's `evidence_scheduled_count` by one, returning the period to the owner's queue; `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` is the resubmission cycle and may be walked repeatedly. `WAIVE` belongs to the `ComplianceLead`, takes `SUBMITTED → WAIVED`, requires only that the engagement is not frozen, and stamps `reviewed_at` and `reviewed_by_user_id` without touching any control counter.

### 3.5. Readiness Assessment, the Gap Ledger, and Remediation Debt

Before the observation period opens, the `ComplianceLead` runs a **readiness assessment** over the engagement. The assessment takes no payload, is permitted while the engagement is in the `READINESS` phase, synthesises a deterministic set of gaps, stamps `readiness_assessed_at` with the logical clock, recomputes `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`, and increments the engagement's `version`.

The synthesis runs in two passes, strictly in the order written, and within each pass in a fixed sort order, so that repeated runs produce a deterministic and non-duplicating gap set. **The first pass walks the engagement's controls whose `in_scope` is true, in ascending `control_ref` order.** A control with a `primary_mapping_count` of 0 yields a gap of severity `HIGH`. Otherwise, a control whose `design_status` is anything other than `DESIGN_APPROVED` yields a gap whose severity mirrors the control's `risk_rating` exactly — `LOW` for a low-risk control, `MODERATE` for a moderate one, `HIGH` for a high one. A control that is both mapped and design-approved yields no gap at all. Each gap created in this pass carries the engagement, the control's identifier as `control_id`, a null `trust_criterion_id`, `source` `READINESS_ASSESSMENT` and `status` `OPEN`. The pass skips a control entirely when a gap already exists with `source` `READINESS_ASSESSMENT`, that same `control_id`, and a status other than `VERIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN`.

**The second pass walks the in-scope criterion set, in ascending `criterion_code` order.** A criterion already covered under the readiness view is skipped. An uncovered criterion yields a gap of severity `HIGH` when its `category` is `SECURITY` and severity `MODERATE` for every other category. Each such gap carries the engagement, a null `control_id`, the criterion's identifier as `trust_criterion_id`, `source` `READINESS_ASSESSMENT` and `status` `OPEN`, and is likewise skipped when a gap already exists with `source` `READINESS_ASSESSMENT`, that same `trust_criterion_id`, and a status other than `VERIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Controls sitting in `DESIGN_CARVED_OUT` never appear in either pass, because their `in_scope` is false.

Gaps also arrive by three other routes, recorded in `source`: `EXCEPTION_CASCADE` when a sample concludes with exceptions, `EVIDENCE_MISS` when a control accumulates missed evidence, and `COVERAGE_LOSS` when a carve-out strips coverage from a criterion. A `ComplianceLead` may also log one by hand, giving `engagement_id`, an optional `control_id`, an optional `trust_criterion_id`, an optional `severity` that defaults to `MODERATE`, and an optional free-text `description`. A hand-logged gap gets `source` `MANUAL` and `status` `OPEN`, its `debt_points` set from its severity, an immediate recompute of the engagement's debt and posture, and a recompute of the control's `open_gap_count` when `control_id` is non-null. Every gap begins with `audit_exception_id` null, `remediation_owner_user_id` and `remediation_due_date` null, and `reopen_count` at 0.

The remediation lifecycle runs `OPEN`, `PLANNED`, `REMEDIATED`, `VERIFIED`, `REOPENED` and the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, driven by `action` values `PLAN`, `REMEDIATE`, `VERIFY`, `REOPEN` and `WITHDRAW` together with optional `remediation_owner_user_id`, `remediation_due_date` and `reason` fields. A `ComplianceLead` plans a gap from `OPEN` or from `REOPENED` into `PLANNED`, and both edges require a non-null `remediation_owner_user_id` and a non-null `remediation_due_date` in the payload, which are stamped onto the row; planning leaves `debt_points` untouched, because `PLANNED` is a non-settled state that still carries debt. The same lead moves `PLANNED → REMEDIATED`, which sets `debt_points` to 0, recomputes the engagement's `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`, and recomputes the control's `open_gap_count` when the gap names a control. An `Auditor` then moves `REMEDIATED → VERIFIED`, and here segregation of duties bites: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the gap's `remediation_owner_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Verification leaves `debt_points` at 0. An `Auditor` may push a verified gap back with `VERIFIED → REOPENED` so long as the engagement has not reached `ISSUED`; reopening increments `reopen_count` by one, restores `debt_points` to the severity's point value, and recomputes the engagement's debt and posture along with the control's `open_gap_count`. `VERIFIED → REOPENED → PLANNED → REMEDIATED → VERIFIED` is the full remediation cycle and may be traversed as often as the examination demands. A `ComplianceLead` may withdraw a gap from `OPEN`, `PLANNED` or `REOPENED`, which zeroes `debt_points` and recomputes the engagement's debt and posture; a `REMEDIATED` gap reaches `WITHDRAWN` only through the abandon cascade or the sample-void cascade, and in that case `debt_points` was already 0 and stays there.

**Remediation debt** is the engagement's single financial-style number, and it is computed in three ordered steps. First, the base: sum the severity point value of every gap of the engagement whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PLANNED` or `REOPENED`. A `LOW` gap is worth 1 point, a `MODERATE` gap 3, a `HIGH` gap 8 and a `CRITICAL` gap 20. Gaps in `REMEDIATED`, `VERIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing. Criterion-anchored gaps with a null `control_id` count on exactly the same footing as control-anchored ones. That sum is `base_debt_points`, an integer.

Second, the tier is selected **from the pre-surcharge base and never from the total it will later be added to**. A base of 10 or less draws a rate of 0 percent; a base from 11 through 25 inclusive draws 15 percent; a base of 26 or more draws 30 percent. Third, the surcharge is the base multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `gap_debt_points` is the base plus the surcharge, rounded half up to two decimal places. The tier is never re-selected from the post-surcharge total. The readiness summary read exposes this decomposition directly, returning the surcharge as `surcharge_points` alongside `base_debt_points` and `gap_debt_points`.

That same summary read is the engagement's single diagnostic surface. It reports `criteria_in_scope_count`, `covered_criteria_count_readiness`, `covered_criteria_count_attestation`, `criteria_coverage_pct_readiness`, `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation`, `readiness_score`, `base_debt_points`, `surcharge_points`, `gap_debt_points`, `gap_posture`, `open_exception_count`, and `open_gap_count` — the last being the number of the engagement's gaps whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PLANNED` or `REOPENED`. `open_exception_count` itself is the number of audit exceptions whose control belongs to this engagement and whose `disposition` is anything other than `VOIDED`.

Abandonment is the emergency exit from the readiness track. A `ComplianceLead` may take an engagement from `SCOPING` or from `READINESS` to `ABANDONED` with `action` value `ABANDON`; the transition has no preconditions. It cascades: every system description of the engagement sitting in `DRAFT` or `IN_REVIEW` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and every gap of the engagement sitting in `OPEN`, `PLANNED`, `REMEDIATED` or `REOPENED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with its `debt_points` set to 0. Those zeroed gaps drive a recompute of `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`, and the engagement's `version` is incremented.

### 3.6. Fieldwork: Drawing Samples, Testing Items, and Evaluating Exceptions

Once the observation window has elapsed, a `ComplianceLead` closes it with `action` value `START_FIELDWORK`, taking the engagement `OBSERVATION → FIELDWORK`. Two conditions gate it: the logical clock must have reached or passed `observation_period_end`, and no evidence request belonging to any control of the engagement may still be sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `SUBMITTED`. The transition increments the engagement's `version`.

Sampling belongs to the `Auditor`. A draw names a `control_id`, a tested window given as `period_start` and `period_end` with the end strictly after the start, and the `expected_control_version` of §2.5. Draws are permitted only while the engagement is in `OBSERVATION` or `FIELDWORK`; the control must be in scope and not carved out, else `ERR_CONTROL_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; the tested window must sit inside the examination window, with `period_start` at or after the engagement's `observation_period_start` and `period_end` at or before its `observation_period_end`, else `ERR_SAMPLE_WINDOW_INVALID`; and every evidence request of the control must already be settled — `ACCEPTED`, `MISSED` or `WAIVED` — with any request still in `SCHEDULED`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` rejected as `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_SETTLED`. The draw stamps `drawn_by_user_id` from the header and `drawn_at` from the logical clock, sets `supersedes_audit_sample_id` to the identifier of the prior non-voided sample on the same control (null when there is none) and voids that prior sample through that supersession, and increments the control's `version` by one.

**Sample size** is derived at draw time from the control alone plus the engagement's report type; no other control's numbers participate. Start from the frequency base: 25 for a `DAILY` control, 15 for `WEEKLY`, 5 for `MONTHLY`, 2 for `QUARTERLY`, 1 for `ANNUAL` and 8 for `ADHOC`. If the control's `automation` is `AUTOMATED`, that base shrinks to 40 percent of itself rounded upward to the next whole number, but never below 1 — take the larger of 1 and that rounded-up value. Add the risk bump: nothing for a `LOW` control, 2 for a `MODERATE` one and 5 for a `HIGH` one. If the engagement's `report_type` is `SOC_1_TYPE_2`, add a further 1. The result is then capped by the population: `sample_size` is the smaller of that computed figure and the control's `population_size`, which is copied onto the sample as `population_size_snapshot` at draw time so that later changes to the control never retro-alter the sample. The deviation budget, `allowed_deviations`, is 10 percent of `sample_size` truncated downward to a whole number. Finally, `zero_population` records whether `population_size_snapshot` is 0.

A sample walks `DRAWN`, `TESTING`, `CONCLUDED_CLEAN`, `CONCLUDED_EXCEPTIONS` and the terminal `VOIDED`. A sample drawn against a control whose population is 0 is a special case: `zero_population` is true, `sample_size` and `allowed_deviations` are both 0, `deviation_rate` is 0.0000 and `projected_failures` is 0.00, and the row is minted **directly into `CONCLUDED_CLEAN` inside the create call, never passing through `TESTING`**. That immediate conclusion moves the control `IN_OBSERVATION → TESTED_EFFECTIVE` and recomputes the engagement's attestation coverage percentage.

Ordinary samples are tested one item at a time. Each item result carries an `outcome` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `NOT_PROVIDED` and an optional `failure_reason`. The item's ordinal is the sample's `items_recorded` value as it stood immediately before the call, so ordinals are zero-based and consecutive. Recording increases `items_recorded` by one and increments the matching tally: `items_passed` for a pass, `items_failed` for both a failure and a not-provided outcome — a missing artifact is treated as a deviation — and `items_not_provided` additionally for the not-provided case, which is therefore a subset of `items_failed`. The first recorded item moves the sample `DRAWN → TESTING`, which requires `sample_size` to be at least 1. Once `items_recorded` has reached `sample_size` no further item may be recorded, and the attempt is `ERR_SAMPLE_FULL`.

**Conclusion** is where the branch happens, and the two arms are not symmetric. Conclusion is refused unless `items_recorded` equals `sample_size`, with a short sample rejected as `ERR_SAMPLE_INCOMPLETE`. Both arms first compute `deviation_rate` — exactly 0.0000 when `sample_size` is 0, and otherwise `items_failed` divided by `sample_size`, rounded half up to four decimal places — and then stamp `projected_failures` as that rounded rate multiplied by `population_size_snapshot`, rounded half up to two decimal places. This sample-level projection adds no allowance; it is the plain proportional effect the auditor reports.

The **clean arm** is taken when `items_failed` is less than or equal to `allowed_deviations`. The sample moves `TESTING → CONCLUDED_CLEAN`, the control moves `IN_OBSERVATION → TESTED_EFFECTIVE` with `tested_item_count` recomputed as the sum of `items_recorded` across the control's non-voided samples, and the engagement's attestation coverage percentage and `readiness_score` are recomputed. No exception row is created and `open_exception_count` is unchanged.

The **exception arm** is taken when `items_failed` is strictly greater than `allowed_deviations`, and its side-effect set is strictly larger. The sample moves `TESTING → CONCLUDED_EXCEPTIONS`. One audit exception row is created for every recorded item whose outcome was `FAIL` or `NOT_PROVIDED`, in ascending `item_ordinal` order, each carrying `audit_sample_id`, the control's identifier denormalised as `control_id`, the breaching item's `item_ordinal`, that item's reason copied into `failure_reason`, a `disposition` of `UNEVALUATED`, a null `severity_assigned`, a `projected_failures_upper` of 0 and a `pervasive_recommended` of false, with `evaluated_by_user_id` and `evaluated_at` null. Creating those rows moves the control `IN_OBSERVATION → TESTED_EXCEPTION`, recomputes `tested_item_count`, and raises the engagement's `open_exception_count` by the number of rows created. The control entering `TESTED_EXCEPTION` in turn creates exactly one gap, with `source` `EXCEPTION_CASCADE`, severity `HIGH`, this control as its `control_id`, and `audit_exception_id` pointing at the created exception with the lowest `item_ordinal`. That gap creation in turn drives the recompute of `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`. Four levels of cascade, all inside one request.

**Evaluating an exception** is the `Auditor`'s judgement call, carrying a `disposition` of either `ISOLATED` or `PERVASIVE` plus an optional `evaluation_note`. It is permitted only when the parent sample's status is `CONCLUDED_EXCEPTIONS` and the engagement is in `FIELDWORK` or `REPORTING`. Both dispositions first stamp the exception-level projection, which is deliberately more conservative than the sample-level one: `projected_failures_upper` is the rounded `deviation_rate` multiplied by `population_size_snapshot`, **rounded upward to the next whole number**, plus a precision allowance keyed to the control's `risk_rating` — 0 for `LOW`, 1 for `MODERATE`, 2 for `HIGH`. The result is an integer and, whenever the allowance is non-zero or the proportional product has a fractional part, is strictly larger than the sample-level `projected_failures` computed from the same data. Both dispositions also stamp `pervasive_recommended`, which is true exactly when `projected_failures_upper` is strictly greater than 20 percent of `population_size_snapshot`. The recommendation is advisory: an auditor may record `ISOLATED` against a true recommendation, in which case the recommendation and the disposition simply disagree on the record. Both dispositions stamp `evaluated_by_user_id` from the header and `evaluated_at` from the logical clock.

The two arms then diverge. `UNEVALUATED → ISOLATED` sets `severity_assigned` to `MODERATE` and stops there; the gap raised by the cascade keeps its severity `HIGH`, because an isolated disposition never de-escalates a gap that already exists. `UNEVALUATED → PERVASIVE` sets `severity_assigned` to `CRITICAL` and moves the control `DESIGN_APPROVED → DESIGN_DEFICIENT`; that design move escalates the gap whose `audit_exception_id` names this exception from severity `HIGH` to severity `CRITICAL`; and that escalation drives the recompute of `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`. A single pervasive finding therefore both strips the control from the attestation view and, through the live-critical clause of §2.8, freezes the engagement.

**Voiding a sample** compensates every effect its conclusion produced. An `Auditor` voids with an optional `reason`, and voiding is refused once the engagement has reached `ISSUED`. Voiding stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock. Voiding a `CONCLUDED_CLEAN` sample moves the control `TESTED_EFFECTIVE → IN_OBSERVATION` and recomputes `tested_item_count`. Voiding a `CONCLUDED_EXCEPTIONS` sample does more: every exception of that sample moves to `disposition` `VOIDED` with `severity_assigned` reset to null, every gap whose `audit_exception_id` names one of those exceptions moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `debt_points` 0, the control moves `TESTED_EXCEPTION → IN_OBSERVATION` with `tested_item_count` recomputed, and `open_exception_count`, `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture` are all recomputed — leaving `open_exception_count` back at its pre-conclusion value. Exceptions reach `VOIDED` only this way, from any of `UNEVALUATED`, `ISOLATED` or `PERVASIVE`, and never by direct request.

### 3.7. The Calendar Ops-Run

Nothing in this system expires on its own. Deadlines pass silently until an operator drives the **calendar ops-run**, a checkpoint available to a `ComplianceLead` or a `PlatformAdmin` that sweeps an engagement against the current logical clock and applies every consequence in one pass. It takes no payload.

The sweep first walks the engagement's evidence requests. Every request whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `REJECTED` and whose `due_date` falls strictly before the logical clock moves to `MISSED`. These are processed in a deterministic order — ascending by the `control_ref` of the owning control, then ascending by `period_index` — so that the downstream cascades fire in a reproducible sequence. Each such move decreases the control's `evidence_scheduled_count` by one and increases its `evidence_missed_count` by one. When that increment brings `evidence_missed_count` to 2 or more, the control moves `IN_OBSERVATION → TESTED_INEFFECTIVE`, a terminal state on the operating track. That operating move in turn creates exactly one gap with `source` `EVIDENCE_MISS`, severity `HIGH` and this control as its `control_id`, and that gap creation in turn drives the recompute of `base_debt_points`, `gap_debt_points` and `gap_posture`.

The sweep then walks the engagement's bridge letters: every letter in `ISSUED` whose own `as_of_date` falls strictly before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, and an expired letter stops qualifying for the bridge bucket of the deflection waterfall described in §3.9.

Finally the run increments the engagement's `calendar_epoch` by one and its `version` by one. The response is a report on what the sweep did rather than the engagement row itself: `calendar_epoch` after the increment, `missed_evidence_count` for the number of evidence requests swept to `MISSED`, `expired_bridge_letter_count` for the number of letters expired, `gaps_created_count` for the number of gaps the cascades synthesised, and the engagement's resulting `gap_posture` and `gap_debt_points`. A run over an engagement with nothing overdue is legitimate and simply reports zeroes with an advanced epoch.

### 3.8. Reaching the Report and Putting It in Customers' Hands

When fieldwork is complete an `Auditor` opens the report track with `action` value `START_REPORTING`, taking the engagement `FIELDWORK → REPORTING`. Four conditions must hold together. The engagement's posture must be exactly `OPEN` — a merely restricted engagement is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`. Every in-scope control must have an `operating_status` of either `TESTED_EFFECTIVE` or `TESTED_EXCEPTION`, so a control still in observation or one that fell to `TESTED_INEFFECTIVE` blocks the track. Every subservice organization of the engagement presented under the carve-out method must have `csoc_articulated` true, else `ERR_CSOC_NOT_ARTICULATED`. And every audit exception whose control belongs to the engagement must have a `disposition` other than `UNEVALUATED`. The transition recomputes `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation` under the attestation view and increments the engagement's `version`.

Issuing the report is the same `Auditor`'s final act, `action` value `ISSUE`, taking the engagement `REPORTING → ISSUED`. It demands that the posture is still exactly `OPEN` and that `criteria_coverage_pct_attestation` stands at exactly 100.00 — every in-scope criterion covered under the strict view, with no partial credit. `ISSUED` is terminal; the engagement's `version` is incremented one last time.

Distribution runs on its own track and begins long before issue. An `AccountExecutive` requests a copy for a named user entity, supplying `engagement_id`, a `recipient_name` of 1 to 200 characters and a `recipient_email` of 1 to 200 characters. The recipient name must be unique within the engagement among rows whose `status` is neither `REVOKED` nor `DENIED`, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The engagement's ceiling is then enforced: the count of that engagement's distribution rows whose `status` is not `REVOKED` and not `DENIED` must be strictly less than `max_distribution_recipients`, else `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Because uniqueness is judged first, a duplicate recipient submitted while the engagement already sits at its ceiling reports the duplicate. New rows start in `REQUESTED` with `nda_reference`, `delivered_at`, `revoked_at`, `report_period_start` and `report_period_end` all null.

The distribution lifecycle runs `REQUESTED`, `NDA_PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `DELIVERED`, and the two terminal outcomes `REVOKED` and `DENIED`, driven by an `action` of `REQUEST_NDA`, `APPROVE`, `DENY`, `DELIVER` or `REVOKE`, with optional `nda_reference` and `reason` fields. An `AccountExecutive` moves `REQUESTED → NDA_PENDING` with `REQUEST_NDA`. A `ComplianceLead` then moves `NDA_PENDING → APPROVED` with `APPROVE`, which requires a non-null `nda_reference` in the payload and stamps it onto the row — a distribution cannot leave `NDA_PENDING` without an executed agreement reference. The same lead may deny the request outright from either `REQUESTED` or `NDA_PENDING`, landing in `DENIED` with no cross-entity consequence.

`APPROVED → DELIVERED`, performed by an `AccountExecutive`, is where the report actually changes hands, and it is doubly gated: the engagement's `status` must be `ISSUED` and its `gap_posture` must be exactly `OPEN`, so a frozen engagement blocks delivery with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN` and a restricted one with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`. Delivery stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock and snapshots the engagement's `observation_period_start` and `observation_period_end` onto the row as `report_period_start` and `report_period_end`. That snapshot is what anchors bridge-letter coverage and the post-period bucket of questionnaire deflection, and it is deliberately frozen at delivery.

Revocation, `DELIVERED → REVOKED`, belongs to the `ComplianceLead`. It stamps `revoked_at` and cascades: every customer questionnaire referencing this distribution whose `status` is not already `CLOSED` moves to `CLOSED` with `deflected_by_report_count`, `deflected_by_bridge_count` and `deflected_by_cuec_count` all reset to 0, `manual_response_count` reset to the questionnaire's `item_count`, `deflection_ratio` reset to 0.0000 and `bridge_letter_id_used` reset to null. Pulling the report back therefore un-deflects every question it had been answering. Questionnaires reach `CLOSED` only through this cascade, and they do so from `RECEIVED`, `DEFLECTED`, `PARTIALLY_DEFLECTED` or `MANUAL_RESPONSE` alike.

### 3.9. Bridging Forward, Articulating User Responsibilities, and Deflecting Questionnaires

A report covers a window that recedes into the past the moment it is issued, and customers ask about the interval since. A **bridge letter** closes that interval. An `AccountExecutive` drafts one against an `engagement_id`, giving a `coverage_start` that must fall on or after the engagement's `observation_period_end` and an `as_of_date` that must fall on or after `coverage_start`. A letter may declare `supersedes_bridge_letter_id`, naming the prior issued letter it extends, or leave it null for the engagement's first letter. The coverage window is **closed**: both endpoints lie inside it, so `coverage_days` is the difference between `as_of_date` and `coverage_start` in seconds, divided by 86400, truncated downward to a whole number, and then increased by one — a single-instant letter whose `as_of_date` equals its `coverage_start` therefore has a `coverage_days` of 1. A letter may not stretch beyond 120 days: a `coverage_days` above that is rejected as a malformed request under `ERR_VALIDATION`. Drafts begin in `DRAFT` with `issued_at` and `issued_by_user_id` null.

Issuing a letter is again the `AccountExecutive`'s act and carries four preconditions. The engagement must be in `ISSUED` and its posture exactly `OPEN`. At least one distribution of the engagement must have reached `DELIVERED`, else `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELIVERED` — there is nothing to bridge forward from until a report has actually been handed over. And the letter's closed window must not intersect the closed window of any *other* letter of the same engagement in `ISSUED`; because both endpoints are inside each window, **two letters that merely touch at a single shared instant do collide** and are rejected with `ERR_BRIDGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. On success the letter moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, stamping `issued_at` from the logical clock and `issued_by_user_id` from the header, and when `supersedes_bridge_letter_id` is non-null the named letter moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`. A letter also leaves `ISSUED` for `EXPIRED` when a calendar ops-run observes the logical clock strictly past its `as_of_date`, as §3.7 describes. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal, and a letter in either state no longer qualifies for the bridge bucket below.

**Complementary user entity controls** are the responsibilities the report pushes back onto the customer. A `ComplianceLead` drafts one against an `engagement_id` and a `trust_criterion_id`, with a `statement` of 1 to 1000 characters describing what the user entity must do and a `deflection_topic` of 1 to 60 characters serving as the topic key the deflection waterfall reads. The referenced criterion must be in scope for the engagement, else `ERR_CRITERION_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. Drafts begin with `articulation_status` `DRAFT` and a null `articulated_at`.

Publication and retirement travel on the same request, carrying `action` of either `ARTICULATE` or `RETIRE`. `ARTICULATE` moves `DRAFT → ARTICULATED` and requires the engagement to be in `READINESS`, `OBSERVATION`, `FIELDWORK` or `REPORTING`, and requires afresh that the referenced criterion's `category` is a member of the engagement's `required_criteria_categories` and that its `applies_to_report_types` contains the engagement's `report_type`. It stamps `articulated_at` from the logical clock. `RETIRE` moves either `ARTICULATED → RETIRED` or `DRAFT → RETIRED` and is permitted while the engagement has not reached `ISSUED`; retiring from `ARTICULATED` removes the statement from the third deflection bucket, while retiring from `DRAFT` has no cross-entity effect. `RETIRED` is terminal. Only rows in `ARTICULATED` are ever counted by the waterfall.

The last workflow in the system is **questionnaire deflection**. An `AccountExecutive` records an inbound customer security questionnaire against `engagement_id` and the `report_distribution_id` through which that customer received the report, naming the originator as `customer_name` and decomposing the questionnaire into three counts, each of which must be at least 0: `criteria_item_count` for items answerable from the report's criteria coverage, `post_period_item_count` for items about the interval after the report period ended, and `cuec_item_count` for items about user-entity responsibilities. The server sets `item_count` to the sum of the three and starts the row in `RECEIVED` with every bucket counter and `deflection_ratio` at 0, `bridge_letter_id_used` null and `as_of_date` null.

Deflection runs the waterfall as of a supplied instant, given as `as_of_date` and stamped onto the row. It is refused unless the questionnaire's own referenced distribution has reached `DELIVERED`, with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELIVERED` otherwise. The three buckets are then filled in order.

The **first bucket is the issued report itself**: `deflected_by_report_count` is the smaller of `criteria_item_count` and the engagement's `covered_criteria_count_attestation` as it stands at the moment of the call. The **second bucket is the bridge letter**. The candidate pool is the engagement's letters in `ISSUED` whose closed coverage window contains the supplied instant — that is, whose `coverage_start` is at or before it and whose own `as_of_date` is at or after it. Letters in `DRAFT`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded. Candidates are ranked by their own `as_of_date` descending, so the letter reaching furthest forward wins, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; the winner's identifier is recorded as `bridge_letter_id_used` and `deflected_by_bridge_count` absorbs the whole of `post_period_item_count`. When the pool is empty the bucket absorbs 0 and `bridge_letter_id_used` stays null. The **third bucket is the articulated complementary user entity controls**: `deflected_by_cuec_count` is the smaller of `cuec_item_count` and the number of that engagement's statements whose `articulation_status` is `ARTICULATED`, with drafts and retired statements excluded.

Whatever remains is the fourth bucket: `manual_response_count` is `item_count` less the three bucket counts. The `deflection_ratio` is exactly 0.0000 when `item_count` is 0, and otherwise `item_count` less `manual_response_count`, divided by `item_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

The outcome branches three ways on that remainder. When `manual_response_count` is 0 and `item_count` is greater than 0, the questionnaire moves `RECEIVED → DEFLECTED` — the report answered everything. When `manual_response_count` is greater than 0 but strictly less than `item_count`, it moves `RECEIVED → PARTIALLY_DEFLECTED`. When `manual_response_count` equals `item_count`, it moves `RECEIVED → MANUAL_RESPONSE`; this arm also absorbs the degenerate case of an empty questionnaire, where `item_count` is 0, every bucket absorbs 0, the remainder equals the item count at 0, and `deflection_ratio` is 0.0000. All three arms stamp the same fields: the four bucket counters, `deflection_ratio`, `as_of_date` and `bridge_letter_id_used`.
