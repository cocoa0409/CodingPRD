# Driver Settlement & Accessorial Pay Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A truckload carrier's settlement office is the place where dispatch, safety, accounting and driver relations all collide. Every week the office must turn a pile of delivered loads, detention tickets, layover reports, fuel-card balances and escrow obligations into one number per driver — and it must be able to explain that number line by line, months later, to a leased operator, an auditor or a regulator. The office pays company solo drivers, company teams, owner-operators and leased operators under different pay packages, computes miles from two different commercial mileage guides that disagree with each other by design, and is legally exposed on both sides: underpay a driver and the carrier faces a wage claim, take an impermissible deduction from a leased operator's escrow and the carrier faces a leasing-regulation violation.

This document specifies the Driver Settlement & Accessorial Pay Administration API, the system of record for that office. It owns pay-package configuration (per-mile, revenue-percentage, hourly and activity-based components), mileage determination under the practical and household-goods guides with a dispute path when a driver contests the distance, accessorial pay for detention, layover, stop-off, tarping and breakdown with per-type documentation requirements, maintenance escrow accounts with a whitelist of permissible deductions and a statutory return timeline, reconciliation of cash advances and fuel-card balances against the pay they are recovered from, per diem allocation and its reclassifying effect on taxable wages, itemized settlement-statement generation, and retroactive correction of pay that has already gone out the door.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is never silently restated**: a settlement that has been approved or paid is not edited in place — it is either voided in full, with every side effect it produced compensated, or corrected forward through an auditable retroactive correction that lands on a later statement. Second, **no single identity can move money end to end**: the clerk who prepares a settlement may not approve it, the approver may not pay it, and the reviewer of a pay dispute may not be the person who rules on it. All amounts in this API are integer cents; all distances are whole miles; all durations are whole minutes.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Settlement-Office Identities and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including every read — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being addressed, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authorization is per endpoint; the authorized roles for each are listed in §4.

The five roles are `PayrollAdmin`, `SettlementClerk`, `SettlementApprover`, `Driver` and `ComplianceAuditor`.

- A `PayrollAdmin` is the settlement-office administrator. It owns pay-package configuration, driver records, escrow, advances, the payment run and the voiding of settlements, and it is the break-glass role: it may perform any clerk or approver operation, while remaining subject to the same segregation-of-duties rules that forbid approving what it prepared and paying what it approved.
- A `SettlementClerk` books loads, adjudicates accessorial documentation, opens and prepares settlements, and takes disputes under review. A `SettlementClerk` may never approve or pay a settlement.
- A `SettlementApprover` approves prepared settlements and rules on disputes. It cannot prepare the settlement it approves, cannot pay, and cannot create the underlying loads or claims, so the prepare-approve-pay chain always spans at least two identities.
- A `Driver` is the driver or leased operator being paid. A `Driver` reads only its own records and may submit accessorial claims and their documentation, file mileage and pay disputes, reopen a denied dispute and withdraw its own dispute.
- A `ComplianceAuditor` is a read-only auditor of pay compliance. It reads pay packages, settlement statements, escrow ledgers and dispute histories across all drivers and writes nothing anywhere in the domain.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollAdmin`, `SettlementClerk`, `SettlementApprover` or `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition asks for it — as `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a settlement, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on an accessorial claim, as `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a dispute, as `cancelled_by_user_id` on a retroactive correction, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on an escrow ledger entry — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The `Driver` role is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Driver`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting driver record, and every ownership check compares the addressed resource's driver reference to that value: `Driver.id` itself, and the `driver_id` carried by a load, an accessorial claim, an advance, a settlement, a dispute or a retroactive correction. A `Driver` that addresses another driver's row — for reading or for writing — is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check is evaluated after role authorization and before any existence, state or money check, so a driver probing another driver's settlement learns only that it is out of scope.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every collection endpoint in this domain is paginated with two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is an invalid field value and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, collections are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest row first — which makes the escrow ledger, the settlement-line list and the advance list readable as chronological registers. The one deliberate exception is the settlement-line collection, which is ordered by the line's own `sequence` ascending so that it prints in statement order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any row remains beyond the current page.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, and a filter value that is not a declared member of the field's enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. Pay packages may be filtered by `status`, by `driver_type` and by `supersedes_pay_package_id`. Drivers may be filtered by `employment_status`, `driver_type`, `escrow_status` and `pay_package_id`. Loads may be filtered by `driver_id`, `settlement_id`, `pay_package_id_snapshot`, `movement_status` and `pay_status`. Accessorial claims may be filtered by `driver_id`, `load_id`, `settlement_id`, `status` and `accessorial_type`. Advances may be filtered by `driver_id`, `status` and `advance_type`. Settlements may be filtered by `driver_id` and `status`, and their lines by `line_type`. Escrow ledger entries may be filtered by `driver_id`, `settlement_id`, `reverses_transaction_id` and `txn_type`. Disputes may be filtered by `driver_id`, `load_id`, `settlement_id`, `resolution_correction_id`, `status` and `dispute_kind`. Retroactive corrections may be filtered by `driver_id`, `origin_settlement_id`, `applied_settlement_id`, `source_load_id`, `source_dispute_id` and `status`. When the acting role is `Driver`, every collection is additionally and unconditionally narrowed to rows whose driver reference equals `X-User-ID`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Append-Only Logs and Version Semantics

Pay packages, drivers, loads, accessorial claims, advances, settlements, disputes and retroactive corrections are the domain's mutable records. Each of them carries:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply it.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock described in §2.5.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations that only touch a derived money field.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the record. A recomputation of a derived field such as `escrow_balance_cents`, `pending_retro_correction_cents` or a year-to-date accumulator is a mutation: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the record that owns the field.

Three record types are append-only logs and are therefore exempt from that convention: pay components, escrow ledger entries and settlement lines. Each of them carries only an `id` (UUID) and a `created_at`; **`updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `PayComponent`, from an `EscrowTransaction` and from a `SettlementLine`**. Once written, a pay component's rate is never edited, an escrow entry is never restated — it is negated by a compensating entry — and a settlement line is never renumbered, only deleted wholesale when its settlement is voided.

### 2.5. The Logical Settlement Clock

Because settlement is a calendar-driven business — pay periods, 45-day escrow return windows, 30-day dispute windows, per-diem day counts — this service does not read wall-clock time. It keeps a **logical clock** that begins at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts and moves only when an operator sets it. Every timestamp the server stamps reads that clock: `created_at` and `updated_at` on every record, `activated_at` and `retired_at` on a pay package, `terminated_at`, `escrow_funded_at` and `escrow_returned_at` on a driver, `dispatched_at` and `delivered_at` on a load, `issued_at` on an advance, and `prepared_at`, `approved_at`, `paid_at` and `voided_at` on a settlement. Every temporal comparison also reads it — whether an escrow return is due, whether a dispute is inside its filing window, which pay period a delivery falls into. The clock must never fall back to real wall-clock time, not even before it has been set for the first time. A clock-setting request carries the target instant as `current_utc`, is available to all five roles, returns HTTP 200, and mutates no entity.

### 2.6. Money, Miles, Basis Points and Rounding

Every monetary field in this domain is an integer number of cents and every field name that ends in `_cents` is to be read that way; there are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in any response. Distances are whole miles, durations are whole minutes, and no rounding is ever applied to either.

Rates come in two shapes. A rate expressed in cents per unit — cents per mile, cents per hour, cents per load — multiplies a quantity directly. A rate expressed in **basis points** is one hundredth of one percent, so a percentage rate is applied by multiplying by the basis-point figure and dividing by 10,000; a rate of 2,800 basis points is 28 percent. Wherever such a division does not come out even, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent: a computed value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next cent.

Where a formula involves several roundings, the order in which they occur is part of the specification and is stated with the formula in §3, because rounding at a different step produces a different cent total. Two orderings in particular are pinned: the escrow accrual rounds each earning line to whole cents and only then sums those rounded per-line amounts (round-then-sum, never sum-then-round), and each linehaul leg is rounded to whole cents independently before the two legs are compared, with the team share applied and rounded after the comparison rather than before it. Signed amounts — retroactive correction deltas in particular — are rounded by rounding the magnitude and re-applying the sign, so a down-grade and an up-grade of the same size round symmetrically.

### 2.7. Nested Addressing and Left-to-Right Path Validation

Some resources are addressed beneath a parent: a pay package's components, a driver's escrow account and its deductions and return, a settlement's lines. For any path containing more than one identifier or naming a parent record, the server validates the path strictly left to right and reports the **first** identifier that does not resolve. Asking for the components of a pay-package id that does not exist yields `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); asking for the lines of a settlement id that does not exist yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; recording an escrow deduction against a driver id that does not exist yields `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`. The same rule governs bodies that reference a parent: a request naming a `load_id`, a `driver_id`, a `pay_package_id`, a `settlement_id` or an `origin_settlement_id` that does not resolve is a 404 on that reference, and the reference is checked before any state, documentation or money rule that depends on the referenced record.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Repeatable Writes

Three write operations in this domain are legitimately repeatable — nothing in any state machine prevents a second call — which means a client acting on a stale read cannot be detected by any state precondition. Those three, and only those three, are protected by optimistic concurrency control. Each requires a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent record; if that value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is mutated.

- `POST /driver-pay/pay-packages/{id}/revise` requires `expected_pay_package_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the pay package being revised. A mismatch with the package's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PAY_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A package may legitimately be revised more than once and the newest revision supersedes it on activation, so the stale-version check is the only deterministic guard against opening a revision from an out-of-date view of the package.
- `POST /driver-pay/settlements/{id}/apply-corrections` requires `expected_settlement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target settlement. A mismatch with the settlement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SETTLEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Sweeping corrections is repeatable while the settlement remains `DRAFT` — a later call simply picks up whatever corrections are pending then — so a stale view of the settlement's totals is detectable only by the version check.
- `POST /driver-pay/drivers/{id}/escrow/deductions` requires `expected_driver_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the driver. A mismatch with the driver's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple deductions against the same driver are legitimate and no state field blocks a second one, so the escrow balance the operator sized the deduction against can only be protected this way.

No other endpoint in this domain accepts a version field, and none applies a version check. In particular, activating, retiring, dispatching, delivering, approving, paying and voiding are all guarded by state preconditions instead, and supplying a version field to them has no effect.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence: Compliance Before Bookkeeping, Bookkeeping Before Money

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the fixed order below and the first failure is reported. The ordering encodes the settlement office's own priorities: identity first, then request shape, then existence, then stale-version protection, then state, then wage and documentation compliance, then structural and temporal constraints, and finally the money limits — which are placed last precisely because they are the only failures a driver can cure by waiting for the next period.

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and the role is one of the five declared names.
2. `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` — the acting role is authorized for the addressed endpoint.
3. `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — when the acting role is `Driver`, `X-User-ID` equals the driver reference of the addressed resource.
4. `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` — every field the endpoint requires is present and not null.
5. `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` — enum fields carry a declared member; money and count fields are integers; client-supplied magnitudes (an `amount_cents` on a request, miles, minutes, `stop_count`, `rate_value`) are not negative; timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC.
6. `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` — the driver addressed by the path, or named by `driver_id` in the body, exists.
7. `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` — the pay package addressed by the path, or named by `pay_package_id` in the body, exists.
8. `ERR_LOAD_NOT_FOUND` — the load addressed by the path, or named by `load_id` in the body, exists.
9. `ERR_ACCESSORIAL_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` — the accessorial claim addressed by the path exists.
10. `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_FOUND` — the advance addressed by the path exists.
11. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — the settlement addressed by the path, or named by `settlement_id` or `origin_settlement_id` in the body, exists.
12. `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` — the dispute addressed by the path exists.
13. `ERR_RETRO_CORRECTION_NOT_FOUND` — the retroactive correction addressed by the path exists.
14. `ERR_ESCROW_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND` — the escrow ledger entry addressed by the path exists.
15. `ERR_STALE_PAY_PACKAGE_VERSION` — on a revision, `expected_pay_package_version` equals the package's current `version`.
16. `ERR_STALE_SETTLEMENT_VERSION` — on a correction sweep, `expected_settlement_version` equals the settlement's current `version`.
17. `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION` — on an escrow deduction, `expected_driver_version` equals the driver's current `version`.
18. `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIVE` — the package is `DRAFT` when a component is appended and when it is activated, and `ACTIVE` when it is revised, retired or assigned to a driver.
19. `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_REVISION_PENDING` — the package's `has_pending_revision` is false; a package whose successor is still `DRAFT` can neither be revised again nor retired.
20. `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE` — the driver's `employment_status` is the one the operation needs: `ACTIVE` to book a load, submit an accessorial claim, issue an advance, open a settlement or record an escrow deduction; `ONBOARDING` or `ACTIVE` to be assigned a pay package; and for an employment-axis move, the requested `new_status` must be reachable from the current `employment_status`.
21. `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_ACTIVE` — the driver's `escrow_status` is `COLLECTING` or `FUNDED` for a deduction and for a reversal, and `CLOSING` for a return.
22. `ERR_LOAD_NOT_DELIVERED` — the load's `movement_status` is the one the operation needs: `TENDERED` to dispatch, `IN_TRANSIT` to deliver, `DELIVERED` to approve an accessorial claim against it or to file a mileage dispute on it.
23. `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_SETTLED` — the accessorial claim is not already attached to a settlement, that is its `status` is not `PAID` and its `settlement_id` is null. This referential check deliberately outranks the claim-status check at rung 49: a claim swept onto a settlement is frozen, and the caller must be told that it is settled rather than that its status is wrong.
24. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` — the settlement's `status` is `DRAFT` to sweep corrections and to prepare, `PREPARED` to approve, `APPROVED` to pay.
25. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_TERMINAL` — the settlement is not already `PAID` or `VOIDED`; both are terminal and accept no further transition, void included. Because this rung sits below rung 24, an attempt to approve a settlement that is already `PAID` surfaces as `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, while an attempt to void it surfaces as `ERR_SETTLEMENT_TERMINAL`.
26. `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID` — the dispute's `status` is `OPEN` to take under review, `UNDER_REVIEW` to resolve, `DENIED` to reopen, and either `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` to withdraw.
27. `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_CANCELLABLE` — the advance's `status` is `ISSUED`; once recovery has begun or finished it can no longer be cancelled.
28. `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_PENDING` — the retroactive correction's `status` is `PENDING`; `APPLIED` and `CANCELLED` corrections are immutable.
29. `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING` — a claim whose `accessorial_type` is `DETENTION`, `TARPING` or `BREAKDOWN` carries a non-null `document_ref` before it may be approved. The documentation requirement is evaluated before any bookkeeping question about the underlying load.
30. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the `X-User-ID` approving a settlement differs from its `prepared_by_user_id`, and the `X-User-ID` paying it differs from its `approved_by_user_id`.
31. `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_PERMISSIBLE` — a manual escrow deduction's `reason_code` is drawn from the permissible list.
32. `ERR_ESCROW_RETURN_NOT_DUE` — the logical clock has reached the driver's `escrow_return_due_at`.
33. `ERR_ITEMIZATION_INCOMPLETE` — when a settlement's `itemization_required` is true, every earning line carries a non-null `reference_id` and a non-null `quantity`, and every accessorial line's source claim carries a non-null `document_ref`.
34. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_HOLD_ACTIVE` — the driver's `settlement_hold` is false.
35. `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT` — a component's `sequence` is unused within its package and the package holds no other component of the same `component_type`.
36. `ERR_COMPONENT_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED` — the `component_type` is legal for the package's `driver_type`.
37. `ERR_PACKAGE_HAS_NO_LINEHAUL_COMPONENT` — a package being activated holds at least one `PER_MILE` or `REVENUE_PERCENT` component.
38. `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID` — `end_at` is strictly after `start_at` on an accessorial claim, and `period_end` is strictly after `period_start` on a settlement.
39. `ERR_ACCESSORIAL_OVERLAP` — a detention, tarping, breakdown or stop-off claim interval does not overlap another non-rejected claim interval on the same load.
40. `ERR_LAYOVER_OVERLAP` — a layover claim shares no UTC calendar date with another non-rejected layover claim of the same driver.
41. `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` — a settlement period does not overlap the period of another non-voided settlement of the same driver.
42. `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` — the dispute is filed inside the filing window measured from its reference instant.
43. `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` — no other dispute of the same `dispute_kind` on the same load or the same origin settlement is currently `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`.
44. `ERR_DISPUTE_REOPEN_LIMIT` — the dispute's `reopen_count` is strictly below the reopen limit before a denied dispute is reopened.
45. `ERR_MILEAGE_CLAIM_UNCHANGED` — a mileage claim differs from what the load was already paid on.
46. `ERR_ZERO_VALUE_ACCESSORIAL` — the computed accessorial `amount_cents` is strictly greater than zero.
47. `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_ACTIVITY` — the prepare sweep finds at least one eligible load, one eligible approved claim, or one retroactive-correction line already swept onto the settlement.
48. `ERR_LOAD_ALREADY_SETTLED` — the load is not already attached to a settlement, that is its `pay_status` is neither `SETTLED` nor `ADJUSTED` and its `settlement_id` is null.
49. `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED` — the accessorial claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED` to approve and to reject, and `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` to attach a document.
50. `ERR_DRIVER_MISMATCH` — the `driver_id` on the body agrees with the driver of the referenced parent record.
51. `ERR_NO_PENDING_CORRECTIONS` — every correction a sweep would take is `PENDING` and belongs to the settlement's driver, and at least one such correction exists.
52. `ERR_DRIVER_HAS_NO_PAY_PACKAGE` — the driver has an assigned `pay_package_id` and that package was `ACTIVE` at the delivery instant, so linehaul can be computed.
53. `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` — the requested deduction magnitude does not exceed the driver's `escrow_balance_cents`.
54. `ERR_ADVANCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — the requested advance principal plus the driver's `advance_outstanding_cents` does not exceed the outstanding-advance ceiling.
55. `ERR_NEGATIVE_NET_PAY` — the `net_pay_cents` a prepare would compute is not below zero.

The precise trigger conditions, thresholds and constants behind these checks live with the workflows they guard, in §3.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties Across the Prepare–Approve–Pay Chain

Three signatures are required to move money out of the carrier, and no two of them may belong to the same identity in the same place. The `X-User-ID` that prepares a settlement is recorded as `prepared_by_user_id`; the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from it, and is recorded as `approved_by_user_id`; the payer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `approved_by_user_id`. A violation at either hop is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The same principle governs dispute adjudication: the `X-User-ID` resolving a dispute must differ from the `reviewed_by_user_id` that took it under review, and for a dispute of kind `PAY` it must additionally differ from the `prepared_by_user_id` of the settlement under dispute — the clerk who built the statement may not be the one who rules that the statement was right. Because `PayrollAdmin` may act at every hop, these identity comparisons — not role membership — are what actually keep the chain honest.

### 2.11. Cascading Recomputation and Derived-Field Consistency

Many of this domain's most important fields are derived, and they are recomputed eagerly rather than at read time: a response never shows a stale derived value. When one write touches several records, every hop of the cascade is part of the specification.

A driver's `escrow_balance_cents` is at all times the sum of `amount_cents` across every escrow ledger entry whose `driver_id` is that driver, with no filtering by any status, because the ledger is append-only; it is 0 when the ledger is empty. Each ledger entry also records `balance_after_cents`, the driver's escrow balance immediately after that entry was appended. A driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents` is the signed sum of `delta_gross_cents` across that driver's retroactive corrections whose `status` is `PENDING`, with `APPLIED` and `CANCELLED` corrections excluded, and is 0 when there are none. `settlement_hold` is derived from it and recomputed on every change to it. The year-to-date accumulators `ytd_gross_pay_cents`, `ytd_taxable_wages_cents` and `ytd_per_diem_non_taxable_cents` are the sums of `gross_pay_cents`, `taxable_wages_cents` and `per_diem_non_taxable_cents` over that driver's settlements whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PAID`; settlements in `DRAFT`, `PREPARED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing. `advance_outstanding_cents` is the sum of `outstanding_cents` over that driver's advances whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `RECOVERING`.

On the package side, `component_count` is the number of pay components whose `pay_package_id` is that package. On the settlement side, `line_count` is the number of settlement lines whose `settlement_id` is that settlement, and each money total is a sum over a subset of those lines, as §3.7 specifies. On the advance side, `recovered_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over advance-recovery lines whose `reference_id` is that advance and whose owning settlement's `status` is `PREPARED`, `APPROVED` or `PAID`, and `outstanding_cents` is the advance's original `amount_cents` less `recovered_cents` — so advance principal is conserved at every observable point: the original principal always equals `outstanding_cents` plus `recovered_cents`.

Because a derived recomputation is a mutation, it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the record it lands on. That matters for the three optimistic-concurrency guards in §2.8: a settlement approval that deposits escrow bumps the driver's `version`, which will invalidate a client's older `expected_driver_version` on its next escrow deduction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring a Pay Package and Its Components

Everything a driver earns is traceable to a **pay package**, and a package is built before it can pay anyone. A `PayrollAdmin` opens one by supplying a human-readable `name`, the `driver_type` the package may be assigned to — one of `COMPANY_SOLO`, `COMPANY_TEAM`, `OWNER_OPERATOR` or `LEASED_OPERATOR` — the `mileage_guide` whose distances will drive per-mile pay, either `PRACTICAL` or `HHG`, the `per_diem_method` to apply at settlement time, one of `NONE`, `DAY_BASED` or `PERCENT_OF_LINEHAUL`, and the boolean `escrow_required` stating whether drivers on this package maintain a maintenance escrow account. Two optional integers accompany the escrow flag: `escrow_target_cents`, the escrow principal the carrier intends to hold, and `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents`, the ceiling on how much escrow a single settlement may withhold. Both default to 0 and neither may be negative. A newly created package is `DRAFT`, its `component_count` is 0, its `has_pending_revision` is false, and its `supersedes_pay_package_id`, `activated_at` and `retired_at` are all null.

While the package is `DRAFT`, the administrator appends **pay components** to it, one at a time; each component is immutable once written. A component names its `component_type` — `PER_MILE`, `REVENUE_PERCENT`, `HOURLY` or `ACTIVITY` — the `unit` its rate is expressed in, the integer `rate_value`, which must be strictly greater than zero, the boolean `taxable` stating whether earnings from this component are taxable wages before any per diem reclassification (a component created without an explicit `taxable` is taxable), and an integer `sequence` of at least 1 that fixes the deterministic order in which the component is applied inside the package. The component's owning package is taken from the request path and stored as `pay_package_id`, and appending a component increments the package's `component_count` and bumps the package's `version`. The `unit` is not free: `PER_MILE` must pair with `MILE`, `REVENUE_PERCENT` with `PERCENT_BPS`, `HOURLY` with `HOUR` and `ACTIVITY` with `LOAD`, and any other pairing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. For the cents-per-unit types the `rate_value` is read as cents per mile, per hour or per load; for `REVENUE_PERCENT` it is read as basis points of linehaul revenue. A package holds at most one component of each `component_type`, and no two components in the same package may share a `sequence` — either collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT` (HTTP 409).

Component legality also depends on who is being paid. Owner-operators and leased operators run their own trucks and are never paid by the hour, so an `HOURLY` component may not be added to a package whose `driver_type` is `OWNER_OPERATOR` or `LEASED_OPERATOR`. Company drivers are not revenue-share partners, so a `REVENUE_PERCENT` component may not be added to a package whose `driver_type` is `COMPANY_SOLO` or `COMPANY_TEAM`. Either attempt is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422).

Activation is the gate between configuration and payroll. A `PayrollAdmin` may activate a package only while its `status` is `DRAFT`; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) otherwise. The package must hold at least one component, and at least one of those components must be of type `PER_MILE` or `REVENUE_PERCENT`, so that every activated package is capable of paying linehaul; a package holding neither is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_HAS_NO_LINEHAUL_COMPONENT` (HTTP 422). If `escrow_required` is true, both `escrow_target_cents` and `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents` must be strictly greater than zero — an escrow package that collects nothing, or that has no target to collect toward, is not activatable, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. A successful activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and increments the package's `version` by 1.

Rates change, and a package in production is never edited. Instead the administrator opens a **revision**: a new `DRAFT` package whose `supersedes_pay_package_id` points at the predecessor and which starts life with a copy of the predecessor's components. The revision request carries `expected_pay_package_version` as described in §2.8 and may override `name`, `per_diem_method`, `escrow_target_cents` and `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents`. The `mileage_guide` is immutable across a revision chain: the successor always inherits the predecessor's guide, so a `mileage_guide` supplied on a revision request does not change it. Opening a revision sets the predecessor's `has_pending_revision` to true and bumps the predecessor's `version`; a package that already has a `DRAFT` successor open cannot be revised again, and the second attempt is rejected with `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_REVISION_PENDING` (HTTP 409). Only an `ACTIVE` package may be revised.

When the successor is activated, the succession completes in one step: the successor moves `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE` with `activated_at` stamped, and because its `supersedes_pay_package_id` is not null, the predecessor is driven `ACTIVE` → `RETIRED` in the same operation — its `retired_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its `has_pending_revision` is cleared back to false, and its `version` is incremented by 1. Drivers already pointing at the predecessor keep their `pay_package_id` untouched; only *new* assignments to the retired package are refused. A package may also be retired directly, which requires it to be `ACTIVE` with `has_pending_revision` false — a package with a `DRAFT` successor pending is rejected with `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_REVISION_PENDING` — and sets `status` to `RETIRED`, stamps `retired_at`, leaves `has_pending_revision` false and increments `version` by 1. `RETIRED` is terminal: a retired package can never be re-activated, revised or newly assigned, yet it stays fully readable, because loads dispatched while it was `ACTIVE` still point at it and are still paid from its components.

### 3.2. Driver Records, Package Assignment and the Employment Axis

A driver record is registered by a `PayrollAdmin` with a `legal_name` as it should print on the settlement statement, a `driver_type` from the same four-member enumeration as pay packages, an optional two-letter `home_state` code for the domicile, and an optional boolean `per_diem_elected` recording the driver's election into the per diem program (absent, the election is false). A new driver begins on the employment axis in `ONBOARDING` with no `pay_package_id`, on the escrow axis in `NOT_REQUIRED`, with every balance and year-to-date accumulator at 0, `settlement_hold` false, and `terminated_at`, `escrow_funded_at`, `escrow_return_due_at` and `escrow_returned_at` all null.

Assignment is what makes a driver payable. A `PayrollAdmin` assigns a package by supplying its `pay_package_id`; the package must be `ACTIVE` (a `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` package is refused with `ERR_PAY_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIVE`), the driver's `employment_status` must be `ONBOARDING` or `ACTIVE` — a package is assigned before the driver is activated, which is why both are admissible — and the driver's `driver_type` must equal the package's `driver_type`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. On success the driver's `pay_package_id` is set, `escrow_target_cents` and `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents` are copied from the package onto the driver, and the driver's `version` is bumped.

The employment axis moves through a single status-setting operation carrying `new_status` and, when the destination is termination, an optional `terminated_at`. `ONBOARDING` → `ACTIVE` requires the driver's assigned package to be `ACTIVE`, which is why a driver with no package cannot be activated. An `ACTIVE` driver may be suspended, `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended one reinstated, `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE`. From either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` the driver may be terminated — `ACTIVE` → `TERMINATED` or `SUSPENDED` → `TERMINATED` — which sets `employment_status` to `TERMINATED` and stamps `terminated_at` — from the request body when supplied, otherwise from the logical clock. Every one of these moves increments the driver's `version` by 1, and a `new_status` that is not reachable from the current `employment_status` along that axis is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). `TERMINATED` is terminal on the employment axis.

What each employment status permits is the practical heart of the axis. A driver must be `ACTIVE` to have a load booked, an accessorial claim submitted, an advance issued, a settlement opened or an escrow deduction recorded; any of those against a driver who is `ONBOARDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ACTIVE`. A suspended or terminated driver accrues no *new* work, but work already performed is still paid: preparing, approving and paying a settlement remain open for drivers who are `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, and so do closing and returning escrow. That asymmetry is deliberate — suspension is a dispatch decision, not a wage forfeiture.

### 3.3. The Maintenance Escrow Account

Owner-operators and leased operators on packages with `escrow_required` true maintain a maintenance escrow account, and the escrow axis runs **independently of employment**: a driver's `escrow_status` moves on its own through `NOT_REQUIRED`, `COLLECTING`, `FUNDED`, `CLOSING` and `RETURNED`. A driver assigned a package whose `escrow_required` is false simply stays in `NOT_REQUIRED`, which is why that is the initial state rather than an unreachable placeholder. Assigning a package whose `escrow_required` is true to a driver in `NOT_REQUIRED` whose `employment_status` is `ONBOARDING` or `ACTIVE` moves the escrow axis `NOT_REQUIRED` → `COLLECTING`, copies `escrow_target_cents` and `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents` from the package, leaves `escrow_balance_cents` at 0 so that the first settlement begins accrual, and increments the driver's `version` by 1.

The escrow account is an **append-only ledger**. Every movement is a new entry recording its `driver_id`, its `txn_type` — one of `DEPOSIT`, `PERMISSIBLE_DEDUCTION`, `REVERSAL` or `RETURN_DISBURSEMENT` — a signed `amount_cents`, an audit `reason_code`, the `recorded_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the `balance_after_cents` observed immediately after the entry landed. A `DEPOSIT` is positive. A `PERMISSIBLE_DEDUCTION` is negative: the request supplies a positive magnitude and the server negates it. A `RETURN_DISBURSEMENT` is negative. A `REVERSAL` is exactly the negation of the `amount_cents` of the entry it reverses, identified by `reverses_transaction_id`, which is null on every other entry type. The `settlement_id` on an entry names the settlement whose approval or void produced it, and is null for manually recorded deductions, for reversals and for the termination return. The `reason_code` enumeration spans both the system-written and the operator-written reasons: `SETTLEMENT_ACCRUAL`, `SETTLEMENT_VOID`, `TERMINATION_RETURN`, `DEDUCTION_DISPUTED`, `ESCROW_FUNDING`, `TRACTOR_LEASE`, `INSURANCE_PREMIUM`, `PERMIT_FEE`, `CARGO_CLAIM_DAMAGE`, `FUEL_TAX_TRUEUP`, `ADVANCE_DEFAULT`, `EQUIPMENT_DAMAGE`, `UNRETURNED_EQUIPMENT` and `FUEL_CARD_DEFAULT`.

Leasing regulation is unforgiving about what a carrier may take out of an operator's escrow, so a manually recorded deduction may only cite one of six permissible reasons: `ESCROW_FUNDING`, `TRACTOR_LEASE`, `INSURANCE_PREMIUM`, `PERMIT_FEE`, `CARGO_CLAIM_DAMAGE` or `FUEL_TAX_TRUEUP`. Any other member of the enumeration is an impermissible deduction from a leased-operator escrow and is rejected with `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_PERMISSIBLE` (HTTP 422). A manual deduction must also carry a non-null `document_ref` identifying the supporting document — system-generated entries carry none — and its magnitude may not exceed the driver's current `escrow_balance_cents`, because a permissible deduction may never drive the escrow principal negative; an oversized request is rejected with `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` (HTTP 422). The driver's `escrow_status` must be `COLLECTING` or `FUNDED` for a deduction to be recorded at all, and `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) is returned when it is not. A deduction also requires `expected_driver_version` per §2.8, and the operation is a creation: it returns HTTP 201 with the new ledger entry, decreases `escrow_balance_cents` and bumps the driver's `version`.

Because an entry is never edited, a mistaken entry is corrected by writing a compensating one. Reversing an existing entry requires a `reason_code` and creates a new `REVERSAL` entry whose `amount_cents` is exactly the negation of the referenced entry's `amount_cents` and whose `reverses_transaction_id` points at it; the original row is untouched. A reversal likewise requires the driver's `escrow_status` to be `COLLECTING` or `FUNDED`.

The escrow balance itself drives two automatic transitions, and they form a cycle rather than a one-way street. Whenever an appended entry raises `escrow_balance_cents` to `escrow_target_cents` or above, the axis moves `COLLECTING` → `FUNDED`, `escrow_funded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the driver's `version` is incremented, and every later escrow-deduction line on that driver's settlements is suppressed — statement generation emits none while the axis is anything other than `COLLECTING`. Whenever an appended entry drops `escrow_balance_cents` back below `escrow_target_cents`, the axis moves `FUNDED` → `COLLECTING`, `escrow_funded_at` is cleared to null, the driver's `version` is incremented, and escrow-deduction line generation is re-enabled on later settlements. This `FUNDED` → `COLLECTING` → `FUNDED` loop fires on permissible deductions and on their reversals alike.

Closing and returning escrow is the end of the leasing relationship. A `PayrollAdmin` may close the account of a driver whose `employment_status` is `TERMINATED` and whose `escrow_status` is `COLLECTING` or `FUNDED`; closing moves the axis `COLLECTING` → `CLOSING` or `FUNDED` → `CLOSING`, computes `escrow_return_due_at` as the driver's `terminated_at` plus 45 days, writes no ledger row at all — the principal is untouched — and bumps the driver's `version`. The statutory wait then runs on the logical clock. A return may only be executed while `escrow_status` is `CLOSING`, only at or after `escrow_return_due_at` (an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_ESCROW_RETURN_NOT_DUE`, HTTP 409), and only while the driver holds no retroactive correction in `PENDING` status, so that money still owed in either direction is resolved before the principal leaves. The request carries a `payment_reference` for the outgoing disbursement. On success the server appends a ledger entry with `txn_type` `RETURN_DISBURSEMENT` and `reason_code` `TERMINATION_RETURN` whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the whole current `escrow_balance_cents`; that append itself drives `escrow_balance_cents` to 0; the axis moves `CLOSING` → `RETURNED`, `escrow_returned_at` is stamped and the driver's `version` is incremented. `RETURNED` is terminal on the escrow axis, and further deductions against it are refused with `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 3.4. Booking a Load: Tender, Dispatch, Delivery and Paid Miles

A load enters the system when a `PayrollAdmin` or a `SettlementClerk` tenders it to an `ACTIVE` driver. The tender carries the `driver_id`, the `linehaul_revenue_cents` the carrier bills the shipper — strictly greater than zero, and the basis of any revenue-percentage pay — and **both** mileage figures for the same movement: `practical_miles`, the longer routed distance reported by the practical guide, and `hhg_miles`, the shorter short-line distance reported by the household-goods guide. Both must be strictly greater than zero, and both are stored regardless of which guide the driver's package actually pays on, because the pair is exactly what a later mileage dispute argues about. Optional fields complete the record: `origin_zip` and `dest_zip` postal codes, `stop_count` — the total number of stops including the final delivery, at least 1, defaulting to 1 when omitted — and `tarp_required`, a boolean defaulting to false that states whether the freight needed tarping. A tendered load starts with `movement_status` `TENDERED`, `pay_status` `UNPAID`, `hours_worked_minutes` 0, and `settlement_id`, `pay_package_id_snapshot`, `dispatched_at` and `delivered_at` all null.

A load carries **two independent state axes**, and this independence is load-bearing. `movement_status` tracks the physical movement through `TENDERED`, `IN_TRANSIT` and `DELIVERED`; `pay_status` tracks the money through `UNPAID`, `ACCRUED`, `SETTLED` and `ADJUSTED`. Once a load reaches `DELIVERED` the movement axis is terminal and neither dispatch nor delivery may fire again, while the pay axis keeps moving underneath it for as long as the money keeps moving.

Dispatching a tendered load advances `TENDERED` → `IN_TRANSIT`, sets `dispatched_at` from the optional `dispatched_at` in the request body or from the logical clock when it is omitted, and — the critical act — **pins** `pay_package_id_snapshot` to the driver's current `pay_package_id`. Dispatch requires the driver to have an assigned package whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a driver with no package is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_HAS_NO_PAY_PACKAGE` (HTTP 409). From this moment on, every pay computation on this load reads the snapshot and never the driver's current package, so a revision activated mid-week cannot retroactively re-rate a truck that is already rolling. Dispatch increments the load's `version` by 1, and a dispatch attempted on a load that is not `TENDERED` is rejected with `ERR_LOAD_NOT_DELIVERED` (HTTP 409) — the same code reports every wrong-movement-state attempt on a load.

Delivery is where the load's pay is computed. The request may carry `delivered_at`, `hours_worked_minutes` — the on-duty minutes logged against the load, which may not be negative — and a corrected `stop_count`. The load must be `IN_TRANSIT`, and `delivered_at` must be at or after `dispatched_at`; an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. On success the movement axis advances `IN_TRANSIT` → `DELIVERED`, `delivered_at` is set from the request or the logical clock, the pay axis advances `UNPAID` → `ACCRUED`, making the load eligible for a settlement sweep, and the load's `version` is incremented by 1.

Paid miles are determined first. When no upheld mileage dispute exists on the load, `applied_mileage_guide` is taken from the `mileage_guide` of the package in `pay_package_id_snapshot`, and `base_guide_miles` is the load's `practical_miles` when that guide is `PRACTICAL` or its `hhg_miles` when that guide is `HHG`. The choice matters: the practical guide's longer distance raises paid miles and can make the per-mile component out-earn the revenue-percentage component, while the household-goods guide's shorter distance lowers paid miles for the identical load and can flip the winning component the other way. A **short-haul floor** then applies: `short_haul_floor_applied` is true only when `base_guide_miles` is strictly below 250 miles, and false at exactly 250; when it is true, `paid_miles` is raised to 250, and otherwise `paid_miles` equals `base_guide_miles`. Miles are whole numbers throughout and are never rounded. Before delivery, `applied_mileage_guide`, `base_guide_miles`, `paid_miles`, `linehaul_pay_cents` and `linehaul_line_type` are all null.

Linehaul pay is then computed from the pay components of the snapshotted package — components added to a later revision never affect an already-delivered load. There are two competing legs. The **per-mile leg** is a mileage band: paid miles up to and including the band break at 500 miles earn the `PER_MILE` component's `rate_value` in cents each, and every mile beyond 500 earns a marginal rate of that `rate_value` plus 3 cents; formally, the lesser of `paid_miles` and 500 multiplied by the rate, plus the excess of `paid_miles` over 500 (zero when there is no excess) multiplied by the rate plus 3. The **revenue-percentage leg** is the load's `linehaul_revenue_cents` multiplied by the `REVENUE_PERCENT` component's `rate_value` in basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents. Each leg is rounded to whole cents independently, and a leg that has no corresponding component in the package simply does not exist.

Selection between the legs is non-linear: when both exist, the greater one wins, and an exact tie is won by the per-mile leg. The winner sets `linehaul_pay_cents` and `linehaul_line_type`, which is `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE` when the per-mile leg wins and `LINEHAUL_PERCENT` when the percentage leg wins. When only one leg exists it wins by default; when neither exists, `linehaul_pay_cents` is 0 and `linehaul_line_type` is `NONE`. Only after the comparison is the **team share** applied: if the load's driver has `driver_type` `COMPANY_TEAM`, `linehaul_pay_cents` is multiplied by 5,000 basis points — one half — and rounded half up, because a team load's linehaul is split between two seats. Applying the share before the comparison would be wrong, since it can change which leg wins.

Two further legs are additive and never enter the comparison. When the snapshotted package holds an `HOURLY` component and the load's `hours_worked_minutes` is greater than zero, hourly pay is `hours_worked_minutes` multiplied by that component's `rate_value` — cents per hour — divided by 60 and rounded half up to whole cents; otherwise hourly pay is zero. When the package holds an `ACTIVITY` component, activity pay is exactly that component's `rate_value`, a flat amount per load; otherwise it is zero. Both are carried onto the settlement as their own lines rather than folded into `linehaul_pay_cents`.

### 3.5. Accessorial Claims: Documentation, Pricing and Adjudication

Detention, layover, stop-off, tarping and breakdown are the events that turn a routine haul into an argument, so each is captured as an **accessorial claim** and each is priced by its own rule. A claim may be submitted by a `PayrollAdmin`, a `SettlementClerk` or the `Driver` itself, and carries the `load_id` of the load the event occurred on, the `driver_id` claiming it, the `accessorial_type` — one of `DETENTION`, `LAYOVER`, `STOP_OFF`, `TARPING` or `BREAKDOWN` — the interval endpoints `start_at` and `end_at`, and optionally a `document_ref` naming the supporting document. The `driver_id` is denormalized from the load's own `driver_id` at creation and never re-derived afterwards, so it must agree with it: a claim naming a driver who did not move the load is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`, and an interval that fails this is rejected with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A new claim is `SUBMITTED`, its `billable_units` and `amount_cents` are 0 until approval, its `settlement_id` and `approved_by_user_id` are null, and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`.

Documentation obligations are derived, not declared: `documentation_required` is computed at submission as true when `accessorial_type` is `DETENTION`, `TARPING` or `BREAKDOWN`, and false for `LAYOVER` and `STOP_OFF`. Detention, tarping and breakdown are the three the shipper will contest, so the carrier will not pay them on the driver's word alone.

Duplicate-event protection differs by shape. For a `DETENTION`, `TARPING`, `BREAKDOWN` or `STOP_OFF` claim, the new interval may not overlap the interval of any other non-rejected claim **on the same load**, where each interval is treated as half-open — its start instant is inside, its end instant is outside — and each already-stored interval is extended by 15 minutes past its `end_at` before the comparison, so two detention tickets filed back-to-back at the same door collide rather than both paying. A collision is `ERR_ACCESSORIAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A `LAYOVER` claim is compared differently: it may share no UTC calendar date with any other non-rejected `LAYOVER` claim **of the same driver**, comparing inclusive calendar-date sets rather than instants, so two layovers on two different loads that touch the same date still collide, and that collision is `ERR_LAYOVER_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A driver cannot be laid over twice on one night.

Documentation can be supplied or replaced after the fact by a `PayrollAdmin`, a `SettlementClerk` or the `Driver`, by submitting a `document_ref` against a claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`; any other status is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409), and a claim already attached to a settlement is refused earlier with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409). Attaching a document to a `REJECTED` claim returns it to `SUBMITTED` so that it can be re-reviewed — a rejection for missing paperwork is not the end of the road.

Approval prices the claim. A `PayrollAdmin` or `SettlementClerk` may approve a claim whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` when its referenced load's `movement_status` is `DELIVERED`, when that load's `pay_status` is not `SETTLED`, when either `documentation_required` is false or `document_ref` is non-null — a missing document on a detention, tarping or breakdown claim is `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422) — and, for a `TARPING` claim specifically, only when the referenced load's `tarp_required` is true, since tarping pay for freight that needed no tarp is not payable. A load already attached to a settlement is refused with `ERR_LOAD_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Approval computes `billable_units` and `amount_cents`, advances the claim `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

Pricing runs off the whole minutes between `start_at` and `end_at`, and each type has its own arithmetic and its own constants:

- **`DETENTION`.** The first 120 minutes at the dock are free. Billable minutes are the total minutes less 120, or zero when the total does not exceed the free allowance. `billable_units` is the count of whole completed 15-minute increments in those billable minutes, truncated toward zero and never rounded up. `amount_cents` is `billable_units` multiplied by 500 cents per increment, and the whole detention charge is capped at 20,000 cents — the lesser of the computed amount and the cap.
- **`LAYOVER`.** Layover is counted in calendar days, not hours. Take the number of distinct UTC calendar dates covered by the interval, counting both the `start_at` date and the `end_at` date. The first of those days is unpaid, so `billable_units` is that count less 1, floored at zero, and then capped at 3 payable days. `amount_cents` is `billable_units` multiplied by 12,500 cents per day.
- **`STOP_OFF`.** The quantity comes from the load, not from the request: `billable_units` is the load's `stop_count` less 1, floored at zero, because the final delivery stop is part of the linehaul. Any units the request's own interval might imply are ignored. `amount_cents` is `billable_units` multiplied by 5,000 cents per extra stop.
- **`TARPING`.** Tarping is a flat event: `billable_units` is 1 and `amount_cents` is 7,500 cents.
- **`BREAKDOWN`.** The first 24 hours of a breakdown are unpaid. `billable_units` is the number of whole 1,440-minute days in the total minutes, truncated toward zero, less 1, floored at zero. `amount_cents` is `billable_units` multiplied by 10,000 cents per day.

All accessorial arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic in cents. A claim whose computed `amount_cents` is exactly zero is not an approvable accessorial — a detention interval that lands precisely on the free allowance, or a layover confined to a single calendar day, earns nothing — and the approval is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_VALUE_ACCESSORIAL` (HTTP 422).

Rejection is the other outcome. A `PayrollAdmin` or `SettlementClerk` may reject a `SUBMITTED` claim with a `reject_reason_code` of `NOT_SUPPORTED_BY_LOG`, `SHIPPER_NOT_LIABLE`, `DUPLICATE_EVENT` or `OUTSIDE_POLICY`; the claim advances `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED`, its `amount_cents` is forced to 0, and its `version` is incremented. An `APPROVED` claim waits to be swept onto a settlement, at which point it becomes `PAID`; if that settlement is later voided, the claim returns to `APPROVED` with its `settlement_id` cleared and its computed `amount_cents` preserved, ready for the next period.

### 3.6. Advances, Fuel Cards and the Recovery Waterfall

Between settlements the office fronts money: cash advances for tolls and repairs, and fuel-card balances. Both are recorded as **advances** by a `PayrollAdmin` or `SettlementClerk` against an `ACTIVE` driver, naming the `advance_type` — `CASH_ADVANCE` or `FUEL_CARD` — the `amount_cents` of original principal, which must be strictly greater than zero, and optionally an `installment_cents` ceiling stating the most this obligation may recover from any single settlement. When `installment_cents` is omitted the whole principal is recoverable from one settlement, and when supplied it must be strictly greater than zero. A new advance is `ISSUED` with `recovered_cents` 0, `outstanding_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, and `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock. Exposure per driver is capped: the requested `amount_cents` plus the driver's current `advance_outstanding_cents` may not exceed 150,000 cents, and a request that would breach that ceiling is rejected with `ERR_ADVANCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

An advance that was never recovered against may be cancelled by a `PayrollAdmin` while its `status` is `ISSUED`; cancellation records the advance as `CANCELLED`, zeroes its `outstanding_cents` and thereby reduces the driver's `advance_outstanding_cents`, and removes it from all future recovery. Once recovery has begun or completed the advance is no longer cancellable and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (HTTP 409).

Recovery itself happens inside settlement preparation, and it is a **waterfall against a capped pool**. The cap looks circular — the recovery ceiling is a share of net pay, while net pay depends on the recovery — so the evaluation order is pinned. First, a provisional net is computed as the settlement's `gross_pay_cents` less its `escrow_deduction_cents`, with recovery deliberately excluded. Second, the recovery pool is that provisional net multiplied by 2,500 basis points — one quarter — divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents. Third, the waterfall is allocated against that pool. Fourth, and only then, `net_pay_cents` is the provisional net less the total recovered. Reading the cap off the final net instead of the provisional net is wrong and yields a smaller recovery.

The waterfall takes the driver's advances whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `RECOVERING`, excluding those already `RECOVERED`, in a fixed order: first every `CASH_ADVANCE`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, then every `FUEL_CARD`, ordered by `created_at` ascending. Cash comes before fuel because a cash advance is a direct loan while a fuel-card balance is collateralized by the card program, and `created_at` ordering needs no further tie-break because it is unique per row at three-fractional-digit precision. For each advance in turn, the amount taken is the least of three quantities: the advance's `installment_cents`, its `outstanding_cents`, and whatever remains in the pool. When that amount is greater than zero an advance-recovery line for exactly that amount is emitted against the advance and the pool is reduced by it; when it is zero the advance is skipped. The settlement's `advance_recovery_cents` is the sum of the emitted line amounts. Whatever is not recovered stays in the advance's `outstanding_cents` and carries into the next settlement — the office never overdraws a driver to chase a balance.

The advance's own status axis moves as recovery commits. `recovered_cents` and `outstanding_cents` follow the recovery lines as soon as they exist on a settlement that is `PREPARED`, `APPROVED` or `PAID`, while the `status` axis advances when the settlement is approved: an advance that was `ISSUED` and is only partly recovered — the allocated amount is greater than zero and less than its `outstanding_cents` — moves `ISSUED` → `RECOVERING`; an advance that was `ISSUED` and is recovered in full in one hit — the allocated amount equals its `outstanding_cents` — goes straight through `ISSUED` → `RECOVERED`; and a `RECOVERING` advance whose `outstanding_cents` reaches zero moves `RECOVERING` → `RECOVERED`. `RECOVERED` is terminal in the forward direction only: if the settlement carrying a recovery line is voided, `recovered_cents` is recomputed with that settlement's lines excluded, restoring `outstanding_cents`, and the advance walks back `RECOVERED` → `RECOVERING` when `outstanding_cents` is above zero and `recovered_cents` is still above zero, or all the way to `ISSUED` when `recovered_cents` returns to zero.

### 3.7. Building the Settlement Statement

A settlement is opened by a `PayrollAdmin` or `SettlementClerk` against an `ACTIVE` driver with a `driver_id` and a half-open pay period: `period_start` is the inclusive lower bound and `period_end` the exclusive upper bound, and `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start` or the request is rejected with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. Pay periods may not double-count work, so the requested window may not overlap the `[period_start, period_end)` window of any other non-voided settlement of the same driver; an overlap is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). At creation the settlement's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `line_count` is 0, every money total is 0, `prepared_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `prepared_at`, `approved_at`, `paid_at`, `voided_at`, `payment_reference` and `void_reason_code` are all null, and `itemization_required` is computed once: it is true when the settled driver's `driver_type` is `LEASED_OPERATOR` and false otherwise. That single boolean governs both the completeness rule below and the shape of the statement the driver receives, because a leased operator is contractually entitled to see every charge broken out.

Before preparing, the office may sweep the driver's outstanding retroactive corrections onto the `DRAFT` settlement. The sweep requires `expected_settlement_version` (§2.8), takes every correction of that driver whose `status` is `PENDING`, and appends one `RETRO_CORRECTION` settlement line per correction carrying that correction's signed `delta_gross_cents`. Each swept correction advances `PENDING` → `APPLIED`, with `applied_at` stamped from the logical clock and `applied_settlement_id` set to this settlement; the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents` and `settlement_hold` are recomputed as a direct consequence, which is exactly how a held driver becomes settleable again. The settlement's `retro_correction_total_cents` is updated and its `version` is bumped. A sweep against a driver holding no `PENDING` correction, or one whose corrections do not belong to the settlement's driver, is rejected with `ERR_NO_PENDING_CORRECTIONS` (HTTP 409), and a sweep against a settlement that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Preparation is the heavy operation of the whole domain. It may only run on a `DRAFT` settlement, only for a driver whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, only while that driver's `settlement_hold` is false — a held driver is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) — and only for a driver who has an assigned `pay_package_id`, with `ERR_DRIVER_HAS_NO_PAY_PACKAGE` returned otherwise.

**Period accrual.** The sweep first selects the work belonging to the period. An eligible load is one whose `driver_id` is the settlement's driver, whose `movement_status` is `DELIVERED`, whose `pay_status` is `ACCRUED` — loads that are `UNPAID`, `SETTLED` or `ADJUSTED` are all excluded — whose `settlement_id` is null, and whose `delivered_at` is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. The window is half-open in both senses that matter: a load delivered exactly at `period_start` belongs to this period, and a load delivered exactly at `period_end` belongs to the next one. Eligible loads are taken in `delivered_at` ascending order, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. An eligible claim is one whose `driver_id` is the settlement's driver, whose `status` is `APPROVED` — `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` and `PAID` claims are excluded — whose `settlement_id` is null, and whose `end_at` falls in the same half-open window; eligible claims are taken in `end_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. If there is no eligible load, no eligible claim, and no `RETRO_CORRECTION` line already swept onto the settlement, there is nothing to settle and the prepare is rejected with `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_ACTIVITY` (HTTP 422). A load that is already attached to another settlement is refused with `ERR_LOAD_ALREADY_SETTLED`.

**Line generation, in pinned order.** Settlement lines are then written. Each line records its owning `settlement_id`, its `line_type` — one of `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY`, `ACTIVITY`, `ACCESSORIAL`, `RETRO_CORRECTION`, `PER_DIEM`, `ESCROW_DEDUCTION` or `ADVANCE_RECOVERY` — a `reference_type` of `LOAD`, `ACCESSORIAL_CLAIM`, `ADVANCE`, `RETRO_CORRECTION` or `DRIVER` together with the `reference_id` it resolves to (a `LOAD` reference resolves to a load's `id`, an `ACCESSORIAL_CLAIM` reference to a claim's `id`, an `ADVANCE` reference to an advance's `id`, a `RETRO_CORRECTION` reference to a correction's `id`, and a `DRIVER` reference to a driver's `id`; the pair is deliberately not a typed foreign key), a `quantity` giving the billable amount behind the line, a `rate_value` in the units of the driving pay component — null on flat and derived lines — a signed `amount_cents`, a boolean `taxable`, and a `sequence` fixing print order. Sequence numbers are assigned ascending from 1 and continue from the highest sequence already present on the settlement, so `RETRO_CORRECTION` lines swept earlier keep the numbers they were given and are never renumbered or deleted by preparation. The generation order is:

1. For each eligible load, in accrual order: one linehaul line typed with the load's own `linehaul_line_type`, whose `quantity` is the load's `paid_miles` when that type is `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE` or exactly 1 when it is `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, whose `rate_value` is the rate of the driving component, whose `amount_cents` is the load's `linehaul_pay_cents`, whose `reference_type` is `LOAD` and `reference_id` the load's `id`, and whose `taxable` is copied from that component's `taxable`. Then, when the load's hourly pay is greater than zero, one `HOURLY` line whose `quantity` is the load's `hours_worked_minutes` divided by 60 and rounded to two decimal places. Then, when the snapshotted package holds an `ACTIVITY` component, one `ACTIVITY` line with `quantity` 1. When the percentage leg won, `paid_miles` is still recorded on the load but earns nothing of its own.
2. For each eligible claim, in accrual order: one `ACCESSORIAL` line whose `quantity` is the claim's `billable_units`, whose `amount_cents` is the claim's `amount_cents`, whose `reference_type` is `ACCESSORIAL_CLAIM`, and whose `taxable` is true.
3. `gross_pay_cents` is then the sum of the `amount_cents` of every line typed `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY`, `ACTIVITY`, `ACCESSORIAL` or `RETRO_CORRECTION`. Equivalently, and as the statement header reports it, `linehaul_total_cents` is the sum over the `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY` and `ACTIVITY` lines, `accessorial_total_cents` the sum over the `ACCESSORIAL` lines, `retro_correction_total_cents` the signed sum over the `RETRO_CORRECTION` lines — which may be negative — and `gross_pay_cents` the sum of those three. `PER_DIEM`, `ESCROW_DEDUCTION` and `ADVANCE_RECOVERY` lines are excluded from gross.
4. Per diem is allocated next, producing at most one `PER_DIEM` line with `reference_type` `DRIVER`, `taxable` false and `amount_cents` equal to `per_diem_non_taxable_cents` — emitted only when that amount is greater than zero. The `PER_DIEM` line is excluded both from gross and from the deduction total, because per diem is a reclassification rather than a payment.
5. The escrow accrual is computed next, producing at most one `ESCROW_DEDUCTION` line with `reference_type` `DRIVER` and `taxable` false.
6. The advance-recovery waterfall of §3.6 runs last, producing zero or more `ADVANCE_RECOVERY` lines with `reference_type` `ADVANCE`.
7. `deduction_total_cents` is `escrow_deduction_cents` plus `advance_recovery_cents`, and `net_pay_cents` is `gross_pay_cents` less `deduction_total_cents`.

**Per diem allocation.** If the driver's `per_diem_elected` is false, or the pay package in force at preparation time has `per_diem_method` `NONE`, then `per_diem_non_taxable_cents` and `per_diem_excess_taxable_cents` are both 0. Otherwise the allocation depends on the method. `days_out_count` is, in both methods, the number of distinct UTC calendar dates in the union — across every load swept into this settlement — of the dates covered by that load's `dispatched_at` through `delivered_at`, counting both endpoint dates; a driver who was out on the same date under two loads is out one day, not two. Under `DAY_BASED`, the gross per diem is `days_out_count` multiplied by 6,900 cents per day, so the allocation scales with days on the road and is completely independent of how much the loads paid — a high-revenue week reclassifies proportionally less. Under `PERCENT_OF_LINEHAUL`, `days_out_count` is still recorded on the statement but is not used in the amount; the gross per diem is instead `linehaul_total_cents` multiplied by 2,800 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up, so the identical set of loads produces a different `per_diem_non_taxable_cents` — and therefore a different `taxable_wages_cents` — than the day-based method would for the same gross.

A statutory ceiling then applies. The cap is `gross_pay_cents` multiplied by 4,000 basis points — 40 percent — divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents, and the cap is rounded *before* the comparison. `per_diem_non_taxable_cents` is the lesser of the gross per diem and that cap, and `per_diem_excess_taxable_cents` is whatever the gross per diem exceeded the cap by, which stays in taxable wages. Finally, `taxable_wages_cents` is `gross_pay_cents` less `per_diem_non_taxable_cents`. Per diem never creates money: gross pay always equals taxable wages plus non-taxable per diem, and that identity is expected to hold on every statement the system produces.

**Escrow accrual.** If the driver's `escrow_status` is anything other than `COLLECTING`, `escrow_deduction_cents` is 0 and no escrow line is written — which is what makes a `FUNDED` driver stop accruing. When the driver is `COLLECTING`, take each earning line typed `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY` or `ACTIVITY`, in ascending `sequence` order, and compute 800 basis points — 8 percent — of that line's `amount_cents`, rounding each result half up to whole cents; the raw accrual is the sum of those per-line rounded figures. **The rounding order is pinned: round each line, then sum. Summing first and rounding once is explicitly wrong and produces a different cent total.** Accessorial and retroactive-correction lines never accrue escrow. `escrow_deduction_cents` is then the least of three quantities: that raw accrual, the driver's `escrow_monthly_deduction_cents`, and the headroom remaining to the target, which is the driver's `escrow_target_cents` less the driver's current `escrow_balance_cents`. If that least value comes out below zero — which happens when the balance already exceeds the target — it is clamped to 0.

**The itemization gate and the net-pay floor.** When the settlement's `itemization_required` is true, every earning line — that is, every line typed `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY`, `ACTIVITY`, `ACCESSORIAL` or `RETRO_CORRECTION` — must carry a non-null `reference_id` **and** a non-null `quantity`, and every `ACCESSORIAL` line's source claim must carry a non-null `document_ref`, so that every accessorial charge on a leased-operator statement is evidenced. Any gap is rejected with `ERR_ITEMIZATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Then, and last, the money floor: if `net_pay_cents` would be below zero — the usual cause being a retroactive down-grade larger than the period's earnings — the whole preparation is rejected with `ERR_NEGATIVE_NET_PAY` (HTTP 422) and **no lines are kept**; the settlement stays `DRAFT` and the pay office must resolve the shortfall rather than hand the driver a negative check.

A successful preparation advances the settlement `DRAFT` → `PREPARED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `prepared_by_user_id`, stamps `prepared_at` from the logical clock, writes `line_count`, `itemization_required` and every total from `linehaul_total_cents` through `net_pay_cents`, and increments the settlement's `version` by 1. It also closes the swept work against this statement: every swept load moves on the pay axis `ACCRUED` → `SETTLED` with its `settlement_id` set to this settlement, and every swept accessorial claim moves `APPROVED` → `PAID` with its `settlement_id` set and its `version` incremented by 1. Those two marks are what stop the same work being paid twice, which is precisely what `ERR_LOAD_ALREADY_SETTLED` and `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_SETTLED` test for — each of them accepting either the advanced status or a non-null `settlement_id` as proof that the record is already attached.

### 3.8. Approval, Payment and the Compensating Void

Approval is where the statement becomes a liability of the carrier, and it is the trigger for the domain's deepest cascade. A `SettlementApprover` or a `PayrollAdmin` may approve a settlement whose `status` is `PREPARED`, provided the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the settlement's `prepared_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409) — and provided the driver's `settlement_hold` is still false. Approval advances the settlement `PREPARED` → `APPROVED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, stamps `approved_at` and increments the settlement's `version` by 1. Then the cascade runs, hop by hop:

1. When the settlement carries an `ESCROW_DEDUCTION` line, an escrow ledger entry is appended with `txn_type` `DEPOSIT`, `reason_code` `SETTLEMENT_ACCRUAL`, `settlement_id` set to this settlement, and `amount_cents` equal to the positive line amount.
2. That append itself recomputes the driver's `escrow_balance_cents` and every balance-derived field on the driver, including the `balance_after_cents` recorded on the new entry.
3. The new `escrow_balance_cents` itself drives the escrow axis `COLLECTING` → `FUNDED` when it reaches `escrow_target_cents`, stamping `escrow_funded_at` and suppressing every future escrow-deduction line for this driver.
4. The driver's `ytd_gross_pay_cents`, `ytd_taxable_wages_cents` and `ytd_per_diem_non_taxable_cents` are recomputed, because this settlement now counts toward them.

In the same operation, every advance the preparation allocated against moves along its own axis as described in §3.6, and the retroactive corrections swept onto this settlement stand `APPLIED` against it with their `applied_at` and `applied_settlement_id` recorded. The driver's `version` is incremented alongside the settlement's.

Payment is the last forward step and belongs to a `PayrollAdmin`. It requires `status` `APPROVED`, an acting `X-User-ID` differing from `approved_by_user_id` — the payer may not be the approver — and a `payment_reference` naming the external disbursement. Payment sets `status` to `PAID`, stamps `paid_at`, records the `payment_reference`, and increments `version` by 1. It moves nothing else: the loads are already `SETTLED` and the claims already `PAID` from preparation, the escrow deposit and the year-to-date accumulators were already posted at approval, and the advances already stand `RECOVERING` or `RECOVERED`. `PAID` is terminal, and `paid_at` becomes the reference instant from which the pay-dispute window is measured.

Voiding is the only way to undo a settlement, and it compensates rather than deletes. A `PayrollAdmin` may void a settlement whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PREPARED` or `APPROVED`, supplying a `void_reason_code` of `CLERICAL_ERROR`, `WRONG_PERIOD`, `DISPUTE_RESOLUTION` or `DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT`. A settlement that is already `PAID` or `VOIDED` accepts no further transition, `PAID` → `VOIDED` included, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). Every void advances the settlement `DRAFT` → `VOIDED`, `PREPARED` → `VOIDED` or `APPROVED` → `VOIDED` as the case may be, stamps `voided_at`, records the `void_reason_code` and increments `version` by 1; what else it does depends on how far the settlement had travelled, and each tier does everything the tier before it does:

- **From `DRAFT`**, nothing else has happened yet, so the void is just those stamps.
- **From `PREPARED`**, the void additionally deletes every settlement line of the settlement and resets `line_count` and all money fields to 0; returns every swept load on the pay axis `SETTLED` → `ACCRUED` with its `settlement_id` cleared to null, making it eligible for a later settlement again; returns every swept accessorial claim `PAID` → `APPROVED` with its `settlement_id` cleared to null and its `amount_cents` preserved, so the claim is eligible for the next settlement without being re-priced; recomputes every touched advance's `recovered_cents` and `outstanding_cents`, walking its axis back to `RECOVERING` or `ISSUED`; and returns every correction marked `APPLIED` against this settlement to `PENDING` with `applied_at` and `applied_settlement_id` cleared to null, which restores the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents` and may re-raise `settlement_hold`.
- **From `APPROVED`**, the void additionally appends an escrow ledger entry with `txn_type` `REVERSAL`, `reason_code` `SETTLEMENT_VOID`, `settlement_id` set to this settlement and `amount_cents` equal to the negation of the `DEPOSIT` this settlement posted; that append itself recomputes the driver's `escrow_balance_cents`, which itself drives the escrow axis `FUNDED` → `COLLECTING` when the balance falls back below `escrow_target_cents`; and it recomputes `ytd_gross_pay_cents`, `ytd_taxable_wages_cents` and `ytd_per_diem_non_taxable_cents` with this settlement excluded. The driver's `version` is incremented.

Because `PAID` is terminal, there is no fourth tier: money that has actually left the carrier is never unwound by a void. A paid statement is corrected forward instead, through the dispute and retroactive-correction path of §3.9, which is why a load whose pay axis reads `SETTLED` on a paid settlement moves to `ADJUSTED` rather than back to `ACCRUED` when its miles turn out to have been wrong.

### 3.9. Disputes, Mileage Re-grades and Retroactive Corrections

Drivers argue about two things: the miles and the money. A dispute captures either, and its `dispute_kind` — `MILEAGE` or `PAY` — decides which fields it must carry and which side effects its resolution produces. A dispute may be filed by the `Driver` itself, by a `PayrollAdmin` or by a `SettlementClerk`, and always carries the `driver_id` and a `narrative` stating the claim, plus an optional `evidence_doc_ref` identifying the supporting evidence package.

A `MILEAGE` dispute contests the distance paid on one load. It requires the `load_id`, the `claimed_guide` the claimant asserts should apply — `PRACTICAL` or `HHG` — and the `claimed_miles` it asserts, which must be strictly greater than zero; its `settlement_id`, `claimed_amount_cents` and `disputed_line_type` must all be null. The `driver_id` is derived from the load's own driver and must match it, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_MISMATCH`. The referenced load must be `DELIVERED`, since there is nothing to argue about before delivery, and an undelivered load is refused with `ERR_LOAD_NOT_DELIVERED`. The claim must also actually claim something: if the pair of `claimed_guide` and `claimed_miles` equals the load's current `applied_mileage_guide` and `base_guide_miles`, there is no dispute to adjudicate and the filing is rejected with `ERR_MILEAGE_CLAIM_UNCHANGED` (HTTP 422).

A `PAY` dispute contests an amount on a statement. It requires the `settlement_id` of the origin settlement, the `claimed_amount_cents` the claimant asserts is owed — strictly greater than zero — and the `disputed_line_type` naming the family of line under dispute, one of `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE`, `LINEHAUL_PERCENT`, `HOURLY`, `ACTIVITY`, `ACCESSORIAL`, `ESCROW_DEDUCTION` or `ADVANCE_RECOVERY`; its `load_id`, `claimed_guide` and `claimed_miles` must all be null. Its `driver_id` is derived from the settlement's driver and must match it.

Two filters guard the filing itself. A dispute must be filed within 30 days of its reference instant, which is the load's `delivered_at` for a mileage dispute on a load that has not been settled and the origin settlement's `paid_at` otherwise; a late filing is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). And no second dispute of the same `dispute_kind` may be filed against the same load or the same origin settlement while an existing one is still `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; the duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409). A new dispute is `OPEN` with `reopen_count` 0, the filer's `X-User-ID` recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`, and `resolution_note`, `resolution_correction_id`, `adjustment_cents`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` all null.

The dispute lifecycle is the one **cyclic** state machine in the domain. A `PayrollAdmin` or `SettlementClerk` takes an `OPEN` dispute under review, moving it `OPEN` → `UNDER_REVIEW` and recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`, with the dispute's `version` incremented. A `SettlementApprover` or `PayrollAdmin` then resolves it, supplying an `outcome` of either `UPHOLD` or `DENY`, an optional `resolution_note` recorded on the dispute, and an optional `adjustment_cents`; the recorded `adjustment_cents` is always the signed delta the server itself derives, never a client-supplied figure. The resolver's `X-User-ID` must differ from `reviewed_by_user_id`, and for a `PAY` dispute it must additionally differ from the `prepared_by_user_id` of the settlement under dispute; either collision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Any of these moves attempted from the wrong status is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — review needs `OPEN`, resolve needs `UNDER_REVIEW`, reopen needs `DENIED`, and withdrawal needs `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`.

A denial advances the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW` → `DENIED`, records the `resolution_note` and the `resolved_by_user_id`, leaves `adjustment_cents` null, creates no correction and touches no load or settlement money field, and increments the dispute's `version`. `DENIED` is not the end: the claimant may reopen it, which moves `DENIED` → `OPEN`, increments `reopen_count` by 1, overwrites `narrative` with the reopen narrative and `evidence_doc_ref` with the reopen evidence reference when one is supplied, and clears `resolution_note`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` back to null, bumping `version`. Reopening is allowed only while `reopen_count` is strictly below 2, so the same dispute row may traverse `OPEN` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `DENIED` → `OPEN` twice before a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_REOPEN_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Withdrawal, available to the `Driver` or a `PayrollAdmin` from either `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, advances the dispute `OPEN` → `WITHDRAWN` or `UNDER_REVIEW` → `WITHDRAWN` and increments `version`; any `reviewed_by_user_id` already recorded is retained as history, no correction is ever created by a withdrawal, and no load's pay axis is touched. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal — a withdrawn dispute can never be reviewed, resolved or reopened, which is exactly what distinguishes it from `DENIED`.

Upholding is where the fork lives. The same `UPHOLD` outcome produces three disjoint side-effect sets, selected by the `dispute_kind` and, for mileage disputes, by the referenced load's `pay_status`:

- **Branch A — a mileage dispute on a load whose `pay_status` is `ACCRUED`.** The load has not been paid yet, so it is simply re-graded in place: `applied_mileage_guide`, `base_guide_miles`, `paid_miles`, `short_haul_floor_applied`, `linehaul_pay_cents` and `linehaul_line_type` are all recomputed from the upheld claim, with both the guide and the miles taken from the dispute's `claimed_guide` and `claimed_miles`, overriding the snapshotted package's guide entirely, while the short-haul floor and the two-leg comparison of §3.4 run again over the same snapshotted components. The pay axis takes the self-transition `ACCRUED` → `ACCRUED`, so the observable `pay_status` is unchanged, and **no correction is created**, because the next preparation will simply pick up the new numbers. The dispute advances `UNDER_REVIEW` → `UPHELD`, `adjustment_cents` is the new `linehaul_pay_cents` less the old one, `resolution_correction_id` stays null, and the `version` of both the dispute and the load is incremented.
- **Branch B — a mileage dispute on a load whose `pay_status` is `SETTLED`.** The money has already gone out, so the re-grade cannot be absorbed in place, and a four-hop cascade runs. Hop 1: the load's pay fields are rewritten exactly as in branch A and its pay axis moves `SETTLED` → `ADJUSTED`, while `movement_status` stays `DELIVERED` and is not touched. Hop 2: that load mutation itself creates a retroactive correction in `PENDING` status with `correction_type` `MILEAGE_REGRADE`, `reason_code` `MILEAGE_DISPUTE_UPHELD`, `origin_settlement_id` taken from the load's `settlement_id`, `source_load_id` the load, `source_dispute_id` the dispute, and `delta_gross_cents` and `delta_taxable_cents` computed as described below. Hop 3: that insert itself recomputes the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents`. Hop 4: the new `pending_retro_correction_cents` itself recomputes the driver's `settlement_hold`, which is true whenever the absolute value of `pending_retro_correction_cents` exceeds 50,000 cents and false otherwise — and once it is true, no new settlement for that driver can be prepared. The dispute advances `UNDER_REVIEW` → `UPHELD` with `adjustment_cents` set and `resolution_correction_id` pointing at the new correction; the dispute's, the load's and the driver's `version` are each incremented. An `ADJUSTED` load is never re-swept onto a settlement; the money moves through the correction instead.
- **Branch C — a pay dispute.** The referenced settlement's `status` must be `APPROVED` or `PAID`. No load field is touched at all. A retroactive correction is created in `PENDING` status with `correction_type` `PAY_ADJUSTMENT`, `reason_code` `PAY_DISPUTE_UPHELD`, `origin_settlement_id` taken from the dispute's `settlement_id`, `source_load_id` null, `source_dispute_id` the dispute, `delta_gross_cents` equal to the dispute's `claimed_amount_cents` — always positive — and `delta_taxable_cents` as below. That insert itself recomputes the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents`, which itself recomputes `settlement_hold`. The dispute advances `UNDER_REVIEW` → `UPHELD` with `adjustment_cents` equal to the `claimed_amount_cents` and `resolution_correction_id` set, and the dispute's and driver's `version` are incremented.

**Computing the correction deltas.** For a mileage re-grade, the old figure is the `amount_cents` of the single `LINEHAUL_PER_MILE` or `LINEHAUL_PERCENT` settlement line on the origin settlement whose `reference_id` is that load, and the new figure is the linehaul computation re-run with the upheld guide and miles, still using the components of the load's `pay_package_id_snapshot`; `delta_gross_cents` is the new figure less the old one, and it is negative on a down-grade. For a pay adjustment, `delta_gross_cents` is simply the dispute's `claimed_amount_cents`. The taxable delta depends on the per diem method of the **origin** package, not the current one: when that method is `PERCENT_OF_LINEHAUL`, `delta_taxable_cents` is `delta_gross_cents` less 2,800 basis points of `delta_gross_cents` (that is, less 28 percent of it, rounded half up on the magnitude with the sign re-applied); for every other method `delta_taxable_cents` equals `delta_gross_cents`.

A retroactive correction is therefore a small, self-contained record of money owed in one direction or the other: it names the `driver_id` whose pay is corrected, the `origin_settlement_id` whose amounts were found wrong, the `applied_settlement_id` of the later settlement that absorbed it (null while `PENDING` or `CANCELLED`), the `source_load_id` re-graded by a `MILEAGE_REGRADE` (null for a `PAY_ADJUSTMENT`), the `source_dispute_id` that produced it, its `correction_type`, its `reason_code` — `MILEAGE_GUIDE_REGRADE`, `MILEAGE_DISPUTE_UPHELD` or `PAY_DISPUTE_UPHELD` — its signed `delta_gross_cents` and `delta_taxable_cents`, and its `status`, which is `PENDING`, `APPLIED` or `CANCELLED`. `applied_at` is stamped when it is swept onto a settlement, and `cancelled_at` and `cancelled_by_user_id` when it is abandoned. A correction that is swept onto a `DRAFT` settlement of the same driver becomes `APPLIED` as described in §3.7; if that settlement is later voided, it moves back `APPLIED` → `PENDING` with `applied_at` and `applied_settlement_id` cleared, and the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents` and `settlement_hold` are recomputed again.

A correction the carrier will never pay may be cancelled by a `PayrollAdmin` while its `status` is `PENDING` and its `applied_settlement_id` is null; `APPLIED` and `CANCELLED` corrections are immutable and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). Cancellation advances the correction `PENDING` → `CANCELLED`, stamps `cancelled_at` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `cancelled_by_user_id`, recomputes the driver's `pending_retro_correction_cents` and therefore `settlement_hold` — this is the escape hatch that releases a held driver — and increments the correction's and the driver's `version`. If the cancelled correction had re-graded a load, that load's pay axis returns `ADJUSTED` → `SETTLED`, but its `applied_mileage_guide`, `base_guide_miles`, `paid_miles`, `linehaul_pay_cents` and `linehaul_line_type` stay at their re-graded values: cancelling the correction abandons the money movement, it does not un-adjudicate the upheld dispute. This is precisely why a settlement's `linehaul_total_cents` on the original statement can permanently disagree with the `linehaul_pay_cents` now stored on the load — and why the settlement-line log, not the load, is the statement of record.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/driver-pay` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint. Endpoints that bring a brand-new record into existence answer with HTTP 201 and that record in `data`; every other successful call answers with HTTP 200 and, unless it is a list endpoint, the affected record in `data`. The `Authorized Role(s)` column is exhaustive: an authenticated caller whose `X-User-Role` is absent from a row receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, and a `Driver` caller reaching a row it is permitted to call but aiming at another driver's record receives `ERR_DRIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
