# Colocation Provisioning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A colocation provider sells three scarce things at once: floor space, electrical power, and physical connectivity. Each of them is finite, each of them is contracted in advance, and each of them is consumed by a different operational team working on a different clock. Sales commits a customer to a cabinet count, a kilowatt figure, a bandwidth number and a bundle of remote-hands minutes. The data-hall owner has to fit those commitments into rows that have busway limits, cooling limits and per-cabinet density design points. Floor engineers energize breakers, patch fibre between panels and carry out hands-and-eyes work on the customer's behalf. Security controls who walks the aisles. Revenue operations turns all of it into invoice lines. Today those five perspectives live in five spreadsheets, and the disagreements between them surface as stranded capacity, oversubscribed breakers, cross-connects billed after they were physically reclaimed, and visitors escorted by nobody.

This document specifies the Colocation Provisioning API, a single system of record mounted under `/colocation` that owns the whole chain: customer and contract commitments, floor-row capacity and posture, cabinet space allocation, power-circuit design and energization, cross-connect ordering with patch-panel port stewardship, remote-hands ticketing with segregated authorization, site access with biometric enrollment and escort capacity, and finally decommissioning with equipment removal, cable reclamation and port release. Every quantity that a human would otherwise recompute by hand — allocated kilowatts, stranded capacity under two competing definitions, commit utilization, billable labour increments, invoice totals — is derived by the server and exposed as a read-only field.

Two design constraints run through the entire specification and should be read as global. First, capacity is allocated pessimistically and released symmetrically: any operation that consumes row density, cooling headroom, breaker headroom, a patch-panel port or an escort slot must be reversible by an operation that gives the exact same quantity back. Second, because several teams write to the same row and the same contract, the two aggregates that arbitrate scarcity — the floor row and the contract — are guarded by optimistic concurrency, so a writer holding a superseded view of available headroom is rejected rather than allowed to overwrite arithmetic it never observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, §2 describes how every request is framed, ordered, validated and rounded.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID` names the acting individual and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which they act. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is missing, empty, or does not name one of the five declared roles is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before the body is parsed, before any path identifier is resolved, and before any concurrency token is compared. A recognised role that is not permitted to invoke the requested operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; this check also precedes existence resolution, so an unauthorized caller can never use 404-versus-403 to probe which identifiers exist.

The five roles are `CustomerAdmin`, `ProvisioningEngineer`, `FacilityManager`, `SecurityOfficer`, and `BillingOperator`.

- A `CustomerAdmin` is the customer-side administrator: it raises cross-connect orders, remote-hands tickets, access visits and decommission orders, and manages its own authorization list.
- A `ProvisioningEngineer` is the floor engineer: it designs and energizes power circuits, proposes cross-connect routes, dispatches and completes remote-hands work, records decommission steps and performs port release.
- A `FacilityManager` is the data-hall owner: it creates and re-limits floor rows, approves cross-connect routes and decommissions, and sends proposed routes back for re-routing.
- A `SecurityOfficer` owns biometric enrollment, escorting, and visit check-in and check-out, and may also authorize remote-hands tickets.
- A `BillingOperator` owns the contract's fiscal state: period closes, credit holds and invoice-line reversals.

Identity binding differs by role and matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's owning-customer chain to `X-User-ID`, so a `CustomerAdmin` reading or writing another customer's contract, cabinet, circuit, cross-connect order, ticket, visit or invoice line is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. That ownership check deliberately runs after existence resolution, so it can compare the loaded row's customer: a `CustomerAdmin` touching a contract that exists but belongs to somebody else receives 403, not 404.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProvisioningEngineer`, `FacilityManager`, `SecurityOfficer` or `BillingOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque staff identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a cross-connect order, as `escort_officer_user_id` on a visit, and as `equipment_removed_by_user_id` and `cables_reclaimed_by_user_id` on a decommission order — and compares it to previously recorded values for segregation-of-duties purposes, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0 and negative values are not accepted. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is an invalid request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every row matching the supplied filters and not merely the rows on the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix, and every resource carries a `created_at` timestamp written in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Customers, contracts, floor rows, cabinets, power circuits, patch-panel ports, cross-connect orders, authorized contacts, access visits, remote-hands tickets and decommission orders additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on each mutation of that row. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation of the row that owns it: when energizing a circuit changes a row's stranded-capacity figures, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though no client wrote to the row directly.

Invoice lines are the single exception. An `InvoiceLine` is an immutable ledger entry and carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. A billing mistake is never edited — it is compensated by a further line, as described in §3.9.

Because deadlines, quarantine cool-downs, visit windows and metering windows all depend on "now", the system reads time from a logical clock rather than from wall time. An operator-facing capability sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and every subsequent timestamp, deadline comparison, quarantine expiry and window test uses the new value. Until it is set for the first time, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Setting the clock mutates no entity; it only changes what later writes observe.

### 2.5. Rejection Precedence

Many operations are guarded by several rules at once, and a request may violate more than one. The order below is normative: rules are evaluated in ascending order and the **first** violated rule determines both the returned `error_id` and the HTTP status. A lower position always wins the conflict, which is why, for example, a contract that is simultaneously `SUSPENDED` and on credit hold reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` and never `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE`.

1. `X-User-Role` is present and names a declared role, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is among the roles authorized for the endpoint, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every identifier in the URL path resolves to a stored row, otherwise the per-entity 404 code for the first unresolved identifier: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CABINET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CROSS_CONNECT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECOMMISSION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVOICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
4. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, otherwise that referenced entity's own 404 code. Body references are resolved left to right in the order the fields are declared for the target entity, and the first unresolvable reference decides the error. Applies to the creation endpoints for contracts, cabinets, power circuits, patch-panel ports, cross-connect orders, remote-hands tickets, access visits, decommission orders and contacts.
5. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerAdmin`, `X-User-ID` equals a `Customer` `id` and every referenced contract, cabinet, circuit, cross-connect order, ticket, visit or invoice line belongs to that customer, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint where `CustomerAdmin` is authorized.
6. The contact referenced by a remote-hands ticket or an access visit is on the owning customer's authorization list and carries the scope the operation needs, otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to remote-hands ticket creation, remote-hands authorization, and access-visit creation.
7. The `X-User-ID` authorizing a remote-hands ticket differs from the `X-User-ID` that created it, otherwise `ERR_SELF_AUTHORIZATION_FORBIDDEN` (409). Applies to the remote-hands authorization endpoint.
8. The `X-User-ID` approving a cross-connect order or a decommission order differs from the `X-User-ID` that created it, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to the cross-connect order state endpoint and the decommission order state endpoint, and only to transitions whose target state is `APPROVED`; later target states may be driven by the creator.
9. `expected_contract_version` equals the parent contract's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /colocation/cross-connect-orders`. It is evaluated before body validation, so a stale writer is told to re-read rather than told its payload is wrong.
10. `expected_row_version` equals the parent floor row's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_ROW_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /colocation/power-circuits/{circuit_id}/energize`.
11. All required body fields are present, every enum value is inside its declared set, every number is inside its declared range and every datetime is ISO 8601 UTC, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This covers unknown values for `phase`, `media_type`, `severity`, `footprint_type`, `billing_mode`, `purpose` and `to_state`, non-positive `breaker_amps`, `volts` or `rated_power_kw`, negative commitments, a `limit` outside 1 through 100 and a negative `offset`. Applies to every write endpoint.
12. `period_end` is strictly after `period_start`, a metered interval's `interval_end` is after its `interval_start`, and every work segment's `ended_at` is greater than or equal to its `started_at`, otherwise `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID` (422). Zero-length work segments are legal and bill nothing; inverted intervals are not legal. Applies to billing close, remote-hands completion and circuit readings.
13. The requested `to_state` is reachable from the entity's current state per §3, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This includes terminal-state lockdown — `TERMINATED`, `RELEASED`, `REMOVED`, `DECOMMISSIONED`, `ABORTED`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` and `CHECKED_OUT` accept no further transitions — and the rejection of a replayed trigger. Applies to every state endpoint and to circuit energization, route proposal, change requests, ticket dispatch, ticket completion, decommission step recording and port release.
14. The targeted invoice line has not already been reversed and is not itself a reversal, otherwise `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_REVERSED` (409). Applies to invoice adjustments.
15. The submitted `step_code` has not already been recorded on this decommission order, otherwise `ERR_DECOMMISSION_STEP_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409). Applies to decommission step recording.
16. The ticket's `authorization_state` is `AUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (409). Applies to ticket dispatch.
17. The order's `revision_count` is strictly below two, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to cross-connect change requests.
18. The contract's `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to cross-connect order creation, cross-connect order state transitions, remote-hands ticket creation, access-visit creation, billing close and cabinet state transitions. On cabinet state transitions the accepted set widens as described in §3.3: moving a cabinet from `AVAILABLE` to `RESERVED` accepts a contract in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE`, while moving it from `RESERVED` to `INSTALLED` requires `ACTIVE`.
19. The floor row's `row_status` is `OPEN` or `COOLING_CONSTRAINED` — never `DENSITY_CAPPED` and never `DECOMMISSIONING` — otherwise `ERR_ROW_NOT_ACCEPTING_SPACE` (409). Applies to cabinet creation, cabinet state transitions targeting `RESERVED` or `INSTALLED`, and circuit energization. On energization the test is narrower: only `decommission_flag` being true rejects, so a merely `DENSITY_CAPPED` row still permits energization of cabinets already installed in it.
20. The customer-side cabinet's `status` is `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (409). Applies to circuit energization, circuit state transitions, cross-connect order creation, access-visit creation, ticket dispatch and decommission order state transitions. On a cross-connect order only `a_end_cabinet_id` is examined. On a circuit state transition the gate guards the `DESIGNED` to `APPROVED` step only, and its accepted set widens to `RESERVED`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`.
21. Neither endpoint port is `RESERVED` or `ASSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_PORT_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to route proposal. A port in `FREE` and a port in `QUARANTINED` both pass this gate.
22. Neither endpoint port is `QUARANTINED` with a `quarantine_until` strictly after the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_PORT_IN_QUARANTINE` (409). Applies to route proposal. The boundary instant itself passes, and a quarantined port therefore reports the cool-down error rather than the generic unavailability error of rule 21.
23. The visiting contact's `biometric_state` is `ENROLLED`, otherwise `ERR_BIOMETRIC_NOT_ENROLLED` (409). Applies to access-visit state transitions targeting `CHECKED_IN`.
24. The contract holds at least one cabinet in `RESERVED`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_NO_CABINET_ASSIGNED` (409). Applies to contract state transitions targeting `ACTIVE`.
25. The projected row `cooling_load_kw` after the energization is less than or equal to the row's `cooling_capacity_kw`, otherwise `ERR_COOLING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to circuit energization. Equality is permitted. This thermal gate sits deliberately above the density gate, so a request that violates both reports the cooling error.
26. The sum of `derated_kw` over the cabinet's `PRIMARY` circuits already committed, plus this circuit's own contribution, is less than or equal to the cabinet's `rated_power_kw` multiplied by 1.25, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_BREAKER_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (422). Applies to circuit energization and to the `DESIGNED` to `APPROVED` circuit state transition.
27. A cabinet whose contract requires dual feeds carries an `APPROVED` or `ENERGIZED` `REDUNDANT` circuit before its `PRIMARY` circuit energizes, otherwise `ERR_REDUNDANT_FEED_MISSING` (422). Applies to circuit energization, and only when the contract's `redundancy_requirement` is `TWO_N`.
28. A visit whose computed `escort_required` is true supplies `escort_officer_user_id` at check-in, and a visit that does not require an escort supplies none, otherwise `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to access-visit state transitions targeting `CHECKED_IN`.
29. The projected row `allocated_power_kw` after the energization is less than or equal to the row's `density_ceiling_kw` multiplied by 1.10, otherwise `ERR_ROW_DENSITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to circuit energization.
30. The projected row `allocated_power_kw` after the energization is less than or equal to the row's `max_row_power_kw`, otherwise `ERR_ROW_POWER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to circuit energization.
31. Both endpoint ports' `media_type` equals the order's `cable_type`, otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to route proposal.
32. `path_hops` is a non-empty list whose first entry equals the A-side port's `panel_code`, whose last entry equals the Z-side port's `panel_code`, and which repeats no `panel_code`, otherwise `ERR_PATH_ROUTE_INVALID` (422). Applies to route proposal.
33. The A-side port's `row_id` equals the A-end cabinet's `row_id` and the Z-side port's `row_id` equals the Z-end cabinet's `row_id`, otherwise `ERR_PORT_ROW_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to route proposal.
34. The customer-side cabinet's `contract_id` equals the referenced contract, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT` (422). Applies to cross-connect order creation, remote-hands ticket creation, access-visit creation and decommission order creation. The customer-side cabinet is `a_end_cabinet_id` on a cross-connect order, `target_cabinet_id` on a ticket, and `cabinet_id` on a visit or a decommission order; `z_end_cabinet_id` is the far side and is deliberately neither scoped to the contract nor required to be operational.
35. No `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` visit on the same cabinet overlaps the requested window, otherwise `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to access-visit creation.
36. The count of overlapping escort-requiring visits already occupying the row is below the row's `escort_slot_count`, otherwise `ERR_ESCORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to access-visit creation.
37. The logical clock lies inside the visit's window, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_VISIT_WINDOW` (409). Applies to access-visit state transitions targeting `CHECKED_IN`.
38. The contract's `credit_hold` is false, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE` (402). Applies to cross-connect order creation, remote-hands ticket creation, circuit energization and contract state transitions. On contract state transitions it guards only the reinstatement of a `SUSPENDED` contract back to `ACTIVE`.
39. The count of the contract's cabinets in `RESERVED`, `CAPACITY_HELD`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, including the one being added, is less than or equal to `committed_cabinet_count`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_COMMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to cabinet creation and cabinet state transitions.
40. The projected contract `provisioned_power_kw` after the energization is less than or equal to `power_commit_kw` multiplied by 1.10, otherwise `ERR_POWER_COMMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to circuit energization.
41. The count of the contract's cross-connect orders that are neither `ABORTED` nor `DECOMMISSIONED`, including the one being created, is less than or equal to `cross_connect_cap`, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_CONNECT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to cross-connect order creation.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Rows and Contracts

Two write operations arbitrate finite pools whose headroom a client must have read before it can reason about them, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency token that the client must supply.

Raising a cross-connect order — `POST /colocation/cross-connect-orders` — requires `expected_contract_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Contract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Cross-connect creation consumes the contract's `cross_connect_cap` headroom, so a writer holding a superseded contract view cannot know how much headroom remains; the stale-version test converts that ambiguity into a deterministic rejection. A successful create is itself a mutation of the contract and increments the contract's `version` by one, which is what makes a second create carrying the same token fail.

Energizing a power circuit — `POST /colocation/power-circuits/{circuit_id}/energize` — requires `expected_row_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `FloorRow`. A value that does not equal the row's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROW_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Energization consumes row density, busway and cooling headroom, and every accepted energization legitimately supersedes the previous view of the row, so the writer whose headroom arithmetic was computed against a superseded snapshot must re-read the row and retry.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no concurrency token, and supplying one has no meaning.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Derived Quantities

All rounding in the system is half-up, and the number of decimal places is fixed by the kind of quantity. Power and capacity figures — `derated_kw`, `allocated_kw`, `cooling_contribution_kw`, the cabinet and row rollups, `provisioned_power_kw`, `stranded_commit_kw`, `stranded_nameplate_kw`, `stranded_measured_kw`, `released_power_kw` and every projected ceiling used by a gate — carry three decimal places. Energy figures in kilowatt-hours carry three decimal places. Money — `mrc_usd`, `amount_usd`, `labor_charge_usd`, `billed_total_usd`, `cross_connect_billed_usd` — carries two decimal places, and `unit_rate_usd` on an invoice line carries four. The single percentage figure, `commit_utilization_pct`, carries one decimal place.

Where a calculation rounds more than once, the order is pinned and the intermediate rounded value is what later steps consume. Two cases matter enough to restate: a circuit's derated capacity is rounded to three decimals first and every later comparison and clamp uses that rounded value rather than the raw product; and remote-hands labour is rounded per segment and only then summed, never summed and then rounded once.

Derived fields are never accepted from clients. `provisioned_power_kw`, `stranded_commit_kw`, `commit_utilization_pct`, `active_cross_connect_count`, `billed_total_usd` and `cross_connect_billed_usd` on a contract; every capacity and posture field on a row; `allocated_power_kw`, `cooling_load_kw` and `energized_primary_circuit_count` on a cabinet; `derated_kw`, `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` on a circuit; `escort_required` on a visit; `billable_increments`, `billed_minutes`, `included_increments_applied`, `labor_charge_usd`, `response_deadline_at` and `sla_breached` on a ticket; `released_port_count` and `released_power_kw` on a decommission order; and `amount_usd`, `quantity`, `unit_rate_usd`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `source_ref_id`, `included_minutes_consumed` and `reversed` on an invoice line are all server-computed and read-only.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system touch three, four or five entities. The rule is that a cascade runs to completion inside the same request, in a fixed inside-out order — the circuit first, then its cabinet, then the cabinet's row, then any cabinets and circuits the row's new posture affects, and finally the contract's rollups — and every entity the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if a client had written to it. A client that reads a row before an energization elsewhere in the same row and then attempts its own energization will therefore be holding a stale `expected_row_version`, which is the intended serialization point between two engineers working the same busway.

Cascades are also symmetric. Every quantity a cascade consumes is given back by the compensating transition: de-energizing a circuit zeroes exactly the kilowatts the energization allocated, releasing a reservation removes exactly the slot the reservation occupied, aborting a cross-connect order frees exactly the ports it reserved, and a reversal line restores exactly the bundled remote-hands minutes the original line consumed.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three separations are enforced structurally rather than by policy documentation. A remote-hands ticket may not be authorized by the `X-User-ID` that created it. A cross-connect order and a decommission order may not be approved by the `X-User-ID` that created it, although the same operator may drive the later steps. And the two recorded decommission steps must be recorded by two different operators: whoever records cable reclamation may not be the operator recorded against equipment removal, and vice versa. The first separation reports `ERR_SELF_AUTHORIZATION_FORBIDDEN`; the second and third report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Every entity in this API is a peer collection mounted directly under `/colocation`; no entity's identifier appears as a path ancestor of another entity, and relationships are carried by explicit reference fields in request bodies instead. Path identifiers are nonetheless resolved left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the 404 code — `customer_id`, `contract_id`, `row_id`, `cabinet_id`, `circuit_id`, `port_id`, `order_id`, `ticket_id`, `contact_id`, `visit_id` and `invoice_line_id` each map to their own not-found code as listed in §2.5. An `invoice_line_id` that resolves to a line belonging to a different contract than the one in the path is treated as unresolved and reports `ERR_INVOICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.

Body references are resolved the same way, in declaration order, before any business rule runs. A cross-connect order that names a non-existent `parent_order_id` reports `ERR_CROSS_CONNECT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; a ticket naming an unknown `requested_by_contact_id` reports `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`; a visit naming an unknown `cabinet_id` reports `ERR_CABINET_NOT_FOUND`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Customers, Contracts and Commercial Commitments

A colocation engagement begins with a customer record: a `legal_name` for paperwork and a short `account_code` used on floor labels and invoices, which must be unique across all customers — a duplicate code is refused as an invalid request. Newly registered customers open with `account_status` set to `ACTIVE`; the field also admits `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, which an operator action moves it to.

Commitments live on the contract, not on the customer, and a customer may hold several. Creating a contract requires the owning `customer_id`, an external `contract_number` that must be unique across contracts, a `billing_mode`, and the commitments themselves: `committed_cabinet_count` (at least 1 cabinet), `power_commit_kw` (strictly greater than zero, carried to three decimals), `committed_bandwidth_mbps` (zero or more Mbps of contracted uplink), `cross_connect_cap` (at least 1), and the term boundaries `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly after the start. Four further commitments are optional and default when omitted: `committed_cage_sqft`, the caged floor area in square feet, defaults to 0 and may not be negative; `redundancy_requirement` defaults to `N` and may also be `N_PLUS_1` or `TWO_N`; `cross_connect_allowance`, the number of active cross-connects waived at each period close, defaults to 0 and may not be negative; and `remote_hands_included_minutes`, the bundle of hands-and-eyes minutes sold with the contract, defaults to 0, may not be negative, and must be a whole multiple of 15 minutes because that is the granularity at which labour is billed.

`billing_mode` is the switch that decides how power overage is derived at period close and admits exactly two values. `METERED_PLUS_COMMIT` derives overage from recorded meter intervals; `COMMITTED_ONLY` derives it from deemed capacity load. Both are elaborated in §3.9.

A newly created contract opens in `DRAFT` with `credit_hold` false, `remote_hands_remaining_minutes` initialised to `remote_hands_included_minutes`, every rollup field (`provisioned_power_kw`, `stranded_commit_kw`, `commit_utilization_pct`, `active_cross_connect_count`, `billed_total_usd`, `cross_connect_billed_usd`) at zero and `last_closed_period_end` unset. Six lifecycle steps are then available, and the `status` field walks them: `DRAFT` → `PENDING_ACTIVATION` → `ACTIVE`, the reinstatement cycle `ACTIVE` ⇄ `SUSPENDED`, and the wind-down path `ACTIVE` → `WIND_DOWN` → `TERMINATED`, with `TERMINATED` accepting nothing further.

Readying a draft for activation is a `FacilityManager` action: the contract must still be in `DRAFT`, its `term_end_date` must be strictly later than its `term_start_date`, and on success the bundled-minutes balance is set from `remote_hands_included_minutes` and the contract's `version` increments. Activation itself is also a `FacilityManager` action and is the point at which the contract stops being a piece of paper: the contract must be in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` and must already hold at least one cabinet in `RESERVED`, `CAPACITY_HELD`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` — a cabinet the row has put on capacity hold still counts, because it still occupies its slot — and a contract with no space at all is refused with `ERR_NO_CABINET_ASSIGNED`.

Suspension and reinstatement belong to `BillingOperator`. Suspending an `ACTIVE` contract deliberately cascades nothing: cabinets stay where they are, energized circuits keep drawing power, and that power keeps accruing charges, because suspension is a commercial posture and not a power-off switch. Reinstating a `SUSPENDED` contract to `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE` while `credit_hold` is true, and the `ACTIVE` ⇄ `SUSPENDED` cycle may repeat without bound. Setting or clearing the hold is a separate `BillingOperator` capability that writes the `credit_hold` flag from the request body and nothing else: existing orders, tickets and visits are never retro-actively cancelled when a hold goes on.

Wind-down is the pre-termination posture. Moving an `ACTIVE` contract to `WIND_DOWN` is a `FacilityManager` action, and from that moment no new cross-connect order and no new remote-hands ticket may be raised against the contract, since both require an `ACTIVE` contract and report `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise. Termination closes the account and is only accepted once the space is genuinely clear: the contract must be in `WIND_DOWN`, no cabinet of the contract may remain in `RESERVED`, `CAPACITY_HELD`, `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE` or `DECOMMISSIONING`, and `active_cross_connect_count` must be zero. A termination attempt that fails either of those two conditions is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success any circuit still energized under the contract is de-energized and the contract's cabinets are released, and the contract's `version` increments.

### 3.2. The Data Hall: Rows, Ceilings and Posture

Floor space is modelled as rows inside data halls, and rows are the aggregate where three independent physical limits meet. A `FacilityManager` creates a row with its `hall_code`, a `row_code` that must be unique within that hall, the number of physical cabinet positions in `cabinet_slot_count` (at least one), the `design_density_kw_per_cabinet` figure the row was engineered to (strictly positive), the hard busway limit `max_row_power_kw` (strictly positive), and the sensible cooling capacity serving the row, `cooling_capacity_kw` (strictly positive). Two operational fields are optional: `escort_slot_count`, the number of concurrently escorted visits the row can host, defaults to 1 and may be zero; and `decommission_flag`, which retires the row from new space allocation, defaults to false. A freshly created row reports `row_status` of `OPEN` and every capacity rollup at zero.

The same manager may later re-limit a live row, adjusting any of `cooling_capacity_kw`, `max_row_power_kw`, `design_density_kw_per_cabinet`, `escort_slot_count` or `decommission_flag`. Re-limiting recomputes the row's whole capacity picture, which means `row_status` can flip — in either direction — without a single cabinet or circuit having changed, and the row's `version` increments on every such adjustment.

Six derived quantities describe a row, and they are recomputed whenever a cabinet is reserved, released, installed, put on capacity hold, decommissioned or released, whenever a circuit becomes energized or de-energized, whenever a meter reading is appended to one of its circuits, and whenever the row's own limits are adjusted. `occupied_slot_count` counts the cabinets in the row whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CAPACITY_HELD`, `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE` or `DECOMMISSIONING`; an `AVAILABLE` or `RELEASED` cabinet occupies nothing. `energized_cabinet_count` counts the cabinets in the row whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `allocated_power_kw` is the sum of `allocated_kw` over the energized circuits of the row's cabinets, rounded to three decimals, and `cooling_load_kw` is the sum of `cooling_contribution_kw` over the same set, likewise rounded to three decimals.

`density_ceiling_kw` is the design density multiplied by the number of occupied slots, rounded to three decimals — a row engineered at 8 kW per cabinet with five occupied slots carries a ceiling of 40 kW, and that ceiling grows and shrinks with occupancy rather than with the row's physical slot count. `effective_ceiling_kw` is the smallest of three numbers: the busway limit `max_row_power_kw`, the density ceiling just derived, and the cooling capacity. Whichever of the three binds first is the row's real limit, and it is the figure both stranded-capacity views measure against.

Stranded capacity is reported twice, deliberately, because capacity planners and finance disagree about what "used" means. The **nameplate view**, `stranded_nameplate_kw`, is the effective ceiling minus `allocated_power_kw`, rounded to three decimals: it charges the row for every breaker plate that has been energized, so a lightly loaded cabinet with a large breaker strands nothing at all. The **measured view**, `stranded_measured_kw`, is the effective ceiling minus `measured_peak_kw`, rounded to three decimals, where `measured_peak_kw` is the largest `peak_kw` recorded across the meter intervals of the row's circuits whose `interval_start` falls at or after 30 days before the logical clock, and is 0 when the trailing window holds no interval at all. Because observed peak draw is usually below energized breaker capacity, the same row normally reports *more* stranded capacity under the measured view than under the nameplate view, and that gap is the number the capacity team sells against.

Posture, exposed as `row_status`, is derived from the same figures and is evaluated as an ordered cascade in which the first matching condition wins and the remaining conditions are not consulted. If `decommission_flag` is true the row is `DECOMMISSIONING`. Otherwise, if `cooling_load_kw` has reached or passed 90 percent of `cooling_capacity_kw` the row is `COOLING_CONSTRAINED`. Otherwise, if at least one slot is occupied and `allocated_power_kw` has reached or passed `density_ceiling_kw` the row is `DENSITY_CAPPED`. Otherwise the row is `OPEN`. The ordering is load-bearing: a row that is both thermally tight and at its density ceiling reports `COOLING_CONSTRAINED`, and the consequences of the two postures differ sharply — a `DENSITY_CAPPED` row holds its reserved cabinets, as §3.3 describes, while a `COOLING_CONSTRAINED` row holds nothing and merely stops accepting new space.

New space is accepted only by rows in `OPEN` or `COOLING_CONSTRAINED` posture. Creating a cabinet in a `DENSITY_CAPPED` or `DECOMMISSIONING` row, or reserving or installing space in one, is refused with `ERR_ROW_NOT_ACCEPTING_SPACE`. Energization applies a narrower version of the same idea: there the only row-level bar is `decommission_flag` being true, so a `DENSITY_CAPPED` row still lets the cabinets already installed in it energize their approved circuits, subject to the numeric gates of §3.4.

### 3.3. Cabinet Space: Reservation, Installation and Release

A cabinet is a position in a row. Creating one requires the `row_id` of the host row, a `cabinet_code` unique within that row, and the nameplate `rated_power_kw` of the enclosure, which must be strictly positive. Two attributes default: `footprint_type` is `CABINET` unless the caller supplies `CAGE_SLOT` or `MEET_ME`, and `u_height`, the number of rack units available, defaults to 42. A `MEET_ME` cabinet is a carrier meet-me panel, and §3.7 explains why every visit to one is escorted. New cabinets open with `status` `AVAILABLE` and no `contract_id`; because an `AVAILABLE` cabinet occupies no slot, creating one does not move the row's density ceiling, though the row's capacity picture is recomputed anyway.

Space moves through `AVAILABLE` → `RESERVED` → `INSTALLED` → `ACTIVE` and eventually to `DECOMMISSIONING` and `RELEASED`, with `RESERVED` ⇄ `CAPACITY_HELD` and `RESERVED` → `AVAILABLE` as the two side paths. Reserving a cabinet to a customer is the operator step that binds space to money, and the request carries the `contract_id` the space is being reserved for. Four conditions must hold: the cabinet is `AVAILABLE`; the named contract is in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the row is in `OPEN` or `COOLING_CONSTRAINED` posture, otherwise `ERR_ROW_NOT_ACCEPTING_SPACE`; and the count of the contract's cabinets already in `RESERVED`, `CAPACITY_HELD`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, together with this one, stays within `committed_cabinet_count`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_COMMIT_EXCEEDED`. Space commitments are hard caps, not soft targets. On success the cabinet's `contract_id` is written, the row's `occupied_slot_count` rises by one, and the row's `density_ceiling_kw`, `effective_ceiling_kw`, both stranded views and `row_status` are recomputed; both the cabinet's and the row's `version` increment.

Releasing a reservation is the exact inverse and is refused while the cabinet still carries committed power: no circuit on the cabinet may be in `APPROVED` or `ENERGIZED`, otherwise the release is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A successful release clears `contract_id` back to unset, lowers `occupied_slot_count` by one, recomputes the row's density ceiling, effective ceiling, both stranded views and posture, and increments both versions. Installing a reserved cabinet — the step that says the enclosure is physically in place — requires the cabinet to be `RESERVED` and its contract to be `ACTIVE`, reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` if it is not, and increments the cabinet's `version` only.

Two of the cabinet's states are never requested by a client; the server drives them. When a row flips to `DENSITY_CAPPED`, every cabinet in that row that is `RESERVED` at that instant is placed on `CAPACITY_HELD`, and every circuit on those cabinets sitting in `DESIGNED` moves to `BLOCKED`; the held cabinet stays inside `occupied_slot_count`, so the density ceiling does not move underneath the row while the hold is in force. When the row's posture returns to `OPEN` — because a circuit was de-energized, because a reservation was released, or because a manager raised the row's limits — every `CAPACITY_HELD` cabinet returns to `RESERVED` and every `BLOCKED` circuit on it returns to `DESIGNED`. Each of these system-driven moves increments the version of every row it touches.

Likewise, a cabinet's `ACTIVE` posture is a consequence of power, not a request. When the first `PRIMARY` circuit on an `INSTALLED` cabinet reaches `ENERGIZED` — that is, when `energized_primary_circuit_count` moves from zero to one — the cabinet advances `INSTALLED` → `ACTIVE`, the row's `energized_cabinet_count` rises by one and the row's `allocated_power_kw`, `cooling_load_kw`, stranded views and posture are recomputed. When the last `PRIMARY` circuit leaves `ENERGIZED` and that count falls from one to zero, the cabinet steps back `ACTIVE` → `INSTALLED`, `energized_cabinet_count` falls by one, and the same row figures are recomputed. Alongside `allocated_power_kw` and `cooling_load_kw`, which sum `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` over the cabinet's energized circuits to three decimals, `energized_primary_circuit_count` is the cabinet's third derived field and counts its circuits that are both `ENERGIZED` and `PRIMARY`.

The last two cabinet states belong to decommissioning and are covered in §3.8: approving a decommission moves an `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` cabinet to `DECOMMISSIONING`, and port release moves it from `DECOMMISSIONING` to `RELEASED`, which is terminal.

### 3.4. Power: Design, Approval, Energization and Metering

Power reaches a cabinet through circuits, and a circuit is designed long before it carries load. A `ProvisioningEngineer` designs one against a `cabinet_id` with a `circuit_code` unique within that cabinet, the busway side it draws from in `feed_side` (`A` or `B`), its `redundancy_role` (`PRIMARY`, which carries load and allocates power, or `REDUNDANT`, which stands by and allocates none), the `breaker_amps` rating of the breaker, the nominal `volts` of the supply, and the `phase` of that supply (`SINGLE` or `THREE`). Breaker rating, voltage and rated cabinet power must all be strictly positive. New circuits open in `DESIGNED`, and their lifecycle is `DESIGNED` → `APPROVED` → `ENERGIZED` ⇄ `DE_ENERGIZED` → `REMOVED`, with the side path `DESIGNED` ⇄ `BLOCKED` driven by row posture as described in §3.3.

Derated capacity is pinned at design time. `derated_kw` is the breaker amperage multiplied by the voltage, multiplied by the phase factor — 1.0 for a `SINGLE`-phase supply and 1.732 for a `THREE`-phase supply — divided by 1000 to convert volt-amperes to kilowatts, then multiplied by the 0.80 continuous-load derate that electrical code requires, and finally rounded half-up to three decimal places. Every later step consumes that rounded figure and never the raw product. A 30 A, 208 V single-phase circuit therefore carries a derated capacity of 4.992 kW.

Two further power fields stay at 0.000 until the circuit actually carries load, and return to 0.000 the moment it stops. While a circuit's `status` is `DESIGNED`, `APPROVED`, `BLOCKED`, `DE_ENERGIZED` or `REMOVED`, both `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` are 0.000. Once a circuit is `ENERGIZED`, the two diverge by role. For a `PRIMARY` circuit, `allocated_kw` is the lesser of its derated capacity and the host cabinet's `rated_power_kw` — the cabinet nameplate clamps what the floor will bill for, and the clamp is applied to the already-rounded derated figure — while `cooling_contribution_kw` is the full derated capacity, because heat is not clamped by a nameplate. For a `REDUNDANT` circuit, `allocated_kw` is 0.000, since a standby feed allocates no billable power, while `cooling_contribution_kw` is one quarter of its derated capacity, rounded to three decimals, reflecting the idle draw of a live standby feed.

Approval is the paperwork gate and is available to a `ProvisioningEngineer` or a `FacilityManager`. The circuit must be `DESIGNED`; its cabinet must be in `RESERVED`, `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL`; and the cabinet's breaker sizing must survive the addition. That breaker test sums `derated_kw` across the cabinet's `PRIMARY` circuits already in `APPROVED` or `ENERGIZED` and adds this circuit's own derated capacity — counted as zero when the circuit being approved is `REDUNDANT` — and the total must not exceed the cabinet's `rated_power_kw` multiplied by the 1.25 headroom ratio the site allows above nameplate. A projection strictly greater than that ceiling is refused with `ERR_CABINET_BREAKER_OVERSUBSCRIBED`; equality passes.

Energization is where the site's scarcity rules concentrate, and it is exclusive to a `ProvisioningEngineer`. The request carries only `expected_row_version`, the client's last observed row version, as described in §2.6. The circuit must be in `APPROVED` or in `DE_ENERGIZED` — a circuit may be cycled between `DE_ENERGIZED` and `ENERGIZED` any number of times as long as its cabinet is not decommissioning — and the identical set of guards applies to both entry paths. Its cabinet must be `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. The row must not be flagged for decommissioning, otherwise `ERR_ROW_NOT_ACCEPTING_SPACE`. If the owning contract's `redundancy_requirement` is `TWO_N` and the circuit being energized is `PRIMARY`, the cabinet must already hold a `REDUNDANT` circuit in `APPROVED` or `ENERGIZED` whose `feed_side` differs from this circuit's, otherwise `ERR_REDUNDANT_FEED_MISSING`; contracts at `N` or `N_PLUS_1` may energize a lone primary feed.

Four numeric projections follow, each computed as though this circuit were already energized, and each permitting equality — only a strictly greater projection is refused. They are evaluated in a fixed order so that a request violating several of them always reports the same code: first the row's projected `cooling_load_kw` against `cooling_capacity_kw`, reported as `ERR_COOLING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; then the cabinet breaker projection against `rated_power_kw` multiplied by 1.25, reported as `ERR_CABINET_BREAKER_OVERSUBSCRIBED`; then the redundant-feed requirement above; then the row's projected `allocated_power_kw` against `density_ceiling_kw` multiplied by the 1.10 density tolerance, reported as `ERR_ROW_DENSITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; then the same projection against the absolute busway limit `max_row_power_kw`, reported as `ERR_ROW_POWER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The 10 percent tolerance on density is deliberate: the posture threshold that flips a row to `DENSITY_CAPPED` sits below the rejection threshold, so a row can legitimately reach its capped posture without any request being refused. Thermal safety outranks density, so a request that breaches both cooling and density reports the cooling error.

Fiscal gates close the sequence. A contract on credit hold cannot energize new power at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE`. Finally the contract's projected `provisioned_power_kw` must stay within `power_commit_kw` multiplied by 1.10 — a customer may provision up to 110 percent of contracted power before the floor refuses — otherwise `ERR_POWER_COMMIT_EXCEEDED`.

A successful energization runs the full cascade described in §2.8. The circuit's `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` are computed by role, `energized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the circuit's `version` increments. The cabinet's `allocated_power_kw`, `cooling_load_kw` and `energized_primary_circuit_count` are recomputed, and the cabinet advances `INSTALLED` → `ACTIVE` when its primary count moved from zero to one. The row's `energized_cabinet_count`, `allocated_power_kw`, `cooling_load_kw`, `measured_peak_kw`, `stranded_nameplate_kw`, `stranded_measured_kw` and `row_status` are recomputed; if the new posture is `DENSITY_CAPPED`, every `RESERVED` cabinet in the row is put on `CAPACITY_HELD` and every `DESIGNED` circuit on those cabinets becomes `BLOCKED`, whereas a flip to `COOLING_CONSTRAINED` holds nothing. The contract's `provisioned_power_kw`, `stranded_commit_kw` and `commit_utilization_pct` are refreshed. Circuit, cabinet and row versions all increment.

De-energizing an `ENERGIZED` circuit is available to a `ProvisioningEngineer` or a `FacilityManager` and compensates that cascade exactly: `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` return to 0.000; the cabinet's rollups are recomputed and the cabinet steps `ACTIVE` → `INSTALLED` when its primary count fell from one to zero; the row's allocated power, cooling load, stranded views and posture are recomputed, and if the posture returns to `OPEN` every `CAPACITY_HELD` cabinet returns to `RESERVED` with its `BLOCKED` circuits back in `DESIGNED`; the contract's provisioning rollups are refreshed; and circuit, cabinet and row versions increment. A `DE_ENERGIZED` circuit may then be retired to `REMOVED`, which is terminal, either by explicit operator action or by the port-release cascade of §3.8.

The contract-level provisioning rollup is worth stating precisely, because three fields depend on it. `provisioned_power_kw` is the sum of `allocated_kw` over circuits that are both `ENERGIZED` and `PRIMARY` on cabinets whose `contract_id` is this contract, rounded to three decimals. `stranded_commit_kw` is the contracted commitment minus that provisioned figure, floored at zero so an over-provisioned contract reports 0.000 rather than a negative number, rounded to three decimals. `commit_utilization_pct` is the provisioned figure divided by `power_commit_kw`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to one decimal place. The same rollup pass also refreshes `active_cross_connect_count`, the count of the contract's cross-connect orders in `ACTIVE`; `billed_total_usd`, the sum of `amount_usd` across every invoice line ever appended to the contract, credits and reversals included, rounded to two decimals; and `cross_connect_billed_usd`, the same sum restricted to lines whose `line_type` is `CROSS_CONNECT_MRC` or `CROSS_CONNECT_SURCHARGE`, likewise rounded to two decimals. This rollup is recomputed on every circuit energization and de-energization, on every cabinet reservation, release, decommission and port release, on every cross-connect activation and decommission, and on every invoice line appended.

Metering is a separate, append-only stream. A `FacilityManager` or a `BillingOperator` appends one interval at a time to a circuit, supplying `interval_start`, `interval_end`, the consumed `kwh` and the observed `peak_kw` for that interval. Intervals are retained in the order they were appended, in the circuit's `meter_intervals` collection, and an interval whose `interval_end` is not after its `interval_start` is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID`. Appending a reading recomputes the host row's `measured_peak_kw` and therefore its `stranded_measured_kw`, which is how a row's measured stranded capacity can change without any electrical work taking place.

### 3.5. Cross-Connects: Ordering, Routing and Port Stewardship

Physical connectivity is inventoried as patch-panel ports. A `FacilityManager` or a `ProvisioningEngineer` creates a port with the `row_id` of the row whose patch field holds it, the `panel_code` printed on the panel — the same string that later appears as a hop in a routed path — the `port_number` of the position on that panel, which must be at least 1 and unique for the combination of row and panel, and the `media_type` the port terminates: `SMF`, `MMF`, `CAT6A` or `COAX`. A new port is `FREE`, with no `assigned_order_id` and no `quarantine_until`. Over its life a port moves between `FREE`, `RESERVED` (pinned by a route proposal), `ASSIGNED` (physically patched) and `QUARANTINED` (reclaimed and cooling down), and `assigned_order_id` names the order holding it while it is reserved or assigned.

An order is raised by a `CustomerAdmin` or a `ProvisioningEngineer` and names the billing `contract_id`, the `requested_by_contact_id` of the contact who asked for it, the customer-side `a_end_cabinet_id`, the far-side `z_end_cabinet_id` — another customer's cabinet or a carrier meet-me panel — and the `cable_type` to be pulled, one of `SMF`, `MMF`, `CAT6A` or `COAX`. An order that re-issues an earlier one after a decommission or an abort carries the earlier order's identifier in the optional `parent_order_id`, forming a re-issue chain that is recorded for audit and imposes no additional rules. The mandatory `expected_contract_version` field and its stale-version rejection are specified in §2.6.

Creation gates read in the order of §2.5: the contract must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the A-end cabinet must be `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, and only the A end is examined — the far side is deliberately exempt from both the operational test and contract scoping; the A-end cabinet's `contract_id` must equal the named contract, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT`; the contract must not be on credit hold, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE`; and the count of the contract's orders that are neither `ABORTED` nor `DECOMMISSIONED`, this one included, must stay within `cross_connect_cap`, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_CONNECT_CAP_EXCEEDED` — aborted and decommissioned orders free a slot for re-use. A created order opens in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_count` at 0, no route pinned, and `mrc_usd` fixed from the media it will use: 300.00 USD for `SMF`, 250.00 USD for `MMF`, 175.00 USD for `CAT6A`, 225.00 USD for `COAX`. That monthly rate is pinned at creation and is never re-derived, even if the same order is later re-routed onto different panels.

Routing is exclusive to a `ProvisioningEngineer` and moves the order `SUBMITTED` → `ROUTE_PROPOSED` or, on a second attempt, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `ROUTE_PROPOSED`; both entry paths apply identical guards. The proposal supplies `a_end_port_id`, `z_end_port_id` and `path_hops`, an ordered list of panel codes describing the physical path with the A-side panel first and the Z-side panel last. Neither endpoint port may currently be `RESERVED` or `ASSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_PORT_UNAVAILABLE`; a `FREE` port and a `QUARANTINED` port both survive this test, so that a reclaimed port falls through to the cool-down check and reports the more specific error. Neither port may be inside its quarantine window — that is, no port may carry a `quarantine_until` strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_PORT_IN_QUARANTINE`; the boundary instant itself is accepted. Both ports' `media_type` must equal the order's `cable_type`, otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Each port must sit in the same row as the cabinet it terminates: the A-side port's `row_id` must equal the A-end cabinet's `row_id` and the Z-side port's `row_id` must equal the Z-end cabinet's `row_id`, otherwise `ERR_PORT_ROW_MISMATCH`. Finally, `path_hops` must be non-empty, must begin with the A-side port's `panel_code`, must end with the Z-side port's `panel_code`, and must not repeat any panel code, otherwise `ERR_PATH_ROUTE_INVALID`; that single rule rejects disconnected, reversed and looping routes alike. On acceptance both ports move `FREE` → `RESERVED` with `assigned_order_id` set to this order, the two port references and the hop list are pinned onto the order, `proposed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the order's `version` increments.

A `FacilityManager` or a `CustomerAdmin` who dislikes a proposed route sends it back with a `reason`, moving `ROUTE_PROPOSED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. The pool is conserved rather than leaked: `revision_count` rises by one, both currently reserved ports return to `FREE` with `assigned_order_id` cleared, and `a_end_port_id`, `z_end_port_id` and `path_hops` are cleared back to unset and an empty list. The cycle `ROUTE_PROPOSED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `ROUTE_PROPOSED` may repeat only while `revision_count` is strictly below two; a third change request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, at which point the order must be approved or abandoned.

Approval belongs to a `FacilityManager`. The order must be in `ROUTE_PROPOSED`, its contract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and not on credit hold (`ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE`), and both pinned ports must still be `RESERVED` with `assigned_order_id` naming this order, otherwise `ERR_PORT_UNAVAILABLE`. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the one that created the order, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `approved_by_user_id` is pinned from the header and the order's `version` increments. A `ProvisioningEngineer` then records physical installation, moving `APPROVED` → `INSTALLED` and taking both ports `RESERVED` → `ASSIGNED`. Bringing the circuit into service, `INSTALLED` → `ACTIVE`, is a `FacilityManager` action that requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, raises the contract's `active_cross_connect_count` by one, and increments both the order's and the contract's `version`.

Abandonment is available from any of `SUBMITTED`, `ROUTE_PROPOSED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and `APPROVED`, and moves the order to `ABORTED`, which is terminal. Every port whose `assigned_order_id` names this order and whose status is `RESERVED` or `ASSIGNED` returns to `FREE` with `assigned_order_id` cleared, and the order's `version` increments. Orders that have reached `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` cannot be aborted; retiring them requires a decommission order, as §3.8 describes, and that route quarantines the ports rather than freeing them immediately.

### 3.6. Remote Hands: Authorization, Dispatch and Increment Billing

A remote-hands ticket carries two state dimensions on one row, and they advance independently. `authorization_state` walks `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` → `AUTHORIZED`, `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` → `DENIED` or `AUTHORIZED` → `REVOKED`, while `execution_state` walks `QUEUED` → `DISPATCHED` → `COMPLETED`, with `CANCELLED` as its abandonment state and `IN_PROGRESS` as an intermediate execution value. Both dimensions start at their initial values when the ticket is raised.

Raising a ticket is available to a `CustomerAdmin` or a `ProvisioningEngineer` and requires the billing `contract_id`, the `requested_by_contact_id` of the requesting contact, the `target_cabinet_id` the work touches, a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `HIGH` or `STANDARD`, and a free-text `description` of the work requested. The contract must be `ACTIVE` and free of credit hold, reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_CREDIT_HOLD_ACTIVE` respectively; the requesting contact must sit on the owning customer's authorization list and hold the `REMOTE_HANDS` scope, otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; and the target cabinet's `contract_id` must equal the named contract, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT`. The cabinet need not be operational at this point — that test is deferred to dispatch.

Authorization is a separate act by a separate person, performed by a `CustomerAdmin` or a `SecurityOfficer`, and names the `authorizing_contact_id` alongside the target `to_state`. The authorizing contact must belong to the contract's customer and hold the `REMOTE_HANDS` scope, otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, and must not be the contact that requested the work; a self-authorization, whether detected through the contact reference or through an `X-User-ID` identical to the ticket's creator, is refused with `ERR_SELF_AUTHORIZATION_FORBIDDEN`. Authorizing a `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` ticket stamps `authorized_at` from the logical clock and derives `response_deadline_at` from it, leaving `execution_state` untouched because the dimensions are independent. Denying a `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` ticket applies the same contact guards and, in addition to setting `authorization_state` to `DENIED`, forces `execution_state` from `QUEUED` to `CANCELLED`. Revoking an already `AUTHORIZED` ticket is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` once execution has reached `COMPLETED`; otherwise it forces `execution_state` to `CANCELLED` from whichever of `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_PROGRESS` it currently holds. Every authorization move increments the ticket's `version`.

The response-time commitment is a function of severity alone. `response_deadline_at` is `authorized_at` advanced by 30 minutes for a `CRITICAL` ticket, by 120 minutes for a `HIGH` ticket, and by 480 minutes — eight hours — for a `STANDARD` ticket. Dispatch is exclusive to a `ProvisioningEngineer`, takes no body fields, and moves `execution_state` from `QUEUED` to `DISPATCHED`. It requires `authorization_state` to be `AUTHORIZED` — a ticket in `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_AUTHORIZED` even though its execution dimension is legitimately `QUEUED` — and requires the target cabinet to be `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. On success `dispatched_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `sla_breached` is set true only when `dispatched_at` is strictly later than `response_deadline_at`; a dispatch landing exactly on the deadline instant has met the commitment, not breached it. Dispatch does not advance execution any further: `DISPATCHED` is an observable state in its own right.

Completion is also exclusive to a `ProvisioningEngineer`, requires the ticket to be `DISPATCHED` with `authorization_state` still `AUTHORIZED` (`ERR_TICKET_NOT_AUTHORIZED` otherwise), and carries `work_segments`, the labour actually performed. The list must be non-empty, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and each segment's `ended_at` must be at or after its `started_at`, otherwise `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID`; a zero-length segment is legal and simply bills nothing. Segments are stored in the order they were submitted and are processed for billing in ascending `started_at`, with ties broken by ascending position in the submitted list.

Labour is billed in 15-minute increments, and the rounding order is pinned. For each segment, the raw duration is the whole minutes between its `started_at` and its `ended_at`, discarding any remaining seconds; a segment of zero whole minutes contributes zero increments, and any other segment contributes its raw minutes divided by 15 and rounded **up** to the next whole increment. Those per-segment increments are then summed into `billable_increments`; summing raw minutes across segments first and taking a single ceiling is expressly not the rule, so two seven-minute segments bill two increments rather than one. `billed_minutes` is `billable_increments` multiplied by 15.

The contract's bundle is then consumed as a waterfall. The included budget is the contract's `remote_hands_remaining_minutes` divided by 15 and rounded **down** to whole increments, and `included_increments_applied` is the lesser of that budget and `billable_increments`. The budget is walked against the segments in the same order they were billed, earliest segment first, so the earliest work is the work that gets absorbed. Money is then computed per segment and rounded per segment: a segment's charge is the increments it contributed *minus* the increments the bundle absorbed from it, multiplied by 62.50 USD per increment, multiplied by an after-hours factor of 1.35 when the segment began outside business hours, and rounded half-up to two decimals. A segment counts as after hours when the UTC hour of its `started_at` is before 08:00 or at or after 20:00. `labor_charge_usd` is the sum of those per-segment charges; summing first and rounding once is not the rule.

The ledger effects follow. The contract's `remote_hands_remaining_minutes` is reduced by `included_increments_applied` multiplied by 15. When `labor_charge_usd` is greater than zero, an invoice line of `line_type` `REMOTE_HANDS` is appended carrying that amount, a `quantity` equal to `billable_increments` minus `included_increments_applied`, and `included_minutes_consumed` equal to the absorbed increments multiplied by 15. When that line exists *and* `sla_breached` is true, a second line of `line_type` `SLA_CREDIT` is appended for the negative of 25 percent of `labor_charge_usd`, rounded to two decimals — a breach with no billable charge produces no credit, because there is nothing to credit against. The ticket's `completed_at` is stamped, `execution_state` becomes `COMPLETED`, the contract's `billed_total_usd` is refreshed, and both the ticket's and the contract's `version` increment.

### 3.7. Site Access: Authorization Lists, Biometrics and Escorts

Who may enter the hall is governed by the customer's authorization list. A `CustomerAdmin` or a `SecurityOfficer` adds a contact under a `customer_id` with a `full_name` and `authorization_scopes`, a non-empty subset of `REMOTE_HANDS`, which permits raising and authorizing hands-and-eyes work, and `SITE_ACCESS`, which permits visits. The optional `access_level` defaults to `ESCORTED_ONLY` and may be set to `UNESCORTED`. Every contact starts with `biometric_state` `NOT_ENROLLED` and no `enrolled_at`.

Enrollment is exclusive to a `SecurityOfficer`, who moves a contact's `biometric_state` to `ENROLLED` or `REVOKED`. Enrolling stamps `enrolled_at` from the logical clock. Revocation is deliberately blunt and cascades beyond the contact record: every non-terminal remote-hands ticket raised by that contact has its `authorization_state` forced to `REVOKED`, and every `SCHEDULED` access visit for that contact is moved to `CANCELLED`, releasing the cabinet window and escort slot each of those visits was holding.

Booking a visit is available to a `CustomerAdmin` or a `SecurityOfficer` and names the `contract_id` under which the visit is booked, the visiting `contact_id`, the `cabinet_id` the visitor will work in, the `purpose` of the visit — `MAINTENANCE`, `CARRIER_INSTALL`, `AUDIT` or `REMOTE_HANDS_ESCORT` — and the half-open window bounded by `window_start`, which is inclusive, and `window_end`, which is exclusive and must be strictly later than the start. The contract must be `ACTIVE`, the contact must be on the customer's list with the scope the operation needs (`ERR_CONTACT_NOT_AUTHORIZED` otherwise), the cabinet must be `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL` otherwise), and the cabinet's `contract_id` must equal the named contract (`ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT` otherwise). New visits open in `SCHEDULED`, and the lifecycle is `SCHEDULED` → `CHECKED_IN` → `CHECKED_OUT`, with `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED` as the abandonment path; `CHECKED_OUT` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

`escort_required` is computed once, at booking, and pinned on the visit. It is true when any one of three conditions holds: the contact's `access_level` is `ESCORTED_ONLY`, or the visit's `purpose` is `CARRIER_INSTALL`, or the target cabinet's `footprint_type` is `MEET_ME`. A carrier engineer entering a meet-me panel and an unescorted-badge customer doing routine maintenance are therefore treated differently by design.

Two occupancy gates are then evaluated against the visits that are still occupying space — those in `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN`; visits in `CHECKED_OUT` or `CANCELLED` have released everything they held. The first gate is cabinet exclusivity, and it treats windows as half-open: a booking collides with an existing visit on the same `cabinet_id` when the new window starts strictly before the existing window ends *and* the existing window starts strictly before the new window ends. Windows that merely touch — one ending at 12:00 and the next starting at 12:00 — do not collide. A collision is refused with `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The second gate applies only when the new visit requires an escort, and it counts rather than excludes: across every cabinet in the same row, the server counts the occupying visits that themselves require an escort and whose window boundaries meet the new window on **closed** intervals — the new window's start at or before the peer's end, and the peer's start at or before the new window's end — excluding the candidate itself. When that count has already reached the row's `escort_slot_count`, the booking is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The asymmetry between the two gates is intentional: a touching window does not consume a cabinet, but it does consume an escort, because a single officer cannot hand over instantaneously. All of these comparisons are made at second resolution with no rounding.

Check-in and check-out belong exclusively to a `SecurityOfficer`. Checking a `SCHEDULED` visit in requires the visiting contact's `biometric_state` to be `ENROLLED`, otherwise `ERR_BIOMETRIC_NOT_ENROLLED` — scheduling before enrollment is allowed, entering is not — and requires the logical clock to lie inside the half-open window, at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_VISIT_WINDOW`; both early and late arrivals are refused. When `escort_required` is true the request must name the escorting officer in `escort_officer_user_id`, and when it is false the request must not supply one; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED`. On success `checked_in_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the escorting officer is recorded, and the visit's `version` increments. Checking a `CHECKED_IN` visit out stamps `checked_out_at`, and both check-out and the cancellation of a still-`SCHEDULED` visit release the cabinet window and the row escort slot the visit was occupying.

### 3.8. Decommissioning: Two Recorded Steps, Then Reclamation

A decommission order is raised by a `CustomerAdmin` or a `ProvisioningEngineer` and names the `contract_id` being wound down, the `cabinet_id` to be emptied and released, and the `requested_by_contact_id` of the contact who asked for it. The named cabinet's `contract_id` must equal the named contract, otherwise `ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT`. A new order sits in `REQUESTED`, and its lifecycle forks and re-joins: `REQUESTED` → `APPROVED` → `IN_PROGRESS` → `READY_FOR_RELEASE` → `CLOSED`, with `REQUESTED` → `CANCELLED` as the only escape. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Approval is a `FacilityManager` or `ProvisioningEngineer` action and is where the power comes off. The order must be in `REQUESTED`, the cabinet must be `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CABINET_NOT_OPERATIONAL` otherwise), the cabinet's `contract_id` must still equal the order's contract (`ERR_CABINET_NOT_ON_CONTRACT` otherwise), and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the one that raised the order (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` otherwise). On acceptance the cabinet moves from `INSTALLED` or `ACTIVE` to `DECOMMISSIONING`; every `ENERGIZED` circuit on it moves to `DE_ENERGIZED` with `allocated_kw` and `cooling_contribution_kw` zeroed; `released_power_kw` records the total allocated kilowatts thereby de-energized, rounded to three decimals, and is 0.000 when the cabinet was merely `INSTALLED` and carried no energized load; the row's `allocated_power_kw`, `cooling_load_kw`, both stranded views and `row_status` are recomputed, which may return a `DENSITY_CAPPED` row to `OPEN` and thereby lift capacity holds elsewhere in the row; the contract's `provisioned_power_kw`, `stranded_commit_kw` and `commit_utilization_pct` are refreshed; and the order's, the cabinet's and the row's `version` all increment. Cancelling a still-`REQUESTED` order cascades nothing at all — the cabinet keeps whatever status it had — and increments only the order's `version`.

Physical work is recorded as two independent steps by a `ProvisioningEngineer`, each identified by a `step_code`: `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL` and `CABLE_RECLAMATION`. Recording the first of them moves the order `APPROVED` → `IN_PROGRESS` and writes either `equipment_removed_at` and `equipment_removed_by_user_id` or `cables_reclaimed_at` and `cables_reclaimed_by_user_id`, taking the timestamp from the logical clock and the operator from `X-User-ID`. A `step_code` that already carries a timestamp on this order is refused with `ERR_DECOMMISSION_STEP_ALREADY_RECORDED` rather than being silently ignored, so each step is recorded exactly once. Recording the second step is where the two branches join, and it carries an additional separation rule: recording `CABLE_RECLAMATION` requires an `X-User-ID` different from `equipment_removed_by_user_id`, and recording `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL` requires one different from `cables_reclaimed_by_user_id`, with a violation refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Once both steps carry timestamps the order auto-advances to `READY_FOR_RELEASE` within that same request — the join is not a separate call — and the order's `version` increments.

Port release is the final `ProvisioningEngineer` action and closes the order, `READY_FOR_RELEASE` → `CLOSED`. It runs the longest cascade in the system, and every hop must be visible in the resulting state. First, `released_port_count` records how many ports the release moved out of `ASSIGNED`. Second, every cross-connect order in `ACTIVE` whose `a_end_cabinet_id` is this order's cabinet moves to `DECOMMISSIONED`. Third, the `ASSIGNED` ports of each such cross-connect become `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_until` set to 24 hours after the logical clock and `assigned_order_id` cleared, which is the cool-down that §3.5's routing gates test against. Fourth, for each decommissioned cross-connect the contract's `active_cross_connect_count` falls by one and an invoice line of `line_type` `CROSS_CONNECT_CREDIT` is appended for the negative of 50 percent of that order's `mrc_usd`, rounded to two decimals, after which the contract's `billed_total_usd` and `cross_connect_billed_usd` are refreshed. Fifth, the cabinet itself moves `DECOMMISSIONING` → `RELEASED`, each of its `DE_ENERGIZED` circuits moves to `REMOVED`, its `contract_id` is cleared, and the row's `occupied_slot_count` falls by one with the density ceiling, effective ceiling, both stranded views and `row_status` recomputed. The decommission order, the cabinet, the row and the contract all have their `version` incremented, and each decommissioned cross-connect order increments its own.

### 3.9. Period Close, Invoice Lines and Compensating Adjustments

Money is recorded as an append-only stream of invoice lines against a contract. Each line names its `contract_id`, a `line_type` drawn from `POWER_COMMIT`, `POWER_OVERAGE`, `CROSS_CONNECT_MRC`, `CROSS_CONNECT_SURCHARGE`, `CROSS_CONNECT_CREDIT`, `REMOTE_HANDS`, `SLA_CREDIT` and `REVERSAL`, a signed `amount_usd` at two decimals where credits and reversals are negative, the billed `quantity` — kilowatts, kilowatt-hours, labour increments or a cross-connect count, depending on the line — and the `unit_rate_usd` applied to that quantity, carried to four decimals. Period lines carry an inclusive `period_start` and an exclusive `period_end`; event lines leave both unset. `source_ref_id` is a polymorphic back-reference resolved by `line_type` — the cross-connect order, the remote-hands ticket, or the reversed line — and it is deliberately never consulted in any ordering or selection decision. `included_minutes_consumed` records the bundled remote-hands minutes a labour line absorbed, and `reversed` flips to true once a compensating reversal has been issued against the line.

Closing a period is exclusive to a `BillingOperator`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise), and carries the `period_start` and `period_end` of the period being closed, with the end strictly after the start (`ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_INVALID` otherwise). Two calculations then run in a fixed order — power first, then cross-connects — and both append their lines in the order stated below. When the close completes, `last_closed_period_end` is set to the supplied `period_end` and `billed_total_usd` and `cross_connect_billed_usd` are refreshed.

Power billing begins with the committed charge, which is `power_commit_kw` multiplied by the standing rate of 175.00 USD per contracted kilowatt per month, rounded to two decimals. The commitment also buys energy: `included_kwh` is `power_commit_kw` multiplied by 730, the hours the site bills as a month, rounded to three decimals. Against that allowance the system computes a billable consumption figure, and this is the point where `billing_mode` bifurcates the calculation. Under `METERED_PLUS_COMMIT`, billable consumption is the sum of `kwh` over the meter intervals of every circuit on the contract's cabinets whose `interval_start` falls at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`, rounded to three decimals. Under `COMMITTED_ONLY`, the meter intervals are ignored altogether and consumption is deemed: the sum of `allocated_kw` over circuits that are both `ENERGIZED` and `PRIMARY` on the contract's cabinets at close time, multiplied by the same 730 hours, then multiplied by a deemed load factor of 0.65, rounded to three decimals. Identical recorded intervals therefore produce a different — and usually lower — overage under `COMMITTED_ONLY` than under `METERED_PLUS_COMMIT`, which is the whole commercial point of the distinction.

Overage energy is billable consumption minus `included_kwh`, rounded to three decimals and floored at zero, so consumption exactly equal to the allowance yields no overage at all. The overage charge walks a marginal three-tier ladder, low tier first: the first 500 kWh of overage bill at 0.1800 USD per kWh, the next 1500 kWh — that is, overage between 500 and 2000 kWh — bill at 0.1550 USD per kWh, and everything beyond 2000 kWh bills at 0.1350 USD per kWh. The three tier products are summed and the sum is rounded once, half-up, to two decimals; the tiers are not rounded individually. Two lines may result, in this order: a `POWER_COMMIT` line whose `amount_usd` is the committed charge and whose `quantity` is `power_commit_kw`, always; and a `POWER_OVERAGE` line whose `amount_usd` is the overage charge and whose `quantity` is the overage energy, appended only when the overage energy is greater than zero.

Cross-connect billing then runs, in six pinned steps. First, the candidates are the contract's cross-connect orders whose status is `ACTIVE` at close time, ranked by `mrc_usd` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; orders in `SUBMITTED`, `ROUTE_PROPOSED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `INSTALLED`, `DECOMMISSIONED` or `ABORTED` are excluded entirely. Second, the first `cross_connect_allowance` candidates in that ranking are waived — they bill 0.00 and do not appear in any line's quantity — which means the allowance always consumes the cheapest cross-connects first and leaves the expensive ones billable. Third, the subtotal is the sum of `mrc_usd` over the remaining candidates, rounded to two decimals. Fourth, the volume tier is keyed on that post-allowance subtotal and nothing else: a subtotal of 800.00 USD or more earns a 10 percent discount, a subtotal of at least 500.00 USD but below 800.00 USD earns 5 percent, and anything below 500.00 USD earns none. Fifth, the discounted subtotal is the subtotal multiplied by one minus the discount fraction, rounded to two decimals. Sixth, the infrastructure surcharge is 20 percent of that discounted subtotal, rounded to two decimals, and the period total is the discounted subtotal plus the surcharge, rounded to two decimals. The surcharge is keyed on the discounted figure, never on the pre-discount subtotal. Two lines are appended, in this order and only when the discounted subtotal is greater than zero: a `CROSS_CONNECT_MRC` line whose `amount_usd` is the discounted subtotal, and a `CROSS_CONNECT_SURCHARGE` line whose `amount_usd` is the surcharge, each carrying a `quantity` equal to the number of billed candidates — the waived ones excluded.

The `CROSS_CONNECT_CREDIT` line is not produced by a period close. It is issued by the port-release cascade of §3.8 at the moment a cross-connect is physically reclaimed, for the negative of half of that order's pinned `mrc_usd`.

Adjustments are the only way to correct the ledger, because lines are immutable. A `BillingOperator` names the `invoice_line_id` to compensate and a `reason`, and the server appends a `REVERSAL` line whose `amount_usd` is the exact negative of the original line's amount and marks the original's `reversed` flag true. A line that already carries `reversed` true, or that is itself a `REVERSAL`, is refused with `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_REVERSED`: each billed line may be compensated exactly once. An `invoice_line_id` that does not resolve, or that resolves to a line on a different contract than the one in the path, is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. Reversal is symmetric with the line it compensates, so reversing a `REMOTE_HANDS` line restores the contract's `remote_hands_remaining_minutes` by exactly that line's `included_minutes_consumed`, and the contract's `billed_total_usd` is recomputed across the whole ledger afterwards.
