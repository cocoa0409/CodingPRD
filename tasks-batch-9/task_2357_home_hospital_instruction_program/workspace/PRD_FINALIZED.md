# Home Hospital Instruction API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a student cannot attend school for an extended medical reason, the district still owes that student instruction, and the district still has to answer for the money it claims on that student's behalf. Today those two obligations are tracked in separate places: a nurse's folder holds the physician's certification, a program secretary keeps a spreadsheet of teacher visits, the campus registrar codes attendance from memory, and the apportionment claim is reconstructed weeks later from whatever paperwork survived. The reconstruction rarely matches what was actually delivered, and when an auditor reverses a single visit there is no mechanism that walks the consequence back through the entitlement, the attendance code, and the claim.

The Home and Hospital Instruction API replaces that folder-and-spreadsheet arrangement with one authoritative record of the homebound program. It carries a case from referral through physician certification, through the computation of an instructional-hours entitlement that respects grade band, delivery mode, diagnosis category, expected absence duration and any IEP floor, through teacher staffing under contractual caseload ceilings, through home-visit safety review and its virtual alternatives, through session-by-session delivery and attendance coding, through extension of the certified window on updated medical documentation, and finally either back into the school building on a coordinated transition plan or out of the program with a reconciled funding claim.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, the service dimension and the funding dimension of a case advance independently: a case can be actively served while its apportionment is held for a service shortfall, and an audit reversal months later can pull a reconciled case back into a hold. Second, every date, window, expiry and elapsed-week rule in the system reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that a certified window, a safety-review expiry and a compensatory-minutes balance are all reproducible and testable. This document specifies the API's contract-level structure in §2, its business rules in §3, its endpoint surface in §4, and its error vocabulary in §5. Every endpoint in this system is mounted under the `/hhi` prefix, with the sole exception of the clock-control capability described in §2.4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. Five roles exist and their spellings are exact: `ProgramAdministrator`, `CaseManager`, `NurseReviewer`, `HomeboundTeacher`, and `ClassroomTeacher`. A request that omits either header, or that supplies an `X-User-Role` value outside those five, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorized for the called endpoint (see the table in §4) is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The `ProgramAdministrator` owns district configuration, activation, funding decisions, voiding and final decisions. The `CaseManager` opens cases, submits documentation, staffs and schedules, coordinates with campuses and plans the return to the building. The `NurseReviewer` is the only role permitted to accept or reject a physician certification. The `HomeboundTeacher` accepts or declines assignments and logs session outcomes. The `ClassroomTeacher` acknowledges or disputes curriculum pacing and assessment accommodations.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` differently depending on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `CaseManager`, or `NurseReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a scaffolded transition writes it — as `created_by_user_id` on a `HomeboundCase`, as `submitted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `PhysicianCertification`, as `finalized_by_user_id` on a `HomeVisitSafetyReview`, as `logged_by_user_id` or `verified_by_user_id` on an `InstructionSession`, as `recorded_by_user_id` on an `AttendanceLedgerEntry`, and as `submitted_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on an `ExtensionRequest` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `HomeboundTeacher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Teacher`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Teacher` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so accepting or declining an assignment requires `X-User-ID` to equal that assignment's `teacher_id`, and logging a session outcome requires `X-User-ID` to equal the `teacher_id` of the assignment the session references. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEACHER`. When `X-User-Role` is `ClassroomTeacher`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Teacher`; acknowledging or disputing a pacing coordination row requires `X-User-ID` to equal that row's `classroom_teacher_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CLASSROOM_TEACHER_OF_RECORD`.

Two operations additionally enforce segregation of duties on top of role membership. These are described where they occur, in §3.6 and §3.8, and both reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1, and has a maximum of 100. `offset` sets how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a payload-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` reflects the number of rows matching the supplied filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching those filters remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. The Logical Service Clock

Every date comparison, window boundary, expiry check, elapsed-week count and gap calculation in this system reads a single server-held logical clock. The system never consults real wall-clock time for any business rule. At process startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that value until it is explicitly moved.

A `ProgramAdministrator` moves the clock by supplying an absolute UTC instant as `to_iso8601` to the clock-control capability (`POST /_clock/set_current_utc`). The clock accepts absolute instants in either direction, and every subsequent request in the process reads the newly set value. Wherever this document speaks of "the clock date" it means the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock instant, and wherever it speaks of "the clock instant" it means the full logical clock timestamp, and wherever such an instant is stamped onto a stored field it is serialized as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix or other decoration, unique across the system.

Most resources — `Student`, `Teacher`, `EntitlementRule`, `HomeboundCase`, `PhysicianCertification`, `TeacherAssignment`, `HomeVisitSafetyReview`, `InstructionSession`, `ExtensionRequest`, `TransitionPlan`, `CurriculumPacingSync`, and `SpedServiceLog` — additionally carry three system-managed fields:

- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only rewrite computed fields.
- `version` (integer): an integer that increments by one on every mutation of the row.

The `AttendanceLedgerEntry` is the exception. Attendance ledger rows are append-only: once written they are never updated, corrected in place, or deleted. Because nothing ever mutates them, they carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an `AttendanceLedgerEntry` entirely. A correction to attendance is expressed by appending a compensating reversal row, never by editing the original.

A cascade counts as a mutation. When one request rewrites a computed field on a parent or sibling record — a case's entitlement, a teacher's committed minutes, a plan's acknowledgement counters — that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if it had been the direct target of the request.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Checks are grouped into fourteen tiers and evaluated strictly in tier order; the first tier that produces a failure determines the response, and no later tier is consulted.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Payload shape.** The body and query conform to the endpoint's input schema: every required field present, no unknown field, every enum value a member of its declared set, every integer inside its declared range, every date formatted as `YYYY-MM-DD`, every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, `physician_npi` exactly ten digits, `hazard_findings` free of duplicate members, `limit` inside the inclusive range 1 to 100, and `offset` not negative. Any violation is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
4. **Path existence, left to right.** Identifiers embedded in the URL are resolved in the order they appear in the path, and the first one that does not resolve determines the 404: `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND` for a student id in the path of a student read or an enrollment change; `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND` for a teacher id in the path of a teacher read or a clearance update; `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_RULE_NOT_FOUND` for an entitlement-rule id in the path of a rule read or a retirement; `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` for a case id in the path of any case-scoped operation; `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` for a certification id in the path of a certification read or review; `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an assignment id in the path of an assignment read, accept, decline or release; `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` for a session id in the path of a session read, log, verify, void, cancel or make-up; `ERR_SAFETY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for a safety-review id in the path of a review read or finalization; `ERR_EXTENSION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for an extension-request id in the path of a request read, start-review, request-info, resubmit or decide; `ERR_TRANSITION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for a plan id in the path of a plan read, coordinate, complete or abort; `ERR_PACING_SYNC_NOT_FOUND` for a pacing-sync id in the path of a sync read, acknowledge or dispute; `ERR_SPED_LOG_NOT_FOUND` for a special-education log id in the path of a log read, delivery recording or continuation change; and `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` for an attendance-entry id in the path of a ledger read. All of these are 404.
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Foreign keys supplied in the request body are resolved in this fixed order: `student_id`, then `teacher_id`, then `case_id`, then `certification_id`, then `assignment_id`, then `session_id`, then `classroom_teacher_id`. A `student_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `teacher_id` or `classroom_teacher_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`; a `case_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; a `certification_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`; an `assignment_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are 404.
6. **Resource ownership for entity-backed roles.** The `HomeboundTeacher` and `ClassroomTeacher` ownership checks described in §2.1, rejecting with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TEACHER` or `ERR_NOT_CLASSROOM_TEACHER_OF_RECORD` (403).
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** The expected-version precondition described in §2.7, rejecting with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (412).
8. **State preconditions.** The from-state of every requested transition, rejecting with the state-specific 409 codes described alongside each workflow in §3.
9. **Segregation of duties.** The two acting-user-versus-recorded-user checks, rejecting with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Safety and compliance.** The home-visit and clearance rules of §3.4 and §3.5, all 422.
11. **Uniqueness and constraint satisfaction.** Duplicate keys and single-record constraint checks, 409 or 422 as catalogued in §5.
12. **Referential and cross-entity consistency.** Cross-record agreement checks, all 422.
13. **Temporal conflicts.** Slot overlap, minimum gap, and return-date readiness, all 409.
14. **Capacity, quota and fiscal limits.** Daily minute limits, caseload ceilings, extension counts, and selector emptiness, all 422.

Within tier 8 two orderings are themselves contractual. When a certification is submitted against a case, the check for an already-pending certification runs before the case from-state check, so a second submission while the case sits in `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW` surfaces as `ERR_CERT_ALREADY_PENDING` rather than as `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. When a case is reconciled, the already-reconciled check runs before the generic funding-state check, so a case whose `funding_status` is already `RECONCILED` surfaces as `ERR_FUNDING_ALREADY_RECONCILED` rather than as `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_ACCRUING`; the generic code is reserved for a `funding_status` of `NOT_REPORTED`, `HELD` or `FORFEITED`.

### 2.7. Expected-Version Preconditions on the Case Record

Three creation operations hang new commitments off a `HomeboundCase` whose own certified window, entitlement and posture are themselves rewritten by other operations. Each of the three therefore requires the client to declare which revision of the case it observed, through a non-optional body field named `expected_case_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `HomeboundCase`. If `expected_case_version` does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written.

- Certification submission (`POST /hhi/certifications`) requires `expected_case_version`. Acceptance of a certification legitimately supersedes the case's prior current certification and rewrites the case's `diagnosis_category`, `certified_start_date`, `certified_end_date` and `weekly_entitled_minutes`. A submitter working from a stale view of the case would re-anchor an eligibility window it never observed.
- Extension request creation (`POST /hhi/extension-requests`) requires `expected_case_version`. An approved extension shifts `certified_end_date` forward from whatever value the case currently holds, and that value is itself rewritten by certification acceptance and by any earlier approved extension, so a stale view would derive the new window from a superseded baseline.
- Transition plan creation (`POST /hhi/transition-plans`) requires `expected_case_version`. A new plan supersedes prior planning state and moves the case out of `ACTIVE`, so a planner whose view predates an intervening certification acceptance, extension decision or funding change would commit a return date derived from a window that no longer holds.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Every other write proceeds on the current state of its target.

### 2.8. Minute Arithmetic, Rounding and Precision

Instructional quantities in this system are whole minutes. Wherever a computation produces a fractional minute, the result is rounded half up — a value of exactly one half rounds away from zero — and the rounding is applied at the specific point each rule names, never earlier and never twice.

Three rounding granularities are in use. Plain whole-minute rounding half up applies to the diagnosis-adjusted entitlement figure, to the special-education floor product, to the recapture amount, and to the risk-score discount. Rounding half up to the nearest multiple of five minutes applies to per-session instructional credit and to each support teacher's weekly allocation; a value is divided by five, rounded half up to a whole number, and multiplied back by five. Finally, `delivery_ratio_pct` is a decimal number carried to exactly two decimal places, rounded half up, and every threshold comparison against it uses that two-decimal value rather than any longer intermediate.

Where a rule counts weeks it says which direction it truncates. Counts of *completed* weeks use floor, so a partial week contributes nothing. Counts of *spanned* weeks use ceiling, so a partial week counts in full. Both forms are spelled out in §3.7.

### 2.9. Parallel Status Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

A `HomeboundCase` carries two independent status dimensions that advance separately and must never be conflated. `service_status` is the instructional lifecycle: `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE`, `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING`, `TRANSITION_PLANNED`, `CLOSED`, or `DENIED`, beginning at `DRAFT` and terminating at `CLOSED` or `DENIED`. `funding_status` is the apportionment posture: `NOT_REPORTED`, `ACCRUING`, `HELD`, `RECONCILED`, or `FORFEITED`, beginning at `NOT_REPORTED` and terminating at `FORFEITED`. A case can be actively served while its funding is held, and a case can be closed while its funding is still accruing.

The server is expected to move records that the client did not name. When a rule's inputs change, the server recomputes the derived field, re-evaluates any guard that reads it, and applies the resulting transition inside the same request — the client sees the settled result, not an intermediate one. This applies to a case's funding hold firing from a recomputed service gap, to a transition plan's readiness firing from recomputed acknowledgement counters, to a teacher's committed minutes and over-cap flag being re-summed after any allocation change, and to sibling records being superseded when a newer record of the same kind takes over. Each such record independently refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

Recomputations are always full recomputations from the current input set, never incremental deltas applied to a stored running figure. A teacher's committed minutes are re-summed across that teacher's active assignments; a case's reported apportionment is re-summed across that case's entire attendance ledger. This makes an accept-then-release pair, or a log-then-void pair, exactly conservative: the second operation restores the figure the first one changed.

### 2.10. The Entitlement Rule Catalogue

Instructional-hours entitlement is not hard-coded; it is configured. An `EntitlementRule` is a district-level row keyed by a `grade_band` — one of `PRIMARY`, `MIDDLE`, or `HIGH` — paired with a `delivery_mode` — one of `IN_HOME`, `VIRTUAL_SYNC`, or `HYBRID`. Each rule declares `base_weekly_minutes`, the weekly instructional minutes before any diagnosis or duration adjustment, which must be greater than zero; `cap_weekly_minutes`, the hard weekly ceiling applied after every adjustment, which must be at least `base_weekly_minutes`; `min_session_minutes`, the shortest permitted single session, greater than zero; `max_session_minutes`, the longest permitted single session and simultaneously the per-session credit ceiling, at least `min_session_minutes`; `max_daily_minutes`, the largest aggregate scheduled minutes permitted for one student on one service date, at least `max_session_minutes`; `min_gap_minutes`, the minimum required separation between two sessions of the same case on one service date, not negative; and `travel_buffer_minutes`, the buffer extended on both ends of an in-home session when checking a teacher's slot conflicts, not negative.

A rule's `status` is either `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED` and defaults to `PUBLISHED`. Only a `PUBLISHED` rule is ever selectable for an entitlement computation, and at most one `PUBLISHED` rule may exist for any given grade-band-and-delivery-mode pair; publishing a second one for the same pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTITLEMENT_RULE`. A `ProgramAdministrator` retires a rule to withdraw it from future resolution; retiring a rule whose `status` is already `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_RULE_ALREADY_RETIRED`. Retirement does not disturb the `entitlement_rule_id` already recorded on existing cases — those cases continue to read the rule they resolved to — but no future resolution will select it. When a case's entitlement must be resolved and no `PUBLISHED` rule matches the pair, the operation fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_ENTITLEMENT_RULE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Students, Teachers, and Opening a Case

A `ProgramAdministrator` registers each district student before any case can be opened. Registration supplies a `student_number`, which must be unique across all students and between one and thirty-two characters long — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_NUMBER` — together with the student's legal `full_name`, a `grade_level` from 0 through 12 inclusive where 0 denotes kindergarten, and a `campus_code` naming the home campus used for classroom coordination. Three further fields are optional at registration. `special_ed_status` describes the special-education posture at referral time and is one of `NONE`, `IEP`, or `SECTION_504`, defaulting to `NONE`. `iep_service_minutes_weekly` records the weekly specialized service minutes the IEP requires; it may not be negative, defaults to 0, must be exactly 0 when `special_ed_status` is `NONE`, and must be greater than 0 when `special_ed_status` is `IEP`. `enrollment_status` is either `ENROLLED` or `WITHDRAWN` and defaults to `ENROLLED`.

From `grade_level` the server derives `grade_band`, which is the key the entitlement catalogue is looked up by: `PRIMARY` when `grade_level` is 5 or below, `MIDDLE` when `grade_level` is 6, 7 or 8, and `HIGH` when `grade_level` is 9 or above. Enrollment posture is maintained separately after registration; a `ProgramAdministrator` may withdraw or re-enroll a student by supplying a new `enrollment_status`, and that change increments the student's `version` by one.

Teachers are registered by the same role and serve in both the itinerant and the classroom capacity. A teacher record carries `full_name`, an `employee_number` unique across all teachers, a non-empty `credential_areas` array whose members are drawn from `MATH`, `ELA`, `SCIENCE`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES`, `GENERAL_ELEMENTARY`, and `SPED`, and a non-empty `grade_bands` array whose members are drawn from `PRIMARY`, `MIDDLE`, and `HIGH` naming which bands this teacher may serve. `max_weekly_caseload_minutes`, which must be greater than zero, is the contractual weekly ceiling on committed homebound instruction minutes. `safety_clearance_status` is one of `CLEARED`, `RESTRICTED`, or `EXPIRED` and defaults to `CLEARED`; `clearance_expires_on` is the date the clearance lapses; and `home_visit_eligible`, defaulting to true, states whether this teacher may perform in-home visits at all. At registration `committed_weekly_minutes` initializes to 0 and `over_cap` initializes to false. A `ProgramAdministrator` may later revise a teacher's `safety_clearance_status`, `clearance_expires_on` and `home_visit_eligible`, which increments that teacher's `version` by one.

Opening a case is a `CaseManager` action. The payload names the `student_id` of the student receiving instruction, the `referral_date` on which the homebound referral was received, the requested `delivery_mode` of `IN_HOME`, `VIRTUAL_SYNC` or `HYBRID`, and optionally a free-text `referral_note` carrying the referral context. The new case starts with `service_status` `DRAFT` and `funding_status` `NOT_REPORTED`, and the server stamps `created_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — a value that later governs who may verify this case's sessions. A case may only be opened for an enrolled student; a `WITHDRAWN` student is rejected with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. A student may hold only one live case at a time: if the student already has a case whose `service_status` is anything other than `CLOSED` or `DENIED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_CASE_EXISTS`.

A case that never reaches eligibility can be shut down by a `ProgramAdministrator`. Denial is permitted only while the case's `service_status` is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW`; from either of those the case moves to `DENIED`, and from any other state the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Denial sets `close_reason` to `DENIED_ELIGIBILITY` and, as a cascade, marks every certification of that case whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` as `REJECTED`. An optional `deny_note` may accompany the request. The case's `version` increments by one.

### 3.2. Physician Certification and the Path to Eligibility

Eligibility rests entirely on a physician's certification of expected absence duration and diagnosis category. A `CaseManager` submits one against a `DRAFT` case, which advances `service_status` from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW` and increments the case's `version` by one. The payload carries the `case_id` being supported, a `physician_npi` of exactly ten digits, a `diagnosis_category` from `MEDICAL_ACUTE`, `MEDICAL_CHRONIC`, `PSYCHIATRIC`, `PREGNANCY_POSTPARTUM`, and `INJURY_REHAB`, the `certified_start_date` and inclusive `certified_end_date` bounding the certified absence window, an `expected_absence_days` count of at least 1 stating the physician's expected absence duration in calendar days, the `signature_date` on which the physician signed, the `expected_case_version` guard described in §2.7, and optionally a free-text `clinical_note`. Three preconditions govern submission: the student's `enrollment_status` must be `ENROLLED`, or the request fails with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`; the supplied `expected_case_version` must equal the case's current `version`; and no other certification of the same case may still be sitting at `SUBMITTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_CERT_ALREADY_PENDING`. The window itself is checked too: `certified_end_date` may not precede `certified_start_date`, `signature_date` may not fall later than the clock date, and `signature_date` may not fall earlier than thirty days before `certified_start_date`. Any of those three failures is `ERR_CERT_WINDOW_INVALID`. A newly submitted certification starts with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `is_current` false, and `submitted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Only a `NurseReviewer` may review a certification, and only one whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` — reviewing anything else is `ERR_CERT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`. The review supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and optionally a `review_note`, and in both cases the reviewer's `X-User-ID` is stamped as `reviewed_by_user_id`. The case's `version` increments by one either way.

Rejection is a revision cycle, not a terminal denial. The certification moves from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, its `is_current` stays false, and the case's `service_status` returns from `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW` back to `DRAFT` so that a corrected certification can be submitted.

Acceptance is the pivot of the whole program. It requires that the certification's `certified_end_date` not precede its `certified_start_date` and that `expected_absence_days` be at least 10 — a shorter expected absence is rejected with `ERR_ABSENCE_DURATION_TOO_SHORT`, on the reasoning that a brief absence is a matter for the campus rather than for the homebound program. On acceptance the certification's `status` becomes `ACCEPTED` and its `is_current` becomes true, and any certification of the same case that previously held `is_current` true is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with its `is_current` set false; exactly one accepted certification per case is current at any moment. The case then advances from `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW` to `ELIGIBLE` and absorbs the accepted document: the case's `diagnosis_category`, `certified_start_date` and `certified_end_date` are copied from the accepted certification, and `certification_count` is recomputed as the number of that case's certifications whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Next the case's `entitlement_rule_id` is resolved by matching the student's `grade_band` and the case's `delivery_mode` against the `PUBLISHED` entitlement catalogue, failing with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_ENTITLEMENT_RULE` if no such rule exists. With a rule in hand the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` is computed as described in §3.3, and finally the weekly allocation is recomputed across that case's active teacher assignments, as described in §3.4. Before any certification has been accepted the case's `diagnosis_category` is null and its `weekly_entitled_minutes` is 0.

### 3.3. Computing the Weekly Instructional-Hours Entitlement

`weekly_entitled_minutes` is the single number every downstream rule leans on: it caps weekly credit, it drives the teacher allocation, it sets the service-gap breach threshold, and it defines the entitled total at reconciliation. It is computed at exactly three moments — when a certification is accepted, when a safety review finalizes to a virtual-only clearance and thereby changes the case's delivery mode, and when an extension request is approved — and it is computed the same way every time.

Start from the single `PUBLISHED` entitlement rule matching the student's `grade_band` and the case's `delivery_mode`. Retired rules never participate. Take that rule's `base_weekly_minutes` and multiply it by a diagnosis factor determined by the case's `diagnosis_category`: the factor is 1.00 for `MEDICAL_ACUTE`, 1.00 for `MEDICAL_CHRONIC`, 0.80 for `PSYCHIATRIC`, 0.90 for `PREGNANCY_POSTPARTUM`, and 1.00 for `INJURY_REHAB`. Round that product half up to a whole number of minutes.

Next determine the duration bonus. The effective absence duration is the current accepted certification's `expected_absence_days` plus the sum of `requested_additional_days` across every extension request of this case whose `status` is `APPROVED` — all of them, with no time window. An effective duration of at least 60 calendar days earns a bonus of 60 minutes; failing that, an effective duration of at least 30 calendar days earns a bonus of 30 minutes; a shorter effective duration earns no bonus. The bonus is added to the rounded diagnosis-adjusted figure; call the sum the adjusted entitlement. The bonus is not itself rounded and is not multiplied by the diagnosis factor.

Special-education students carry a floor. When the student's `special_ed_status` is `IEP`, the floor is the student's `iep_service_minutes_weekly` multiplied by 0.60 and rounded half up to a whole minute; for any other special-education status the floor is 0. The entitlement is then the larger of the adjusted entitlement and the floor, and that larger value is finally clamped down to the rule's `cap_weekly_minutes` if it exceeds it. In other words: take the greater of adjusted entitlement and floor, then take the lesser of that result and the cap. Both roundings — the diagnosis product and the floor product — happen once each, before the bonus is added and before the floor comparison and the cap clamp are applied.

### 3.4. Staffing the Case and the Weekly Minute Allocation

A `CaseManager` proposes a teacher for a case by naming the `case_id` and a `role` of `PRIMARY`, `SUPPORT`, or `SPED_SERVICE`. A new assignment starts with `status` `PROPOSED` and `weekly_committed_minutes` 0. A case may hold only one primary staffing commitment: if another assignment of the same case has `role` `PRIMARY` and `status` either `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`, the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIMARY_ASSIGNMENT`. Unlike sessions, coordination rows and continuation logs, an assignment is not gated on the case being in service — staffing necessarily happens before activation, because activation itself demands an active primary assignment.

The `teacher_id` field is optional, and omitting it is meaningful. When the caller names a `teacher_id` explicitly, the only cross-record check applied is that the named teacher's `grade_bands` array contains the student's `grade_band`; a mismatch is `ERR_TEACHER_BAND_MISMATCH`. When the caller omits `teacher_id`, the server runs a selector policy and writes the winner into `teacher_id`. The candidate set is every teacher who satisfies all four eligibility conditions: the teacher's `safety_clearance_status` is `CLEARED`, except that when the case's `delivery_mode` is `VIRTUAL_SYNC` either `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED` is acceptable; the teacher's `grade_bands` contains the student's `grade_band`; the teacher's `home_visit_eligible` is true whenever the case's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` or `HYBRID`; and the teacher's current `committed_weekly_minutes` plus the allocation this role would receive on this case does not exceed that teacher's `max_weekly_caseload_minutes`. Candidates are ranked by `committed_weekly_minutes` ascending, so the least-loaded eligible teacher wins, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. When the candidate set is empty the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TEACHER`.

Acceptance is the assigned teacher's own act. A `HomeboundTeacher` whose `X-User-ID` equals the assignment's `teacher_id` accepts a `PROPOSED` assignment, moving it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and stamping `accepted_at` with the clock instant. Accepting anything not in `PROPOSED` is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_PROPOSED`. A teacher whose `safety_clearance_status` is `EXPIRED` may not accept at all, and that rejection carries `ERR_TEACHER_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`. The caseload ceiling is enforced at acceptance: if the teacher's `committed_weekly_minutes` plus the allocation this assignment would receive would exceed `max_weekly_caseload_minutes`, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_TEACHER_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED`. On a successful acceptance the case's weekly allocation is recomputed across its active assignments including the new one, and every teacher whose allocation changed has `committed_weekly_minutes` and `over_cap` recomputed.

Declining is the mirror action, also restricted to the assigned teacher and also only from `PROPOSED`. A decline supplies a free-text `decline_reason`, moves the assignment `PROPOSED → DECLINED`, and sets `weekly_committed_minutes` to 0.

Release is a `ProgramAdministrator` action against an `ACTIVE` assignment; releasing anything else is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An assignment may not be released while it still has instructional work on the books: if any session referencing it has `status` `SCHEDULED`, the release is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_HAS_SCHEDULED_SESSIONS`. A release records a `release_reason` from `CASE_CLOSED`, `MODE_CHANGE`, `CASELOAD_REBALANCE`, `CLEARANCE_LAPSED`, and `STAFF_LEAVE`, moves the assignment `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` with the clock instant, sets its `weekly_committed_minutes` to 0, recomputes the allocation across the case's remaining active assignments, and recomputes `committed_weekly_minutes` and `over_cap` for every teacher whose allocation changed.

The allocation itself distributes the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` across its active assignments and nothing else. Only assignments whose `status` is `ACTIVE` participate; `PROPOSED`, `DECLINED` and `RELEASED` assignments are excluded and always hold `weekly_committed_minutes` 0. The participating rows are ordered by role rank — primary first, support second, special-education service third — and within a rank by `created_at` ascending. Support staff share thirty percent of the entitlement: when the case has at least one active support assignment, multiply `weekly_entitled_minutes` by 0.30, divide by the number of active support assignments, divide by five, round half up to a whole number, and multiply back by five; every active support assignment receives that same per-support figure, and the support total is that figure multiplied by the number of support assignments. When the case has no active support assignment the support total is 0. Every active special-education-service assignment always receives 0. The primary assignment nominally carries the remaining seventy percent, and concretely it receives the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` minus the support total — so the primary row absorbs the entire rounding residual and the sum of `weekly_committed_minutes` across active assignments equals `weekly_entitled_minutes` exactly. When a case has no active primary assignment the allocation writes nothing and every active assignment keeps 0. The allocation is recomputed on assignment acceptance, on assignment release, on certification acceptance, on extension approval, and on a safety finalization that forces a virtual delivery mode.

A teacher's own `committed_weekly_minutes` is the sum of `weekly_committed_minutes` across every assignment of that teacher whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, across every case, with no time window. It is recomputed as a complete re-sum rather than an incremental adjustment whenever an allocation changes, which is what makes accept-and-release pairs exactly conservative. The derived flag `over_cap` is true when `committed_weekly_minutes` is strictly greater than that teacher's `max_weekly_caseload_minutes`, and false when the two are equal.

### 3.5. Home Visit Safety Review and the Virtual Alternative

Before anyone is sent into a student's home, the home environment is reviewed. A `CaseManager` records a review against a `case_id`, declaring the `evidence_source` as either `PARENT_ATTESTATION` or `ONSITE_WALKTHROUGH`, the `review_date` on which the review was conducted, optionally a `hazard_findings` array of distinct hazard codes drawn from `AGGRESSIVE_ANIMAL`, `NO_SECONDARY_EXIT`, `WEAPON_PRESENT_UNSECURED`, `SMOKE_OR_AIR_QUALITY`, `ACTIVE_INFECTION_RISK`, `UNLIT_ACCESS`, and `NO_ADULT_PRESENT` and defaulting to an empty array, and optionally `escort_available` stating whether a district escort can accompany the teacher, defaulting to false. Duplicate members in `hazard_findings` are a payload-shape violation. The new review starts with `status` `DRAFT` and `clearance` `PENDING`. From `evidence_source` the server derives `scoring_variant`: `SELF_REPORTED` when the evidence source is `PARENT_ATTESTATION`, and `ONSITE_VERIFIED` when it is `ONSITE_WALKTHROUGH`. The server also derives `expires_on` as the `review_date` advanced by sixty days, and computes an initial `risk_score`.

The risk score begins as the sum of hazard weights over the review's distinct findings: `AGGRESSIVE_ANIMAL` weighs 25 points, `NO_SECONDARY_EXIT` 15, `WEAPON_PRESENT_UNSECURED` 40, `SMOKE_OR_AIR_QUALITY` 10, `ACTIVE_INFECTION_RISK` 30, `UNLIT_ACCESS` 5, and `NO_ADULT_PRESENT` 20. What happens next depends on the scoring variant. Under `SELF_REPORTED`, the raw sum is multiplied by 0.75, rounded half up to a whole point, and then capped at 100; `escort_available` is ignored entirely, on the reasoning that a parent attestation cannot vouch for an escort arrangement. Under `ONSITE_VERIFIED`, the raw sum is used undiscounted, a flat 10 points of escort mitigation is subtracted when `escort_available` is true and nothing is subtracted when it is false, the result is floored at 0, and it is capped at 100. The two variants genuinely diverge: the finding set of `AGGRESSIVE_ANIMAL`, `NO_SECONDARY_EXIT` and `UNLIT_ACCESS` with `escort_available` true sums to 45 raw and scores 34 as `SELF_REPORTED` but 35 as `ONSITE_VERIFIED`, which lands the two on opposite sides of a clearance band.

`clearance` is banded from `risk_score` with inclusive lower bounds: `BLOCKED` at 60 points or above, otherwise `VIRTUAL_ONLY` at 35 points or above, otherwise `CLEARED_WITH_ESCORT` at 15 points or above, otherwise `CLEARED`. While a review's `status` is `DRAFT` its `clearance` reads `PENDING` regardless of the computed score.

Only a finalized review governs anything. A `CaseManager` finalizes a `DRAFT` review — finalizing one that is not `DRAFT` is `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_FINALIZED` — and finalization is the authoritative re-score: the `risk_score` is recomputed from the same inputs and the `clearance` is re-banded, the review moves `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, its `finalized_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and any other review of the same case whose `status` was `FINALIZED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. A superseded review no longer governs scheduling; the newer finalized review does. Finalization refreshes the case's `escort_required`, setting it true when the resulting `clearance` is `CLEARED_WITH_ESCORT` and false otherwise, and increments the case's `version` by one.

A `VIRTUAL_ONLY` outcome forces the case onto a virtual alternative, and the cascade is extensive. When the resulting `clearance` is `VIRTUAL_ONLY` and the case's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` or `HYBRID`, the case's `delivery_mode` is rewritten to `VIRTUAL_SYNC`; its `entitlement_rule_id` is re-resolved against the student's `grade_band` paired with `VIRTUAL_SYNC`, failing with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_ENTITLEMENT_RULE` when no published rule covers that pair; its `weekly_entitled_minutes` is recomputed per §3.3; the weekly allocation is recomputed across the case's active assignments; `committed_weekly_minutes` and `over_cap` are recomputed for every affected teacher; and every session of that case whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose own `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` is moved to `CANCELLED_ADMIN`. A `VIRTUAL_ONLY` finalization on a case already in `VIRTUAL_SYNC` leaves the delivery mode alone.

### 3.6. Activation, Session Scheduling, Delivery and Attendance Coding

Activation is where a case starts consuming instruction and generating a funding claim, and a `ProgramAdministrator` performs it against an `ELIGIBLE` case. Four preconditions apply. Exactly one certification of the case must carry `is_current` true with `status` `ACCEPTED`. The student's `enrollment_status` must still be `ENROLLED`, or the activation fails with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. At least one assignment of the case must have `role` `PRIMARY` and `status` `ACTIVE`, or the activation fails with `ERR_NO_PRIMARY_ASSIGNMENT`. And when the case's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` or `HYBRID`, a review of that case must exist with `status` `FINALIZED` and `clearance` either `CLEARED` or `CLEARED_WITH_ESCORT`: no finalized review at all is `ERR_SAFETY_REVIEW_MISSING`, and a finalized review whose `clearance` is `BLOCKED` is `ERR_SAFETY_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED`. Activation advances `service_status` `ELIGIBLE → ACTIVE`, sets `service_start_date` to the clock date, refreshes `escort_required` from the governing finalized review, moves `funding_status` `NOT_REPORTED → ACCRUING` in the same request with `service_gap_minutes` initialized to 0, and increments the case's `version` by one. Attempting to activate from any state other than `ELIGIBLE` is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

Scheduling a session is a `CaseManager` action naming the `case_id`, the `assignment_id` of the active assignment that will serve it, the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` instants, the session's own `delivery_mode` of either `IN_HOME` or `VIRTUAL_SYNC`, the `subject` from `MATH`, `ELA`, `SCIENCE`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES`, and `GENERAL_ELEMENTARY`, and optionally `escort_confirmed`. `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start` and both must fall on the same UTC calendar date. From those two instants the server derives `scheduled_minutes` as the whole-minute span between them and `service_date` as the UTC calendar date of `scheduled_start`. A new session starts with `status` `SCHEDULED` and `credited_minutes` 0. Sessions may only be created against a case whose `service_status` is `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING` or `TRANSITION_PLANNED`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The referenced assignment must have `status` `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and its own `case_id` must equal the `case_id` supplied for the session, or the request fails with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CASE_MISMATCH`. The `service_date` must fall inside the case's certified window, on or after `certified_start_date` and on or before `certified_end_date`; outside that window the request is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_CERT_WINDOW`.

The safety and compliance rules that guard scheduling are the same rules described in §3.5, applied at the moment a visit is put on the calendar. An in-home session requires a finalized review for the case, absent which the request is `ERR_SAFETY_REVIEW_MISSING`. That finalized review's `expires_on` must be on or after the session's `service_date`; an in-home session scheduled beyond the sixty-day validity of the review is rejected with `ERR_SAFETY_REVIEW_EXPIRED`. A finalized review whose `clearance` is `BLOCKED` blocks the schedule with `ERR_SAFETY_CLEARANCE_BLOCKED`. A session `delivery_mode` of `IN_HOME` is refused outright with `ERR_IN_HOME_NOT_PERMITTED` when the case's own `delivery_mode` is `VIRTUAL_SYNC` or when the governing finalized review's `clearance` is `VIRTUAL_ONLY` — which is also why a session's `delivery_mode` must be `VIRTUAL_SYNC` whenever the case's is. The serving teacher must not have `safety_clearance_status` `EXPIRED`, and must have `home_visit_eligible` true whenever the session's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME`; either failure is `ERR_TEACHER_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`. Finally, when the case's `escort_required` is true and the session's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME`, the payload must supply `escort_confirmed` as true, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED_NOT_CONFIRMED`. All six of these safety rules apply identically to ordinary scheduling and to make-up scheduling, and the first three plus the clearance-blocked rule also guard activation as noted above.

Three more scheduling constraints read the resolved entitlement rule. The session's `scheduled_minutes` must lie between the rule's `min_session_minutes` and `max_session_minutes`, inclusive on both ends; outside that range the request is `ERR_SESSION_DURATION_INVALID`. The serving teacher may not be double-booked: the new session's busy interval must not overlap the busy interval of any other session of the same teacher whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED`. A busy interval is the half-open span from `scheduled_start` up to but not including `scheduled_end`, extended on both ends by the rule's `travel_buffer_minutes` when that particular session's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` and extended by nothing at all when it is `VIRTUAL_SYNC`. An overlap is `ERR_TEACHER_SLOT_CONFLICT`. And two sessions of the same case on the same `service_date` must be separated by at least the rule's `min_gap_minutes`, measured as the closed-interval gap against every existing session of that case on that date whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED`: a gap exactly equal to `min_gap_minutes` passes, while any smaller gap — including endpoints that merely touch — fails with `ERR_SESSION_GAP_TOO_SHORT`. Finally, the student's day is capped: the sum of `scheduled_minutes` over the case's sessions with `status` `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` on the same `service_date`, plus the new session's own `scheduled_minutes`, may not exceed the rule's `max_daily_minutes`; a total exactly equal to `max_daily_minutes` passes, and any excess is `ERR_STUDENT_DAILY_LIMIT`.

Logging the outcome belongs to the serving teacher. A `HomeboundTeacher` whose `X-User-ID` equals the `teacher_id` of the session's assignment supplies an `outcome` and, depending on it, `actual_minutes`, an `absence_kind`, or a free-text `outcome_reason` recording why the visit did not deliver instruction. Logging is permitted only from `SCHEDULED`, and logging against a session in any other status is `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_LOGGED`. The case must be in service — `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING` or `TRANSITION_PLANNED` — and the clock date must be on or after the session's `service_date`, so a visit cannot be logged before it was due. Four outcomes exist and each writes exactly one attendance ledger row.

A `DELIVERED` outcome requires `actual_minutes` of at least 1 and advances the session `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED`. `credited_minutes` is computed from the rule the case's `entitlement_rule_id` names: take the lesser of the reported `actual_minutes` and the rule's `max_session_minutes`; multiply that capped figure by 0.90 when the session's `delivery_mode` is `VIRTUAL_SYNC` and leave it unchanged when the mode is `IN_HOME`; then divide by five, round half up to a whole number, and multiply back by five. The rounding happens once, per session, at logging time, before the value reaches the ledger; no cross-session aggregation participates. The session's `logged_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. A ledger row is appended carrying `attendance_code` `HB_PRESENT` when the session's `delivery_mode` is `IN_HOME` and `HB_VIRTUAL_PRESENT` when it is `VIRTUAL_SYNC`, with `apportionment_minutes` equal to the session's `credited_minutes`.

A `STUDENT_UNAVAILABLE` outcome requires the payload to carry an `absence_kind` of either `MEDICAL` or `UNEXCUSED`, advances the session `SCHEDULED → STUDENT_UNAVAILABLE`, sets `credited_minutes` to 0, and appends a ledger row with `attendance_code` `HB_ABSENT_MEDICAL` when `absence_kind` is `MEDICAL` and `HB_ABSENT_UNEXCUSED` when it is `UNEXCUSED`, with `apportionment_minutes` 0. A `TEACHER_CANCELLED` outcome advances the session `SCHEDULED → TEACHER_CANCELLED`, sets `credited_minutes` to 0, and appends a ledger row with `attendance_code` `HB_NOT_SERVED` and `apportionment_minutes` 0. A `SAFETY_ABORTED` outcome advances the session `SCHEDULED → SAFETY_ABORTED`, sets `credited_minutes` to 0, and likewise appends a `HB_NOT_SERVED` row with `apportionment_minutes` 0. All three of these non-delivery outcomes add the session's `scheduled_minutes` to the case's `makeup_obligation_minutes`.

After any of the four outcomes the case's `reported_apportionment_minutes` is recomputed as the sum of `apportionment_minutes` across that case's entire attendance ledger, its `service_gap_minutes` is recomputed per §3.7 — which may itself move the case's funding posture — and its `makeup_obligation_minutes` is recomputed. The case's `version` increments by one. Concretely, `makeup_obligation_minutes` is the sum of `scheduled_minutes` over that case's sessions whose `status` is `STUDENT_UNAVAILABLE`, `TEACHER_CANCELLED` or `SAFETY_ABORTED` and which are not yet pointed at by a `DELIVERED` make-up session. Every ledger row also records `recorded_by_user_id` as the `X-User-ID` of the actor whose action produced it, along with a `service_date` and `delivery_mode` copied from the session that produced it and the `session_id` and `case_id` identifying it.

A make-up session is scheduled by a `CaseManager` against a missed session, and the new session carries `makeup_for_session_id` pointing at the missed one. Its payload supplies `assignment_id`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `delivery_mode`, `subject` and optionally `escort_confirmed`; the `case_id` is taken from the missed session, and the referenced assignment's own `case_id` must equal it or the request fails with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CASE_MISMATCH`. The session being made up must have `status` `STUDENT_UNAVAILABLE`, `TEACHER_CANCELLED` or `SAFETY_ABORTED`, and it must not already be referenced by another session's `makeup_for_session_id`; either failure is `ERR_MAKEUP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Every scheduling check listed above — duration, teacher slot conflict, case minimum gap, daily limit, certified window, safety review presence and expiry, blocked clearance, in-home permission, teacher clearance, escort confirmation, active assignment, and in-service case — applies to a make-up session identically. The new session starts at `SCHEDULED` and the case's `makeup_obligation_minutes` is recomputed.

Verification is the second-person control on delivered instruction, and it is shared between `ProgramAdministrator` and `CaseManager`. Verification is permitted only against a session whose `status` is `DELIVERED` — anything else is `ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERED` — and only once: a session that already carries `verified_at` is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. Here segregation of duties bites: the verifying `X-User-ID` must differ from the `created_by_user_id` of the session's case, and a self-verification is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful verification stamps `verified_at` with the clock instant and `verified_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Only verified sessions ever enter the final apportionment described in §3.7.

Two administrative corrections round out the session lifecycle. A `ProgramAdministrator` may cancel a `SCHEDULED` session, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED_ADMIN`; no ledger row is written and the case's `makeup_obligation_minutes` is unchanged. Cancelling a session that is not `SCHEDULED` is `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_LOGGED`. Administrative cancellation also arrives as a cascade from case closure, from transition-plan completion, and from a virtual-only safety finalization. Separately, a `ProgramAdministrator` may void a `DELIVERED` session, recording a `void_reason` of `DOCUMENTATION_DEFECT`, `DUPLICATE_ENTRY`, `AUDIT_REVERSAL`, or `MODE_INELIGIBLE`; voiding a session that is not `DELIVERED` is `ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERED`. Voiding moves the session `DELIVERED → VOIDED`, sets its `credited_minutes` to 0, and clears both `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id`. It appends a compensating ledger row with `attendance_code` `HB_REVERSAL`, whose `reversal_of_entry_id` points at the entry being reversed and whose `apportionment_minutes` is the arithmetic negation of that entry's `apportionment_minutes`. The entry being reversed is the one with the largest `created_at` among that session's ledger rows whose `attendance_code` is `HB_PRESENT` or `HB_VIRTUAL_PRESENT`. Because the reversal is exactly the negation, the case's `reported_apportionment_minutes` — recomputed net of the reversal — returns precisely to its pre-log value. The case's `service_gap_minutes` is then recomputed, which may pull the funding posture into a hold from either `ACCRUING` or `RECONCILED`, and the case's `version` increments by one.

### 3.7. Funding: Accrual, Service Gaps, Holds and Reconciliation

The funding dimension of a case begins at `NOT_REPORTED` and moves to `ACCRUING` at the moment the case is activated. From there it is governed by one derived quantity and two reporting operations.

The derived quantity is `service_gap_minutes`, which answers "how far behind is this case against what it owed by now." Count the completed service weeks as the number of whole seven-day spans between the case's `service_start_date` and the clock date, taking the floor so a partial week contributes nothing. Multiply that count by the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` to get the minutes expected to date. Subtract the case's `reported_apportionment_minutes` from that expectation and floor the difference at 0; the result is `service_gap_minutes`. The `reported_apportionment_minutes` input is the running sum over every one of that case's attendance ledger rows with no time window, and because reversal rows carry negative `apportionment_minutes`, they reduce it. A case that has never been activated — one whose `service_status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERT_REVIEW`, `ELIGIBLE` or `DENIED`, and whose `service_start_date` is therefore null — always holds `service_gap_minutes` 0.

`service_gap_minutes` drives a threshold trigger on the funding dimension. When the recomputed gap reaches or exceeds exactly one weekly entitlement — that is, when `service_gap_minutes` is greater than or equal to `weekly_entitled_minutes` — and the case's `funding_status` is `ACCRUING` or `RECONCILED`, the funding posture moves to `HELD`. When the recomputed gap falls back below one weekly entitlement and the case's `funding_status` is `HELD`, the posture returns to `ACCRUING`. This recomputation runs after every attendance ledger write — delivery, student unavailability, teacher cancellation, safety abort, and void — and after every provisional funding report. Entering `HELD` carries a conditional cascade into campus coordination: every pacing coordination row of that case whose `ack_status` is `ACKED` is reset to `PENDING` with `acked_at` cleared, while rows already at `DISPUTED` are left untouched, and any transition plan of that case whose `status` is `READY` falls back to `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING`. Leaving `HELD` for `ACCRUING` refreshes the case's `updated_at` but does *not* automatically re-acknowledge anything: the classroom teachers must acknowledge again. The reversal path matters because it is the second way into a hold — an audit void against a case that had already reconciled reopens it, moving the funding posture from `RECONCILED` straight to `HELD` under the same threshold.

A `ProgramAdministrator` reports provisional apportionment while the case is being served. The provisional figure is forward-looking. Its input set is that case's sessions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` and whose `service_date` falls between `service_start_date` and `certified_end_date` inclusive on both ends; sessions that are `VOIDED`, `CANCELLED_ADMIN`, `STUDENT_UNAVAILABLE`, `TEACHER_CANCELLED` or `SAFETY_ABORTED` never participate, and verification is ignored entirely. Each `DELIVERED` session contributes its `credited_minutes`. Each still-`SCHEDULED` session contributes a projected credit computed the same way delivered credit is computed but from the plan rather than from the report: take the lesser of that session's `scheduled_minutes` and the rule's `max_session_minutes`, multiply by 0.90 when the session's `delivery_mode` is `VIRTUAL_SYNC` and by 1.00 when it is `IN_HOME`, divide by five, round half up, and multiply back by five. Those contributions are summed into a raw provisional total, and the total is capped once — over the whole certified window rather than week by week — at the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` multiplied by the number of window weeks, where window weeks is the number of days separating `service_start_date` from `certified_end_date`, plus one, divided by seven and rounded up to a whole week. The result is written to `provisional_apportionment_minutes`. Reporting also recomputes `service_gap_minutes`, which may fire a hold or release one, and increments the case's `version` by one. Reporting is permitted only from the case service states declared for it, and a case in a state that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

Reconciliation at closeout is the same administrator's act and uses a strictly narrower, chronological figure. The final input set is that case's sessions whose `status` is `DELIVERED` *and* which carry a non-null `verified_at`, whose `service_date` falls between `service_start_date` and `certified_end_date` inclusive on both ends. Delivered-but-unverified sessions are excluded from this set even though they counted provisionally; voided, administratively cancelled, student-unavailable, teacher-cancelled and safety-aborted sessions are excluded from both sets. The qualifying sessions are walked in order of `service_date` ascending and, within one service date, `created_at` ascending. Each session is bucketed by the ISO-8601 week — Monday-start — of its `service_date`. For each session in turn, compute the room remaining in its week as the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` minus the minutes already accepted into that week's bucket, floor that room at 0, take the lesser of the session's `credited_minutes` and that floored room, add the taken amount to the week's bucket, and add it to the running accepted total. Any excess above a week's entitlement is dropped, never carried forward into the next week. The running total at the end is the raw final verified figure. Because verification is required and because the cap binds inside each ISO week rather than across the whole window, the same data yields a strictly smaller final figure than the provisional one whenever sessions cluster inside a single ISO week or remain unverified. No further rounding occurs in either variant; the per-session rounding has already been applied.

From that raw final figure the reconciliation derives the settlement. Count elapsed weeks as the number of days separating `service_start_date` from the clock date, plus one, divided by seven and rounded up to a whole week; count window weeks the same way, from `service_start_date` to `certified_end_date`; and take service weeks as the lesser of the two, so a case reconciled early is measured against elapsed time and a case reconciled late is measured against its window. The entitled total is the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes` multiplied by service weeks. `delivery_ratio_pct` is 0.00 when the entitled total is zero, and otherwise the raw final figure divided by the entitled total, rounded half up to two decimal places. The ratio is always computed on the raw final figure before any recapture, never on the net.

The ratio selects one of four bands, all with inclusive lower bounds and all compared against the two-decimal value. A ratio of 0.90 or above recaptures nothing. A ratio below 0.90 but at least 0.75 recaptures ten percent. A ratio below 0.75 but at least 0.50 recaptures twenty-five percent. A ratio below 0.50 forfeits. Note carefully that a ratio of exactly 0.50 lands in the twenty-five-percent band and does not forfeit.

In the three non-forfeit bands the shortfall is the entitled total minus the raw final figure, floored at 0; `final_apportionment_minutes` is set to the raw final figure; `recapture_minutes` is the shortfall multiplied by the band's recapture rate, rounded half up to whole minutes; `net_apportionment_minutes` is the raw final figure minus `recapture_minutes`, floored at 0; and the funding posture moves `ACCRUING → RECONCILED`. In the forfeit band `final_apportionment_minutes` is set to the raw final figure, `recapture_minutes` is set to that same raw final figure, `net_apportionment_minutes` is 0, and the funding posture moves `ACCRUING → FORFEITED`, which is terminal. `delivery_ratio_pct` is written in both cases. Reconciliation requires the case's `funding_status` to be `ACCRUING`, rejecting `NOT_REPORTED`, `HELD` and `FORFEITED` with `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_ACCRUING` and rejecting an already-reconciled case with the more specific `ERR_FUNDING_ALREADY_RECONCILED`, and it requires the case's `service_status` to be `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING`, `TRANSITION_PLANNED` or `CLOSED`, rejecting others with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Reconciliation increments the case's `version` by one.

A read-only funding summary is available to `ProgramAdministrator` and `CaseManager` for any one case, and it exists to let a reviewer see the two apportionment variants side by side before deciding. It returns the `case_id`, the case's `weekly_entitled_minutes`, the `window_weeks` and `service_weeks` counts, the `entitled_total_minutes` those counts imply, the `provisional_minutes` and `final_verified_minutes` figures, a `projected_unrounded_minutes` diagnostic, the case's current `reported_apportionment_minutes`, `service_gap_minutes`, `delivery_ratio_pct`, `recapture_minutes`, `net_apportionment_minutes`, and `funding_status`. The `projected_unrounded_minutes` field is the one place in the system where per-session rounding is *not* applied first: it sums the un-rounded intermediate credit values across all of the case's delivered sessions and applies the round-half-up-to-the-nearest-five step exactly once at the end. It therefore diverges from the sum of `credited_minutes` whenever per-session rounding accumulates — two virtual sessions with `actual_minutes` of 75 each yield `credited_minutes` of 70 apiece for a ledger total of 140, but a `projected_unrounded_minutes` of 135. The authoritative values remain the per-session rounded ones on the ledger; `projected_unrounded_minutes` is a read-model diagnostic only.

### 3.8. Extending the Certified Window

A medical absence often outlasts its first certification, and the program allows at most two extensions per case. A `CaseManager` requests one against an `ACTIVE` case, which advances `service_status` `ACTIVE → EXTENSION_PENDING` and increments the case's `version` by one. The payload names the `case_id`, the `certification_id` of the updated medical documentation backing the request, the `requested_additional_days` beyond the case's current `certified_end_date` — at least 1 and at most 90 calendar days — a free-text `justification`, and the `expected_case_version` guard of §2.7. The referenced certification must belong to the same case, or the request fails with `ERR_CERT_CASE_MISMATCH`, and its `status` must be `ACCEPTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_CERT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The case's `extension_count` — the number of that case's extension requests whose `status` is `APPROVED` — must be less than 2, and a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Requesting an extension from any state other than `ACTIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A new request starts with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `resubmit_count` 0, and `submitted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Review is a `ProgramAdministrator` sequence with a revision loop in the middle. Starting review moves the request `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, refreshing `updated_at` and changing nothing on the case. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the administrator may send the request back for more documentation, recording a required `info_note` and moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`. The case manager then resubmits with newer accepted documentation, supplying a `certification_id` that must belong to the same case and carry `status` `ACCEPTED`; resubmission replaces the request's `certification_id` with that newer accepted certification, increments `resubmit_count` by one, and moves the request `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. This `UNDER_REVIEW ↔ INFO_REQUESTED` loop is bounded: `resubmit_count` must be less than 2 at the moment of resubmission, so a request can cycle back into review at most twice, and a further attempt is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_RESUBMIT_LIMIT`. Any of these four actions attempted from the wrong posture — start-review from anything but `SUBMITTED`, request-info or decide from anything but `UNDER_REVIEW`, resubmit from anything but `INFO_REQUESTED` — is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_STATE_INVALID`.

The decision comes from a `ProgramAdministrator` acting on a request in `UNDER_REVIEW`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `DENIED` and optionally a `decision_note`, against a case whose `service_status` is `EXTENSION_PENDING`. Segregation of duties applies: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's `submitted_by_user_id`, and a self-decision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either decision stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the case `EXTENSION_PENDING → ACTIVE`, and increments the case's `version` by one — the loop `ACTIVE → EXTENSION_PENDING → ACTIVE` is deliberately cyclic, and `extension_count` reaching 2 is what terminates it.

An `APPROVED` decision moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and cascades into the case: `certified_end_date` is advanced by `requested_additional_days` calendar days from its current value, `extension_count` increments by one, `weekly_entitled_minutes` is recomputed per §3.3 — where the newly approved days now feed the effective absence duration and may cross a duration-bonus threshold — the weekly allocation is recomputed across the case's active assignments, and `committed_weekly_minutes` is recomputed for every affected teacher. A `DENIED` decision moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` and returns the case to `ACTIVE` with no change to `certified_end_date` and no other case field change beyond `service_status` and `updated_at`.

### 3.9. Campus Coordination, Service Continuation, and the Return to the Building

Homebound instruction only works if it stays aligned with what the student's classroom is doing. A `CaseManager` opens a coordination row per subject, naming the `case_id`, the `classroom_teacher_id` of the campus classroom teacher of record for that subject, the `subject`, a `pacing_target_unit` naming the unit or chapter the homebound teacher must reach to stay aligned with the classroom, and an `assessment_accommodation` of `NONE`, `EXTENDED_TIME`, `ORAL_ADMINISTRATION`, or `ALTERNATE_SETTING_PROCTOR` agreed for the homebound period. Coordination rows may only be opened against a case whose `service_status` is `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING` or `TRANSITION_PLANNED`, and otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Each subject is coordinated once per case: a second row for the same subject is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PACING_SUBJECT`. A new row starts with `ack_status` `PENDING`, and its creation recomputes the acknowledgement counters of any open transition plan on the case, which may pull a `READY` plan back to `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING`.

The classroom teacher of record responds. A `ClassroomTeacher` whose `X-User-ID` equals the row's `classroom_teacher_id` may acknowledge the pacing target and accommodation, moving `ack_status` to `ACKED` from either `PENDING` or `DISPUTED`, and stamping `acked_at` with the clock instant; that recomputes the plan counters and may advance a plan from `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` to `READY`. The same teacher may instead dispute, but only from `PENDING`, recording a required `dispute_note` and moving `ack_status` `PENDING → DISPUTED`; that too recomputes the counters and may pull a `READY` plan back to `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING`. Acknowledging a row that is already `ACKED`, or disputing a row that is not `PENDING`, is rejected with `ERR_PACING_ALREADY_ACKED`. A row can also be reset without any teacher acting, as described in §3.7: when the parent case's funding posture enters `HELD`, every `ACKED` row of that case reverts to `PENDING` with `acked_at` cleared, while `DISPUTED` rows are left exactly as they are.

Special education services do not pause because a student is at home. A `CaseManager` opens one continuation log per related service, naming the `case_id`, the `service_type` from `SPEECH`, `OT`, `PT`, `COUNSELING`, and `SPECIALIZED_ACADEMIC`, and the `required_weekly_minutes` the IEP demands for that service, which must be greater than zero. Logs may only be opened against a case in service, otherwise `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and each service type is tracked once per case — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SPED_SERVICE_TYPE`. A new log starts with `continuation_status` `CONTINUED` and `delivered_cumulative_minutes` 0.

Delivered related-service minutes are recorded by either a `CaseManager` or a `HomeboundTeacher`, supplying the `minutes` delivered and the `service_date` on which they were delivered. Recording adds `minutes` to `delivered_cumulative_minutes` and sets `last_service_date` to the supplied `service_date`.

`compensatory_minutes_owed` measures what the district still owes the student. Count the weeks elapsed as the number of whole seven-day spans between the case's `service_start_date` and the clock date, taking the floor, and treating the count as 0 when `service_start_date` is null. Multiply that count by the log's `required_weekly_minutes`, subtract `delivered_cumulative_minutes`, and floor the difference at 0. This is recomputed when a log is opened, whenever a delivery is recorded, and on every continuation-status change.

The continuation posture itself is a `CaseManager` decision, supplied as a `to_status` together with a free-text `continuation_reason` recorded on the change. Four transitions are declared and no self-transition is permitted; a requested transition that is not declared for the log's current `continuation_status` is rejected with `ERR_SPED_CONTINUATION_INVALID`. From `CONTINUED` the log may move to `MODIFIED_VIRTUAL`, which records the reason and recomputes `compensatory_minutes_owed`. From either `CONTINUED` or `MODIFIED_VIRTUAL` the log may move to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_IEP`, which freezes `delivered_cumulative_minutes` — while a log is suspended, any attempt to record delivered minutes against it is rejected with `ERR_SPED_SERVICE_SUSPENDED` — while `compensatory_minutes_owed` keeps accruing with each elapsed week, so the suspension window accumulates as a debt. From either `MODIFIED_VIRTUAL` or `SUSPENDED_PENDING_IEP` the log may return to `CONTINUED`, which recomputes `compensatory_minutes_owed` against the elapsed service weeks so that the suspension window is owed back in full.

Coordination and continuation are the two prerequisites for the last act of the case, which is getting the student back into the building. Returning a student to the building is a coordinated event, not a switch. A `CaseManager` opens a plan against an `ACTIVE` case, which advances the case `ACTIVE → TRANSITION_PLANNED` and increments its `version` by one. The payload names the `case_id`, a `target_return_date` on which the student re-enters the building, a `reintegration_mode` of `FULL_DAY`, `PARTIAL_DAY_RAMP`, or `VIRTUAL_BRIDGE`, the `expected_case_version` guard of §2.7, and optionally `ramp_weeks` between 0 and 8 inclusive, defaulting to 0. Ramp weeks must be consistent with the reintegration shape: exactly 0 when `reintegration_mode` is `FULL_DAY`, and at least 1 when it is `PARTIAL_DAY_RAMP` or `VIRTUAL_BRIDGE`. An inconsistent pairing is rejected with `ERR_RAMP_WEEKS_INVALID`. The `target_return_date` must be strictly later than the clock date. Only one plan may be in flight per case: if the case already has a plan whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` or `READY`, the request is refused, and opening a plan from any case state other than `ACTIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A new plan starts with `status` `DRAFT`, and its `required_ack_count` and `classroom_ack_count` are computed on creation.

Those two counters are what make the plan's readiness a compound, derived condition. `required_ack_count` is the number of coordination rows for the plan's case whose `ack_status` is `PENDING`, `ACKED` or `DISPUTED`; `classroom_ack_count` is the number of those rows whose `ack_status` is `ACKED`. Both counters are recomputed with no time window whenever a coordination row is created, acknowledged or disputed, whenever the plan is sent out for coordination, and whenever the parent case's funding posture enters `HELD`.

A `CaseManager` sends a `DRAFT` plan out for classroom coordination, moving it `DRAFT → CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING`. Coordination requires at least one coordination row on the case — a `required_ack_count` of at least 1 — and a case with no coordination rows at all is rejected with `ERR_NO_PACING_COORDINATION`. It also requires that no special-education continuation log on the case be sitting at `SUSPENDED_PENDING_IEP`; a suspended service blocks coordination with `ERR_SPED_SERVICE_SUSPENDED`, on the reasoning that a student cannot be planned back into the building while an IEP service is frozen pending a meeting. Coordinating a plan that is not `DRAFT` is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Coordination recomputes both counters, and when the recomputed counters already satisfy the readiness guard the plan advances to `READY` inside the same request.

Readiness is derived rather than requested. A plan in `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` advances to `READY` exactly when `required_ack_count` is at least 1, `classroom_ack_count` equals `required_ack_count`, and the parent case's `funding_status` is anything other than `HELD` — so the plan depends jointly on its coordination children and on the case's parallel funding dimension. Conversely, a plan in `READY` falls back to `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` as soon as either `classroom_ack_count` drops below `required_ack_count` or the case's `funding_status` becomes `HELD`. Adding a new coordination row, disputing an existing one, or the case entering a funding hold are all paths back to `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING`. Neither counter depends on the plan's own status, so this guard settles in a single pass.

Completion is a `ProgramAdministrator` act against a `READY` plan on or after the target date. Completing a plan whose `status` is not `READY` is `ERR_PLAN_NOT_READY`, and completing before the clock date has reached `target_return_date` is `ERR_RETURN_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. Completion moves the plan `READY → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at` with the clock instant, and closes the case: the case advances `TRANSITION_PLANNED → CLOSED` with `close_reason` `RETURNED_TO_CAMPUS`, every assignment of the case whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is moved to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `CASE_CLOSED` and `weekly_committed_minutes` 0, `committed_weekly_minutes` is recomputed for each released teacher, every session of the case whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED_ADMIN`, and the case's `version` increments by one.

Planning can also fail, and the case re-enters service when it does. A `CaseManager` aborts a plan whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` or `READY`, recording an `abort_reason` of `MEDICAL_RELAPSE`, `PARENT_WITHDRAWAL`, `CAMPUS_CAPACITY`, or `DOCUMENTATION_DEFECT`. The plan moves to `ABORTED` and the case returns from `TRANSITION_PLANNED` to `ACTIVE`, incrementing the case's `version` by one — which means a case may enter planning again later, the second cyclic path through the service dimension. Aborting a plan that is already `COMPLETED`, `ABORTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`.

Finally, a case can be closed without a completed transition. A `ProgramAdministrator` closes a case whose `service_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `ACTIVE` or `EXTENSION_PENDING`, supplying a `close_reason` from `WITHDRAWN`, `CERT_EXPIRED`, `DENIED_ELIGIBILITY`, and `ADMIN_CLOSURE`; `RETURNED_TO_CAMPUS` is reserved for a completed transition plan and is not accepted here, and closing from any other service state is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Closure moves the case to `CLOSED` and cascades: every assignment of the case whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `CASE_CLOSED` and `weekly_committed_minutes` 0, with `committed_weekly_minutes` recomputed for each released teacher; every session of the case whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED_ADMIN`; every plan of the case whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `CLASSROOM_COORD_PENDING` or `READY` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, after which it neither blocks nor enables any case transition; and every extension request of the case whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` becomes `DENIED` with its `decision_note` recorded as "case closed". The case's `version` increments by one.
