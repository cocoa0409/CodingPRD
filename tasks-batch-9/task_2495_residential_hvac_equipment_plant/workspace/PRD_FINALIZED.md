# Residential HVAC Equipment Plant API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A residential heating and cooling equipment plant no longer competes on a single product. It competes on a **product platform** replicated across efficiency tiers, sold into jurisdictions whose minimum efficiency standards differ, built with refrigerants that regulators are actively phasing down, and assembled from compressors and control boards that arrive in monthly allocations far smaller than distributor demand. The plant that ships late loses the cooling season; the plant that ships a unit into the wrong region ships an uncertifiable product; the plant that keeps building on a refrigerant past its manufacture cutoff creates inventory it is legally barred from selling. Each of these failures is cheap to prevent at the moment of the decision and ruinously expensive to unwind afterwards.

This API is the plant's system of record for those decisions. It governs the design lifecycle of a platform from a draft rating sheet through independent engineering review to production release; the efficiency-rating certification filings that unlock each region; the refrigerant transition calendar with its manufacture cutoff and inventory sell-through deadline; the fabrication of build lots through coil fab, brazing, leak testing by helium mass spectrometry or pressure decay, refrigerant charging, and performance run testing against the platform's rated capacity and efficiency; the allocation of constrained components across competing distributor demand; the pre-season warehouse leveling that smooths a seasonal demand curve; and the warranty feedback loop that scores a platform's field reliability and derates the supplier lot behind a failure pattern.

Two design commitments run through the whole surface. First, **derived state is propagated, never patched**: whether a platform may be sold in a region, how much of a supply pool remains drawable, and what warranty posture a platform carries are all recomputed from their inputs on every event that could change them, including events several entities away. Second, **the person who performs a step is not the person who judges it**: the engineer who submits a design cannot release it, the engineer who submits a certification cannot decide it, the technician who passed a lot's leak test cannot run its performance test, and the analyst who filed a warranty claim cannot close the corrective action it triggered. Every rule below is written so that both commitments hold under cascades, concurrent writers, and regulatory deadlines.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that apply across the entire API surface, which is mounted under `/plant` with the sole exception of the logical-clock control described in §2.4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the individual acting, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Eleven roles are recognized, spelled exactly as follows: `PlantAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `ReliabilityEngineer`, `CertificationEngineer`, `RegulatoryAuditor`, `SupplyPlanner`, `ProductionPlanner`, `ProductionTechnician`, `QualityTechnician`, `DistributorRep`, and `WarrantyAnalyst`. If `X-User-Role` is absent, is not one of those eleven strings, or is not among the roles authorized for the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403 Forbidden). This authorization check is the very first gate applied to any request: an unauthorized caller who also sends a malformed body receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, never a schema complaint.

Role responsibilities divide as follows. A `PlantAdmin` owns plant operations — refrigerant programs, warehouses, test cells, program lifecycle advancement, platform retirement, and the logical clock. A `DesignEngineer` owns the platform design record and submits it for review. A `ReliabilityEngineer` is the independent judge of that design and also closes supplier corrective actions. A `CertificationEngineer` prepares and submits efficiency-rating filings region by region; a `RegulatoryAuditor` publishes regional standards and decides submitted filings. A `SupplyPlanner` registers component supply months and draws component kits against distributor orders. A `ProductionPlanner` schedules build lots and pre-season pallet space, releases finished lots to stock, and stages and ships allocated orders. A `ProductionTechnician` runs the line — coil fabrication, brazing, and charging. A `QualityTechnician` records leak tests and run tests. A `DistributorRep` places and cancels distributor demand. A `WarrantyAnalyst` files field warranty claims and reads the warranty analysis that feeds design and supplier corrective action.

All eleven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is any of `PlantAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `ReliabilityEngineer`, `CertificationEngineer`, `RegulatoryAuditor`, `SupplyPlanner`, `ProductionPlanner`, `ProductionTechnician`, `QualityTechnician`, `DistributorRep`, or `WarrantyAnalyst`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. What the server does instead is **record** it, and later compare recorded values to each other. A platform under review stores its submitter in `submitted_by_user_id`; a certification record stores its submitter in `submitted_by_user_id` and its decider in `decided_by_user_id`; a leak test and a run test each store the technician in `performed_by_user_id`; a warranty claim stores its author in `filed_by_user_id`; a corrective action stores its closer in `closed_by_user_id`; and a distributor order stores the rep who placed it in `created_by_user_id`. Ownership and separation checks are then expressed as comparisons between the acting `X-User-ID` and one of these stored strings — see §2.10 for the separation rules and §3.6 for the single ownership rule, in which only the `DistributorRep` whose identifier equals a distributor order's `created_by_user_id` may cancel that order, and any other caller is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED` (403 Forbidden).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the row's creation timestamp, `created_at`, ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2.

### 2.4. The Logical Plant Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every deadline, cutoff, occupancy window, cooldown buffer, and audit timestamp is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlantAdmin` may advance or rewind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-control capability described in §4; the call stores no row and returns no resource payload, but every subsequent evaluation of a refrigerant program's `manufacture_cutoff_utc` and `sell_through_deadline_utc`, of a reservation's occupied calendar days, of a regional standard's `effective_from_utc`, and of a test cell's cooldown buffer uses the new instant. Where a rule below says "the logical clock", it means this value, and where it says "the logical clock's date", it means that instant's UTC calendar date.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Fields, and Versioning

Every stored row exposes an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most rows additionally expose `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. This applies to refrigerant programs, regional standards, product platforms, certification records, component supplies, allocation lines, distributor orders, warehouses, warehouse reservations, test cells, build orders, warranty claims, and supplier corrective actions.

Two record types are deliberately append-only and therefore carry **neither** `updated_at` **nor** `version`: a leak test and a run test hold only `id` and `created_at` alongside their own fields. There is no endpoint that updates either one; a corrected measurement is a new record, not an edit.

A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation. When a platform's `blocked_region_codes`, `salable_region_count`, `warranty_posture`, `rolling_claim_rate_pct`, `units_built_released`, or `units_shipped_total` is recomputed, the platform's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one — even though no client wrote a field directly. The same holds for a warehouse whose `pallets_reserved_current` is recomputed, for a component supply whose `effective_allocatable_units`, `reserved_units`, `committed_units`, or `available_units` is recomputed, and for a refrigerant program whose `affected_platform_count` is recomputed.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding

Every rounding operation in this system uses the **round half up** convention. The rounded value is also the value that is stored, returned, and compared — no comparison is ever made against an unrounded intermediate.

- Efficiency ratings — a regional standard's `min_seer2_standard`, `min_seer2_high`, and `min_seer2_premium`, a platform's `rated_seer2` and `rated_eer`, and a filing's `rated_seer2_submitted` — carry two decimal places, and SEER2 comparisons are made at two decimal places.
- A leak test's `applied_limit` carries three decimal places when the method is helium mass spectrometry and two decimal places when the method is pressure decay. Because the comparison uses the rounded limit, a measurement exactly equal to the limit passes.
- A run test's `corrected_capacity_btuh` is a whole number of BTU per hour. Its `capacity_ratio` and `efficiency_ratio` each carry exactly four decimal places, and `measured_ambient_c` is supplied to one decimal place. A ratio that rounds up to its tolerance satisfies that tolerance.
- A platform's `rolling_claim_rate_pct` and a corrective action's `trigger_claim_rate_pct` carry two decimal places. Threshold and band comparisons read that two-decimal value, so a raw rate of 4.995 percent becomes 5.00 and does trip the corrective-action threshold.
- Component allocation deals in whole units. A supply pool's effective capacity truncates downward, so a 25 percent cut applied to 101 committed units yields 75 rather than 75.75, and a demand-weighted fair share also truncates downward, which can leave units undrawn in the pool rather than over-granting.
- Warehouse occupancy is counted in whole pallet units, and the comparison against a warehouse's `pallet_capacity` is inclusive: peak occupancy exactly equal to capacity is accepted.
- Carrying cost is reported as `carrying_cost_cents`, a whole number of cents, alongside `pallet_week_total`, which carries four decimal places.
- Test-cell cooldown is counted in whole minutes, and the overlap comparison is strict, which is what makes a gap of exactly the cooldown length legal.

### 2.7. Sub-Resource Reads and Path Resolution

Collections in this system are flat: every resource is addressed as a top-level collection under `/plant`, and a parent is named by a foreign-key field inside a request body rather than by a nested URL segment. Reverse-reachability reads do exist — the certification records, warranty claims, leak tests, run tests, warehouse reservations, allocation lines, and corrective actions belonging to a given parent can each be listed from that parent's own path — and those reads resolve their identifiers strictly left to right. The path identifier is resolved first, and if it names nothing the request fails immediately with the not-found error belonging to that identifier's type: `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFRIGERANT_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGIONAL_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAREHOUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_CELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`. Only after the path resolves are the payload's own foreign keys checked, and those failures carry the same error family. Filters supplied on a sub-resource read narrow the collection; they never change which not-found error a bad path produces.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Certification Filing and Component Allocation

Two write operations in this system legitimately destroy work another writer may not have seen, and both are therefore guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency field. These are the only two write operations that take a version field; every other write in the API accepts none, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

**Creating a certification record** requires `expected_platform_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the `version` of the target `ProductPlatform` that the certification engineer last observed. The reason is that the create supersedes every earlier draft, submitted, or rejected filing for the same platform and region: the engineer is asserting a view of which filings currently exist. If `expected_platform_version` does not equal the stored platform's current `version` — because the platform has since been re-certified, restricted, retired, or filed against again — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLATFORM_VERSION` (HTTP 412 Precondition Failed), and no record is created.

**Drawing an allocation for a distributor order** requires `expected_component_supply_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the `version` of the order's *primary compressor supply row* as the planner last observed it. That row is defined precisely in §3.5: among the eligible compressor supply rows for the platform's `compressor_part_number` and the order's `requested_delivery_month`, it is the one with the earliest `created_at`. If that row's `version` has advanced since the planner read it, the draw is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_VERSION` (HTTP 412 Precondition Failed). The pool the planner priced the grant against no longer has the shape they priced it in — an earlier granted draw has already moved units out of `available_units` — so the grant is refused rather than recomputed silently.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Several operations in this plant reach far beyond the row the caller named. Those reaches are **transitive**: each hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop performed, not by the original request. Entering the sell-through window on a refrigerant program restricts its released platforms; each restriction places that platform's open build lots on regulatory hold; each hold releases those lots' active warehouse reservations; each release recomputes the holding warehouse's pallet occupancy. Retiring a platform withdraws its certifications, which forces a salability recomputation, which is independent of the build cancellations and order cancellations the same retirement performs. Filing a warranty claim can rescore a platform's posture, which can open a supplier corrective action, which derates a supply pool, which revokes reserved allocation lines, which regresses the allocation dimension of the distributor orders that held them.

Three ordering rules govern all of it. Rows swept **into** a cascade are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Allocation lines revoked to bring an over-derated pool back into balance are processed in **descending** `created_at` order — newest reservations lose their units first. And every row a cascade touches is mutated in its own right: `updated_at` refreshed, `version` incremented by one, exactly as §2.5 requires.

Two conserved identities must hold again at the end of every cascade. For a component supply, `effective_allocatable_units` equals `available_units` plus `reserved_units` plus `committed_units`; a derate that would drive `available_units` below zero is never stored as a negative balance, and the cascade revokes reserved lines until the identity holds with `available_units` at zero or above. For a warehouse, `pallets_reserved_current` equals the summed `pallet_units` of the active reservations covering the logical clock's date.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four points in the plant's workflow require two different people, and all four are enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a value the earlier step recorded. Releasing a platform, or returning it to draft, is refused when the caller's identifier equals the platform's `submitted_by_user_id`. Deciding a certification filing is refused when the caller's identifier equals that record's `submitted_by_user_id`. Recording a run test on a lot is refused when the caller's identifier equals the `performed_by_user_id` of the most recent passing leak test on that same lot. Closing a supplier corrective action is refused when the caller's identifier equals the `filed_by_user_id` of the warranty claim that action cites. All four rejections carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409 Conflict).

Note that `submitted_by_user_id` is retained on a platform even after the review closes, precisely so that the audit trail survives the release; the return-to-draft transition also leaves it in place rather than clearing it.

### 2.11. Order of Rule Evaluation

When more than one rule could reject a request, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed ladder. Rules are evaluated in the following order, and the first failing rule ends the request:

1. **Role authorization.** The `X-User-Role` gate of §2.1, applying to every endpoint, rejecting with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Evaluated before the body is inspected at all.
2. **Payload shape.** Applying to every write endpoint: a missing required field, an enum value outside its declared set, a numeric field that is non-numeric or negative where a positive value is required, or a timestamp that is not RFC 3339, all rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three shape rules sit in this band with narrower scopes: the leak-test measurement-pairing rule on the leak-test recording endpoint, the component-pairing rule on the warranty-claim filing endpoint, and the interval-ordering rule on the warehouse-reservation creation and run-test recording endpoints, the last of which rejects with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.
3. **Referential integrity and uniqueness.** Applying to every endpoint that carries an identifier: unresolvable path identifiers and unresolvable payload foreign keys yield the appropriate not-found error from the family listed in §2.7. Two uniqueness rules share this band: a duplicated `model_code` on platform creation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a second certified filing for a platform-and-region pair that already holds one yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION`.
4. **Stale-version guards.** The two rules of §2.8 — `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_VERSION` on the allocation draw and `ERR_STALE_PLATFORM_VERSION` on certification creation.
5. **State and stage gates.** Platform state (`ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`), refrigerant program state (`ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`), certification state (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`), build stage (`ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`), distributor order state in either dimension (`ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`), reservation state (`ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`), corrective-action state (`ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`), and the reservation's build-eligibility rule (`ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_BUILD`).
6. **Actor-identity authorization.** The four separation rules of §2.10 (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) and the order-ownership rule on order cancellation (`ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`).
7. **Regulatory gates.** `ERR_MANUFACTURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_MANUFACTURE_CUTOFF_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_SELL_THROUGH_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_SELL_THROUGH_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_FLAMMABLE_REFRIGERANT_PROHIBITED`, `ERR_EFFICIENCY_BELOW_REGIONAL_MINIMUM`, `ERR_REGION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and `ERR_WARRANTY_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.
8. **Safety limits.** `ERR_A2L_SAFETY_LIMIT_UNSET` on engineering-review submission, `ERR_FLAMMABLE_CHARGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` on charging, and `ERR_LEAK_TEST_HOLD_TOO_SHORT` on a pressure-decay leak test.
9. **Capacity limits.** `ERR_WAREHOUSE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` on reservation creation, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUPPLY` on an allocation draw, `ERR_TEST_CELL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` on a run test, and `ERR_NO_WAREHOUSE_RESERVATION` on releasing a pre-season lot to stock.
10. **Whole-aggregate checks.** `ERR_PLATFORM_HAS_OPEN_BUILDS` on platform retirement and `ERR_SHIP_UNITS_EXCEED_ALLOCATION` on shipping.

Three pairings are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get backwards:

- On an allocation draw, the stale-version guard is checked **before** the regional-salability gate. A request that is both stale and aimed at a region the platform may not be sold into returns `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_VERSION` with 412; the region gate is never reached.
- On charging a lot, the build-stage gate is checked **before** the flammable-charge safety limit. Charging an over-limit mass onto a lot that has not passed its leak test returns `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`; the safety limit is reported only for a lot that is otherwise chargeable.
- On deciding a certification filing, the separation-of-duties rule is checked **before** the flammable-refrigerant prohibition. A submitter who tries to certify their own flammable-refrigerant filing into a region that bans flammable refrigerant returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the prohibition surfaces only when a different auditor decides.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Refrigerant Transition Calendar

Everything the plant designs sits inside a **refrigerant program** — the regulatory envelope for one refrigerant, registered by a `PlantAdmin`. A program is identified by its `refrigerant_code`, the ASHRAE designation such as R-410A or R-454B, which is between 1 and 32 characters long and must be unique across all programs; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The registration also carries the refrigerant's `gwp`, its global warming potential as an integer of at least 1, and its `flammability_class`, which is either `A1` for a non-flammable refrigerant or `A2L` for a mildly flammable one. The class is the single most consequential input in the whole system: it tightens leak-test limits, forces a design charge ceiling, relaxes some run-test tolerances but not others, and can bar a platform from a jurisdiction outright.

The calendar itself is two instants. `manufacture_cutoff_utc` is the moment after which no build lot on an affected platform may be created or charged. `sell_through_deadline_utc` is the moment after which existing finished inventory may no longer be shipped, and it must fall strictly after `manufacture_cutoff_utc`; a registration whose deadline is at or before the cutoff is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A new program opens in `ACTIVE` status with `sell_through_entered_at` and `expired_at` both unset, and its `affected_platform_count` reports how many platforms name this program and currently sit in `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `RELEASED`, or `RESTRICTED` — retired platforms drop out of the count. Programs can be listed and filtered by `status` and by `flammability_class`.

A program advances through exactly two transitions, both driven by a `PlantAdmin`. The first moves it `ACTIVE → SELL_THROUGH`. It requires that the program still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`, and that the logical clock have reached or passed `manufacture_cutoff_utc`, otherwise `ERR_MANUFACTURE_CUTOFF_NOT_REACHED`. On success the program's `status` becomes `SELL_THROUGH`, `sell_through_entered_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the program's `version` increases by one. Then the depth-four cascade of §2.9 runs, each hop caused by the hop above it:

- Every platform naming this program whose `status` is `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → RESTRICTED`, processed oldest first by `created_at`. Its `blocked_region_codes` and `salable_region_count` are recomputed by the salability rule of §3.4, its `updated_at` is refreshed, and its `version` increases by one. From that point on, opening a new build lot against that platform is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`, while stock that is already finished may still ship until the sell-through deadline arrives.
- Caused by each platform entering `RESTRICTED`, every build lot of that platform whose `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `COIL_FAB`, `BRAZED`, `REWORK`, `LEAK_PASSED`, `CHARGED`, or `RUN_PASSED` moves to `HOLD_REGULATORY`, processed oldest first, with `hold_reason` set to `REFRIGERANT_SELL_THROUGH` and `version` increased by one.
- Caused by each lot entering `HOLD_REGULATORY`, every warehouse reservation of that lot whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, processed oldest first, with `released_at` stamped from the logical clock, `release_reason` set to `BUILD_ON_REGULATORY_HOLD`, and `version` increased by one.
- Caused by each reservation entering `RELEASED`, the holding warehouse's `pallets_reserved_current` is recomputed and that warehouse's `version` increases by one.

The second transition moves the program `SELL_THROUGH → EXPIRED`. It requires the program to be in `SELL_THROUGH`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`, and the logical clock to have reached or passed `sell_through_deadline_utc`, otherwise `ERR_SELL_THROUGH_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED`. On success `status` becomes `EXPIRED`, `expired_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` increases by one. Every platform naming this program whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ENG_REVIEW`, `RELEASED`, or `RESTRICTED` is then driven to `RETIRED`, processed oldest first — so `DRAFT → RETIRED`, `ENG_REVIEW → RETIRED`, and `RESTRICTED → RETIRED` are all reachable here — and for each of those platforms the complete retirement cascade described at the end of §3.3 runs. A platform that never reached release simply finds no certification, build, or order rows to unwind, and the cascade terminates after its first hop. `EXPIRED` is terminal: the program never returns to an earlier phase.

### 3.2. Publishing Regional Efficiency Standards

A jurisdiction's efficiency floor is published as a **regional standard** by either a `PlantAdmin` or a `RegulatoryAuditor`. The `region_code` names the jurisdiction — for example `SOUTHEAST` — is between 1 and 32 characters drawn from uppercase letters and the underscore, and must be unique among standards whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Three minimums are published together, one per efficiency tier: `min_seer2_standard`, `min_seer2_high`, and `min_seer2_premium`, each greater than zero and carried to two decimal places. A jurisdiction that bans mildly flammable refrigerant sets `prohibits_flammable_refrigerant` to true; when the flag is omitted it defaults to false.

Publication is dated. `effective_from_utc` marks the instant from which the standard participates in salability computation at all: a standard whose `effective_from_utc` is still in the logical clock's future is stored, is listable, and is entirely ignored when the plant works out which regions a platform may be sold into. Standards open with `status` set to `ACTIVE`, and that is the only value the field ever takes — the plant models no withdrawal of a published standard. Standards can be listed and filtered by `region_code`, by `status`, and by `prohibits_flammable_refrigerant`.

### 3.3. The Platform Design Record: Draft, Review, Release, Retire

A `DesignEngineer` opens a **product platform** by declaring its `model_code`, a 1-to-32-character plant model code that must be unique across all platforms (a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`); its `efficiency_tier`, one of `STANDARD`, `HIGH`, or `PREMIUM`; the `refrigerant_program_id` of the program it is designed around; its nameplate ratings `rated_capacity_btuh` (a whole number of BTU per hour, at least 6000), `rated_eer` (full-load BTU per hour per watt, greater than zero at two decimals), and `rated_seer2` (greater than zero at two decimals); and the two constrained part numbers it consumes one-per-unit, `compressor_part_number` and `control_board_part_number`. A redesign that replaces an earlier generation names its predecessor in `supersedes_platform_id`; a first-generation platform leaves the field unset, and it defaults to null. Where the program is flammable the design must eventually carry a `max_charge_grams` safety ceiling; the field may be supplied at creation, defaults to null, and is optional for a non-flammable program.

Creation is gated three ways. The named `refrigerant_program_id` must resolve, otherwise `ERR_REFRIGERANT_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; that program's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`; and a supplied `supersedes_platform_id` must resolve to a stored platform, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`. A new platform opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `blocked_region_codes` empty, `salable_region_count` at 0, `warranty_posture` at `OK`, `rolling_claim_rate_pct` at 0.00, `units_built_released` at 0, `units_shipped_total` at 0, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `released_at`, and `last_revision_notes` all null. The naming program's `affected_platform_count` is recomputed as part of the same write. Platforms are listable and can be filtered by `refrigerant_program_id`, by `supersedes_platform_id`, by `efficiency_tier`, by `status`, and by `warranty_posture`.

While the platform is still in `DRAFT`, its `DesignEngineer` may revise the design record — the three ratings, the two part numbers, and the charge ceiling — one field at a time or all at once. Editing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE` from any other state, and a successful edit increments the platform's `version`.

Submitting the design for engineering review advances `DRAFT → ENG_REVIEW` and is the `DesignEngineer`'s last act on that revision. The platform must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`, and the governing program must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The safety gate lives here: when the program's `flammability_class` is `A2L`, the platform's `max_charge_grams` must be set and must be at least 1, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_A2L_SAFETY_LIMIT_UNSET`. A flammable design cannot enter review without a declared charge ceiling, because that ceiling is what the charging step later enforces on the line. On success, `revision_round` increases by one and `submitted_by_user_id` records the submitting `X-User-ID`.

Review is decided by a `ReliabilityEngineer`, and never by the submitter. Returning the design advances `ENG_REVIEW → DRAFT`: it requires `ENG_REVIEW` status (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`) and a caller whose identifier differs from `submitted_by_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and it stores the reviewer's `revision_notes` from the request into the platform's `last_revision_notes` while leaving `submitted_by_user_id` in place for audit. `revision_round` is *not* reset, so a design that goes round the `DRAFT → ENG_REVIEW → DRAFT` loop three times and is then resubmitted carries a `revision_round` of 4. Releasing the design advances `ENG_REVIEW → RELEASED` under the same two checks plus one more — the governing program must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` — and stamps `released_at` from the logical clock before recomputing `blocked_region_codes` and `salable_region_count` by the rule in §3.4. Only a `RELEASED` platform can receive new build lots; only a `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED` platform can receive new distributor orders or new certification filings.

Retirement is a `PlantAdmin` act and it unwinds a great deal. It is admitted from `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED` only — any other state yields `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE` — and it is blocked outright while any build lot of the platform sits in `COIL_FAB`, `BRAZED`, `REWORK`, `LEAK_PASSED`, `CHARGED`, or `RUN_PASSED`, in which case the request is refused with `ERR_PLATFORM_HAS_OPEN_BUILDS`. An optional `reason` may accompany the request. The `PlantAdmin`-driven form therefore covers both `RELEASED → RETIRED` and `RESTRICTED → RETIRED` with identical preconditions and identical effects; the system-driven form reached from program expiry runs the same effects but arrives only after the sell-through cascade has already parked those lots on regulatory hold, so the open-builds check has nothing left to trip on. Once `status` becomes `RETIRED` and `version` increments, four hops follow:

- Every certification record of this platform whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `CERTIFIED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, processed oldest first, with `withdrawn_at` stamped from the logical clock and `version` increased by one.
- Caused by those withdrawals, `blocked_region_codes` is recomputed and now contains every participating region code, because no certified record survives, and `salable_region_count` becomes 0; the platform's `version` increases again.
- Every build lot of this platform whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `HOLD_REGULATORY` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed oldest first, with `closed_reason` set to `PLATFORM_RETIRED` and `version` increased by one. Caused by each cancellation, every `ACTIVE` warehouse reservation of that lot moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `BUILD_CANCELLED`, and the holding warehouse's `pallets_reserved_current` is recomputed.
- Every distributor order of this platform whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING` has its `fulfillment_status` set to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped from the logical clock, processed oldest first. Caused by each cancellation, every `RESERVED` allocation line of that order becomes `RELEASED` with `closed_reason` set to `PLATFORM_RETIRED`; each touched component supply recomputes its `reserved_units` and `available_units` and returns `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` when `available_units` ends above zero; and the order's `allocated_units` resets to 0 with `allocation_status` set to `UNALLOCATED`.

`RETIRED` is terminal. A retired platform is permanently unsalable everywhere, which condition (4) of the salability rule below encodes directly.

### 3.4. Efficiency-Rating Certification and Regional Salability

A platform earns the right to be sold in a jurisdiction one filing at a time. A `CertificationEngineer` opens a **certification record** by naming the `platform_id`, the target `region_code`, the `rated_seer2_submitted` value that will go to the certification body (greater than zero, two decimals), and the `expected_platform_version` described in §2.8. The platform must resolve, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`; the stale-version guard fires next as `ERR_STALE_PLATFORM_VERSION`; the platform's `status` must then be `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`; and no existing record for the same `platform_id` and `region_code` may already hold `status` `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION`. The new record opens in `DRAFT` with `governing_standard_id` null. Every earlier record for the same platform-and-region pair whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `REJECTED` is driven `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` (or from `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` to `SUPERSEDED`), processed oldest first, each one's `version` increasing by one. The platform's own `version` increases by one as well, which is precisely what makes any other engineer's held `expected_platform_version` stale on their next write.

Submission advances the filing `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is again the `CertificationEngineer`'s act. The record must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`; the platform must be `RELEASED` — a restricted platform may hold a draft filing but may not submit it — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`; a governing standard must exist for the record's `region_code`, otherwise `ERR_REGIONAL_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`; and `rated_seer2_submitted` must be at least the governing standard's minimum for the platform's `efficiency_tier`, otherwise `ERR_EFFICIENCY_BELOW_REGIONAL_MINIMUM`. The tier selects which minimum applies: a `STANDARD` platform is measured against `min_seer2_standard`, a `HIGH` platform against `min_seer2_high`, and a `PREMIUM` platform against `min_seer2_premium`. Success stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, `submitted_at` with the logical clock, and pins the resolved standard's identifier into `governing_standard_id`, where it stays for the rest of the record's life — a standard published afterwards does not retroactively govern a filing already in flight.

The regulator's decision is recorded by a `RegulatoryAuditor` who is not the submitter, and it forks into two branches with deliberately different consequences. Both branches require `status` to be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, and both require the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `decision` supplied on the request selects the branch.

A `decision` of `CERTIFIED` advances `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`. Before it can, one regulatory gate applies: if the pinned governing standard has `prohibits_flammable_refrigerant` set to true **and** the platform's program carries `flammability_class` `A2L`, the certification is refused with `ERR_FLAMMABLE_REFRIGERANT_PROHIBITED`. On success `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are stamped, the record's `version` increases by one, and the platform's `blocked_region_codes` and `salable_region_count` are recomputed — the newly certified region leaves the blocked list unless some other blocking condition still applies to it — with the platform's `version` increasing by one.

A `decision` of `REJECTED` advances `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. This branch requires a `rejection_reason` in the request body; omitting it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the supplied text is stored in the record's `rejection_reason`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are stamped exactly as on the certified branch, and the record's `version` increases by one. The platform's `blocked_region_codes` and `salable_region_count` are recomputed here too, and with its `version` incremented — but the region **stays** blocked, because after the rejection no certified record exists for it. No certification timestamp beyond `decided_at` is written on this branch, and the record does not become terminal: a fresh filing may be opened for the same pair, which supersedes this rejected one.

A `CERTIFIED` record leaves the certified state in exactly one way: the platform retirement cascade of §3.3 drives it, along with any `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` sibling, to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_at` stamped. `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal. Certification records are listable and filterable by `platform_id`, by `region_code`, by `status`, and by `governing_standard_id`.

**Regional salability** is the derived answer to "where may this platform be sold", and it is a propagated derivation rather than a snapshot: a decision three entities away rewrites it, and it is always recomputed from scratch rather than incrementally patched. The recomputation runs on platform release and retirement, on certification creation, submission, decision, and withdrawal, and on refrigerant program sell-through and expiry. It considers only **participating** standards, meaning those whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from_utc` is at or before the logical clock. For each participating standard, that standard's `region_code` is blocked for this platform when any one of four conditions holds:

1. No certification record for this platform and that region carries `status` `CERTIFIED`.
2. That standard's `prohibits_flammable_refrigerant` is true and the platform's program carries `flammability_class` `A2L`.
3. The platform's `rated_seer2` is strictly less than that standard's minimum for the platform's `efficiency_tier` — `min_seer2_standard`, `min_seer2_high`, or `min_seer2_premium` as the tier dictates. A `rated_seer2` exactly equal to the minimum does **not** block.
4. The platform's `status` is `RETIRED`.

`blocked_region_codes` is the list of qualifying region codes sorted ascending, and a region blocked by two or three conditions at once appears exactly once. `salable_region_count` is the number of participating standards minus the length of `blocked_region_codes`.

### 3.5. Constrained Components and the Allocation of Kits

Compressors and control boards arrive by the month, by the supplier lot, and never in the quantity the distributors want. A `SupplyPlanner` registers each arrival as a **component supply** pool: its `component_type` is either `COMPRESSOR` or `CONTROL_BOARD`; its `part_number` is matched against a platform's `compressor_part_number` or `control_board_part_number` respectively; its `supplier_lot_code` is the supplier's lot identifier, which is what later lets a warranty claim be attributed back to this pool; its `supply_month` is the delivery month the pool covers, written as a four-digit year and two-digit month joined by a hyphen; and `total_allocatable_units` is the quantity the supplier committed for that month, at least 1, before any corrective-action cut. The pool also carries an `allocation_policy` of either `PRIORITY_WATERFALL` or `DEMAND_PRORATA`, defaulting to `PRIORITY_WATERFALL` when the field is omitted. A fresh pool opens with `status` `ACTIVE` and `derate_pct` at 0. Pools are listable and filterable by `component_type`, by `part_number`, by `supply_month`, and by `status`.

Four counters describe a pool at any instant, and all four are derived rather than written. `effective_allocatable_units` is `total_allocatable_units` reduced by the current cut: take the percentage that survives, which is 100 minus `derate_pct`, multiply the committed total by it, divide by 100, and truncate downward to a whole unit — a 25 percent cut of 101 units therefore leaves 75, not 75.75. Only four cut values are ever stored in `derate_pct`: 0, 25, 50, and 100. `reserved_units` is the summed `units` of this pool's allocation lines whose `status` is `RESERVED`; `committed_units` is the same sum over lines whose `status` is `COMMITTED`; lines that have been revoked or released count toward neither. `available_units` is `effective_allocatable_units` less `reserved_units` less `committed_units`, and the conserved identity of §2.9 must hold after every allocation, revocation, release, cancellation, shipment, derate, and derate restoration. A derate that would push `available_units` below zero is never persisted as a negative balance; the cascade revokes reserved lines until the balance is zero or positive.

A pool's `status` moves on its own. When a draw brings `available_units` to exactly 0, it goes `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`. When a revocation, a release, or a derate restoration brings `available_units` back above 0 **and** `derate_pct` stands at 0, it goes `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE`. The remaining two moves belong to the corrective-action story in §3.9: a pool goes `ACTIVE → DERATED` (also reachable from `EXHAUSTED`) when a supplier corrective action opens against it, `DERATED → ACTIVE` when that action closes as effective, and `DERATED → CLOSED` when it closes as ineffective. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Demand arrives as a **distributor order** placed by a `DistributorRep`. The order names the `distributor_code` of the account placing it, the `platform_id` being ordered, the `region_code` the units will be sold into, the `requested_units` of demand (at least 1), a `priority_score` between 1 and 100 inclusive that serves as the waterfall sort key, and the `requested_delivery_month` in the same year-and-month form used by supply pools. The platform must resolve, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`, and its `status` must be `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`. A new order opens with `allocation_status` `UNALLOCATED`, `fulfillment_status` `PENDING`, `allocated_units` at 0, `shipped_units` at 0, `staged_at`, `shipped_at` and `cancelled_at` all null, and `created_by_user_id` recording the placing rep's `X-User-ID`. The two status fields are **independent dimensions**: `allocation_status` moves only in response to allocation grants and revocations, `fulfillment_status` only in response to logistics actions, and neither ever forces the other. Orders are listable and filterable by `platform_id`, by `distributor_code`, by `region_code`, by `requested_delivery_month`, and by either dimension.

Drawing components against an order is the `SupplyPlanner`'s act, and it is the single most heavily gated write in the system. The order's `allocation_status` must be `UNALLOCATED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` and its `fulfillment_status` must be `PENDING`; either failure yields `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The `expected_component_supply_version` of §2.8 must match, otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_VERSION` at 412. The order's `region_code` must not appear in the platform's `blocked_region_codes`, otherwise `ERR_REGION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And the plan must actually yield something: a computed grant below one unit is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUPPLY`.

The grant itself is computed as follows. The **eligible compressor pools** are those component supplies whose `component_type` is `COMPRESSOR`, whose `part_number` equals the platform's `compressor_part_number`, whose `supply_month` equals the order's `requested_delivery_month`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DERATED`, and whose `available_units` is at least 1. The **eligible board pools** are found by the same filter with `component_type` `CONTROL_BOARD` and `part_number` equal to the platform's `control_board_part_number`. `compressor_pool` is the summed `available_units` of the eligible compressor pools and `board_pool` the summed `available_units` of the eligible board pools. Because one finished unit consumes exactly one compressor and exactly one control board, `kit_capacity` is the smaller of `compressor_pool` and `board_pool`. `remaining_need` is the order's `requested_units` less its current `allocated_units`. `total_open_demand` is the sum, over every distributor order for the same `platform_id` and the same `requested_delivery_month` whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING` and whose `allocation_status` is not `FULLY_ALLOCATED`, of that order's `requested_units` less its `allocated_units` — and the order being allocated is itself included in that sum.

Which of the two policies converts those figures into `granted_units` is decided not by the caller but by the **primary compressor supply row**: among the eligible compressor pools, the one with the earliest `created_at`. Its `allocation_policy` governs the draw, and that same row is the version parent for `expected_component_supply_version`. Under `PRIORITY_WATERFALL`, `granted_units` is the smaller of `remaining_need` and `kit_capacity` — the order in front takes everything it can carry and later orders live on whatever is left, which is why `priority_score` decides who is served first. Under `DEMAND_PRORATA`, a fair share is computed first by multiplying `kit_capacity` by `remaining_need`, dividing by `total_open_demand`, and truncating downward to a whole unit; `granted_units` is then the smaller of `remaining_need` and that fair share. Pro-rata deliberately prevents an order in front from draining the pool, at the cost of leaving units undrawn when the truncation bites; when the fair share truncates to zero the draw is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUPPLY`.

Units are then physically drawn from the eligible pools of each component type in ascending order of `supply_month` and, within a month, ascending `created_at`, taking from each pool the smaller of its `available_units` and the units still to draw. Every pool drawn from produces exactly one **allocation line**, so a grant that spans two supplier lots produces two lines per component type. A line records the `distributor_order_id` it was granted to, the `component_supply_id` it draws from, the `component_type` copied from that pool, and the `units` it drew (at least 1), and opens with `status` `RESERVED` and `closed_reason` null. Each drawn pool then recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units`, may move `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`, and has its `version` incremented. The order recomputes `allocated_units` — defined as the summed `units` of its allocation lines whose `component_type` is `COMPRESSOR` and whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, so that a kit is counted once and not twice — and its `version` is incremented. Its `allocation_status` becomes `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` when the grant leaves `allocated_units` short of `requested_units`, giving `UNALLOCATED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, and becomes `FULLY_ALLOCATED` the moment `allocated_units` equals `requested_units`, giving either `UNALLOCATED → FULLY_ALLOCATED` on a single sufficient grant or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → FULLY_ALLOCATED` on a later grant that closes the gap exactly. In every case `fulfillment_status` is left untouched.

The dimension also moves backwards, but only through system action. When a derate cascade revokes some of an order's reserved lines and leaves `allocated_units` at 1 or more, the order regresses `FULLY_ALLOCATED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`. When a derate cascade, an order cancellation, or a platform retirement removes every reserved line so that `allocated_units` recomputes to 0, the order regresses `FULLY_ALLOCATED → UNALLOCATED` with `allocated_units` set to 0. Both regressions increment the order's `version` and leave `fulfillment_status` alone.

Allocation lines themselves have three exits, all system-driven. A line goes `RESERVED → COMMITTED` when its owning order ships, and the pool it drew from moves those units out of `reserved_units` and into `committed_units`. A line goes `RESERVED → REVOKED` when a derate or a pool closure leaves the drawn pool's `available_units` negative; `closed_reason` is set, the pool recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units`, and — critically — **every sibling line of the same distributor order that is still `RESERVED` and carries a different `component_type` is revoked with it**, because a unit needs one compressor and one control board to remain a kit and half a kit is worth nothing. A line goes `RESERVED → RELEASED` when its owning order is cancelled or its platform is retired, again setting `closed_reason` and recomputing the pool. `COMMITTED`, `REVOKED`, and `RELEASED` are all terminal. Lines are listable in their own right, filterable by `distributor_order_id`, by `component_supply_id`, by `component_type`, and by `status`, and also readable as sub-collections of a pool or of an order.

Because the policy that governs a draw is a property of the pool rather than of the caller, planners need to see the arithmetic before committing to it. A computed allocation-preview read prices what a given order would receive without drawing anything: it accepts an optional `policy` override so that both variants can be compared, and it reports back the `order_id`, the `policy` it evaluated, the `compressor_pool`, the `board_pool`, the `kit_capacity`, the `remaining_need`, the `total_open_demand`, and the resulting `granted_units`. It stores nothing and increments no `version`.

### 3.6. Staging, Shipping, and Cancelling Distributor Demand

The fulfillment dimension is the `ProductionPlanner`'s domain, with one exception noted below. Staging advances `PENDING → STAGED` and carries the only cross-dimension guard in the system: the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `PENDING` **and** its `allocation_status` must be `FULLY_ALLOCATED`, and either failure is refused with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. Logistics may not run ahead of supply. Success stamps `staged_at` from the logical clock, increments `version`, and leaves `allocation_status` exactly where it was.

Shipping advances `STAGED → SHIPPED` and is where the refrigerant calendar bites for the last time. The order must be `STAGED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The `shipped_units` supplied on the request must equal the order's `allocated_units` exactly — neither more nor fewer — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SHIP_UNITS_EXCEED_ALLOCATION`. The logical clock must still be strictly before the governing refrigerant program's `sell_through_deadline_utc`; at or after that instant the shipment is refused with `ERR_SELL_THROUGH_DEADLINE_PASSED`. And the order's `region_code` must still be absent from the platform's `blocked_region_codes`, otherwise `ERR_REGION_NOT_CERTIFIED` — a certification withdrawn between allocation and shipment stops the truck. On success `fulfillment_status` becomes `SHIPPED`, the supplied count is stored in `shipped_units`, `shipped_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the order's `version` increments. Then every allocation line of the order whose `status` is `RESERVED` becomes `COMMITTED`, processed oldest first, with each touched pool moving those units from `reserved_units` into `committed_units` and incrementing its own `version`. Finally the platform's `units_shipped_total` is recomputed as the summed `shipped_units` of its orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED`, its `rolling_claim_rate_pct` and `warranty_posture` are recomputed by the rules in §3.9, and the platform's `version` increments.

Cancellation advances `PENDING → CANCELLED` and is reserved to the `DistributorRep` who placed the order: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`. The order must be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. An optional `reason` may accompany the request. On success `fulfillment_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, `cancelled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` increments. Every allocation line of the order still in `RESERVED` becomes `RELEASED` with `closed_reason` set to `ORDER_CANCELLED`, processed oldest first; each touched pool recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units`, returns `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` when `available_units` ends above zero, and increments its `version`, restoring the conserved identity of §2.9. The order's `allocated_units` falls to 0 and its `allocation_status` returns to `UNALLOCATED`. Both `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for the fulfillment dimension.

### 3.7. Seasonal Levelling: Warehouses and Pre-Season Pallet Space

Residential cooling demand is violently seasonal, so the plant builds ahead of the season and parks the output. A `PlantAdmin` registers each finished-goods **warehouse** with a `name` and a `pallet_capacity` — the maximum number of pallets it can hold on any single calendar day, at least 1. The warehouse also exposes `pallets_reserved_current`, a derived count of the pallets its active reservations occupy on the logical clock's own date.

A `ProductionPlanner` books space as a **warehouse reservation**, naming the `warehouse_id` that will hold the stock and the `build_order_id` whose finished units will occupy the pallets, together with a `start_date` (the first occupied calendar day, inclusive) and an `end_date` (the first free calendar day, exclusive), both written as a four-digit year, two-digit month, and two-digit day joined by hyphens, plus the `pallet_units` occupied on every day of that half-open interval, at least 1. The warehouse must resolve, otherwise `ERR_WAREHOUSE_NOT_FOUND`; the build lot must resolve, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; and `end_date` must fall strictly after `start_date`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

Only a pre-season lot may hold pallet space, and only while it is still in production. A reservation is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_BUILD` when the named lot's `is_preseason` is false, or when its `status` is any of `LEAK_PASSED`, `CHARGED`, `RUN_PASSED`, `RELEASED_TO_STOCK`, `SCRAPPED`, `CANCELLED`, or `HOLD_REGULATORY` — which leaves `DRAFT`, `COIL_FAB`, `BRAZED`, and `REWORK` as the four stages at which space may be booked.

Occupancy is computed on whole UTC calendar days over half-open intervals. A reservation occupies day D when its `start_date` is at or before D and its `end_date` is strictly after D, so a reservation that ends on the day another begins does not overlap it and the two may share the last pallet of capacity. The occupancy of a warehouse on day D is the summed `pallet_units` of that warehouse's reservations whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and which occupy D; released reservations are excluded from every day, including days already elapsed. For a candidate reservation, the peak occupancy is the largest value, taken across every day in the candidate's own interval, of that day's existing occupancy plus the candidate's `pallet_units`. Acceptance requires that peak to be at or below the warehouse's `pallet_capacity`; a peak exactly equal to capacity is accepted, and any excess is refused with `ERR_WAREHOUSE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful booking opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `released_at` and `release_reason` null, and triggers a recomputation of the warehouse's `pallets_reserved_current` — which is simply the occupancy of the logical clock's UTC date — and an increment of that warehouse's `version`.

Space returns to the pool when a reservation moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`. A `ProductionPlanner` may do this directly, supplying an optional `release_reason`; the same transition is also driven by the regulatory-hold cascade of §3.1, by a lot cancellation, and by a lot being scrapped, each of which supplies its own reason string. The reservation must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`. On success `status` becomes `RELEASED`, `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `release_reason` is recorded, and the reservation's `version` increments; the warehouse then recomputes `pallets_reserved_current`, increments its own `version`, and the conserved occupancy identity of §2.9 holds again. `RELEASED` is terminal — space is re-booked by creating a new reservation, never by reviving an old one. Reservations are readable individually and listable per warehouse or per lot, filterable by `status` and, on the warehouse view, by `build_order_id`.

Two computed reads sit on top of this. The first reports pallet occupancy without storing anything: given an optional `on_date` it answers for that single day, and given an optional `window_start` and `window_end` pair it answers across each day of that window, returning the `warehouse_id`, the `pallet_capacity`, the `on_date` it evaluated, the `occupancy_units` on that day, and the `peak_occupancy_units` across the range.

The second read prices the money that pre-season inventory costs. Carrying stock is billed at 1165 cents — eleven dollars and sixty-five cents — per pallet per seven-day week. Over a requested window bounded by a mandatory `window_start` and `window_end`, the read considers only reservations of that warehouse whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose interval intersects the window, taken in ascending `created_at` order, and reports how many there were as `reservation_count`. For each such reservation, its occupied days are the number of whole days in the intersection of its own half-open interval with the requested half-open window. The `pallet_week_total` is then the sum, across those reservations, of the reservation's `pallet_units` multiplied by its occupied days and divided by seven, rounded to four decimal places; that figure is reported identically under both bases. The `carrying_cost_cents` figure depends on an optional `basis` parameter, which defaults to `PER_RESERVATION` because that is what the plant bills against:

- Under `PER_RESERVATION`, each reservation is invoiced as its own line: multiply the reservation's `pallet_units` by its occupied days, divide by seven, multiply by the 1165-cent weekly rate, round that line to a whole cent, and only then add the lines together.
- Under `POOLED`, the pallet-weeks are pooled first: add up each reservation's `pallet_units` multiplied by its occupied days and divided by seven across all of them, multiply that single pooled figure by the 1165-cent weekly rate, and round once at the end.

The two bases can differ by a cent on the very same set of reservations, whenever the individual line remainders and the pooled remainder round in opposite directions. That divergence is a genuine accounting difference between two billing conventions, not a tolerance to be smoothed over, and both answers are correct for their own basis.

### 3.8. Building a Lot: Coil Fabrication, Leak Test, Charge, and Run Test

A **build order** is one lot of identical units. A `ProductionPlanner` opens it against a platform by naming the `platform_id`, the `quantity_units` in the lot (at least 1), and the `scheduled_start_utc` at which coil fabrication is planned to begin, and by flagging `is_preseason` when the lot is a pre-season levelling build; the flag defaults to false when omitted. Four gates apply. The platform must resolve, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`. Its `status` must be `RELEASED` — not `DRAFT`, not `ENG_REVIEW`, and notably not `RESTRICTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`. Its `warranty_posture` must not be `DESIGN_CHANGE_REQUIRED`, otherwise the lot is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. And the logical clock must be strictly before the governing refrigerant program's `manufacture_cutoff_utc`; at or after that instant the lot is refused with `ERR_MANUFACTURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A new lot opens in `DRAFT` with `rework_count` at 0, `charge_grams` at 0, `hold_reason`, `closed_reason` and `released_to_stock_at` null, and both `latest_leak_verdict` and `latest_run_verdict` null. Lots are listable and filterable by `platform_id`, by `status`, and by `is_preseason`.

A `ProductionTechnician` runs the line. Starting fabrication advances `DRAFT → COIL_FAB`, and the very same action advances `REWORK → COIL_FAB` for a lot sent back for repair, which is what closes the repair cycle `COIL_FAB → BRAZED → REWORK → COIL_FAB`. Either way the lot must be in `DRAFT` or `REWORK`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`, and the platform must still be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE`. Completing brazing advances `COIL_FAB → BRAZED` and requires the lot to be in `COIL_FAB`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`. Both increment the lot's `version`.

A brazed lot must prove it is sealed. A `QualityTechnician` records a **leak test** naming the `build_order_id` under test and the `method`, which is either `HELIUM_MASS_SPEC` or `PRESSURE_DECAY`; there is no default, and the method chosen decides which measurement fields are legal. Helium mass spectrometry requires `measured_leak_rate_g_per_year`, the tracer leak rate in grams per year, at zero or above to two decimals, and forbids both `decay_kpa` and `hold_minutes`. Pressure decay requires `decay_kpa`, the pressure drop over the hold in kilopascals at zero or above to two decimals, and `hold_minutes`, the hold duration as a whole number of minutes at zero or above, and forbids `measured_leak_rate_g_per_year`. A payload that omits a field its method requires, or that carries a field its method forbids, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a request offering both measurement styles is rejected outright rather than having one silently ignored. A pressure-decay test must also hold pressure for at least 15 minutes; a shorter `hold_minutes` is refused with `ERR_LEAK_TEST_HOLD_TOO_SHORT`. The lot must resolve, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, and it must be in `BRAZED`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`.

The verdict is computed from a single strictness factor and one of two limits. The strictness factor is 1.000 for a lot whose platform sits on an `A1` program, and exactly one half — 0.500 — for a lot whose platform sits on an `A2L` program, because a flammable charge must not leak. Under `HELIUM_MASS_SPEC`, the `applied_limit` is the plant's base helium allowance of 3.000 grams per year multiplied by that strictness factor and rounded to three decimal places; the `verdict` is `PASS` when `measured_leak_rate_g_per_year` is at or below `applied_limit` and `FAIL` otherwise. Under `PRESSURE_DECAY`, the `applied_limit` is the plant's allowance of 2.00 kilopascals for every 15 minutes of hold — that is, 2.00 multiplied by `hold_minutes` divided by 15 — multiplied by the strictness factor and rounded to two decimal places; the `verdict` is `PASS` when `decay_kpa` is at or below `applied_limit` and `FAIL` otherwise. The two variants share the strictness factor and the "at or below" comparison direction, but they consume different measurement fields, different constants, and different physical units, so one variant's `applied_limit` is never a meaningful limit for the other. Each record also stamps `performed_by_user_id` with the technician's `X-User-ID` and `performed_at` with the logical clock. Leak tests are append-only records: they are listable and filterable by `build_order_id`, by `method`, and by `verdict`, but never updated.

Recording the test moves the lot. A `PASS` advances `BRAZED → LEAK_PASSED` and sets the lot's `latest_leak_verdict` to `PASS`. A `FAIL` costs the lot one unit of a repair budget of two: `rework_count` increases by one, and if the incremented count is 2 or below the lot goes `BRAZED → REWORK` with `latest_leak_verdict` set to `FAIL`. If the increment would take `rework_count` past 2 — that is, on the third failure of the lot across leak and run testing combined — the lot goes `BRAZED → SCRAPPED` instead, with `closed_reason` set to `REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `latest_leak_verdict` set to `FAIL`; every `ACTIVE` warehouse reservation of the scrapped lot becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `BUILD_SCRAPPED`, and each touched warehouse recomputes `pallets_reserved_current`. In all three cases the lot's `version` increments. The lot's `latest_leak_verdict` always reflects that lot's most recent leak test ordered by `created_at` descending, and reads null while none exists.

Charging is again a `ProductionTechnician` act and advances `LEAK_PASSED → CHARGED`. The lot must be in `LEAK_PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`. When the platform's program carries `flammability_class` `A2L`, the `charge_grams` supplied on the request must not exceed the platform's `max_charge_grams`, otherwise the charge is refused with `ERR_FLAMMABLE_CHARGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; on an `A1` program no ceiling applies. The manufacture window binds here as well as at lot creation: the logical clock must be strictly before the program's `manufacture_cutoff_utc`, otherwise `ERR_MANUFACTURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Note the ordering consequence spelled out in §2.11 — an over-limit charge onto a lot that is not `LEAK_PASSED` is reported as a stage error, not a safety error. On success the supplied mass is stored in the lot's `charge_grams` and `version` increments.

Performance is verified in a **test cell**. A `PlantAdmin` registers each cell with a `cell_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique across cells, and an optional `cooldown_minutes` between 0 and 240 that defaults to 30 when omitted; the cell's `status` is set to `ACTIVE` at creation and cells are never retired in this scope. The cooldown is a mandatory idle buffer applied on **both** sides of every run-test window that occupies the cell.

A `QualityTechnician` records a **run test** naming the `build_order_id` and the `test_cell_id`, the window it occupies as `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, and three measurements: `measured_capacity_btuh`, the measured cooling capacity as a whole number at zero or above; `measured_power_w`, the measured total input power in whole watts, at least 1; and `measured_ambient_c`, the outdoor ambient temperature in Celsius to one decimal place. The lot must resolve, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; the cell must resolve, otherwise `ERR_TEST_CELL_NOT_FOUND`; the lot must be in `CHARGED`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`; and `window_end_utc` must fall strictly after `window_start_utc`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Separation of duties applies here: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `performed_by_user_id` of this lot's most recent passing leak test, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Finally the window must fit the cell. Taking the cell's `cooldown_minutes` as the buffer, the candidate window conflicts with an already-recorded run test on the same cell when the candidate's `window_start_utc` falls strictly before that test's `window_end_utc` advanced by the buffer **and** that test's `window_start_utc` falls strictly before the candidate's `window_end_utc` advanced by the buffer. Every run test previously recorded against the cell participates in the check regardless of its own `verdict`, because a failing run still occupied the cell. Because both comparisons are strict, two windows separated by a gap of exactly the cooldown length are accepted while a gap one minute shorter is refused; and because windows are half-open, a candidate beginning exactly when an existing window ends is still refused unless the full cooldown gap is present. When a conflict is found the request is refused with `ERR_TEST_CELL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the earliest-created conflicting run test is the one identified in the error detail. Run tests are listable and filterable by `build_order_id`, by `test_cell_id`, and by `verdict`, and like leak tests they are append-only.

The verdict is computed in four steps. **First**, the measurement is corrected to the plant's 35.0-degree-Celsius rating point: multiply `measured_capacity_btuh` by one plus 0.0040 — four tenths of one percent per degree — times the number of degrees by which `measured_ambient_c` exceeds 35.0, which is a negative quantity when the cell ran cooler than the rating point, and round the product to a whole BTU per hour to give `corrected_capacity_btuh`. **Second**, two ratios are formed against the platform's published nameplate: `capacity_ratio` is `corrected_capacity_btuh` divided by the platform's `rated_capacity_btuh`, rounded to four decimal places; and the measured full-load efficiency is `corrected_capacity_btuh` divided by `measured_power_w`, whose quotient divided by the platform's `rated_eer` and rounded to four decimal places gives `efficiency_ratio`. **Third**, the two tolerances are read from a single cell of a two-dimensional table keyed jointly by the platform's `efficiency_tier` and the program's `flammability_class`. Both `applied_capacity_tolerance` and `applied_efficiency_tolerance` must come from the same cell:

- `STANDARD` tier on an `A1` program: capacity tolerance 0.9500, efficiency tolerance 0.9300.
- `STANDARD` tier on an `A2L` program: capacity tolerance 0.9400, efficiency tolerance 0.9200.
- `HIGH` tier on an `A1` program: capacity tolerance 0.9700, efficiency tolerance 0.9500.
- `HIGH` tier on an `A2L` program: capacity tolerance 0.9600, efficiency tolerance 0.9400.
- `PREMIUM` tier on an `A1` program: capacity tolerance 0.9800, efficiency tolerance 0.9600.
- `PREMIUM` tier on an `A2L` program: capacity tolerance 0.9800, efficiency tolerance 0.9600.

The table is deliberately **not** separable into a tier allowance plus a class allowance. A flammable refrigerant buys one hundredth of relief at the `STANDARD` and `HIGH` tiers, and buys nothing at all at `PREMIUM`, where the `A2L` column is identical to the `A1` column. Whatever the table yields, `applied_efficiency_tolerance` is never permitted below 0.90; with the values above that floor is never the binding constraint. **Fourth**, the `verdict` is `PASS` when `capacity_ratio` is at or above `applied_capacity_tolerance` **and** `efficiency_ratio` is at or above `applied_efficiency_tolerance`, and `FAIL` otherwise. Because the rounded four-decimal ratio is what gets compared, a ratio that rounds up onto its tolerance passes. The record also stamps `performed_by_user_id` and `performed_at`.

Recording the run test moves the lot exactly as the leak test does, out of `CHARGED` instead of `BRAZED`, and against the same shared repair budget. A `PASS` advances `CHARGED → RUN_PASSED` with `latest_run_verdict` set to `PASS`. A `FAIL` increments `rework_count`; if the incremented value is 2 or below the lot goes `CHARGED → REWORK` with `latest_run_verdict` set to `FAIL` **and `charge_grams` reset to 0**, because the refrigerant is recovered from the lot before it is opened for repair. If the increment would take `rework_count` past 2 the lot goes `CHARGED → SCRAPPED` with `closed_reason` set to `REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `charge_grams` reset to 0, and — exactly as on the leak-test scrap path — every `ACTIVE` warehouse reservation of the lot is released with `release_reason` `BUILD_SCRAPPED` and each touched warehouse recomputes `pallets_reserved_current`. The lot's `version` increments in all three cases, and `latest_run_verdict` always mirrors the lot's most recent run test ordered by `created_at` descending, reading null while none exists.

Releasing a lot to finished stock is the `ProductionPlanner`'s closing act and advances `RUN_PASSED → RELEASED_TO_STOCK`. The lot must be in `RUN_PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`, and the platform must be `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLATFORM_STATE` — a restricted platform's already-fabricated stock is still allowed to complete. A pre-season lot carries one extra condition: at least one warehouse reservation of that lot must have `status` `ACTIVE` and must cover the logical clock's date, meaning its `start_date` is at or before that date and its `end_date` strictly after it; otherwise the release is refused with `ERR_NO_WAREHOUSE_RESERVATION`. On success `released_to_stock_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the lot's `version` increments; the platform then recomputes `units_built_released` as the summed `quantity_units` of its lots whose `status` is `RELEASED_TO_STOCK`, recomputes `rolling_claim_rate_pct` and `warranty_posture` per §3.9, and increments its own `version`.

A lot can also be closed without ever being built. Cancellation is a `ProductionPlanner` act admitted only from `DRAFT` or `HOLD_REGULATORY`, giving `DRAFT → CANCELLED` and `HOLD_REGULATORY → CANCELLED`; from any other stage it is refused with `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`. An optional `reason` may accompany the request and is stored in `closed_reason`; when the cancellation instead arrives from the platform retirement cascade, `closed_reason` is set to `PLATFORM_RETIRED`. Either way every `ACTIVE` warehouse reservation of the lot becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `BUILD_CANCELLED`, each touched warehouse recomputes `pallets_reserved_current`, and the versions of the lot, of each released reservation, and of each touched warehouse all increment. The system-driven `DRAFT → HOLD_REGULATORY` move, and its siblings out of `COIL_FAB`, `BRAZED`, `REWORK`, `LEAK_PASSED`, `CHARGED`, and `RUN_PASSED`, belong to the sell-through cascade of §3.1: they set `hold_reason` to `REFRIGERANT_SELL_THROUGH` and release the lot's active reservations as a consequence of the hold. `RELEASED_TO_STOCK`, `SCRAPPED`, and `CANCELLED` are the three terminal stages of a lot.

### 3.9. Warranty Feedback: Claim Rate, Design Posture, and Supplier Corrective Action

Field failures are the plant's only honest measurement of its own design and supplier quality, so filing a claim is not a bookkeeping act — it can restrict a design and cut a supplier's monthly allocation within the same request. A `WarrantyAnalyst` files a **warranty claim** naming the `platform_id` the failed unit belongs to, the `failure_code` describing the field failure mode — one of `COMPRESSOR_FAILURE`, `CONTROL_BOARD_FAULT`, `COIL_LEAK`, `CHARGE_LOSS`, or `FAN_MOTOR` — and the `claim_cost_cents` the failure cost the plant, a whole number of cents at zero or above. Where the lot is known, `build_order_id` names it and must resolve, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; where it is not, the field is omitted and defaults to null. Where a constrained component is blamed, `component_type` names it as either `COMPRESSOR` or `CONTROL_BOARD` and `supplier_lot_code` names the lot to be matched against a component supply pool's own `supplier_lot_code`. Those two fields move together: supplying exactly one of them without the other is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a claim that names neither is a non-component failure which never opens a corrective action. The platform must resolve, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`. A filed claim opens with `status` `OPEN`, `filed_by_user_id` recording the analyst's `X-User-ID`, and `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock — that timestamp, together with a shipped order's `shipped_at`, is what the rolling warranty window reads. Claims are listable and filterable by `platform_id`, by `build_order_id`, by `status`, by `component_type`, by `supplier_lot_code`, and by `failure_code`.

Filing then triggers a four-hop cascade. **The first hop** rescores the platform. The counted claim population is every warranty claim of that platform whose `status` is `OPEN` or `LINKED_TO_CAPA`; claims that have reached `CLOSED` drop out of the population entirely. That count is reported as `counted_claim_count`. The denominator, reported as `denominator_units`, is the platform's `units_built_released` or 1, whichever is larger — the floor of one unit exists purely to keep the division defined. The `rolling_claim_rate_pct` is then 100 multiplied by `counted_claim_count`, divided by `denominator_units`, rounded to two decimal places, and it is stored on the platform. That stored figure always uses the built basis; the shipped basis exists only as a read-only cohort view for the reliability team and is never written to the platform. The recomputation runs on claim filing, on corrective-action closure, on a lot's release to stock, and on an order's shipment, and the platform's `version` increments each time.

`warranty_posture` is then derived from that rate, and it is gated before it is banded. When the platform's `units_built_released` is below 20 units, or `counted_claim_count` is below 3, `warranty_posture` is `OK` no matter what the rate says — two claims against five units is a 40 percent rate and it is noise, not a signal. Once both gates are cleared the bands are evaluated from the top down: a `rolling_claim_rate_pct` of 10.00 or more yields `DESIGN_CHANGE_REQUIRED`; otherwise a rate of 5.00 or more yields `CAPA_REQUIRED`; otherwise `OK`. Both thresholds are inclusive against the two-decimal rate, so exactly 5.00 percent is `CAPA_REQUIRED` while 4.99 percent is `OK`. The two non-`OK` postures do different work: `DESIGN_CHANGE_REQUIRED` blocks the creation of any new build lot for that platform, as §3.8 describes, while `CAPA_REQUIRED` does not block builds but does arm the supplier cascade below. Either posture is enough to arm it.

**The second hop** opens a **supplier corrective action**, but only when three conditions all hold: the platform's posture after the recompute is `CAPA_REQUIRED` or `DESIGN_CHANGE_REQUIRED`; the filing claim carries a non-null `component_type` whose `supplier_lot_code` matches a component supply pool; and no corrective action with `status` `OPEN` already cites that same pool. The pool is resolved as the component supply whose `component_type` equals the claim's `component_type`, whose `supplier_lot_code` equals the claim's `supplier_lot_code`, whose `part_number` equals the platform's part number for that component type — `compressor_part_number` for a compressor, `control_board_part_number` for a control board — and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, taking the earliest `created_at` when more than one month of that lot is still on hand. **When no pool matches, no corrective action is created and the claim stays `OPEN` — but the platform's posture change stands regardless.**

The size of the cut is read from a second two-dimensional table, keyed by the claim's `component_type` and by which band the platform's `rolling_claim_rate_pct` falls into. The bands are inclusive at the bottom and exclusive at the top, so a rate of exactly 7.50 belongs to the middle band:

- A rate from 5.00 up to but not including 7.50: `COMPRESSOR` is cut 25 percent, `CONTROL_BOARD` is cut 25 percent.
- A rate from 7.50 up to but not including 10.00: `COMPRESSOR` is cut 50 percent, `CONTROL_BOARD` is cut 25 percent.
- A rate of 10.00 or above: `COMPRESSOR` is cut 100 percent, `CONTROL_BOARD` is cut 50 percent.

This table is likewise not the product of a component factor and a band factor. A compressor is a long-lead, single-source part, so a severe field rate stops the lot outright; a control board is second-sourced, so even the worst band only halves it, and the control-board column is flat at 25 percent across the two lower bands while the compressor column is not.

The new corrective action opens with `status` `OPEN` and records the pool it governs in `component_supply_id`, the claim that crossed the threshold in `warranty_claim_id`, the `supplier_lot_code` and `component_type` copied from that claim, the platform's `rolling_claim_rate_pct` at the instant of opening in `trigger_claim_rate_pct` at two decimal places, the cut it applies in `derate_pct` — which is only ever 25, 50, or 100 — and in `cited_claim_count` the number of warranty claims it swept in. That sweep is the rest of the hop: every claim of the same platform, `component_type`, and `supplier_lot_code` whose `status` is `OPEN` moves `OPEN → LINKED_TO_CAPA` with its `version` incremented, and `cited_claim_count` is exactly the count of claims of that same platform, component type, and supplier lot whose `status` is `OPEN` or `LINKED_TO_CAPA`. The governed pool moves `ACTIVE → DERATED` (or `EXHAUSTED → DERATED`) with `derate_pct` set from the table, `effective_allocatable_units` recomputed by the truncating formula of §3.5, and its `version` incremented. The action's `resolution`, `effectiveness_note`, `closed_by_user_id`, and `closed_at` are all null while it remains open. Corrective actions are listable and filterable by `component_supply_id`, by `warranty_claim_id`, by `status`, and by `component_type`, and are also readable as a sub-collection of the pool they govern.

**The third hop** is caused by the pool's new, smaller `effective_allocatable_units`. Allocation lines on that pool whose `status` is `RESERVED` are revoked in descending `created_at` order — newest first — until `available_units` is back at zero or above, each revocation dragging its kit siblings with it exactly as §3.5 describes. **The fourth hop** is caused by those revocations: every distributor order that lost lines recomputes `allocated_units` and regresses its `allocation_status` to `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` or `UNALLOCATED` accordingly, while its `fulfillment_status` is left completely untouched. A staged order does not un-stage because its components were revoked; the two dimensions remain independent.

A corrective action is closed by a `ReliabilityEngineer` who is not the analyst who filed the cited claim. The action must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cited claim's `filed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `resolution` supplied on the request selects one of two sharply divergent branches, and an optional `effectiveness_note` records the closer's free-text findings on either one.

A `resolution` of `EFFECTIVE` moves the action `OPEN → CLOSED_EFFECTIVE`. Its `resolution` is stored as `EFFECTIVE`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_at` are stamped from the caller and the logical clock, and its `version` increments. The governed pool moves `DERATED → ACTIVE` — permitted only when no other corrective action with `status` `OPEN` still cites that pool — with `derate_pct` returned to 0, `effective_allocatable_units` restored to the full `total_allocatable_units`, `available_units` recomputed, and its `version` incremented. Every cited claim whose `status` is `LINKED_TO_CAPA` moves `LINKED_TO_CAPA → CLOSED` with its `version` incremented, which drops it out of the counted claim population; the platform's `rolling_claim_rate_pct` and `warranty_posture` are therefore recomputed and the rate falls, potentially relaxing the posture, with the platform's `version` incremented.

A `resolution` of `INEFFECTIVE` moves the action `OPEN → CLOSED_INEFFECTIVE`. Its `resolution` is stored as `INEFFECTIVE`, `closed_by_user_id` and `closed_at` are stamped identically, and its `version` increments. The governed pool moves `DERATED → CLOSED` with `derate_pct` set to 100 and `effective_allocatable_units` set to 0, and its `version` increments; because that leaves nothing drawable, every allocation line on the pool whose `status` is `RESERVED` is revoked with `closed_reason` set to `SUPPLY_CLOSED` in descending `created_at` order, and every distributor order that lost lines recomputes `allocated_units` and regresses its `allocation_status`. The cited claims are **not** closed on this branch: they stay `LINKED_TO_CAPA`, so they remain in the counted population, the platform's `rolling_claim_rate_pct` is unchanged, and `warranty_posture` stays exactly where it was. `CLOSED_EFFECTIVE` and `CLOSED_INEFFECTIVE` are both terminal. The asymmetry is the point: an effective fix restores the supplier's allocation and clears the claims off the platform's record, while an ineffective one kills the supply row for good and leaves the claims counted against the design.

Finally, a computed **warranty analysis** read lets the reliability team see the same arithmetic under either denominator. It accepts an optional `basis` parameter of `BUILT_BASIS` or `SHIPPED_BASIS`, defaulting to `BUILT_BASIS`, and returns the `platform_id`, the `basis` it evaluated, the `counted_claim_count`, the `denominator_units` that basis produced — `units_built_released` or 1 under the built basis, `units_shipped_total` or 1 under the shipped basis — the resulting `rolling_claim_rate_pct`, and the `warranty_posture`. The posture is always the one the **stored** built basis produces, whichever basis was requested for the rate, and the read stores nothing and increments no `version`.
