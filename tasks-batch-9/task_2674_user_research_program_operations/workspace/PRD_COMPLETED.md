# User Research Program Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A product organisation that takes research seriously accumulates a peculiar kind of debt. Requests for research arrive faster than they can be fielded; the loudest request wins rather than the one attached to the most expensive decision; recordings and transcripts pile up long past the retention period a participant agreed to; the same twelve customers get contacted for every study until they stop answering; and six months later nobody can say which piece of evidence actually moved a roadmap decision. This document specifies the API for a User Research Program Operations platform that treats each of those failures as a first-class constraint rather than a process guideline. Every research request is scored against the decision it claims to inform. Every participant contact is metered against a contact-fatigue window. Every recording, transcript and personally identifying note is tethered to a consent record with a hard retention horizon, and when that consent falls away the platform withdraws the evidence, re-scores the findings that rested on it, re-weighs the repository themes those findings rolled into, and — if confidence drops below the bar — unlocks the roadmap decision that was locked on the strength of it.

The platform serves five kinds of operator whose duties are deliberately separated: product and design partners who own decisions and answer readouts, researchers who run studies end to end, research leads who triage intake and publish findings, privacy officers who own consent and retention, and research operations administrators who own panels, incentive money and the batch invite selector. Separation of duties is not decorative here. A research lead may author a finding, which is precisely why publishing a finding is refused when the publisher is also its author — the platform makes the constraint observable rather than trusting a convention.

Two further properties shape the whole surface. First, money is a conserved quantity: a participant pool's incentive budget is partitioned at all times into available, reserved and paid cents, and every reservation, release, forfeiture and payment is a movement between those three buckets rather than a fresh number. Second, time is explicit. The server runs on a logical clock that tests and operators drive directly; nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Deadlines, retention horizons, rolling contact windows and cycle-time metrics all read that single clock, and time-driven state changes only happen when a sweep is asked to apply them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the two test-driving endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header whose value is nothing but whitespace is indistinguishable from an empty one and is rejected the same way. No further processing takes place; the platform never falls back on a default role or an anonymous identity.

There are exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows:

- `Stakeholder` — the product or design partner who owns roadmap decisions, raises research requests, responds to readouts and opens follow-up action items.
- `Researcher` — runs studies end to end: writes moderator guides, invites and screens participants, schedules and moderates sessions, registers artifacts, drafts findings and drafts readouts.
- `ResearchLead` — triages intake, approves moderator guides and publishes findings. A `ResearchLead` may also author findings, which is exactly why publication carries a separate author check.
- `PrivacyOfficer` — owns consent and retention: grants and revokes consent, runs the retention sweep, and reads every artifact and evidence link.
- `ResearchOpsAdmin` — owns research operations: participant pools, panel enrollment, the batch invite selector, incentive settlement, the logical clock and the retention sweep.

If `X-User-Role` holds any string outside those five, or holds one of the five but that role is not listed as authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The authorised-role list for each endpoint is given in the §4 table and is the single source of truth for this check.

**Operator identity is opaque for all five roles.** When `X-User-Role` is `Stakeholder`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it in several places — as `requester_user_id` on a `ResearchRequest` at intake, and as the `owner_user_id` a follow-up is opened against — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `Researcher`, `ResearchLead`, `PrivacyOfficer` and `ResearchOpsAdmin`: each of those roles supplies an opaque `X-User-ID` which the server may persist (as `lead_user_id` when a study is opened, as `moderator_user_id` when a session is booked, as `authored_by_user_id` when a finding is drafted) but which is never resolved against a stored entity identifier. Ownership and separation-of-duties checks in this system are therefore always **string comparisons between `X-User-ID` and a previously recorded operator-id field**, never foreign-key lookups.

Three such comparisons exist. Reframing a research request requires that `X-User-ID` equal the request's recorded `requester_user_id`. Responding to a readout requires that `X-User-ID` equal the `requester_user_id` of the `ResearchRequest` reached from the readout's study through that study's `research_request_id`. Completing a follow-up requires that `X-User-ID` equal that follow-up's `owner_user_id`. Any of the three failing yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Separately, publishing a finding requires that `X-User-ID` **differ** from the finding's `authored_by_user_id`; if they are equal the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; the prefix is followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`-`Z` and the digits `0`-`9`, and a fresh value is minted for every response including every error response.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from host time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources. Those two values and the `"error"` of the error envelope are the only three values this field ever takes.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for the paginated list endpoints of §2.3 and only for those. An array payload that is not a paginated list carries only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` in `meta`: the batch-invitation response is the single case, answering with `response_type` `"array"` and the recruitments it created in `data`. The one 204 response in this API carries no body at all, not an envelope with an empty `data`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and must be at least 0. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being clamped silently. Both parameters must parse as whole decimal integers: a non-numeric value, a value with a fractional part such as `2.5`, and a parameter supplied with an empty value (`?limit=`) are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while surrounding whitespace is tolerated. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error — it yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. Query parameters the endpoint does not define are ignored.

List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"` and populate four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective starting index), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Unless an endpoint's description in §4 names a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four list endpoints deliberately depart from that default and their orderings are contractual: the research requests raised against one decision are ordered by `priority_score` descending, then `created_at` ascending; the repository theme index is ordered by `evidence_weight` descending, then `created_at` ascending; the findings list is ordered by `evidence_strength` descending, then `created_at` ascending; and a readout's follow-ups are ordered by `due_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock can stamp many rows with the same instant, every one of these orderings — the `created_at` default included — takes **server insertion order** as its final tie-break, so rows sharing every sort key come back in the order they were created. On the per-decision research-request listing a request that has not been triaged yet carries a null `priority_score`; those rows sort **after** every scored row, and among themselves by `created_at` ascending then insertion order.

Filters on list endpoints are additive: supplying several narrows the result set, and pagination is applied after filtering. The filters available per endpoint are named in §3 alongside the workflows they support. Filter values are matched exactly and case-sensitively. An enumerated filter carrying a value outside its declared members is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an empty value counts as outside them, so `?status=` is rejected rather than ignored; the same holds for the one boolean filter, `is_admissible`, which accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and rejects everything else — the empty value included — with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two free-text filters, `segment` on a pool's participant listing and `label_contains` on the theme index, are taken verbatim, so an empty value is a real filter rather than an omission: `segment` is compared for exact equality and an empty value therefore matches nothing, while `label_contains` is a substring test and an empty value matches every theme. An id-valued filter, by contrast, is treated as omitted when it is supplied empty.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a canonical lower-case hyphenated UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`), stamped from the logical clock at creation time. Every field a resource declares is **always present** in that resource's JSON projection, carrying `null` when it has no value; no field is ever omitted, and no field beyond the declared set plus `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` is ever exposed. Timestamp fields render in the `created_at` format or as `null`; decimal fields render as JSON numbers as described in §2.8; money fields render as JSON integers; array-valued and object-valued fields render as JSON arrays and objects respectively.

All thirteen resource types in this system — `RoadmapDecision`, `ResearchRequest`, `ParticipantPool`, `Participant`, `Study`, `Recruitment`, `ConsentRecord`, `ResearchArtifact`, `Theme`, `Finding`, `EvidenceLink`, `Readout` and `FollowUp` — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format as `created_at`; at creation it equals `created_at`, and it is refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of that resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. A mutation here means any change the platform makes to the row, whether the client asked for it directly or a cascade produced it: when a no-show forfeits a reservation and the pool's ledger fields move as a consequence, the pool's own `version` and `updated_at` advance too. Three consequences of reading "mutation" as *an actual change of stored state* are load-bearing. First, the counter advances **once per request per row, not once per field written**: a request that touches several fields of one row, and a cascade that walks back over the same row repeatedly, both leave that row exactly one version higher than it started — which is why a settlement walk that moves the ledger many times still "increments the pool's version by 1". Second, a write that leaves every stored field at the value it already held is not a mutation at all, so neither `version` nor `updated_at` moves; repeating an idempotent write, or running a recompute whose inputs have not changed, is observable as a version that stands still. Third, every row mutated by one request receives the same `updated_at`, and the representation a mutating request returns already carries the incremented `version` and the refreshed `updated_at` rather than the pre-mutation pair.

Two `version` counters are load-bearing beyond bookkeeping, because clients are required to echo them back — see §2.9.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical clock that is the sole source of "now" for the entire platform. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's wall-clock time. A `ResearchOpsAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, which answers **200 OK** with `response_type` `"object"` and a `data` object whose single key is `current_utc`, carrying the new instant in the same three-fractional-digit format. `to_iso8601` is required and must parse as an ISO 8601 instant, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a trailing `Z` and an explicit numeric UTC offset are both accepted and the value is normalised to UTC, and an instant supplied with no zone designator at all is read as UTC. The clock may be set backwards as freely as forwards, and setting it is not itself a mutation of any resource. Every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `granted_at`, `triaged_at`, `locked_at`, `fielded_at`, `fielding_closed_at`, `revoked_at`, `purged_at`, `published_at`, `delivered_at`, `responded_at` and `completed_at` value is read from that clock, as are the deadline arithmetic in priority scoring, the retention horizon arithmetic, the rolling contact-fatigue window and every cycle-time metric.

Moving the clock forward does **not** by itself expire a consent record, purge an artifact or mark a follow-up overdue. Time-driven transitions are applied only when the retention sweep capability is invoked, and the sweep applies exactly those transitions that are due at the clock's current value. This split — set the clock, then sweep — is what makes retention behaviour deterministic and testable.

### 2.6. The Order in Which Checks Run

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the platform evaluates checks in a fixed precedence and returns the **first** failure it finds. The order below is normative; an implementation that reports a later failure when an earlier one also applies is wrong, because the earlier code is the one the caller needs to act on.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation** — `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and is listed for the endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Ownership** — the three `X-User-ID` equality checks described in §2.1, on `POST /research-requests/{id}/resubmit`, `POST /readouts/{id}/respond` and `POST /follow-ups/{id}/complete`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (403).
4. **Segregation of duties** — on `POST /findings/{id}/publish`, `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Steps 3 and 4 sit ahead of existence resolution, so the wrong operator learns they are the wrong operator before they learn anything about the row's state — but both are comparisons against an operator id recorded on a row, and a row that is not there records nothing. When the resource addressed by the path does not exist, there is nothing to compare and the request falls through to the 404 of step 5 rather than being refused with a 403.
5. **Existence, resolved in the order the identifiers are listed below** — a referenced row that does not exist yields its own 404 code before any body validation, state check or business rule is considered. The list is a **total order over entity types, not over the places identifiers arrive from**: a body foreign key naming an earlier type outranks a path segment naming a later one, so a single-participant invitation carrying an unknown `participant_id` against an unknown study answers `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, and evidence-link creation carrying an unknown `artifact_id` against an unknown finding answers `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`. A body foreign key is resolved only when it is actually present as a non-empty string; absent, `null`, empty or of the wrong JSON type it is not an existence failure at all but a malformed field for step 6, which runs later. The same applies to the id-valued query filters of the list endpoints named below, except that there an empty value counts as omitted rather than as malformed. Note also that the `theme_id` filter of the findings list is deliberately not among the places a `Theme` is resolved, so an unknown `theme_id` there narrows the result to nothing instead of raising a 404:
   - a `RoadmapDecision` named in a path segment or in a `roadmap_decision_id` field, else `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, on decision locking, decision reads, the per-decision research-request listing, research-request intake, readout drafting and the readout listing;
   - a `ResearchRequest` named in a path segment or in `research_request_id`, else `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, on triage, resubmit, request reads, study creation and the study listing;
   - a `ParticipantPool` named in a path segment or in `participant_pool_id`, else `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, on participant enrollment, incentive settlement, pool reads, the pool's participant listing, study creation and the study listing;
   - a `Participant` named in a path segment or in `participant_id`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, on participant reads, single-participant invitation, consent creation, the recruitment listing and the consent-record listing;
   - a `Study` named in a path segment or in `study_id`, else `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, on every study-scoped path, study reads, the study metrics read, consent creation, finding creation, incentive settlement, and the recruitment, finding and readout listings;
   - a `Recruitment` named in a path segment, else `ERR_RECRUITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on every recruitment-scoped path, recruitment reads and a recruitment's artifact listing;
   - a `ConsentRecord` named in a path segment, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, on consent revocation and consent reads;
   - a `ResearchArtifact` named in a path segment or in `artifact_id`, else `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`, on artifact reads, an artifact's evidence-link listing and evidence-link creation;
   - a `Theme` named in a path segment, in `theme_id` or in `parent_theme_id`, else `ERR_THEME_NOT_FOUND`, on theme reads, the theme index, theme creation and finding creation;
   - a `Finding` named in a path segment or inside `cited_finding_ids`, else `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, on every finding-scoped path, finding reads and readout drafting;
   - an `EvidenceLink` named in a path segment, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_LINK_NOT_FOUND`, on evidence-link deletion;
   - a `Readout` named in a path segment, else `ERR_READOUT_NOT_FOUND`, on readout delivery, readout response, follow-up creation, readout reads and a readout's follow-up listing;
   - a `FollowUp` named in a path segment, else `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_FOUND`, on follow-up completion.
6. **Request-body and query-string validity** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This covers a missing required field, a field carrying the wrong JSON type (a number or array where a string is declared; where an integer is declared, a string, a boolean, or any JSON number written with a decimal point — `3.0` is as invalid as `3.5`; a non-boolean where a boolean is declared; a non-array, or an array with a non-string element, where an array of strings is declared; a non-object where an object is declared), a string that is empty where a non-empty value is declared or longer than its declared maximum, a timestamp field that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant, an enum field carrying a value outside its declared members — whether that enum arrived in the request body or as a query-string filter — an integer that is negative or zero where the constraint demands otherwise, `fielding_end` not strictly after `fielding_start`, `scheduled_end` not strictly after `scheduled_start`, a `screening_criteria` or `screening_answers` object that does not carry exactly its declared keys, a `scopes` array that is empty or contains duplicates or a member outside `RECORDING`, `TRANSCRIPT` and `PII_QUOTE`, a `count` below 1 on batch invitation, and a `limit` or `offset` outside the ranges in §2.3. Two conventions govern the edges of this step. A field explicitly set to `null` is treated exactly as an absent field, so `null` in a required field is the missing-field error and `null` in an optional field selects that field's documented default. And fields the endpoint does not declare are **ignored** wherever they appear at the top level of a body — a spare key is never itself an error, which is what makes a client-supplied `id` or an unexpected version field harmless; the only objects held to an exact key set are the nested `screening_criteria` and `screening_answers`. A request that carries no parsable JSON body is read as an empty object and therefore fails on the first required field it lacks, whereas a body that parses as JSON but is not an object — an array, a bare string, a number — is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **State legality** — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) when the requested transition does not exist out of the resource's current state. This single code also covers rescheduling beyond the reschedule limit, closing fielding while a recruitment is still `SCHEDULED`, creating a second study for a request already `IN_STUDY`, and writing or deleting evidence links on a finding that is `PUBLISHED` or `RETRACTED`.
8. **Guide state** — `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_APPROVED` (409) when a study's `guide_status` is not what the operation needs: approving a guide that is not `DRAFT`, or fielding a study whose guide is not `APPROVED`.
9. **Governing-decision state** — `ERR_DECISION_LOCKED` (409) when the governing `RoadmapDecision` is `LOCKED`; this guards study fielding and a readout response whose `outcome` is `ACCEPTED`. A `DEFERRED` response never consults the decision's lock at all.
10. **Remaining state-machine guards, in this order** — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) on resubmit; `ERR_RECRUITMENT_CLOSED` (409) on both invitation endpoints; `ERR_POOL_CLOSED` (409) on both invitation endpoints; `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED` (409) on batch invitation only; `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_PERMITTED` (409) and then `ERR_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409) on incentive settlement; `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_FINDINGS` (409) on readout delivery.
11. **Stale-version rejection** — `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION` (412) on readout drafting and `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (412) on incentive settlement, as specified in §2.9.
12. **Business rules** — evaluated last, in this order: `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`, `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_MISSING`, `ERR_ARTIFACT_PURGED`, `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_FIELDING`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SESSION_CONFLICT`, `ERR_POOL_FIELDING_CAPACITY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECRUITMENT`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUPPRESSED`, `ERR_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_METHOD_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_THEME_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_STUDY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CITATION_STUDY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NO_ADMISSIBLE_EVIDENCE`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CORROBORATION`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_TOO_WEAK`, `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_LOCK_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_INCENTIVE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_LABEL`. The trigger condition for each of these is stated in the §3 narrative that owns the workflow it guards.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve calling out because they are easy to get backwards. A request to lock an already-`LOCKED` decision fails at step 7 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not at step 12 with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_LOCK_THRESHOLD`, even when confidence is also short of the bar. And a batch invitation against a `CLOSED` pool fails with `ERR_POOL_CLOSED` rather than `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED`, because the closed-pool check precedes the restricted-pool check.

### 2.7. Nested Resource Paths

Many resources are addressed through a parent segment — participants inside a pool, recruitments and readouts inside a study, artifacts inside a recruitment, evidence links inside a finding, follow-ups inside a readout. Path parameters are always resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Asking for the artifacts of a recruitment id that does not exist yields `ERR_RECRUITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, never an empty list. When a nested creation carries the parent's identifier in the path, the stored foreign-key field on the new row is taken from that path segment and not from the body: a participant's `pool_id`, a recruitment's `study_id`, an artifact's `recruitment_id`, an evidence link's `finding_id` and a follow-up's `readout_id` are all filled in this way.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Unless a specific rule says otherwise, every derived decimal quantity in this system is rounded to **2 decimal places using round-half-up** — that is, a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so a raw 0.125 stored at two decimals becomes 0.13. Scores (`priority_score`, `screen_score`), strengths and weights (`evidence_strength`, `contribution`, `own_evidence_weight`, `evidence_weight`), confidences (`evidence_confidence`, `decision_confidence_before`, `decision_confidence_after`) and the impact score all follow that rule. Rounding fixes the *value*, not its printed shape: every one of these quantities is carried in JSON as an ordinary number rather than as a string, so trailing zeros are not preserved and a value rounded to 0.60 appears as `0.6`, a value rounded to 0.00 appears as `0.0`, and a whole-numbered score appears as e.g. `46.0`. Comparisons against the thresholds in this document are always comparisons of the rounded numeric value.

Three departures are deliberate and are restated where they matter. First, all money in this platform is **integer cents** — `incentive_budget_cents`, `incentive_reserved_cents`, `incentive_paid_cents`, `incentive_available_cents`, `last_estimate_cents`, `incentive_amount_cents`, `reserved_cents` and `paid_cents` are integers throughout, and the only rounding that touches money is the surcharge term of a reservation, which is rounded **up** to the next whole cent. Second, two intermediate quantities are held at **4 decimal places** before their final 2-decimal rounding: the evidence density term inside the corroborated strength variant, and the mean published-finding strength inside a readout's confidence contribution. The recruitment-efficiency ratio reported by the metrics read is likewise a 4-decimal value. Third, whole-day counts — days to a decision deadline, study cycle time, fielding duration — are **floored** to a whole number of days, where one day means 86,400 seconds.

Pool utilization is never rounded at all. It is compared as an exact ratio of integer cents, so a pool holding 800 reserved cents against an 1,000-cent budget sits exactly at the 0.80 boundary rather than near it.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Rejection on Study and Pool Writes

Two write operations require the client to prove that its view of a parent resource is current. Both use the same mechanism: a **non-optional** field in the request body carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent, compared for exact equality against that parent's stored `version`.

Drafting a readout against a study requires `expected_study_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Study`. If that value does not equal the study's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created. This check exists because a new draft readout legitimately supersedes any earlier draft for the same study, so no state precondition can catch a writer whose picture of the study — its findings, its counters, its prior draft — has gone out of date; the version equality is the only thing that can. On success the study's `version` is incremented by 1, which means a client that drafts two readouts in sequence must re-read the study between them.

Running an incentive settlement against a participant pool requires `expected_pool_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ParticipantPool`. A mismatch with the pool's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no money moves. Settlements for different studies draw on the same pool budget and each one legitimately succeeds on its own merits, so again no state precondition covers the case; the version equality stops a settlement that was computed against an out-of-date view of the ledger. A successful settlement increments the pool's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Fielding a study, screening a recruitment, publishing a finding, revoking consent and every other mutation are guarded by state preconditions and business rules alone, and supplying an unexpected version field to them has no effect.

### 2.10. The Incentive Ledger Invariant

A participant pool's money is a partition, not a set of independent counters. At every observable instant, and for every pool, the following must hold: `incentive_available_cents` plus `incentive_reserved_cents` plus `incentive_paid_cents` equals `incentive_budget_cents`. `incentive_available_cents` is always derived as the budget minus reserved minus paid, never accumulated independently.

Consequently every money-moving operation is a transfer. Inviting a participant moves cents from available into reserved. Withdrawing an invitation moves exactly the same number of cents — the recruitment's stored `reserved_cents`, not a recomputed amount — from reserved back to available. A no-show moves that same amount out of reserved. Settlement moves cents from reserved into paid for the rows it pays and back into available for the rows it releases. `last_estimate_cents` is the one exception to the partition and is explicitly outside it: it is an advisory projection written by a non-binding settlement run and it never moves money.

### 2.11. Derived Postures and Recompute Discipline

Several enumerated fields in this system are **derived**, meaning no client ever sets them: a pool's `status`, a participant's `status`, a theme's `status`, a request's `priority_score` and `recommended_method`, a finding's `evidence_strength` and `strength_variant`, a decision's `evidence_confidence`, and every counter field (`participant_count`, `suppressed_participant_count`, `contacts_in_window`, `invited_count`, `screened_in_count`, `completed_count`, `admissible_link_count`, `distinct_participant_count`, `reuse_count`, `depth`). Each is recomputed by a named rule at the moments §3 specifies, stored on the row, and returned by reads. A derived field is never left stale behind a mutation that could have changed it, and recomputing it counts as a mutation of its row for the purposes of `updated_at` and `version` in §2.4 — but only when the recompute actually lands a different value, since a recompute that reproduces the value already stored changes nothing and therefore bumps nothing.

Recompute order matters wherever one derived value feeds another. The platform never recomputes a parent before its children: theme weights roll up from a theme to its ancestors one level at a time, and a decision's confidence is recomputed only after the findings and readouts it draws on have settled. The single longest chain in the system is the five-hop evidence-withdrawal cascade described in §3.6, which starts at a consent record and ends at a roadmap decision; every hop of it is mandatory and runs to completion inside the request that triggered it, because this platform's consistency model is immediate — there are no deferred jobs and no eventual convergence.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Decisions to Inform, and the Intake Funnel That Serves Them

Everything in this platform hangs off a **roadmap decision** — the product choice research is expected to inform. A `Stakeholder` (or a `ResearchOpsAdmin` acting on their behalf) registers one by supplying a `title`, which must be non-empty and at most 200 characters; a `decision_deadline`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant by which the choice must be made; a `reversibility` of either `IRREVERSIBLE` or `REVERSIBLE`, describing how costly the decision would be to undo; and an `impacted_user_count`, a whole number of end users the decision touches that must be zero or greater. A newly registered decision opens in `status` `OPEN`, with `evidence_confidence` at 0.00 and `locked_at` null.

Research requests are raised against exactly one decision. Intake takes the `roadmap_decision_id` of the decision the request claims to inform, a `question` — the framed research question, non-empty and at most 500 characters — a `question_type` drawn from `DISCOVERY`, `EVALUATIVE`, `BEHAVIORAL_LONGITUDINAL` and `MEASUREMENT`, and an `evidence_gap` of `NONE`, `PARTIAL` or `FULL` describing how much existing evidence is missing. The request lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0, its `requester_user_id` set to the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, and `priority_score`, `recommended_method`, `approved_method`, `triage_reason` and `triaged_at` all null. Scoring does not happen at intake — nothing is stamped on the request until a `ResearchLead` triages it.

**Triage is where the two intake scoring rules run**, and they run on *every* outcome, not only on approval. The `ResearchLead` supplies an `outcome` of `APPROVED`, `NEEDS_REFRAMING` or `REJECTED`, optionally a `reason` — a string with no maximum length which may be the empty string, and an empty string is stored verbatim rather than read as absent — and an optional `method_override`, which is checked against the four method members on *every* outcome, so a value outside them is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even on a `REJECTED` triage, while the value itself is consumed only on the `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` transition and otherwise simply discarded. Whichever outcome is chosen, the request's `priority_score` and `recommended_method` are recomputed against the current logical clock and stored, `triaged_at` is set to the clock instant, and `triage_reason` is set to the supplied `reason` (or to null when none was supplied). A `SUBMITTED` request may go to `APPROVED`, to `NEEDS_REFRAMING`, or straight to `REJECTED`. A `NEEDS_REFRAMING` request has exactly one triage outcome available to it — `REJECTED`; asking for `APPROVED` on a request that is sitting in `NEEDS_REFRAMING` is not a transition this state machine has, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On the `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` transition, and only there, `approved_method` is stamped: it takes the value of `method_override` when triage supplied one, and otherwise the freshly computed `recommended_method`. The `SUBMITTED → NEEDS_REFRAMING` and both `→ REJECTED` transitions leave `approved_method` null.

The **priority score** is a weighted composite of three inputs plus an urgency multiplier, and its evaluation order is pinned. Only two rows feed it: the single `RoadmapDecision` named by the request's `roadmap_decision_id` — whatever that decision's own `status` happens to be — and the request's own `evidence_gap`. Nothing else enters the score. The steps, in order:

1. **Decision points.** An `IRREVERSIBLE` decision contributes 40.0 points; a `REVERSIBLE` one contributes 22.0.
2. **Gap points.** An `evidence_gap` of `NONE` contributes 0.0, `PARTIAL` contributes 12.0, and `FULL` contributes 25.0.
3. **Reach points, computed first among the three.** Divide the decision's `impacted_user_count` by 10,000, round that quotient **up** to the next whole number of ten-thousand-user buckets, and award 5.0 points per bucket — then cap the result at 30.0 points. A decision touching a single user therefore already earns a full 5.0-point bucket, one touching exactly 10,000 users still earns 5.0, one touching 10,001 earns 10.0, and anything at or above 60,000 users is held at the 30.0 ceiling. A decision touching zero users earns zero reach points.
4. **Base.** Add decision points, gap points and reach points together.
5. **Urgency multiplier.** Take the difference between the decision's `decision_deadline` and the current logical clock, express it in whole days by flooring the number of 86,400-second periods, and read the tier: 14 days or fewer gives a multiplier of 1.30; more than 14 but 45 days or fewer gives 1.15; more than 45 days gives 1.00.
6. **Cap, then round.** Multiply the base by the multiplier, hold the product at a ceiling of 100.0, and round the capped value half-up to two decimal places. The rounding happens exactly once, at the end — never on the base and never on the product before the cap.

The **method recommendation** runs in the same triage call and is a small decision table over `question_type`: a `DISCOVERY` question recommends `INTERVIEW`, an `EVALUATIVE` question recommends `USABILITY_TEST`, a `BEHAVIORAL_LONGITUDINAL` question recommends `DIARY_STUDY`, and a `MEASUREMENT` question recommends `SURVEY`. One override exists, because a measurement question over too small an audience is statistically under-powered: when `question_type` is `MEASUREMENT` **and** the referenced decision's `impacted_user_count` is below 5,000, the recommendation is downgraded to `INTERVIEW` instead. No rounding is involved; the output is one of the four method enumeration values.

Reframing is the requester's move, not the lead's. The operator whose `X-User-ID` matches the request's `requester_user_id` resubmits with a fresh `question`, `question_type` and `evidence_gap`, carrying the request `NEEDS_REFRAMING → SUBMITTED`. All three submitted values overwrite the stored ones verbatim, `revision_round` is incremented by 1, and `priority_score`, `recommended_method` and `approved_method` are all cleared back to null so that nothing stale survives the loop. The two triage stamps are deliberately *not* cleared: `triage_reason` and `triaged_at` keep whatever the last triage wrote, so a request back in `SUBMITTED` still carries the record of why it was sent for reframing. At most **two** reframing rounds are permitted: a resubmit attempted while `revision_round` already stands at 2 is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and from that point the only way the request can leave `NEEDS_REFRAMING` is a triage `REJECTED`, whose `reason` lands in `triage_reason` as the record of why the loop was closed off. Anyone other than the recorded requester attempting a resubmit is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.

Two transitions on a request are driven by the platform rather than by an operator. When the first study is opened against an `APPROVED` request, the request moves `APPROVED → IN_STUDY`, and while it sits there no second study may be created for it — the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When a `Stakeholder` accepts a readout of that study, the request moves `IN_STUDY → CLOSED`, which is terminal: neither triage nor resubmit will be entertained afterwards. `REJECTED` is the other terminal state.

Locking a decision is the `Stakeholder`'s prerogative and the platform's strongest statement that research is done. On the `OPEN → LOCKED` transition the decision's `evidence_confidence` is recomputed from its accepted readouts (the rule is spelled out in §3.8) and stored, and the transition is permitted only when that recomputed confidence is **at least 0.60**; below that bar the request is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_LOCK_THRESHOLD`. A refused lock stores nothing at all: the recomputed figure is discarded, the decision's stored `evidence_confidence` is left exactly as it was, and neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` moves. On success `locked_at` is set to the current logical clock instant. A `LOCKED` decision is frozen against further fielding: no study whose research request points at it may be put into the field, and no readout naming it may be responded to — both are refused with `ERR_DECISION_LOCKED`. Attempting to lock a decision that is already `LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The lock is not permanent, though: should the evidence later be withdrawn and the recomputed `evidence_confidence` fall below 0.60, the platform moves the decision `LOCKED → OPEN`, overwrites `evidence_confidence` with the recomputed value and clears `locked_at` back to null.

Stakeholders reviewing a decision's pipeline can list the research requests raised against it, optionally narrowing by `status`; that listing is ordered by `priority_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending, so the most urgent unserved question sits at the top.

### 3.2. Panels, Contact Fatigue and the Posture of a Pool

Participants are never recruited from thin air; they come from a **participant pool**, which is both a recruitment source and a budget envelope. A `ResearchOpsAdmin` creates one with a `name` that must be non-empty, carries no maximum length, and must be unique across all pools — uniqueness is exact, case-sensitive string equality, so two names differing only in letter case coexist happily while a true collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LABEL`, and that collision is checked only after every field of the body has passed validation — a `pool_type` of `CUSTOMER_LIST`, `EXTERNAL_PANEL` or `INTERNAL_DOGFOOD`, a `contact_cap_per_window` of at least 1, a `contact_window_days` of at least 1, and an `incentive_budget_cents` of zero or more. A fresh pool opens with `status` `OPEN`, `incentive_reserved_cents` and `incentive_paid_cents` both at 0, `incentive_available_cents` equal to the whole `incentive_budget_cents`, `settled_study_ids` an empty list, `last_estimate_cents` null, and both `participant_count` and `suppressed_participant_count` at 0.

Enrollment adds a pseudonymous person to that pool. The `ResearchOpsAdmin` supplies an `external_ref` — the person's handle in the source system, non-empty and unique inside the pool under the same exact, case-sensitive comparison, with a collision refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF` — a non-empty `segment` label describing their behaviour, a non-empty BCP-47 `locale`, and a `pii_on_file` boolean recording whether directly identifying data is held for them (true when omitted). The participant's `pool_id` comes from the path the enrollment was addressed to. They begin `ELIGIBLE`, with `contacts_in_window` at 0, `no_show_strikes` at 0 and `last_contacted_at` null. Enrollment raises the pool's `participant_count` by one and re-derives the pool's posture, because the suppression ratio that feeds posture has just changed denominator.

**Contact fatigue is metered by a rolling window.** A participant's `contacts_in_window` is the number of recruitment rows naming that participant whose `created_at` falls **strictly after** the instant obtained by subtracting `contact_window_days` days from the current logical clock, and at or before the clock's current value. The lower bound is exclusive, which matters at the boundary: an invitation created exactly `contact_window_days` ago has already left the window and no longer counts. Every recruitment counts regardless of how it ended — a withdrawn invitation, a screen-out and a no-show all consume contact budget for the full length of the window, because the person was contacted whether or not the session happened. Withdrawal therefore does not hand contact budget back.

From those two counters the platform derives a participant's contact posture. If `no_show_strikes` has reached **2**, the participant is `SUPPRESSED`. Otherwise, if `contacts_in_window` has reached the pool's `contact_cap_per_window`, they are in `COOLDOWN`. Otherwise they are `ELIGIBLE`. This is re-derived whenever a single-participant invitation or a batch invitation is created, whenever a no-show is recorded, and during a retention sweep. Crossing into `COOLDOWN` (`ELIGIBLE → COOLDOWN`) removes the participant from the batch selector's candidate set and causes a single-participant invitation naming them to be refused with `ERR_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`; that transition requires `no_show_strikes` still to be below 2, because two strikes take precedence and suppress instead. The reverse move `COOLDOWN → ELIGIBLE` happens only during a retention sweep, once the clock has advanced far enough that `contacts_in_window` has fallen back below `contact_cap_per_window` and `no_show_strikes` is still below 2, and it puts the participant back into the candidate set. `SUPPRESSED` is absorbing: whether the participant was `ELIGIBLE` or in `COOLDOWN` when their second strike landed, the transition into `SUPPRESSED` is one-way, it increments the pool's `suppressed_participant_count` and then re-runs the pool's posture derivation, and every future invitation naming them is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUPPRESSED`.

**A pool's own `status` is likewise derived and never set by a client.** Utilization is the pool's committed money — `incentive_reserved_cents` plus `incentive_paid_cents` — divided by `incentive_budget_cents`, compared as an exact ratio of integer cents with no intermediate rounding. The suppression ratio is `suppressed_participant_count` divided by `participant_count`, taken as 0.0 when the pool has no participants at all. The derivation then reads, in this order: at a utilization of **0.95 or more** the pool is `CLOSED`; otherwise, at a utilization of **0.80 or more**, *or* with a suppression ratio strictly **greater than 0.50**, it is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. The same pass recomputes `incentive_available_cents` as the budget minus reserved minus paid. Note the asymmetry between the two thresholds — utilization is compared inclusively at its band edges, whereas the suppression ratio must strictly exceed one half, so a pool with exactly half its participants suppressed and low utilization stays `OPEN`.

Because the derivation is a pure function of those fields, the pool's posture transitions are recomputes rather than commands, and they run after every mutation of `incentive_reserved_cents`, `incentive_paid_cents` or `suppressed_participant_count` — that is, after a single-participant invitation, after each row of a batch invitation, after a withdrawal, after a no-show, after a settlement and after a participant is suppressed. Rising utilization takes a pool `OPEN → RESTRICTED` when it lands in the band from 0.80 up to but not including 0.95, `RESTRICTED → CLOSED` when it reaches 0.95, and `OPEN → CLOSED` directly when a single recompute jumps it to 0.95 or beyond with no intervening recompute in the restricted band. Money coming back — a withdrawal, a no-show or a settlement release — moves the pool the other way: `CLOSED → RESTRICTED` once utilization is back inside the 0.80-to-0.95 band, and `RESTRICTED → OPEN` once utilization is below 0.80 **and** the suppression ratio is at most 0.50.

The posture is not cosmetic. While a pool is `RESTRICTED`, batch invitation is refused with `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED` but naming a single participant still works — the intent is to stop the platform spending the last fifth of a budget automatically while leaving a researcher free to place a specific, deliberate invitation. While a pool is `CLOSED`, **every** recruitment-creating operation against it is refused with `ERR_POOL_CLOSED`, single and batch alike. Recovery is automatic: as soon as released money drops utilization back into the restricted band, single invitations resume, and once it falls below 0.80 with the suppression ratio in check, batch invitation becomes available again.

Reads over this area are deliberately plain. A pool read returns the derived posture together with the full ledger. A pool's participants can be listed with optional `status` and `segment` filters, ordered by `created_at` ascending. A single participant read exposes `contacts_in_window`, `no_show_strikes`, `last_contacted_at` and their derived posture, which is how a researcher discovers why an invitation was refused.

### 3.3. Opening a Study, Pinning a Guide, and Getting into the Field

A study is the execution vehicle for an approved request, and it is opened by a `Researcher` or a `ResearchLead`. The payload names the `research_request_id` of the request being executed and the `participant_pool_id` of the single pool the study will recruit from; it declares the `method` to be used, one of `INTERVIEW`, `USABILITY_TEST`, `DIARY_STUDY` or `SURVEY`; it sets a `target_participant_count` of at least 1 as the completed-session goal; it carries the `screening_criteria` rubric; it fixes the base per-participant `incentive_amount_cents`, which must be zero or greater; and it declares the intended fielding window as `fielding_start` and `fielding_end`, where `fielding_end` must be strictly after `fielding_start`.

The `screening_criteria` object carries exactly four keys and no others: `required_segment`, a string; `required_locale`, a string; `target_answers`, an array of strings that earn credit; and `disqualifying_answers`, an array of strings that end the screen immediately. Any other shape — a missing key, a spare key — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

**The method is not the study author's free choice.** Creation is refused with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_APPROVED` unless the referenced request is in `status` `APPROVED` *and* the submitted `method` is exactly equal to that request's stored `approved_method`. This is the join that makes triage binding: a lead who overrode the recommendation at triage has, in effect, dictated the method the study must use. Nothing else about the two parents is consulted at creation time: the pool's derived posture is not looked at, so a study may be opened against a `RESTRICTED` or even a `CLOSED` pool because posture gates recruitment rather than planning, and the governing roadmap decision's lock state is not looked at either, because that guard belongs to fielding. On success the study is stamped with `lead_user_id` taken from the creating operator's `X-User-ID`, opens with `execution_status` `PLANNED` and `recruitment_status` `NOT_STARTED`, has `guide_status` `NONE` with `guide_version_label`, `guide_question_count` and `guide_version_pinned` all null, has `invited_count`, `screened_in_count` and `completed_count` at 0 and `fielded_at` and `fielding_closed_at` null — and it drags its request from `APPROVED` to `IN_STUDY`.

The **moderator guide** lives on the study as three fields rather than as a separate resource. Writing or rewriting it supplies a `guide_version_label`, a non-empty string of unbounded length, and a `guide_question_count`, which must be an integer but carries no lower bound of any kind — zero and even negative counts are accepted and stored exactly as supplied — and it is permitted only while `execution_status` is `PLANNED` or `GUIDE_APPROVED`; from `FIELDING` onward the write is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because sessions already in flight are pinned to a version that must not change underneath them. Every write sets `guide_status` to `DRAFT` and clears `guide_version_pinned` back to null — including a rewrite of a guide that had already been approved, which is exactly the point: a rewritten guide is an unapproved guide.

Approval is the `ResearchLead`'s exclusive act and it has two shapes with identical mechanics. From a study still in `PLANNED`, approval carries `execution_status` `PLANNED → GUIDE_APPROVED`, sets `guide_status` to `APPROVED`, and pins `guide_version_pinned` to the current `guide_version_label`. From a study already in `GUIDE_APPROVED` whose guide was rewritten since the last approval, approval is a self-loop `GUIDE_APPROVED → GUIDE_APPROVED`: it sets `guide_status` back to `APPROVED`, re-pins `guide_version_pinned` to the current `guide_version_label`, and deliberately leaves `execution_status` untouched — this self-loop is the only route back to a fieldable guide after a rewrite. In both shapes the precondition is the same: `guide_status` must be `DRAFT`. Approving a guide that is already `APPROVED`, or one that has never been written at all and still reads `NONE`, is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_APPROVED`. Both shapes also require `execution_status` to be `PLANNED` or `GUIDE_APPROVED`, and that check runs ahead of the guide's own: an approval attempted once the study has reached `FIELDING` or beyond is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` whatever `guide_status` happens to say.

Putting a study into the field carries `execution_status` `GUIDE_APPROVED → FIELDING` and is guarded by three conditions beyond the state itself. The guide must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_APPROVED`. The governing roadmap decision — reached from the study's `research_request_id` to that request's `roadmap_decision_id` — must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_DECISION_LOCKED`; a decision that has already been locked on existing evidence does not get more studies fielded against it. And the pool must have concurrent-fielding headroom: **at most two studies of a single pool may share any instant in the field**, so the request is refused with `ERR_POOL_FIELDING_CAPACITY` when two or more *other* studies of the same `participant_pool_id`, each with `execution_status` `FIELDING`, already have a fielding window that overlaps this study's window. Overlap here is closed-interval arithmetic on both windows: a single shared endpoint day already counts, so a study running to a `fielding_end` of the 10th overlaps a study whose `fielding_start` is the 10th. On success `fielded_at` is set to the current logical clock instant and the study's recruitment operations open up. Fielding does **not** touch `recruitment_status`, which stays at `NOT_STARTED` until the study's first invitation is actually created.

Closing fielding carries `execution_status` `FIELDING → ANALYSIS`, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` while any recruitment of the study is still sitting in `SCHEDULED` — every booked session must be resolved as completed, a no-show or a withdrawal before analysis begins. On success `fielding_closed_at` is set to the clock instant and two doors open: findings of this study may now be locked, and the binding form of incentive settlement becomes permitted. Closing fielding is also the moment recruitment failure is recognised. A study that reaches this point from `RECRUITING` with a `completed_count` of 0 moves `RECRUITING → RECRUITMENT_FAILED`; a study that never left `NOT_STARTED` because its `invited_count` is 0 moves `NOT_STARTED → RECRUITMENT_FAILED`; in either case the study's impact score permanently loses its recruitment-efficiency term, because that term is computed from a completed-to-invited ratio that is zero. Any other study leaves `recruitment_status` exactly where the recruitment dimension had already put it — `RECRUITING` or `QUOTA_MET`. `RECRUITMENT_FAILED` is terminal on that dimension.

The last two steps of the execution dimension are driven from the readout workflow rather than from the study itself: delivering a readout of the study carries it `ANALYSIS → REPORTED` and increments the study's `version` by 1, and a stakeholder's acceptance carries it `REPORTED → CLOSED`, again incrementing `version` by 1 and closing the study's research request in the same breath. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Studies can be read individually — the read exposes both state dimensions and all three recruitment counters — or listed with optional `research_request_id`, `participant_pool_id`, `execution_status` and `recruitment_status` filters in `created_at` ascending order.

### 3.4. Invitations, the Batch Selector, and What an Invitation Costs

There are two ways a participant enters a study, and they differ in who decides. A `Researcher`, `ResearchLead` or `ResearchOpsAdmin` may invite **one named participant** by supplying that person's `participant_id` together with their `screening_answers`. Alternatively — and only a `ResearchOpsAdmin` may do this — the platform can be asked to pick, by supplying a `count` of at least 1 and letting the selector choose. Either way, invitation is gated first on the study and then on the pool: the study's `execution_status` must be `FIELDING` and its `recruitment_status` must not already be `QUOTA_MET`, else `ERR_RECRUITMENT_CLOSED`; and the pool's derived posture must not be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_POOL_CLOSED`. Batch invitation adds one further posture condition — the pool must be `OPEN`, so a `RESTRICTED` pool rejects the batch with `ERR_POOL_RESTRICTED` while still accepting named invitations.

For a named invitation three participant-level rules follow, in this order. A recruitment must not already exist for this study-and-participant pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECRUITMENT`. The participant's derived posture must not be `SUPPRESSED`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUPPRESSED`. And their `contacts_in_window` must still be strictly below a `contact_cap_per_window` — specifically the cap carried by the **participant's own pool**, with the study's pool used only as a fallback should that participant's pool row not be found — else `ERR_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. This gate reads `contacts_in_window` exactly as it stands stored on the row; the counter is not recounted against the current clock before the comparison, so a value that has gone stale because the clock moved on since the last recompute is what the gate sees. Note too that a named invitation never requires the participant to be enrolled in the study's own pool: the reservation is always applied to the pool named by the study's `participant_pool_id`, while the contact cap is read from wherever the participant actually sits.

**What an invitation costs is not simply the study's base amount.** Each reservation is computed as follows, and the order is pinned because the answer depends on state this very reservation is about to change. First, read the pool's utilization **from the state before this reservation is applied**: committed money, meaning `incentive_reserved_cents` plus `incentive_paid_cents`, divided by `incentive_budget_cents`. From that pre-call utilization read a scarcity surcharge percentage: below 0.50 the surcharge is zero; from 0.50 up to but not including 0.80 it is five percent; at 0.80 or above it is ten percent. Then take the study's `incentive_amount_cents`, multiply it by the surcharge percentage, round that surcharge term **up** to the next whole cent, and add it to the base — the sum is the recruitment's `reserved_cents`. A study offering 1,000 cents therefore reserves 1,000 cents in an unstressed pool, 1,050 cents once the pool crosses the halfway mark, and 1,100 cents from 0.80 utilization upward; a base of 333 cents at the five-percent tier reserves 333 plus 17, because 16.65 cents rounds up to a whole 17.

That amount is then checked against the same pre-call state: if `reserved_cents` exceeds the pool's `incentive_available_cents`, the invitation is refused with `ERR_INCENTIVE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` and nothing is created. Otherwise the pool's `incentive_reserved_cents` rises by exactly `reserved_cents` and `incentive_available_cents` is re-derived as budget minus reserved minus paid, keeping the ledger partition of §2.10 intact.

A successful invitation writes a recruitment in `status` `INVITED` with `incentive_state` `RESERVED` and its `reserved_cents` stored, sets `screen_score`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `moderator_user_id`, `actual_duration_min` and `closure_reason` to null with `reschedule_count` at 0 and `paid_cents` at 0, and copies the study's `guide_version_pinned` onto the recruitment's own `guide_version_pinned` so the session is forever tied to the guide version that was in force. The `study_id` comes from the path. In the same request the pool's posture is re-derived, the participant's `last_contacted_at` is set to the current logical clock instant, their `contacts_in_window` is recounted and their contact posture re-derived, and the study's `invited_count` is recomputed as the number of recruitment rows belonging to that study in any status whatsoever, withdrawn ones included. When the study had no recruitments at all before this call, this is also the moment `recruitment_status` moves `NOT_STARTED → RECRUITING`.

**The batch selector is fully deterministic**, and its candidate set is explicit. A participant is a candidate when their `pool_id` equals the study's `participant_pool_id`, their derived posture is `ELIGIBLE`, their `segment` is exactly equal to the study's `required_segment` from the screening rubric, their `no_show_strikes` is below 2, their `contacts_in_window` is below the pool's `contact_cap_per_window`, and no recruitment already exists for this study naming them. Candidates are ranked by `contacts_in_window` ascending, then by `no_show_strikes` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and finally — because the logical clock can stamp several enrollments with the same instant — by server insertion order, which makes the ranking total with no randomness anywhere. If the candidate set is empty the whole call fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` (HTTP 422).

The selector then walks the ranked list from the top, creating one recruitment at a time with exactly the side effects of a named invitation and applying each reservation before computing the next — so the second candidate's surcharge tier reflects the money the first candidate just reserved, and a batch can straddle a surcharge boundary mid-walk. The walk stops when `count` rows have been created, when the candidate list is exhausted, or when the next candidate's reservation would exceed the pool's remaining `incentive_available_cents`. **A short batch is a success**: fewer rows than `count` are returned with a 201, and the rows already created stay committed. Only a batch that created *nothing* fails — with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` when there were no candidates to begin with, or with `ERR_INCENTIVE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` when the very first candidate was already unaffordable. Batch invitation carries no screening payload; the `count` value is its entire body, and every row the selector creates therefore stores `screening_answers` as null.

### 3.5. Screening, Booking a Session, and How Sessions End

Screening turns an invitation into an admitted or rejected candidate. It takes no body of its own — the answers were captured at invitation time as `screening_answers`, an object carrying exactly three keys and no others: a `segment` string, a `locale` string, and an `answers` array of strings. Screening is available to a `Researcher`, `ResearchLead` or `ResearchOpsAdmin` while the recruitment is `INVITED` and the study's `execution_status` is `FIELDING`; either precondition failing is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The **screen score** is computed only from the recruitment's own `screening_answers` and the study's `screening_criteria`. The `Participant` row's own `segment` and `locale` values are deliberately *not* consulted here — the screener answers are what the person said this time, and they are what is scored. Evaluation begins with a hard gate that short-circuits everything else: if any element of the submitted `answers` array appears in the rubric's `disqualifying_answers`, the score is exactly 0.00 and no further term is computed. Otherwise three terms are added. A submitted `segment` exactly equal to the rubric's `required_segment` earns 40.0 points, and nothing otherwise. A submitted `locale` exactly equal to `required_locale` earns 20.0 points, and nothing otherwise. Each element of the submitted `answers` array that appears in the rubric's `target_answers` counts as one hit; multiply the number of hits by 15.0 and hold the result at a ceiling of 40.0 points — so one hit earns 15.0, two earn 30.0, and three or more all earn the capped 40.0. The three terms are summed and rounded half-up to two decimal places, giving `screen_score`. A recruitment the batch selector created carries no `screening_answers` at all, and such a row is scored as though every answer were absent: no disqualifying answer can match, no segment or locale point is earned and no target hit is counted, so the score is exactly 0.00.

The threshold for admission is **60.00**. A score of at least 60.00 carries the recruitment `INVITED → SCREENED_IN`; anything below — including the 0.00 produced by the disqualifying-answer gate — carries it `INVITED → SCREENED_OUT`, which is terminal. Consider the arithmetic that makes the cap bite: a candidate with the wrong segment but the right locale and four target hits scores 0.0 plus 20.0 plus a capped 40.0, exactly 60.00, and is admitted; the same candidate with only one target hit scores 35.00 and is not. Either way `screen_score` is stored, and admission recomputes the study's `screened_in_count`, which counts every recruitment of the study whose `status` is `SCREENED_IN`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`. On **both** outcomes the money is left alone: `incentive_state` stays `RESERVED` and `reserved_cents` is untouched, because a screened-out candidate still gave up their time and keeps their reservation until settlement converts it into the screen-out stipend of 200 cents.

Booking a session carries a screened-in candidate `SCREENED_IN → SCHEDULED`. A `Researcher` or `ResearchLead` supplies `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end` — which must be strictly after the start — and a `moderator_user_id`, a required non-empty string of unbounded length recording who will moderate, which is stored verbatim and never checked against any registry of users. Three business conditions apply, in this order. First, consent must already be on file: a `ConsentRecord` in `status` `GRANTED` must exist for this recruitment's participant and this recruitment's study, else the booking is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. Second, the session must sit inside the study's fielding window, treated as running from `fielding_start` inclusive up to one day past `fielding_end` **inclusive** — so a session may start on the final fielding day and run into the small hours, and an end landing exactly twenty-four hours after `fielding_end` is still accepted while anything strictly beyond that grace boundary is not. The comparison is one-sided on each edge: only `scheduled_start` is tested against the lower bound and only `scheduled_end` against the upper, so nothing may begin before `fielding_start` and nothing may end past the grace boundary, but the pair is not otherwise required to fall within the declared days. A violation is `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_FIELDING`. Third, the participant must be free: no *other* recruitment of the same participant that is currently `SCHEDULED` may occupy an interval intersecting the requested window widened by **thirty minutes on each side**, evaluated as a half-open interval from thirty minutes before `scheduled_start` up to thirty minutes after `scheduled_end`; a clash is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SESSION_CONFLICT`. Two sessions exactly thirty minutes apart therefore do not clash, whereas twenty-nine minutes apart do. On success the two instants and the `moderator_user_id` are stored, the recruitment keeps the `guide_version_pinned` it received at invitation, and the study's `recruitment_status` is recomputed — which may take it `RECRUITING → QUOTA_MET`.

Rescheduling is the same act performed on a recruitment that is already `SCHEDULED`, a `SCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` self-loop. It is permitted at most **twice** per recruitment: with `reschedule_count` already at 2 the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The consent, window and participant-overlap checks are all re-run identically, with one adjustment — this recruitment's own current interval is excluded from the conflict candidate set, so a session is never treated as conflicting with itself. `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end` and `moderator_user_id` are overwritten, `reschedule_count` is incremented by 1, and `guide_version_pinned` is deliberately left at its original value: a rescheduled session keeps the guide version that was pinned when it was first booked.

Sessions end in one of three ways, all of them requiring the study still to be `FIELDING` (except withdrawal, which has no such requirement).

**Completion** carries `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and records `actual_duration_min`, the duration in minutes the moderator reports, which must be an integer but carries no lower bound — zero and negative durations are accepted and stored exactly as supplied. The study's `completed_count` is recomputed as the number of its recruitments standing in `COMPLETED`, and its `recruitment_status` re-derived: once the number of the study's recruitments sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` reaches `target_participant_count`, the study moves `RECRUITING → QUOTA_MET` and further invitations are refused with `ERR_RECRUITMENT_CLOSED`. Completion moves no money at all: the recruitment keeps `incentive_state` `RESERVED` and its stored `reserved_cents` until a binding settlement turns the reservation into payment. It changes what the operations-metrics read of §3.9 will compute, but no metric is ever stored on the study — those figures are derived at read time. The participant's `contacts_in_window` is not affected either — completing a session neither consumes nor returns contact budget.

**A no-show** carries `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` and stores the supplied `reason` — a required string that must be non-empty and has no maximum length — as `closure_reason`. It is the trigger for a three-hop chain. Hop one: the recruitment's `incentive_state` moves `RESERVED → FORFEITED` with `paid_cents` left at 0, and the participant's `no_show_strikes` is incremented by 1. Hop two: the forfeiture removes exactly `reserved_cents` from the pool's `incentive_reserved_cents` — the row's own `reserved_cents` is left standing as the record of what was forfeited — which re-derives the pool's posture and may take it `CLOSED → RESTRICTED` or `RESTRICTED → OPEN`. Hop three: the incremented `no_show_strikes` re-derives the participant's contact posture, and at two strikes moves them into `SUPPRESSED`, which increments the pool's `suppressed_participant_count` and re-runs the pool's posture derivation a second time. Finally the study's `recruitment_status` is recomputed, which may take it `QUOTA_MET → RECRUITING` because the seat just fell through.

**A withdrawal** is available from `INVITED`, from `SCREENED_IN` and from `SCHEDULED` — three separate transitions into the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state, all available to a `Researcher`, `ResearchLead` or `ResearchOpsAdmin`, and all behaving identically: the supplied `reason` — likewise a required, non-empty string of unbounded length — is stored as `closure_reason`, and the recruitment's `incentive_state` moves `RESERVED → RELEASED` with `paid_cents` left at 0, returning **exactly** the recruitment's stored `reserved_cents` — never a recomputed figure — out of the pool's `incentive_reserved_cents` and therefore back into `incentive_available_cents`. The recruitment's own `reserved_cents` stays on the row as the record of what was released. The pool's posture is re-derived in every case. Withdrawing from `SCREENED_IN` or from `SCHEDULED` additionally re-derives the study's `recruitment_status`, and from `SCHEDULED` that can take the study `QUOTA_MET → RECRUITING`. What withdrawal does *not* do is refund contact budget: the invitation still counts toward `contacts_in_window` for the remainder of the pool's window.

Recruitments can be read individually — the read exposes both the workflow `status` and the `incentive_state` side by side — or listed in `created_at` ascending order with optional `study_id`, `participant_id`, `status` and `incentive_state` filters.

### 3.6. Consent, Retention, and the Five-Hop Evidence-Withdrawal Cascade

Nothing may be recorded from a session that consent does not cover, and nothing may be kept longer than consent allows. A `Researcher`, `ResearchLead` or `PrivacyOfficer` records consent for exactly one participant and one study by supplying the `participant_id`, the `study_id`, a `scopes` array and a `retention_days` figure of at least 1. `scopes` must be a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `RECORDING`, `TRANSCRIPT` and `PII_QUOTE`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The record opens in `status` `GRANTED` with `granted_at` set to the current logical clock instant, `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` null, and a computed `retention_until`.

**The retention horizon is the tighter of what was asked for and what the scopes permit.** Each scope carries its own maximum: a `RECORDING` may be kept at most 180 days, a `TRANSCRIPT` at most 365 days, and a `PII_QUOTE` at most 90 days. Take the smallest maximum across every scope the record grants, take the smaller of that figure and the requested `retention_days`, and add that many whole days to `granted_at` to obtain `retention_until`, preserving the millisecond component of the instant exactly. A consent record granting `TRANSCRIPT` alone with a requested 300 days therefore expires 300 days after grant; the same request granting `TRANSCRIPT` and `PII_QUOTE` together expires after 90, because the tightest scope governs the whole record. A request for 30 days is honoured as 30 regardless of scope.

Artifacts are registered against a session by a `Researcher` or `ResearchLead`, who supplies an `artifact_type` of `RECORDING`, `TRANSCRIPT` or `PII_NOTE` and a `contains_pii` boolean stating whether the body holds directly identifying data; the `recruitment_id` comes from the path. Each artifact type demands a matching consent scope — a `RECORDING` needs the `RECORDING` scope, a `TRANSCRIPT` needs the `TRANSCRIPT` scope, and a `PII_NOTE` needs the `PII_QUOTE` scope. The platform looks for a `GRANTED` consent record whose `participant_id` and `study_id` match those of the recruitment and whose `scopes` include the required scope; when several records qualify the **earliest-created** one governs, taking `created_at` ascending with server insertion order breaking ties, and no other qualifying record is consulted; if there is none, registration is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_MISSING`. When one is found, the artifact inherits that record's `retention_until` verbatim and opens in `lifecycle_state` `RETAINED` with `purged_at` and `purge_reason` null.

Consent can end in two ways, and both run the same cascade. A `PrivacyOfficer` — and only a `PrivacyOfficer` — may revoke a `GRANTED` record with a supplied `reason` — a required, non-empty string of unbounded length — carrying it `GRANTED → REVOKED`, setting `revoked_at` to the clock instant and storing the `reason` as `revocation_reason`. Alternatively the retention sweep carries a `GRANTED` record whose `retention_until` is at or before the current clock instant to `GRANTED → EXPIRED`; on that path `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` both stay null. `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal.

**The cascade has five hops and every one of them is mandatory.** It runs to completion inside the request that triggered it — there is no deferred processing.

1. **Artifacts purge.** Every artifact whose `recruitment_id` resolves to a recruitment sharing the consent record's `participant_id` and `study_id`, whose `artifact_type` maps to a scope the consent record granted, and whose `lifecycle_state` is still `RETAINED`, moves `RETAINED → PURGED`. Its `purged_at` is set to the clock instant and its `purge_reason` is set to `CONSENT_REVOKED` on the revocation path or `RETENTION_EXPIRED` on the expiry path. Artifacts of a type the record did not cover are untouched, as are artifacts of other participants or other studies.
2. **Evidence links go inadmissible.** For each artifact that just became `PURGED`, every evidence link citing it whose `is_admissible` is still true is flipped to false with `inadmissible_reason` set to `ARTIFACT_PURGED`. The link row is not deleted — it remains readable as a record of evidence that was once cited. From this point on, a fresh evidence link citing that artifact is refused with `ERR_ARTIFACT_PURGED`.
3. **Findings re-score and may retract.** Every finding that just lost an admissible link has its `admissible_link_count`, `distinct_participant_count` and `evidence_strength` recomputed under **its own current variant** — a draft finding is re-scored provisionally, a locked, published or retracted one under the corroborated variant. A finding whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose recomputed `evidence_strength` has fallen **below 0.45**, *or* whose recomputed `distinct_participant_count` has fallen **below 3**, moves `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED` with `retraction_reason` set to `EVIDENCE_WITHDRAWN`. Either condition alone is sufficient. `RETRACTED` is terminal. Hops 4 and 5 are gated on this hop having actually moved something: when every finding touched here comes out of the recompute carrying exactly the `status` and the `evidence_strength` it went in with, the cascade stops at hop 3.
4. **Themes re-weigh.** Every theme referenced by a finding whose `status` *or* `evidence_strength` actually changed in hop 3 — not merely those that retracted, so a `PUBLISHED` finding that lost strength while staying above the bar, and a `DRAFT` finding that merely re-scored, both trigger this hop — is recomputed — its own weight first, then the rolled-up weight, then the same rollup applied up the `parent_theme_id` chain one level at a time to the root — which may move a theme `ESTABLISHED → EMERGING`.
5. **Decisions may unlock.** Every roadmap decision reachable through an `ACCEPTED` readout of a study that owns one of the findings hop 3 actually changed — again by `status` or by `evidence_strength`, not only by retraction — has its `evidence_confidence` recomputed. A decision whose `status` is `LOCKED` and whose recomputed confidence has fallen **below 0.60** moves `LOCKED → OPEN`, keeps the recomputed value in `evidence_confidence`, and has `locked_at` cleared back to null.

The retention sweep is the platform's only clock-driven actor, and it applies exactly what is due at the clock's present value. It works in five passes in a fixed order, each pass walking its rows in `created_at` ascending order with insertion order breaking ties. It expires consent records whose `retention_until` has been reached, running each record's own cascade to completion before it moves to the next record; then purges artifacts in their own right when the artifact's `retention_until` has been reached, collecting the whole due set first and cascading from it once; then marks a follow-up `OPEN → OVERDUE` when its `due_date` is strictly earlier than the clock instant; then re-derives participant contact posture so that a `COOLDOWN` participant whose window has rolled past returns to `ELIGIBLE`; and finally re-derives every pool's `participant_count`, `suppressed_participant_count`, `incentive_available_cents` and posture, which is why the participant pass necessarily runs ahead of the pool pass. It answers with six counters describing what it did, each counting **distinct rows** so that a row two cascades in one sweep both touch is counted exactly once: `expired_consent_count`, `purged_artifact_count`, `inadmissible_link_count`, `retracted_finding_count`, `overdue_follow_up_count` and `unlocked_decision_count`. Both a `ResearchOpsAdmin` and a `PrivacyOfficer` may run it.

Because this whole area holds participant-level personal data, reads over it are narrower than elsewhere. Consent records may be read individually or listed with optional `participant_id`, `study_id` and `status` filters; a recruitment's artifacts may be listed with optional `artifact_type` and `lifecycle_state` filters; an artifact may be read on its own, exposing its retention horizon and lifecycle state; and the evidence links citing one artifact may be listed with an optional `is_admissible` filter. A `Stakeholder` is excluded from every one of those five reads, and a `PrivacyOfficer` is excluded from the study metrics read — the segregation runs in both directions.

### 3.7. The Insight Repository: Themes, Findings and the Evidence Behind Them

Themes are the repository's filing system and they form a tree. A `Researcher` or `ResearchLead` creates one with a `label` that must be non-empty and unique across every theme in the repository — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LABEL` — and optionally a `parent_theme_id` naming the theme it nests under — a field treated as absent whenever it does not arrive as a non-empty string, so `null`, `""` and a value of the wrong JSON type all yield a root theme rather than an error. A theme with no parent is a root and its `depth` is 1; a nested theme's `depth` is its parent's `depth` plus 1. **The tree is capped at three levels**: nesting that would give the new theme a `depth` above 3 is refused with `ERR_THEME_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, so a theme may be created under a root or under a second-level theme but never under a third-level one. A new theme opens `EMERGING` with `own_evidence_weight` and `evidence_weight` both 0.00 and `reuse_count` at 0.

Findings are the claims the repository actually holds. Creation names the `study_id` that produced the finding and the `theme_id` it rolls up into, and supplies a `statement` — the one-sentence claim, non-empty and at most 500 characters — plus a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`. The finding opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `strength_variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `evidence_strength` 0.00, `admissible_link_count` and `distinct_participant_count` at 0, `retraction_reason` and `published_at` null, and `authored_by_user_id` set to the creating operator's `X-User-ID`. Creating a finding does **not** touch the theme's `reuse_count`; that counter tracks something else entirely, as described below.

Evidence links are what connect a claim to the sessions that support it. Creation names the `artifact_id` being cited, an `excerpt_ref` — a non-empty stable pointer into the artifact, a timecode or a paragraph anchor — and a `weight_class` describing the epistemic quality of the excerpt: `DIRECT_QUOTE`, `OBSERVED_BEHAVIOR` or `SELF_REPORT`. The `finding_id` comes from the path. Three conditions guard the write, and the §2.6 precedence fixes which of them speaks first: the finding's own state is tested ahead of the artifact's lifecycle, which is tested ahead of the study match, so citing a purged artifact on a `PUBLISHED` finding answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_ARTIFACT_PURGED`. The cited artifact's `lifecycle_state` must be `RETAINED`, else `ERR_ARTIFACT_PURGED`. The artifact must belong to the same study as the finding — resolved from the artifact's `recruitment_id` to that recruitment's `study_id`, compared against the finding's `study_id` — else `ERR_EVIDENCE_STUDY_MISMATCH`, which is how the platform stops evidence being borrowed across studies. And the finding must not be `PUBLISHED` or `RETRACTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The new link opens with `is_admissible` true and `inadmissible_reason` null.

Adding a link to a finding that was `EVIDENCE_LOCKED` deliberately **unlocks it**: the finding moves `EVIDENCE_LOCKED → DRAFT` and its `strength_variant` reverts to `PROVISIONAL` before the strength is recomputed. New evidence invalidates a lock; there is no way to bolt an extra excerpt onto a locked finding and keep the lock. Removing a link is permitted only while the finding is `DRAFT` — on an `EVIDENCE_LOCKED`, `PUBLISHED` or `RETRACTED` finding it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and it reverses the link's effect exactly, recomputing `admissible_link_count`, `distinct_participant_count` and `evidence_strength` back to precisely the values they held before that link was added. Removal answers with 204 and no body.

**Evidence strength has two variants that return different numbers from the same links.** Both begin identically. Every evidence link of the finding whose `is_admissible` is true gets a `contribution`: multiply the weight of its `weight_class` — 1.00 for `DIRECT_QUOTE`, 0.85 for `OBSERVED_BEHAVIOR`, 0.50 for `SELF_REPORT` — by the multiplier of the study's `method` — 1.20 for `USABILITY_TEST`, 1.10 for `DIARY_STUDY`, 1.00 for `INTERVIEW`, 0.70 for `SURVEY` — and round the product half-up to two decimal places. **The rounding order here is pinned and it matters**: each link's `contribution` is rounded to two decimals *first*, and the raw total is the sum of those already-rounded terms. Summing the unrounded products and rounding at the end is explicitly not the rule. Links whose artifact has been purged are excluded from the total, and so are the participants reachable only through them. Alongside the total, `admissible_link_count` is the number of admissible links, and `distinct_participant_count` is the number of distinct participants reached by walking each admissible link to its artifact, the artifact to its recruitment, and the recruitment to its participant.

While the finding is `DRAFT` the **provisional** variant applies: divide the raw total by 4.00, hold the quotient at a ceiling of 1.00, and round half-up to two decimals. This is the working estimate a researcher watches while curating evidence, and it is generous by design — a thin single-participant body of evidence still scores something. Once the finding is `EVIDENCE_LOCKED`, `PUBLISHED` or `RETRACTED` the **corroborated** variant applies, and it is computed in three steps: divide the raw total by the tighter 3.00, hold it at a ceiling of 1.00 and round to **four** decimal places to obtain a density; divide `distinct_participant_count` by 5, hold that at a ceiling of 1.00 and round half-up to two decimals to obtain a corroboration factor; then multiply density by corroboration factor and round half-up to two decimals. Breadth of participation therefore matters in the corroborated variant and not at all in the provisional one, and a body of evidence spanning five or more distinct participants earns the full factor of 1.00. The `strength_variant` field records which variant produced the stored value.

Locking a finding is how a `Researcher` or `ResearchLead` declares its evidence set final, carrying it `DRAFT → EVIDENCE_LOCKED`. Four conditions apply. The finding must be `DRAFT`. Its study must have reached analysis — `execution_status` in `ANALYSIS`, `REPORTED` or `CLOSED` — else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because evidence sets are not finalised while sessions are still running. It must carry at least one admissible link, else `ERR_NO_ADMISSIBLE_EVIDENCE`. And its `distinct_participant_count` must be **at least 3**, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CORROBORATION` — three separate people, not three excerpts from one. On success `strength_variant` is set to `CORROBORATED` and `evidence_strength` is recomputed under that variant, which typically moves the number: the same links that scored provisionally against a divisor of 4.00 now score against 3.00 and are then scaled by participant breadth. Locking does **not** re-run theme weights, because only published findings carry weight.

Publication is the `ResearchLead`'s or `ResearchOpsAdmin`'s act and it carries the finding `EVIDENCE_LOCKED → PUBLISHED`. The publisher's `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `authored_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and since a `ResearchLead` is entitled to author findings, this is a live constraint rather than a formality. The finding's `evidence_strength` must be **at least 0.45**, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_TOO_WEAK`. On success `published_at` is set to the current logical clock instant and the theme weights are recomputed for the finding's theme and every ancestor of it.

**Theme weight is a bottom-up rollup.** A theme's `own_evidence_weight` is the sum, over every `PUBLISHED` finding whose `theme_id` is that theme, of the finding's `evidence_strength` multiplied by its severity multiplier — 1.50 for `HIGH`, 1.00 for `MEDIUM`, 0.60 for `LOW` — with each per-finding term rounded half-up to two decimals before it enters the sum. Findings in `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_LOCKED` or `RETRACTED` contribute exactly 0.00. The theme's `evidence_weight` is then its own weight plus the `evidence_weight` of every theme whose `parent_theme_id` names it, the total again rounded half-up to two decimals. Evaluation order is pinned: children are evaluated before parents in a post-order walk of the tree, and then the affected theme's ancestors are refreshed from that theme upward to the root one level at a time. A theme whose `evidence_weight` is **at least 1.20** is `ESTABLISHED`; below that it is `EMERGING`, and the two transitions between those postures are pure consequences of the rollup, never a command. Separately, a theme's `reuse_count` is the number of `ACCEPTED` readouts whose `cited_finding_ids` include at least one finding belonging to that theme *or to any of its descendants* — this is the repository's reuse signal, and it is why drafting a finding leaves the counter alone.

Reads over the repository are the reuse mechanism. The theme index lists themes ordered by `evidence_weight` descending then `created_at` ascending, with optional `parent_theme_id`, `status` and `label_contains` filters, the last matching themes whose `label` contains the supplied substring. A theme read returns its rolled-up weight and reuse count. Findings can be read individually or listed with optional `study_id`, `theme_id` and `status` filters, ordered by `evidence_strength` descending then `created_at` ascending. A finding's own evidence links can be listed with an optional `is_admissible` filter in `created_at` ascending order.

### 3.8. Readouts, Decision Confidence, and the Follow-Ups That Trail Them

A readout is the study's answer to the decision that prompted it. A `Researcher` or `ResearchLead` drafts one against a study by naming the `roadmap_decision_id` the readout feeds, a `recommendation` narrative that must be non-empty and at most 1,000 characters, a machine-readable `recommended_action` of `PROCEED`, `PIVOT` or `HOLD`, a `cited_finding_ids` array that must be non-empty and free of duplicates, and the `expected_study_version` required by §2.9 — which must be present and must be an integer, but is bounded neither above nor below, so a zero or negative figure is well-formed and simply fails the equality check with `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION`. The `study_id` comes from the path. Every id in `cited_finding_ids` must resolve to a finding — an id that resolves to nothing is `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND` — and every one of those findings must belong to the same study as the readout, else `ERR_CITATION_STUDY_MISMATCH`.

A successful draft opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `delivered_at`, `responded_at`, `response_note`, `decision_confidence_before` and `decision_confidence_after` all null, and it increments the target study's `version` by 1. It also supersedes its predecessors: every other readout of the same study that is still sitting in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — a superseded readout can never be delivered. This is precisely why the write is protected by a stale-version check rather than by a state precondition: superseding is legitimate, so the only thing that can distinguish a deliberate revision from a writer working off an outdated picture of the study is the `version` equality.

Delivery carries the readout `DRAFT → DELIVERED` and is available to a `Researcher` or `ResearchLead`. The study must be in `ANALYSIS`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. And the citation set must still hold up: `cited_finding_ids` must be non-empty and **every** cited finding must be in `status` `PUBLISHED` at the moment of delivery, else `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_FINDINGS` — a finding that was published when the draft was written and has since been retracted by a privacy cascade will block delivery. On success `delivered_at` is set to the current logical clock instant, the study moves `ANALYSIS → REPORTED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the study's operations metrics are refreshed.

Responding is the `Stakeholder`'s exclusive act, and only the operator whose `X-User-ID` equals the `requester_user_id` of the research request behind the readout's study may perform it — anyone else gets `ERR_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`. The `outcome` is either `ACCEPTED` or `DEFERRED`, and an optional `note` is stored as `response_note`. Both outcomes require the readout to be `DELIVERED` and set `responded_at` to the clock instant, and both are terminal. There the symmetry ends.

**Acceptance** additionally requires the decision named by `roadmap_decision_id` to be `OPEN`, else `ERR_DECISION_LOCKED`. On the `DELIVERED → ACCEPTED` transition the platform captures the decision's current `evidence_confidence` into the readout's `decision_confidence_before`, recomputes that decision's `evidence_confidence` from scratch, and stores the recomputed figure into the readout's `decision_confidence_after` as well as onto the decision itself. It then increments `reuse_count` by exactly one for every theme referenced by a cited finding and for each of that theme's ancestors up to the root — one increment per theme per accepted readout, however many of the cited findings point at that same theme or at sibling themes sharing an ancestor with it. Finally it closes the chain of work: the study moves `REPORTED → CLOSED` and the study's research request moves `IN_STUDY → CLOSED`.

**Deferral** requires only that the readout be `DELIVERED` and that the responder own it. On the `DELIVERED → DEFERRED` transition `responded_at` and `response_note` are stored and nothing else moves: the decision's `evidence_confidence` is **not** recomputed, because only accepted readouts contribute confidence; `decision_confidence_before` and `decision_confidence_after` both stay null; theme `reuse_count` values are untouched; and the study's `execution_status` stays at `REPORTED`, which is deliberate — it leaves room for a fresh draft readout to be written against the same study and delivered again.

**Decision confidence is an aggregation over accepted readouts only.** Readouts in `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED`, `DELIVERED` or `DEFERRED` contribute nothing at all. Walk the decision's `ACCEPTED` readouts in `created_at` ascending order and give each one a contribution. Collect the findings of that readout's study whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`; if there are none, the readout contributes exactly 0.00. Otherwise take the mean of those findings' `evidence_strength` values — their sum divided by their count — rounded half-up to **four** decimal places, then multiply that mean by the method multiplier of the readout's study (1.20 for `USABILITY_TEST`, 1.10 for `DIARY_STUDY`, 1.00 for `INTERVIEW`, 0.70 for `SURVEY`) and round half-up to two decimals; that is the readout's contribution. Sum the contributions across all accepted readouts, divide by a divisor of 1.00 — the sum is used as-is — hold the result at a ceiling of 1.00, and round half-up to two decimals to obtain the decision's `evidence_confidence`. Two accepted readouts each contributing 0.35 therefore give a confidence of 0.70, which clears the 0.60 lock threshold, while one alone at 0.35 does not.

Follow-ups are the accountability trail. A `Stakeholder`, `ResearchLead` or `ResearchOpsAdmin` opens one against a readout — the natural moment is once the readout has been accepted — by naming the `owner_user_id` accountable for the action, which must be non-empty, a `description` of the action item that must be non-empty and at most 500 characters, and a `due_date`. The `readout_id` comes from the path. No state precondition guards the creation, though: an item may be opened against a readout in any `status` — `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` included — and the readout itself is not mutated by it. It opens in `status` `OPEN` with `completed_at` null. A retention sweep marks an item `OPEN → OVERDUE` once its `due_date` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock instant; the item stays fully actionable, only the label changes. Completion is the owner's act and the owner's alone: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `owner_user_id`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`. Completion works identically from `OPEN → DONE` and from `OVERDUE → DONE`, setting `completed_at` to the clock instant. `DONE` is terminal, and once *every* follow-up of *every* readout of a study is `DONE`, the study's impact score gains its follow-up term.

Readouts can be read individually — the read shows the citations alongside the before-and-after confidence figures — or listed with optional `study_id`, `roadmap_decision_id` and `status` filters in `created_at` ascending order. A readout's follow-ups are listed with an optional `status` filter, ordered by `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

### 3.9. Settling the Money, and Measuring the Program

Settlement is the `ResearchOpsAdmin`'s exclusive act and it always names one study against one pool: a `study_id` identifying whose recruitments are being settled, a `mode`, and the `expected_pool_version` required by §2.9 — which must be present and must be an integer, but is bounded neither above nor below, so a zero or negative figure is a perfectly well-formed value that simply fails the equality check with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION`. The two modes look at the same pool and the same recruitments and deliberately return different numbers.

**`FORWARD_ESTIMATE` is advisory and moves no money.** It may be requested at any time, including while the study is still `FIELDING`, and from any `execution_status` whatsoever; it may be run repeatedly. It includes every recruitment whose participant belongs to this pool, whose `study_id` equals the supplied one, whose `incentive_state` is `RESERVED`, and whose `status` is `INVITED`, `SCREENED_IN`, `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. The estimate is the plain sum of those rows' `reserved_cents` — the assumption being that every open recruitment completes at its full reserved amount. Screened-out rows are ignored entirely by this mode. The figure is written to the pool's `last_estimate_cents` and **no recruitment row is mutated**.

**`CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL` is binding and permanently moves cents from reserved to paid.** It is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_PERMITTED` unless the named study's `execution_status` is `ANALYSIS`, `REPORTED` or `CLOSED`, and refused with `ERR_ALREADY_SETTLED` when that `study_id` already appears in the pool's `settled_study_ids` — a study's incentives are settled bindingly once and only once. Its candidate set is wider than the estimate's: every recruitment whose participant belongs to this pool, whose `study_id` matches, and whose `incentive_state` is `RESERVED`, **regardless of `status`**. Screened-out rows therefore enter the binding run even though they never entered the estimate, which is exactly why the two modes disagree.

The run walks those rows ordered by `created_at` ascending, breaking ties on the participant's `external_ref` ascending and then on server insertion order, and processes each one fully before moving to the next:

1. **Release first.** Subtract the row's `reserved_cents` from the pool's `incentive_reserved_cents`, which raises `incentive_available_cents` by the same amount.
2. **Determine the gross entitlement.** A `COMPLETED` row is entitled to its full `reserved_cents`. A `SCREENED_OUT` row is entitled to the screen-out stipend of **200 cents** — a token payment for the person's time, and the reason a screened-out row keeps its reservation all the way to settlement. A row still in `INVITED`, `SCREENED_IN` or `SCHEDULED`, or one already `WITHDRAWN`, is entitled to nothing.
3. **Pay the smaller of entitlement and money on hand.** The actual payment is the lesser of the gross entitlement and the pool's `incentive_available_cents` as it stands after that release.
4. **Record the outcome.** When the payment is greater than zero, the row's `paid_cents` is set to it, its `incentive_state` moves `RESERVED → PAID`, and the pool's `incentive_paid_cents` rises by the same amount. When the payment is zero — whether because the row was not entitled to anything or because the pool had nothing left — the row's `paid_cents` stays 0 and its `incentive_state` moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`.

When the walk is done, the settled `study_id` is appended to the pool's `settled_study_ids` — even when the candidate set was empty and not a single cent moved, so a second binding run for that study is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_SETTLED` either way — and the pool's posture is re-derived from its new ledger. Both modes answer **200 OK** with the pool's own representation, and a successful run in either mode advances the pool's `version` by exactly 1 even when nothing it wrote differs from what was already stored, so a client running two settlements against one pool must re-read the pool in between. Because releases happen row by row before each payment, the ledger invariant of §2.10 holds at every step of the walk, not merely at the end: available plus reserved plus paid always equals `incentive_budget_cents`. Rows whose `incentive_state` is already `FORFEITED` from a no-show, or already `RELEASED` from a withdrawal, are outside both modes' candidate sets — their money was accounted for when the session fell through.

Research-operations metrics are a computed read over one study, available to a `Stakeholder`, `Researcher`, `ResearchLead` or `ResearchOpsAdmin`, and they answer with exactly five figures and nothing else: the `data` object carries `cycle_time_days`, `fielding_days`, `recruitment_efficiency`, `published_findings` and `impact_score`, and deliberately does not echo the study's own id back.

`cycle_time_days` measures intake-to-answer latency. Find the earliest-delivered readout of the study — the one with the smallest `delivered_at` among readouts whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DEFERRED` and whose `delivered_at` is not null, breaking ties on `created_at` ascending and then on server insertion order — then take the difference between that `delivered_at` and the study's `created_at` and floor it to a whole number of 86,400-second days. When no readout of the study has ever been delivered, `cycle_time_days` is null. `fielding_days` is the difference between `fielding_closed_at` and `fielded_at`, floored the same way; it is null while the study's `execution_status` is `PLANNED`, `GUIDE_APPROVED` or `FIELDING`, because fielding has not closed yet. `recruitment_efficiency` is `completed_count` divided by `invited_count`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and exactly 0.0000 when `invited_count` is 0. `published_findings` is the count of the study's findings whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`; retracted findings are excluded. The readout `status` filter belongs to `cycle_time_days` alone: the acceptance term and the follow-up term of the impact score below walk **every** readout of the study whatever its `status`, so a follow-up hanging off a `SUPERSEDED` or still-`DRAFT` readout counts toward the follow-up term exactly as one hanging off an `ACCEPTED` readout does.

`impact_score` is the sum of four terms, each rounded to two decimals before it enters the sum, with the sum itself rounded half-up to two decimals:

- **Acceptance term:** 40.0 points when any readout of the study has `status` `ACCEPTED`, and 0.0 otherwise. This is the largest single term, because a decision actually taken on the evidence is the point of the exercise.
- **Findings term:** divide `published_findings` by a target of 3, hold the ratio at a ceiling of 1.0, multiply by 30.0 and round half-up to two decimals. One published finding is worth 10.0, two are worth 20.0, and three or more all earn the full 30.0.
- **Recruitment term:** multiply `recruitment_efficiency` by 20.0 and round half-up to two decimals. A study that completed sessions with half the people it invited earns 10.0; a study whose `recruitment_status` ended in `RECRUITMENT_FAILED` has a zero efficiency and earns nothing here.
- **Follow-up term:** 10.0 points when the study has at least one follow-up **and** every follow-up of every readout of the study is `DONE`; 0.0 otherwise. A study with no follow-ups at all earns nothing from this term — the points are for closing loops, not for never opening any.
