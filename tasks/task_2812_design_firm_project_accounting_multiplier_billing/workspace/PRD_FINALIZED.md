# Design Firm Project Accounting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Architecture and engineering practices sell hours, not widgets. A firm's entire economics rest on a single ratio — the billing multiplier applied to raw labour cost — and on the discipline with which earned fee, unbilled work in progress, consultant commitments and reimbursable expenses are kept apart from one another in the books. In most firms these live in a spreadsheet lattice that nobody fully trusts: the principal quotes a target multiplier from memory, the project accountant re-derives labour from hours at invoice time and lands two cents away from the timesheet, a public-agency client refuses a bill because the indirect cost rate was never certified, and a subconsultant is paid before the client paid the firm.

This document specifies the API for a design-firm project accounting and multiplier billing platform. It covers project setup against a contract type and a fee structure mapped onto a work breakdown structure; maintenance of labour cost rates and billing multipliers with overhead rate audit; percentage-of-completion revenue recognition with two competing earned-value assessments per phase; consultant subcontract management with tiered markup and pay-when-paid release terms; reimbursable expense capture under client-specific allowability rules; invoice preparation with backup-documentation requirements and a bounded client review cycle; work-in-progress and unbilled aging management with routed write-off approvals; and firm-wide overhead rate certification that gates what may be billed to a public agency.

Two properties drive nearly every rule that follows. First, **every currency figure the firm reports must reconcile**: the value of a charge is conserved as it moves from unbilled work in progress, to billed-and-open, to paid or written off, and every compensating action — voiding an invoice, rejecting an already-approved consultant payable — must return the books to exactly the figure they held before. Second, **preparation and approval are never the same person**: whoever writes a timesheet, a consultant payable, an invoice or a write-off may not be the one who blesses it, and for larger or older write-offs the approval authority itself escalates.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised roles, or is a recognised role that the target operation does not admit, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are spelled exactly `FirmAdmin`, `Controller`, `ProjectManager`, `ProjectAccountant` and `ClientContact`.

A `FirmAdmin` is the firm-level administrator responsible for reference data, project setup and the logical clock; advancing the logical clock is theirs alone. A `Controller` owns firm finance governance: publishing labour cost rates, setting billing multipliers, locking accounting periods, reviewing expenses held for missing backup, releasing consultant payments, recording invoice payments, voiding invoices, certifying overhead rates and returning audit findings are all exclusively theirs, and a `Controller` additionally shares in almost every operation the other firm-side roles can perform. A `ProjectManager` runs their own projects: charge capture, physical completion assessment and first-level approvals. A `ProjectAccountant` handles timesheet and expense entry, consultant payable entry, invoice preparation, submission and revision, and write-off preparation. A `ClientContact` is the client's own reviewer, whose only exclusive capability is accepting or requesting revision on an invoice under client review, and whose reads are confined to the invoices of their own client's projects.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `FirmAdmin`, `Controller`, `ProjectManager` or `ProjectAccountant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server records it wherever a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a time entry, `incurred_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on an expense, `entered_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a consultant payable, `prepared_by_user_id` or `submitted_by_user_id` on an invoice, `prepared_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a write-off, and `prepared_by_user_id` or `certified_by_user_id` on an overhead certification — and it is compared only against other recorded operator identifiers, never resolved as a foreign key. A project's `project_manager_user_id` is likewise an opaque operator identifier: it holds the `X-User-ID` string of the manager who owns the project and is compared against the acting `X-User-ID`, never against any entity's `id`.

`ClientContact` is the one **entity-backed** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ClientContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Client` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for any invoice a client contact reaches, the owning project's `client_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CLIENT` (HTTP 403). This applies to a client review decision on an invoice and to both invoice read paths — the single-invoice retrieval and the invoice list.

A parallel ownership rule constrains project managers. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectManager` and the operation mutates a project's own records, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal that project's `project_manager_user_id`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_MANAGER` (HTTP 403). The operations under this rule are: creating a phase, recording a phase completion assessment, completing a phase, submitting a time entry, approving a time entry, drafting a consultant subcontract, executing a consultant subcontract, approving a consultant payable, rejecting a consultant payable, preparing a write-off, approving a write-off, and activating a project.

Segregation of duties is a third, independent authorisation gate. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `X-User-ID` of the user who created the record now under approval. Four operations enforce it: approving a time entry (against its `submitted_by_user_id`), approving a consultant payable (against its `entered_by_user_id`), approving a write-off (against its `prepared_by_user_id`) and submitting an invoice to the client (against its `prepared_by_user_id`). A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Certifying an overhead rate carries the same restriction: the certifying `X-User-ID` must differ from the certification's `prepared_by_user_id`, and a self-certification is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Finally, write-off approval has an **approval-level** gate layered on top of role membership. A write-off sitting in `PENDING_CONTROLLER_APPROVAL` may only be approved by an acting `Controller`. A `ProjectManager` is authenticated and is an admissible role for the write-off approval operation in general, but is not authorised for that particular row; the attempt is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when the caller omits it, it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by their creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order.

List responses report pagination state in the envelope's `meta` object through four fields: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and the Mutation Counter

Every resource the API persists is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock described in §2.6 at the moment the row was written.

Most resources are additionally mutable and therefore carry two more system-managed fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every change to the resource, including changes to purely derived figures. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource. Projects, phases, billing multipliers, time entries, expense items, consultant subcontracts, consultant payables, invoices, write-offs and overhead rate certifications all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Two entities are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field at all. A `Client` is one: its billing configuration is fixed at registration. A `LaborRate` is the other: published rate history is append-only and is never edited in place; a change of rate is a new row over a new effective window.

A derived-field refresh counts as a mutation. When approving a timesheet raises a phase's accumulated cost and, through it, a project's earned revenue and work in progress, both the phase and the project have their `updated_at` refreshed and their `version` incremented, even though no client sent a request naming them. The version counters are therefore the audit trail of the cascade, not merely of the directly addressed row.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All arithmetic on money and on rates is performed in decimal, never in binary floating point, and every rounding step in this specification uses the **round half up** method — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero.

Monetary figures are carried and returned at exactly 2 decimal places. This includes contract amounts, phase fees, budgets, raw costs, billable amounts, invoice subtotals, discounts, retainage, net amounts due, write-off amounts, commitment figures and every project-level rollup.

Ratio-valued figures are carried and returned at exactly 4 decimal places: billing multipliers and the target multiplier, the achieved net multiplier, retainage and expense markup fractions, consultant markup fractions, volume discount percentages, percent-complete figures, overhead rates and the certified multiplier ceiling. Where a nullable rate is not yet known — a final overhead rate before certification, a certified ceiling on a project with no certification in force — the field is `null` rather than zero.

A request carrying money with more than 2 decimal places, a ratio outside its declared range, a negative amount where a non-negative one is required, a malformed calendar date, an unknown enumeration member, a missing required field or a value of the wrong JSON type is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The same error covers a fee field that contradicts the phase's declared fee basis.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Accounting Period Locks

The server keeps a **logical clock** which is the single source of "now" for the entire system: aging bucket computation, effective-date resolution of rates and multipliers, matching a certification's fiscal year to the current calendar year, and the `created_at` and `updated_at` stamps written on every row. Before the clock is ever set explicitly, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `FirmAdmin`-only administrative capability moves the clock to a supplied instant, passed as `to_iso8601`; after the move, billing posture and aging buckets are re-evaluated against the new instant the next time they are read.

Separately, a `Controller` may close an accounting period on a project by setting `locked_through_date`, an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The field is `null` until a period is first locked. Once set, a charge whose service date falls **on or before** `locked_through_date` may neither be created nor approved: the service date is `work_date` for a time entry, `incurred_on` for an expense and `invoice_date` for a consultant payable. The rejection is `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` (HTTP 422), and it is reachable on time entry creation, expense creation, consultant payable creation and time entry approval.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Project Version

Two write operations are optimistically concurrent on the owning project, and both key on the project's `version` counter. Each requires a non-optional request field named `expected_project_version` carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `Project`. If the supplied value does not equal the project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The first is creating a `BillingMultiplier`. A successor multiplier legitimately supersedes the incumbent and truncates the incumbent's effective window, so a writer holding a stale view of the project would silently supersede a multiplier it never saw. The second is creating an `Invoice`. Invoice preparation sweeps every eligible unbilled charge into itself, so a writer holding a stale view of the project would bill a charge population it never observed.

No other operation takes a version field. Endpoints outside these two neither require nor accept `expected_project_version`, and they perform no concurrency check.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the earliest rule in the ladder below. The ladder is fixed, and its shape encodes a deliberate policy choice — regulatory and compliance safety (overhead rate certification, unallowable cost screening, accounting period locks) is evaluated **ahead of** fiscal capacity (budgets, commitment ceilings, earned-value headroom), because a public-agency billing built on an uncertified rate is a compliance defect whereas an over-budget billing is merely a money defect.

The ladder, earliest first:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognition and role admission for the operation — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Project-manager ownership — `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_MANAGER`.
4. Client-contact ownership — `ERR_NOT_PROJECT_CLIENT`.
5. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. Write-off approval level — `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`.
7. Request and query shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. Existence of the resources named in the URL path, resolved strictly left to right — the not-found errors of §2.9.
9. Resolution of foreign keys carried in the request body — `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE`.
10. Optimistic concurrency on the project version — `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`.
11. Owning-project status — `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACTIVE`.
12. Declared-transition legality for the target row's current state — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
13. Compliance and safety — `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, then `ERR_MULTIPLIER_ABOVE_CERTIFIED_CEILING`, then `ERR_EXPENSE_UNALLOWABLE`.
14. Temporal integrity — `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`, then `ERR_RATE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_MULTIPLIER_BACKDATED`.
15. Structural constraints on the work breakdown structure and on charge placement — `ERR_PHASE_NOT_LEAF`, then `ERR_WBS_CYCLE`, then `ERR_WBS_CODE_DUPLICATE`, then `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_LABOR_RATE`, then `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_MULTIPLIER`, then `ERR_PAY_WHEN_PAID_NOT_SATISFIED`, then `ERR_PHASE_HAS_OPEN_CHARGES`, then `ERR_CERTIFICATION_YEAR_DUPLICATE`, then `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_BACKUP_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`, then `ERR_WBS_FEE_ROLLUP_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_PROJECT_UNDER_BILLING_REVIEW`.
16. Fiscal capacity, last of all — `ERR_CONSULTANT_BUDGET_OVERCOMMIT`, then `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_BILLING_EXCEEDS_EARNED`, then `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE`, then `ERR_WRITE_OFF_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`, then `ERR_WRITE_OFF_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

Two consequences are worth stating explicitly. A write-off approval attempted by the wrong-level approver on a row the approver also prepared reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, because segregation of duties sits above the approval-level gate. And an invoice prepared for a public-agency client with no certified overhead rate reports `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_CERTIFIED` even if that same invoice would also have breached its fixed-fee headroom, because compliance outranks fiscal capacity.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Reverse Discovery

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned. A `project_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; a `phase_id`, `ERR_PHASE_NOT_FOUND`; a `client_id`, `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `subcontract_id`, `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; an `invoice_id`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`; a `write_off_id`, `ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_FOUND`; a `certification_id`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`; and a `billing_multiplier_id`, `ERR_MULTIPLIER_NOT_FOUND`. The three charge identifiers — `time_entry_id`, `expense_id` and `consultant_invoice_id` — share a single code, `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`. All eight are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a request **body** are a separate check with a separate code. When `client_id`, `project_id`, `phase_id`, `parent_phase_id`, `subcontract_id` or `invoice_id` appears in a creation payload and does not resolve — or when a referenced `phase_id` resolves but belongs to a project other than the referenced `project_id` — the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). This applies to project creation, phase creation, time entry creation, expense creation, subcontract creation, consultant payable creation and write-off creation.

The collection topology is flat: each entity has a top-level collection, one single-resource read at that collection plus the resource identifier, and one filtered list read at the collection itself. Every inbound foreign key is exposed as an optional filter on the corresponding list, which is what makes cascade-created links discoverable. Filtering time entries by `billed_invoice_id` is the only way to learn which entries an invoice swept; filtering consultant payables by `client_invoice_id` is the only way to learn which payables it swept. The other available filters are `is_public_agency` on clients; `client_id`, `project_manager_user_id` and `status` on projects; `project_id`, `parent_phase_id` and `status` on phases; `role_code` and `effective_on` on labour rates, where `effective_on` resolves the window covering a given date; `project_id` and `status` on billing multipliers; `project_id`, `phase_id`, `status` and `submitted_by_user_id` on time entries; `project_id`, `phase_id`, `allowability_status`, `billing_status` and `billed_invoice_id` on expenses; `project_id`, `phase_id` and `status` on subcontracts; `subcontract_id`, `approval_status`, `payment_status` and `billing_status` on consultant payables; `project_id` and `status` on invoices; `project_id`, `invoice_id`, `status` and `prepared_by_user_id` on write-offs; and `fiscal_year` and `status` on overhead certifications.

### 2.10. The Conserved Charge Ledger

Each project maintains a live set of rollup figures that together form the firm's project accounting position, and they are recomputed on every mutation touching the project's charges, invoices, phases or write-offs — and also on every read of the project itself, so a retrieval never returns a stale figure.

`raw_labor_cost_to_date` is the sum of `raw_cost` over the project's time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `BILLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. `unbilled_wip` is the billable value of everything approved or allowable that no invoice has claimed: the sum of `billable_amount` over time entries with `status` `APPROVED` and no `billed_invoice_id`, plus the sum of `billable_amount` over expense items whose `allowability_status` is `ALLOWED` and whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`, plus the sum of `billable_amount` over consultant payables whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` and whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`. `billed_open` is the sum of `gross_billed` over the project's invoices whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. `paid_gross_to_date` is the sum of `gross_billed` over invoices whose `status` is `PAID`. `written_off_gross` is the sum of `gross_billed` over invoices whose `status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`, plus the sum of `billable_amount` over time entries whose `status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`. Invoices in `VOID` contribute to none of the four buckets.

`total_charge_value` is the sum of those four buckets — `unbilled_wip` plus `billed_open` plus `paid_gross_to_date` plus `written_off_gross` — and it is a **conserved quantity**. The identity must hold after every request and after every hop of every cascade. No forward action and its compensating counterpart may leave drift: voiding an invoice must return `unbilled_wip` to exactly the figure it held before the invoice was prepared and must reduce `billed_open` by exactly that invoice's `gross_billed`, leaving `total_charge_value` unchanged.

The same recompute-on-read discipline applies one level down. Retrieving a single phase re-evaluates that phase's earned-value figures before returning them, and retrieving a single consultant subcontract re-evaluates its commitment rollup — `approved_to_date`, `released_to_date` and `committed_remaining` — before returning it. A read therefore never serves a figure that lags the underlying charges.

`earned_revenue_to_date` is the sum of `recognized_earned_value` over the project's **leaf** phases only; a non-leaf phase contributes nothing, because its fee is already represented by its children. `net_multiplier_achieved` is the project's billable labour value divided by its raw labour cost: the sum of `billable_amount` over time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `BILLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, divided by `raw_labor_cost_to_date`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. When `raw_labor_cost_to_date` is exactly 0.00, `net_multiplier_achieved` reports 0.0000 rather than being undefined. The figure is reported against the project's `target_multiplier` so that the practice can see the gap between what it intended to earn on the project and what it actually earned.

### 2.11. Billing Posture: the Project-Level Gate

Every project carries a derived `billing_posture` with three possible values — `OPEN`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED` and `HOLD` — recomputed on every mutation touching the project, on every read of the project, and additionally for every public-agency project whenever an overhead rate certification is certified. It is evaluated as an ordered sequence of clauses in which the first matching clause wins:

1. If the project's `status` is `CLOSED`, the posture is `OPEN`. A closed project reports `OPEN` because no gate can apply to it any more.
2. Otherwise, if the project's client has `is_public_agency` set to true and there exists no `OverheadRateCertification` whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `fiscal_year` equals the calendar year of the logical clock, the posture is `HOLD`.
3. Otherwise, if `unbilled_wip` is strictly greater than the project's `wip_alert_threshold`, the posture is `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The comparison is strict: `unbilled_wip` exactly equal to `wip_alert_threshold` leaves the posture `OPEN`.
4. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

`wip_alert_threshold` is supplied at project creation and, when omitted, defaults to 50000.00.

The two non-`OPEN` postures gate different things, and the asymmetry is intentional. `HOLD` blocks invoice preparation, refused with `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409). `REVIEW_REQUIRED` blocks the execution of a consultant subcontract, refused with `ERR_PROJECT_UNDER_BILLING_REVIEW` (HTTP 409), but never blocks invoicing — invoicing is precisely the mechanism by which the excess work in progress is cleared, so blocking it would be self-defeating.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Client, a Project and Its Work Breakdown Structure

Everything begins with the client, because the client's contract dictates what the firm may bill. A `FirmAdmin` or a `Controller` registers a client by supplying its legal `name`, a boolean `is_public_agency` flag marking it as a public agency subject to overhead rate certification and statutory unallowable-cost rules, a `retainage_pct` giving the fraction of each invoice withheld as retainage (from 0.0000 through 0.2000, carried at 4 decimal places), an `expense_markup_pct` giving the markup applied to allowable reimbursable expenses (from 0.0000 through 0.2500, likewise 4 decimal places), a `disallowed_categories` array naming the expense categories this client contractually refuses to reimburse, a `receipt_required_above` amount (non-negative, 2 decimal places) at or above which an expense must have a receipt attached before it can be treated as allowable, and a `review_cycle_days` integer between 1 and 90 recording the contractual number of days the client has to review an invoice. `review_cycle_days` is captured for reporting only — no rule in this specification reads it. The `disallowed_categories` array must be a subset of `TRAVEL`, `MILEAGE`, `PRINTING`, `MEALS`, `ENTERTAINMENT` and `PERMIT_FEE`, and may be empty. A client's configuration is fixed once written.

A project is then created in the `SETUP` state by a `FirmAdmin` or a `Controller`, carrying a `client_id` naming its billing client, a `name`, a `contract_type` of `FIXED_FEE`, `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` or `COST_PLUS_FIXED_FEE`, a `contract_amount` strictly greater than 0.00 at 2 decimal places, a `project_manager_user_id`, a `percent_complete_method` of either `EFFORT_BASED` or `ASSESSED`, a `target_multiplier` between 1.0000 and 6.0000 at 4 decimal places, and optionally a `wip_alert_threshold`. On creation the project's `version` is initialised to 1, all of its rollup figures described in §2.10 are initialised to zero, and its `billing_posture` is computed for the first time. Its `locked_through_date` is `null`, its `overhead_adjustment_required` flag is false, and its `certified_multiplier_ceiling` is `null` unless a certification is already in force for a public-agency client.

Before a project can be opened for business it must have both a shape and a price. Activation — moving a project's `status` from `SETUP` to `ACTIVE`, which a `FirmAdmin`, a `Controller` or the owning `ProjectManager` may perform — requires that the project already be in `SETUP`, that at least one phase exist on it, and that at least one billing multiplier with `status` `ACTIVE` exist on it. Activation recomputes `billing_posture` and increments the project's `version` by 1. Time entries, expenses, consultant subcontracts, consultant payables, invoices and write-off preparations are all accepted only while the project's `status` is `ACTIVE`; attempting any of them, or a phase assessment or a phase completion, while the project is still `SETUP` or already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Creating a billing multiplier is the one deliberate exemption: because a project cannot be activated until an `ACTIVE` multiplier exists, the first multiplier is necessarily written while the project is still in `SETUP`, so for multiplier creation the only status that triggers `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACTIVE` is `CLOSED`.

The project's shape is a **work breakdown structure**: a self-referential tree of phases. A `FirmAdmin`, `Controller` or `ProjectManager` adds a node by supplying its `project_id`, a `wbs_code`, a `fee_basis`, optionally a `parent_phase_id` naming the node's parent (omitted or null for a top-level phase), a `budget_labor_cost` and a `consultant_budget` (both non-negative, 2 decimal places). Every phase begins with `status` `PLANNED` and `assessed_percent_complete` of 0.0000. The `wbs_code` must be unique within the project; a collision is refused with `ERR_WBS_CODE_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). A supplied `parent_phase_id` must reference a phase of the same project and must not close a cycle in the tree; either fault is refused with `ERR_WBS_CYCLE` (HTTP 422). Each phase reports a computed `depth` — 0 for a top-level phase, and one more than its parent's `depth` otherwise — and a computed `is_leaf` flag, which is true exactly when no other phase declares this phase as its `parent_phase_id`.

Fee basis drives the phase's price and constrains which fee field the payload may carry. When `fee_basis` is `PERCENT_OF_CONTRACT`, a `fee_percent` between 0.0001 and 1.0000 at 4 decimal places is required and `lump_sum_fee` must be omitted; the resulting `phase_fee` is the project's `contract_amount` multiplied by `fee_percent`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. When `fee_basis` is `LUMP_SUM`, a `lump_sum_fee` strictly greater than 0.00 is required and `fee_percent` must be omitted; `phase_fee` is then simply that `lump_sum_fee`. Supplying the fee field that contradicts the declared basis, or omitting the one it requires, is a malformed request and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A further structural rule protects fee arithmetic across the tree: a phase may not be created such that the sum of the `phase_fee` values of a parent's children would exceed that parent's own `phase_fee`. A violation is refused with `ERR_WBS_FEE_ROLLUP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Only leaves carry money. Charges — time entries, expenses and consultant subcontracts — may be booked only to a phase whose `is_leaf` is true; booking one to a phase that has child phases is refused with `ERR_PHASE_NOT_LEAF` (HTTP 422). The corollary bites during setup: adding a child under a phase that was previously a leaf makes that parent non-leaf, and it is no longer chargeable from that moment on.

Finally, a `Controller` may close an accounting period on the project by supplying `locked_through_date`, as described in §2.6. Locking a period increments the project's `version` by 1 and makes `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` reachable for every charge dated inside the closed window.

### 3.2. Rate Governance: Cost Rates and Billing Multipliers

The firm's cost side is governed by a firm-wide, append-only table of labour cost rates that only a `Controller` may publish. Each entry names a `role_code` — one of `PRINCIPAL`, `PROJECT_ARCHITECT`, `DESIGNER` or `ADMIN` — a `raw_cost_rate` strictly greater than 0.00 at 2 decimal places giving the hourly raw cost of that labour grade, an inclusive `effective_from` calendar date, and optionally an `effective_to` calendar date which, when supplied, must be strictly later than `effective_from`. Omitting `effective_to` leaves the window open-ended. The interval is **half-open**: a rate covers a date when its `effective_from` is on or before that date and either its `effective_to` is absent or the date is strictly earlier than its `effective_to`. Because of that, two windows for the same `role_code` that merely touch — where the earlier window's `effective_to` equals the later window's `effective_from` — are adjacent, leave neither gap nor overlap, and are entirely legal. Two windows for the same `role_code` that genuinely overlap are refused outright with `ERR_RATE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), and it is this rejection that guarantees at most one rate can ever cover a given role and date.

The firm's revenue side is governed per project by billing multipliers, which only a `Controller` may set. A multiplier entry names its `project_id`, a `multiplier` between 1.0000 and 6.0000 at 4 decimal places giving the factor applied to rounded raw labour cost to produce a billable amount, an inclusive `effective_from` date, optionally an `effective_to` date, and the `expected_project_version` required by §2.7. A new multiplier is created with `status` `ACTIVE`.

Where labour rates reject overlap, multipliers **truncate on supersede**, and the difference is deliberate. When a successor multiplier is created on a project that already has an `ACTIVE` one, the incumbent's `effective_to` is set to the successor's `effective_from` — a half-open truncation, raising no overlap error at all — and the incumbent's `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, incrementing that multiplier's `version` by 1. The successor's `effective_from` must be strictly later than the incumbent's `effective_from`, and it must not fall earlier than the `work_date` of any charge that has already been billed; either violation is refused with `ERR_MULTIPLIER_BACKDATED` (HTTP 409). A `SUPERSEDED` multiplier is not dead history: it still governs every work date that falls inside its now-truncated interval, so both `ACTIVE` and `SUPERSEDED` rows participate in multiplier resolution. Creating a multiplier also increments the owning project's `version` by 1.

One compliance rule caps the revenue side. On a project whose client has `is_public_agency` set to true, a new `multiplier` may not exceed the project's `certified_multiplier_ceiling` — the ceiling derived from the certified final overhead rate as described in §3.9. A multiplier above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_MULTIPLIER_ABOVE_CERTIFIED_CEILING` (HTTP 422).

### 3.3. Timesheet Capture and the Frozen Labour Charge

A timesheet line is entered by a `ProjectManager` or a `ProjectAccountant` against a leaf phase, carrying the `project_id`, the `phase_id`, the `role_code` of the labour grade performing the work, a `work_date`, and `hours` strictly greater than 0.00 and no more than 24.00 at 2 decimal places. The submitter's identifier is captured from the request header into `submitted_by_user_id`. The line lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` and, crucially, carries no money yet: its `raw_cost` and `billable_amount` are both 0.00 and its `applied_multiplier` is `null`. A `SUBMITTED` line contributes to no project rollup and appears in no aging bucket. Its `approved_by_user_id` is `null` and its `billed_invoice_id` is `null`.

Approval is where the money is made, and it is performed by a `ProjectManager` or a `Controller`, moving the line from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`. Four conditions guard the transition beyond the line simply being `SUBMITTED`: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the line's `submitted_by_user_id`, the owning project's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, a labour rate window must cover the pair of `role_code` and `work_date`, and a billing multiplier window on the project must cover the `work_date`. If no rate window covers the pair, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_LABOR_RATE` (HTTP 422); if no multiplier window covers the date, `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_MULTIPLIER` (HTTP 422). Rate resolution considers every published labour rate for that `role_code` regardless of the order in which the rows were created, and multiplier resolution considers every multiplier of the project in `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` status; in both cases a row covers the date when its `effective_from` is on or before the `work_date` and either it has no `effective_to` or the `work_date` is strictly earlier than that `effective_to`. Dates are compared as calendar dates with no time-of-day component. Approval is also subject to the accounting period lock: a `work_date` on or before the project's `locked_through_date` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`, both at entry and again at approval.

Once the rate and multiplier are resolved, the charge is computed and **frozen** onto the line, and the order of the two rounding steps is pinned. First, `raw_cost` is the `hours` multiplied by the resolved `raw_cost_rate`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Second, `applied_multiplier` is set to the resolved `multiplier` value and stored at 4 decimal places. Third, `billable_amount` is the already-rounded `raw_cost` multiplied by `applied_multiplier`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The intermediate rounding is not cosmetic: consider 7.35 hours at a raw cost rate of 62.50 with a multiplier of 3.0500. Rounding the raw cost first gives 459.38, and 459.38 multiplied by 3.0500 rounds to 1401.11. Multiplying all three factors and rounding only once would give 1401.09, which is the wrong answer. Invoices later sum these already-rounded per-line values; they never re-derive labour from `hours`.

Approval sets `approved_by_user_id` from the request header and then propagates in four hops, each of which must be visible in the same response cycle. The first hop is the freeze itself. The second hop raises the phase's `actual_labor_cost_to_date` by the new `raw_cost`, which re-derives that phase's `effort_based_percent_complete`, `effort_based_earned_value` and `recognized_earned_value`. The third hop carries the phase re-derivation up to the project, raising `earned_revenue_to_date` and `unbilled_wip` and refreshing `raw_labor_cost_to_date`, `net_multiplier_achieved` and `total_charge_value`. The fourth hop re-evaluates `billing_posture` on the new `unbilled_wip`: when the figure passes strictly above `wip_alert_threshold` the posture moves from `OPEN` to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and from that moment the execution of a consultant subcontract on that project is blocked. Alongside all of this, a phase still in `PLANNED` moves to `IN_PROGRESS` on the first approval it receives. The transition increments the time entry's `version`, the phase's `version` and the project's `version` by 1 each.

An approved line then travels a small cycle of its own. When an invoice sweeps it, the line moves `APPROVED → BILLED` and its `billed_invoice_id` is set to that invoice; when that invoice is voided the line moves `BILLED → APPROVED` and its `billed_invoice_id` returns to `null`. A line may make that round trip any number of times, and its frozen `raw_cost`, `applied_multiplier` and `billable_amount` never change as it does. The only terminal destination is `WRITTEN_OFF`, reached from `APPROVED` when a write-off that allocated the line is approved.

### 3.4. Reimbursable Expenses and Client Allowability

A `ProjectManager` or a `ProjectAccountant` records a reimbursable expense against a leaf phase with the `project_id`, the `phase_id`, a `category` drawn from `TRAVEL`, `MILEAGE`, `PRINTING`, `MEALS`, `ENTERTAINMENT` and `PERMIT_FEE`, an `amount` strictly greater than 0.00 at 2 decimal places representing the incurred cost before markup, an `incurred_on` date, and a boolean `receipt_attached` stating whether backup documentation is on file. The submitter's header identifier is captured into `incurred_by_user_id`, and `billing_status` starts as `UNBILLED` with `billed_invoice_id` `null`.

Allowability is adjudicated immediately at capture by an **ordered** set of clauses in which the first matching clause wins, and the clause order is pinned:

1. If the `amount` is at or above the client's `receipt_required_above` and `receipt_attached` is false, the expense is held with `allowability_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` and `allowability_reason` `RECEIPT_MISSING`. The threshold comparison is inclusive — an `amount` exactly equal to `receipt_required_above` already requires a receipt.
2. Otherwise, if the client has `is_public_agency` set to true and the `category` is `MEALS` or `ENTERTAINMENT`, the expense is `DISALLOWED` with `allowability_reason` `STATUTORY_UNALLOWABLE`.
3. Otherwise, if the `category` appears in the client's `disallowed_categories`, the expense is `DISALLOWED` with `allowability_reason` `CLIENT_EXCLUDED`.
4. Otherwise the expense is `ALLOWED` with `allowability_reason` `ALLOWED_BY_RULE`.

Placing the missing-receipt clause **above** both exclusion clauses is a deliberate policy choice: allowability cannot honestly be adjudicated before the backup is in hand. An undocumented expense is therefore held in `PENDING_REVIEW` even when its category would have been excluded outright, and the exclusion is applied later, at review.

`billable_amount` follows from the decision. On an `ALLOWED` expense it is the `amount` multiplied by one plus the client's `expense_markup_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; in every other case it stays 0.00. Only an `ALLOWED` expense enters the project's `unbilled_wip`, and when it does the project's rollups and `billing_posture` are re-evaluated in the same request and both the expense's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1. A `DISALLOWED` expense costs the firm rather than the client: its `billable_amount` stays 0.00, it never enters `unbilled_wip`, and only the expense's own `version` increments. `DISALLOWED` is terminal.

A `Controller` alone adjudicates an expense held for missing backup, supplying a `decision` of either `ALLOW` or `DISALLOW` together with a `justification`, which is recorded on the expense as `review_justification`; the adjudicator's header identifier is recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id`. The expense's `allowability_status` must be `PENDING_REVIEW`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). A `DISALLOW` decision is always available and always lands `DISALLOWED` with `allowability_reason` `DISALLOWED_BY_REVIEW` and `billable_amount` 0.00. An `ALLOW` decision, however, is refused with `ERR_EXPENSE_UNALLOWABLE` (HTTP 422) when the expense would have been excluded had the receipt been present — that is, when the client has `is_public_agency` set to true and the `category` is `MEALS` or `ENTERTAINMENT`, or when the `category` appears in the client's `disallowed_categories`. Neither exclusion is overridable by review. An `ALLOW` decision on an otherwise allowable expense held only for the missing receipt sets `allowability_reason` to `ALLOWED_BY_REVIEW`, computes the marked-up `billable_amount` by the same formula as the capture path, and admits it to `unbilled_wip` in the same request. The same error code also guards invoicing: a `DISALLOWED` expense may never be swept onto an invoice.

### 3.5. Consultant Subcontracts, Payables and Pay-When-Paid

Subconsultants are engaged against a leaf phase's consultant capacity. A `FirmAdmin`, `Controller` or `ProjectManager` drafts a subcontract with the `project_id`, the `phase_id`, the `consultant_name` of the subconsultant firm, a `committed_amount` strictly greater than 0.00 giving the total contractual commitment, a boolean `pay_when_paid` flag, and an inclusive commitment window bounded by `commitment_start` and `commitment_end`, where `commitment_end` must not precede `commitment_start`. The client-facing markup is deliberately **not** supplied here; it is derived per payable at approval time. A drafted subcontract lands in `status` `DRAFT`, consumes no capacity at all, and has its `committed_remaining` initialised to the full `committed_amount` while `approved_to_date` and `released_to_date` are 0.00.

Execution — moving a subcontract from `DRAFT` to `EXECUTED`, performed by a `FirmAdmin`, `Controller` or `ProjectManager` — is the moment capacity is consumed, and it is guarded on four counts: the subcontract must be in `DRAFT`, the owning project's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, the project's `billing_posture` must not be `REVIEW_REQUIRED` (otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_UNDER_BILLING_REVIEW`, HTTP 409), and the phase's aggregate consultant exposure must fit within its budget. That exposure is computed over the **closed-inclusive** commitment windows of the phase's other `EXECUTED` subcontracts: a peer subcontract counts when it sits on the same phase, is not the candidate itself, has `status` `EXECUTED`, and its `commitment_start` is on or before the candidate's `commitment_end` while its `commitment_end` is on or after the candidate's `commitment_start`. Two windows that merely touch at a single date therefore **do** overlap. The exposure is the sum of the `committed_amount` of every such peer plus the candidate's own `committed_amount`, and it may not exceed the phase's `consultant_budget`; the cap comparison is strict, so an exposure exactly equal to `consultant_budget` is permitted while anything above it is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_BUDGET_OVERCOMMIT` (HTTP 409). Pairwise overlap between two individual subcontracts is perfectly legal — only the aggregate is capped. Subcontracts in `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` contribute nothing to the exposure, which means closing a subcontract frees its capacity for a new overlapping commitment. Execution raises the phase's `consultant_committed_open` by the `committed_amount`, moves a phase still in `PLANNED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and increments both the subcontract's `version` and the phase's `version` by 1.

Each payable the subconsultant sends in is entered by a `ProjectAccountant` or a `Controller` with the `subcontract_id` it draws against, an `amount` strictly greater than 0.00 representing the firm's cost, an `invoice_date`, and a boolean `backup_attached` stating whether the consultant's supporting documentation is on file. The enterer's header identifier is captured into `entered_by_user_id`. A payable carries **three independent status axes**: `approval_status`, which begins at `RECEIVED`; `payment_status`, which begins at `WITHHELD`; and `billing_status`, which begins at `UNBILLED`. Until it is approved, its `applied_markup_pct` is 0.0000, its `markup_amount` is 0.00 and its `billable_amount` is 0.00; `approved_by_user_id`, `rejection_reason` and `client_invoice_id` are all `null`. Payable entry is also subject to the accounting period lock: an `invoice_date` on or before `locked_through_date` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`.

Approving a payable — a `ProjectManager` or `Controller` action moving `approval_status` from `RECEIVED` to `APPROVED` — requires that the payable be `RECEIVED`, that its subcontract be `EXECUTED`, that the owning project be `ACTIVE`, and that the `amount` not exceed the subcontract's `committed_remaining`; an over-draw is refused with `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `entered_by_user_id`.

Approval also fixes the markup, and four inputs interact non-linearly to produce it. The system first measures `approved_cost_to_date`: the sum of `amount` over every payable belonging to **this project** across all of its subcontracts whose `approval_status` is already `APPROVED`, evaluated before the current approval is applied, so the row being approved is excluded from its own cumulative base. The tier is a project-level cumulative volume, not a per-subcontract one. A base markup fraction of 0.1000 applies as standard; once `approved_cost_to_date` reaches 250000.00 the base drops to 0.0600, and the threshold is inclusive, so cumulative approved consultant cost of exactly 250000.00 already selects the lower tier. If the project's `contract_type` is `FIXED_FEE`, a further 0.0200 is added to that base, because on a fixed fee the firm carries the coordination risk itself. Finally, if the client has `is_public_agency` set to true, the result is capped at 0.0500 — the effective `applied_markup_pct` is the lesser of the computed base and that cap — while for a private client the computed base stands unchanged. `applied_markup_pct` is stored at 4 decimal places. From it, `markup_amount` is the payable's `amount` multiplied by `applied_markup_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `billable_amount` is the `amount` plus that `markup_amount`. All three figures are frozen on the payable at approval.

Approval then draws down the commitment and admits the marked-up value to work in progress. The subcontract's `approved_to_date` rises by the `amount` and its `committed_remaining` falls by the same amount; `committed_remaining` is always the `committed_amount` less `approved_to_date`, and `released_to_date` is always the sum of `amount` over the subcontract's payables whose `payment_status` is `RELEASED`. Rejected payables contribute to neither sum. The project's `unbilled_wip` rises by the payable's `billable_amount`, which re-runs `billing_posture`. The payable's `version`, the subcontract's `version` and the project's `version` each increment by 1.

Rejection is available on two branches, and they differ. A `ProjectManager` or `Controller` may reject a payable that is still `RECEIVED`, supplying a `reason` which is recorded as `rejection_reason`; its `billable_amount` stays 0.00 and only the payable's `version` increments. The same actors may also reject a payable that has already been `APPROVED`, but only while its `billing_status` is still `UNBILLED`. That branch is a full compensating action: `approved_to_date` falls by the `amount` and `committed_remaining` rises by the same amount, restoring the identity that a subcontract's `committed_amount` always equals its `committed_remaining` plus its `approved_to_date`; the project's `unbilled_wip` falls by the previously frozen `billable_amount`; and `applied_markup_pct`, `markup_amount` and `billable_amount` are reset to 0.0000, 0.00 and 0.00 respectively. The payable's, the subcontract's and the project's `version` all increment by 1. A `RECEIVED → APPROVED → REJECTED` round trip therefore returns `committed_remaining` to exactly its starting value. `REJECTED` is terminal on the approval axis, and a rejected payable's `payment_status` stays `WITHHELD` permanently — it can never be released.

Payment release moves `payment_status` from `WITHHELD` to `RELEASED` and reaches that state by two different routes. A `Controller` may release explicitly, which requires the payable's `approval_status` to be `APPROVED` and its `payment_status` to be `WITHHELD`, and — when the subcontract's `pay_when_paid` is true — additionally requires that the payable's `client_invoice_id` be non-null and that the client invoice it names have `status` `PAID`. A release attempted on a pay-when-paid subcontract before the client invoice that billed the payable reached `PAID`, including the case where the payable was never billed to the client at all, is refused with `ERR_PAY_WHEN_PAID_NOT_SATISFIED` (HTTP 409). The system also releases automatically: when the client invoice carrying the payable reaches `PAID`, or reaches `WRITTEN_OFF`, every payable on it whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, whose `payment_status` is `WITHHELD` and whose `billing_status` is `BILLED_TO_CLIENT` moves to `RELEASED`. A written-off receivable does not excuse the firm from paying its subconsultant, which is why the write-off path releases the payable too. Either route raises the subcontract's `released_to_date` by the `amount` and increments both the payable's `version` and the subcontract's `version` by 1.

When a subcontract's `released_to_date` reaches or passes its `committed_amount`, the subcontract moves `EXECUTED → CLOSED`. Closure lowers the phase's `consultant_committed_open` by the `committed_amount` and increments both the subcontract's `version` and the phase's `version` by 1 — and, as §3.6 explains, it is what clears the open-commitment obstacle to completing that phase.

On the third axis, an approved payable moves `UNBILLED → BILLED_TO_CLIENT` when a client invoice sweeps it, taking a `client_invoice_id`, and moves back `BILLED_TO_CLIENT → UNBILLED` with `client_invoice_id` cleared when that invoice is voided — but only while its `payment_status` is still `WITHHELD`. The three axes genuinely evolve independently: a payable can be `RELEASED` while still `BILLED_TO_CLIENT`, and can be rejected only while `UNBILLED`.

### 3.6. Earned Value, Phase Completion and Project Closure

Revenue recognition is per phase, and each phase computes **two competing earned-value figures** side by side, of which the project's `percent_complete_method` selects one for the books.

The cost-to-cost figure starts from `actual_labor_cost_to_date`, the sum of `raw_cost` over the phase's time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `BILLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; entries still in `SUBMITTED` are excluded, so an unapproved timesheet moves neither figure. From it, `effort_based_percent_complete` is `actual_labor_cost_to_date` divided by `budget_labor_cost`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places and then capped at 1.0000 by taking the lesser of the two; when `budget_labor_cost` is exactly 0.00 the figure is 0.0000 rather than undefined. `effort_based_earned_value` is then the `phase_fee` multiplied by `effort_based_percent_complete`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The physical-completion figure starts from `assessed_percent_complete`, a value between 0.0000 and 1.0000 at 4 decimal places written by a `ProjectManager` or `Controller` recording their judgement of how much of the phase is actually built. `assessed_earned_value` is the `phase_fee` multiplied by `assessed_percent_complete`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Recording an assessment requires the phase to be `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and the owning project to be `ACTIVE`; it recomputes both earned-value figures and refreshes the project's `earned_revenue_to_date`, though it moves `recognized_earned_value` only when the project's method is `ASSESSED`.

`recognized_earned_value` — the single figure that rolls up into the project — is chosen in this order: it is the full `phase_fee` whenever the phase's `status` is `COMPLETE`; otherwise it is `effort_based_earned_value` when the project's `percent_complete_method` is `EFFORT_BASED`; otherwise it is `assessed_earned_value`. The two variants really do diverge on identical timesheets. Take a phase with a `phase_fee` of 200000.00, a `budget_labor_cost` of 60000.00, an `actual_labor_cost_to_date` of 1084.38 and an `assessed_percent_complete` of 0.2500: the cost-to-cost route recognises 3620.00 while the assessed route recognises 50000.00. A computed read exposes both routes side by side for exactly this reason, returning `phase_id`, `phase_fee`, `budget_labor_cost`, `actual_labor_cost_to_date`, `effort_based_percent_complete`, `effort_based_earned_value`, `assessed_percent_complete`, `assessed_earned_value`, `selected_method` and `recognized_earned_value`. That payload is a computed report, not a persisted entity, and it therefore carries none of the common resource properties of §2.4.

Completing a phase — a `ProjectManager` or `Controller` action moving `status` from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETE` — is the reconciliation event. It requires that the phase be `IN_PROGRESS`, that no time entry on the phase remain in `SUBMITTED`, that no expense on the phase remain in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and that no `EXECUTED` subcontract on the phase still have a `released_to_date` below its `committed_amount`. Open charges are refused with `ERR_PHASE_HAS_OPEN_CHARGES` (HTTP 409). On success, `recognized_earned_value` is forced to the full `phase_fee` regardless of the project's `percent_complete_method`, so completion settles the delta between the selected variant and the full fee in a single step; that change re-derives the project's `earned_revenue_to_date` and, through it, the reported `total_charge_value` position; the project's closure condition is re-evaluated; and both the phase's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1. `COMPLETE` is terminal for a phase.

Project closure is not requested by anyone — it is a **bubble-up** transition the system re-evaluates at the end of any transition that changes a phase's `status`, an invoice's `status` or a write-off's `status`. A project moves from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED` when every leaf phase of the project has reached `COMPLETE` (non-leaf phases, which can carry no charges, are not required to reach `COMPLETE` — their fee is represented by their children), the project's `unbilled_wip` is exactly 0.00, and every invoice of the project is in `PAID`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `VOID`. Closure sets the project's `status` to `CLOSED`, recomputes `billing_posture` — which, per §2.11, always reports `OPEN` for a closed project because no gate can apply any more — and increments the project's `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.7. The Client Invoice: Preparation, Review Cycle and Settlement

Invoice preparation is a sweep. A `ProjectAccountant` or a `Controller` prepares a draft invoice by naming the `project_id`, a `period_end` acting as the inclusive cut-off date for the sweep, and the `expected_project_version` demanded by §2.7. The owning project must be `ACTIVE`, and its `billing_posture` must not be `HOLD` — a public-agency project with no certified overhead rate for the current year is refused here with `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409). The preparer's header identifier is captured into `prepared_by_user_id`, the invoice lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, and its `submitted_by_user_id`, `client_decision_comment`, `paid_on` and `void_reason` are all `null`.

The sweep gathers only charges of the invoice's own project that no other invoice has claimed and that are dated on or before `period_end`. `labor_billed` is the sum of `billable_amount` over time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED`, whose `billed_invoice_id` is null and whose `work_date` is on or before `period_end`. `expense_billed` is the sum of `billable_amount` over expenses whose `allowability_status` is `ALLOWED`, whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED` and whose `incurred_on` is on or before `period_end`. `consultant_billed` is the sum of `billable_amount` over consultant payables whose subcontract belongs to this project, whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED` and whose `invoice_date` is on or before `period_end`. Time entries still in `SUBMITTED`, expenses in `PENDING_REVIEW` or `DISALLOWED`, and payables still `RECEIVED` or already `REJECTED` are all excluded. `gross_billed` is `labor_billed` plus `consultant_billed` plus `expense_billed`, and if it comes to exactly 0.00 there is no invoice to make: the request is refused with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_INVOICE` (HTTP 422).

From `gross_billed` the remaining money figures follow in a **pinned order** whose apparent circularity is only apparent. First, the volume discount tier is keyed on the **pre-discount** `gross_billed`: `volume_discount_pct` is 0.0200 once `gross_billed` reaches 250000.00, otherwise 0.0100 once it reaches 100000.00, otherwise 0.0000. Both tier comparisons are inclusive. Second, `discount_amount` is `gross_billed` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Third, `retainage_amount` is keyed on the **post-discount** base and never on `gross_billed` itself: it is `gross_billed` less `discount_amount`, multiplied by the client's `retainage_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Fourth, `net_due` is `gross_billed` less `discount_amount` less `retainage_amount`. Reversing the first two steps gives a different bill: on a `gross_billed` of 100500.00 against a `retainage_pct` of 0.0500, the pinned order yields a `volume_discount_pct` of 0.0100, a `discount_amount` of 1005.00, a `retainage_amount` of 4974.75 and a `net_due` of 94520.25, whereas withholding retainage first and tiering the remainder would have produced no discount at all and a `net_due` of 95475.00.

The invoice also records what it swept. `swept_labor_count`, `swept_expense_count` and `swept_consultant_count` are the numbers of time entries, expenses and consultant payables attached to it, and `backup_missing_count` is the count of swept consultant payables whose `backup_attached` is false. The sweep's side effects are that every swept time entry moves to `BILLED` with its `billed_invoice_id` set to this invoice, every swept expense moves to `BILLED` with its `billed_invoice_id` set, every swept payable moves to `BILLED_TO_CLIENT` with its `client_invoice_id` set, the project's `unbilled_wip` falls to whatever residual remains and its `billed_open` rises by `gross_billed`, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

One fiscal ceiling then applies, and it applies only on a project whose `contract_type` is `FIXED_FEE`. Projects on `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` or `COST_PLUS_FIXED_FEE` are not subject to it. On a fixed-fee project, the firm may not bill ahead of what it has earned. `already_billed` is the project's `billed_open` plus `paid_gross_to_date` plus `written_off_gross`, measured **excluding** the invoice under construction — so it counts every invoice of the project in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, while invoices in `VOID` are excluded entirely. The headroom is the project's `earned_revenue_to_date` less `already_billed`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. If the new invoice's `gross_billed` is strictly greater than that headroom, the preparation is refused with `ERR_BILLING_EXCEEDS_EARNED` (HTTP 422); a `gross_billed` exactly equal to the headroom is permitted. The same ceiling is re-tested whenever a revision re-runs the sweep.

Submitting the invoice to the client moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW` and is performed by a `ProjectAccountant` or a `Controller`. The invoice must be in `DRAFT`, its `gross_billed` must be strictly greater than 0.00, and — for a client whose `is_public_agency` is true — its `backup_missing_count` must be 0, because a public agency will not accept a bill that passes through a subconsultant's charge with no supporting documentation. A public-agency submission with any missing backup is refused with `ERR_BACKUP_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING` (HTTP 409). Backup completeness is enforced here and deliberately not at preparation, giving the accountant a window in which to chase the paperwork. The submitter's header identifier is captured into `submitted_by_user_id` and must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`. From `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW` onward the invoice is read-only for sweeps until it returns to `DRAFT`.

The client's own reviewer then decides. A `ClientContact` whose `X-User-ID` equals the project's `client_id` supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, optionally with a `comment`, which is recorded on the invoice as `client_decision_comment`. `APPROVE` moves the invoice `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW → APPROVED`. `REQUEST_REVISION` first increments `revision_round` by 1 and then moves the invoice `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`. The cycle is bounded: `revision_round` ranges from 0 to 3, and a `REQUEST_REVISION` attempted once `revision_round` has already reached 3 is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409), which leaves the client only `APPROVE` while the firm retains the option to void. Either decision increments the invoice's `version` by 1.

A revision-requested invoice is returned to draft by a `ProjectAccountant` or a `Controller`, moving it `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT` and closing the loop `DRAFT → PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`. Charges already attached stay attached; the sweep re-runs and additionally attaches every newly eligible charge dated on or before `period_end`. Because the charge set has changed, `gross_billed`, `volume_discount_pct`, `discount_amount`, `retainage_amount` and `net_due` are all recomputed from the new set by the same pinned order. The invoice's `submitted_by_user_id` is cleared, so the segregation-of-duties check applies afresh on the next submission, and the invoice's `version` increments by 1.

Settlement is a `Controller` action recording receipt of the invoice's net due in full, moving the invoice `APPROVED → PAID` with a supplied `received_on` date stored as `paid_on`. Its consequences run four hops deep. First, the project's `billed_open` falls by `gross_billed` while `paid_gross_to_date` rises by the same value. Second, the settlement satisfies pay-when-paid, so every consultant payable whose `client_invoice_id` is this invoice and whose `payment_status` is `WITHHELD` moves `WITHHELD → RELEASED`. Third, each release raises its subcontract's `released_to_date`, and any subcontract whose `released_to_date` reaches or passes its `committed_amount` moves `EXECUTED → CLOSED`. Fourth, that closure lowers the phase's `consultant_committed_open`, which clears the open-commitment obstacle blocking that phase's move from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETE`. The project's closure condition is then re-evaluated, `billing_posture` is recomputed, and both the invoice's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1.

Voiding is the compensating action, reserved to a `Controller`, and it is available from `DRAFT`, from `PENDING_CLIENT_REVIEW` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED` — three transitions with identical release-and-restore behaviour. The `reason` supplied is stored as `void_reason`. Every attached time entry moves `BILLED → APPROVED`, every attached expense moves `BILLED → UNBILLED`, and every attached consultant payable moves `BILLED_TO_CLIENT → UNBILLED`, each with its invoice link cleared. The project's `unbilled_wip` returns to exactly its pre-invoice value and its `billed_open` falls by `gross_billed`, so `total_charge_value` is unchanged — this is the conservation guarantee of §2.10 in its sharpest form. The voided invoice's own recorded figures are not rewritten by the void: `labor_billed`, `expense_billed`, `consultant_billed`, `gross_billed`, `volume_discount_pct`, `discount_amount`, `retainage_amount`, `net_due`, `swept_labor_count`, `swept_expense_count`, `swept_consultant_count` and `backup_missing_count` are all left exactly as the last sweep computed them rather than being zeroed, even though the charges they measured are now detached; the row simply stops contributing to any of the four buckets of §2.10. The invoice's `version` and the project's `version` each increment by 1. `PAID`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `VOID` are all terminal for an invoice; an `APPROVED` invoice cannot be voided, and any attempt to repeat a transition already applied or to leave a terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

### 3.8. Unbilled Aging and Routed Write-Offs

Work in progress that nobody bills eventually stops being an asset. A `Controller`, `ProjectManager` or `ProjectAccountant` may read a project's unbilled position bucketed by age against the logical clock. The report's `as_of_date` is the calendar date of the logical clock. Each unbilled charge contributes its `billable_amount` at an age measured in whole calendar days between its own charge date and `as_of_date`, floored at zero so that a charge dated in the future contributes at age zero rather than a negative age; the charge date is `work_date` for a time entry, `incurred_on` for an expense and `invoice_date` for a consultant payable. The population is precisely the unbilled population of §2.10: time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED` with no `billed_invoice_id`, expenses whose `allowability_status` is `ALLOWED` and `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`, and consultant payables whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` and `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`. `bucket_0_30` holds the value of charges aged 30 days or fewer, `bucket_31_60` those aged 31 through 60 days inclusive, `bucket_61_90` those aged 61 through 90 days inclusive, and `bucket_over_90` those aged 91 days or more. `total_unbilled` is the sum of the four buckets and must equal the project's `unbilled_wip`. `write_off_eligible_amount` is exactly `bucket_over_90`. Alongside those figures the report echoes the `project_id`; like the earned-value report it is a computed payload rather than a persisted entity.

Note the deliberate asymmetry between the report and the approval routing that follows: the over-90 bucket is strictly more than 90 days old, while write-off routing escalates to a `Controller` at 90 days or more. A charge exactly 90 days old therefore still sits in `bucket_61_90` and is not counted as write-off eligible, yet a write-off touching it already demands `Controller` authority.

A write-off is prepared by a `ProjectAccountant`, a `ProjectManager` or a `Controller` on an `ACTIVE` project, naming the `project_id`, a `target_type` of either `UNBILLED_LABOR_WIP` or `INVOICE_RECEIVABLE`, an `amount` strictly greater than 0.00, a free-text `reason` giving the business justification, and — when and only when `target_type` is `INVOICE_RECEIVABLE` — an `invoice_id` naming the receivable. The `invoice_id` must be omitted when `target_type` is `UNBILLED_LABOR_WIP`. The preparer's header identifier is captured into `prepared_by_user_id`, and `approved_by_user_id` is `null` until approval. The `target_type` selects which charge population the `amount` is allocated against, and the two paths behave quite differently.

For `UNBILLED_LABOR_WIP` the amount is allocated against unbilled approved labour, and only against labour: expenses and consultant payables are never written off through this path. The candidate list is the project's time entries whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `billed_invoice_id` is null, ordered by `work_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. Walking that ordered list, each candidate's `billable_amount` is added to a running total and its identifier appended to the allocation. The walk succeeds the moment the running total equals the write-off `amount` exactly. If the running total ever passes above the `amount`, or if the list is exhausted with the running total still short of it, the preparation is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_AMOUNT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE` (HTTP 422): the amount must equal an exact prefix sum of the ordered candidate list. On success, `allocated_time_entry_ids` holds the ordered list of identifiers the amount was allocated to, and `aging_days` is frozen at the greatest age in whole days among those allocated entries, measured from each entry's `work_date` against the logical clock.

For `INVOICE_RECEIVABLE` there is no allocation at all: `allocated_time_entry_ids` is empty, the `amount` must equal the named invoice's `net_due` exactly — any other figure is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — and `aging_days` is frozen at the age in whole days of that invoice's `period_end`.

With `amount` and `aging_days` fixed, the write-off is **routed** to an approval level, and the routing is what forks its lifecycle. `required_approver` is set to `CONTROLLER` when the `amount` is at or above 5000.00 or the `aging_days` is at or above 90 — both thresholds inclusive, so an amount of exactly 5000.00 and an age of exactly 90 days each escalate — and to `PROJECT_MANAGER` otherwise. An `INVOICE_RECEIVABLE` write-off always routes to `CONTROLLER` regardless of its amount or age. The write-off's initial `status` is `PENDING_CONTROLLER_APPROVAL` when `required_approver` is `CONTROLLER` and `PENDING_PM_APPROVAL` when it is `PROJECT_MANAGER`.

The two branches converge on the single terminal state `APPROVED` but carry different side effects. On the project-manager branch, a `ProjectManager` or a `Controller` approves a write-off sitting in `PENDING_PM_APPROVAL`; when the acting role is `ProjectManager`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the project's `project_manager_user_id`, and in either case the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`. This branch leaves the project's `overhead_adjustment_required` flag **unchanged**. Every time entry named in `allocated_time_entry_ids` moves `APPROVED → WRITTEN_OFF`, each such entry's `version` incrementing by 1; the project's `unbilled_wip` falls by the write-off `amount` while `written_off_gross` rises by the same amount; `billing_posture` is re-run and the project's closure condition re-evaluated; and both the write-off's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1.

On the controller branch, only an acting `Controller` may approve a write-off sitting in `PENDING_CONTROLLER_APPROVAL` — a `ProjectManager` attempting it is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` — and the acting `X-User-ID` must likewise differ from `prepared_by_user_id`. This branch, and only this branch, sets the project's `overhead_adjustment_required` flag to true, because a write-off approved at controller level must be disclosed in the firm's next overhead certification. For an `UNBILLED_LABOR_WIP` target the labour side effects are exactly those of the project-manager branch: the allocated entries move `APPROVED → WRITTEN_OFF`, `unbilled_wip` falls by the `amount` and `written_off_gross` rises by the `amount`. For an `INVOICE_RECEIVABLE` target — a case only this branch can reach — the named invoice moves `APPROVED → WRITTEN_OFF`, which requires that invoice to be in `APPROVED` and the write-off `amount` to equal its `net_due`; the project's `billed_open` falls by that invoice's `gross_billed` while `written_off_gross` rises by that same `gross_billed`. The write-off amount is the `net_due` but the rollup moves the gross, which is exactly what keeps `total_charge_value` conserved. Writing off the receivable also releases its withheld consultant payables, as §3.5 describes, because the subconsultant is owed regardless. `billing_posture` is re-run, the project's closure condition re-evaluated, and both the write-off's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1.

### 3.9. Overhead Rate Certification and Public-Agency Compliance

A firm that bills public agencies must be able to prove its indirect cost rate. Certification is firm-wide rather than per project: a `Controller` or a `FirmAdmin` submits a fiscal year's figures, naming the `fiscal_year` between 2020 and 2100, a `ytd_months` count between 1 and 12 giving how many months of actuals the submitted figures contain, a `direct_labor_base` strictly greater than 0.00, an `indirect_cost_pool` strictly greater than 0.00 and inclusive of unallowable costs, and an `unallowable_costs` figure of at least 0.00 giving the portion of that pool that is statutorily unallowable. The submitter's header identifier is captured into `prepared_by_user_id`, the row lands in `status` `SUBMITTED`, and its `final_overhead_rate`, `certified_multiplier_ceiling`, `certified_by_user_id` and `findings` are all `null`. At most one certification per `fiscal_year` may exist in `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED` status at a time; a second submission for an already-occupied year is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_YEAR_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409).

Two rates are computed from the very same submitted figures, and they deliberately differ. The **provisional** rate is computed at submission for interim billing guidance. It annualises the year-to-date figures and leaves unallowable costs inside the pool: the annualised pool is the `indirect_cost_pool` multiplied by 12 and divided by `ytd_months`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the annualised base is the `direct_labor_base` multiplied by 12 and divided by `ytd_months`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; and `provisional_overhead_rate` is the annualised pool divided by the annualised base, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The **final** rate is computed at certification on an actual basis with no annualisation and with unallowable costs removed: `final_overhead_rate` is the `indirect_cost_pool` less `unallowable_costs`, divided by the `direct_labor_base`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Because the provisional variant keeps unallowable costs in the pool, it reports the higher of the two figures for identical inputs. Consider a pool of 1980000.00 against a base of 1200000.00 with 90000.00 of unallowable costs and six months of actuals: `provisional_overhead_rate` comes to 1.6500 while `final_overhead_rate` comes to 1.5750.

From the final rate the firm derives the only multiplier a public client may be billed against. `certified_multiplier_ceiling` is one plus `final_overhead_rate`, multiplied by one plus a profit margin cap of 0.1000, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. On the figures above, the ceiling is 2.8325.

Certification is a `Controller` action moving the row `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the certification's `prepared_by_user_id`; a self-certification is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It runs in three hops. First, `final_overhead_rate` and the derived `certified_multiplier_ceiling` are computed and `certified_by_user_id` is recorded from the request header. Second, every project whose client has `is_public_agency` set to true recomputes its own `certified_multiplier_ceiling` from the new `final_overhead_rate`. Third, those projects re-run `billing_posture`, so a project that was held for a missing certification moves from `HOLD` to `OPEN` and becomes invoiceable. The certification's `version` increments by 1 and so does the `version` of every affected project.

Certification may also be refused. A `Controller` may return an audit finding instead, moving the row `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with a supplied `findings` text. No final rate is computed — `final_overhead_rate` stays `null` — no `certified_multiplier_ceiling` is published, public-agency invoicing stays blocked, and the certification's `version` increments by 1. Rejection frees the `fiscal_year` slot, so a corrected certification may be submitted for the same year. Both `CERTIFIED` and `REJECTED` are terminal, and only a `SUBMITTED` row may be certified or returned with findings.

A project's own `certified_multiplier_ceiling` mirrors the certification that is in force: it is the ceiling published by the `CERTIFIED` certification whose `fiscal_year` matches the calendar year of the logical clock, and it is `null` whenever no such certification exists. At most one such row can exist, so the ceiling is never ambiguous.
