# MOB Leasing & Referral Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A medical office building is not an ordinary commercial asset. Its landlord leases space to physician practices who are simultaneously the referral sources of the health system anchoring the building, which means every commercial decision — the rent per square foot, the size of a build-out allowance, whether a late invoice is forgiven, who gets the covered staff stalls — is also a regulatory decision. The landlord's exposure is not merely a bad debt; it is the accusation that space was priced as remuneration for patient referrals. This API exists to make that exposure measurable and auditable: it holds the tenant-mix balance between health-system space and independent practice space, it prices space against a defensible fair-market-value band supported by third-party appraisals, it forces documentation for master-lease and sublease arrangements, and it treats timely rent payment as a compliance signal rather than an accounting one.

The system that leasing agents, compliance officers, property managers and facilities engineers work in today is a folder of spreadsheets and a shared drive of PDFs. Nobody can answer, on demand, what fraction of leased area a health system holds in a given building, which appraisal is the controlling opinion behind a certified rent, whether an imaging suite was shielded before a tenant took possession, or how much rent the landlord has effectively forgiven a single practice over the last twelve months. Worse, the answers drift: a lease is amended after certification and the certification is never revisited; a controlling appraisal quietly ages past its first anniversary; a suite is re-leased before its predecessor's decommissioning window closes. Each of those drifts is a finding waiting to be written up.

This document specifies a single HTTP service that owns all of it. Business collections sit at the root of the URL space — buildings, suites, tenants, leases, appraisals, tenant-improvement projects, rent invoices, compliance cases, shared-space bookings and parking allocations are all top-level paths — because a lease is genuinely a hub that points at a building, a suite, a tenant and, for a sublease, at another lease. Nothing here runs on wall-clock time: the service keeps a logical clock that tests and operators advance explicitly, and every date-driven consequence (delinquency, appraisal expiry, holdover, remediation deadlines, booking completion) fires only when a daily close is run against that clock. The remainder of this document sets out the global contracts (§2), the business rules and workflows the implementation must reproduce (§3), the endpoint surface (§4) and the error vocabulary (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including reads, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the addressed operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PortfolioAdmin`, `LeasingAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PropertyManager`, `FacilitiesEngineer` and `TenantPortalUser`, spelled exactly as shown.

`PortfolioAdmin` is the owner-side portfolio operator. It establishes physical and commercial reference data — buildings, suites, shared spaces and tenant records — and it operates the system epoch (the logical clock and the daily close). It deliberately holds no authority over any individual lease's rent, compliance disposition or occupancy.

`LeasingAgent` is the deal-side operator: it drafts, amends, withdraws and submits leases for review, executes certified leases, files compliance attestations, and runs the tenant-improvement design and construction-start surface. It cannot certify fair market value and it cannot commence or terminate occupancy.

`ComplianceOfficer` is the referral-compliance authority: it files and adjudicates appraisals, certifies fair market value, issues lease compliance dispositions, sets and clears the building mix freeze, opens and disposes of compliance cases, and writes off delinquent rent. It cannot draft, amend or execute a lease — that exclusion is what makes certification a genuine second pair of eyes.

`PropertyManager` is the operations-side operator: it commences and terminates occupancy, issues invoices and records payments, grants or revokes after-hours access, and allocates and revokes parking. It cannot alter lease economics and it cannot certify fair market value.

`FacilitiesEngineer` is the technical reviewer for tenant-improvement work. It owns the shielding and utility review dimensions and the construction close-out that reclassifies a suite. It cannot open, cancel or fund a project, so the party that certifies shielding is never the party that budgeted it.

`TenantPortalUser` is a tenant practice acting on its own behalf.

For the five operator roles — `PortfolioAdmin`, `LeasingAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PropertyManager` and `FacilitiesEngineer` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule requires it (as `fmv_submitted_by_user_id` on a lease at review submission, as `opened_by_user_id` on a compliance case, as `written_off_by_user_id` on a written-off invoice) but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `TenantPortalUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tenant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a tenant compare the resource's tenant reference to `X-User-ID`, resolving through the lease where the addressed resource is a lease, a tenant-improvement project, a line item, an appraisal, an invoice, a booking or a parking allocation. A `TenantPortalUser` reaching a resource that resolves to a different tenant is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than told the resource does not exist, so the portal cannot be used to enumerate other practices' deals.

Two duty separations are enforced by comparing raw `X-User-ID` values rather than roles, so they bind even when one human holds two role headers. The `ComplianceOfficer` who certifies a lease's fair market value must not be the user recorded as having submitted that lease for review, and the `ComplianceOfficer` who writes off delinquent rent must not be the user recorded as having opened that tenant's rent-delinquency case. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Paged responses populate four fields inside `meta`: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before paging, and `has_more` indicating whether rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters combine conjunctively — a request naming both a `status` and a `lease_id` returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`. It is a UUID and it carries no type prefix — a lease identifier and a building identifier are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must not attempt to infer a resource's type from its identifier.

Most resources also carry three further common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `updated_at` follows the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations applied by a cascade rather than by a direct request against that row. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation, again including cascaded mutations. A read never advances `version`.

`TILineItem` is the single exception. A construction line item is immutable once appended and is never deleted, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Optimistic concurrency for line items is therefore expressed against the owning project's `version`, never against the line item's own.

Timestamps and dates are distinguished deliberately. `created_at`, `updated_at`, `window_start` and `window_end` are full ISO 8601 UTC instants. `term_start`, `term_end`, `appraisal_date`, `expires_on`, `due_date`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `paid_on`, `terminated_on`, `revoked_on`, `last_vacated_on`, `opened_on`, `remediation_due_on` and `closed_on` are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, interpreted in UTC. `opens_at` and `closes_at` are UTC times of day in `HH:MM` form. Every date comparison in this specification is a comparison of calendar dates, so the time of day the logical clock happens to hold never changes a day count.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Daily Close

The service does not read wall-clock time for any business decision. It keeps a logical clock whose value, before any client has set it, is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the implementation must never fall back to real time. A `PortfolioAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The clock may only move forward: a value earlier than the clock's current reading is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Advancing the clock changes the reference instant for lease commencement, appraisal expiry, invoice aging, the rolling risk window, remediation deadlines and shared-space windows — but it changes no entity's status by itself.

Status changes that depend only on the passage of time are produced exclusively by the daily close, a separate `PortfolioAdmin` capability that accepts an optional `close_note` and sweeps the whole estate against the clock's current reading. The close performs six kinds of work, and the order across those kinds is fixed and must not be rearranged: first qualified appraisals that have aged past their expiry, then issued invoices that have aged past grace together with the full delinquency cascade they trigger, then active leases that have run past their term end and roll into holdover, then remediation-planned compliance cases whose deadline has passed, then booked shared-space slots whose window has ended, and finally a sweep that recomputes both risk-score variants and the watch status for every tenant. Within each kind, rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. The final whole-estate tenant sweep is what allows the rolling twelve-month score to fall as old events age out of the window even on a close where no new delinquency occurred.

### 2.6. Money, Share Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities are decimal values carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding is half-up: a value whose third decimal digit is 5 or greater rounds the second decimal digit away from zero. Money supplied on a request may not be negative and may not carry more than 2 decimal places; a violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Share and concentration percentages are decimals carried to 4 decimal places, also rounded half up: `patient_min_reserve_pct`, `landlord_share_pct`, `landlord_share_pct_override`, `effective_share_pct`, `anchor_share_pct` and `parking_waiver_tier_pct` all follow that convention, and the three share inputs must lie between 0.0000 and 1.0000 inclusive. `fmv_variance_pct` is likewise carried to 4 decimal places half-up but is signed, because a rent negotiated below the published rate card produces a negative variance.

Two rounding disciplines are load-bearing and are stated here because getting them wrong changes published totals by cents. First, tenant-improvement cost shares are rounded **per line item, before any summation**; summing unrounded products and rounding the total once is wrong. Second, the fair-market-value band takes its minimum and maximum across already-rounded sub-bands, never across unrounded intermediates.

Risk scores and stall counts are integers and involve no rounding at all. Square footage is an integer. Day counts are whole days taken between calendar dates. Term length is a whole number of months truncated toward zero. The patient parking reserve minimum is the ceiling of a fractional product, so a fractional reserve always rounds up in the patients' favour.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Four write operations attach to, or amend, a parent whose state the caller must have observed. Each one takes a non-optional version field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent, and each rejects a mismatch outright rather than attaching to a parent that has moved.

Filing an appraisal against a lease — `POST /leases/{id}/appraisals` — requires `expected_lease_version` to equal that lease's current `version`. An opinion of value is only meaningful against the rent and term the appraiser saw, so if the lease has been amended, attested, certified or touched by a cascade since the client read it, the file is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and the client must re-read before retrying.

Amending a lease — `PATCH /leases/{id}` — likewise requires `expected_lease_version` to equal the lease's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Lease amendment is a read-modify-write over rent and term, and this rejection is what prevents an editor holding an older read from overwriting an amendment it never observed.

Allocating parking stalls — `POST /parking-allocations` — requires `expected_building_version` to equal the referenced building's current `version`. Stalls are drawn from a pool shared by every lease in the building, and that pool moves on every allocation, revocation, lease execution and lease termination; a create carrying an older value is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUILDING_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Appending a construction line item — `POST /ti-projects/{id}/line-items` — requires `expected_ti_project_version` to equal the addressed project's current `version`. A line item consumes a fixed allowance and is cost-shared against the project's default landlord share, so a caller who read the project before another line item landed, or before the default share was amended, holds a stale value and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TI_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these four must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Request Rejection Precedence

A single request can breach several rules at once. When it does, the response is determined by the **first** breach in the ladder below and no later check is evaluated. The ordering is not incidental: a referral-compliance landlord must report a regulatory defect even when the same request also busts a budget, which is why the safety, compliance and use-restriction rules sit above the fiscal ones.

Path parameters are resolved left to right before the body is examined. For a nested collection such as a building's suites or a lease's appraisals, the parent named in the path is looked up first, so a request against a suite collection under a building that does not exist reports the missing building and never reports anything about the suite payload. Where a request names both a building and a suite, the suite must also belong to that building.

The ladder, in strict ascending priority order:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The body and query string parse; every required input field is present and non-null; every enum-typed value is a declared member of its enumeration; every money field is a non-negative decimal with at most 2 decimal places; every date is a valid ISO-8601 value; `period_end` is on or after `period_start`; `term_end` is strictly later than `term_start`; `window_end` is strictly later than `window_start`; `opinion_high_psf` is at least `opinion_low_psf`; `stall_count` is at least 1; `occupant_count` is at least 1; `landlord_share_pct` and `landlord_share_pct_override` both lie between 0.0000 and 1.0000 inclusive; a clock value is not earlier than the current logical clock; and a `payment_amount` is exactly the invoice's outstanding balance. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. A `building_id` appearing in the path or the body identifies an existing building — otherwise `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_FOUND` (404). This guards `GET /buildings/{id}`, `GET /buildings/{id}/suites`, `GET /buildings/{id}/shared-spaces`, `POST /buildings/{id}/suites`, `POST /buildings/{id}/shared-spaces`, `POST /buildings/{id}/mix-freeze`, `POST /leases` and `POST /parking-allocations`.
5. A `suite_id` appearing in the path or the body identifies an existing suite, and where a building is named in the same request the suite's `building_id` matches it — otherwise `ERR_SUITE_NOT_FOUND` (404). This guards `GET /suites/{id}`, `POST /leases` and `POST /leases/{id}/ti-projects`.
6. A `tenant_id` appearing in the path or the body identifies an existing tenant — otherwise `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND` (404). This guards `GET /tenants/{id}`, `GET /tenants/{id}/compliance-cases`, `POST /leases` and `POST /compliance-cases`.
7. A lease reference in the path or the body — including a `master_lease_id` on a sublease and a `lease_id` on a booking or a parking allocation — identifies an existing lease, otherwise `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). This guards `GET /leases/{id}`, `GET /leases/{id}/appraisals`, `GET /leases/{id}/rent-invoices`, `PATCH /leases/{id}`, `DELETE /leases/{id}`, `POST /leases`, `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review`, `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-decisions`, `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`, `POST /leases/{id}/execute`, `POST /leases/{id}/commence`, `POST /leases/{id}/terminate`, `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-attestations`, `POST /leases/{id}/after-hours-authorization`, `POST /leases/{id}/appraisals`, `POST /leases/{id}/ti-projects`, `POST /leases/{id}/rent-invoices`, `POST /shared-space-bookings` and `POST /parking-allocations`.
8. Any other addressed resource — an appraisal, a tenant-improvement project, a line item, a rent invoice, a compliance case, a shared space, a shared-space booking or a parking allocation — identifies an existing row of that type, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). This guards `POST /appraisals/{id}/decision`, `GET /ti-projects/{id}`, `GET /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/transitions`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/reviews`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/complete`, `POST /rent-invoices/{id}/payments`, `POST /rent-invoices/{id}/write-off`, `POST /compliance-cases/{id}/transitions`, `POST /shared-space-bookings`, `GET /shared-space-bookings/{id}`, `POST /shared-space-bookings/{id}/cancel`, `GET /parking-allocations/{id}` and `POST /parking-allocations/{id}/revoke`.
9. When the acting role is `TenantPortalUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the tenant that owns the addressed resource, resolved through the lease where the resource is a lease, a project, a line item, an appraisal, an invoice, a booking or an allocation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This guards `GET /leases/{id}`, `GET /leases`, `GET /leases/{id}/appraisals`, `GET /leases/{id}/rent-invoices`, `GET /tenants/{id}`, `GET /tenants/{id}/compliance-cases`, `GET /ti-projects/{id}`, `GET /ti-projects`, `GET /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`, `GET /shared-space-bookings`, `GET /shared-space-bookings/{id}`, `GET /parking-allocations`, `GET /parking-allocations/{id}`, `POST /shared-space-bookings` and `POST /shared-space-bookings/{id}/cancel`.
10. `expected_lease_version` equals the addressed lease's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (412). This guards `PATCH /leases/{id}` and `POST /leases/{id}/appraisals`.
11. `expected_building_version` equals the referenced building's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_BUILDING_VERSION` (412). This guards `POST /parking-allocations`.
12. `expected_ti_project_version` equals the addressed project's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TI_PROJECT_VERSION` (412). This guards `POST /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`.
13. The addressed lease occupies a status from which the requested transition is declared: `DRAFT` for review submission, amendment and withdrawal; `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW` for certification and for a return-for-revision decision; `FMV_CERTIFIED` for amendment and execution; `EXECUTED` for commencement; `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` for termination; `SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW` for a reinstatement decision; and any status other than `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` for filing attestations, appraisals, projects, invoices, bookings and allocations. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE` (409). This guards `PATCH /leases/{id}`, `DELETE /leases/{id}`, `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review`, `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-decisions`, `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`, `POST /leases/{id}/execute`, `POST /leases/{id}/commence`, `POST /leases/{id}/terminate`, `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-attestations`, `POST /leases/{id}/after-hours-authorization`, `POST /leases/{id}/appraisals`, `POST /leases/{id}/ti-projects`, `POST /leases/{id}/rent-invoices`, `POST /shared-space-bookings` and `POST /parking-allocations`.
14. The addressed tenant-improvement project occupies a status from which the requested transition is declared — `DESIGN` for a review submission and for appending a line item, `IN_REVIEW` for recording a review, `PERMITTED` for starting construction, `CONSTRUCTION` for close-out, and `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `PERMITTED` for cancellation — and the review dimension being addressed is `PENDING`. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_TI_STATE` (409). This guards `POST /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/transitions`, `POST /ti-projects/{id}/reviews` and `POST /ti-projects/{id}/complete`.
15. The addressed appraisal, rent invoice, compliance case or shared-space booking occupies a status from which the requested transition is declared — `SUBMITTED` to qualify; `SUBMITTED` or `QUALIFIED` to disqualify; `ISSUED` or `DELINQUENT` to accept a payment; `DELINQUENT` to write off; `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` to cure; `OPEN` to record a remediation plan or to report; `BOOKED` to cancel. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE` (409). This guards `POST /appraisals/{id}/decision`, `POST /rent-invoices/{id}/payments`, `POST /rent-invoices/{id}/write-off`, `POST /compliance-cases/{id}/transitions` and `POST /shared-space-bookings/{id}/cancel`.
16. The governing building's `tenant_mix_posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_FROZEN` (409). A freeze halts every leasing-lifecycle write in that building regardless of the request's own merits, and it guards `POST /leases`, `PATCH /leases/{id}`, `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review`, `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`, `POST /leases/{id}/execute`, `POST /leases/{id}/commence` and `POST /buildings/{id}/suites`.
17. The addressed lease's `revision_round` is at most 1, so that a return-for-revision or a material amendment would not push it past the cap of 2 — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). This guards `PATCH /leases/{id}` and `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-decisions`.
18. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user recorded as having taken the paired step — the lease's `fmv_submitted_by_user_id` on a certification, and the `opened_by_user_id` of any `OPEN` rent-delinquency case on the tenant on a write-off. A collision is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv` and `POST /rent-invoices/{id}/write-off`.
19. A lease commencing into an `IMAGING` suite requires that suite's `shielding_certified` to be true, and a utility review that requires piped medical gas requires the suite's `medical_gas_available` to be true. Unshielded occupancy of an imaging suite is a radiation-safety defect and outranks every commercial consideration, so a violation is `ERR_SHIELDING_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` (422). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/commence` and `POST /ti-projects/{id}/reviews`.
20. At utility review, the suite's `power_amps_available` is at least the project's `power_amps_requested`, and the suite provides medical gas whenever `medical_gas_requested` is true — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UTILITY_CAPACITY` (422). This guards `POST /ti-projects/{id}/reviews`.
21. The tenant's `compliance_watch_status` is `CLEAR`; a tenant on referral-compliance watch may not receive new after-hours access or new shared-space bookings. A violation is `ERR_TENANT_ON_COMPLIANCE_WATCH` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/after-hours-authorization` and `POST /shared-space-bookings`.
22. The addressed lease's `rent_concession_locked` is false; a locked lease may not have its `base_rent_psf_annual` reduced by an amendment. A violation is `ERR_RENT_CONCESSION_LOCKED` (422), guarding `PATCH /leases/{id}`.
23. No other lease in the building whose status is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` holds an `exclusive_use_specialty` equal to this lease's tenant's `primary_specialty` — otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVE_USE_CONFLICT` (409). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/execute` and `POST /leases/{id}/commence`.
24. No other lease in the building whose status is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` shares this lease's tenant's `primary_specialty` while carrying a non-null `radius_restriction_miles` — otherwise `ERR_RADIUS_RESTRICTION_CONFLICT` (409). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/execute` and `POST /leases/{id}/commence`.
25. Where the tenant's `affiliation` is `HEALTH_SYSTEM_ANCHOR` or `HEALTH_SYSTEM_AFFILIATED`, the building's `tenant_mix_posture` is not `RESTRICTED`; and for the same reason a reinstatement decision on a `SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW` lease is refused while the posture is still `RESTRICTED`. A violation is `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_RESTRICTED` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/execute` and `POST /leases/{id}/compliance-decisions`.
26. For a `SUBLEASE`, `master_lease_id` is non-null, the lease it names has `lease_type` `MASTER`, and at execution time that master lease's status is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MASTER_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE` (422). This guards `POST /leases`, `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review` and `POST /leases/{id}/execute`.
27. For a `SUBLEASE` being executed, `master_lease_consent_on_file` is true — otherwise `ERR_MASTER_LEASE_CONSENT_MISSING` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/execute`.
28. For any lease being executed, `signed_lease_on_file` is true — otherwise `ERR_SIGNED_LEASE_NOT_ON_FILE` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/execute`.
29. A booking of a `WAITING_ROOM` or `PROCEDURE_ROOM` shared space requires the lease's `space_sharing_schedule_on_file` to be true — otherwise `ERR_SPACE_SHARING_SCHEDULE_MISSING` (422), guarding `POST /shared-space-bookings`.
30. The lease's derived `term_months` is at least 12 — otherwise `ERR_MIN_TERM_NOT_MET` (422). This guards `POST /leases`, `PATCH /leases/{id}` and `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review`.
31. For a `SUBLEASE`, `term_start` is on or after the master lease's `term_start` and `term_end` is on or before the master lease's `term_end` — otherwise `ERR_SUBLEASE_TERM_EXCEEDS_MASTER` (422). This guards `POST /leases`, `PATCH /leases/{id}` and `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review`.
32. No incumbent lease on the same suite conflicts once the class-dependent turnover buffer is applied to the closed term intervals — otherwise `ERR_SUITE_TERM_CONFLICT` (409), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/execute`.
33. The clock date is on or after `term_start` for a commencement, and a booking cancellation is attempted strictly before `window_start` — otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_YET_STARTED` (422). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/commence` and `POST /shared-space-bookings/{id}/cancel`.
34. A booking whose window falls wholly or partly outside the space's operating window requires the lease's `after_hours_authorized` to be true — otherwise `ERR_AFTER_HOURS_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (422), guarding `POST /shared-space-bookings`.
35. The requested invoice period does not overlap an existing invoice period on the same lease that is not written off, and lies within the lease term unless the lease is in `HOLDOVER` — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/rent-invoices`.
36. The referenced suite's `occupancy_status` is `VACANT` at execution and no other lease holds it with status `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` — otherwise `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/execute`.
37. The aggregate seat load over every swept instant of the requested window does not exceed the shared space's `capacity_seats` — otherwise `ERR_SHARED_SPACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding `POST /shared-space-bookings`.
38. A lease being commenced has no tenant-improvement project left in `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `PERMITTED`, `CONSTRUCTION` or `ON_HOLD`, and a project entering construction belongs to a lease in status `EXECUTED` or `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_TI_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE` (409). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/commence` and `POST /ti-projects/{id}/transitions`.
39. A lease being terminated for a `termination_reason` other than `DEFAULT` has no invoice still in status `ISSUED` or `DELINQUENT` — otherwise `ERR_OUTSTANDING_RENT_BALANCE` (409), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/terminate`.
40. For a physician-owned tenant, at least one appraisal on the lease is `QUALIFIED` with an `expires_on` on or after the clock date, so that a controlling opinion exists — otherwise `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_APPRAISAL` (422). This guards `POST /leases/{id}/submit-fmv-review` and `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`.
41. An appraisal being qualified has `independence_attested` true — otherwise `ERR_APPRAISAL_INDEPENDENCE_MISSING` (422), guarding `POST /appraisals/{id}/decision`.
42. At certification, `base_rent_psf_annual` does not exceed the band's `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf` — otherwise `ERR_RENT_ABOVE_FMV_CEILING` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`.
43. At certification, `base_rent_psf_annual` is not below the band's `certified_fmv_floor_psf` — otherwise `ERR_RENT_BELOW_FMV_FLOOR` (422), guarding `POST /leases/{id}/certify-fmv`.
44. The landlord share of a new line item does not drive the project's `allowance_remaining` below zero — otherwise `ERR_TI_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding `POST /ti-projects/{id}/line-items`.
45. The requested `stall_count` does not exceed the building's `unallocated_stalls` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNALLOCATED_STALLS` (422), guarding `POST /parking-allocations`.
46. A staff allocation leaves at least the patient reserve minimum of stalls outside staff use — otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_PARKING_RESERVE_BREACH` (422), guarding `POST /parking-allocations`.

Because rule 45 precedes rule 46, a stall request that both exceeds the pool and breaches the patient reserve reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNALLOCATED_STALLS`. Because rule 42 precedes rule 43, a certification cannot report both band failures at once. And because rule 21 sits above rules 29, 34 and 37, a booking request from a tenant on watch reports the watch rather than a missing schedule, an after-hours defect or a capacity breach.

### 2.9. Tenant-Mix Posture as a Building-Wide Gate

Anchor concentration is the single most consequential derived quantity in the system, because a building where a health system holds nearly all the leased area is a building where the landlord can no longer argue that independent practices are being dealt with at arm's length. The building therefore carries a posture that is recomputed from its lease book every time that book changes, and that posture is a hard control rather than a report.

The counted lease set for a building comprises the leases whose `building_id` is that building, whose status is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, and whose `lease_type` is `DIRECT` or `MASTER`. Leases of type `SUBLEASE` are deliberately excluded: the master lease already counts that area, and counting both would double-count the same square feet. Leases in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW`, `SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW`, `FMV_CERTIFIED`, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all excluded as well — a certified lease holds no area until it is executed.

From that set, `anchor_rentable_sf` is the sum of the `rentable_sf` of the demised suites of the counted leases whose tenant's `affiliation` is `HEALTH_SYSTEM_ANCHOR` or `HEALTH_SYSTEM_AFFILIATED`; both affiliations count toward the anchor. `independent_rentable_sf` is the same sum restricted to counted leases whose tenant's `affiliation` is `INDEPENDENT_PRACTICE`. `leased_rentable_sf` is the sum of those two figures, and `vacant_rentable_sf` is the building's `total_rentable_sf` less `leased_rentable_sf` — the two are conserved partners and must always add back to `total_rentable_sf`. `anchor_share_pct` is `anchor_rentable_sf` divided by `leased_rentable_sf`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; when `leased_rentable_sf` is zero the share reads 0.0000 rather than being undefined. Note that the share is a share of **leased** area, not of total area, so a mostly empty building with one anchor lease reads a very high share.

`tenant_mix_posture` is then resolved in a strict order. If `mix_freeze_override` is true the posture is `FROZEN` and nothing else is consulted — a frozen building never reads `RESTRICTED` even when its anchor share is 1.0000. Otherwise, if `anchor_share_pct` is strictly greater than 0.7500 the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise, if it is strictly greater than 0.6000 the posture is `ANCHOR_CONCENTRATED`; otherwise it is `BALANCED`. Both thresholds use strict comparison, so a share of exactly 0.7500 reads `ANCHOR_CONCENTRATED` and a share of exactly 0.6000 reads `BALANCED`.

The posture then gates behaviour system-wide. `FROZEN` blocks lease creation, lease amendment, review submission, certification, execution, commencement and suite creation in that building, reported as `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_FROZEN`. `RESTRICTED` blocks execution of a lease belonging to a health-system-affiliated tenant and blocks reinstatement of a suspended lease, reported as `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_RESTRICTED`. `ANCHOR_CONCENTRATED` and `BALANCED` impose no gate; they are the informational rungs of the same ladder.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

The system is immediately consistent. Every derived field an operation affects is recomputed and persisted within that same request; there is no deferred reconciliation, no eventual settlement and no background worker other than the explicit daily close. A caller that reads a building immediately after executing a lease in it sees the new tenant-mix figures.

Whenever an operation mutates a row other than the one it addresses, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1 exactly as if it had been written directly. Executing a lease therefore advances the lease's `version` and the building's `version`; approving a utility review advances the project's `version` and the suite's `version`; the delinquency sweep advances the invoice's `version` and the tenant's `version`. Where a cascade touches a set of rows, the set is always processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the outcome of a multi-row cascade is deterministic.

Four conserved relationships must hold after every operation, and they are the fastest way to detect a cascade that has been implemented only halfway. A building's `leased_rentable_sf` plus its `vacant_rentable_sf` equals `total_rentable_sf`. A building's `staff_allocated_stalls` plus `patient_allocated_stalls` plus `unallocated_stalls` equals `total_parking_stalls`, and that holds after every allocation, every revocation and every lease-termination cascade. A project's `landlord_committed_amount` plus its `allowance_remaining` equals its `authorized_allowance_total`. And on every line item, `landlord_share_amount` plus `tenant_share_amount` equals `cost_amount` exactly, which is guaranteed because the tenant's share is derived by subtraction from the line cost rather than by a second independent rounding.

A small number of fields are live rather than stored: `days_past_due` on an invoice is recomputed against the clock on every read as the whole number of calendar days from its `due_date` to the clock date, floored at zero so that an invoice whose due date has not yet arrived reads 0 rather than a negative figure, and it therefore grows even on days when no close has run. Its frozen counterpart, `days_past_due_at_flag`, is written once from the unclamped day count observed at the flagging close and never revised. Reading a resource never mutates it, never advances its `version` and never opens a compliance case; only the operations described in §3 and the daily close do that.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Portfolio Reference Data: Buildings, Suites, Shared Spaces and the Mix Freeze

A `PortfolioAdmin` opens the portfolio by registering a building. The registration carries the building's `name`, its `total_rentable_sf` (at least 1 square foot), the published rate card as `market_base_rent_psf_annual` (strictly positive, 2 decimal places), the budgeted common-area recovery as `operating_expense_annual_psf` (zero or more, 2 decimal places), and the size of the parking pool as `total_parking_stalls` (zero or more). A sixth input, `patient_min_reserve_pct`, is optional and expresses the fraction of the pool that must remain available to patients; when omitted it defaults to 0.60, and it is a 4-decimal fraction between 0.0000 and 1.0000. A freshly registered building has no lease book and no allocations, so all its derived figures start from the empty set: `anchor_rentable_sf`, `independent_rentable_sf` and `leased_rentable_sf` are 0, `vacant_rentable_sf` equals `total_rentable_sf`, `anchor_share_pct` reads 0.0000, `tenant_mix_posture` reads `BALANCED`, `mix_freeze_override` is false, `staff_allocated_stalls` and `patient_allocated_stalls` are 0, and `unallocated_stalls` equals `total_parking_stalls`.

Suites are added to a building one at a time, and the `building_id` of a new suite is taken from the building addressed in the path rather than from the payload. The payload supplies `suite_number`, which must be unique within that building; `floor`, at least 1; `rentable_sf`, at least 1; and `suite_class`, which is one of `STANDARD_EXAM`, `IMAGING`, `PROCEDURE` or `INFUSION`. It also declares the unreserved electrical service at the suite panel as `power_amps_available`, and may optionally declare `medical_gas_available` (whether piped medical gas and vacuum are present, defaulting to false), `shielding_certified` (defaulting to false) and `lead_thickness_mm` (the verified lead-equivalent thickness, null until a shielding review certifies one). Every new suite begins `VACANT` with `last_vacated_on` null, and adding a suite advances the owning building's `version` by 1. A building's suite roster can be listed and narrowed by `suite_class` and by `occupancy_status`.

Suite occupancy is never set directly by any client-facing operation. It is derived entirely from the lease transitions that reference the suite: `VACANT → RESERVED` when a lease over that suite is executed (admissible only when no other lease holds the suite in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`), `RESERVED → OCCUPIED` when that lease commences and reaches `ACTIVE`, `OCCUPIED → VACANT` when an active or holdover lease terminates, and `RESERVED → VACANT` when an executed lease is terminated before it ever commenced. Both routes back to `VACANT` stamp `last_vacated_on` with the clock date and advance the suite's `version`.

Shared clinical space is registered the same way. A shared space belongs to the building named in the path, carries a `name` and a `space_type` of `WAITING_ROOM`, `PROCEDURE_ROOM` or `INFUSION_BAY`, holds a `capacity_seats` figure of at least 1 counting patients plus escorts, and declares a daily operating window from `opens_at` inclusive to `closes_at` exclusive as UTC times of day, where `closes_at` must be later than `opens_at`. A building's shared spaces can be listed and narrowed by `space_type`.

One further control belongs to reference data rather than to any lease. A `ComplianceOfficer` may freeze or unfreeze a building's tenant mix by supplying a boolean `frozen` together with a `reason`. The boolean is written to `mix_freeze_override`, and the building's posture is then recomputed — which means that clearing a freeze restores the computed posture derived from the current lease book rather than resetting it to `BALANCED`. The freeze advances the building's `version` by 1. While a building reads `FROZEN`, every leasing-lifecycle write in it is refused with `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_FROZEN`, including the creation of further suites, and that refusal takes precedence over the merits of the request itself.

### 3.2. Tenant Practices and Referral-Compliance Risk

A tenant record is registered by a `PortfolioAdmin` and describes the practice, not the deal. It carries the practice's `legal_name`; an `affiliation` of `HEALTH_SYSTEM_ANCHOR`, `HEALTH_SYSTEM_AFFILIATED` or `INDEPENDENT_PRACTICE`, which is the tenant-mix classification and the input to anchor concentration; a `primary_specialty` drawn from `PRIMARY_CARE`, `CARDIOLOGY`, `ORTHOPEDICS`, `RADIOLOGY`, `ONCOLOGY`, `OPHTHALMOLOGY` and `GASTROENTEROLOGY`, which is what exclusive-use and radius conflict detection compares; a boolean `is_physician_owned`, true when physicians hold an ownership interest, which is the flag that decides whether third-party appraisal support is required at all; and an `npi`, the National Provider Identifier, which must be exactly 10 digits. A new tenant starts with `delinquency_event_count` at 0, both risk scores at 0 and `compliance_watch_status` reading `CLEAR`.

`delinquency_event_count` is a running tally: it counts the invoices across every one of that tenant's leases whose status is `DELINQUENT` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, and it increases by 1 each time an invoice is flagged delinquent by the daily close.

Timely rent payment is a referral-compliance matter, not merely a receivables matter, and the system quantifies it as a remuneration-risk score. Only invoices that have actually carried a delinquency weight participate: an invoice whose `delinquency_tier_weight` is greater than zero is an event, and an invoice paid on time contributes nothing. Because the weight is retained when arrears are cured, an invoice that went delinquent and was later paid in full still contributes — the historical remuneration event happened and paying up does not erase it. A written-off invoice contributes the write-off weight instead of its aging tier, never both.

Two variants of the score are maintained side by side, and they are genuinely different numbers. `referral_compliance_risk_score` is the **rolling twelve-month** operational figure: it considers only those events whose `due_date` falls on or after the clock date minus 365 days, sums their weights, and then caps the result at 100. `strict_cumulative_risk_score` is the **audit** figure: it sums the weights of every qualifying event of that tenant with no date window at all and applies no cap, which is the number an external reviewer reconstructs from the ledger. The two diverge for two independent reasons. An event whose `due_date` has aged past a year leaves the rolling figure entirely while remaining in the strict figure forever, so merely advancing the clock and running a close separates the two without any new data arriving. And the rolling figure saturates while the strict one keeps climbing, so a practice with four maximum-tier events reads 100 rolling and 200 strict. Both figures are integers and no rounding is involved anywhere in their derivation, which is why no rounding argument can reconcile them.

Derivation order matters. The tenant's leases are resolved first, then their invoices; the invoices are filtered to those carrying a weight above zero; for the rolling variant only, they are then filtered by the inclusive lower bound on `due_date` (never on `paid_on`); the weights are summed; the rolling sum alone is capped at 100; and finally the **rolling** figure — never the strict one — is compared to the watch threshold.

`compliance_watch_status` reads `WATCH` when the rolling `referral_compliance_risk_score` is at least 45, or when the tenant has any compliance case in status `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` or `REPORTED`; otherwise it reads `CLEAR`. The strict figure never raises the flag on its own, so a practice can legitimately read a strict score of 50 while still `CLEAR`. Being on watch has teeth: while a tenant reads `WATCH`, new after-hours access and new shared-space bookings are refused with `ERR_TENANT_ON_COMPLIANCE_WATCH`.

A tenant's compliance-case history is readable in its own right, filterable by `status` and by `case_type`, and it is the only discovery channel for cases the system opened by itself.

### 3.3. Drafting, Amending and Withdrawing a Lease

A `LeasingAgent` opens every deal as a draft. The drafting payload names the three parties to the space — the `building_id` of the building, the `suite_id` of the demised suite, which must belong to that building, and the `tenant_id` of the practice taking the space — and declares the deal's shape through `lease_type`, which is `DIRECT` for a landlord-to-practice lease, `MASTER` for a landlord-to-health-system lease that may be sublet, or `SUBLEASE` for a health-system-to-practice lease sitting under a master. A sublease must name the master it sits under through `master_lease_id`, and that reference must point at a lease whose own `lease_type` is `MASTER`; for a direct or master lease `master_lease_id` must be null. The commercial terms are the inclusive occupancy dates `term_start` and `term_end` and the negotiated `base_rent_psf_annual`, a strictly positive per-square-foot annual figure with 2 decimal places — the number that fair-market-value certification will later test against the band. Two use-restriction inputs are optional: `exclusive_use_specialty` names the specialty this lease claims exclusivity over inside the building and is null when no exclusive is granted, and `radius_restriction_miles` is a non-negative 2-decimal figure that defaults to 0, where 0 disables the restriction.

A new lease is created in status `DRAFT` with `fmv_certification_status` reading `NOT_STARTED`, `revision_round` at 0 and `version` at 1. The six document-and-guardrail booleans — `signed_lease_on_file`, `master_lease_consent_on_file`, `space_sharing_schedule_on_file`, `after_hours_authorized`, `rent_concession_locked` and `holdover_premium_applied` — all start false. `termination_reason` and `terminated_on` are null until termination records them.

Term length is derived at draft time as `term_months`, and the derivation is subtle because `term_end` is the **last day of occupancy** rather than the day after it. The count is therefore taken against the exclusive end, which is the day following `term_end`. Take the difference in years between that exclusive end and `term_start` and multiply by 12, add the difference in their month numbers, and then, if the exclusive end's day of the month is earlier than `term_start`'s day of the month, subtract one whole month because the anniversary day has not been reached. The result is truncated to whole months. A term running from 1 September 2026 through 31 August 2027 therefore yields exactly 12 months and is admissible, while the same term ending one day earlier, on 30 August 2027, yields 11 and is refused with `ERR_MIN_TERM_NOT_MET`; a term of 31 January 2026 through 28 February 2026 yields 1. Using `term_end` directly instead of the exclusive end is the classic off-by-one here: it makes a clean twelve-month term read 11. The minimum term of 12 months is not an arbitrary policy — a one-year minimum is what the space-lease exception requires of a physician lease — and it is re-checked on every amendment and again at review submission.

Amendment of a lease is a read-modify-write and therefore carries `expected_lease_version` alongside any subset of `base_rent_psf_annual`, `term_start`, `term_end`, `exclusive_use_specialty` and `radius_restriction_miles`. Changing any of the first four is a **material** amendment. Changing only `radius_restriction_miles` is non-material: it advances `version` and leaves both `status` and `fmv_certification_status` exactly as they were. When term or rent fields change, `term_months` and the provisional rent are re-derived. A material amendment applied to a lease sitting in `FMV_CERTIFIED` is the system's second cycle back to the drawing board: the lease returns `FMV_CERTIFIED → DRAFT`, `fmv_certification_status` becomes `STALE`, `certified_fmv_floor_psf`, `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf`, `certified_base_rent_annual`, `fmv_variance_annual` and `fmv_variance_pct` are all cleared to null, every appraisal on the lease has its `is_controlling` flag cleared to false, `revision_round` increases by 1, the provisional rent is recomputed, and `version` advances. Because the round counter is capped at 2, an amendment is admissible only while `revision_round` is at most 1; a third return is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Separately, a lease whose `rent_concession_locked` is true may not have its `base_rent_psf_annual` lowered at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RENT_CONCESSION_LOCKED`.

Withdrawal is available only while a lease is still `DRAFT`, and only when no tenant-improvement project on the lease survives in any status other than `CANCELLED`. Withdrawal takes the lease `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal — the row is retained rather than deleted — and it disqualifies the paper trail behind it: every appraisal on that lease still in `SUBMITTED` or `QUALIFIED` moves to `DISQUALIFIED` with `decision_reason` recorded as "lease withdrawn". The lease's `version` advances by 1.

Submitting a draft for fair-market-value review moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_FMV_REVIEW` and accepts an optional `submission_note`. Five conditions must hold. The building must not read `FROZEN`. `term_months` must be at least 12. A sublease must name a master whose `lease_type` is `MASTER`, and its term must sit inside the master's term — `term_start` on or after the master's `term_start` and `term_end` on or before the master's `term_end`, otherwise `ERR_SUBLEASE_TERM_EXCEEDS_MASTER`. And where the tenant's `is_physician_owned` is true, at least one appraisal on the lease must be `QUALIFIED` with an `expires_on` on or after the clock date, otherwise `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_APPRAISAL`; a tenant that is not physician-owned needs no appraisal at all. On success, `fmv_certification_status` becomes `PENDING`, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `fmv_submitted_by_user_id` — the comparand the certifier will be checked against — the provisional rent is recomputed and frozen at the rate card as it stands at that moment, and `version` advances by 1.

A `ComplianceOfficer` who is not ready to certify has a disposition surface separate from certification, driven by an `action` together with a mandatory `decision_note`. The `RETURN_FOR_REVISION` action sends a lease in review back to the agent, `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW → DRAFT`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1, resetting `fmv_certification_status` to `NOT_STARTED`, clearing `fmv_submitted_by_user_id` to null and advancing `version`. It is admissible only while `revision_round` is at most 1, so at most three review submissions can ever occur on one lease; a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The same surface carries the `REINSTATE` action described in §3.5.

Compliance documents are recorded against the lease rather than uploaded: a `LeasingAgent` files an attestation naming an `attestation_type`, a boolean `on_file` and a `document_reference` identifying the paper. An `attestation_type` of `SIGNED_LEASE` writes `on_file` to `signed_lease_on_file`, `MASTER_LEASE_CONSENT` writes it to `master_lease_consent_on_file`, and `SPACE_SHARING_SCHEDULE` writes it to `space_sharing_schedule_on_file`. The filing is a write onto the lease and nothing more: no attestation row of its own is persisted, so the `document_reference` is a required non-empty string on the request that is not retained on any resource, and the call answers HTTP 200 with the updated lease in `data` rather than 201 with a newly created resource. Each attestation advances the lease's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a client holding an older read of the lease cannot then file an appraisal against it. Attestations may be filed at any point in the lease's life except after it has reached `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN`.

Leases can be listed and narrowed by `building_id`, `suite_id`, `tenant_id`, `master_lease_id`, `status` and `lease_type`; the `master_lease_id` filter is how the subleases hanging under a hospital master lease are discovered.

### 3.4. Appraisal Support and Fair-Market-Value Certification

Third-party appraisal support exists for exactly one reason: when the tenant is physician-owned, the rent the landlord charges is rent charged to a referral source, and the landlord must be able to show the rent was set by someone with no interest in referral volume. A `ComplianceOfficer` files an appraisal against a specific lease, and because the opinion is only meaningful against the rent and term the appraiser saw, the filing carries `expected_lease_version`. The payload names the `appraiser_firm`, the `appraisal_date` on which the opinion of value was formed — which may not be later than the logical clock's current date — and the two ends of the per-square-foot annual rent opinion, `opinion_low_psf` and `opinion_high_psf`, both strictly positive with 2 decimal places, where the high end must be at least the low end. It also carries `independence_attested`, the appraiser's own attestation of independence from the tenant and from referral volume.

Filing computes two fields immediately. `opinion_midpoint_psf` is the mean of the two ends, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `expires_on` is `appraisal_date` plus 365 days, expressed as a UTC date — an opinion of value is stale after a year. The new row begins in `SUBMITTED` with `is_controlling` false, `decision_reason` null, and the owning lease's `version` advances by 1 because the lease now has new evidence attached to it. A lease's appraisals can be listed and narrowed by `status`.

Adjudication is a single disposition surface taking an `action` of `QUALIFY` or `DISQUALIFY` together with a `decision_reason`, and the reason is recorded on the row in both directions. Qualification moves an appraisal `SUBMITTED → QUALIFIED` and requires three things: `independence_attested` must be true, or the request is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_INDEPENDENCE_MISSING`, since a firm that has not attested independence cannot support an opinion of fair market value; `expires_on` must be on or after the clock date, so an opinion that has already aged out cannot be qualified retroactively; and the owning lease must not be `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN`.

Disqualification is admissible from either `SUBMITTED` or `QUALIFIED` and carries no preconditions of its own, but its blast radius depends entirely on what the row was doing at the time. Disqualifying a row that was **not** controlling — including any row still in `SUBMITTED` — has no lease-side effect at all: the row moves to `DISQUALIFIED`, records its `decision_reason`, and nothing else changes. Disqualifying a row that **was** controlling clears its `is_controlling` flag and then reaches into the lease, and here the two branches are deliberately different. If the lease is sitting in `FMV_CERTIFIED`, the certification is unwound: `fmv_certification_status` becomes `STALE`, the lease moves `FMV_CERTIFIED → DRAFT`, `revision_round` increases by 1, the four certified figures `certified_fmv_floor_psf`, `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf`, `certified_base_rent_annual` and both variance fields are cleared to null, `effective_base_rent_annual` is recomputed under the provisional published variant, and the lease's `version` advances. If instead the lease has already reached `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, the lease status is **not** rolled back — the space is already committed or occupied — but `fmv_certification_status` becomes `STALE`, the lease's `version` advances, no rent re-carry runs on this branch — the certified band figures, `certified_base_rent_annual`, `effective_base_rent_annual` and both variance fields are all left exactly as certification recorded them, until some later operation re-carries the lease — and the system opens a compliance case against the tenant on its own initiative: an `FMV_DEVIATION` case in status `OPEN` at `HIGH` severity, measured under the rolling twelve-month variant, with `opened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal value `system`.

The controlling appraisal is not chosen by the operator; it is derived. Among the appraisals of a lease, the candidates are those in status `QUALIFIED` whose `expires_on` is on or after the clock date. Rows in `DISQUALIFIED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUBMITTED` are excluded. The candidates are ordered by `appraisal_date` descending, ties broken by `created_at` descending, and the first is the controlling opinion. If the tenant is physician-owned and no candidate exists, there is no band to compute and the caller receives `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_APPRAISAL` rather than a fabricated band.

The admissible rent band itself is a nested clamp of two independent bands, so that neither an unusually generous appraisal nor an unusually stingy one can push rent outside the landlord's own published rate card. Start from the published figure: take the building's `market_base_rent_psf_annual` and add the class premium of the demised suite, which is 0.00 for a `STANDARD_EXAM` suite, 6.50 for an `IMAGING` suite, 4.25 for a `PROCEDURE` suite and 3.00 for an `INFUSION` suite. The published floor is 80% of that figure and the published ceiling is 115% of it, each rounded half up to 2 decimal places. For a tenant whose `is_physician_owned` is false, that published pair **is** the band: `certified_fmv_floor_psf` is the published floor and `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf` is the published ceiling, and no appraisal is consulted. For a physician-owned tenant, the controlling appraisal supplies a second band — 90% of `opinion_midpoint_psf` as its floor and 110% of that midpoint as its ceiling, each rounded half up to 2 decimal places — and the two bands are then intersected: `certified_fmv_floor_psf` is the **greater** of the appraisal floor and the published floor, and `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf` is the **lesser** of the appraisal ceiling and the published ceiling. The tighter side wins on each end independently, so the two halves of the answer may well come from different sources. Both comparisons operate on the already-rounded sub-bands, never on unrounded intermediates. As an illustration: on a physician-owned imaging suite in a building published at 30.00 per square foot, the published figure is 36.50 and the published pair is 29.20 and 41.98; an appraisal of 38.00 to 42.00 gives a midpoint of 40.00 and an appraisal pair of 36.00 and 44.00; the clamp yields a floor of 36.00 from the appraisal and a ceiling of 41.98 from the rate card.

Rent itself is carried in two forms at once, and both are retained because the difference between them is exactly what a referral-compliance reviewer asks about. Before certification — that is, while `fmv_certification_status` reads `NOT_STARTED`, `PENDING` or `STALE` — the lease is carried at the rate card: `provisional_base_rent_annual` is the published per-square-foot figure (market rate card plus the suite class premium) multiplied by the suite's `rentable_sf`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; `effective_base_rent_annual`, the figure actually billed, equals it; and `certified_base_rent_annual`, `fmv_variance_annual` and `fmv_variance_pct` are all null. Once `fmv_certification_status` reads `CERTIFIED`, the lease is carried at the negotiated rate: `certified_base_rent_annual` is `base_rent_psf_annual` multiplied by the suite's `rentable_sf`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `effective_base_rent_annual` equals it — except while the lease is in `HOLDOVER`, where the effective figure is the certified figure multiplied by 1.50 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The reconciling delta is `fmv_variance_annual`, the certified annual figure less the provisional annual figure, and `fmv_variance_pct` is that delta divided by the provisional annual figure, rounded half up to 4 decimal places and signed, so a rent negotiated below the rate card produces a negative percentage. The division is taken between the two **rounded** annual figures, never between unrounded products.

The evaluation order between the two variants is pinned. `provisional_base_rent_annual` is computed and frozen when the lease enters review and is **not** recomputed at certification, so the variance is measured against the rate card as it stood at submission. Then the certified annual figure, then the holdover multiplier if applicable, then the delta, then the percentage. The two variants also diverge structurally, not just numerically: only the certified variant applies the holdover multiplier and only the certified variant produces a non-null variance, which means a lease that rolls into `HOLDOVER` while its certification is `STALE` is carried at the plain published figure with no premium at all.

Certification is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s act and accepts an optional `certification_note`. It moves the lease `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW → FMV_CERTIFIED` and demands five conditions: the building must not read `FROZEN`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the lease's `fmv_submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; a physician-owned tenant must have a qualifying appraisal, or `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_APPRAISAL`; the negotiated `base_rent_psf_annual` must not exceed `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf`, because rent above fair market value paid by a referral source is impermissible remuneration, or `ERR_RENT_ABOVE_FMV_CEILING`; and it must not fall below `certified_fmv_floor_psf`, because rent below fair market value granted to a referral source is equally impermissible, or `ERR_RENT_BELOW_FMV_FLOOR`. The ceiling is tested before the floor.

On success the lease records the band into `certified_fmv_floor_psf` and `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf`, sets `fmv_certification_status` to `CERTIFIED`, marks the selected controlling appraisal with `is_controlling` true while clearing that flag to false on every other appraisal of the same lease, recomputes `certified_base_rent_annual`, `effective_base_rent_annual`, `fmv_variance_annual` and `fmv_variance_pct` under the certified variant, and advances `version` by 1. One further consequence fires conditionally: when the **absolute value** of `fmv_variance_pct` is strictly greater than 0.1500, the system opens an `FMV_DEVIATION` compliance case against the tenant in status `OPEN`, measured under the rolling twelve-month variant, with `opened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal value `system`, and then re-evaluates that tenant's `compliance_watch_status`. A variance of 0.1501 opens a case where 0.1479 does not, and the gate can turn over inside a fraction of a dollar per square foot — well inside the admissible band — which is why the percentage arithmetic must be reproduced exactly.

A qualified appraisal does not stay qualified forever. When a daily close finds the clock date past a `QUALIFIED` appraisal's `expires_on`, the row moves `QUALIFIED → EXPIRED`, its `is_controlling` flag is cleared, and its `version` advances. If the expiring row was the controlling opinion behind a lease sitting in `FMV_CERTIFIED`, that lease's `fmv_certification_status` becomes `STALE` and its `version` advances, and because the status no longer reads `CERTIFIED` the same close re-carries that lease under the provisional variant: `effective_base_rent_annual` falls back to the already-frozen `provisional_base_rent_annual`, which is not itself re-derived, and `certified_base_rent_annual`, `fmv_variance_annual` and `fmv_variance_pct` are cleared to null, while the recorded band figures `certified_fmv_floor_psf` and `certified_fmv_ceiling_psf` are left standing. But its `status` is deliberately left at `FMV_CERTIFIED`, because an appraisal simply ageing out is not a compliance finding and opens no case.

### 3.5. Occupancy Lifecycle: Execution, Commencement and Termination

Execution is the gate where every commercial and use-restriction rule in the building converges, and it is the `LeasingAgent`'s act. It accepts an optional `execution_note` and moves a certified lease `FMV_CERTIFIED → EXECUTED`. Eight conditions must hold. The building must not read `FROZEN`. `signed_lease_on_file` must be true, because a written signed lease is itself a precondition of the space-lease exception and no lease may be executed without one; the refusal is `ERR_SIGNED_LEASE_NOT_ON_FILE`. A `SUBLEASE` must additionally have `master_lease_consent_on_file` true, refused as `ERR_MASTER_LEASE_CONSENT_MISSING`, and its master lease must be in status `ACTIVE` at that moment, refused as `ERR_MASTER_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE` — a sublease cannot be executed under a master that has not yet commenced or has already ended. Where the tenant's `affiliation` is `HEALTH_SYSTEM_ANCHOR` or `HEALTH_SYSTEM_AFFILIATED`, the building's `tenant_mix_posture` must not be `RESTRICTED`, refused as `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_RESTRICTED`, because further health-system concentration in an already-restricted building is exactly what the control exists to prevent. There must be no exclusive-use conflict, no radius-restriction conflict and no suite term conflict. And the demised suite must read `occupancy_status` `VACANT`, with no other lease holding it in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`; otherwise `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

An exclusive-use conflict exists when some **other** lease in the same building, in status `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, holds an `exclusive_use_specialty` equal to the entering tenant's `primary_specialty`; the refusal is `ERR_EXCLUSIVE_USE_CONFLICT`. The test is deliberately one-directional. An incumbent's exclusive blocks an entrant of that specialty, but an entrant's own requested `exclusive_use_specialty` never retroactively blocks an incumbent who is already in occupancy. A radius-restriction conflict is the mirror-image concern from the incumbent's side: it exists when some other lease in the building, again in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, belongs to a tenant of the **same** `primary_specialty` and carries a non-null `radius_restriction_miles`, and the refusal is `ERR_RADIUS_RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`. A `radius_restriction_miles` of 0 disables the restriction on the lease that declares it.

The suite term conflict guards a single suite against being promised to two tenants over overlapping periods, with a class-dependent buffer for the work of decommissioning specialised space. The incumbents considered are the leases on the same suite, other than the candidate itself, in status `FMV_CERTIFIED`, `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`. Note that this counted set differs from the tenant-mix set in two ways that matter: a `FMV_CERTIFIED` lease holds the suite even though it contributes no area to the tenant mix, and `SUBLEASE` rows are **not** excluded here, because a sublessee physically occupies the space. The turnover buffer is drawn from the suite's class: 0 days for `STANDARD_EXAM`, a full 30 days for `IMAGING`, and a fortnight — 14 days — for both `PROCEDURE` and `INFUSION`. For each incumbent, widen its term symmetrically by the buffer: the low edge is its `term_start` less the buffer in days and the high edge is its `term_end` plus the buffer in days. The buffer is expanded around the **incumbent only** and never around the candidate as well, which would double-count it. Lease terms are **closed** intervals — the opposite convention from the shared-space sweep described in §3.9 — so the candidate conflicts whenever its `term_start` is on or before the incumbent's high edge **and** the incumbent's low edge is on or before the candidate's `term_end`; both comparisons are non-strict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_SUITE_TERM_CONFLICT`. One pair is exempt: if the candidate's `master_lease_id` names that very incumbent, the pair is skipped, because otherwise no sublease could ever be executed over space its own master lease holds. To illustrate the endpoint inclusivity: on a `PROCEDURE` suite whose incumbent runs 1 September 2026 through 31 August 2029, the buffered window runs 18 August 2026 through 14 September 2029, so a candidate starting on 14 September 2029 still conflicts and one starting 15 September 2029 is clean.

Executing a lease reserves the space and re-derives the building. The demised suite moves `VACANT → RESERVED`, its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by 1. Because the lease has entered the counted set, the building's `anchor_rentable_sf`, `independent_rentable_sf`, `leased_rentable_sf`, `vacant_rentable_sf`, `anchor_share_pct` and `tenant_mix_posture` are all recomputed and the building's `version` advances by 1. The lease's own `version` advances by 1.

Commencement is the `PropertyManager`'s act, accepts an optional `commencement_note`, and moves a lease `EXECUTED → ACTIVE`. The clock date must have reached `term_start`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_YET_STARTED`. The building must not read `FROZEN`. A lease commencing into a suite whose `suite_class` is `IMAGING` requires that suite's `shielding_certified` to be true, refused as `ERR_SHIELDING_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` — the patient-safety rule that outranks every commercial consideration in this system. The exclusive-use and radius-restriction tests are re-run here rather than trusted from execution time, because a peer lease may have taken its exclusive in the interval between the two acts. And no tenant-improvement project on the lease may remain in flight: any project still in `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `PERMITTED`, `CONSTRUCTION` or `ON_HOLD` refuses the commencement with `ERR_TI_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE`, so every project must have reached `COMPLETE` or `CANCELLED`. On success the suite moves `RESERVED → OCCUPIED` with its `version` advanced, the building's tenant mix is recomputed with the building's `version` advanced, and the lease's `version` advances.

Termination is also the `PropertyManager`'s act and is admissible from `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`. It carries a mandatory `termination_reason` of `EXPIRATION`, `MUTUAL` or `DEFAULT` plus an optional `termination_note`. Unless the reason is `DEFAULT`, no invoice on the lease may still be in status `ISSUED` or `DELINQUENT`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_RENT_BALANCE`. A default termination bypasses that check precisely because unpaid rent is the usual reason for it.

Termination is the root of the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs in a fixed sequence of hops. **Hop 1** stamps `terminated_on` with the clock date, moves the demised suite to `VACANT`, sets that suite's `last_vacated_on` to the clock date and advances the suite's `version`. **Hop 2**, fired because the suite has left the counted set, recomputes the building's `anchor_rentable_sf`, `independent_rentable_sf`, `leased_rentable_sf`, `vacant_rentable_sf`, `anchor_share_pct` and `tenant_mix_posture`, and advances the building's `version`. **Hop 3** fires if and only if hop 2 moved `tenant_mix_posture` to `RESTRICTED`: every lease in that building whose status is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW` and whose tenant's `affiliation` is `HEALTH_SYSTEM_ANCHOR` or `HEALTH_SYSTEM_AFFILIATED` is suspended, processed in `created_at` ascending order. A suspended draft moves `DRAFT → SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW`, has its `fmv_certification_status` reset to `NOT_STARTED` and advances its `version` by 1; a suspended lease already in review moves `PENDING_FMV_REVIEW → SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW`, has its `fmv_certification_status` reset to `NOT_STARTED`, additionally has `fmv_submitted_by_user_id` cleared to null because the submission it recorded is no longer live, and advances its `version` by 1. **Hop 4** fires if and only if hop 3 suspended at least one lease: for each suspended lease, every tenant-improvement project on it whose status is `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `PERMITTED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, records its current status into `on_hold_from_status`, moves to `ON_HOLD` and advances its `version` by 1. That is a depth-four transitive chain — lease to suite, suite to building posture, building posture to peer draft leases, peer draft leases to their construction projects — and hops 3 and 4 exist only as consequences of hops 2 and 3 respectively.

Termination also settles the terminating lease's own dependents, independently of whether the posture moved. Every parking allocation on the lease still in status `ACTIVE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_on` set to the clock date, after which the building's `staff_allocated_stalls`, `patient_allocated_stalls` and `unallocated_stalls` are recomputed. Every shared-space booking on the lease still in status `BOOKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_source` recorded as `LEASE_TERMINATION`. `after_hours_authorized` is cleared to false. And where the terminating lease's `lease_type` is `MASTER`, every sublease naming it through `master_lease_id` that stands in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` is itself terminated, in `created_at` ascending order, inheriting the master's `termination_reason` and running its own hop 1 and hop 2 — so a hospital master lease collapsing takes its subtenants' suites and the building's mix figures with it. The lease's own `version` advances by 1.

Terminating a lease that was executed but never commenced runs the same hops 1 through 4, with one difference: hop 1 releases the suite from `RESERVED` rather than from `OCCUPIED`, since the tenant never took possession, and `last_vacated_on` is still stamped with the clock date. Without this route a signed-but-collapsed deal would leave a `RESERVED` suite that could never be returned to the market.

A suspension is not a punishment of the suspended tenant and has a compensating action. Through the same disposition surface that carries `RETURN_FOR_REVISION`, a `ComplianceOfficer` may issue a `REINSTATE` action with a `decision_note`, moving a lease `SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW → DRAFT`. Reinstatement is admissible only while the building's `tenant_mix_posture` is neither `RESTRICTED` nor `FROZEN`; attempting it while the posture is still `RESTRICTED` is refused with `ERR_BUILDING_MIX_RESTRICTED`. Reinstatement also unwinds hop 4: every tenant-improvement project on that lease sitting in `ON_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, returns to the status recorded in its `on_hold_from_status`, has that field cleared to null and advances its `version` by 1 — a project held from `DESIGN` returns `ON_HOLD → DESIGN`, one held from `IN_REVIEW` returns `ON_HOLD → IN_REVIEW`, and one held from `PERMITTED` returns `ON_HOLD → PERMITTED`. Reinstatement deliberately leaves `revision_round` untouched, because a suspension was never the agent's fault.

### 3.6. Tenant-Improvement Projects and the Two Engineering Reviews

Imaging, procedure and infusion space cannot simply be handed over; it has to be built. A `LeasingAgent` opens a tenant-improvement project against a lease, and the project's `lease_id` comes from the lease addressed in the path. The payload repeats the `suite_id`, which must equal the lease's own demised suite, and declares what the build-out delivers through `target_suite_class` — one of `STANDARD_EXAM`, `IMAGING`, `PROCEDURE` or `INFUSION`. It carries the designed lead-equivalent shielding thickness as `lead_thickness_mm`, which is optional in general but must be present and strictly positive when the target class is `IMAGING`; the additional dedicated electrical service sought at the suite panel as `power_amps_requested`, zero or more; a boolean `medical_gas_requested` stating whether the build-out needs piped medical gas and vacuum, defaulting to false; the landlord's `authorized_allowance_total`, zero or more with 2 decimal places; and `landlord_share_pct`, the project-wide default cost share as a 4-decimal fraction between 0.0000 and 1.0000.

A new project starts in `DESIGN` with both review dimensions reading `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `line_item_count` at 0, `landlord_committed_amount` and `tenant_committed_amount` at 0.00, `allowance_remaining` equal to `authorized_allowance_total`, `power_amps_drawn` at 0 and `on_hold_from_status` null. Projects can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id`, `suite_id` and `status`.

Scope of work is appended one line at a time, and only while the project is still in `DESIGN`. Because a line consumes a fixed allowance and is cost-shared against a project-level percentage that may itself have moved, the append carries `expected_ti_project_version`. Each line names a `description` of the scope, a `category` drawn from `SHIELDING`, `POWER`, `MEDICAL_GAS`, `PLUMBING` and `FINISHES`, a strictly positive `cost_amount` with 2 decimal places, and optionally a `landlord_share_pct_override` to displace the project default for that line alone; a null override means the line inherits. The server sets the line's `ti_project_id` from the addressed project rather than from the request body, and assigns `sequence_no`, a 1-based index that increases monotonically within the project and is the pinned processing order for every cost figure derived below. Line items are immutable and are never deleted, so the input set only ever grows; a project's lines are readable in `sequence_no` order and can be narrowed by `category`.

The share applied to a line resolves through a three-level precedence chain, highest level winning. First, the category rule: a line whose `category` is `SHIELDING` on a project whose `target_suite_class` is `IMAGING` is borne entirely by the landlord, so its `effective_share_pct` is 1.0000 — the landlord pays 100% of radiation shielding in an imaging build-out regardless of anything else the project or the line says. Second, the line override: where the category rule does not apply and `landlord_share_pct_override` is non-null, `effective_share_pct` takes the override. Third, the project default: otherwise `effective_share_pct` takes the project's `landlord_share_pct`. From the resolved share, `landlord_share_amount` is the line's `cost_amount` multiplied by `effective_share_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `tenant_share_amount` is the line's `cost_amount` **less** that rounded landlord amount. Deriving the tenant's share by subtraction rather than by a second independent rounding is what guarantees the two shares add back to the line cost exactly, on every line and therefore in aggregate.

The project's totals are then sums of the **already-rounded** per-line figures, taken over the lines in `sequence_no` order: `landlord_committed_amount` sums the per-line landlord amounts, `tenant_committed_amount` sums the per-line tenant amounts, and `allowance_remaining` is `authorized_allowance_total` less `landlord_committed_amount`. Rounding per line and then summing is pinned; summing the unrounded products and rounding once is a different answer and is wrong here. Consider a project with a default share of 0.6000, an `IMAGING` target class and an authorised allowance of 500000.00, carrying four lines in sequence: a `SHIELDING` line of 88000.00, which the category rule forces to a landlord share of 88000.00 and a tenant share of 0.00; a `POWER` line of 12345.04 at the default share, giving 7407.02 and 4938.02; a second `POWER` line of 23456.09, giving 14073.65 and 9382.44; and a `FINISHES` line of 34567.14, giving 20740.28 and 13826.86. The committed totals are 130220.95 and 28147.32 and the remaining allowance is 369779.05. Summing the three unrounded default-share products and rounding once would yield a landlord figure one cent higher, which is precisely why the order is pinned. Appending a line also increments `line_item_count` by 1 and advances the project's `version` by 1. A line whose landlord share would drive `allowance_remaining` below zero is refused with `ERR_TI_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`.

The project's leasing-side transitions are driven by an `action` with an optional `note`. `SUBMIT_REVIEWS` moves the project `DESIGN → IN_REVIEW` and is the fork point of the whole design: it requires at least one line item, requires a non-null and strictly positive `lead_thickness_mm` when the target class is `IMAGING`, and requires the owning lease to be in none of `TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUSPENDED_MIX_REVIEW`. It then sets **both** `shielding_review_status` and `utility_review_status` to `PENDING` in the same transition and advances `version`. `START_CONSTRUCTION` moves the project `PERMITTED → CONSTRUCTION` and requires the owning lease to be in `EXECUTED` or `ACTIVE` — no construction proceeds on a lease that has not been executed, and a violation is `ERR_TI_PROJECT_NOT_COMPLETE` — as well as an `allowance_remaining` of zero or more. `CANCEL` is admissible from `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `PERMITTED` and moves the project to `CANCELLED`.

The two review dimensions are genuinely independent and are recorded by a `FacilitiesEngineer` on a single review surface taking a `review_type` of `SHIELDING` or `UTILITY`, a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, an optional `lead_thickness_verified_mm` and an optional `review_note`. In every case the addressed dimension must currently read `PENDING`. Approving the shielding dimension requires, when the target class is `IMAGING`, that `lead_thickness_verified_mm` be non-null and at least the designed `lead_thickness_mm`; it then sets `shielding_review_status` to `APPROVED`, sets the suite's `shielding_certified` to true, writes `lead_thickness_verified_mm` into the suite's `lead_thickness_mm`, and advances the suite's `version`. Approving the utility dimension requires the suite's `power_amps_available` to be at least the project's `power_amps_requested`, refused as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UTILITY_CAPACITY`, and requires the suite's `medical_gas_available` to be true whenever `medical_gas_requested` is true, refused as `ERR_SHIELDING_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`; it then sets `utility_review_status` to `APPROVED`, reduces the suite's `power_amps_available` by `power_amps_requested`, records the amount actually drawn in the project's `power_amps_drawn`, and advances the suite's `version`. The two effect sets are strictly disjoint: the shielding branch touches `shielding_certified` and `lead_thickness_mm` and never electrical capacity, and the utility branch touches `power_amps_available` and never shielding. Each approval leaves the project in `IN_REVIEW`, advances the project's `version`, and joins: if the **other** dimension already reads `APPROVED`, the same request also moves the project to `PERMITTED`.

Rejecting either dimension cycles the project `IN_REVIEW → DESIGN` with full compensation, so that no drift accumulates across review rounds. The addressed dimension is set to `REJECTED` and the other dimension is reset to `NOT_SUBMITTED`. If the other dimension had already been approved, its effects on the suite are reversed: an approved shielding branch is compensated by setting the suite's `shielding_certified` back to false and its `lead_thickness_mm` back to null, and an approved utility branch is compensated by adding `power_amps_drawn` back to the suite's `power_amps_available` and resetting `power_amps_drawn` to 0. The suite's `version` advances where the suite is touched, and the project's `version` advances.

Cancellation applies the same compensation from whichever of `DESIGN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `PERMITTED` the project occupied, and the three cancellation routes are identical in effect. Any drawn electrical capacity is added back to the suite's `power_amps_available` and `power_amps_drawn` is reset to 0; if `shielding_review_status` reads `APPROVED`, the suite's `shielding_certified` is set back to false and its `lead_thickness_mm` to null; the suite's `version` advances. On the project itself, `landlord_committed_amount` and `tenant_committed_amount` are reset to 0.00 and `allowance_remaining` is restored to the full `authorized_allowance_total`, and `version` advances.

Close-out is the `FacilitiesEngineer`'s act, accepts an optional `completion_note`, and moves the project `CONSTRUCTION → COMPLETE`. It requires both `shielding_review_status` and `utility_review_status` to read `APPROVED` and requires `allowance_remaining` to be zero or more. It is the moment the suite legally becomes what it was built to be: the suite's `suite_class` is overwritten with the project's `target_suite_class`, and where `medical_gas_requested` was true the suite's `medical_gas_available` is set to true. The suite's `version` and the project's `version` both advance by 1. Reclassifying the suite matters commercially as well as clinically, because the class premium in §3.4 and the turnover buffer in §3.5 both key off `suite_class`.

### 3.7. Occupancy Billing, Arrears Aging and Holdover

A `PropertyManager` issues a monthly occupancy invoice against a lease, naming the billed month through `period_start` and `period_end` — the first and last days of the period, both inclusive — and the `due_date` on which payment falls due, which is the aging basis for everything that follows. The requested period may not overlap the period of any existing invoice on the same lease that is not written off, and it must lie inside the lease term unless the lease has rolled into `HOLDOVER`; a violation is `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new invoice begins in `ISSUED` with `amount_paid` at 0.00, `days_past_due` at 0, `delinquency_tier_weight` at 0, and `days_past_due_at_flag`, `paid_on`, `write_off_justification` and `written_off_by_user_id` all null. A lease's invoices can be listed and narrowed by `status`.

The invoice total is built in six numbered steps, and the order may not be rearranged. **Step 1** derives `base_rent_amount` as the lease's `effective_base_rent_annual` divided by 12 — the annual figure is a year's rent and a month is a twelfth of it — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Which annual figure that is depends on the variant selection in §3.4, so an uncertified lease bills the rate-card figure and a certified one bills the negotiated figure. **Step 2** derives `cam_amount`, the common-area recovery, as the building's `operating_expense_annual_psf` multiplied by the suite's `rentable_sf` and divided by 12, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. **Step 3** derives `parking_fee_gross` from the lease's parking allocations that are currently in status `ACTIVE`: each allocation contributes its `stall_count` multiplied by its monthly per-stall fee, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the contributions are summed. Staff stalls are billable at 50.00 per stall per month while patient stalls are free at 0.00 per stall per month, so patient allocations are counted in the input set but priced at nothing. Revoked allocations are excluded entirely, which is why revoking a stall re-prices every subsequent invoice on that lease. **Step 4** derives `pre_waiver_total` as the sum of the three components — base rent, common-area recovery and gross parking.

**Step 5** selects the parking waiver. The tier is keyed on `pre_waiver_total`, the **combined** figure from step 4, and not on the parking component alone. Walk the tiers in order and take the first whose upper bound is satisfied: a combined total of at most 5000.00 selects a `parking_waiver_tier_pct` of 0.00, a combined total of at most 12000.00 selects 0.25, and anything above that selects 0.50. The bounds are inclusive upper bounds, so a combined total of exactly 5000.00 sits in the 0.00 tier and 5000.01 sits in the 0.25 tier. `parking_waiver_amount` is then `parking_fee_gross` multiplied by the selected `parking_waiver_tier_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — the waiver applies to the parking component only, never to rent or recovery. **Step 6** derives `total_amount`, the amount owed, as `pre_waiver_total` less `parking_waiver_amount`.

The apparent circularity — the tier depends on a sum that parking itself contributes to — is broken by evaluating gross parking in step 3 and the waiver last in step 6, and it has a genuinely counter-intuitive consequence that the implementation must reproduce rather than "fix". A 1500 square foot suite billed at an annual figure of 43200.00 in a building recovering 9.60 per square foot, with 4 staff stalls, produces 3600.00 of base rent, 1200.00 of recovery and 200.00 of parking, for a combined total of exactly 5000.00: the 0.00 tier applies and the invoice is 5000.00. Add a fifth staff stall and the components become 3600.00, 1200.00 and 250.00 for a combined 5050.00, which crosses into the 0.25 tier, waives 62.50 of parking, and produces a total of 4987.50. The tenant with more parking owes 12.50 less. A larger illustration: a lease certified at 160000.00 annually on a 5000 square foot suite in a building recovering 9.60 per square foot, with 20 staff and 30 patient stalls active, produces 13333.33, 4000.00 and 1000.00 for a combined 18333.33, which selects the 0.50 tier, waives 500.00, and totals 17833.33.

Payment is recorded by a `PropertyManager` and must settle the invoice in one act: the supplied `payment_amount` added to the current `amount_paid` must equal `total_amount` exactly. A partial payment and an overpayment are both refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Payment is admissible from `ISSUED` or from `DELINQUENT`, and both routes converge: `amount_paid` becomes `total_amount`, `paid_on` is stamped with the clock date, `days_past_due` is refreshed to the whole number of days between the clock date and `due_date` (never below zero), and `version` advances. Curing arrears from `DELINQUENT` is emphatically **not** a compensation. `days_past_due_at_flag` and `delinquency_tier_weight` are both retained unchanged, so the tenant's `referral_compliance_risk_score` does not fall when the money finally arrives — the remuneration event happened, and the only relief is the rolling window eventually ageing it out.

Aging is measured in whole calendar days as the clock date less `due_date`, and the figure is negative before the due date arrives. An invoice becomes delinquent only when that day count is **strictly greater than** the ten-day grace period, so a count of exactly 10 leaves the invoice in `ISSUED` carrying no weight. The weight tiers are keyed on the day count itself rather than on days past grace, which means the day an invoice becomes delinquent it is already worth 15 points: a day count of at most 30 carries a weight of 15, a count of at most 60 carries 30, and a count beyond 60 carries 50. At the daily close on which the invoice is first flagged, the day count is frozen into `days_past_due_at_flag` and the corresponding weight into `delinquency_tier_weight`, and neither is revised on any later close even though `days_past_due` continues to grow. An invoice due on 5 August 2026 still reads 10 days on 15 August and stays `ISSUED`; on 16 August the count is 11, so the close moves it `ISSUED → DELINQUENT`, freezes the flag at 11 and the weight at 15. Had no close run until 5 September the count would have been 31 and the frozen weight 30; had none run until 6 October the count would have been 62 and the frozen weight 50.

Flagging an invoice delinquent is the root of the system's referral-compliance cascade, and it too runs in fixed hops. **Hop 1** writes `days_past_due` and `days_past_due_at_flag` from the day count and freezes `delinquency_tier_weight` at the corresponding tier. **Hop 2** increases the tenant's `delinquency_event_count` by 1, recomputes both `referral_compliance_risk_score` and `strict_cumulative_risk_score`, and advances the tenant's `version`. **Hop 3** fires when the recomputed **rolling** score is at least 45: the tenant's `compliance_watch_status` becomes `WATCH` and the system opens a compliance case against that tenant with `case_type` `RENT_DELINQUENCY` in status `OPEN`, at `HIGH` severity when the recomputed rolling score is at least 90 and `MEDIUM` otherwise, with `scoring_variant` `ROLLING_12M`, `risk_score_at_open` set to that recomputed rolling score, `opened_on` set to the clock date, `opened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal value `system`, and `remediation_due_on` set to the clock date plus 30 days. **Hop 3b** fires only when hop 3 actually raised the watch, and it is the hop implementers most often miss: every lease of that tenant whose status is anything other than `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` — drafts and certified deals very much included, because a pending deal is exactly where an improper concession would be granted — is processed in `created_at` ascending order to set `rent_concession_locked` to true, clear `after_hours_authorized` to false and advance that lease's `version` by 1; and on each of those leases, every shared-space booking still in `BOOKED` whose `window_start` is later than the current clock moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_source` recorded as `COMPLIANCE_CASCADE`. The invoice's own `version` advances. The chain is therefore three deep from a single system act: invoice, to tenant score, to a compliance case together with the tenant's whole lease book and their future bookings.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may write off a delinquent invoice, supplying a non-empty `write_off_justification`. The act is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` matches the `opened_by_user_id` of any `OPEN` rent-delinquency case on that tenant — the officer who raised the alarm may not also forgive the debt. On success the invoice moves `DELINQUENT → WRITTEN_OFF`, the justification and the acting `X-User-ID` are recorded as `write_off_justification` and `written_off_by_user_id`, and `delinquency_tier_weight` is **replaced** by 40 rather than adjusted, because forgiving rent owed by a referral source is a larger remuneration exposure than merely tolerating late rent. Both score variants are then recomputed, the tenant's `compliance_watch_status` is re-evaluated, and both the tenant's and the invoice's `version` advance.

Finally, a lease that outlives its own term rolls into holdover. When a daily close finds the clock date strictly past the `term_end` of a lease in status `ACTIVE`, the lease moves `ACTIVE → HOLDOVER`, `holdover_premium_applied` is set to true, `effective_base_rent_annual` is recomputed as `certified_base_rent_annual` multiplied by 1.50 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `version` advances. The suite stays `OCCUPIED` and the building's tenant mix does not move, because `HOLDOVER` is one of the counted statuses. The practical effect is that the next invoice issued on that lease bills a monthly base rent 50% above the certified rate — a holdover premium the tenant can end only by signing a new lease or vacating.

### 3.8. Compliance Cases, the Watch Cascade and Remediation

A compliance case is the durable record of a referral-compliance concern about one practice. Cases arrive by two routes. The system opens them without human involvement — a rent-delinquency case from the arrears cascade in §3.7, and a fair-market-value deviation case from the two triggers in §3.4 — and in every such case `opened_by_user_id` is recorded as the literal value `system` and `scoring_variant` is `ROLLING_12M`, because the rolling window is the internal management view. A `ComplianceOfficer` may also open a case by hand, supplying the `tenant_id` under review, the `case_type` — one of `RENT_DELINQUENCY`, `FMV_DEVIATION` or `SUBLEASE_DOCUMENTATION` — a narrative `basis`, a `severity` and, decisively, the `scoring_variant` the case is to be measured under. Choosing `STRICT_CUMULATIVE` records the uncapped, unwindowed score, which is the figure an outside reviewer would reconstruct from the whole history; choosing `ROLLING_12M` records the internal figure. A manually opened case begins in `OPEN` with `reopen_count` at 0, `opened_on` stamped with the clock date, `opened_by_user_id` taken from the `X-User-ID` header, `risk_score_at_open` frozen to the tenant's score **under the requested variant**, and `remediation_due_on` set to the clock date plus 30 days. Opening a case by hand always puts the tenant on `WATCH` and advances the tenant's `version`, whatever the score says. Although `severity` is accepted on the request, the stored value is always derived from `risk_score_at_open`: a frozen score of at least 90 is `HIGH`, at least 45 is `MEDIUM`, and anything lower is `LOW`. Two cases opened the same day on the same tenant can therefore carry different severities purely because they chose different variants — the strict figure ignores the 100-point cap and the 365-day window, so it is the larger of the two whenever the tenant's history reaches back beyond a year. A tenant's cases can be listed, and that listing is the only channel through which cascade-opened cases become visible.

Disposition is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s work and moves through three named actions. `REMEDIATION_PLAN` is admissible only from `OPEN`, requires a non-empty `remediation_summary`, records that text, resets `remediation_due_on` to the clock date plus 30 days, advances `version`, and moves the case `OPEN → REMEDIATION_PLANNED`. `CURE` is admissible from either `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`, and both routes behave identically. `REPORT` is admissible only from `OPEN`, requires `severity` to have reached `HIGH` and a non-empty `report_reference`, records that reference, stamps `closed_on` with the clock date, advances `version`, and moves the case `OPEN → REPORTED`. A reported case leaves the tenant's `compliance_watch_status` at `WATCH` unconditionally and permanently, because a self-disclosure to an outside authority is not something later good behaviour erases. `CURED` and `REPORTED` are both terminal.

A missed remediation deadline is what makes the case lifecycle cyclic. When a daily close finds a case in `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` whose `remediation_due_on` is strictly earlier than the clock date, the case moves `REMEDIATION_PLANNED → OPEN`, `reopen_count` increases by 1, the tenant's `compliance_watch_status` is forced back to `WATCH` with the tenant's `version` advanced, and the case's own `version` advances. When `reopen_count` reaches 2 or more, `severity` is raised to `HIGH` — and since `HIGH` is exactly the gate `REPORT` demands, the loop is guaranteed an exit: an officer who plans remediation twice and misses both deadlines has, by the second miss, produced a case that can be reported and closed. Note the asymmetry: reopening escalates severity but never de-escalates it, and it does not touch `risk_score_at_open`, which stays frozen at the figure observed when the case was first opened.

Curing a case is the system's only true compensating action on the referral-compliance side, and it is deliberately conditional on the underlying defect actually being gone. A `RENT_DELINQUENCY` case may be cured only when no rent invoice of that tenant remains in `DELINQUENT` — writing off or paying every delinquent invoice is the precondition, not a formality. An `FMV_DEVIATION` case may be cured only when every lease of that tenant whose status is `FMV_CERTIFIED`, `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` carries `fmv_certification_status` of `CERTIFIED`, so a single lease left `STALE` by a disqualified controlling appraisal blocks the cure. A `SUBLEASE_DOCUMENTATION` case carries no such precondition and may be cured at the officer's discretion. On success `closed_on` is stamped with the clock date and the case moves to `CURED`.

The cure then unwinds the concession lock, but only partly, and only when it is safe to do so. When the cured case is a `RENT_DELINQUENCY` case **and** no other rent-delinquency case of that tenant is still in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`, every lease of that tenant whose status is neither `TERMINATED` nor `WITHDRAWN` is processed in `created_at` ascending order to set `rent_concession_locked` back to false and advance that lease's `version`. `after_hours_authorized` is **not** restored. The asymmetry is intentional: locking a concession is a paper control that the cure genuinely reverses, whereas after-hours building access is a physical privilege that must be re-granted by an explicit `PropertyManager` act, so the cascade in §3.7 revokes it and no cure ever gives it back. Separately, and for cases of any type, the tenant's `compliance_watch_status` returns to `CLEAR` with the tenant's `version` advanced only when no other case of that tenant remains in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`; if one does, the tenant stays on `WATCH` and the leases stay locked.

### 3.9. Shared Clinical Space Bookings and the Parking Pool

A building's shared clinical spaces are registered by a `PortfolioAdmin`, each with a `name`, a `space_type` of `WAITING_ROOM`, `PROCEDURE_ROOM` or `INFUSION_BAY`, a `capacity_seats` figure of at least 1 counting patients plus escorts, and a daily operating window given as `opens_at` and `closes_at` — times of day in hours and minutes, where `opens_at` is inclusive, `closes_at` is exclusive, and `closes_at` must be later than `opens_at`. A building's spaces can be listed and narrowed by `space_type`.

A booking is created by a `LeasingAgent` or by the `TenantPortalUser` themselves, naming the `shared_space_id`, the `lease_id` consuming the space, the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the occupancy — again inclusive start, exclusive end, with the end required to be later than the start — and the `occupant_count` of at least 1 that this booking contributes to the room's load. The booking is created in `BOOKED` and its `is_after_hours` flag is derived: it is true when any part of the requested window falls outside the space's operating window, and false only when the whole window sits inside it.

Three gates stand in front of a booking, and their order is fixed. First, a tenant whose `compliance_watch_status` is `WATCH` may not obtain a new shared-space booking at all. Second, a booking of a `WAITING_ROOM` or a `PROCEDURE_ROOM` requires the lease's `space_sharing_schedule_on_file` to be true, because shared clinical space between potential referral sources must be documented on a written schedule; an `INFUSION_BAY` carries no such requirement. Third, a booking that is after hours requires the lease's `after_hours_authorized` to be true, and this check runs **before** the capacity sweep — an unauthorised 20:00 request in a room open until 19:00 is refused on the authorization ground even if the room is completely empty, and even if it would also have been refused on capacity.

Only then does the seat sweep run. The load constraint is on total seats occupied at any instant, never on the number of concurrent bookings, and the input set is the bookings of that space still in `BOOKED` — cancelled and completed rows are excluded, so cancelling a booking immediately frees its seats for a later request in the same window. Intervals are half-open on both sides, and overlap is tested with strict inequalities in both directions: two bookings overlap only when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends. A booking that begins exactly when another ends therefore does not overlap it and the two never compete for seats. To evaluate a candidate, collect the candidate's own start and end together with the starts and ends of every existing booking that overlaps the candidate, sort those instants, and walk each adjacent pair whose earlier instant lies inside the candidate's own window. At each such instant the load is the candidate's `occupant_count` plus the `occupant_count` of every existing booking whose start is at or before that instant and whose end is strictly after it. The rejection test is a load **strictly greater than** `capacity_seats`, so a load exactly equal to capacity is admitted. In a 24-seat waiting room open from 07:00 to 19:00, a booking from 09:00 to 11:00 for 10 occupants is accepted; a second from 10:00 to 12:00 for 14 occupants brings the load on the overlapping hour to exactly 24, which is not greater than 24, so it is accepted too; a third from 10:30 to 11:30 for a single occupant would bring that same hour to 25 and is refused. With only the first booking present, a request from 11:00 to 12:00 for the full 24 occupants is accepted, because the two windows touch but do not overlap.

A `LeasingAgent` or the `TenantPortalUser` may cancel a booking, optionally supplying a `cancellation_note`. Cancellation is admissible only while `window_start` is still later than the clock — a booking already under way cannot be cancelled — and it moves the booking `BOOKED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_source` recorded as `REQUESTED` and `version` advanced. That field is the provenance record distinguishing a tenant's own cancellation from the two the system performs: `COMPLIANCE_CASCADE` when the delinquency watch cascade in §3.7 cancels a tenant's future bookings, and `LEASE_TERMINATION` when the termination cascade in §3.5 clears the lease's book. Bookings can be listed and narrowed by `shared_space_id`, by `lease_id` and by `status`, which is the only channel through which cascade-cancelled bookings become visible. Separately, each daily close moves every booking still in `BOOKED` whose `window_end` is at or before the clock to `COMPLETED`, advancing `version`; `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are both terminal, and both leave the seat-load input set.

After-hours access is itself a `PropertyManager` act on the lease, carrying a single `authorized` boolean that is written straight into `after_hours_authorized`. Granting access is refused while the tenant is on `WATCH`, which is what makes the revocation in §3.7 durable: the cascade clears the flag, the cure in §3.8 pointedly does not restore it, and the manager cannot re-grant it until the tenant's watch is lifted by curing every open case.

Parking is drawn from one pool per building and is the system's fourth optimistic-concurrency site. A `PropertyManager` allocates stalls by supplying `expected_building_version` together with the `building_id`, the `lease_id` receiving the stalls, a `category` of `STAFF` or `PATIENT`, and a `stall_count` of at least 1; the allocation is created in `ACTIVE`. The building's three derived counters are then recomputed: `staff_allocated_stalls` is the summed `stall_count` of active staff allocations, `patient_allocated_stalls` the summed `stall_count` of active patient allocations, and `unallocated_stalls` is `total_parking_stalls` less those two sums. Only active allocations count, so revoked stalls are genuinely back in the pool, and the identity that the three counters sum to `total_parking_stalls` holds after every allocation, every revocation and every lease-termination cascade.

Two admission tests guard an allocation, in a fixed order. The pool-capacity test comes first: a requested `stall_count` greater than the building's current `unallocated_stalls` is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNALLOCATED_STALLS`. The patient-reserve test comes second and applies only to `STAFF` requests: the reserve minimum is `total_parking_stalls` multiplied by `patient_min_reserve_pct` and raised to the next whole stall by ceiling — fractional reserves always round up in the patients' favour — and the request is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_PARKING_RESERVE_BREACH` when `total_parking_stalls` less the post-request staff total would fall below that minimum. The reserve is measured against every stall **not** allocated to staff, that is patient stalls plus unallocated stalls, and not against `patient_allocated_stalls` alone; measuring it the narrow way would make the very first staff stall in an empty building a breach. Because the pool test runs first, a request that both overdraws the pool and breaches the reserve reports the insufficient-stalls error. In a 400-stall building reserving 0.60, the minimum is 240 stalls and staff may therefore hold at most 160: a first staff request for 160 leaves exactly 240 non-staff stalls and is accepted, while a request for 161 would leave 239 and is refused. A `PATIENT` request is never reserve-tested and is bounded only by `unallocated_stalls`.

A `PropertyManager` may revoke an allocation with an optional `revocation_note`, which moves it to `REVOKED`, stamps `revoked_on` with the clock date, returns the stalls to the pool by recomputing the three counters, and advances the building's `version`. Revocation has a billing consequence as well as a spatial one: because the invoice build in §3.7 prices only active allocations, revoking staff stalls re-prices every invoice issued on that lease afterwards. Allocations can be listed and narrowed by `building_id`, by `lease_id`, by `category` and by `status`.
