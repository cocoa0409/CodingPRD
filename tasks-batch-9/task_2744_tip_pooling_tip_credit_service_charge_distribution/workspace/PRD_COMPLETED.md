# Tip Pooling Payroll API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Multi-unit restaurant groups carry one of the most litigated payroll problems in the economy. A single server's paycheck can depend on which state the unit sits in, whether the unit claims a tip credit against the minimum wage, how many minutes of that server's shift were spent polishing glassware instead of waiting tables, how much of the guest's card tip the processor's fee legitimately consumes, whether a banquet's mandatory service charge counts as a wage or as a tip, and whether a busser who never touched a table is nevertheless entitled to a share of the night's pooled tips. Getting any of these wrong produces back wages, liquidated damages, and a Department of Labor file.

This document specifies the Tip Pooling Payroll API — the system of record for tipped compensation across a restaurant chain. It governs the classification of job roles as tipped or non-tipped, the recording of shifts with their sales and side-work minutes, the nightly computation of shift-level tip pools with their mandatory contributions and permitted card-fee deductions, the weekly computation of pay lines with tip credit and minimum-wage top-up, the reporting test that generates allocated tips, and the dispute-and-correction machinery that repairs the result when a computation turns out to be wrong. Every money figure is an integer number of cents and every duration is a whole number of minutes; no floating-point value is ever stored or returned.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as requirements rather than as commentary. First, **the pay period is a cycle, not a pipeline**: a period moves from open to computed to approved, and it can be driven back to open again — either deliberately by a compliance operator or automatically when one of its inputs is invalidated by a cascade. Second, **money is never silently rewritten**: a pool that must be undone is undone by appending offsetting ledger rows rather than editing the original rows, and a pay line that must change is superseded rather than patched. Implementations that shortcut either commitment will produce audit trails that cannot be defended.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies to every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, arithmetic discipline, record identity, concurrency control, and the order in which requests are validated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is missing or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. The system recognises exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows: `PayrollAdmin`, `PayrollPreparer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ShiftManager`, and `Employee`. If the value of `X-User-Role` is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the work along regulatory lines. A `PayrollAdmin` is the chain configuration operator: it registers restaurant units, defines chain-wide job roles, hires employees, records role assignments, drafts tip-pool policies, and drives the server's logical clock. A `PayrollPreparer` is the unit payroll operator: it records shifts and service charges, closes shifts, builds and distributes tip pools, voids shifts and service charges, and computes pay periods. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the wage-and-hour compliance authority: it activates tip-pool policies, approves and reopens pay periods, reverses pools, adjudicates disputes, and issues back-wage corrections. A `ShiftManager` is a front-of-house supervisor who records and closes shifts, records service charges, and may declare tips on behalf of staff. An `Employee` is a tipped or non-tipped worker who declares tips on their own shifts and raises disputes on their own pay lines. Both a `ComplianceOfficer` and a `PayrollPreparer` may post a pending back-wage correction to its pay line.

Three of the five roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true when `X-User-Role` is `PayrollPreparer` and when `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`: in both cases `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier, and although the server records it on the resources those operators touch — as `computed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a pay period, as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a dispute, and as `issued_by_user_id` and `applied_by_user_id` on a back-wage correction — it never resolves it against a stored row.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an acting `Employee` may declare tips only on a shift whose `employee_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may raise a dispute only where the supplied `raised_by_employee_id` equals `X-User-ID` and only against a pay line whose `employee_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read only those pay lines, shifts and pool-allocation rows whose `employee_id` equals `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ShiftManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Employee`, but the ownership check is widened one level: a `ShiftManager` is confined to the `location_id` of the `Employee` row that `X-User-ID` names, and may act on and read shifts, pay lines, allocation rows and disputes belonging to that unit rather than only to that one person. Any breach of either scope is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Because both of these roles are resolved against the stored `Employee` collection, an `X-User-ID` that names no stored `Employee` row leaves nothing at all inside the actor's scope: every ownership check that actor meets fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and every scoped collection read comes back as an empty page rather than as an error.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters A through Z and the digits 0 through 9.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the server's real wall-clock time and is the one timestamp in the system that is not drawn from the logical clock of §2.6, so it advances between two responses even while the logical clock stands still.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before collecting the page; it defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. Because the logical clock of §2.6 stands still until a client moves it, many rows legitimately share one `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were written, earliest write first.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters, not merely the rows on the current page, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current page. Where the acting role's read scope narrows a collection as §2.11 describes, that narrowing is applied to the filtered rows before the count is taken, so both figures describe only the rows the actor is entitled to see.

### 2.4. Statutory Wage Floors and the Tip-Credit Ceiling

Two federal constants anchor every wage computation in the system. The federal minimum wage is 725 cents per hour, and the federal floor on the direct cash wage payable to a tipped employee is 213 cents per hour. Neither is configurable; both are properties of the platform.

Each restaurant unit declares the state whose wage and card-fee rules govern it through `state_code`, which must be one of `CA`, `NY`, `TX` or `MA`, together with that state's minimum wage in cents per hour as `state_min_wage_cents`, which may not be below 725. From those two inputs the server derives `applicable_min_wage_cents` as the greater of the federal minimum wage of 725 cents and the unit's `state_min_wage_cents`. That derived figure is the wage floor every worked hour at the unit must reach, and it is what a top-up exists to close.

A unit that claims a tip credit sets `tip_credit_enabled` to true; the flag defaults to false when omitted. The direct hourly cash wage the unit pays its tipped staff is `cash_wage_cents`, which may not be below the federal cash-wage floor of 213 cents and may not exceed `applicable_min_wage_cents`. Where the unit claims a tip credit, that claim is capped per hour: the server derives `max_tip_credit_per_hour_cents` as `applicable_min_wage_cents` minus `cash_wage_cents`. Where `tip_credit_enabled` is false, `max_tip_credit_per_hour_cents` is 0 and no tip credit is ever available at that unit, whatever its cash wage happens to be. A unit that sets `tip_credit_enabled` to true must satisfy the stricter form of the cash-wage rule: `cash_wage_cents` must be at least 213 and strictly below `applicable_min_wage_cents`, and a registration that breaks either bound is rejected with `ERR_CASH_WAGE_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422).

### 2.5. Cents, Minutes, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. All durations are integer counts of whole minutes. All rates that are not wages — pool contribution rates, card processing fee rates, statutory fee caps — are expressed in **basis points**, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth. Applying a basis-point rate to an amount therefore means multiplying the amount by the rate and dividing by ten thousand.

Where a division or a rate application yields a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** — a remainder of exactly one half of a cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Three refinements of that rule matter enough to be stated once here and repeated where they bite:

- **Rate bands are rounded individually, then summed.** When a wage figure is built from two or more hourly rates applied to two or more blocks of minutes, each rate-and-block product is rounded half up on its own and the rounded results are then added. Blending the rates first and rounding the blended product once is a different and incorrect answer.
- **Card fees are rounded per contributing shift, then summed.** The card-processing fee withheld from a tip pool is computed and rounded half up separately for each contributing shift, and the rounded per-shift figures are then added. Summing the unrounded per-shift products and rounding the total once is forbidden; the two orders can differ by as much as one cent fewer than the number of contributing shifts.
- **Money that must be split across people is split by floor plus a largest-remainder pass.** Each recipient's base share is the total multiplied by that recipient's weight, divided by the sum of all weights, with any fraction discarded (floored). The cents left over after all base shares are taken are then handed out one cent each, in an order that each split defines explicitly, until the leftover is exhausted. Because every base share is floored, the leftover is always strictly smaller than the number of recipients, so no recipient ever receives more than one extra cent, and the distributed total always equals the amount being split exactly. One degenerate case is the exception: where the split has no recipient at all, or where the recipients' weights sum to zero, every share is 0 and none of the amount is distributed.

Dispute adjudication amounts and back-wage correction amounts are exact integers and involve no rounding at all.

### 2.6. Record Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API stores carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Nothing is seeded either: the store is completely empty on every process boot, so every row that can be read arrived through one of the endpoints of §4.

Most resources also carry a standard set of system-managed audit fields:

- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations of purely derived fields performed by a cascade rather than by a direct client request on that row.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each subsequent mutation of the row. A single request advances a given row's `version` by at most 1, however many separate writes that request's direct mutation and its cascades make to that one row; and a row that is created and then further mutated inside the same request stays at `version` 1.

One resource is deliberately different. A pool-allocation ledger row is immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its response payload. That is the mechanical expression of the ledger principle stated in the introduction — a wrongly credited allocation is corrected by appending an offsetting row, never by editing the row that was wrong.

Date-valued fields that identify a calendar day rather than an instant — `business_date`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `effective_from`, `effective_to` and `separation_date` — are supplied and returned as ISO 8601 UTC instants at midnight, that is with a time component of `00:00:00.000`. Instant-valued fields such as `start_at`, `end_at`, `tip_credit_notice_acknowledged_at`, `activated_at`, `distributed_at`, `reversed_at`, `closed_at`, `applied_at` and `voided_at` may name any instant of the day. Every such input is normalised before it is stored rather than being required in one exact spelling: a supplied instant may carry a `Z` suffix, a numeric UTC offset such as `+02:00` or `-0500` (which is converted to UTC), or no zone designator at all (which is read as already being UTC); it may omit its seconds; and it may carry between one and nine fractional digits, of which only the first three are kept, truncated rather than rounded. A date-valued field may equally be supplied as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` or as any instant of that day, and either spelling is stored and returned as that calendar day at `00:00:00.000Z` — an instant carrying a non-midnight time component is truncated down to its day rather than refused.

The server keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; that startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. A `PayrollAdmin` may move the clock to an arbitrary UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`. From that moment on, every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `activated_at`, `distributed_at`, `reversed_at`, `closed_at` and `applied_at` stamp the server writes reads from the new logical instant, and every temporal validation — future-dating of shifts and service charges in particular — compares against it.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system re-derive state on a parent record that other operations also advance, so both require the caller to present the parent's version as it was last observed. No other endpoint takes a version field, and supplying one where it is not specified is an unknown key and therefore a malformed request.

**Activating a tip-pool policy** requires `expected_location_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the unit that owns the policy. The server compares it to the stored `version` of that `Location`. If the two differ, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOCATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). On success the server increments that unit's `version` by exactly 1 — activation is in fact the only writer of a unit's `version` — so replaying the same activation with the same `expected_location_version` is reliably rejected. The check exists because activation retires whatever policy was previously active at the unit and re-derives the unit's `pending_pool_cents`, and a client that had not observed the previous activation would otherwise silently retire a policy it never saw.

**Issuing a back-wage correction** requires `expected_pay_line_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PayLine`. The server compares it to that line's stored `version` and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_PAY_LINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). On success the line's `version` increases by exactly 1. Because computing a period, approving it, reopening it, adjudicating a dispute on the line into an award, and applying an earlier correction to the line all bump that line's `version` — a `DENIED` adjudication alone leaving it where it stood — a client that read the line before any of those operations is reliably rejected rather than being allowed to overwrite `pending_correction_cents` it never observed.

### 2.8. Cascades, Recomputation and Cross-Aggregate Consistency

Tip money and payroll money live in different aggregates but describe the same dollars, so mutations propagate. The system reconciles immediately and synchronously within the request that triggers the change: by the time a response is returned, every derived figure the change touches has been rewritten, and there is no eventual-consistency window. Each such reconciliation is a mutation of the affected row in its own right, so it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments that row's `version` — except on pool-allocation rows, which have neither field, and except on a `Location`, whose `version` only a policy activation ever advances as §2.7 requires, so that a cascade recomputing a unit's `pending_pool_cents` refreshes that unit's `updated_at` alone and leaves its `version` exactly where it stood.

Four propagation families recur, and each is spelled out in full where the workflow that triggers it is described in Section 3:

1. **Rollup recomputation.** Unit-level and period-level aggregates — `pending_pool_cents` on a unit; `total_credited_minutes`, `gross_receipts_cents`, `total_tip_credit_cents`, `total_topup_cents`, `reported_tips_cents`, `total_correction_cents` and `allocation_shortfall_cents` on a pay period; `trailing_pool_receipts_cents` on an employee — are always recomputed from the surviving rows that feed them, never adjusted by a delta, the one exception being the `total_topup_cents` increment that applying a `TIP_CREDIT_INVALID` correction makes as §3.9 describes.
2. **Policy supersession.** Activating a policy retires the incumbent policy at that unit, and retiring an incumbent reverses its still-undistributed pools.
3. **Pool reversal.** Reversing a pool restores every contributing shift, appends offsetting ledger rows for every distribution already made, and invalidates the pay periods that consumed those figures.
4. **Pay-line invalidation.** Whenever a computed or approved pay period loses one of its inputs — a reversed pool, a voided shift, or a voided service charge — the period is driven back to `OPEN` by the server itself, with no client request on the period, and every pay line of that period is marked `SUPERSEDED`.

Because the entity model is flat, no URL in this API nests one resource inside another; children are always addressed at their own top-level collection and discovered through filters. Where a path does carry an identifier, existence is checked before anything else about the request is examined, and where a path carries more than one identifier they are resolved strictly left to right so that the leftmost unresolvable identifier is the one whose `404` is returned. A request to a path that matches no route declared in §4, or to a declared path with a method that path does not declare, is refused with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — a method mismatch is reported as that same 404 and never as a 405 — and because such a request never reaches an endpoint it is answered that way whether or not it carried the two authentication headers. A trailing slash is ignored on every path, so `/locations` and `/locations/` address the same collection.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two operations in the payroll cycle may not be performed by the same human being, and the system enforces this by comparing `X-User-ID` values rather than by comparing roles. The operator who approves a pay period must not be the operator whose compute call produced its current pay lines: if `X-User-ID` on the approval equals the period's `computed_by_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Likewise, the operator who applies a back-wage correction must not be the operator who issued it: if `X-User-ID` on the apply equals that correction's `issued_by_user_id`, the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) is returned. Because both checks are on identifiers and not on roles, the two payroll operator roles cannot be collapsed onto one person even where the role list would permit it.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation taste. Every rule in the system occupies a fixed rung on a single precedence ladder, and the rule on the lowest-numbered rung that the request violates is the rule that produces the response; every rule below it on the ladder is never reached. The ladder below is the authoritative ordering. Section 3 supplies the business reasoning behind each rung; this list supplies only the order, the error identifier, the HTTP status, and the operations each rung guards.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401, on every endpoint.
2. The value of `X-User-Role` appears in the target endpoint's authorised role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403, on every endpoint.
3. The request body and query string are structurally valid: every required input present, no unknown key, every enum value inside its declared domain, every field whose name ends in `_cents`, `_minutes` or `_bps` a non-negative integer, every field whose name ends in `_at` an ISO 8601 UTC instant, `end_at` strictly after `start_at`, `end_date` not before `start_date`, `limit` within the inclusive range 1 to 100 and `offset` not negative — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400, on every endpoint. This rung is strict in five further respects that recur on every endpoint. An integer-valued field must arrive as a JSON integer, so a number carrying a fractional part, a numeric string and a boolean are all refused; and *every* integer-valued field must be non-negative, not merely the `_cents`, `_minutes` and `_bps` families — a negative `expected_location_version` or `expected_pay_line_version` is therefore a 400 on this rung and never reaches the 412 comparison of rungs 23 and 24. A supplied string field must be non-empty even where the field itself is optional, so `""` is refused for `reason`, `description` and `narrative` alike. An explicit JSON `null` is read exactly as though the field had been omitted: an optional field takes its declared default and a required field raises the missing-field rejection. An absent or whitespace-only request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an action endpoint all of whose fields are optional may be called with no body at all, whereas a body that is not parseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than an object, is refused. And the `Content-Type` header is not inspected, so a well-formed JSON body is accepted whatever that header claims. The unknown-key rule bites on the query string only at the collection list endpoints, which are the only endpoints that declare query parameters at all; a single-resource read and a lifecycle action ignore any query string handed to them, including `limit` and `offset`.
4. Every templated identifier in the path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right, all 404, on every endpoint that has a templated path segment. Each identifier has its own code: a `location_id` that names no stored Location raises `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`; an `employee_id` or `raised_by_employee_id` that names no stored Employee raises `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`; a `policy_id` that names no stored TipPoolPolicy raises `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; a `pay_period_id` that names no stored PayPeriod raises `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`; a `shift_id` that names no stored Shift raises `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`; a `pool_id` that names no stored ShiftTipPool raises `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`; a `pay_line_id` that names no stored PayLine raises `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`; a `dispute_id` that names no stored Dispute raises `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`; a `correction_id` or `supersedes_correction_id` that names no stored BackWageCorrection raises `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_FOUND`; and a `job_role_id`, `role_assignment_id`, `service_charge_id` or `allocation_id` that names no stored JobRole, EmployeeRoleAssignment, ServiceChargeEvent or PoolAllocation respectively raises `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order in which those fields are declared on the entity being created — the same 404 family (`ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`) — on `POST /employees`, `POST /role-assignments`, `POST /tip-pool-policies`, `POST /pay-periods`, `POST /shifts`, `POST /service-charges`, `POST /shift-tip-pools`, `POST /disputes` and `POST /back-wage-corrections`.
6. Resource ownership for the two entity-backed roles, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403, on `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/declare-tips`, `POST /disputes`, `GET /pay-lines/{pay_line_id}`, `GET /shifts/{shift_id}` and `GET /pool-allocations/{allocation_id}`.
7. Segregation of duties, as described in §2.9 — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/approve` and `POST /back-wage-corrections/{correction_id}/apply`.
8. The target shift's `time_status` is `OPEN` — `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_OPEN`, 409, on `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/close`.
9. The target shift's `time_status` is `CLOSED` — `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_CLOSED`, 409, on `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/declare-tips`.
10. The target shift's `shift_tip_pool_id` is null, so its declaration is not frozen by an existing pool — `ERR_SHIFT_ALREADY_POOLED`, 409, on `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/declare-tips` and `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/void`.
11. The target policy's `status` is `DRAFT` — `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`, 409, on `POST /tip-pool-policies/{policy_id}/activate`.
12. The governing pay period's `status` is `OPEN` — for a shift creation the period named by `pay_period_id`, for shift close, declare-tips and void the period owning the target shift, for a pool creation the period covering the requested `business_date` at that unit, and for a compute the target period itself — `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, 409, on `POST /shifts`, `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/close`, `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/declare-tips`, `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/compute` and `POST /shift-tip-pools`. One further condition answers with this same identifier but sits below rung 17 rather than here: the fiscal precondition on `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/reverse` described in §3.7, which is reached only once that pool's `contribution_status` has proved to be `CONTRIBUTED`.
13. The target pay period's `status` is `COMPUTED` — `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_COMPUTED`, 409, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/approve`.
14. The target pay period's `status` is either `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED` — `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_COMPUTED`, 409, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/reopen`.
15. No back-wage correction with `status` `PENDING` exists on any pay line of the target period — `ERR_PENDING_CORRECTION_EXISTS`, 409, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/reopen`.
16. The target pool's `distribution_status` is `PENDING` — `ERR_POOL_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`, 409, on `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/distribute`, and, read against the pool a service charge was absorbed into, on `POST /service-charges/{service_charge_id}/void`.
17. The target pool's `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` — `ERR_POOL_REVERSED`, 409, on `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/distribute` and `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/reverse`.
18. For every pairing of unit and operating date that has at least one `CLOSED` shift in the period, if that unit holds a policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` then a pool for that pairing exists whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` and whose `distribution_status` is `DISTRIBUTED` — `ERR_POOL_NOT_DISTRIBUTED`, 409, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/compute`.
19. The target dispute's `status` is `OPEN` — `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_OPEN`, 409, on `POST /disputes/{dispute_id}/adjudicate`.
20. The target dispute's `status` is one of `UPHELD`, `PARTIALLY_UPHELD` or `DENIED`, and for the two award-bearing outcomes the dispute's back-wage correction has `status` `APPLIED` — `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_ADJUDICATED`, 409, on `POST /disputes/{dispute_id}/close`.
21. The target correction's `status` is `PENDING` — `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_PENDING`, 409, on `POST /back-wage-corrections/{correction_id}/apply`.
22. The pay line named by `pay_line_id` has `status` `FINAL` — `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FINAL`, 409, on `POST /disputes`, `POST /back-wage-corrections`, `POST /back-wage-corrections/{correction_id}/apply` and, read against the line the target dispute names rather than against a request field, `POST /disputes/{dispute_id}/adjudicate`.
23. `expected_location_version` equals the stored `version` of the policy's unit — `ERR_STALE_LOCATION_VERSION`, 412, on `POST /tip-pool-policies/{policy_id}/activate`.
24. `expected_pay_line_version` equals the stored `version` of the correction's target line — `ERR_STALE_PAY_LINE_VERSION`, 412, on `POST /back-wage-corrections`.
25. No shift whose job role is a management role may contribute to or receive from a mandatory tip pool, so a pool whose only contributing shifts or only participating shifts are management shifts is refused — `ERR_MANAGER_POOL_PARTICIPATION`, 422, on `POST /shift-tip-pools`.
26. A policy whose `pool_type` is `ALL_NON_MANAGEMENT` may only be activated at a unit whose `tip_credit_enabled` is false — `ERR_POOL_TYPE_REQUIRES_NO_TIP_CREDIT`, 422, on `POST /tip-pool-policies/{policy_id}/activate`.
27. A policy whose `cc_fee_pass_through` is true may not be activated at a unit whose `cc_fee_policy` is `PROHIBITED` — `ERR_CC_FEE_DEDUCTION_PROHIBITED`, 422, on `POST /tip-pool-policies/{policy_id}/activate`.
28. `cash_wage_cents` is at least 213 and, where the unit's `tip_credit_enabled` is true, strictly below `applicable_min_wage_cents`, or where `tip_credit_enabled` is false, not above `applicable_min_wage_cents` — `ERR_CASH_WAGE_BELOW_FLOOR`, 422, on `POST /locations`.
29. `cc_fee_cap_bps` is required and must be greater than 0 when `cc_fee_policy` is `CAPPED_BPS`, and must be omitted for the other two policies — `ERR_CC_FEE_CAP_REQUIRED`, 422, on `POST /locations`.
30. `side_work_minutes` greater than 0 may only be recorded on a shift whose job role has `side_work_eligible` true — `ERR_SIDE_WORK_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 422, on `POST /shifts`.
31. The shift's employee holds a role assignment for the requested `job_role_id` whose date interval contains `business_date` — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT`, 422, on `POST /shifts`.
32. The shift's `location_id` equals both the employee's `location_id` and the pay period's `location_id`; and a dispute's `raised_by_employee_id` matches the disputed pay line's `employee_id` — `ERR_EMPLOYEE_LOCATION_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /shifts` and `POST /disputes`.
33. The pool's unit has exactly one policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` to pin — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POOL_POLICY`, 422, on `POST /shift-tip-pools`.
34. Every `CLOSED` shift at the pool's unit and operating date has `declaration_status` either `DECLARED` or `AMENDED` — `ERR_UNDECLARED_SHIFT_IN_POOL`, 422, on `POST /shift-tip-pools`.
35. Every `CLOSED` shift of the period has `declaration_status` either `DECLARED` or `AMENDED` — `ERR_UNDECLARED_SHIFT_IN_PERIOD`, 422, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/compute`.
36. Every `RECORDED` service-charge event inside the period whose resolved `treatment` is `WAGES` has at least one `CLOSED` shift on its `business_date` to allocate against — `ERR_SERVICE_CHARGE_UNALLOCATABLE`, 422, on `POST /pay-periods/{pay_period_id}/compute`.
37. `supersedes_correction_id`, when supplied, names a correction whose `status` is `PENDING` on the same `pay_line_id`; and it is required whenever such a correction already exists on that line — `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /back-wage-corrections`.
38. The new shift's half-open interval from `start_at` up to but excluding `end_at` does not intersect the interval of any non-`VOIDED` shift of the same `employee_id`; touching endpoints are permitted — `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, 409, on `POST /shifts`.
39. The new period's closed inclusive date interval from `start_date` through `end_date` does not intersect the interval of any existing period of the same `location_id` — `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, 409, on `POST /pay-periods`.
40. The new role assignment's interval, treating a null `effective_to` as unbounded, does not intersect an existing assignment of the same `employee_id` and `job_role_id` — `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP`, 409, on `POST /role-assignments`.
41. `business_date` is not after the logical clock's date, and `end_at` is not after the logical clock's instant — `ERR_SHIFT_FUTURE_DATE`, 422, on `POST /shifts` and `POST /service-charges`.
42. `business_date` falls inside the named period's interval from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive, and both `start_at` and `end_at` fall on `business_date` or on the calendar day immediately after it — `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`, 422, on `POST /shifts`.
43. `end_date` equals `start_date` plus exactly six days, so every period is one seven-day workweek — `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_LENGTH`, 422, on `POST /pay-periods`.
44. The employee has no `separation_date`, or the shift's `business_date` is not after it and a new assignment's `effective_from` is not after it — `ERR_EMPLOYEE_SEPARATED`, 422, on `POST /shifts` and `POST /role-assignments`.
45. `side_work_minutes` does not exceed the shift's whole minutes, and `max_continuous_side_work_minutes` does not exceed `side_work_minutes` — `ERR_SIDE_WORK_EXCEEDS_SHIFT`, 422, on `POST /shifts`.
46. No pool whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` already exists for the same unit and `business_date` — `ERR_POOL_DATE_DUPLICATE`, 409, on `POST /shift-tip-pools`.
47. The employee has no other dispute on the same `pay_line_id` with the same `category` whose `status` is not `CLOSED` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE_CATEGORY`, 409, on `POST /disputes`.
48. `awarded_cents` is required and must be greater than 0 and strictly less than `claimed_amount_cents` when `resolution` is `PARTIALLY_UPHELD`, and must be absent for `UPHELD` and for `DENIED` — `ERR_PARTIAL_AMOUNT_INVALID`, 422, on `POST /disputes/{dispute_id}/adjudicate`.
49. `code` is unique across all job roles — `ERR_DUPLICATE_JOB_ROLE_CODE`, 409, on `POST /job-roles`.
50. `declared_charged_tips_cents` does not exceed the shift's `charged_sales_cents` — `ERR_DECLARED_CHARGED_TIPS_EXCEED_CHARGED_SALES`, 422, on `POST /shifts/{shift_id}/declare-tips`.
51. `charged_sales_cents` does not exceed `gross_sales_cents` on a shift, and a service charge's `amount_cents` is strictly greater than 0 — `ERR_CHARGED_SALES_EXCEED_GROSS_SALES`, 422, on `POST /shifts` and `POST /service-charges`.
52. `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents` is strictly greater than 0, checked both at pool creation and again at distribution — `ERR_EMPTY_POOL_NO_CONTRIBUTIONS`, 422, on `POST /shift-tip-pools` and `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/distribute`.
53. `participant_count` is strictly greater than 0 at distribution — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS`, 422, on `POST /shift-tip-pools/{pool_id}/distribute`.
54. `amount_cents` does not exceed the linked dispute's `adjudicated_amount_cents` when `dispute_id` is supplied, and is strictly greater than 0 — `ERR_CORRECTION_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`, 422, on `POST /back-wage-corrections`.
55. The sum of `amount_cents` over the line's already-`APPLIED` corrections plus this one does not exceed the line's `minimum_wage_obligation_cents` — `ERR_CORRECTION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`, 422, on `POST /back-wage-corrections/{correction_id}/apply`.

### 2.11. Collection Filters and Read Scoping

Because the entity model is flat, every child collection is discovered by filtering its own top-level collection rather than by walking a nested path. All filters are optional, all are exact-match equality on the named field, and multiple filters combine conjunctively — a row must satisfy every supplied filter to appear. A supplied filter value is itself structurally validated before any matching is attempted: a value outside the declared domain of an enum-valued filter, and a value other than `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case for a boolean-valued filter such as `is_management`, are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than quietly matching nothing, while a date-valued filter such as `business_date` is normalised exactly as a date-valued input field is. Alongside `limit` and `offset`, the collections accept the following filters: restaurant units by `state_code`; job roles by `tip_status` and `is_management`; employees by `location_id`; role assignments by `employee_id` and `job_role_id`; tip-pool policies by `location_id` and `status`; pay periods by `location_id` and `status`; shifts by `employee_id`, `location_id`, `job_role_id`, `pay_period_id`, `shift_tip_pool_id`, `business_date`, `time_status` and `declaration_status`; service charges by `location_id`, `allocated_pool_id`, `allocated_pay_period_id`, `business_date`, `status` and `treatment`; tip pools by `location_id`, `tip_pool_policy_id`, `business_date`, `contribution_status` and `distribution_status`; pool-allocation rows by `shift_tip_pool_id`, `employee_id`, `shift_id`, `entry_type` and `business_date`; pay lines by `pay_period_id`, `employee_id`, `status` and `variant`; disputes by `pay_line_id`, `raised_by_employee_id`, `status` and `category`; and back-wage corrections by `pay_line_id`, `dispute_id`, `supersedes_correction_id`, `status` and `reason_code`.

Read access is broad for the three operator roles: a `PayrollAdmin`, a `PayrollPreparer` and a `ComplianceOfficer` may read every collection and every single resource without scoping. The two entity-backed roles are narrowed as described in §2.1 — a `ShiftManager` sees only its own unit's records and an `Employee` sees only its own — and single-resource reads outside that scope on a pay line, a shift or a pool-allocation row are refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. On the list endpoints that narrowing is applied to the filtered rows before the page window is cut and before `total_count` is counted, and which field it reads varies by collection: pay lines and pool-allocation rows are narrowed through their own `employee_id`, resolved to that employee's `location_id` where the actor is a `ShiftManager`; shifts through their own `employee_id` for an `Employee` and their own `location_id` for a `ShiftManager`; disputes through `raised_by_employee_id`; and back-wage corrections through the `employee_id` of the pay line their `pay_line_id` names. The remaining collections — restaurant units, job roles, employees, role assignments, tip-pool policies, pay periods, service charges and tip pools — are narrowed for no role at all.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chain Configuration: Restaurant Units and Job Roles

Setting up the chain begins with registering each restaurant unit. A `PayrollAdmin` registers a unit by supplying its trading `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters, along with the wage and card-fee posture described in §2.4: `state_code`, `state_min_wage_cents`, `cash_wage_cents`, the optional `tip_credit_enabled` flag, `cc_fee_policy`, the conditional `cc_fee_cap_bps`, and `service_charge_treatment`. The 725-cent floor on `state_min_wage_cents` is enforced as a structural bound on the field, so a lower figure is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` on rung 3 rather than with any wage-floor code.

The card-fee posture deserves its own paragraph because it is the one piece of configuration that is purely a creature of state law. `cc_fee_policy` takes one of three values. `PROHIBITED` means the state forbids charging any part of the card processing fee against the guest's tip — no fee may ever be withheld at that unit, regardless of what any individual card batch actually cost. `ACTUAL_ONLY` means the unit may withhold the fee its processor genuinely charged, no more. `CAPPED_BPS` means the state permits a deduction but caps it, and in that case `cc_fee_cap_bps` is required, must be greater than 0, and must lie between 1 and 1000 basis points inclusive. Supplying `cc_fee_cap_bps` under either of the other two policies is just as wrong as omitting it under `CAPPED_BPS`, and both mistakes surface as `ERR_CC_FEE_CAP_REQUIRED`. The 1-to-1000 band is enforced a rung earlier than that pairing check: a `cc_fee_cap_bps` above 1000 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` whatever the policy, whereas a supplied value of exactly 0 clears the band and then fails the `ERR_CC_FEE_CAP_REQUIRED` check. The unit's default posture on banquet money is carried by `service_charge_treatment`, which is either `WAGES` or `TIPS`.

Registration derives three further figures on the unit and nothing else: `applicable_min_wage_cents` and `max_tip_credit_per_hour_cents` per §2.4, and `pending_pool_cents`, which is set to 0 and whose `version` therefore begins at 1. `pending_pool_cents` is a live measure of money in transit — the sum, across every tip pool of that unit whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` and whose `distribution_status` is `PENDING`, of that pool's `total_contributed_cents` plus its `service_charge_tips_cents`. It is money that has already been taken out of employees' shifts but has not yet been paid out to anybody. A pool leaves the sum the instant its `distribution_status` becomes `DISTRIBUTED` and equally the instant either of its dimensions becomes `REVERSED`. A registration whose cash wage breaks whichever bound applies to it is refused with `ERR_CASH_WAGE_BELOW_FLOOR` — the strict bound of at least 213 and strictly below `applicable_min_wage_cents` where `tip_credit_enabled` is true, and the inclusive bound of at least 213 and not above `applicable_min_wage_cents` where it is false — and both regulatory checks are reached only after the request has proved structurally sound — with the cash-wage check evaluated before the card-fee-cap check when a single registration manages to break both.

Job roles are defined chain-wide rather than per unit, because a `SERVER` at one unit is a `SERVER` at all of them. A `PayrollAdmin` defines a role with a `code` — uppercase letters A through Z and the underscore character only, between 2 and 40 characters — and a human-readable `title`. `code` must be unique across every job role in the chain; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JOB_ROLE_CODE`, while a `code` outside that charset or length is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before uniqueness is ever examined. The role's `tip_status` is either `TIPPED`, meaning the role is customarily and regularly tipped, or `NON_TIPPED`. The `is_management` flag, false unless supplied, marks managers and supervisors, who are excluded from every mandatory tip pool in both directions — they may neither contribute to one nor receive from one.

Three numeric role attributes drive the pooling arithmetic later. `pool_contribution_pct_bps` is the mandatory contribution rate in basis points, defaulting to 0 and constrained to the inclusive range 0 through 5000; `pool_share_points` is the relative weight the role carries when the net pool is split, defaulting to 0 and constrained to the inclusive range 0 through 100. A role with `pool_share_points` of 0 contributes but never receives. Both attributes must be 0 when `is_management` is true; a management role supplied with either attribute non-zero, and a value outside either inclusive range, are alike malformed requests refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than with any 422, and that rejection too precedes the uniqueness check on `code`. Finally, `side_work_eligible`, false unless supplied, controls whether shifts worked in this role may record non-tip-producing side-work minutes at all.

One further administrative capability exists purely so that time-dependent behaviour can be exercised deterministically. A `PayrollAdmin` may move the server's logical clock to any UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`; §2.6 describes what that instant then governs. The capability returns no resource payload.

### 3.2. Employees, Job Assignments and the Tip-Credit Notice

A `PayrollAdmin` hires an employee into a unit by supplying that unit's `location_id` together with the employee's `full_name` and `external_payroll_ref`, the identifier by which the upstream payroll system knows this person. Two optional inputs matter later. `monthly_tip_baseline_cents`, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative, carries forward the tips this employee already declared earlier in the current calendar month under a prior pay period; it exists so that the monthly tipped-employee test in §3.8 can look at a whole month rather than a single week. `separation_date` records the day the employee left and is null while they remain employed. Hiring also sets `trailing_pool_receipts_cents` to 0; thereafter it is the running sum of `allocated_cents` over every pool-allocation ledger row naming this employee, counting both `DISTRIBUTION` rows and `REVERSAL` rows, so that a reversed pool leaves it back where it started.

An employee cannot work a shift in a role they do not hold. A `PayrollAdmin` grants the right by recording a role assignment: the `employee_id`, the `job_role_id` being granted, and `effective_from`, the first date on which the assignment authorises shifts. `effective_to` is the last such date and may be omitted, in which case the assignment is open-ended; where it is supplied it may not be before `effective_from`, and a reversed interval is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` ahead of both temporal guards below. `is_primary`, false unless supplied, marks which job is the employee's principal one when they hold more than one.

The single most consequential field on an assignment is `tip_credit_notice_acknowledged_at`, the instant at which the employee acknowledged the written tip-credit notice for that role. It defaults to null, and null means no notice is on file, which forbids any tip credit whatsoever on shifts worked in that role. The notice must also predate the work: an acknowledgement recorded strictly after a shift began does not cover that shift, so a shift is treated as having no notice unless its authorising assignment carries an acknowledgement instant at or before the shift's `start_at`.

Two temporal guards apply when an assignment is recorded, and the overlap guard is reached before the separation guard. First, the new assignment's date interval — treating a null `effective_to` as extending indefinitely — may not intersect the interval of any existing assignment for the same `employee_id` and the same `job_role_id`; an intersection is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP`. Second, if the employee carries a `separation_date`, the new assignment's `effective_from` may not be after it, and a breach is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_SEPARATED`.

The system also derives `dual_job` on each assignment, and this is the field that makes the dual-jobs doctrine visible. `dual_job` is true on an assignment when the same employee holds another assignment whose date interval intersects this one — on the same reading of a null `effective_to` as unbounded that the overlap guard uses — and whose job role carries a different `tip_status` — a bartender who also works mornings as a prep cook, for instance. Recording a new assignment therefore inspects the employee's other overlapping assignments and, where the tipped classifications differ, flips `dual_job` to true on both the new row and the pre-existing row it overlaps. `dual_job` is false by default and on an employee holding a single job it stays false.

### 3.3. Governing the Pool: Drafting and Activating a Policy

How a unit's tips are pooled is not a property of the unit but of a versioned policy over that unit, so that a change in pooling rules leaves the history of already-settled nights intact. A `PayrollAdmin` drafts a policy against a `location_id` with four decisions.

`pool_type` decides who may receive from the pool. `TIPPED_ONLY` admits only shifts whose job role has `tip_status` of `TIPPED`. `ALL_NON_MANAGEMENT` admits every non-management role, back-of-house included, and is lawful only at a unit that claims no tip credit. `contribution_basis` decides what each contributing shift's rate is applied to: `TIPS_RECEIVED_PCT` applies it to the tips the shift actually declared, while `GROSS_SALES_PCT` applies it to the shift's food and beverage sales instead. `share_basis` decides how the net pool is weighted on the way out: `POINTS_ONLY` weights each participant by its role's `pool_share_points` alone, while `HOURS_WEIGHTED_POINTS` weights it by `pool_share_points` multiplied by the shift's scheduled minutes, so a double shift earns twice the share of a single one. `cc_fee_pass_through`, false unless supplied, decides whether the unit's card processing fee is withheld from the pool before it is split.

A drafted policy has `status` `DRAFT` and `activated_at` null. Only one policy per unit may hold `status` `ACTIVE` at a time, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — a retired policy is never revived, only replaced.

Activation is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and is the sole operation in the whole system that advances a unit's `version`; that is precisely what makes the optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7 observable. Activation drives the policy from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` and is guarded by four checks, evaluated in this order. The policy's `status` must be `DRAFT` — activating one already `ACTIVE` or already `SUPERSEDED` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`. Then the supplied `expected_location_version` must equal the unit's stored `version`, failing which the activation is refused with `ERR_STALE_LOCATION_VERSION`. Then, if `pool_type` is `ALL_NON_MANAGEMENT`, the unit's `tip_credit_enabled` must be false, because back-of-house employees may share in a pool only where no tip credit is taken; otherwise `ERR_POOL_TYPE_REQUIRES_NO_TIP_CREDIT`. Then, if `cc_fee_pass_through` is true, the unit's `cc_fee_policy` must not be `PROHIBITED`, because that state forbids deducting processing fees from tips at all; otherwise `ERR_CC_FEE_DEDUCTION_PROHIBITED`.

A successful activation sets a cascade running that reaches five records deep. First, the newly active policy's `activated_at` is stamped with the current logical instant. Second, if another policy at the same `location_id` currently holds `status` `ACTIVE`, that incumbent is driven from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` — a system-driven transition with no client request of its own. Third, retiring the incumbent reaches its pools: every tip pool whose pinned policy is the now-retired one and whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` while its `distribution_status` is still `PENDING` is driven `CONTRIBUTED → REVERSED` on the contribution dimension and `PENDING → REVERSED` on the distribution dimension, with the full unwinding described in §3.7, because an undistributed pool may not be paid out under a retired policy. The fiscal precondition that guards a client-initiated reversal is not evaluated on this server-initiated path: supersession unwinds every such pool unconditionally. Pools of the retired policy that were already `DISTRIBUTED` are deliberately left untouched: they keep their `policy_version_pinned` and stand as the audit record of what was actually paid. Fourth, the unit's `pending_pool_cents` is recomputed from whichever pools survive as `CONTRIBUTED` and `PENDING`. Fifth, the unit's `version` is incremented by exactly 1, so any second activation replayed with the same `expected_location_version` fails.

### 3.4. The Workweek: Opening a Pay Period and Recording Shifts

Payroll at a unit is organised into pay periods, each of which is exactly one seven-day workweek. Either a `PayrollAdmin` or a `PayrollPreparer` opens one by naming the unit's `location_id` and the period's `start_date` and `end_date`. The length rule is exact: `end_date` must equal `start_date` plus exactly six days, so that the closed inclusive interval covers seven calendar days; anything else is refused with `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_LENGTH`. Periods at a single unit may not overlap — the new period's inclusive interval from `start_date` through `end_date` may not intersect the interval of any existing period of the same `location_id` — and an intersection is refused with `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, evaluated before the length rule.

A newly opened period has `status` `OPEN`, `compute_variant` null, `computed_by_user_id` null, `approved_by_user_id` null, and every rollup at 0. Those rollups are the period's reporting surface and each has a single fixed definition. `total_credited_minutes` is the sum of `scheduled_minutes` over the period's shifts whose `time_status` is `CLOSED`. `gross_receipts_cents` is the sum of `gross_sales_cents` over the same set of shifts — the establishment's food and beverage receipts for the week, which is what the reporting test of §3.8 measures tips against. `total_tip_credit_cents` and `total_topup_cents` are the sums of `tip_credit_cents` and of `topup_cents` respectively over the period's pay lines whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, `total_topup_cents` carrying in addition whatever the applied `TIP_CREDIT_INVALID` corrections of §3.9 have added to it since the last compute or approval, and `reported_tips_cents` is likewise the sum of the pay lines' own `reported_tips_cents` over that same non-superseded set. `total_correction_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over back-wage corrections whose `status` is either `PENDING` or `APPLIED` and whose `pay_line_id` names a pay line of this period; a correction that has been `VOIDED` drops out of the sum entirely. `allocation_shortfall_cents` records the amount by which the period's reported tips fell short of the reporting threshold at approval time, and is 0 whenever no allocation was required.

Recording a worked shift is the busiest write in the system and is available to a `ShiftManager` or a `PayrollPreparer`. The shift is bound to four records at once — `employee_id`, `location_id`, `job_role_id` and `pay_period_id` — and the role named by `job_role_id` is what decides the shift's tipped classification, its contribution rate and its share points. Timekeeping is supplied as `business_date`, the operating date the shift is booked to, together with `start_at` and `end_at`. A shift occupies the half-open interval that begins at `start_at` and runs up to but does not include `end_at`, and `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`. From that interval the server derives `scheduled_minutes`, the whole number of minutes between the two instants, with any fraction of a minute discarded rather than rounded — an interval shorter than one whole minute therefore yields a `scheduled_minutes` of 0.

The commercial side of the shift is supplied through three optional money and rate inputs, each defaulting to 0 when omitted: `gross_sales_cents`, the food and beverage sales this shift rang; `charged_sales_cents`, the portion of those sales settled on payment cards, which may not exceed `gross_sales_cents`; and `cc_processing_fee_bps`, the actual card processing fee rate for this shift's card batch in basis points, constrained to the inclusive range 0 through 1000. The fee rate lives on the shift rather than on the unit because a card batch rate genuinely varies by day and by terminal, whereas the statutory cap and the outright prohibition live on the unit because they are state law. Side work is supplied as `side_work_minutes`, the minutes of non-tip-producing work inside the shift, and `max_continuous_side_work_minutes`, the longest uninterrupted block of that work; both default to 0.

A newly recorded shift starts with `time_status` `OPEN` and `declaration_status` `UNDECLARED`, with `shift_tip_pool_id` null and with `declared_cash_tips_cents`, `declared_charged_tips_cents`, `declaration_count`, `non_tip_credit_minutes`, `tip_credit_eligible_minutes`, `pool_contribution_cents`, `retained_tips_cents` and `shift_tip_credit_cents` all at 0. The two status dimensions are genuinely independent: timekeeping advances without regard to whether tips have been declared, and declaration advances without regard to anything but the timekeeping state it requires as a precondition.

Ten checks guard a shift creation, and their relative order matters whenever a single request breaks more than one. The governing period named by `pay_period_id` must be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. Then, if `side_work_minutes` is greater than 0, the shift's job role must have `side_work_eligible` true, failing which `ERR_SIDE_WORK_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Then the employee must hold a role assignment for the requested `job_role_id` whose date interval contains `business_date`, failing which `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT`. Then the shift's `location_id` must equal both the employee's own `location_id` and the period's `location_id`, failing which `ERR_EMPLOYEE_LOCATION_MISMATCH`. Then the shift's half-open interval must not intersect the interval of any non-`VOIDED` shift of the same `employee_id`, though intervals that merely touch at an endpoint are permitted; an intersection is `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Then neither `business_date` may be after the logical clock's date nor `end_at` after the logical clock's instant, failing which `ERR_SHIFT_FUTURE_DATE`. Then `business_date` must fall inside the period's inclusive date interval and both `start_at` and `end_at` must fall on `business_date` itself or on the calendar day immediately after it — an overnight close is expected, a shift two days long is not — failing which `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`. Then, if the employee carries a `separation_date`, `business_date` may not be after it, failing which `ERR_EMPLOYEE_SEPARATED`; a terminated employee's pre-separation history may still be recorded, but no work may be dated past the separation. Then `side_work_minutes` may not exceed the shift's whole minutes and `max_continuous_side_work_minutes` may not exceed `side_work_minutes`, failing which `ERR_SIDE_WORK_EXCEEDS_SHIFT`. Finally `charged_sales_cents` may not exceed `gross_sales_cents`, failing which `ERR_CHARGED_SALES_EXCEED_GROSS_SALES`.

A shift recorded in error is voided by a `PayrollPreparer`, optionally with a free-text `reason`. Voiding is available from either timekeeping state — a shift whose `time_status` is `OPEN` and a shift whose `time_status` is `CLOSED` are both legal targets, and both land in `VOIDED`, which is terminal — and unlike almost every other write on a shift it does not require the owning period to be `OPEN`, precisely because its job is to correct periods that have already moved on. What it does require is that the shift not still be attached to live pooled money: `shift_tip_pool_id` must be null, or the pool it names must already have `contribution_status` `REVERSED`. A shift folded into a pool that has not been reversed is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_ALREADY_POOLED`, and the pool must be reversed first, which as §3.7 explains clears the link anyway. That pooled-money check is the only precondition: the void is not itself guarded on the timekeeping state, so voiding a shift whose `time_status` is already `VOIDED` is accepted rather than refused, re-writing `VOIDED`, re-zeroing the same three figures and advancing the shift's `version`; because such a shift was not `CLOSED` at the moment of the void, that repeat void never drives the owning period back to `OPEN`.

Voiding zeroes the shift's `non_tip_credit_minutes`, `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` and `retained_tips_cents`, then recomputes the owning period's `total_credited_minutes` and `gross_receipts_cents` from whichever shifts survive as `CLOSED`. Voiding a shift that was `CLOSED` carries one further consequence that voiding an `OPEN` shift does not: if the owning period's `status` is `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED`, the void drives that period back to `OPEN` through the server-initiated route described in §3.8, which marks every pay line of the period `SUPERSEDED`. Voiding a closed shift is in this sense the exact compensating action for creating and closing it — after the void, the period's `total_credited_minutes` returns to the value it held before the shift was ever recorded.

### 3.5. Closing a Shift: Side-Work Classification and Tip Declaration

Closing a shift's timekeeping is performed by a `ShiftManager` or a `PayrollPreparer` and advances `time_status` from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`. Two preconditions apply, in this order: the shift's `time_status` must be `OPEN`, so that a shift already `CLOSED` or already `VOIDED` yields `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_OPEN`; and the shift's pay period must still be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. Closing recomputes the period's `total_credited_minutes` and `gross_receipts_cents`, since the shift has only now become one of the period's `CLOSED` shifts.

The substantive work of closing is **side-work classification** — the calculation that decides how many of the shift's minutes may lawfully be paid at the reduced cash wage. It is evaluated against the shift being closed, the job role its `job_role_id` names, the role assignment that authorises it (that is, the assignment of that employee and that job role whose date interval contains `business_date`), and the shift's unit. All of its arithmetic is in whole minutes.

It begins with a disqualifying gate. The shift earns no tip-credit-eligible time at all — meaning `non_tip_credit_minutes` is set equal to `scheduled_minutes` — if any one of three conditions holds: the unit's `tip_credit_enabled` is false; or the job role's `tip_status` is `NON_TIPPED`; or the authorising assignment's `tip_credit_notice_acknowledged_at` is null, or no authorising assignment can be located at all, or the acknowledgement is an instant strictly after the shift's `start_at` — each of which means no written tip-credit notice was on file before the shift began. Any one of the three is sufficient; the gate does not require all three.

If the gate does not fire, two independent limbs are measured. The **percentage limb** implements the eighty-twenty rule: the shift's side-work allowance is twenty percent of `scheduled_minutes`, with any fraction of a minute discarded, and the percentage excess is `side_work_minutes` minus that allowance, or 0 if the subtraction would go negative. The **continuous limb** implements the thirty-minute rule: the continuous excess is `max_continuous_side_work_minutes` minus 30 minutes, or 0 if the subtraction would go negative.

The two limbs are emphatically **not** added together. Whichever limb binds harder wins: `non_tip_credit_minutes` is the greater of the percentage excess and the continuous excess. `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` is then `scheduled_minutes` minus `non_tip_credit_minutes`, and it is the only quantity a tip credit may ever be claimed against. Because the recording-time guards of §3.4 already reject a `max_continuous_side_work_minutes` above `side_work_minutes` and a `side_work_minutes` above the shift's whole minutes, this classification never has to reconcile contradictory inputs; it never clamps, it only measures.

Tip declaration is the other dimension, and it is the one place in the system where an `Employee` writes their own money. An acting `Employee` declares tips on a shift whose `employee_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; a `ShiftManager` may declare on behalf of any staff member at the manager's own unit. Either way the payload carries `declared_cash_tips_cents` and `declared_charged_tips_cents`, the cash and card tips respectively; both are required non-negative integers with no default, so a declaration that omits either — or supplies an explicit `null` for either — is refused on rung 3, and an amendment must restate both figures rather than only the one it is changing.

The declaration dimension moves `UNDECLARED → DECLARED` on the first declaration, `DECLARED → AMENDED` on the second, and thereafter cycles `AMENDED → AMENDED` without bound for as long as the period stays `OPEN` and the shift stays unpooled. Each of the three transitions requires the same two situational preconditions — the shift's `time_status` must be `CLOSED`, failing which `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_CLOSED`, and the shift's period must be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` — and each requires that `declared_charged_tips_cents` not exceed the shift's `charged_sales_cents`, since a card tip cannot exceed the card sale that carried it; a breach is `ERR_DECLARED_CHARGED_TIPS_EXCEED_CHARGED_SALES`. Each of the three transitions writes the two declared amounts, overwriting whatever was there before, increments `declaration_count` by 1 — so a `declaration_count` of 1 marks an original declaration and 2 or more marks amendments — and recomputes `retained_tips_cents`.

Amendment additionally requires that the shift not have been folded into a pool: once `shift_tip_pool_id` is set, the declaration is frozen and any further declaration is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_ALREADY_POOLED`, because the pool has already been computed from the numbers as they stood. That freeze governs the move out of `DECLARED` and the cycle inside `AMENDED` alike. Within the priority ladder the timekeeping check is reached before the pooled check, and both are reached before the card-tip ceiling.

`retained_tips_cents` is the shift's own money after pooling: `declared_cash_tips_cents` plus `declared_charged_tips_cents`, less whatever `pool_contribution_cents` the shift has been debited. While the shift is unpooled its `pool_contribution_cents` is 0, so its retained tips are simply everything it declared.

### 3.6. Mandatory Service Charges: Wages or Tips

A banquet contract's mandatory eighteen percent is not a tip in the legal sense, and how it is treated changes an employee's overtime rate. A `PayrollPreparer` or a `ShiftManager` records one by naming the unit's `location_id`, the `business_date` on which it was collected, and `amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than 0. An optional `description` carries a free-text note naming the banquet or party the charge came from, and an optional `treatment_override` — either `WAGES` or `TIPS` — overrides the unit's default for this single event.

The server resolves and stores `treatment` at recording time: it is `treatment_override` when one was supplied, and the unit's `service_charge_treatment` otherwise. The `treatment_override` field is itself kept on the row alongside the resolved `treatment` and reads back as null where none was supplied, as does an omitted `description`; neither the resolved `treatment` nor the override is ever rewritten afterwards, so a later change to the unit's default leaves already-recorded charges alone. The distinction is the whole point of the entity. A `WAGES` amount is employer revenue paid out to staff as ordinary compensation, and it therefore enters the overtime regular rate, raising the overtime premium. A `TIPS` amount instead joins the shift tip pool for its operating date and is excluded from the regular rate altogether. Mistreating a charge as `TIPS` when it should have been `WAGES` understates the overtime premium, which is exactly the defect the `SERVICE_CHARGE_MISCLASSIFIED` correction reason code exists to repair.

A recorded charge has `status` `RECORDED` with both `allocated_pool_id` and `allocated_pay_period_id` null. Two guards apply at recording, the future-date guard first: `business_date` may not be after the logical clock's date, failing which `ERR_SHIFT_FUTURE_DATE`, and `amount_cents` must be strictly positive, failing which `ERR_CHARGED_SALES_EXCEED_GROSS_SALES`.

A charge is absorbed, not consumed. The server drives it `RECORDED → ALLOCATED` in one of two situations, never by direct client request: a `TIPS`-treated charge is absorbed when a tip pool is created for the same unit and the same `business_date`, and a `WAGES`-treated charge is absorbed when the pay period covering its `business_date` is computed. In the `TIPS` case the absorption requires that such a pool be under creation, stamps `allocated_pool_id` with that pool's identifier, and contributes `amount_cents` to the pool's `service_charge_tips_cents`. In the `WAGES` case the absorption requires that at least one `CLOSED` shift exist at that unit on that `business_date`, stamps `allocated_pay_period_id` with the computing period's identifier, and contributes to the affected employees' `service_charge_wages_cents`. In both cases the charge's `status` must have been `RECORDED` to begin with.

The `WAGES` split is a weighted allocation, and it is performed **independently for each qualifying event** rather than on the pooled total of all of them. The qualifying set for a compute is every service charge of the period's unit whose `status` is `RECORDED`, whose resolved `treatment` is `WAGES`, and whose `business_date` falls inside the period's inclusive interval from `start_date` through `end_date`. For each such event, taken on its own: each `CLOSED` shift on that event's `business_date` carries a weight equal to that shift's `scheduled_minutes`; the total weight is the sum of those weights; each shift's base share is the event's `amount_cents` multiplied by that shift's weight, divided by the total weight, with any fraction discarded; the leftover is `amount_cents` less the sum of the base shares; and one extra cent goes to each of the first however-many shifts the leftover permits, taken in descending order of weight and, where two shifts carry the same weight, in ascending order of their employees' `created_at`, then of the order in which those employees were written, then of the shifts' own `created_at` and write order. Each shift's resulting share accrues to the `service_charge_wages_cents` of that shift's employee's pay line. Where every `CLOSED` shift of that event's `business_date` carries a `scheduled_minutes` of 0 the total weight is 0, so by the degenerate rule of §2.5 every share is 0 and none of the event's money reaches a pay line; the event is nevertheless driven to `ALLOCATED` with its `allocated_pay_period_id` stamped. Management shifts **are** included in this split, in deliberate contrast to the tip-pool split of §3.7: a service charge treated as wages is ordinary compensation and a manager may lawfully receive it. A qualifying event whose `business_date` carries no `CLOSED` shift at all cannot be allocated, and the compute is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_CHARGE_UNALLOCATABLE` rather than silently dropping the money.

Voiding a service charge is reserved to a `PayrollPreparer`, takes an optional `reason`, and is legal from either `RECORDED` or `ALLOCATED`, both landing in the terminal `VOIDED`. One precondition constrains it: if the charge has already been absorbed into a pool — that is, if `allocated_pool_id` is not null — that pool's `distribution_status` must still be `PENDING`, failing which the void is refused with `ERR_POOL_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`, because money already paid out cannot be pulled back through a service-charge void. Every void clears both `allocated_pool_id` and `allocated_pay_period_id`. Voiding a charge that was still `RECORDED` does nothing further, since nothing had absorbed it, and voiding one that is already `VOIDED` is accepted on exactly that footing rather than refused — its `allocated_pool_id` is null by then, so the precondition passes, the row is re-stamped `VOIDED` with its `version` advanced, and no unwinding runs. Voiding a charge that was `ALLOCATED` unwinds whichever absorption occurred: if `allocated_pool_id` was set, `amount_cents` is subtracted from that pool's `service_charge_tips_cents` and from its `net_pool_cents`, and the unit's `pending_pool_cents` is then recomputed; if `allocated_pay_period_id` was set, that period is driven back to `OPEN` by the server, which marks every pay line of the period `SUPERSEDED`.

### 3.7. Shift Tip Pools: Contribution, Card Fees, Distribution and Reversal

A tip pool is computed once per operating date per unit. A `PayrollPreparer` builds one by naming only the `location_id` and the `business_date`; everything else about the pool is derived. The pool immediately pins the unit's governing policy: `tip_pool_policy_id` records which policy was `ACTIVE` at creation time and `policy_version_pinned` records the `version` that policy carried at that moment. Distribution later re-reads the pinned policy row and never a newer one, so a policy activated between creation and distribution cannot retroactively change how an already-computed pool is split.

Six checks guard pool creation, in this order. The period covering `business_date` at that unit must be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` — which is equally the answer where that unit has no period covering that `business_date` at all, since there is then no open period to build the pool inside. Then no shift whose job role is a management role may contribute to or receive from the pool, so a pool that would be built solely from management shifts on either side is refused with `ERR_MANAGER_POOL_PARTICIPATION`. That single check stands for both directions and is made over the unit's shifts of that `business_date` whose `time_status` is `CLOSED` and whose `shift_tip_pool_id` is still null — a set taken before any policy is pinned, and therefore without regard to declaration status, contribution rate, share points or tipped classification — and it fires only where that set is non-empty and every shift in it carries a management job role; a date with no such shift at all falls straight through to the checks below. Then the unit must have exactly one policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` to pin, failing which `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POOL_POLICY`. Then every `CLOSED` shift at that unit on that `business_date` must have `declaration_status` of either `DECLARED` or `AMENDED` — an undeclared closed shift would make the pool wrong — failing which `ERR_UNDECLARED_SHIFT_IN_POOL`. Then no pool whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` may already exist for the same unit and `business_date`, failing which `ERR_POOL_DATE_DUPLICATE`; note that a pool whose contribution side has been reversed does not block a fresh one, which is what makes reversal followed by rebuild a usable correction path. Finally the pool must hold money: `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents` must be strictly greater than 0, failing which `ERR_EMPTY_POOL_NO_CONTRIBUTIONS`.

**Contribution.** The shifts that feed the pool are those at the requested unit and `business_date` whose `time_status` is `CLOSED`, whose `declaration_status` is `DECLARED` or `AMENDED`, and whose `shift_tip_pool_id` is still null, and whose job role is not a management role and carries either a `pool_contribution_pct_bps` greater than 0 or a `pool_share_points` greater than 0; and additionally, where the pinned policy's `pool_type` is `TIPPED_ONLY`, whose job role's `tip_status` is `TIPPED`. A role carrying `pool_share_points` above 0 but a `pool_contribution_pct_bps` of 0 — the classic busser — is inside that input set and is stamped into the pool with a contribution of 0, because it must still be recognised as a participant when the pool is paid out. Management shifts are excluded from the input set unconditionally.

For each shift in that set the server computes the tips it received as `declared_cash_tips_cents` plus `declared_charged_tips_cents`. The basis the contribution rate applies to depends on the pinned policy's `contribution_basis`: under `TIPS_RECEIVED_PCT` the basis is those tips received; under `GROSS_SALES_PCT` the basis is the shift's `gross_sales_cents` instead. The raw contribution is the job role's `pool_contribution_pct_bps` applied to that basis as a basis-point rate — that is, the basis multiplied by the rate and divided by ten thousand — rounded half up to a whole cent. The shift's actual contribution is the lesser of that raw figure and the tips the shift received, so a shift never contributes more money than it holds; that ceiling bites in practice under `GROSS_SALES_PCT`, where a heavy-sales, light-tips shift could otherwise be asked for more than it took in.

Each contributing shift then has its `pool_contribution_cents` set to that contribution, its `shift_tip_pool_id` stamped with the new pool, and its `retained_tips_cents` reset to the tips it received less the contribution. On the pool itself, `total_contributed_cents` becomes the sum of the contributions across the contributing shifts and `contributing_shift_count` becomes the number of those shifts whose contribution came out greater than 0 — a stamped-in busser with a contribution of 0 is therefore not counted there. Creation also absorbs the date's `TIPS`-treated service charges into `service_charge_tips_cents` as described in §3.6 — only those whose `status` is still `RECORDED`, so a charge an earlier pool of that date already absorbed is not absorbed a second time even where that pool was later reversed and left the charge `ALLOCATED` — sets `net_pool_cents` to `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents`, no card fee having been deducted yet, leaves `cc_fee_deducted_cents`, `distributed_cents`, `participant_count` and `total_share_weight` at 0 with `distributed_at` and `reversed_at` null, and recomputes the unit's `pending_pool_cents` — which the new pool now joins, since it is `CONTRIBUTED` and `PENDING`.

**The card-fee deduction.** Distribution is performed by a `PayrollPreparer` and begins by deciding how much of the card processing fee may lawfully be withheld from the pool. If the pinned policy's `cc_fee_pass_through` is false, or the unit's `cc_fee_policy` is `PROHIBITED`, then `cc_fee_deducted_cents` is 0 and the fee calculation stops there. Otherwise a fee is computed for each contributing shift — that is, each shift of the pool whose `pool_contribution_cents` is greater than 0 — according to the unit's policy. Under `ACTUAL_ONLY`, a shift's fee is that shift's `cc_processing_fee_bps` applied as a basis-point rate to that shift's `declared_charged_tips_cents`, rounded half up. Under `CAPPED_BPS`, the effective rate for a shift is the lesser of that shift's `cc_processing_fee_bps` and the unit's `cc_fee_cap_bps`, and the shift's fee is that effective rate applied to the same `declared_charged_tips_cents`, rounded half up. Under `PROHIBITED`, every shift's fee is 0 whatever its `cc_processing_fee_bps` says.

The evaluation order of that sum is pinned and is not an implementation detail. Each shift's fee is rounded half up on its own, and `cc_fee_deducted_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-shift figures. It is emphatically not computed by summing the unrounded per-shift products and rounding the total once; the two orders can differ by as much as one cent fewer than `contributing_shift_count`. One final ceiling applies: the fee can never exceed the money actually in the pool, so `cc_fee_deducted_cents` is capped at `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents`.

**The split.** With the fee settled, `net_pool_cents` is `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents` less `cc_fee_deducted_cents`. The participants — the shifts eligible to receive — are those whose `shift_tip_pool_id` names this pool, whose `time_status` is `CLOSED`, whose job role is not a management role and carries a `pool_share_points` greater than 0, and, where the pinned policy's `pool_type` is `TIPPED_ONLY`, whose job role's `tip_status` is `TIPPED`. `participant_count` is the number of such shifts.

Each participant's `share_weight` follows the pinned policy's `share_basis`. Under `POINTS_ONLY` it is simply the job role's `pool_share_points`. Under `HOURS_WEIGHTED_POINTS` it is `pool_share_points` multiplied by the shift's `scheduled_minutes`, giving an exact integer count of points-minutes; no division is taken, so no rounding can enter the weight. `total_share_weight` is the sum of the participants' weights. Note that the weight uses `scheduled_minutes` and **not** `tip_credit_eligible_minutes`: a side-work overage shrinks a worker's tip credit without also shrinking that worker's pool share, and a pool at a unit that claims no tip credit still has non-zero weights.

Each participant's base share is `net_pool_cents` multiplied by that participant's `share_weight`, divided by `total_share_weight`, with any fraction discarded. The leftover is `net_pool_cents` less the sum of the base shares, and because every base share was floored the leftover is strictly smaller than `participant_count`. Exactly one extra cent goes to each of the first however-many participants the leftover permits, taken in descending order of `share_weight` and, where two participants tie on weight, in ascending order of their shifts' `created_at`. The tie-break is the shift's creation timestamp and never the shift's `id`: opaque identifiers must not decide who receives money. Where two participants share a weight and a `created_at` alike — which the standing logical clock of §2.6 makes ordinary — the cent goes to whichever shift was written first, as §2.3 breaks such ties everywhere else. A participant that wins a leftover cent has `remainder_cent_awarded` set true on its ledger row; every other row carries false.

Distribution appends exactly one pool-allocation ledger row per participant, each carrying its `shift_tip_pool_id`, the receiving `employee_id`, the earning `shift_id`, an `entry_type` of `DISTRIBUTION`, the participant's `share_weight`, its `allocated_cents` — the base share plus the leftover cent where won — its `remainder_cent_awarded` flag, and a copy of the pool's `business_date`, carried on the row so that allocations can be read and filtered without joining back to the pool. The pool's `distributed_cents` becomes the sum of the rows' `allocated_cents`, which equals `net_pool_cents` exactly — save in the degenerate case of §2.5 where every participant's `share_weight` comes out 0, when every row carries an `allocated_cents` of 0 and `distributed_cents` is 0 while `net_pool_cents` stands as computed. `distributed_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, `distribution_status` moves `PENDING → DISTRIBUTED`, the unit's `pending_pool_cents` is recomputed with this pool now dropped from the pending sum, each recipient employee's `trailing_pool_receipts_cents` is recomputed over every ledger row naming that employee, and each recipient's credit will raise that employee's `pool_tips_cents` at the next compute. Distribution touches neither the pool's `total_contributed_cents` nor `service_charge_tips_cents`, and it leaves the covering pay period's own rollups alone.

Four checks guard a distribution, in this order: the pool's `distribution_status` must be `PENDING`, so distributing a pool already `DISTRIBUTED` or already `REVERSED` yields `ERR_POOL_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`; the pool's `contribution_status` must be `CONTRIBUTED`, failing which `ERR_POOL_REVERSED`; `total_contributed_cents` plus `service_charge_tips_cents` must be strictly greater than 0, failing which `ERR_EMPTY_POOL_NO_CONTRIBUTIONS`; and `participant_count` must be strictly greater than 0, failing which `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PARTICIPANTS` — that count being taken from the participant set computed live for this distribution, since the pool's stored `participant_count` is still 0 until this distribution writes it. That last check is what stops money being stranded in a pool that has contributors but nobody eligible to receive.

**Reversal.** Reversing a pool is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, takes an optional `reason`, and is the compensating action for creating and distributing one. It is also raised by the server itself, without any client request, when the pool's pinned policy is superseded while the pool is still `PENDING`, as §3.3 describes. Reversal first requires that `contribution_status` still be `CONTRIBUTED`, failing which `ERR_POOL_REVERSED`; the pool's `distribution_status` is not examined at all, since both `PENDING` and `DISTRIBUTED` are legal starting points. Only once that state check has passed is the single further precondition evaluated, and it is a fiscal one: no pay period covering the pool's `business_date` at the pool's unit may be `APPROVED` while carrying a pay line with an already-`APPLIED` back-wage correction, because that money has been paid and settled. A reversal that breaks it is refused with `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409), which therefore sits a rung below the `ERR_POOL_REVERSED` check rather than at rung 12.

Reversal drives both status dimensions in a single request, and it reaches five records deep. First, `contribution_status` moves `CONTRIBUTED → REVERSED` and `reversed_at` is stamped with the current logical instant; `total_contributed_cents`, `service_charge_tips_cents`, `cc_fee_deducted_cents` and `net_pool_cents` all become 0 as a consequence, while `participant_count`, `total_share_weight` and `distributed_at` are deliberately left exactly as the distribution wrote them, as the record of who was paid and when. Second, the distribution dimension is unwound, and here the two starting points differ in what work they require. A pool whose `distribution_status` was `DISTRIBUTED` moves `DISTRIBUTED → REVERSED`, and for each `DISTRIBUTION` row of the pool the server appends one further ledger row whose `entry_type` is `REVERSAL` and whose `allocated_cents` is the exact negation of that `DISTRIBUTION` row's amount, copying that row's `shift_tip_pool_id`, `employee_id`, `shift_id`, `share_weight`, `remainder_cent_awarded` and `business_date` across unchanged; the original `DISTRIBUTION` rows themselves are never mutated, so every recipient's net sum of `allocated_cents` for this pool returns to 0 and `distributed_cents` returns to 0 with it. A pool whose `distribution_status` was still `PENDING` moves `PENDING → REVERSED` and appends no ledger rows at all, because there are no `DISTRIBUTION` rows to negate; only the contribution side needs unwinding. Both starting points land in the terminal `REVERSED`. Third, every shift whose `shift_tip_pool_id` names this pool has that link cleared back to null, its `pool_contribution_cents` zeroed, and its `retained_tips_cents` restored to `declared_cash_tips_cents` plus `declared_charged_tips_cents` — the shift is whole again and free to be declared, pooled or voided. Fourth, for every pay period containing one of those shifts, if that period's `status` is `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED` it is driven back to `OPEN` by the server, which marks every pay line of that period `SUPERSEDED`. Fifth, the unit's `pending_pool_cents` is recomputed over that unit's surviving `CONTRIBUTED` and `PENDING` pools, and each affected period's `total_credited_minutes` and `gross_receipts_cents` are recomputed from its surviving `CLOSED` shifts.

The invariant that reversal preserves is worth stating plainly, because it is the test of a correct implementation: at every quiescent moment, the sum of all tips declared on a unit's shifts equals the sum of those shifts' `retained_tips_cents` plus the pool's `cc_fee_deducted_cents` plus its `net_pool_cents`. That identity holds before a pool is created and holds again after it is reversed. Note also that the two status dimensions are independent in one direction only: distribution requires `contribution_status` to be `CONTRIBUTED`, whereas reversal drives both dimensions to `REVERSED` together.

### 3.8. The Payroll Cycle: Computing, Approving and Reopening a Period

**Computing.** A `PayrollPreparer` computes a pay period, moving it `OPEN → COMPUTED`. Four preconditions apply, in this order. The period's `status` must be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. Then, for every pairing of unit and operating date that has at least one `CLOSED` shift of this period, if that unit holds a policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` then a pool for that pairing must exist whose `contribution_status` is `CONTRIBUTED` and whose `distribution_status` is `DISTRIBUTED`; a night that should have been pooled but was not yields `ERR_POOL_NOT_DISTRIBUTED`. Then every `CLOSED` shift of the period must carry `declaration_status` of `DECLARED` or `AMENDED`, failing which `ERR_UNDECLARED_SHIFT_IN_PERIOD`. Then every service charge of this unit whose `status` is `RECORDED`, whose resolved `treatment` is `WAGES` and whose `business_date` falls inside the period must have at least one `CLOSED` shift on that `business_date` to allocate against, failing which `ERR_SERVICE_CHARGE_UNALLOCATABLE`.

A compute does five things. It marks every pre-existing pay line of the period whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL` as `SUPERSEDED` **before** inserting the new set, so that a recompute never leaves two live lines for one employee. It then creates exactly one pay line per employee holding at least one `CLOSED` shift in the period, written in ascending order of those employees' own `created_at` so that the new lines list in that order, each with `variant` `WEEKLY_AGGREGATE` and `status` `DRAFT`, populating it by running the service-charge wages allocation of §3.6 first and the wage computation below second — that order matters, because the wage computation reads the service-charge figure the allocation writes. It drives every `RECORDED`, `WAGES`-treated service charge inside the period from `RECORDED` to `ALLOCATED` and stamps each one's `allocated_pay_period_id`. It sets the period's `compute_variant` to `WEEKLY_AGGREGATE` and its `computed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`. And it recomputes the period's `total_credited_minutes`, `total_tip_credit_cents`, `total_topup_cents`, `gross_receipts_cents` and `reported_tips_cents` — and those five alone, so a `total_correction_cents` left standing by an earlier cycle is carried across untouched. A period holding no `CLOSED` shift at all clears all four preconditions vacuously and computes successfully, producing no pay line whatsoever and leaving each of those five rollups at 0.

**The wage computation.** This is the heart of the system. For a given employee it reads every shift of the pay period belonging to that employee whose `time_status` is `CLOSED`, the pool-allocation rows naming that employee within the period, and that employee's `service_charge_wages_cents` as already written by the service-charge allocation. Its arithmetic is in cents, rounded half up, with each rate band rounded once and the rounded bands then summed. **The evaluation order below is pinned: the steps run in exactly this sequence and any reordering changes the result.** Five constants participate: the federal minimum wage of 725 cents per hour, the federal cash-wage floor of 213 cents per hour, a monthly tipped-employee threshold of 3000 cents — thirty dollars a month — an overtime threshold of forty hours, which is 2,400 minutes, an overtime premium multiplier of one half, and an overtime obligation multiplier of one and one half.

*Step 0 — the tipped-employee test.* `tipped_employee_qualified` is true only if all three of the following hold: the unit's `tip_credit_enabled` is true; at least one `CLOSED` shift of the period has a `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` greater than 0, which is the evidence that a valid pre-shift notice was on file; and the employee's `monthly_tip_baseline_cents`, plus the sum over the period's `CLOSED` shifts of `declared_cash_tips_cents` plus `declared_charged_tips_cents`, reaches at least the monthly threshold of 3000 cents. If `tipped_employee_qualified` comes out false, `tip_credit_cents` is 0 for the whole line.

*Step 1 — minutes.* `total_minutes` is the sum of the shifts' `scheduled_minutes`. `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` is the sum of the shifts' own `tip_credit_eligible_minutes`, except that it is forced to 0 when `tipped_employee_qualified` is false. `non_tip_credit_minutes` is `total_minutes` less `tip_credit_eligible_minutes`. `overtime_minutes` is `total_minutes` less the 2,400-minute overtime threshold, or 0 if that subtraction would go negative. `regular_minutes` is `total_minutes` less `overtime_minutes`.

*Step 2 — straight cash.* `straight_cash_cents` is built from two rate bands, each rounded half up on its own and then added: the unit's `cash_wage_cents` applied to `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` converted to hours by dividing by sixty, plus the unit's `applicable_min_wage_cents` applied to `non_tip_credit_minutes` likewise converted to hours. Rounding a single blended rate instead is a different and incorrect answer.

*Step 3 — service-charge wages.* `service_charge_wages_cents` is taken as already written by the allocation of §3.6. Tips and `TIPS`-treated service charges never enter it.

*Step 4 — the regular rate.* `regular_rate_cents_per_hour` is `straight_cash_cents` plus `service_charge_wages_cents`, divided by the number of hours worked — that is, by `total_minutes` divided by sixty — rounded half up, evaluated as the single exact quotient of `(straight_cash_cents + service_charge_wages_cents) × 60` by `total_minutes`. Where `total_minutes` is 0 the rate is 0 rather than an error, and the overtime premium of the next step is 0 with it. Tips, `TIPS`-treated service charges, the tip credit and the top-up are all excluded from this rate; the top-up is excluded deliberately, because including a figure that is itself derived from the rate would make the formula self-referential.

*Step 5 — the overtime premium.* `overtime_premium_cents` is one half of `regular_rate_cents_per_hour`, multiplied by `overtime_minutes` divided by sixty, rounded half up — one exact quotient, `regular_rate_cents_per_hour × overtime_minutes` divided by 120, rounded once at the end, with the rate never halved to a whole cent first. It is a half-time premium because the straight-time component of those overtime hours is already inside `straight_cash_cents`.

*Step 6 — the minimum-wage obligation.* `minimum_wage_obligation_cents` is again two bands, each rounded half up and then summed: the unit's `applicable_min_wage_cents` applied to `regular_minutes` divided by sixty, plus one and one half times that same `applicable_min_wage_cents` applied to `overtime_minutes` divided by sixty. That overtime band is likewise one exact quotient — `3 × applicable_min_wage_cents × overtime_minutes` divided by 120 — rounded half up once, so the one-and-a-half-times rate is never itself rounded to a whole cent before the minutes are applied.

*Step 7 — the three-way tip-credit cap.* `retained_tips_cents` on the line is the sum of the shifts' `retained_tips_cents`. `pool_tips_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` over the pool-allocation rows naming this employee whose `business_date` falls inside the period, counting `DISTRIBUTION` rows positively and `REVERSAL` rows negatively — so a reversed pool nets out to nothing. Each such row is attributed to the shift its `shift_id` names, and only rows naming one of the employee's `CLOSED` shifts of this period are picked up. `tips_available_cents` is `retained_tips_cents` plus `pool_tips_cents`. The per-hour ceiling on the credit is the unit's `max_tip_credit_per_hour_cents` applied to `tip_credit_eligible_minutes` divided by sixty, rounded half up. The residual is `minimum_wage_obligation_cents` less `straight_cash_cents`, less `service_charge_wages_cents`, less `overtime_premium_cents`. `tip_credit_cents` is then the smallest of those three figures — the per-hour ceiling, the tips actually available, and the residual — floored at 0 so it can never be negative. The credit is capped three ways at once and which of the three binds varies from one week to the next.

*Step 8 — the top-up.* `topup_cents` is `minimum_wage_obligation_cents` less `straight_cash_cents`, less `service_charge_wages_cents`, less `overtime_premium_cents`, less `tip_credit_cents`, floored at 0. It is the cash the employer must add so that the line reaches its minimum-wage obligation.

*Step 9 — the totals.* `total_gross_cents` is `straight_cash_cents` plus `service_charge_wages_cents` plus `overtime_premium_cents` plus `topup_cents` plus `correction_applied_cents`. `reported_tips_cents` is `retained_tips_cents` plus `pool_tips_cents`, and is the figure the reporting test measures.

**The two variants.** Steps 0 through 5 and step 9 are identical in both variants; steps 6, 7 and 8 are where they diverge, and the divergence is real money rather than a rounding artefact. Under `WEEKLY_AGGREGATE`, which is the variant a compute produces, steps 6 through 8 run **once** across the employee's whole workweek: a single obligation, a single three-way cap and a single top-up, all derived from the summed minutes and the summed tips. Under `PER_SHIFT`, which is the variant an approval produces, steps 6 through 8 run **separately for each `CLOSED` shift**, each using only that shift's minutes and only that shift's own tips, and the per-shift results are summed into the line. Each shift's own `regular_minutes` and `overtime_minutes` are re-derived from that shift's `scheduled_minutes` against the same 2,400-minute threshold — so a shift shorter than forty hours carries no overtime minutes of its own — and that shift's straight-cash bands, minimum-wage obligation and per-hour credit ceiling are all rebuilt from its own minutes, while `tipped_employee_qualified` stays the single period-level verdict of step 0 and forces that shift's eligible minutes to 0 when it is false. Only `tip_credit_cents`, `topup_cents` and the `total_gross_cents` they feed are overwritten on the line: the per-shift obligations and minute splits are never written back, so the line keeps the `minimum_wage_obligation_cents`, `regular_minutes`, `overtime_minutes`, `retained_tips_cents`, `pool_tips_cents` and `tips_available_cents` that the whole-week steps produced. Because the period-level `service_charge_wages_cents` and `overtime_premium_cents` cannot be attributed to any one shift, they are subtracted from the residual cap of the earliest shift only, taken in ascending order of `business_date` and, within a date, ascending order of `created_at`. The per-shift credit is additionally written back onto each shift as `shift_tip_credit_cents`, which stays 0 until the owning period is approved. Aggregating lets a heavy-tip shift subsidise a light-tip shift; the per-shift reading forbids that, and the two variants therefore produce different `tip_credit_cents` and `topup_cents` on the same fixture.

**Approving.** A `ComplianceOfficer` approves a period, moving it `COMPUTED → APPROVED`. Two preconditions apply in order, the segregation-of-duties comparison first as rung 7 of §2.10 requires: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the period's `computed_by_user_id`, failing which `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the period's `status` must be `COMPUTED`, failing which `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_COMPUTED`. Because a period that is `OPEN` carries a null `computed_by_user_id`, which no supplied `X-User-ID` can equal, approving an `OPEN` period still answers `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_COMPUTED`. Approval recomputes every non-`SUPERSEDED` pay line of the period under the `PER_SHIFT` variant, overwriting `tip_credit_cents`, `topup_cents` and `total_gross_cents`; runs the reporting test below, writing each line's `allocated_tips_cents` and the period's `allocation_shortfall_cents`; moves every non-`SUPERSEDED` pay line from `DRAFT` to `FINAL`; sets the period's `compute_variant` to `PER_SHIFT` and its `approved_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`; and recomputes the period's `total_tip_credit_cents` and `total_topup_cents` from the new `PER_SHIFT` figures.

**The reporting test and allocated tips.** At approval the establishment's tip reporting is measured against eight percent of its receipts. The threshold is eight percent of the period's `gross_receipts_cents`, rounded half up. The period's `reported_tips_cents` is the sum of `reported_tips_cents` over the period's non-`SUPERSEDED` pay lines. The comparison is **strictly less than**: if reported tips are strictly below the threshold, `allocation_shortfall_cents` is the threshold less the reported tips; if reported tips equal or exceed the threshold, `allocation_shortfall_cents` is 0 and every line's `allocated_tips_cents` is 0. Where a shortfall exists it is split across the **directly tipped** lines only — those whose employee worked at least one `CLOSED` shift on a job role whose `tip_status` is `TIPPED` and whose `is_management` is false — pro rata by each employee's summed `gross_sales_cents` across their shifts. Each such line's base share is the shortfall multiplied by that employee's sales, divided by the total sales of all such employees, with any fraction discarded; the leftover cents go out one each in descending order of that employee's sales and, on a tie, ascending order of the employee's `created_at` and then of the order in which those employees were written. Where no line qualifies as directly tipped, or where every qualifying employee's summed sales is 0, the period still records the shortfall in `allocation_shortfall_cents` while every line's `allocated_tips_cents` stays 0. Allocated tips are a reporting figure and nothing more: they are written to `allocated_tips_cents`, are never added to `total_gross_cents`, and never enlarge `tips_available_cents` for the purpose of the tip credit.

**Reopening.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may drive a period back to `OPEN` so that its inputs can be corrected, optionally supplying a `reason`. Both `COMPUTED → OPEN` and `APPROVED → OPEN` are legal and both carry an identical guard and an identical side-effect set. The guards, in order: the period's `status` must be `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED`, so reopening a period that is already `OPEN` yields `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_NOT_COMPUTED`; and no back-wage correction whose `status` is `PENDING` may exist on any pay line of the period, failing which `ERR_PENDING_CORRECTION_EXISTS` — an unresolved correction must be applied or superseded before the numbers underneath it are torn down. Reopening marks every pay line of the period whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL` as `SUPERSEDED`; sets `compute_variant`, `computed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` all back to null and `allocation_shortfall_cents` back to 0; zeroes `total_tip_credit_cents`, `total_topup_cents` and `reported_tips_cents`; and releases every service charge whose `allocated_pay_period_id` names this period from `ALLOCATED` back to `RECORDED`, clearing `allocated_pay_period_id` to null so the charge is available to be absorbed again by the next compute. It leaves the period's `total_correction_cents` where it stood, and it leaves each shift's `shift_tip_credit_cents` exactly as the approval wrote it rather than zeroing it. Reopening is what makes the period machine cyclic — `OPEN → COMPUTED → APPROVED → OPEN` — and the second compute may legitimately produce different numbers, because the shift inputs changed in between.

**Server-initiated reopening.** The same return to `OPEN` also happens without any client request on the period, from `COMPUTED` and from `APPROVED` alike, whenever a cascade mutates one of the period's inputs — a reversed pool, a voided shift, or a voided service charge. The two server-initiated transitions carry an identical side-effect set to each other: every pay line of the period whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL` is marked `SUPERSEDED`; `compute_variant`, `computed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` are set to null; `total_tip_credit_cents`, `total_topup_cents`, `allocation_shortfall_cents` and `reported_tips_cents` are zeroed; and `total_credited_minutes` and `gross_receipts_cents` are then recomputed from whichever shifts survive as `CLOSED`. That set differs from the client-initiated reopen's in two respects, both deliberate: the server-initiated route recomputes those two shift rollups, which the client route does not, and it does **not** release the period's `ALLOCATED` service charges, each of which keeps its `status` and its `allocated_pay_period_id`.

**The pay line's own lifecycle.** A pay line is born `DRAFT` at compute. Approval drives it `DRAFT → FINAL`: its `variant` is switched from `WEEKLY_AGGREGATE` to `PER_SHIFT`, its wage figures are recomputed under that variant, its `allocated_tips_cents` is written by the reporting test, and its `version` is incremented by 1. From either `DRAFT` or `FINAL` a line goes to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` when the owning period re-enters `OPEN` or when a fresh compute replaces the pay-line set; in both cases the line's `version` is incremented by 1, which invalidates any `expected_pay_line_version` a client was holding. A line superseded out of `FINAL` carries its `pending_correction_cents` and `correction_applied_cents` across unchanged, so that the audit trail of what was owed and what was paid survives the supersession. One further figure on a line moves without the line's `version` moving at all: `open_dispute_count`, the number of disputes on the line whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, rises when a dispute is raised and falls when one is closed, and because no money moves in either event the `version` is deliberately left alone even though the line's `updated_at` is still refreshed. `pending_correction_cents` and `correction_applied_cents` are the sums of `amount_cents` over the line's corrections whose `status` is `PENDING` and whose `status` is `APPLIED` respectively.

### 3.9. When the Numbers Are Wrong: Disputes and Back-Wage Corrections

An employee who believes a settled paycheck understates what they earned raises a dispute. Only an `Employee` may raise one, and only against their own money: the payload names the `pay_line_id` being challenged, the `raised_by_employee_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` — the `category` of computation being challenged, the `claimed_amount_cents` the employee says was underpaid, which must be strictly greater than 0 — a supplied 0 clears the non-negative bound of rung 3 and is then refused on that same rung with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than with any 422 — and an optional `narrative` giving the employee's account of the alleged error, which reads back as null where none was supplied. `category` is one of `POOL_SHARE`, `TIP_CREDIT`, `CC_FEE` or `SERVICE_CHARGE`, naming which of the four computations is in question.

Four checks guard a dispute, in this order. The acting employee must own the line, meaning both that `raised_by_employee_id` equals `X-User-ID` and that the line's own `employee_id` equals `X-User-ID`, failing which `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Then the line's `status` must be `FINAL` — a `DRAFT` line has not been approved and a `SUPERSEDED` line no longer represents anything — failing which `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FINAL`. Then `raised_by_employee_id` must match the disputed line's `employee_id`, failing which `ERR_EMPLOYEE_LOCATION_MISMATCH`. Then the employee must have no other dispute on the same `pay_line_id` with the same `category` whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`, failing which `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE_CATEGORY`; one open challenge per computation per line is the limit.

A newly raised dispute has `status` `OPEN`, `adjudicated_amount_cents` 0, `adjudicated_by_user_id` null and `closed_at` null. Raising it increments the line's `open_dispute_count` but deliberately does not increment the line's `version`, because no money has moved.

**Adjudication** is the branching heart of this workflow and is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`. The payload carries `resolution`, which selects the branch; an `awarded_cents` amount, whose presence or absence is dictated by the branch; and an optional `reason_code` naming the regulatory ground, which is carried onto the back-wage correction an award-bearing branch creates. Where `reason_code` is omitted that correction takes the reason code matching the dispute's own `category` — `POOL_SHARE` to `POOL_SHARE_SHORTFALL`, `TIP_CREDIT` to `TIP_CREDIT_INVALID`, `CC_FEE` to `CC_FEE_OVERDEDUCTION` and `SERVICE_CHARGE` to `SERVICE_CHARGE_MISCLASSIFIED` — and on the `DENIED` branch, which creates no correction at all, a supplied `reason_code` is accepted and simply goes unused. Three preconditions are common to all three branches, in this order: the dispute's `status` must be `OPEN`, so adjudicating one already adjudicated or already closed yields `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_OPEN`; the disputed line's `status` must still be `FINAL`, failing which `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FINAL`; and `awarded_cents` must satisfy the branch's own rule, failing which `ERR_PARTIAL_AMOUNT_INVALID`. All three branches stamp `adjudicated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. The arithmetic itself involves exact integers and no rounding whatsoever.

The `OPEN → UPHELD` branch grants the claim in full. `awarded_cents` must be absent from the payload, and `adjudicated_amount_cents` is set equal to `claimed_amount_cents`. Because the award is positive, the branch then runs a four-hop cascade: it creates exactly one back-wage correction with `status` `PENDING`, its `dispute_id` naming this dispute and its `amount_cents` equal to `adjudicated_amount_cents`; it drives any pre-existing `PENDING` correction on the same pay line from `PENDING` to `VOIDED`, with the new correction's `supersedes_correction_id` naming the one it displaced; it sets the pay line's `pending_correction_cents` to the new correction's amount and increments that line's `version` by 1; and it recomputes the owning period's `total_correction_cents` from the `PENDING` and `APPLIED` corrections across all of that period's pay lines.

The `OPEN → PARTIALLY_UPHELD` branch grants part of the claim. Here `awarded_cents` is required and must be strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than `claimed_amount_cents` — the bound is strict at both ends — and `adjudicated_amount_cents` is set equal to `awarded_cents`. Because this award is also positive, the branch then runs exactly the same four-hop cascade as the full-grant branch: one `PENDING` back-wage correction created with `dispute_id` set and `amount_cents` equal to `adjudicated_amount_cents`, any pre-existing `PENDING` correction on the line driven `PENDING → VOIDED` and named by the new correction's `supersedes_correction_id`, the line's `pending_correction_cents` set to the new amount with its `version` incremented by 1, and the period's `total_correction_cents` recomputed.

The `OPEN → DENIED` branch grants nothing, and it is the divergent arm of the fork. `awarded_cents` must be absent from the payload, and `adjudicated_amount_cents` is set to 0. Because the award is zero, **no** back-wage correction is created, the line's `pending_correction_cents` is left exactly as it was, no pre-existing `PENDING` correction is voided, the line's `version` is left untouched, and the period's `total_correction_cents` is unchanged. The reason `DENIED` exists as its own branch rather than as a partial award of zero is precisely the strict lower bound above: an `awarded_cents` of 0 under `PARTIALLY_UPHELD` is rejected with `ERR_PARTIAL_AMOUNT_INVALID`, so denial is the only way to award nothing. Symmetrically, an `awarded_cents` equal to `claimed_amount_cents` under `PARTIALLY_UPHELD` is also rejected — full grant is `UPHELD`'s job — and supplying `awarded_cents` at all under `UPHELD` or `DENIED` is rejected the same way.

**Closing** a dispute moves it to the terminal `CLOSED` and is likewise a `ComplianceOfficer` action, and all three adjudicated arms join there. From `UPHELD` the dispute may be closed only once the dispute's back-wage correction carries `status` `APPLIED`; the same requirement attaches from `PARTIALLY_UPHELD`, since that arm too produced an award that must actually be paid. From `DENIED` there is no such requirement, because that arm produced no correction — the join is deliberately asymmetric. In all three cases the dispute's `status` must already be one of `UPHELD`, `PARTIALLY_UPHELD` or `DENIED`; closing one still `OPEN`, or closing an award-bearing one whose correction has not been applied, yields `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_ADJUDICATED`. Closing stamps `closed_at` with the current logical instant and decrements the line's `open_dispute_count`, again without incrementing the line's `version`, because no money moves.

**Issuing a correction directly.** Not every correction comes out of a dispute; an audit can find an error nobody complained about. A `ComplianceOfficer` issues one against a `pay_line_id`, optionally naming the `dispute_id` that produced it — null for corrections issued by audit, and not itself required to name this same pay line, since all that is read from it is its `adjudicated_amount_cents` — and optionally naming the `supersedes_correction_id` of a `PENDING` correction on the same line that this one replaces. `amount_cents` is the back wages owed and must be strictly greater than 0. `reason_code` records the regulatory ground and is one of `POOL_SHARE_SHORTFALL`, `TIP_CREDIT_INVALID`, `CC_FEE_OVERDEDUCTION`, `SERVICE_CHARGE_MISCLASSIFIED` or `SIDE_WORK_OVERAGE`. `expected_pay_line_version` is required and carries the optimistic-concurrency semantics of §2.7.

Four checks guard the issue, in this order: the target line's `status` must be `FINAL`, failing which `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FINAL`; `expected_pay_line_version` must equal that line's stored `version`, failing which `ERR_STALE_PAY_LINE_VERSION`; `supersedes_correction_id`, when supplied, must name a correction whose `status` is `PENDING` on the very same `pay_line_id`, and it is **required** whenever such a correction already exists on that line, so both a wrong target and a missing one yield `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_MISMATCH`; and where `dispute_id` is supplied, `amount_cents` may not exceed that dispute's `adjudicated_amount_cents`, failing which `ERR_CORRECTION_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`, which is also the code for a non-positive amount.

A newly issued correction has `status` `PENDING`, its `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `applied_by_user_id`, `applied_at` and `voided_at` all null. Issuing it voids the superseded `PENDING` correction it displaced — that transition stamps the displaced row's `voided_at` with the current logical instant and removes its amount from the line's `pending_correction_cents` — raises the line's `pending_correction_cents` by the new amount, increments the line's `version` by exactly 1, and recomputes the owning period's `total_correction_cents`. Only a `PENDING` correction may be voided in this way; an `APPLIED` correction is terminal, and because `amount_cents` must itself be strictly greater than 0 no offsetting negative correction can be issued against it either.

**Applying a correction.** Posting a `PENDING` correction to its line drives it `PENDING → APPLIED` and may be done by either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PayrollPreparer` — but never by the same person who issued it. Four checks apply, in this order: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the correction's `issued_by_user_id`, failing which `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the correction's `status` must be `PENDING`, so applying one already `APPLIED` or already `VOIDED` yields `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_PENDING`; the target line's `status` must be `FINAL`, failing which `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FINAL`; and the sum of `amount_cents` over the line's already-`APPLIED` corrections plus this one may not exceed the line's `minimum_wage_obligation_cents`, failing which `ERR_CORRECTION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`.

The posting itself happens in a fixed order. The correction's `amount_cents` is subtracted from the line's `pending_correction_cents`; the same amount is added to the line's `correction_applied_cents`; the same amount is added to the line's `total_gross_cents`; the line's `version` is incremented by 1; and `applied_at` is stamped with the current logical instant while `applied_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. The owning period's `total_correction_cents` is then recomputed as the sum of `amount_cents` over corrections whose `status` is `PENDING` or `APPLIED` on that period's pay lines — a figure this application leaves unchanged, because the amount has merely moved from the `PENDING` half of that rollup to the `APPLIED` half. One reason code alone has a further effect: where `reason_code` is `TIP_CREDIT_INVALID`, the correction is by definition a minimum-wage top-up, so the period's `total_topup_cents` is additionally raised by `amount_cents`. Under every other reason code the period's `total_topup_cents` is left alone.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below returns the standard envelope described in §2.1 and §2.2, requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers, and enforces the role list in its final column before any other check runs. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 and place the created resource in `data`. Endpoints that read, list, or modify an existing resource — including every lifecycle action, every void and every reversal — respond with HTTP 200 and place the resulting resource in `data`, except the logical-clock endpoint, which responds with HTTP 200 and an empty `data` object. Request bodies, response payload shapes, validation ladders and side effects are specified in §2 and §3 and are deliberately not repeated here.
