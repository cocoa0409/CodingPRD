# Season Pass & Blockout Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional theme park sells its season by the year, not by the day. Guests buy a multi-tier season pass in the spring, hand over either the whole price or a down payment plus a run of monthly installments, come to the pass office to have a photograph taken and an identity document bound to the card, and then arrive at a turnstile any number of times between opening day and the last day of the operating calendar. Between those two moments sit almost all of the park's commercial risk: a pass that is shared between three households consumes capacity the park never sold, a payment plan that quietly stops paying leaves the park financing a guest's summer, and a single unexpectedly hot Saturday can push a day past its gate capacity while every tier still believes it has unrestricted access.

This document specifies the back-office API that the season pass programme runs on. It covers tier design (park access breadth, blockout calendars, parking entitlement, in-park discount), payment plan enrolment and monthly billing with a delinquency-to-default ladder, pass processing with photo capture and identity binding, the transfer-prohibition rules that keep a pass attached to one named human, blockout date assignment by tier including the capacity-driven blockouts the system installs on its own when a forecast crosses the lock threshold, peak-day reservation requirements with day-level and arrival-window-level slot budgets, upgrade path pricing that credits amounts already paid or unconsumed prorated value, renewal campaigns with early-bird pricing and retention credits, pass sharing detection with an investigation-and-appeal case workflow that can revoke a pass and forfeit what has been paid, and the revenue, visitation-frequency and in-park-spend analysis the programme office reports on.

Two structural properties shape almost every rule that follows. First, a season pass carries **two independent status dimensions** on one record — a park-access dimension and a billing dimension — and they move on different events; a pass can be perfectly current at the gate while its plan is in default, or fully paid while suspended for suspected sharing. Second, almost every write **cascades**: a forecast update installs blockouts, which release reservations, which rewrite a day's slot counters; a missed installment suspends a pass, which releases its future reservations; a substantiated sharing case revokes a pass, which terminates its plan, which voids its installments, which moves money from recognised revenue to forfeited revenue — and a successful appeal walks all of that backwards. Implementers should treat the cascade descriptions in §3 as mandatory, not advisory: the depth of the cascade is the product.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All collections are mounted at the top level; there is no shared API prefix. Every resource family — programmes, tiers, park days, blockout assignments, passholders, season passes, payment plans, installments, reservations, visits, sharing cases and upgrade orders — is addressed directly.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is absent, empty, or is not one of the five role names declared below, the request is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the missing-identifier check is evaluated before the role-name check. Once both headers are present and well-formed, the role is matched against the set of roles the target endpoint permits, and a role outside that set is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These three checks run ahead of every other validation in the system, including request-body parsing and resource existence.

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `PassOfficeClerk`, `GateAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `Passholder`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the season programme office. It designs programmes, tiers and the operating calendar, re-forecasts operating days, assigns and lifts blockout dates, runs monthly billing and sets the logical clock. A `PassOfficeClerk` staffs the pass office counter: it registers passholders, sells passes, processes them, enrols and cures payment plans, takes installment and upgrade payments, quotes and applies upgrades, renews passes and books or cancels reservations on a guest's behalf. A `GateAgent` operates the turnstiles: it records gate entries, closes visits with the in-park spend, and opens pass-sharing cases from gate evidence. A `ComplianceOfficer` handles adjudication: it takes sharing cases under review, decides them, reverses applied upgrades, and reads the revenue analysis. A `Passholder` is the guest who holds the pass.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PassOfficeClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `GateAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — a sharing case opened by an agent stores the header value in the case's `opened_by_user_id` field alongside an `opened_by_role` of `GATE_AGENT` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `decided_by_user_id` when the officer decides a sharing case, and compares it to the case's stored `opened_by_user_id` for the segregation-of-duties rule described below, but never to an entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Passholder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Passholder` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Passholder` compare the resource's `passholder_id` to `X-User-ID`.

Two role-derived constraints run system-wide. First, **ownership**: a caller acting as `Passholder` who touches a season pass whose `passholder_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_NOT_PASS_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This applies to reservation creation, reservation cancellation, appealing a sharing case, and reading a single season pass. A `Passholder` also has no access whatsoever to the passholder directory listing, nor to any write reserved for `ProgramAdmin`, `PassOfficeClerk`, `GateAgent` or `ComplianceOfficer`. Ownership scoping stops at those four places and the season-pass listing: no other read is narrowed by identity, so a `Passholder` reading the reservation, visit, payment-plan, installment, sharing-case or upgrade-order collections — or any single record of those types, or any single passholder — sees every record, unfiltered. Second, **segregation of duties**: the `ComplianceOfficer` who adjudicates a sharing case must not be the user who opened it. A decide request whose `X-User-ID` equals the case's `opened_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Cases the system opened by itself carry the literal string `SYSTEM` in `opened_by_user_id`, so any officer may decide those. The same principle separates upgrade application from upgrade reversal: a clerk applies an upgrade, but only a compliance officer may reverse it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional pagination query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of records to return; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, an `offset` below 0, or a filter query parameter carrying a value outside its declared enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every filter parameter on every list endpoint is optional, so a bare list call with no query string is always legal. A query parameter present but empty (`?limit=`, `?status=`) is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, and a query parameter an endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected. Identifier-valued filters (`program_id`, `tier_id`, `season_pass_id`, `park_day_id`, `passholder_id`, `renewed_from_pass_id`, `payment_plan_id`, `redeemed_visit_id`, `reservation_id`, `source_tier_id`, `target_tier_id`) are matched literally and are never existence-checked, so a filter naming nothing simply returns an empty page rather than a 404.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list endpoints sort by `created_at` ascending. Six collections override that default and their ordering is part of the contract:

- Pass programmes are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending, with `id` ascending used only as a stable tiebreak between records that share a timestamp.
- Pass tiers are ordered by `tier_rank` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Park days are ordered by `calendar_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Season passes are ordered by `media_number` ascending.
- Installments are ordered by `due_date` ascending, then `sequence_no` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Visits are ordered by `entry_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- Blockout assignments, passholders, payment plans, reservations, sharing cases and upgrade orders all use the `created_at` ascending default.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties

Every one of the twelve persisted record types in this system — pass programme, pass tier, park day, blockout assignment, passholder, season pass, payment plan, installment, reservation, visit, sharing case and upgrade order — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never parse it.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, e.g. `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recorded when the record was first inserted.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on insert and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

`created_at` and `updated_at`, like the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp`, are minted from the host's wall clock and are strictly increasing across inserts; they are **not** read from the logical clock of §2.5, so setting the clock backwards does not affect them and record ordering by `created_at` always equals insertion order. A record inserted during a request is stamped once at insert and its `version` stays at 1 for that request even if later steps of the same request touch it again. There are no records in this system that omit `updated_at` or `version`. A **mutation** for these purposes includes recomputation of derived fields: when a park day's slot counters are rewritten because a reservation was released, that park day's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments; when a pass's money ledger is rewritten because an installment settled, the pass's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. This is the single most important consequence of the cascade rules in §2.6 — versions move on records the client never named in the request, which is precisely what the stale-read protection in §2.7 detects.

Human-facing identifiers are separate from `id` and are not UUIDs. A season pass additionally carries a `media_number`, the number printed on the physical card. Media numbers are minted sequentially in creation order — the first pass ever created is `SP-000001`, the second `SP-000002`, and so on, always six digits zero-padded after the `SP-` prefix, allocated by ascending `created_at`.

### 2.5. The Logical Season Clock

Every date-sensitive rule in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than the host machine's wall clock. The early-bird deadline comparison, the prorated upgrade credit's remaining-days ratio, the anti-passback buffer between two gate entries, upgrade quote expiry, installment settlement timestamps and the reservation past-date guard all resolve against it. Until it has been set explicitly, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ProgramAdmin` may move it to any instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value, and the response reports the new value as `current_utc`, echoed back byte-for-byte as it was supplied rather than reformatted. Setting the clock modifies no business record, mints no record and bumps no `version`. No business endpoint moves time. Wherever the clock's instant is *written into* a business record — an installment's `settled_at` when `paid_at` was omitted, an upgrade order's `quoted_at` — it is stored in that same verbatim spelling, so a clock set to `2026-09-05T10:00:00Z` yields exactly `2026-09-05T10:00:00Z` in those fields rather than a three-fraction-digit rendering; an installment's `settled_at` taken from a supplied `paid_at` likewise stores that value verbatim. Values the server *derives* from the clock, by contrast, are rendered in the canonical three-fraction-digit form of §2.4, which is what an upgrade order's `quote_expires_at` carries. Before the clock has ever been set its verbatim spelling is `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Two comparison conventions apply throughout. Where a rule compares dates — a park day's `calendar_date`, an installment's `due_date`, a programme's `early_bird_deadline`, a billing run's `as_of_date` — it is a calendar-date comparison, performed on the date portion alone. Where a rule compares instants — the anti-passback buffer, quote expiry against `quote_expires_at` — it is a full timestamp comparison.

### 2.6. Cascades, Reconciliation and Derived Counters

Derived fields in this system are never stale. Whenever a write changes a fact that some other record's derived field summarises, that other record is reconciled inside the same request, and the reconciliation is itself a mutation in the §2.4 sense. Cascades run to their full depth. A forecast update that locks a day installs blockout assignments; because those blockouts became active, confirmed reservations covered by them are released; because those reservations were released, the day's counters are rewritten. A billing run that marks an installment missed pushes the plan's missed count up; because the missed count crossed a threshold, the pass is suspended; because the pass was suspended, its future confirmed reservations are released and every affected day's counters are rewritten. A sharing case that is substantiated revokes the pass; because the pass was revoked, its active plan is terminated; because the plan was terminated, its scheduled installments are voided and the amount already paid moves out of recognised revenue into forfeited revenue on the programme.

Compensation is symmetric. Where a cascade can be undone — a dismissed appeal after a substantiated case, a reversed upgrade — every level of the original cascade is walked backwards with the same arithmetic in the opposite direction, so that no ledger drifts. The one deliberate asymmetry, called out again in §3.5 and §3.6, is that reservations released by a suspension or a blockout are **never** restored: the slots they occupied were returned to the day and may already have been resold, so reinstating a pass does not reinstate its reservations.

A park day's counters deserve special mention because one of them is deliberately non-reversible. `reserved_count` is the number of that day's reservations currently in `CONFIRMED`; `released_count` is the number in any of `CANCELLED_BY_HOLDER`, `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` or `CANCELLED_BY_BLOCKOUT`; `redeemed_count` is the number in `REDEEMED`; `no_show_count` is the number in `NO_SHOW`; and `remaining_slot_count` is the day's `reservation_slot_capacity` less its `reserved_count`, never allowed below zero. Distinct from all of these, `grant_count` is a monotonic audit counter: it starts at 0 and increases by 1 every time a reservation is successfully created for that day, and it is never decremented, not by a cancellation, not by a blockout release, not by a no-show sweep.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Pass and Park-Day Writes

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent record's `version`. In both cases the guard exists because nothing in the state machine would otherwise reject the write, and yet the write is priced or scoped against a parent the client may have read some time ago.

Enrolling a season pass in a payment plan requires the request to carry `expected_season_pass_version`, holding the `version` of the target season pass as the client last observed it. The field is not optional. If it does not equal the pass's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PASS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second enrolment legitimately supersedes an existing active plan — that is how a plan is re-amortised — so no state precondition blocks it; the stale version is the only signal that the clerk computed the new schedule against an outstanding balance that has since moved, and an installment settlement, an applied upgrade or a superseding plan each advance the pass's `version`.

Creating a manual blockout assignment requires the request to carry `expected_park_day_version`, holding the `version` of the target park day as the client last observed it. The field is not optional. If it does not equal the park day's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARK_DAY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A manual blockout for a different tier on the same day is a perfectly legitimate write, so no state precondition rejects it; the stale version catches the case where a forecast update has re-classified the day and already installed capacity-driven blockouts the operator has not yet seen.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where a request body has no `expected_season_pass_version` or `expected_park_day_version` named for it in §3 or §4, none is expected and none may be required.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

Validation is strictly ordered, and the **first** rule whose condition holds produces the response; later rules are never reached. This ordering is contractual, because several rules would otherwise mask each other. The bands, in order:

1. **Authentication** — missing `X-User-ID`, then a missing or unrecognised `X-User-Role`, both yielding `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation** — the role is not permitted on this endpoint, yielding `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Schema** — on write endpoints, an unknown field, a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, a malformed date or timestamp, a numeric value outside a declared range, or a value outside a declared enumeration, all yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On list endpoints, an out-of-bounds `limit` or `offset` or an out-of-enum filter value, likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted — so an optional field explicitly nulled falls back to its default and a required field explicitly nulled is rejected as missing — and a request with no body at all is read as an empty object, which is legal on any endpoint that declares no required field. Three uniqueness collisions also answer with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even though they cannot be seen from the body alone, and each of them is therefore evaluated *after* the existence and state bands rather than inside this one: a pass tier's `name` or `tier_rank` already taken within the programme, a park day's `calendar_date` already taken within the programme, and a passholder's `email` already taken globally.
4. **Existence** — path identifiers and body-borne foreign keys are resolved in a fixed order, described below.
5. **Ownership and segregation of duties** — `ERR_NOT_PASS_OWNER`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. **Concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_PASS_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_PARK_DAY_VERSION`.
7. **Gate readiness** — the pass-office processing check on gate entry, `ERR_PHOTO_NOT_CAPTURED`. This band sits deliberately **above** the general state band that follows: a pass can only be missing its photograph or its identity binding while it is still awaiting processing, so placing this check any later would leave it permanently shadowed by the not-active check and the error unreachable. A turnstile must be able to say "no photograph on file", while the not-active error remains reachable at a gate through a suspended or revoked pass.
8. **State** — programme, tier, pass, plan, installment, reservation, visit, case, upgrade-order and blockout state preconditions.
9. **Business safety** — the transfer-prohibition hold, then the anti-passback buffer.
10. **Business temporal** — programme mismatch on a park day, entry-date mismatch, past-dated park day, malformed arrival window, then blockout coverage.
11. **Business referential** — tier/programme mismatch, duplicate live pass, duplicate blockout, duplicate reservation, not-an-upgrade, invalid evidence.
12. **Business constraint** — installment count range, minimum down payment, peak reservation quota, peak reservation requirement.
13. **Business fiscal** — day capacity full, overlapping-window slots full, installment amount mismatch, upgrade payment mismatch, cure amount mismatch.

Within the existence band, identifiers resolve in this order: pass programme (`ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`), pass tier (`ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`), park day (`ERR_PARK_DAY_NOT_FOUND`), passholder (`ERR_PASSHOLDER_NOT_FOUND`), season pass (`ERR_PASS_NOT_FOUND`), payment plan (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`), installment (`ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`), reservation (`ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`), visit (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`), sharing case (`ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`), upgrade order (`ERR_UPGRADE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`), blockout assignment (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`). The rule covers both path variables and body-borne references, so a body `program_id` that names nothing yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; a `tier_id`, `source_tier_id` or `target_tier_id` that names nothing yields `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`; a body `park_day_id` yields `ERR_PARK_DAY_NOT_FOUND`; a body `passholder_id` yields `ERR_PASSHOLDER_NOT_FOUND`; a body `season_pass_id` or `renewed_from_pass_id` yields `ERR_PASS_NOT_FOUND`; a body `waive_installment_id` yields `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, as does an installment that exists but does not belong to the pass's active plan; and any element of a submitted `evidence_visit_ids` array that names nothing yields `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`. Where a path contains several identifiers, they resolve left to right, so the outer resource's absence is reported before the inner one is looked for.

The URL path variables are spelled exactly as follows and resolve to the record types just listed: `program_id` for a pass programme, `tier_id` for a pass tier, `park_day_id` for a park day, `assignment_id` for a blockout assignment, `passholder_id` for a passholder, `pass_id` for a season pass, `plan_id` for a payment plan, `installment_id` for an installment, `reservation_id` for a reservation, `visit_id` for a visit, `case_id` for a sharing case, and `upgrade_order_id` for an upgrade order.

### 2.9. Money, Counts and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, named with a `_cents` suffix, and is carried and returned as a JSON integer — never a string, never a decimal fraction. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the model.

Rounding is therefore only ever introduced where a division or a percentage appears, and the method used is pinned per calculation because the choices differ deliberately:

- **Round half up** is used for the level installment amount when a financed principal is divided by the number of installments, for the early-bird discount amount, for a park day's forecast utilisation (to four decimal places), and for the visits-per-active-pass analytic (to two decimal places).
- **Truncation toward zero (floor)** is used for the prorated upgrade credit, for the retention credit cap, for a day's reservation slot capacity, for the per-window slot capacity, and for the in-park spend-per-visit analytic.

Two ratios are stored as decimal numbers rather than integers: a park day's `forecast_utilization_pct`, held to four decimal places, and a tier's `in_park_discount_pct`, supplied to at most two decimal places. Both are plain JSON numbers expressed as fractions of one, so a fifteen percent discount is `0.15`, not `15`. "Held to four decimal places" constrains the rounding, not the rendering: `forecast_utilization_pct` is emitted as a bare JSON number with trailing zeros dropped, so a utilisation rounded to `0.9000` appears in the payload as `0.9` and one rounded to `1.0000` as `1.0`. The same applies to `visits_per_active_pass`, which is rounded to two places but rendered without padding, so an exact two visits per pass appears as `2.0` and the zero-denominator case as `0.0`.

Risk scores are integers with no rounding: the sharing risk weights are whole numbers, so no fractional residue can accumulate.

Every calculation that divides guards its zero denominator explicitly. Where a denominator can be zero — no park days in a programme, no active passes, no closed visits — the specification names the value the calculation produces instead of dividing, and §3 states each of those values.

### 2.10. The Pass Ledger Identity

A season pass carries a five-field money ledger that must satisfy one identity at all times: `billed_total_cents` equals `paid_to_date_cents` plus `credited_amount_cents` plus `outstanding_balance_cents`. Concretely, `outstanding_balance_cents` is always computed as `billed_total_cents` less `paid_to_date_cents` less `credited_amount_cents`; it is never stored independently.

The three inputs to that identity accumulate from named events and nothing else. `contract_value_cents` is the tier list price the pass carries: it is set to the selling tier's `base_price_cents` at sale, replaced by the target tier's `base_price_cents` when an upgrade is applied, and restored to the source tier's `base_price_cents` when an upgrade is reversed. `billed_total_cents` is `contract_value_cents` plus every payment plan `setup_fee_cents` ever charged onto the pass plus, for every applied upgrade order, that order's `gross_delta_cents` and `admin_fee_cents`. `paid_to_date_cents` is the sum of down payments collected, settled installment amounts and applied upgrade payments; it is reduced when an upgrade is reversed. `credited_amount_cents` is the sum of `credit_cents` over the pass's upgrade orders that are in `APPLIED`.

Waiving an installment during reinstatement reduces `billed_total_cents` by that installment's `amount_cents` rather than increasing `paid_to_date_cents` — the park forgives the charge instead of pretending it was paid — and the outstanding balance is recomputed from the identity afterwards. Every reversal in this system is written so that the identity still holds after the reversal, with no residue.

### 2.11. Programme Status Gating

The pass programme is the lifecycle gate for almost every child operation, and each operation names the programme statuses it tolerates. When the programme's `status` is not one of those an operation requires, the request is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATUS_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The requirements are pinned, per operation:

- Designing a pass tier requires the programme to be in `DESIGN` or `SALES_OPEN`.
- Adding an operating day to the calendar requires `DESIGN`, `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.
- Selling a season pass requires `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.
- Creating a reservation requires `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.
- Recording a gate entry requires `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.
- Quoting an upgrade requires `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.
- Processing a pass at the pass office requires `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`.

- Re-forecasting an operating day requires `DESIGN`, `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`, the same set the calendar itself takes.

That list is exhaustive: **every** other operation in this system carries no programme-status requirement whatsoever and can never answer `ERR_PROGRAM_STATUS_INVALID`. Creating and lifting blockout assignments are the deliberate headline case, because the blockout calendar must remain editable in any non-closed programme status; but the monthly billing run, installment settlement, reinstatement, renewal quoting, upgrade application and upgrade reversal, reservation cancellation, visit closing, and every sharing-case action are all equally unguarded, and publishing or retiring a tier answers `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED` rather than this code.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing a Season: Programmes and Tiers

Everything in this system hangs off a **pass programme**, the annual sales campaign for one park. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a programme by naming the `season_year` it sells passes for and the `park_name` operating it, and by fixing the `early_bird_deadline` — an ISO date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, on or before which a renewal quote earns the early-bird discount, the deadline day itself included. Six further knobs may be supplied at creation and each has a default the programme falls back to when the field is omitted: `capacity_peak_threshold_pct` defaults to `0.85`, `capacity_lock_threshold_pct` to `0.97`, `capacity_lock_blockout_max_rank` to `2`, `delinquency_suspension_threshold` to `2`, `default_threshold` to `3`, and `sharing_case_auto_open_score` to `65`. The two capacity thresholds must bracket each other and stay in band: `capacity_peak_threshold_pct` must be at least `0.50` and strictly below `capacity_lock_threshold_pct`, while `capacity_lock_threshold_pct` must be strictly above `capacity_peak_threshold_pct` and at most `1.20` — a forecast may legitimately exceed a day's nominal capacity, which is why the ceiling sits above one. Likewise `delinquency_suspension_threshold` must be at least 1 and strictly below `default_threshold`, and `default_threshold` must be at least 2 and at most 12; a programme in which a pass would default before it was ever suspended is not a valid programme. The remaining knobs are bounded only from below: `capacity_lock_blockout_max_rank` and `sharing_case_auto_open_score` must each be at least 0 and have no upper bound, and `season_year` is any integer. Every one of these bracket and range violations, including the two strictly-below cross-field checks, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the capacity bracket is checked before the delinquency bracket.

A programme is born in `DESIGN`, and its five computed money-and-population fields — `base_revenue_cents`, `recognized_revenue_cents`, `deferred_revenue_cents`, `forfeited_revenue_cents` and `active_pass_count` — all initialise to 0. Its `status` is never written directly; it advances only through the administrator's lifecycle action, which takes a `target_status` and applies the transition's full effect. The permitted path is `DESIGN → SALES_OPEN → SEASON_ACTIVE → RENEWAL_OPEN → CLOSED`, with exactly one sanctioned shortcut: `SEASON_ACTIVE → CLOSED`. Any other pairing — a jump from `DESIGN` or `SALES_OPEN` straight to `CLOSED`, a step backwards, a move out of the terminal `CLOSED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), and so is a legal pairing whose precondition fails. Moving `DESIGN → SALES_OPEN` requires that at least one pass tier of this programme has reached `PUBLISHED`; without a sellable tier the programme cannot open for sales. Moving `SALES_OPEN → SEASON_ACTIVE` requires that at least one operating day exists for this programme; a park with no calendar cannot open its gates. Moving `SEASON_ACTIVE → RENEWAL_OPEN` and either route into `CLOSED` carry no preconditions. Each of the five transitions increments the programme's `version` by 1.

The effect of each step is what makes it meaningful. Reaching `SALES_OPEN` permits pass sales, payment-plan enrolment and reservations. Reaching `SEASON_ACTIVE` permits gate entries. Reaching `RENEWAL_OPEN` makes this programme a legal renewal target for passes sold under other programmes while leaving gate entries permitted. Reaching `CLOSED` runs the season-end expiry: every season pass of this programme whose `pass_status` is `PENDING_PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` becomes `EXPIRED`; because those passes expired, each of their reservations still in `CONFIRMED` becomes `NO_SHOW` and every affected park day recomputes its `no_show_count`, `reserved_count` and `remaining_slot_count`; and the programme's `active_pass_count`, `base_revenue_cents`, `recognized_revenue_cents` and `deferred_revenue_cents` are all recomputed. Both routes into `CLOSED`, from `RENEWAL_OPEN` and from `SEASON_ACTIVE`, produce exactly this same cascade.

**Pass tiers** describe what a pass buys. An administrator designs a tier under a programme by supplying that programme's identifier as `program_id`, a `name` such as BRONZE or GOLD which must be unique within the programme, and a `tier_rank` between 1 and 9 inclusive which must also be unique within the programme and which expresses access breadth — a higher rank means broader park access. Commercially the tier is defined by `base_price_cents`, the season list price, which must be at least 1000 cents; note that price is deliberately **not** required to increase with rank, so an implementation must not assume monotonicity. Three benefits ride on the tier: `parking_benefit`, one of `NONE`, `DISCOUNT` or `INCLUDED`; `in_park_discount_pct`, the merchandise and food discount applied to in-park spend, expressed as a fraction of one between `0` and `0.30` inclusive with at most two decimal places; and the peak-day policy pair `reservation_required_on_peak`, a boolean, together with `peak_reservation_quota`, an integer between 1 and 20 inclusive that caps how many confirmed reservations a pass on this tier may hold at once for crowded upcoming days. A tier whose `program_id` does not match the programme the operation targets is rejected with `ERR_TIER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

A tier enters life in `DRAFT` and is unsellable there. Publishing it moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and requires the owning programme to be in `DESIGN` or `SALES_OPEN`; once published the tier becomes sellable and becomes a legal upgrade and renewal target. Retiring it moves `PUBLISHED → RETIRED`, the terminal state, and requires that no season pass of that programme sits on this tier with a `pass_status` of `PENDING_PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — the park will not retire a tier out from under a live passholder. A `target_status` unreachable from the tier's current `status`, or a retirement attempt that still has live passes on the tier, is rejected with `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409); the same code answers a publish attempt made while the programme has already moved past `SALES_OPEN`. Either successful transition increments the tier's `version` by 1. Selling a pass, renewing into a tier, or quoting an upgrade whose referenced *target* tier is not in `PUBLISHED` is also refused with `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED`; an upgrade's `source_tier_id` is not status-checked, because a pass may legitimately be sitting on a tier the park has since retired. The programme collection may be filtered on `status` or `season_year`, and the tier collection on `program_id` or `status`.

### 3.2. The Operating Calendar: Capacity Posture and Blockout Dates

The **operating calendar** is the set of park days a programme sells access to. An administrator adds a day by naming its `program_id`, its `calendar_date` as an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` value which must be unique within that programme, its `capacity_total` — the gate capacity of the day, at least 1 — and its current `forecast_attendance`, which must be zero or greater. Everything else about a day is derived, immediately and on every subsequent re-forecast.

The derivation, which the park calls the day's **capacity posture**, runs in a fixed order. First, `forecast_utilization_pct` is the day's `forecast_attendance` divided by its `capacity_total`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Second, `day_class` is selected from that ratio against the owning programme's two thresholds: the day is `CAPACITY_LOCKED` when its utilisation is greater than or equal to `capacity_lock_threshold_pct`; otherwise it is `PEAK` when its utilisation is greater than or equal to `capacity_peak_threshold_pct`; otherwise it is `STANDARD`. Both thresholds are inclusive, so a utilisation exactly equal to the peak threshold classifies the day `PEAK`, not `STANDARD`, and a utilisation exactly equal to the lock threshold classifies it `CAPACITY_LOCKED`. Third, `reservation_slot_capacity` — the number of reservable slots the day offers as a whole — is the smaller of two quantities, and then floored at zero: the first quantity is one tenth of `capacity_total` truncated to a whole number, because the park never reserves away more than ten percent of a day's gate; the second is `capacity_total` less `forecast_attendance`, because the park never reserves away capacity the forecast has already consumed. Fourth, `window_slot_capacity` — the number of reservable slots available to any one band of overlapping arrival windows — is half of `reservation_slot_capacity` truncated to a whole number, but never less than 1, so that a day with any slots at all can always take a first reservation in any window; that floor of 1 is applied unconditionally, so a day whose `reservation_slot_capacity` is 0 still reports a `window_slot_capacity` of 1 and is simply turned away one band earlier, by the day-level capacity check. Fifth, `remaining_slot_count` is `reservation_slot_capacity` less the day's `reserved_count`, never reported below zero. As a worked illustration of the two capacities: a day with a gate capacity of 40 and a forecast of 36 sits at a utilisation of `0.9000`, is therefore `PEAK` under the default peak threshold, offers the smaller of 4 and 4 slots for the day as a whole, and permits 2 confirmed reservations within any band of mutually overlapping arrival windows. A day may be born already `CAPACITY_LOCKED` if the forecast supplied at creation crosses the lock threshold, and when it is, the creation runs the same capacity-lock blockout install cascade described below, at its first level, right there in the create request.

Re-forecasting is the administrator's lever on all of this. A forecast update supplies a new `forecast_attendance` and may optionally also supply a replacement `capacity_total`; the day's whole posture is then re-derived, its `version` increments by 1, and `day_class` may move in **either** direction any number of times over the season. `STANDARD → PEAK`, `PEAK → CAPACITY_LOCKED`, `CAPACITY_LOCKED → PEAK`, `PEAK → STANDARD`, `STANDARD → CAPACITY_LOCKED` and `CAPACITY_LOCKED → STANDARD` are all reachable. Any re-forecast requires the owning programme to be in `DESIGN`, `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`. Every one of these transitions recomputes `reservation_slot_capacity`, `window_slot_capacity` and `remaining_slot_count`. Beyond that, the transitions differ in ways that must be implemented separately:

- Entering `PEAK` from `STANDARD` turns on the peak-day reservation requirement: gate entry for any tier whose `reservation_required_on_peak` is true now demands a confirmed reservation on that day.
- Leaving `PEAK` for `STANDARD` turns that requirement back off; the day stops demanding reservations.
- Entering `CAPACITY_LOCKED`, whether from `PEAK` or directly from `STANDARD`, runs a three-level cascade, and both routes run the identical cascade. At the first level, for every pass tier of the programme whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose `tier_rank` is less than or equal to the programme's `capacity_lock_blockout_max_rank` and which does not already have an active blockout on this day, the system creates a blockout assignment with `source` set to `CAPACITY_ADJUSTED` and `status` set to `ACTIVE`; candidate tiers are processed in ascending `tier_rank`, and among tiers of equal rank in ascending `created_at`. At the second level, because those blockouts became active, every reservation on this day whose pass sits on a now-blocked tier and whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED_BY_BLOCKOUT`. At the third level, because those reservations were released, the day's `reserved_count`, `released_count` and `remaining_slot_count` are recomputed. From this point new reservations on the day are refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_CAPACITY_LOCKED` (HTTP 409).
- Leaving `CAPACITY_LOCKED`, whether for `PEAK` or for `STANDARD`, runs the release, and both routes release identically: every blockout assignment on this day whose `source` is `CAPACITY_ADJUSTED` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `LIFTED` with its `lift_reason` set to the literal string `CAPACITY_RELEASED`, and each such assignment's `version` increments by 1. Blockouts whose `source` is `MANUAL` are untouched by the release and survive it. The reservations cancelled on the way in are **not** restored.

**Blockout assignments** are the (tier, day) pairs on which a tier has no access. An administrator creates one manually by naming the `tier_id` and the `park_day_id`, together with the `expected_park_day_version` that §2.7 requires. A manual creation records `source` as `MANUAL` and `status` as `ACTIVE`, leaving `lift_reason` null while the assignment remains active. Because a new blockout instantly removes a tier's access to that day, any reservation of that tier on that day still in `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED_BY_BLOCKOUT`, the day's counters are recomputed, and the day's `version` increments by 1. Two guards protect the pairing: a tier whose programme differs from the park day's programme is rejected with `ERR_PARK_DAY_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and a second active blockout for the same `tier_id` and `park_day_id` pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BLOCKOUT_ASSIGNMENT` (HTTP 422). Because blockout editing carries no programme-status requirement, the calendar stays editable throughout the season; nor is the named tier's own `status` inspected, so a `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` tier may be blocked out just as a `PUBLISHED` one may, and a day already `CAPACITY_LOCKED` still accepts new manual blockouts even though it refuses to release any.

Lifting a blockout moves it `ACTIVE → LIFTED`, records the supplied `reason` — a required string field, not an optional one — into `lift_reason`, returns the tier's access to the day for future reservations and gate entries, and increments the assignment's `version` by 1. Two preconditions guard the lift and they are evaluated in this order: an assignment that is not in `ACTIVE` is rejected first with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_LIFTED` (HTTP 409); only then is the cross-entity guard applied, refusing the lift with `ERR_PARK_DAY_CAPACITY_LOCKED` (HTTP 409) while the park day's `day_class` is `CAPACITY_LOCKED`. A locked day may neither release its blockouts nor accept new reservations — the lock is the park's last line of capacity defence, and an operator cannot hand-wave it away one tier at a time. Reviewers can inspect the calendar by filtering the assignment collection on `tier_id`, `park_day_id`, `status` or `source`, and the day collection on `program_id` or `day_class`.

### 3.3. Selling and Processing a Pass

Before a pass can be sold there must be a person to sell it to. A `PassOfficeClerk` registers a **passholder** with a `full_name` — the legal name printed on the pass — an `email` which must be globally unique across all passholders, and a `date_of_birth` as an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` value. A new passholder starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `sharing_risk_score` 0 and `photo_mismatch_count` 0. The `status` dimension has exactly two values, `ACTIVE` and `BANNED`; a holder becomes `BANNED` only through the sharing-case route described in §3.8. `sharing_risk_score` and `photo_mismatch_count` are both derived and are described where they are produced, in §3.7. The holder directory may be filtered by `email` or `status`, and is readable by every role except `Passholder`.

Selling a **season pass** is the clerk's core counter transaction. The payload names the `program_id` the pass is sold under, the `tier_id` it is sold on, the `passholder_id` it is bound to, and the `payment_mode`, which is either `PAY_IN_FULL` or `PAYMENT_PLAN`. Four guards apply beyond the programme-status and tier-published checks already stated: the tier's programme must match the programme being sold under, else `ERR_TIER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`; and the holder must not already own a pass in this programme whose `pass_status` is `PENDING_PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_PASS` (HTTP 422). One human, one live pass, per programme.

On a successful sale the pass takes `pass_status` `PENDING_PROCESSING` and is issued the next `media_number` in sequence. Its `contract_value_cents` is set to the selling tier's `base_price_cents` and its `billed_total_cents` starts equal to that figure. The `payment_mode` then decides the billing dimension immediately: a `PAY_IN_FULL` sale settles the contract on the spot, so `paid_to_date_cents` becomes equal to `contract_value_cents`, `outstanding_balance_cents` becomes 0, and `billing_status` moves `UNBILLED → PAID_IN_FULL`; a `PAYMENT_PLAN` sale leaves `billing_status` at `UNBILLED` until a plan is enrolled, with the whole contract value outstanding. The pass's other computed fields start empty or zero: `credited_amount_cents`, `visit_count`, `in_park_spend_cents` and `suspension_count` are all 0, `renewed_from_pass_id` is null for a new sale, and the programme's revenue rollups are recomputed to take the new pass in. `photo_captured` and `identity_bound` are both false, and `photo_hash` and `government_id_last4` are both null.

**Processing** is what turns a sold pass into a usable one, and it is the moment identity is bound. A clerk processes a pass by supplying the `photo_hash` — the digest of the photograph taken at the counter — and the `government_id_last4`, the last four digits of the identity document presented. Both are required strings and neither is validated beyond that — no digest shape is imposed on `photo_hash` and no length or numeric check is imposed on `government_id_last4` — so the counter can record whatever the document actually shows. On success the pass stores both values, sets `photo_captured` and `identity_bound` to true, advances `pass_status` from `PENDING_PROCESSING` to `ACTIVE`, causes the programme's `active_pass_count` to be recomputed, and increments the pass's `version` by 1. Processing requires the owning programme to be in `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`. It also happens exactly once per pass: a pass whose `pass_status` is anything other than `PENDING_PROCESSING` is refused with `ERR_PASS_ALREADY_PROCESSED` (HTTP 409). A pass that has not been processed cannot be used at a turnstile at all, which §3.7 enforces separately.

The binding is permanent in the strongest sense the model allows: `passholder_id` is fixed at creation and can never be changed by any operation in this API. Transfer of a pass from one human to another is prohibited outright — there is no endpoint that reassigns a pass, and the sharing-detection machinery in §3.8 exists precisely to catch attempts to work around that prohibition informally. A pass's `tier_id`, by contrast, does move, but only through the upgrade apply and upgrade reverse operations in §3.9.

A pass carries two independent status dimensions on the one record and they must be implemented as such. The **park-access dimension**, `pass_status`, takes the values `PENDING_PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`; only `EXPIRED` is terminal, and `REVOKED` deliberately is not, because a dismissed appeal restores a revoked pass. The **billing dimension**, `billing_status`, takes the values `UNBILLED`, `PLAN_CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT`, `DEFAULTED` and `PAID_IN_FULL`, has no terminal state at all, and moves only on plan enrolment, installment settlement, billing runs and reinstatement — never on a gate or access event. The two evolve independently: a pass may be `SUSPENDED` and `DEFAULTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `PLAN_CURRENT`, `ACTIVE` and `DELINQUENT`, or `EXPIRED` and `PAID_IN_FULL`. Reads of a single pass expose both dimensions and the entire money ledger; the collection may be filtered on `program_id`, `tier_id`, `passholder_id`, `renewed_from_pass_id`, `pass_status` and `billing_status`, and a caller acting as `Passholder` sees only their own passes in the listing.

### 3.4. Financing the Pass: Payment Plans and Installments

A guest who chose `PAYMENT_PLAN` at the counter pays over the season, and a `PassOfficeClerk` sets that up by enrolling the pass in a **payment plan**. The enrolment payload carries the `down_payment_cents` collected at the counter, the `installment_count` — the number of monthly installments, which must be at least 3 and at most 10 — the `first_due_date` on which installment number one falls, and the `expected_season_pass_version` that §2.7 requires. The plan records the pass it finances as `season_pass_id`, opens with `plan_status` `ACTIVE`, and charges a standard enrolment fee of 1500 cents onto the pass, recorded on the plan as `setup_fee_cents`.

Enrolment happens in one of two shapes and both must be implemented. The first enrolment applies to a pass whose `payment_mode` is `PAYMENT_PLAN` and whose `billing_status` is still `UNBILLED`; it moves `billing_status` from `UNBILLED` to `PLAN_CURRENT`, adds the plan's `setup_fee_cents` to `billed_total_cents`, adds `down_payment_cents` to `paid_to_date_cents`, recomputes `outstanding_balance_cents`, and increments the pass's `version` by 1. The second shape is **re-amortisation**: a pass that already has a plan in `plan_status` `ACTIVE` may be enrolled again, and the new plan supersedes the old one. `billing_status` stays at `PLAN_CURRENT`, the prior plan moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented by 1, every installment of that prior plan whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `VOIDED`, while every installment of that prior plan whose `status` is `MISSED` is carried over completely unchanged and still counts toward the cure amount at reinstatement. The new plan's `setup_fee_cents` is added to `billed_total_cents` and the new `down_payment_cents` is added to `paid_to_date_cents` — an operator who re-amortises three times has been charged three setup fees — and the pass's `version` increments by 1.

Enrolment is guarded in a fixed order, and that order is the whole of the guard: neither the pass's `payment_mode` nor its `billing_status` is inspected, so a `PAY_IN_FULL` pass or one already at `PAID_IN_FULL` may be enrolled just as a `PAYMENT_PLAN` pass may, and the two shapes above are distinguished purely by whether an `ACTIVE` plan already exists. The stale-version check of §2.7 comes first, yielding `ERR_STALE_PASS_VERSION`. Next, the pass's `pass_status` must be `ACTIVE`; a pass in any other access state — still awaiting processing, suspended, revoked or expired — is refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two commercial floors then apply, in this order. First, an `installment_count` outside the inclusive range 3 through 10 is rejected with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_COUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). Second, the park requires a fifth of the contract as a down payment: the minimum acceptable down payment is the pass's `contract_value_cents` multiplied by twenty hundredths, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and a `down_payment_cents` strictly below that figure is rejected with `ERR_DOWN_PAYMENT_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 422). Note that the minimum is measured against the contract value, not against the outstanding balance.

**Amortisation** produces the schedule and runs as a three-stage chain in which each stage consumes the previous stage's output.

Stage one produces the principal. `financed_amount_cents` is the pass's `outstanding_balance_cents` as it stands at the instant the plan is created, less the `down_payment_cents`, plus the 1500-cent enrolment fee.

Stage two splits that principal into level payments. `monthly_amount_cents` is `financed_amount_cents` divided by `installment_count`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Every installment from `sequence_no` 1 through `installment_count` minus 1 carries that figure as its `amount_cents`.

Stage three absorbs the residual so the schedule cannot drift. `final_installment_cents` is `financed_amount_cents` less `monthly_amount_cents` multiplied by `installment_count` minus 1, and the installment whose `sequence_no` equals `installment_count` carries `final_installment_cents` as its `amount_cents`. Rounding is introduced at stage two and nowhere else; the final installment takes whatever remainder that rounding produced, which means it may be a cent or two below — or above — the level payment. Worked through: a pass with 30000 cents outstanding, a 6000-cent down payment and seven installments finances 30000 less 6000 plus 1500, which is 25500; dividing by seven and rounding half up gives a level payment of 3643 cents for sequences one through six, totalling 21858, leaving 25500 less 21858, which is 3642 cents, for sequence seven. A pass with 12000 cents outstanding, a 3000-cent down payment and four installments finances 10500 and splits into four exactly equal installments of 2625 with no residual at all.

The schedule invariant that follows from those three stages must hold in every implementation: the sum of `amount_cents` across all installments of the plan equals `financed_amount_cents`, and equals the pass's `outstanding_balance_cents` immediately after the plan is created, once the setup fee has been billed and the down payment credited.

Due dates walk the calendar month by month. The installment at `sequence_no` k falls due on `first_due_date` advanced by k minus 1 calendar months, with the day of month clamped downward to the last day of the target month when the target month is short — a plan whose first due date is the 31st of January has its second installment due on the 28th or 29th of February, not on a nonexistent date and not spilling into March. Each installment also carries `payment_plan_id` naming its owning plan, a `status` and a `settled_at` timestamp that is null until the installment is paid.

An installment's `status` starts at `SCHEDULED` and may reach `SETTLED`, `MISSED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`. `SETTLED`, `WAIVED` and `VOIDED` are terminal in normal operation; `MISSED` is deliberately revisitable, because `SCHEDULED → MISSED → SETTLED` is the ordinary cure cycle. The one route back out of a terminal state is the compensation described in §3.8: `VOIDED → SCHEDULED` when a dismissed appeal restores a plan that revocation had terminated, which recomputes the pass's `outstanding_balance_cents` and increments the installment's `version` by 1.

Taking a monthly payment is the clerk's routine action. The settlement payload states the `amount_cents` tendered and may optionally state `paid_at`, the instant the money was taken; when `paid_at` is omitted the logical clock's current instant is used. Three preconditions guard the write, and their errors are ordered: an installment whose `status` is neither `SCHEDULED` nor `MISSED`, or whose owning plan's `plan_status` is not `ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE` (HTTP 409); a tendered `amount_cents` that does not exactly equal the installment's own `amount_cents` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the park takes the scheduled amount or nothing, neither part payment nor overpayment; and an installment on a pass whose `pass_status` is `REVOKED` is not payable either. A successful settlement drives `SCHEDULED → SETTLED` or `MISSED → SETTLED`, records `settled_at`, recomputes the plan's `settled_count` and `missed_count`, adds the amount to the pass's `paid_to_date_cents`, recomputes `outstanding_balance_cents`, recomputes the programme's `recognized_revenue_cents` and `deferred_revenue_cents`, and increments the installment's `version` by 1.

Settlement also drives two status transitions further up. If the settlement was of the last remaining `MISSED` installment, so that the active plan's `missed_count` has returned to 0, the pass's `billing_status` moves `DELINQUENT → PLAN_CURRENT` and its `version` increments by 1. If after the write the active plan holds no installment at all in `SCHEDULED` or in `MISSED`, the plan moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the pass's `billing_status` moves `PLAN_CURRENT → PAID_IN_FULL` with `outstanding_balance_cents` recomputed — which lands at 0 when no upgrade balance remains outstanding — and the pass's `version` increments by 1.

A plan therefore leaves `ACTIVE` for exactly one of three destinations, each with its own effects: `COMPLETED` when everything has been paid, `SUPERSEDED` when a replacement plan is enrolled, and `TERMINATED_ON_REVOCATION` when the owning pass is revoked. All three are terminal in ordinary operation, and only the third has a documented route back. Plans may be listed filtered by `season_pass_id` or `plan_status`, and installments filtered by `payment_plan_id` or `status`, so that a clerk can audit a schedule before taking money.

### 3.5. The Monthly Billing Run: Delinquency, Suspension, Default and Cure

Once a month a `ProgramAdmin` runs billing for a programme, supplying the `as_of_date` the run is evaluated against. The run is the only thing in the system that marks money late, and its ordering is contractual. Installments belonging to every plan of the programme whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` are examined in ascending `due_date`, then within a due date in ascending `sequence_no`, then in ascending `created_at` — never by identifier — so that out-of-order record creation cannot change the outcome.

For each installment whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `due_date` falls strictly before the run's `as_of_date`, taken in that order, the run drives `SCHEDULED → MISSED` and then walks a three-level cascade. At level one the owning plan's `missed_count` is recomputed and rises by one. At level two the delinquency ladder is applied against the programme's two thresholds: once the plan's `missed_count` is at least 1, the pass's `billing_status` moves `PLAN_CURRENT → DELINQUENT`; once `missed_count` reaches or exceeds the programme's `delinquency_suspension_threshold`, the pass's `pass_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and its `suspension_count` rises by one; and once `missed_count` reaches or exceeds the programme's `default_threshold`, the pass's `billing_status` moves `DELINQUENT → DEFAULTED`. Note carefully that while `missed_count` remains below the suspension threshold, `pass_status` is not touched at all — one missed payment makes a pass delinquent but leaves the guest walking through the gate — and that when the default threshold is reached, `pass_status` is already `SUSPENDED` from the earlier rung. At level three, because the pass became `SUSPENDED`, every reservation of that pass still in `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` and each affected park day recomputes its `reserved_count`, `released_count` and `remaining_slot_count`. The installment's `version` increments by 1, as does the pass's on each dimension change.

After the installment pass, the run performs two sweeps, and every sweep — like the installment pass itself — is scoped to this programme alone: the no-show sweep looks only at reservations whose park day belongs to this programme, and the quote sweep only at upgrade orders whose pass belongs to it, so a run on one programme never touches another's records. First, reservations: every reservation still in `CONFIRMED` whose park day's `calendar_date` falls strictly before the run's `as_of_date` becomes `NO_SHOW`, and each affected day's `reserved_count` drops by one while its `no_show_count` rises by one and `remaining_slot_count` is recomputed; separately, reservations on days falling on or after `as_of_date` that belong to a pass this run suspended become `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` instead, with `reserved_count` dropping by one, `released_count` rising by one and `remaining_slot_count` recomputed. The date split is the whole point: a past-dated reservation is a guest who did not show up, and a future-dated reservation on a suspended pass is a booking the park is taking back. Second, upgrade quotes: every upgrade order still in `QUOTED` whose `as_of_date` has reached or passed the calendar date of its `quote_expires_at` moves `QUOTED → EXPIRED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and can never be applied afterwards.

The run reports what it did, returning `missed_installment_count`, `delinquent_pass_count`, `defaulted_pass_count`, `suspended_pass_count`, `released_reservation_count`, `no_show_reservation_count` and `expired_upgrade_order_count` — and nothing else; the response `data` is that bare seven-field report rather than a programme record. Each of the three pass counts is a count of *distinct passes this run itself moved* into the state named, so a pass already `DELINQUENT` when the run began is not counted again however many further installments it misses, and a pass that was not `ACTIVE` on the access dimension is never counted as suspended. `missed_installment_count` by contrast counts installments, one per transition. Consider a plan with installments due on the first of September and the first of October, under the default thresholds of two and three. A run as of the fifteenth of September marks one installment missed, so the pass becomes `DELINQUENT` and stays `ACTIVE`. A run as of the second of October marks the second, so `missed_count` reaches two, the pass remains `DELINQUENT` on the billing dimension but moves to `SUSPENDED` on the access dimension, and its future confirmed reservations are released.

Curing is the way back. A `PassOfficeClerk` reinstates a suspended pass by tendering `cure_amount_cents` and optionally naming one `waive_installment_id` the park is forgiving. The required cure amount is the sum of `amount_cents` over every installment of the pass's active plan whose `status` is `MISSED`, less the `amount_cents` of the waived installment when one is named. A tendered `cure_amount_cents` that does not exactly equal that figure is rejected with `ERR_CURE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and at most one installment may be waived per reinstatement. Reinstatement requires the pass's `pass_status` to be `SUSPENDED`; a pass in any other access state is refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409), and this check precedes the cure-amount arithmetic. A named `waive_installment_id` that does not exist, or that exists but does not belong to the pass's active plan — including the case where the pass has no active plan at all — is refused with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND` earlier still. Membership of the active plan is the only test applied to the named installment; its `status` is not checked, so naming an installment that is not itself `MISSED` still subtracts that installment's `amount_cents` from the required cure amount, yet waives nothing and leaves `billed_total_cents` untouched, because only `MISSED` installments are transitioned.

A successful reinstatement moves `pass_status` from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` and does five things. Every `MISSED` installment of the active plan moves `MISSED → SETTLED` with `settled_at` set to the logical clock's current instant, except the one named by `waive_installment_id`, which moves `MISSED → WAIVED` instead; a waived installment reduces the pass's `billed_total_cents` by its `amount_cents` rather than crediting anything to `paid_to_date_cents`, and the plan's `missed_count` is recomputed either way. The tendered `cure_amount_cents` is added to `paid_to_date_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed. The `billing_status` returns to `PLAN_CURRENT`, or straight to `PAID_IN_FULL` when the cure leaves no installment in `SCHEDULED`; a pass whose `billing_status` had reached `DEFAULTED` cures the same way, moving `DEFAULTED → PLAN_CURRENT` — default is not a dead end, and reinstatement remains permitted once the full cure amount is paid. Reservations cancelled by the suspension are **not** restored: released slots stay released, and the guest must book again. Finally the pass's `version` increments by 1. A pass may be suspended and reinstated any number of times over a season, and `suspension_count` records how often it has happened.

### 3.6. Reserving a Crowded Day

On days the park expects to fill, access is rationed by **reservation**. Either a `PassOfficeClerk` acting for a guest or the `Passholder` directly books one, naming the `season_pass_id` doing the reserving, the `park_day_id` being reserved, and a local arrival window given as `arrival_window_start` and `arrival_window_end` in `HH:MM` form. The window is half-open: the start instant is inside it and the end instant is outside it. The park gates open at 08:00 and stop admitting at 22:00, so `arrival_window_start` must be at or after 08:00 and `arrival_window_end` must be at or before 22:00; values outside those bounds are malformed input and are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An `arrival_window_end` at or before `arrival_window_start`, which would describe an empty window, is refused with `ERR_ARRIVAL_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A new reservation opens in `status` `CONFIRMED`, leaves `redeemed_visit_id` null, raises the day's `reserved_count` by one, raises the day's monotonic `grant_count` by one, and recomputes `remaining_slot_count`. Getting there requires clearing a long ladder of checks, evaluated in this order, and the order is what determines which error a caller sees when several conditions hold at once:

1. A caller acting as `Passholder` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the pass's `passholder_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_PASS_OWNER`.
2. A programme not in `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATUS_INVALID`.
3. A pass whose `pass_status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
4. A pass whose `billing_status` is `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED` is refused with `ERR_PASS_BILLING_NOT_CURRENT` (HTTP 409) — the park does not hand out scarce capacity to a guest who is behind on payments.
5. A park day whose `day_class` is `CAPACITY_LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_CAPACITY_LOCKED`.
6. A park day whose `program_id` differs from the pass's `program_id` is refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`.
7. A park day whose `calendar_date` falls strictly before the logical clock's current date is refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422).
8. A malformed arrival window is refused with `ERR_ARRIVAL_WINDOW_INVALID`.
9. An active blockout assignment covering the pass's `tier_id` and this `park_day_id` is refused with `ERR_BLOCKOUT_DATE` (HTTP 422).
10. A pass that already holds a reservation on that day whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `REDEEMED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESERVATION` (HTTP 422) — one booking per pass per day; a previously cancelled or no-showed reservation does not block a rebooking.
11. A pass whose count of `CONFIRMED` reservations on park days classified `PEAK` or `CAPACITY_LOCKED`, taken over days whose `calendar_date` falls on or after the logical clock's current date, has already reached its tier's `peak_reservation_quota` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
12. A day whose `reserved_count` has reached or passed its `reservation_slot_capacity` is refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_CAPACITY_FULL` (HTTP 422) — the day as a whole is sold out.
13. Finally, the window check: the number of `CONFIRMED` reservations on that day whose own half-open interval from `arrival_window_start` up to but not including `arrival_window_end` overlaps the requested interval, if it has already reached the day's `window_slot_capacity`, is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_SLOT_FULL` (HTTP 422). Two windows overlap when each begins strictly before the other ends. This is a distinct budget from the day-level one: a day can have slots left overall while the mid-morning band is already full.

A reservation's `status` may reach any of five terminal outcomes besides `CONFIRMED`, and the distinction between them is the audit trail the park needs. `REDEEMED` means the guest arrived and the entry redeemed the booking. `CANCELLED_BY_HOLDER` means the guest or the clerk gave the slot back voluntarily. `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` means the pass was suspended or revoked and the park took the slot back. `CANCELLED_BY_BLOCKOUT` means a blockout — installed manually, installed by the capacity lock cascade, or newly applicable after an upgrade moved the pass onto a different tier — now covers the (tier, day) pair. `NO_SHOW` means the day passed with the booking unredeemed.

Voluntary cancellation is available to a `PassOfficeClerk` or to the owning `Passholder` and takes no request body. It drives `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED_BY_HOLDER`, drops the day's `reserved_count` by one, raises its `released_count` by one, recomputes `remaining_slot_count`, leaves `grant_count` untouched, and increments the reservation's `version` by 1. A reservation whose `status` is anything other than `CONFIRMED` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (HTTP 409). The three system-driven cancellations — by suspension, by blockout, by no-show sweep — each adjust the same counters in the same way, differing only in which counter receives the released unit, and each increments the reservation's `version` by 1. Reservations may be listed filtered on `season_pass_id`, `park_day_id`, `status` or `redeemed_visit_id`.

### 3.7. The Turnstile: Gate Entries, In-Park Spend and Sharing Risk

A **visit** is one gate entry. A `GateAgent` records it by naming the `season_pass_id` presented, the `park_day_id` being entered, the `entry_at` instant of the scan as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, and the `gate_code` of the turnstile bank used, which is one of `NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST` or `WEST`. The agent may also report the turnstile's photograph comparison as `photo_match`, one of `MATCH`, `MISMATCH` or `NOT_CHECKED`; when the field is omitted it defaults to `NOT_CHECKED`, because many turnstiles are unattended and compare nothing.

Gate entry is the most heavily guarded write in the system. Its checks are evaluated in the following order, and one of them is deliberately out of its natural place:

1. **Pass-office readiness first.** A pass whose `photo_captured` is false or whose `identity_bound` is false is refused with `ERR_PHOTO_NOT_CAPTURED` (HTTP 422). This precedes the general access-state check below, because those two flags can only be false while the pass is still `PENDING_PROCESSING`; if the check ran later, the not-active check would permanently shadow it and a turnstile could never tell a guest that no photograph is on file.
2. A programme not in `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATUS_INVALID`.
3. A pass whose `pass_status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE` — this is the code a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` pass meets at a gate, which is why it remains reachable despite the reordering above.
4. **The transfer-prohibition hold.** A pass with a sharing case in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEAL_PENDING`, or whose passholder's `status` is `BANNED`, is refused with `ERR_PASS_TRANSFER_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). Merely opening a case, before any officer looks at it, is enough to stop the card at the gate.
5. **Anti-passback.** If any earlier visit of the same pass has an `entry_at` falling within 90 minutes before the requested `entry_at`, the entry is refused with `ERR_ANTIPASSBACK_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). The buffer is a closed one at both ends: a second scan at exactly 90 minutes after the first is still a violation, only a gap strictly greater than an hour and a half is permitted, and a prior visit stamped at exactly the requested instant violates too. The comparison is one-directional — it looks only at existing visits at or before the requested `entry_at`, so back-dating a scan behind an existing one is not itself a violation — and it ignores which park day either visit belongs to. This is the front line against a card being handed back over a fence.
6. A park day whose `program_id` differs from the pass's `program_id` is refused with `ERR_PARK_DAY_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`.
7. An `entry_at` whose calendar date differs from the park day's `calendar_date` is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_DATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
8. An active blockout assignment covering the pass's `tier_id` and this `park_day_id` is refused with `ERR_BLOCKOUT_DATE`.
9. **The peak-day reservation requirement.** When the park day's `day_class` is `PEAK` or `CAPACITY_LOCKED`, and the pass's tier has `reservation_required_on_peak` set true, and the pass holds no reservation in `CONFIRMED` on that day, the entry is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

A successful entry creates the visit in `status` `OPEN` with `spend_cents` 0 and with `discount_applied_pct` already snapshotted from the entering tier's `in_park_discount_pct` — the snapshot is taken at entry, not deferred to the close, so an `OPEN` visit already reports it and a later upgrade cannot rewrite history. If the pass holds a reservation on that day whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose half-open arrival window contains the `entry_at` instant — the window's `HH:MM` bounds being compared against the UTC hour and minute of `entry_at` — that reservation is redeemed; should more than one confirmed reservation somehow match, the earliest-created one is taken and the rest are left alone: it moves `CONFIRMED → REDEEMED`, its `redeemed_visit_id` is set to the new visit's `id`, the visit's own `reservation_id` is set to the reservation's `id`, the day's `reserved_count`, `redeemed_count` and `remaining_slot_count` are recomputed, and the reservation's `version` increments by 1. On a day that required no reservation and where none was held, the visit's `reservation_id` stays null. The entry also raises the pass's `visit_count` — which counts the pass's visits in `OPEN` or `CLOSED` — and recomputes the holder's `photo_mismatch_count`, the number of visits across every pass of that holder whose `photo_match` is `MISMATCH`.

**Sharing risk** is re-scored on every gate entry, after the new visit row has been recorded, and the scoring model deliberately depends on which kind of day the guest walked into. Two variants exist, exactly one is applied per entry, and the selector is the entered park day's `day_class`.

When the entered day's `day_class` is `PEAK` or `CAPACITY_LOCKED`, the **gate-cluster** variant applies: on a crowded day the abuse signal is one media number appearing at several turnstile banks. Take, for each calendar date on which this pass has any visit, the number of distinct `gate_code` values used by this pass on that date, and let the distinct-gate figure be the largest of those per-date counts. Let the mismatch figure be the number of visits of this pass whose `photo_match` is `MISMATCH`, over the pass's whole history. The score is 40 points for each distinct gate beyond the first — that is, 40 multiplied by the distinct-gate figure less one, treated as zero if the subtraction would go negative — plus 25 points for each mismatch.

When the entered day's `day_class` is `STANDARD`, the **rolling-window** variant applies: on an ordinary day the abuse signal is unusual visit frequency in the trailing week. Count the visits of this pass whose `entry_at` falls in the seven days up to and including the logical clock's current instant — that is, at or after the instant seven days before the clock and at or before the clock itself — and call that the window entry count. Count the same set restricted to visits whose `photo_match` is `MISMATCH`, and call that the window mismatch count. The park allows three free entries a week before frequency itself becomes suspicious, so the score is 15 points for each window entry beyond the third — 15 multiplied by the window entry count less three, treated as zero if the subtraction would go negative — plus 35 points for each window mismatch.

The two variants are genuinely independent and neither dominates the other. Take a pass with three entries in the trailing seven days, two of them on one calendar date through the `NORTH` and `SOUTH` banks, exactly one of the three showing `photo_match` of `MISMATCH`. Scored as a gate cluster, that is 40 for the second distinct gate plus 25 for the single mismatch, which is 65. Scored as a rolling window, that is nothing for frequency, because three entries are exactly the free allowance, plus 35 for the mismatch, which is 35. Identical visit history, two very different numbers — and under the default auto-open threshold of 65 the first fires an investigation while the second stays silent.

Whichever variant applied, the resulting integer becomes the passholder's `sharing_risk_score`; there is no rounding, since every weight is a whole number. If that score is greater than or equal to the programme's `sharing_case_auto_open_score`, and the pass does not already have a sharing case in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEAL_PENDING`, the system opens a sharing case by itself: the case takes `case_status` `OPEN`, `review_round` 0, `opened_by_user_id` the literal string `SYSTEM`, `opened_by_role` the value `SYSTEM`, and `risk_score_at_open` the score just computed. A system-opened case cites no evidence and carries no observation, so its `evidence_visit_ids` is the empty array and its `note` is null — the evidence rules that bind an agent do not apply to the machine. The consequence lands on the very next scan, because a pass with an open case is under a transfer-prohibition hold.

**Closing a visit** posts the guest's in-park spend for the day. A `GateAgent` closes it by supplying `spend_cents`, the amount to record, which must be zero or greater. The visit moves `OPEN → CLOSED` and `spend_cents` is stored exactly as tendered; the figure the agent supplies is already understood to be net of the `discount_applied_pct` the visit snapshotted at entry, so the close applies no arithmetic of its own to it, the pass's `in_park_spend_cents` — the sum of `spend_cents` over its visits in `CLOSED` — is recomputed, the programme's spend-per-visit analytics are recomputed, and the visit's `version` increments by 1. A visit whose `status` is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_VISIT_ALREADY_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). Visits may be listed filtered on `season_pass_id`, `park_day_id`, `status` or `reservation_id`.

### 3.8. Sharing Cases: Investigation, Revocation and Appeal

A **sharing case** is the park's investigation record, and its workflow is built around a hard separation of duties: only a `GateAgent` may open one, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` — acting under a different `X-User-ID` than the opener — may decide one.

An agent opens a case by naming the `season_pass_id` under investigation, an `evidence_visit_ids` array citing the gate entries that raised the suspicion, and a free-text `note` recording the observation. The evidence must be real: an empty `evidence_visit_ids` array, or any cited visit whose `season_pass_id` differs from the case's `season_pass_id`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVIDENCE` (HTTP 422); a cited visit that does not exist at all is refused earlier with `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`. On success the case opens with `case_status` `OPEN` and `review_round` 0, records `opened_by_user_id` as the agent's `X-User-ID` and `opened_by_role` as `GATE_AGENT`, and captures `risk_score_at_open` from the holder's `sharing_risk_score` as it stands at that moment. `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_rationale` and `revocation_forfeit_cents` are all null until a decision is taken. `note` is a required string, and the evidence check is the only business guard on the write: the pass's `pass_status` is not inspected, and neither is the presence of an earlier case, so an agent may open a second case against a pass that already has one in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEAL_PENDING` — the one-case-at-a-time restraint applies only to the system's own auto-open path in §3.7. From the instant the case exists, the pass is under a transfer-prohibition hold and further gate entries are refused.

A `ComplianceOfficer` takes the case under review, which drives `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW` and raises `review_round` from 0 to 1, incrementing the case's `version` by 1. The same action serves a case returning from appeal, driving `APPEAL_PENDING → UNDER_REVIEW` and raising `review_round` from 1 to 2. Taking under review requires the case to be in `OPEN` or `APPEAL_PENDING`; any other `case_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Adjudication takes a `decision` — either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED` — and a `rationale`. It requires the case to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, and again `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` answers any other state. The segregation-of-duties check, refusing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the officer's `X-User-ID` equals the case's `opened_by_user_id`, is evaluated before the state check. Both outcomes record `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_rationale`, and both increment the case's `version` by 1. Beyond that they diverge sharply, and both branches carry equally binding detail.

A decision of `SUBSTANTIATED` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → SUBSTANTIATED` and snapshots `revocation_forfeit_cents` from the pass's `paid_to_date_cents` at that instant — the figure the park is about to keep. It then runs a three-level revocation cascade. At level one the pass's `pass_status` moves to `REVOKED`, from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and park access is denied at every gate; the pass's `version` increments by 1. At level two, because the pass was revoked, its payment plan whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED_ON_REVOCATION`, and every installment of that plan whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `VOIDED`, with the plan's and each installment's `version` incremented by 1 and the pass's `outstanding_balance_cents` recomputed. At level three, because the plan was terminated, the programme's `forfeited_revenue_cents` is increased by the pass's `paid_to_date_cents`, and the programme's `base_revenue_cents`, `recognized_revenue_cents`, `deferred_revenue_cents` and `active_pass_count` are all recomputed with this pass no longer in the revenue set. Independently, every reservation of the pass still in `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` and the affected park days recompute their counters. And if this is the **second** case reaching `SUBSTANTIATED` against any pass of that holder, the passholder's `status` moves `ACTIVE → BANNED` — one strike revokes a pass, two strikes bar the person.

A decision of `DISMISSED` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → DISMISSED`, the case's terminal state, and its effect depends entirely on `review_round`. When `review_round` is 1, the case had never been substantiated, the pass was never revoked, and nothing changes beyond the case's own fields — the hold simply lifts and the guest walks in again. When `review_round` is 2 or more, the case had been substantiated and then appealed, so the dismissal is a full compensation of the revocation cascade, walked backwards in three levels. At the first level the pass's `pass_status` returns `REVOKED → ACTIVE`. At the second level, because the revocation was undone, the payment plan whose `plan_status` is `TERMINATED_ON_REVOCATION` returns to `ACTIVE` and every installment of that plan whose `status` is `VOIDED` returns to `SCHEDULED`. At the third level, because the plan was restored, the programme's `forfeited_revenue_cents` is reduced by the case's stored `revocation_forfeit_cents`, and `base_revenue_cents`, `recognized_revenue_cents`, `deferred_revenue_cents` and `active_pass_count` are recomputed with the pass back in the revenue set. Each touched record's `version` increments by 1. Reservations that the revocation cancelled are not restored.

Between substantiation and that second review sits the **appeal**, and only the guest may lodge it. A `Passholder` whose `X-User-ID` equals the pass's `passholder_id` appeals by supplying a `statement`, which is recorded into the case's `decision_rationale`. The appeal drives `SUBSTANTIATED → APPEAL_PENDING` and increments the case's `version` by 1; the pass stays `REVOKED` throughout the appeal. Exactly one appeal is permitted per case, enforced by the requirement that `review_round` be less than 2 at the time of the appeal; a case whose `review_round` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409), and that check follows the `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` check that requires the case to be in `SUBSTANTIATED`. The cycle `SUBSTANTIATED → APPEAL_PENDING → UNDER_REVIEW` therefore runs at most once, and the second visit to `UNDER_REVIEW` is the last: from there the case either returns to `SUBSTANTIATED`, where the appeal allowance is exhausted, or reaches `DISMISSED` with the full compensation. Cases may be listed filtered on `season_pass_id` or `case_status`.

### 3.9. Moving Up and Coming Back: Upgrades, Renewals and Season Reporting

Mid-season, a guest may want more park. The **upgrade order** is a two-phase instrument: a `PassOfficeClerk` first produces a binding quote, and then, when the guest pays, applies it. Quoting names the `season_pass_id` upgrading, the `source_tier_id` the pass currently sits on, the `target_tier_id` requested, and the `credit_mode` deciding how the amounts already committed are credited, which is either `PAID_TO_DATE` or `PRORATED_VALUE`. The guards run in this order: the programme must be in `SALES_OPEN`, `SEASON_ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_OPEN`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATUS_INVALID`; the target tier must be `PUBLISHED`, else `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the pass's `pass_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`; both the source and the target tier must belong to the pass's own programme, else `ERR_TIER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`; and finally, if the target tier's `base_price_cents` is less than or equal to the source tier's `base_price_cents`, or if the supplied `source_tier_id` is not in fact the pass's current `tier_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_AN_UPGRADE` (HTTP 422). This is the reason tier prices are not required to rise with `tier_rank`: broader access is not always dearer, and only price decides whether a move is an upgrade.

The **credit** is where the two modes part company, and they are independent of one another. Under `PAID_TO_DATE`, the raw credit is simply the pass's `paid_to_date_cents` — every cent the guest has actually handed over on the source tier, comprising the down payment, all settled installments and any prior upgrade payments. Under `PRORATED_VALUE`, the raw credit is the unconsumed share of the source tier's list price, measured in remaining operating days: count all park days belonging to the pass's programme and call that the total-days figure; count those of them whose `calendar_date` falls on or after the logical clock's current date and call that the future-days figure; the remaining ratio is the future-days figure divided by the total-days figure, rounded half up to four decimal places, except that when the programme has no park days at all the ratio is taken as `0.0000` rather than dividing; and the raw credit is the source tier's `base_price_cents` multiplied by that ratio, truncated down to a whole number of cents. The modes therefore move in opposite directions over a season — the paid-to-date credit rises as installments settle and never looks at the calendar, while the prorated credit falls as the season is consumed and never looks at how much has been paid — and on identical data either can be the larger. A Bronze pass listed at 12000 cents upgrading to Gold, with 8250 cents paid to date and three of the programme's four park days still ahead, credits 8250 under `PAID_TO_DATE`; under `PRORATED_VALUE` the ratio is `0.7500` and the raw credit is 12000 multiplied by that, which is 9000.

The **settlement total** is then assembled in six steps whose order is pinned, because permuting them changes the answer:

1. `gross_delta_cents` is the target tier's `base_price_cents` less the source tier's `base_price_cents`. A gross delta of zero or less is the `ERR_NOT_AN_UPGRADE` condition already stated.
2. `raw_credit_cents` is the credit produced by the requested `credit_mode`, exactly as described above.
3. `credit_cents` is the smaller of `raw_credit_cents` and `gross_delta_cents`. This cap is what stops an upgrade from ever refunding money: the park credits at most the price difference, never more.
4. `net_delta_cents` is `gross_delta_cents` less `credit_cents`.
5. `admin_fee_cents` is a banded fee keyed on `net_delta_cents` — and on the net delta, not on the gross. When `net_delta_cents` is 5000 cents or less, the fee is 0. Otherwise, when `net_delta_cents` is 20000 cents or less, the fee is 1000 cents. Above 20000 cents, the fee is 2500 cents.
6. `total_due_cents` is `net_delta_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`.

Everything here is whole cents and no rounding is introduced after step 2. Step 5's dependence on the post-credit figure is the trap worth stating twice: a Gold pass listed at 30000 cents moving to Platinum at 55000 cents, under `PAID_TO_DATE` with 24215 cents paid, has a gross delta of 25000, a raw credit of 24215, a capped credit of 24215, a net delta of 785, and therefore a fee of nothing at all, for a total due of 785 cents. Keying the band off the gross delta instead would have charged 2500.

A new order opens with `status` `QUOTED`, records the computed `gross_delta_cents`, `raw_credit_cents`, `credit_cents`, `net_delta_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents`, stamps `quoted_at` with the logical clock's current instant, sets `quote_expires_at` to fourteen days after `quoted_at`, and leaves `payment_amount_cents` and `reverse_reason` null. As §3.5 describes, a billing run whose `as_of_date` has reached the calendar date of `quote_expires_at` moves the order `QUOTED → EXPIRED`, after which it can never be applied.

**Applying** the upgrade takes the money and moves the pass. The clerk supplies `payment_amount_cents`; the order must be in `QUOTED`, the pass's `pass_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the pass must still sit on the order's `source_tier_id` — a quote overtaken by another upgrade is dead. A pass that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`; an order in the wrong status, or one whose pass has moved off the expected tier, is refused with `ERR_UPGRADE_ORDER_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (HTTP 409); and a `payment_amount_cents` that does not exactly equal the order's `total_due_cents` is refused with `ERR_UPGRADE_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), in that order. On success the order moves `QUOTED → APPLIED` and five things happen: the pass's `tier_id` becomes the `target_tier_id` and its `contract_value_cents` becomes the target tier's `base_price_cents`; `billed_total_cents` rises by `gross_delta_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`; `credited_amount_cents` rises by `credit_cents`, `paid_to_date_cents` rises by `payment_amount_cents`, and `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed from the §2.10 identity; blockout exposure is re-derived from the new tier for all future days, so that any reservation still in `CONFIRMED` on a day now blocked for the new tier becomes `CANCELLED_BY_BLOCKOUT` with the affected park day counters recomputed; and both the pass's `version` and the order's `version` increment by 1. A guest who upgrades into a tier the capacity-lock cascade has blocked loses the booking — which is why the clerk should read the calendar before selling the upgrade.

**Reversing** an applied upgrade is reserved for a `ComplianceOfficer`, never the clerk who applied it. The officer supplies a `reason`, which is recorded as `reverse_reason`. The order must be in `APPLIED` and the pass must still sit on the order's `target_tier_id`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_UPGRADE_ORDER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. On success the order moves `APPLIED → REVERSED`, its terminal state, and every ledger effect is compensated exactly: the pass's `tier_id` returns to `source_tier_id` and `contract_value_cents` returns to the source tier's `base_price_cents`; `billed_total_cents` falls by `gross_delta_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`; `credited_amount_cents` falls by `credit_cents`; `paid_to_date_cents` falls by `payment_amount_cents`; `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed so that the §2.10 ledger identity still holds with no residue; and both the pass's and the order's `version` increment by 1. The order keeps its recorded `payment_amount_cents` after the reversal rather than having it cleared back to null, so the reversed order remains a complete record of what was once taken. The reversal inspects neither the programme's status nor the pass's `pass_status`, so a suspended, revoked or expired pass can still have an applied upgrade unwound. Reservations cancelled by the upgrade's blockout re-derivation are not restored. Orders may be listed filtered on `season_pass_id`, `source_tier_id`, `target_tier_id` or `status`.

**Renewal campaigns** run at the other end of the season, once a programme has reached `RENEWAL_OPEN`. Anyone with a legitimate interest — a `ProgramAdmin`, a `PassOfficeClerk`, or the guest themselves — may price a renewal offer for a pass. The quote is a read, so it optionally takes its `target_tier_id` as a query parameter rather than in a body, and that tier is resolved — yielding `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND` when it names nothing — before the path's own `pass_id` is looked up; when it is omitted, the offer is priced like-for-like on the pass's current tier. A pass whose `pass_status` is `PENDING_PROCESSING` or `REVOKED`, or a pass that some other pass has already been renewed from — that is, a pass which some existing season pass already names in its `renewed_from_pass_id` — is not renewable and both quoting and renewing are refused with `ERR_PASS_NOT_RENEWABLE` (HTTP 409). Renewability is the *only* business guard on the quote: quoting checks no programme status and does not require the target tier to be `PUBLISHED`, so an offer can be priced against a `DESIGN`-stage programme or a draft tier long before the renewal window opens. Those two checks bite only on the renewal itself. One renewal per pass, and no renewing a pass that was never processed or that the park revoked.

The quote is computed in four movements and reports each of them. `standard_price_cents` is the target tier's `base_price_cents`. `early_bird_applied` is true when the logical clock's current date is on or before the `early_bird_deadline` of the *target tier's* owning programme — the programme being renewed into, not the one the prior pass was sold under, which matters only when the two differ — the deadline day itself counting as inside the window, and false otherwise. `discounted_price_cents` is `standard_price_cents` reduced by fifteen percent of itself — that is, `standard_price_cents` less the product of `standard_price_cents` and fifteen hundredths rounded half up to a whole number of cents — when `early_bird_applied` is true, and is simply `standard_price_cents` when it is false. `retention_credit_cents` rewards loyalty against the prior pass's `visit_count`, capped so that the credit can never swallow the price: the raw retention credit is 3000 cents when the prior pass's `visit_count` is 10 or more, otherwise 1500 cents when it is 5 or more, otherwise 0; the cap is twenty percent of `discounted_price_cents`, truncated down to a whole number of cents; and `retention_credit_cents` is the smaller of the raw credit and that cap. Finally `quoted_price_cents` is `discounted_price_cents` less `retention_credit_cents`. Worked through: a target Gold tier at 30000 cents, with the logical clock at the first of November 2026 and the programme's `early_bird_deadline` at the fifteenth, and a prior pass with a `visit_count` of 6 — early bird applies, the discount is 4500, the discounted price is 25500, the raw retention credit is 1500, the cap is twenty percent of 25500 truncated, which is 5100, so the credit stays 1500 and the quoted price is 24000. Alongside those five figures the quote response echoes the `season_pass_id` it was priced for and the `target_tier_id` it resolved to — seven fields in all — and it reports no intermediate raw retention figure.

Executing the renewal is the clerk's write. It names the `target_program_id` to renew into, the `target_tier_id` to renew onto, and the `payment_mode` for the new pass. The target programme's `status` must be exactly `RENEWAL_OPEN`; any other status is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_IN_RENEWAL` (HTTP 409), and that check precedes the `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED` check on the target tier, which in turn precedes the `ERR_PASS_NOT_RENEWABLE` check and then the `ERR_TIER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` check requiring the target tier to belong to the target programme. On success the system mints a brand-new season pass in the target programme whose `renewed_from_pass_id` names the pass being renewed and whose `contract_value_cents` equals the `quoted_price_cents` the renewal quote produces — the renewal price, not the tier list price. The new pass starts in `pass_status` `PENDING_PROCESSING` and `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, or `PAID_IN_FULL` when its `payment_mode` is `PAY_IN_FULL`, and it must be processed at the pass office like any other new pass before it will open a turnstile. The duplicate-live-pass guard of §3.3 does **not** apply here: renewal mints its pass regardless of whether the holder already owns a live one in the target programme, since the four checks listed above are the whole of the renewal's guard. The prior pass is left completely untouched, keeping its own status, ledger and history.

**Season reporting** closes the loop. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` reads the programme's revenue and visitation analysis, which is computed over four explicitly-defined sets. The **revenue set** is every season pass of this programme whose `pass_status` is `PENDING_PROCESSING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` — every live or spent pass, with `REVOKED` passes excluded. The **forfeit set** is every season pass of this programme whose `pass_status` is `REVOKED`. The **visit set** is every visit whose `season_pass_id` belongs to a pass in the revenue set. The **spend set** is the visit set restricted to visits whose `status` is `CLOSED`.

From those sets, nine figures are reported. `base_revenue_cents` is the sum of `contract_value_cents` over the revenue set. `recognized_revenue_cents` is the sum of `paid_to_date_cents` over the revenue set. `deferred_revenue_cents` is the sum of `outstanding_balance_cents` over the revenue set. `forfeited_revenue_cents` is the sum of `paid_to_date_cents` over the forfeit set. `active_pass_count` is the number of the programme's passes whose `pass_status` is `ACTIVE`. `total_visit_count` is the number of members of the visit set. `visits_per_active_pass` is `total_visit_count` divided by `active_pass_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places, except that when `active_pass_count` is zero the figure is reported as `0.00` rather than dividing. `total_in_park_spend_cents` is the sum of `spend_cents` over the spend set. `spend_per_visit_cents` is `total_in_park_spend_cents` divided by the number of members of the spend set, truncated down to a whole number of cents, except that when the spend set is empty the figure is reported as `0` rather than dividing. The same nine derived figures are also surfaced on the programme record itself for `base_revenue_cents`, `recognized_revenue_cents`, `deferred_revenue_cents`, `forfeited_revenue_cents` and `active_pass_count`. The analysis response carries the identifier of the programme it describes as `program_id` alongside the nine figures, and nothing further.

The interaction between revocation and this report is worth stating plainly, because it is the one place money appears to move without any payment being taken. A revoked pass leaves the base, recognised and deferred figures entirely and reappears inside `forfeited_revenue_cents`; a dismissed appeal moves it back the other way. No cash changes hands in either direction — only the classification does.
