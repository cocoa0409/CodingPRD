# Lubricant Blending & Packaging API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An independent lubricant blender lives or dies on two things it cannot buy back once lost: the integrity of what is in the tank, and the credibility of the approval marks printed on the label. This document specifies the API for a mid-sized blending and packaging operation that buys base oils, viscosity-index improvers, additive packages and dyes by the lot, formulates them into finished-product specifications, blends them in shared vessels that never come back perfectly empty, tests and retains what comes out, packs the result into house-brand and private-label formats, and sells it either as bulk tanker litres or as palletised units. The same system also runs the used-oil take-back arm of the business, because collections are scheduled against delivery orders and the resulting credit is drawn back down against future invoices.

Three problems dominate the design. First, **tank heels**: every blend leaves a residue behind, and the next product charged into that vessel inherits it, so chemistry compatibility and the ratio of residue to fresh blend have to be adjudicated at the moment of charging rather than discovered at the laboratory bench. Second, **claim control**: an API, ILSAC or OEM approval is asserted against a specification at a particular revision, and the instant that specification moves, every claim pinned to the old revision, every label carrying that claim's printed code, and every open order line quoting that label must be walked back automatically. Third, **cost and price duality**: the provisional raw-material cost booked when a batch is charged is deliberately computed differently from the final cost frozen when blending completes, and the display price of a single packaged unit is deliberately not a factor of the extended line total. Both pairs of figures are expected to disagree, and both halves of each pair must be reported.

Every actor in the plant reaches the system through the same HTTP surface, mounted at the server root, and is separated from every other actor by role. The design deliberately splits testing from disposition, formulation from certification, and the blend floor from the commercial desk, so that no single operator can both create a quality record and act on it. The sections that follow define the global contract first, then the business narrative that an implementer must reconstruct the workflows from, then the endpoint and error catalogues.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent — a header that is present but carries an empty string counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The eight roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ProductManager`, `BlendOperator`, `QCTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SalesRep` and `Customer`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the master-data operator: it registers buying accounts, books in component lots, registers blending vessels and filling lines, drives the logical clock, and takes filling lines out of and back into service. A `ProductManager` owns finished-product specifications, blend recipes, package configurations and the specification-change workflow. A `BlendOperator` runs the blend floor — creating batches, charging tanks, completing blends, reworking quarantined material, flushing vessels and scheduling and running packaging — and is deliberately barred from recording any laboratory result or releasing any batch. A `QCTechnician` is the laboratory analyst: it records test results, books retain jars and assays incoming used-oil loads. A `QualityManager` holds disposition authority: releasing, quarantining, disposing and waiving batches, quarantining component lots, disposing of retain jars, and ruling on the viscosity branch of a specification change. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns approval claims end to end and rules on the certification branch of a specification change. A `SalesRep` staffs the commercial desk: opening orders and their lines, confirming, holding, resuming, invoicing and voiding them, applying take-back credit, and running collections. A `Customer` is the buying account acting for itself.

Identity semantics differ between the staff roles and the buying account, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ProductManager`, `BlendOperator`, `QCTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `SalesRep`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `technician_user_id` on a laboratory result, where the transition that writes the result stamps it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting customer account, and ownership checks on any resource that references a customer account compare that resource's customer reference to `X-User-ID` directly.

That comparison is enforced as a scope rule. A caller acting as `Customer` may only read or write resources whose `customer_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`; any attempt to read another account, another account's orders or order lines, or another account's take-back collections, or to schedule a collection naming another account, is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. The scope rule guards account reads, single-order and order-list reads, single-collection and collection-list reads, and collection creation. On collection creation it is the named `customer_id` alone that is compared: an `order_id` supplied alongside it is resolved for existence but is not itself scope-checked. On the two list reads a `Customer` caller supplying no `customer_id` filter is silently narrowed to its own rows rather than refused, and one supplying a `customer_id` naming another account is refused.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any row remains beyond the current window. The default ordering for every list endpoint is `created_at` ascending, oldest first. Because the logical clock of §2.4 only moves when it is driven, rows created under the same clock reading carry an identical `created_at`; every such tie is broken by the order in which the rows were inserted, earliest insertion first, and never by `id`. The single deliberate exception is the component-lot listing, which is ordered `created_at` descending so that the most recently received material appears first — and there the tie-break inverts along with the primary key, latest insertion first.

Filter parameters are additive: supplying several narrows the result set to rows matching all of them, and an unrecognised filter value that is not a member of the relevant enumeration is an invalid request. Filters that take a free-form string or an identifier rather than an enumeration — `component_code`, `product_code` and the various `*_id` filters — are matched literally and are never existence-checked: one naming a row that does not exist is a well-formed request that simply matches nothing and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation as well. Two resource kinds are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry neither field: recipe components — the individual material lines of a blend recipe — and laboratory results. Both of those carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, and nothing in the system ever mutates them.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** which reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` before it is first set and never falls back to real time thereafter. A `PlatformAdmin`-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, taking a single field `to_iso8601` and returning the resulting `current_utc`; it advances or rewinds the clock and mutates no entity. Every server-stamped instant in the system is read from that clock: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `tested_at`, `retained_at`, `retention_until`, `valid_until`, `effective_from`, `last_flush_at` and `last_credit_event_at`. That list is exhaustive — the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` sits deliberately outside it and is stamped from real wall-clock time, so it neither follows the logical clock nor is disturbed by it.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. Each templated segment is looked up in the order it appears in the URL, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the response — so a request naming a non-existent batch and a non-existent retain jar reports the batch as missing, never the retain jar. Each resource kind has its own not-found identifier: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLEND_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLEND_TANK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLEND_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAIN_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_CONFIGURATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGING_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAKEBACK_COLLECTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SPEC_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. All of them carry HTTP 404.

The same identifiers are used when a reference supplied inside a request body fails to resolve. Body-level reference resolution applies to recipe creation, batch creation, batch charging, package-configuration creation, packaging-run scheduling, order creation, order-line creation, take-back collection scheduling and approval-claim creation; in each case a `customer_id`, `product_spec_id`, `blend_recipe_id`, `blend_tank_id`, `blend_batch_id`, `package_configuration_id`, `packaging_line_id` or `order_id` that names no existing row produces the corresponding not-found identifier with HTTP 404. Where one body carries several references they too are resolved in a fixed order and the first failure decides: packaging-run scheduling resolves `blend_batch_id`, then `package_configuration_id`, then `packaging_line_id`; take-back collection scheduling resolves `customer_id` and then `order_id`; package-configuration creation resolves `product_spec_id` and then an optional `customer_id`; and charging resolves the path's batch before the body's `blend_tank_id`.

Reads that traverse a relationship backwards are available throughout: the draws taken from a component lot, the components of a recipe, the draws, laboratory results and retain jars of a batch, the order lines quoting a package configuration, and the recipes, claims, configurations and change requests belonging to a specification are all reachable, either as a dedicated sub-collection or through a filter on the parent's identifier.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder

Several checks can fail on the same request, and which identifier comes back is not a matter of implementation taste. Every check in the system occupies a fixed rung, and the rungs are evaluated in strictly ascending order; the **first** check that fails decides the response and no later check is consulted. Where a rung does not apply to the endpoint being called, it is simply skipped and evaluation continues at the next rung. The complete ladder, in evaluation order, is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the eight declared roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorisation — the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised list; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape — every required body field is present, every enumerated value is a declared member, every numeric field satisfies its declared range, and every datetime parses as ISO 8601 UTC; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint and to every read endpoint that accepts query parameters. Conditional requirements are request shape too, and are checked here rather than later: the `licence_number` that an `APPROVE` verdict on an approval claim must carry is a rung-3 check, so an `APPROVE` with no licence number aimed at a claim that does not exist reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and never `ERR_APPROVAL_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. A field of the wrong JSON type fails here as well — a number supplied as a string, or a boolean supplied where a number is expected.
4. Path existence — every templated path segment resolves, checked left to right; otherwise the matching not-found identifier (404). Applies to every endpoint with a templated segment.
5. Body reference existence — every reference supplied in the request body resolves; otherwise the matching not-found identifier (404).
6. Account scope — a `Customer` caller touches only its own rows; otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
7. Segregation of duties — the caller's `X-User-ID` does not appear as `technician_user_id` on any laboratory result of the target batch; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards batch release and QC waiver.
8. Specification staleness — `expected_product_spec_version` equals the target specification's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_SPEC_VERSION` (412). Guards opening a change request against a specification.
9. Account staleness — `expected_customer_version` equals the order's account's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` (412). Guards drawing take-back credit against an order.
10. Own-state legality — the target resource sits in a declared originating state for the requested transition; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every write that drives a state transition, and additionally to drawing take-back credit, which requires the order to be `CONFIRMED` even though it changes no status field. This rung also absorbs every companion precondition the catalogue of §5 gives no dedicated identifier for, whether it reads the target's other state dimension, the state of some related row, or a bare eligibility condition; all of the following report an undifferentiated `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409): an approval claim still `ACTIVE` blocking a specification withdrawal; a batch still `CHARGING` or `BLENDED` blocking a vessel flush; a packaging run still `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` blocking a configuration retirement; a batch whose `qc_status` is not `PENDING` at charge cancellation or on the rework path into `BLENDED`; a batch whose `qc_status` is neither `PASSED` nor `WAIVED` at release; a batch whose `qc_status` is not `FAILED` at rework; a batch whose `qc_status` is neither `PENDING` nor `SAMPLED` when a laboratory result is recorded; an order carrying no `OPEN` line at confirmation; an order whose `total_cents` is not greater than zero at invoicing; and a filling line already sitting in the very `target_status` being requested of it.
11. Tank availability — the named vessel is `AVAILABLE` at the instant of the charge; otherwise `ERR_TANK_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Guards charging.
12. Recipe activity — the batch's recipe is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Guards charging.
13. Specification activity — the governing specification is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_SPEC_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Guards six operations: recipe activation, charging, approval-claim submission, package-configuration activation, change-request submission, and making a cleared change effective.
14. Batch release state — the batch being packed has `production_status` `RELEASED`; otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED` (409). Guards packaging-run scheduling.
15. Configuration activity — the referenced package configuration is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Guards five operations: packaging-run scheduling, packaging-run start, order-line creation, order confirmation and order resumption.
16. Line availability — the filling line is `AVAILABLE`; otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Guards packaging-run scheduling and packaging-run start.
17. Food-grade barrier — the absolute chemistry barrier described in §3.4; otherwise `ERR_FLUSH_REQUIRED` (422). Guards charging.
18. Heel ratio ceiling — the residue share sits at or below its applicable limit; otherwise `ERR_HEEL_CONTAMINATION_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards charging.
19. Blocked stock — a shortfall that quarantined lots of the same material code would have covered; otherwise `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` (422). Guards charging.
20. Treat rate floor — the recipe's additive treat rate meets the specification's minimum; otherwise `ERR_TREAT_RATE_BELOW_SPEC` (422). Guards recipe activation.
21. Predicted viscosity band — the recipe's predicted viscosity lies inside the specification band; otherwise `ERR_PREDICTED_VISCOSITY_OUT_OF_SPEC` (422). Guards recipe activation.
22. Claim substantiation — a claim-bearing pack may only be filled from a claim-eligible batch, and a QC waiver may only be granted on a batch that is not claim-eligible; otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (422). Guards packaging-run scheduling and QC waiver.
23. Retain floor — the batch holds at least the required number of retain jars; otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RETAINS` (422). Guards batch release.
24. Tank window collision — the requested occupancy window does not collide on the same vessel; otherwise `ERR_TANK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards charging.
25. Line capacity — the requested packaging window does not exhaust the line's heads; otherwise `ERR_LINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards packaging-run scheduling.
26. Window sanity — `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). Guards charging and packaging-run scheduling.
27. Retention window — the logical clock has reached the jar's `retention_until`; otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_WINDOW_ACTIVE` (422). Guards retain disposal.
28. Fraction sum — the recipe's mass fractions total exactly 100.00; otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_FRACTION_SUM` (422). Guards recipe creation and recipe activation.
29. Pinned revision currency — the recipe's pinned specification revision equals the specification's current `revision`; otherwise `ERR_SPEC_REVISION_STALE` (409). Guards recipe activation and charging.
30. Claim set integrity — every printed claim code on the configuration matches an `ACTIVE` claim on the same specification; otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ACTIVE` (422). Guards package-configuration activation.
31. Eligible recipe — the specification has at least one `ACTIVE` recipe pinned to the current revision; otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RECIPE` (409). Guards approval-claim submission.
32. Take-back agreement — the account's `takeback_agreement_status` is `ACTIVE` wherever take-back is involved; otherwise `ERR_TAKEBACK_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE` (409). Guards order confirmation when the order is take-back linked, collection scheduling, and recording a collection pickup.
33. Duplicate claim — the combination of specification, `claim_body` and `claim_code` is not already held by a claim in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409). Guards approval-claim creation.
34. Duplicate code — `product_code`, `tank_code`, `batch_code` and `line_code` are globally unique and `retain_code` is unique within its batch; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Guards specification, tank, batch, filling-line and retain-jar creation.
35. Material shortage — the available stock of every required material code covers the requirement, with no quarantined lot of that code able to close the gap; otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_SHORTAGE` (422). Guards charging.
36. Credit limit — the order's pre-credit total does not exceed the account's `credit_limit_cents`; otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards order confirmation and order resumption.
37. Credit availability — the order is take-back linked and the account's credit balance is greater than zero; otherwise `ERR_NO_TAKEBACK_CREDIT` (422). Guards drawing take-back credit.
38. Tank capacity — the charge volume plus the existing heel fits inside the vessel; otherwise `ERR_TANK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards charging.
39. Viscosity branch clearance — clearing the viscosity branch requires every active recipe on the specification to sit inside the requested band; otherwise `ERR_VISCOSITY_REVIEW_BLOCKED` (422). Guards the viscosity review verdict.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. A charge request whose window is malformed but whose tank is already occupied reports the occupancy collision, not the malformed window, because collision sits at rung 24 and window sanity at rung 26. And a charge request that both overflows the vessel and starves on material reports the material shortage, because shortage sits at rung 35 and capacity at rung 38.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Monetary Discipline

Unless a rule below says otherwise, every rounding operation in this system is **round half up**, and every rounding operation is applied exactly once, at the end of the computation it belongs to; intermediate sums and products are accumulated at full precision and are never rounded on the way.

All money is carried as whole cents in integer fields, and every monetary result is rounded to whole cents. The one deliberate exception to round-half-up is the take-back credit cap, which is computed with a **floor** — the fractional part is discarded — so that the cap never rounds upward in the customer's favour.

Physical and analytical quantities each have a fixed number of decimal places. A recipe's predicted viscosity is reported to three decimal places, and its additive treat rate to two. A batch's charge mass and every per-material requirement derived from it are reported to three decimal places, rounded independently of one another. Drawn masses and lot availabilities are reported to three decimal places. The heel contamination ratio is reported to six decimal places, and every comparison against a heel limit uses the already-rounded six-decimal value — so a raw ratio of 0.0050004 becomes 0.005000 and is accepted. Order line volumes and the order's total volume are reported to two decimal places. Viscosity band endpoints are computed to three decimal places, while a measured laboratory reading is compared exactly as supplied, without rounding. A tank heel volume rewritten at release is rounded to three decimal places.

Instants are compared at whole-second resolution in UTC, and are emitted as ISO 8601 UTC strings with three fractional digits.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Specifications and Accounts

Two write operations carry an explicit expected-version field, because in both cases a second concurrent write is a legitimate product operation rather than a state violation, and the only thing that distinguishes a valid second write from a dangerous one is whether the client was looking at current data.

Opening a specification change request requires a non-optional `expected_product_spec_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target specification. A specification may legitimately carry more than one open change request at a time, so a second create is allowed on its own merits; but if the supplied value no longer equals the specification's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SPEC_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The rejection is not about timing — it is about the client's view of `target_kv100_cst`, `min_additive_treat_pct` and `revision` having gone stale, so that the deltas it computed were computed against a specification that has since moved.

Drawing used-oil credit against an order requires a non-optional `expected_customer_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the order's buying account. A customer may legitimately draw credit against more than one order, so applying credit to a second order is allowed on its own merits; but if the supplied value no longer equals that account's current `version`, the draw is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the caller's view of `takeback_credit_balance_cents` predates an earlier grant or draw-down and the amount it intends to apply is stale.

No other endpoint accepts an expected-version field, and none may be invented. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no version field at all.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Where one operation mutates more than one entity, the secondary effects are as binding as the primary one. Every entity touched by a cascade has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly; a cascade is never a silent write. Purely derived counters are the single carve-out, because they are recomputed from the underlying rows every time they are read rather than being stored and nudged: a component lot's `available_kg`, a batch's `retain_count` and a filling line's `scheduled_run_count` all fall in that class, and refreshing one of them is not of itself a mutation of the row that reports it — it advances neither `version` nor `updated_at`. Link 3 of cascade B therefore leaves the affected filling lines unversioned, and scheduling, starting, completing or cancelling a run never versions the line either; the same holds for a lot whose availability merely falls and for a batch whose `retain_count` falls because a jar was disposed of. A row is still versioned wherever the same operation writes a genuinely stored field on it — a lot flipping `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED`, or the batch a retain jar has just been booked against.

Two named cascades run through the system and are referred to throughout §3. **Cascade A** is the specification-change cascade: making a cleared change effective advances the specification's `revision` (link 1), which suspends every active approval claim pinned to a superseded revision (link 2), which blocks every active package configuration printing a suspended claim's code (link 3), which puts every open order line quoting a blocked configuration — and the confirmed order owning it — on hold (link 4), and which independently retires every active recipe pinned to a superseded revision (link 5). **Cascade B** is the batch-quarantine cascade: a batch entering quarantine (link 1) cancels every scheduled or running packaging run against it (link 2), which recomputes the affected filling lines' scheduled run counts (link 3), and — only where the vessel is still charged by that batch — quarantines the vessel too (link 4).

Two quantities are conserved exactly and must remain conserved at every observable instant. The first is **mass**: for every component lot, its `available_kg` plus the summed `drawn_kg` of every draw against it that is still reserved or consumed equals its `received_kg`. A charge followed by its compensating cancellation must leave `available_kg` numerically identical to its pre-charge value. The second is **money**: for every account, granted take-back credit minus applied take-back credit is invariant. Voiding an order zeroes that order's applied credit and drops it out of the applied sum, so an apply-then-void sequence restores `takeback_credit_balance_cents` exactly.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The plant separates the person who produces a quality record from the person who acts on it. Every laboratory result written against a batch stamps `technician_user_id` from the recording caller's `X-User-ID`. When a batch is later released, or when its quality gate is waived, the acting `QualityManager`'s `X-User-ID` is compared against the `technician_user_id` of every laboratory result on that batch; if it matches any of them, the operation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. The check is a pure identity comparison and applies regardless of how many results the batch carries or how long ago they were recorded.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Product Specifications, Revisions and Change Control

Everything the plant makes begins as a **product specification** — the finished-product definition a `ProductManager` writes before any material is ordered. Creating one supplies a `product_code`, which must be unique across every specification ever created, an `sae_grade` label such as 0W-20, a nominal `target_kv100_cst` giving the intended kinematic viscosity at 100 degrees Celsius in centistokes, a symmetric `kv100_tolerance_pct` expressing the half-width of the acceptance band as a percentage of that target, a `min_additive_treat_pct` naming the smallest total additive-package mass fraction any recipe for the product may carry, a `base_stock_group` drawn from `GROUP_I`, `GROUP_II`, `GROUP_III` and `GROUP_IV`, and an `additive_family` drawn from `ZDDP_PCMO`, `LOW_SAPS`, `HDEO_API_CK4`, `GEAR_EP` and `FOOD_GRADE_H1`. The target viscosity must be greater than zero; the tolerance must be greater than zero and at most 25; the minimum treat rate must be at least zero and at most 100. A new specification is born in `DRAFT` at `revision` 1, with `effective_from` still unset.

Activation moves the specification `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which is legal only while it is `DRAFT`. Activation stamps `effective_from` from the logical clock and advances the specification's `version`. Withdrawal moves it `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN` and carries a `reason`; it is legal only while the specification is `ACTIVE` **and** no approval claim on it is `ACTIVE`. Withdrawal retires every recipe on the specification that is currently `ACTIVE`, driving each of them `ACTIVE → RETIRED`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal — a withdrawn specification never comes back.

Changing an active specification is not an edit. It is a governed workflow, opened by a `ProductManager` against the specification and guarded by the optimistic-concurrency rule of §2.8: the create carries `expected_product_spec_version`, and a mismatch against the specification's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_SPEC_VERSION`. Opening a request imposes no status requirement of its own — one may be opened against a specification sitting in `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN` as readily as against an `ACTIVE` one, because the `ACTIVE` requirement of rung 13 bites at submission rather than at creation. A successful create advances the specification's own `version` by 1 and produces a **specification change request** in `DRAFT`, carrying its `product_spec_id`, the four proposed replacements — `requested_kv100_cst`, `requested_kv100_tolerance_pct`, `requested_min_additive_treat_pct` and `requested_additive_family` — and both review branches, `viscosity_review` and `approval_review`, sitting at `PENDING`. A freshly opened request carries `claims_to_suspend_count` at 0 and `effective_revision` null. The proposed tolerance obeys the same bounds as the field it would replace: greater than zero and at most 25; the proposed minimum treat rate at least zero and at most 100; the proposed viscosity greater than zero; the proposed family one of the five declared chemistries. A `DRAFT` request may be abandoned with a `reason`, moving it `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`.

Submission is where the workflow forks. A `ProductManager` submits a `DRAFT` request while the specification is `ACTIVE`; the request passes through `SUBMITTED` and lands at `IN_REVIEW` inside that single call, with both branches open at `PENDING`. Note the consequence for reporting: an observer never sees a change request resting in `SUBMITTED`. Note also the consequence for versioning: the two hops are a single write, so the request's `version` advances by exactly 1 — traversing an intermediate state never costs a second increment anywhere in this system.

The two branches are decided independently, by different roles, and each carries an optional `note` alongside its `decision`, which is either `CLEAR` or `BLOCK`. The `note` is accepted and validated as a string but is persisted nowhere on the request and appears in no response. A `QualityManager` rules the viscosity branch while the request is `IN_REVIEW` and `viscosity_review` is still `PENDING`; the branch writes `viscosity_review` to `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` and performs no claim counting whatsoever. A `ComplianceOfficer` rules the certification branch while the request is `IN_REVIEW` and `approval_review` is still `PENDING`; that branch writes `approval_review` to `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` and, uniquely to this branch, records `claims_to_suspend_count` as the number of approval claims on the specification whose status is `ACTIVE` at that exact instant. Each verdict is a self-loop — the request stays `IN_REVIEW` while a branch is decided — and each advances the request's `version`.

Clearing the viscosity branch is conditional. When the verdict is `CLEAR`, the system derives the proposed acceptance band by taking the proposed viscosity multiplied by one minus the proposed tolerance divided by one hundred as the lower endpoint, and the proposed viscosity multiplied by one plus the proposed tolerance divided by one hundred as the upper endpoint, each rounded half up to three decimal places. Every recipe on the specification whose status is `ACTIVE` must have its predicted viscosity sitting at or above the lower endpoint and at or below the upper endpoint — both ends inclusive. Recipes in `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` are ignored entirely, and where the specification has no active recipes at all the candidate set is empty and the branch clears. A `CLEAR` verdict that would leave an active recipe outside the proposed band is refused with `ERR_VISCOSITY_REVIEW_BLOCKED`. A `BLOCK` verdict is never subject to this check.

The join fires automatically as soon as both branches have decided. If `viscosity_review` and `approval_review` are both `CLEARED`, the request moves `IN_REVIEW → CLEARED`. If either branch is `BLOCKED`, the request moves `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED`. Both `CLEARED` and `REJECTED` are reached by the system rather than by an operator. The join is folded into the very write that recorded the closing verdict rather than applied as a second one, so a verdict that closes the fork advances the request's `version` by exactly 1 in total — the same single increment the verdict alone would have cost. `REJECTED` is terminal, as is `WITHDRAWN`.

Making a cleared request effective is the origin of cascade A, and only a `ProductManager` may do it, only while the request is `CLEARED` and the specification is `ACTIVE`. The specification undergoes a self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`: its `revision` increases by 1, the four requested values are written through onto `target_kv100_cst`, `kv100_tolerance_pct`, `min_additive_treat_pct` and `additive_family`, and its own `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole operation, however many further rows the cascade below goes on to touch. The specification row itself is never replaced, so every reference pointing at it stays valid while every pinned revision recorded elsewhere goes stale. The request records `effective_revision` — null up to this point — as the newly produced `revision`, moves `CLEARED → EFFECTIVE` — a terminal state — and advances its own `version`.

Because the revision advanced, the rest of cascade A follows in the same operation. Every approval claim on the specification whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose `pinned_spec_revision` is now behind the specification's `revision` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which in turn blocks the package configurations printing its claim code and puts their open order lines and confirmed orders on hold, as described in §3.6 and §3.8. Independently, every recipe on the specification whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose `spec_revision_pinned` is now behind the specification's `revision` moves `ACTIVE → RETIRED`. Each of those rows has its own `version` advanced.

### 3.2. Formulation: Blend Recipes and Their Components

A **blend recipe** is a `ProductManager`'s formulation of one specification. Creating one names the `product_spec_id` it formulates, a `total_mass_basis_kg` giving the nominal batch mass the mass fractions are quoted against — a documentation figure that must be greater than zero and that never enters any calculation — and a `components` array. That array must be present and must carry at least one entry — an absent, empty or non-array `components` field is an invalid request. Each entry in that array carries a `component_code` naming the material, a `component_type` from `BASE_OIL`, `VI_IMPROVER`, `ADDITIVE_PACKAGE` and `DYE`, a `mass_fraction_pct` greater than zero and at most 100, a datasheet `nominal_kv100_cst` greater than zero, and an `additive_family` which defaults to `NONE` and is otherwise one of `ZDDP_PCMO`, `LOW_SAPS`, `HDEO_API_CK4`, `GEAR_EP` and `FOOD_GRADE_H1`. Each stored component records the `blend_recipe_id` of the recipe that owns it. Components are immutable: once written they are never edited, and consistent with §2.4 they carry only `id` and `created_at`.

The recipe is created in `DRAFT` and copies the specification's current `revision` into `spec_revision_pinned` at that moment; the specification may be in any status when that happens, since the `ACTIVE` requirement bites only at activation. Creation is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_FRACTION_SUM` unless the supplied mass fractions total exactly 100.00.

Three figures are computed on the components at creation. The **additive treat rate**, `additive_treat_pct`, is the sum of `mass_fraction_pct` over the components whose `component_type` is `ADDITIVE_PACKAGE`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `component_count` is simply the number of component rows on the recipe. The **predicted blended viscosity**, `predicted_kv100_cst`, follows the ASTM D341 log-log blending relation and is non-linear in every input. For each component, add a viscosity offset of 0.7 to its `nominal_kv100_cst`, take the base-ten logarithm of that sum, and take the base-ten logarithm of the result; call that the component's blending index. Multiply each component's blending index by its `mass_fraction_pct` divided by one hundred, and add those products together, walking the component rows in ascending `created_at` order and accumulating at full precision with no intermediate rounding. Raise ten to the power of that accumulated sum, then raise ten to the power of the figure just obtained, and subtract the same 0.7 offset. Round that final result half up to three decimal places. Three or more component rows always participate, and the same offset of 0.7 is used on the way in and on the way out.

Activation drives the recipe `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is the plant's formulation gate. It is legal only while the recipe is `DRAFT`. The governing specification must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SPEC_NOT_ACTIVE`. The recipe's `spec_revision_pinned` must still equal the specification's current `revision`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SPEC_REVISION_STALE` — this is how a recipe left behind by cascade A is prevented from being resurrected. The mass fractions must still total exactly 100.00, or `ERR_RECIPE_FRACTION_SUM` applies here too. The `additive_treat_pct` must be at least the specification's `min_additive_treat_pct`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_TREAT_RATE_BELOW_SPEC`. Finally, `predicted_kv100_cst` must sit inside the specification's acceptance band — at or above the target multiplied by one minus the tolerance divided by one hundred, and at or below the target multiplied by one plus the tolerance divided by one hundred, each endpoint rounded half up to three decimal places, both comparisons inclusive — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PREDICTED_VISCOSITY_OUT_OF_SPEC`. These five checks are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6, so specification activity is consulted before pinned-revision currency, treat rate before predicted viscosity, and the fraction sum after both of the safety checks that precede it.

An active recipe leaves `ACTIVE → RETIRED` only by system action: either its specification's `revision` advanced past its `spec_revision_pinned`, or its specification was withdrawn. `RETIRED` is terminal.

### 3.3. Component Lots, Draws and Blend Costing

Material arrives at goods receipt as a **component lot**, booked in by a `PlatformAdmin`. The booking supplies the `component_type`, the `component_code` shared by every lot of the same material, the `received_kg` mass — greater than zero and never changed afterwards — the landed `unit_cost_cents_per_kg` in whole cents per kilogram, greater than zero, and two certificate-of-analysis figures: `kv100_cst`, the kinematic viscosity at 100 degrees Celsius, and `density_kg_per_l`, the density at 15 degrees Celsius, both greater than zero. The `additive_family` may be supplied and defaults to `NONE`. A `coa_viscosity_verified` flag records whether the incoming certificate viscosity was independently re-tested; it defaults to true, and when it is false it will force any batch drawing from this lot to be ineligible for approval claims. On receipt, `available_kg` is initialised to `received_kg`, `status` is initialised to `AVAILABLE`, and `quarantine_reason` is null.

A `QualityManager` may block a lot from further draws, moving it `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED` and recording the supplied `reason` into `quarantine_reason`. Two further lot transitions are driven by the system rather than by an operator: a lot whose `available_kg` reaches exactly zero as a result of a charge moves `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED`, and a `DEPLETED` lot whose reserved draw is returned by a cancelled charge moves `DEPLETED → AVAILABLE` with its availability restored. `DEPLETED` is the lot's terminal state in the ordinary sense, but the return path above is the one exception that reopens it.

Availability is never stored as a running counter that is nudged up and down. It is always recomputed as the lot's `received_kg` minus the summed `drawn_kg` of every draw against that lot whose status is either `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Returned draws contribute nothing to the deduction, which is precisely what makes the mass conservation of §2.9 hold.

Each **component draw** records the `blend_batch_id` that drew the material, the `component_lot_id` it came from, the `component_code` copied from the lot at draw time, the `drawn_kg` mass to three decimal places, a snapshot of the lot's `unit_cost_cents_per_kg` taken at draw time, and a `status` that begins at `RESERVED` and moves to `CONSUMED` when blending completes or to `RETURNED` when the charge is compensated back.

The order in which lots are drawn down is fixed and is shared by both costing variants. For each required material code, the candidate lots are those whose `component_code` equals the required code **and** whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Candidates are ordered by `created_at` ascending; ties are broken by `received_kg` descending; remaining ties are broken by `unit_cost_cents_per_kg` ascending, and any tie surviving all three of those keys is broken by the order in which the lots were booked in, earliest booking first. A lot's `id` is never used as a sort key under any circumstances. Walking that order, each candidate contributes the smaller of its own `available_kg` and the requirement still outstanding, until the outstanding requirement reaches zero.

Two different costs are produced for every batch, and they are expected to disagree. The **provisional** figure, `estimated_component_cost_cents`, is written when the batch is charged and uses an availability-weighted moving average. For each required material code, take the sum over every candidate lot of that lot's `available_kg` multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents_per_kg`, and divide by the sum over the same lots of their `available_kg`; that quotient is the code's average cost. Crucially, this average is evaluated over **every** candidate lot of that code — including lots the draw never reaches — and it is evaluated **before** any availability is decremented by this charge. Multiply each code's required mass by its average cost, add those products together across all required codes at full precision, and round the total half up to whole cents exactly once at the end. Because cheap early layers are diluted by expensive later layers the draw may never touch, this figure systematically differs from the final one.

The **final** figure, `component_cost_cents`, is written when blending completes and prices only what was actually consumed. Take the sum over this batch's draws whose status is `CONSUMED` of `drawn_kg` multiplied by that draw's snapshotted `unit_cost_cents_per_kg`, accumulate at full precision, and round half up to whole cents once at the end. Reserved and returned draws are excluded outright. Where earlier lots are cheaper than later ones, this figure is strictly lower than the provisional one, and the difference is real rather than a rounding artefact.

### 3.4. Charging a Vessel: Heel Chemistry, Occupancy and Capacity

Blending vessels are registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `tank_code` painted on the shell — unique across every vessel — and a working `capacity_l` in litres, greater than zero. A vessel may be registered pre-loaded with a declared residue: `heel_volume_l`, defaulting to 0.0 and never negative, `heel_additive_family`, defaulting to `NONE`, and `heel_product_code`, naming the `product_code` of the last product to occupy the shell. New vessels start `AVAILABLE`, and `last_flush_at` is null until the vessel is first flushed.

A `BlendOperator` flushes a vessel clean. Flushing sets `heel_volume_l` to 0.0, sets `heel_additive_family` to `NONE`, clears `heel_product_code` to null, and stamps `last_flush_at` from the logical clock. It is permitted from `AVAILABLE` — a self-loop that leaves the vessel `AVAILABLE` — and from `QUARANTINED`, in which case the vessel returns `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`. Either way it is refused while any batch on that vessel sits in `CHARGING` or `BLENDED` production status. A flush is the only route out of `QUARANTINED` for a vessel.

A **blend batch** is created by a `BlendOperator` naming the `blend_recipe_id` to be blended, a `batch_code` unique across every batch, and a `planned_volume_l` greater than zero. The named recipe may be in any status at creation — the `ACTIVE` requirement bites at charging, not here. The batch is born with `production_status` `PLANNED` and `qc_status` `PENDING` — two dimensions that evolve entirely independently of one another for the rest of the batch's life. At creation the recipe's `predicted_kv100_cst` is snapshotted onto the batch, `claim_eligible` is initialised to false, both `estimated_component_cost_cents` and `component_cost_cents` are initialised to 0, `retain_count` is initialised to 0, and `blend_tank_id`, `charge_volume_l`, `charge_mass_kg`, `heel_ratio_actual`, `measured_kv100_cst`, `window_start` and `window_end` are all null.

Charging is the plant's most heavily gated operation. The `BlendOperator` supplies the `blend_tank_id` of the target vessel, the `charge_volume_l` of fresh blend, and the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the occupancy the batch claims on that vessel. The batch must be `PLANNED`; the recipe must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_ACTIVE` applies; the specification must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SPEC_NOT_ACTIVE` applies; the recipe's `spec_revision_pinned` must equal the specification's current `revision`, or `ERR_SPEC_REVISION_STALE` applies; the vessel must be `AVAILABLE`, or `ERR_TANK_NOT_AVAILABLE` applies; and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` applies. These gates are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6 rather than in the order they are listed here, so a vessel that is not `AVAILABLE` is reported before a recipe that is not `ACTIVE`.

The mass requirement is derived first. Multiply the requested `charge_volume_l` by a fixed blending density of 0.860 kilograms per litre and round half up to three decimal places; that is the batch's `charge_mass_kg`. Then, for each component row on the batch's recipe, multiply `charge_mass_kg` by that row's `mass_fraction_pct` divided by one hundred and round half up to three decimal places; that is the required mass for that material code. The rounding is applied to the charge mass first and then independently to each per-material requirement.

**Heel chemistry** decides whether the charge is safe at all. Take the vessel exactly as it stands immediately before the charge, and the specification reached through the batch's recipe. The heel is a *cross-family* heel when the vessel's `heel_additive_family` is not `NONE` and differs from the specification's `additive_family`; it is a *same-family* heel when the vessel's `heel_additive_family` equals the specification's `additive_family`; and when the vessel's `heel_additive_family` is `NONE`, it is neither. Food-grade chemistry gets an absolute barrier that ignores quantity altogether: if the specification's `additive_family` is `FOOD_GRADE_H1` and the vessel's `heel_additive_family` is anything other than `NONE` or `FOOD_GRADE_H1`, the charge is refused with `ERR_FLUSH_REQUIRED`; and symmetrically, if the vessel's `heel_additive_family` is `FOOD_GRADE_H1` and the specification's `additive_family` is anything else, the charge is refused with `ERR_FLUSH_REQUIRED`. That barrier holds at any heel ratio, including a vanishingly small one.

Where the barrier does not apply, quantity decides. The batch's `heel_ratio_actual` is the vessel's `heel_volume_l` divided by the sum of that same `heel_volume_l` and the requested `charge_volume_l`, rounded half up to six decimal places. A cross-family heel may not exceed a ratio of 0.005000; a same-family heel may not exceed 0.020000; a vessel whose heel family is `NONE` is unconstrained on ratio. Both ceilings are inclusive upper bounds — a ratio landing exactly on the limit passes — and the comparison uses the rounded six-decimal figure, so a raw ratio of 0.0050004 rounds to 0.005000 and is accepted. Exceeding the applicable ceiling refuses the charge with `ERR_HEEL_CONTAMINATION_EXCEEDED`.

**Claim eligibility** is decided in the same pass, but against tighter ceilings, and it is a verdict rather than a rejection. The batch's `claim_eligible` is set true only when all three of the following hold: the `heel_ratio_actual` is at or below 0.001000 for a cross-family heel, at or below 0.010000 for a same-family heel, or unconstrained where the vessel's heel family is `NONE`; every component lot touched by a draw of this charge has `coa_viscosity_verified` true; and the recipe's `spec_revision_pinned` equals the specification's `revision` at charge time. If any of the three fails, `claim_eligible` is false — the batch is still perfectly blendable and sellable, it simply may not be packed under a printed approval claim.

**Occupancy** is adjudicated as a half-open interval. A window runs from its start inclusive to its end exclusive, so two batches whose windows merely touch at an endpoint do not collide. Where the incoming chemistry is a cross-family heel, and only then, the incoming window's left edge is widened backwards by a two-hour changeover buffer to force a gap for the changeover; the buffer never widens the right edge and never widens the windows of batches already on the vessel. A collision exists when some other batch on the same vessel — a different batch, with a window recorded, whose `production_status` is `PLANNED`, `CHARGING`, `BLENDED` or `RELEASED` — has a window whose end lies strictly after the buffered incoming start and whose start lies strictly before the incoming `window_end`. Batches in `QUARANTINED`, `REWORKED` or `DISPOSED` release their windows and never collide. A collision refuses the charge with `ERR_TANK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**Material availability** carries a deliberate distinction that decides which of two identifiers comes back. For each required material code, sum the `available_kg` of the lots of that code whose status is `AVAILABLE`. If that sum falls short of the requirement, look at whether the quarantined lots of the same code would have closed the gap had they been available: if adding them in would have covered the requirement, the shortfall is attributable to blocked stock and the charge is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`; if even they would not have covered it, the charge is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHORTAGE`. The blocked-stock verdict sits far higher on the ladder of §2.6 than the plain shortage verdict, so blocked stock is always reported in preference to shortage. Finally, the existing `heel_volume_l` plus the requested `charge_volume_l` must fit within the vessel's `capacity_l`, or the charge is refused with `ERR_TANK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A charge that clears every gate does a great deal at once. The batch moves `PLANNED → CHARGING`, taking `blend_tank_id`, `charge_volume_l`, `window_start` and `window_end` from the request and recording `charge_mass_kg`, `heel_ratio_actual`, `claim_eligible` and `estimated_component_cost_cents`. One component draw is created per consumed lot layer, in the draw order of §3.3, each with status `RESERVED`. Each drawn lot's `available_kg` is recomputed downwards, and any lot whose availability lands exactly on zero moves `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED`. The vessel moves `AVAILABLE → CHARGED`. Both the batch's and the vessel's `version` advance, and so does the `version` of every lot that flipped to `DEPLETED`; a lot merely drawn down without reaching zero is left unversioned, its availability being derived rather than stored, per §2.9. The same asymmetry runs backwards through the cancellation below — only a lot returning `DEPLETED → AVAILABLE` is versioned.

Charging has an exact compensating action. A `BlendOperator` may cancel the charge, supplying a `reason`, while the batch is `CHARGING` and its `qc_status` is still `PENDING`. Cancellation moves the batch `CHARGING → PLANNED`, moves every draw on the batch whose status is `RESERVED` to `RETURNED`, restores each affected lot's `available_kg` — returning any `DEPLETED` lot to `AVAILABLE` — resets `blend_tank_id`, `charge_volume_l`, `charge_mass_kg`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `heel_ratio_actual` to null, resets `estimated_component_cost_cents` to 0 and `claim_eligible` to false, and hands the vessel back `CHARGED → AVAILABLE` **with its heel fields left exactly as they were before the charge**. The vessel's `heel_volume_l`, `heel_additive_family` and `heel_product_code` are not rewritten by a cancellation. Mass conservation holds exactly across the charge-then-cancel pair.

Completing the blend moves the batch to `BLENDED` and freezes the final cost. It is reachable from two originating states: `CHARGING → BLENDED`, which moves every `RESERVED` draw on the batch to `CONSUMED` and computes `component_cost_cents` by the consumption-layer method of §3.3; and `REWORKED → BLENDED`, permitted only while the batch's `qc_status` is `PENDING`, which recomputes `component_cost_cents` over the unchanged set of `CONSUMED` draws — the figure comes out identical, because rework consumes no additional material — and closes the quarantine-rework loop. Both advance the batch's `version`, and neither touches the vessel: it stays `CHARGED` until a release, a quarantine or a flush moves it.

### 3.5. The Quality Dimension: Testing, Retains and Disposition

The `qc_status` dimension runs in parallel with production and is driven exclusively by a `QCTechnician` recording laboratory results and by a `QualityManager` exercising disposition authority. A result names a `test_type` — one of `KV100`, `KV40`, `TBN` and `FLASH_POINT` — and a `measured_value` greater than zero in that test's native unit. The stored result carries the `blend_batch_id` under test, the computed `result`, a `tested_at` instant stamped from the logical clock, and `technician_user_id` stamped from the recording caller's `X-User-ID`. Results are immutable and, per §2.4, carry no `updated_at` and no `version` of their own.

The verdict depends on the test. For a `KV100` test, the acceptance band is derived from the governing specification's **current** `target_kv100_cst` and `kv100_tolerance_pct` — never from the revision the recipe happens to be pinned to. The lower endpoint is the target multiplied by one minus the tolerance divided by one hundred; the upper endpoint is the target multiplied by one plus the tolerance divided by one hundred; each endpoint is rounded half up to three decimal places. The `result` is `PASS` when the `measured_value` is at or above the lower endpoint and at or below the upper endpoint, both ends inclusive, and `FAIL` otherwise; the `measured_value` itself is compared exactly as supplied, with no rounding. A `KV100` result also writes `measured_kv100_cst` onto the batch, which therefore always reflects the most recent `KV100` reading. For every other test type the `result` is `PASS` when the `measured_value` is greater than zero, and `FAIL` otherwise.

Recording results walks the quality dimension. The first result on a batch, when it passes, moves the batch `PENDING → SAMPLED`; this is permitted only while the batch's `production_status` is `CHARGING`, `BLENDED` or `REWORKED`, and it leaves the production dimension entirely untouched. A subsequent result that completes the required test set moves the batch `SAMPLED → PASSED`; that transition requires the batch's results to cover both of the required tests — `KV100` and `TBN` — and it is the **latest** result of each of those two types, taken in ascending `created_at` order with ties broken by recording order, that must carry `PASS`. An earlier result of the same test type is superseded by a later one and no longer counts against the batch, which is exactly what lets a reworked batch reach `PASSED` over the failure that quarantined it; results of any test type outside the required pair are ignored for this purpose entirely. A failing result moves the batch to `FAILED` from either `PENDING` or `SAMPLED`. Where a failing result lands on a batch whose `production_status` is `BLENDED`, cascade B fires in the same operation and the batch also moves `BLENDED → QUARANTINED`. Every result recorded advances the batch's `version`.

**Retain jars** are the laboratory's archive, booked by a `QCTechnician` against a batch in any production or quality state whatsoever — no gate on either dimension guards the booking. Each supplies a `retain_code`, unique within that batch, and a `volume_ml` of at least 100 millilitres. The jar records the `blend_batch_id` it was drawn from, a `retained_at` instant stamped from the logical clock, a `status` beginning at `HELD`, and a computed `retention_until`. The retention window depends on whether the batch's specification carries approval claims: if any approval claim on that specification has status `ACTIVE` at the instant the jar is booked, the window is 1,095 calendar days from `retained_at`; otherwise it is 365 calendar days. The window is frozen at creation and is deliberately **not** recomputed when claims later change state. Booking a jar recomputes the batch's `retain_count`, which is the number of jars on the batch whose status is `HELD`, and advances the batch's `version`. A `QualityManager` may dispose of a jar, moving it `HELD → DISPOSED` with a `reason`; the disposal is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_WINDOW_ACTIVE` until the logical clock has reached the jar's `retention_until`, and a successful disposal recomputes the batch's `retain_count` and advances the jar's own `version` — the batch's `version`, unlike on booking, does not move, because on this path nothing stored on the batch changes.

**Release** is the `QualityManager`'s statement that the batch may be sold. It moves the batch `BLENDED → RELEASED` and requires four things: the `production_status` is `BLENDED`; the `qc_status` is either `PASSED` or `WAIVED`; the batch holds at least two retain jars, or the release is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RETAINS` — the zero-jar case included; and the caller's `X-User-ID` matches no `technician_user_id` on any result of the batch, or the release is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Release hands the vessel back `CHARGED → AVAILABLE` and, unlike a cancelled charge, **rewrites** the vessel's heel from this batch: `heel_volume_l` becomes the batch's `charge_volume_l` multiplied by a heel retention rate of 0.5 percent, rounded half up to three decimal places; `heel_additive_family` becomes the released batch's specification `additive_family`; and `heel_product_code` becomes that specification's `product_code`. Both the batch's and the vessel's `version` advance.

**Quarantine** is reachable from two originating production states and behaves differently in each. From `BLENDED`, a `QualityManager` quarantining the batch — with a `reason` — moves it `BLENDED → QUARANTINED`, cancels every packaging run against the batch whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` with `cancel_reason` `BATCH_QUARANTINED`, recomputes each affected filling line's `scheduled_run_count`, and moves the vessel `CHARGED → QUARANTINED`. From `RELEASED` — a market recall of material already handed over — the batch moves `RELEASED → QUARANTINED` and the packaging-run cancellations and line recomputations fire exactly as before, but the vessel hop is skipped: the vessel was already handed back at release, so its status is left untouched. In both cases every affected row carrying a stored change — the batch, each cancelled run, and where the hop applies the vessel — advances its own `version`; the filling lines do not, their `scheduled_run_count` being derived, per §2.9.

Two exits lead out of quarantine. A `BlendOperator` may send a quarantined, QC-failed batch back for rework, supplying a `reason`; that requires the `production_status` to be `QUARANTINED` and the `qc_status` to be `FAILED`, moves the batch `QUARANTINED → REWORKED`, resets the quality dimension `FAILED → PENDING` and clears `measured_kv100_cst` back to null. The vessel is left `QUARANTINED` and must still be flushed separately — rework does not clean the shell. Alternatively a `QualityManager` may scrap the batch with a `reason`, moving it `QUARANTINED → DISPOSED`, which is terminal; disposal returns no mass to any lot, and the batch's draws stay `CONSUMED`.

**Waiving** the quality gate is the narrow escape hatch, exercised by a `QualityManager` with a written `justification`. It moves `qc_status` `FAILED → WAIVED` or `SAMPLED → WAIVED`. It is available only when the batch's `claim_eligible` is false; attempting to waive a claim-eligible batch is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`, on the principle that material intended to carry an approval mark can never be released on a waiver. The same segregation-of-duties comparison that guards release guards the waiver: a caller whose `X-User-ID` appears as `technician_user_id` on any result of the batch is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 3.6. Approval Claims and Label Configurations

An **approval claim** is the assertion that a specification meets a certifying or approving body's requirements. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens one against a `product_spec_id`, naming the `claim_body` — one of `API`, `ILSAC`, `OEM_MB`, `OEM_VW`, `OEM_CAT` and `JASO` — and the `claim_code`, the string actually printed on a label, such as API SP. The claim opens in `DRAFT` with `pinned_spec_revision` null, `licence_number` null, `valid_until` null, `requires_recertification` false and `suspension_reason` null. The combination of specification, `claim_body` and `claim_code` may be held by only one claim at a time that is still in play: if an existing claim on the same triple sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`. Claims in `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` do not block a new one.

Submission is the act of sending the claim to the body, and it is reachable from two originating states: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` for a first approval, and `SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED` for a re-approval after a specification change. Both paths require the specification to be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SPEC_NOT_ACTIVE` applies, and both require the specification to carry at least one recipe that is `ACTIVE` **and** whose `spec_revision_pinned` equals the specification's current `revision` — a claim cannot be substantiated without a formulation that is actually current — or the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RECIPE`. Both paths write `pinned_spec_revision` from the specification's current `revision`; on the re-approval path this re-pins the claim to the new revision, which is what closes the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED → ACTIVE` cycle.

The body's verdict arrives as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, accompanied by a `licence_number` that is required for an approval and ignored for a rejection, and an optional `reason`. An approval moves the claim `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE`, stores the `licence_number`, clears `requires_recertification` back to false, and computes `valid_until` as the logical clock's current instant plus 730 calendar days. It does **not** clear `suspension_reason`: a claim that came back round the `SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED → ACTIVE` cycle keeps the text of the suspension that sent it there, as the record of why re-approval was needed. A rejection stores no `licence_number` even where one was supplied. It also repairs the label estate: every package configuration sitting in `CLAIM_BLOCKED` whose printed claim codes are now **all** carried by `ACTIVE` claims on the same specification moves `CLAIM_BLOCKED → ACTIVE`, advancing its own `version`. A rejection moves the claim `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and changes nothing else.

Three non-decision lifecycle actions are available, distinguished by an `action` value with an optional `reason`. `REVISE` moves a rejected claim `REJECTED → DRAFT` and resets `pinned_spec_revision` to null, closing the submit-reject-revise loop. `SUSPEND` moves an active claim `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, sets `requires_recertification` to true and records the supplied text into `suspension_reason` — the `reason` being optional here, an operator suspension that omits it leaves `suspension_reason` null. The same `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` hop is driven by the system as link 2 of cascade A, and on that system path `suspension_reason` is written with the fixed literal `SPEC_REVISION_ADVANCED`. `WITHDRAW` is reachable from two originating states: `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN` and `SUSPENDED → WITHDRAWN`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

The distinction between those two withdrawal paths matters. Whenever a claim leaves `ACTIVE` — by suspension, whether operator-driven or cascade-driven, or by withdrawal directly from `ACTIVE` — every package configuration whose `claim_codes` array contains that claim's `claim_code` and whose status is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → CLAIM_BLOCKED`. A withdrawal out of `SUSPENDED` fires no such cascade, because the configurations were already blocked when the claim was suspended.

A **package configuration** is a pack format plus a label. A `ProductManager` creates one naming the `product_spec_id` being packaged, a `package_type` from `BULK`, `IBC_1000L`, `DRUM_205L`, `PAIL_20L` and `CASE_12X1L`, a `label_brand` printed on the label, and a `fill_volume_l` greater than zero giving the litres delivered per ordered unit. A `private_label` flag defaults to false and states whether the label belongs to the buyer rather than to the blender; a `customer_id` names the private-label owner and is null for house-brand configurations; `units_per_case` gives the bottles per case and is meaningful only for `CASE_12X1L`, being null for every other pack format. A `claim_codes` array lists the approval claim codes printed on the label and defaults to empty, which means the label carries no approval claim at all. The configuration opens in `DRAFT` with `superseded_by_id` null.

Activation moves the configuration `DRAFT → ACTIVE` while the specification is `ACTIVE`, and every entry in `claim_codes` must match an approval claim on the same `product_spec_id` whose status is `ACTIVE`; otherwise the activation is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation also supersedes: any other configuration that is currently `ACTIVE` and shares the same combination of `product_spec_id`, `package_type` and `customer_id` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_id` set to the newly activated configuration's `id`, and has every order line quoting it that is still `OPEN` moved `OPEN → HOLD` — and, exactly as on the claim-blocking path of §3.8, any owning order sitting in `CONFIRMED` follows its line into `HOLD` with `hold_reason` `CLAIM_BLOCKED_LINE`, while an owning order in any other status keeps its own status and only its line moves. That is how a private-label relabel silently freezes the orders written against the old artwork.

A configuration that has been blocked returns to service only through the claim-repair path described above, moving `CLAIM_BLOCKED → ACTIVE` once every one of its printed claim codes is again matched by an `ACTIVE` claim on the same specification. Retirement is the manual exit: a `ProductManager` retires a configuration with a `reason` from `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `CLAIM_BLOCKED`, provided no packaging run on that configuration is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`. `RETIRED` is terminal.

### 3.7. Filling Lines and Packaging Runs

A **filling line** is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `line_code` unique across every line and a `max_concurrent_runs` count of filling heads, which defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. A new line opens `AVAILABLE` with `scheduled_run_count` at 0. Taking a line out of service or returning it to service is also a `PlatformAdmin` action, driven by a `target_status` of either `AVAILABLE` or `DOWN`; the line moves `AVAILABLE → DOWN` or `DOWN → AVAILABLE` accordingly and advances its `version`.

A **packaging run** books a released batch onto a line in a particular pack format. Scheduling one is a `BlendOperator` action naming the `blend_batch_id` to be packed, the `package_configuration_id` giving the format and label, the `packaging_line_id` to occupy, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding that occupancy with the end strictly after the start, and the `planned_units` to be filled, which must be greater than zero. The run opens `SCHEDULED` with `produced_units` null and `cancel_reason` null.

Four gates guard scheduling, in the ladder order of §2.6. The batch's `production_status` must be `RELEASED`, or `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED` applies — quarantined, blended and still-charging material may not be packed. The configuration must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE` applies. The line must be `AVAILABLE`, or `ERR_LINE_NOT_AVAILABLE` applies. And claim substantiation must hold: where the configuration's `claim_codes` array is non-empty, the batch's `claim_eligible` must be true, or the scheduling is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`. A configuration with an empty `claim_codes` array imposes no such requirement and may be filled from any released batch.

Line capacity is aggregate rather than exclusive, and — in deliberate contrast to the vessel rule of §3.4 — packaging windows are **closed** intervals, so runs that merely touch at an endpoint *do* collide. Count the runs on the same line whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` and whose window overlaps the requested one, where overlap means the requested `window_start` is at or before the existing run's `window_end` **and** the existing run's `window_start` is at or before the requested `window_end`. If that count has already reached the line's `max_concurrent_runs`, the scheduling is refused with `ERR_LINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the line tolerates overlaps strictly below that number. Completed and cancelled runs free their slot immediately and are never counted. Every scheduling recomputes the line's `scheduled_run_count`, which is simply the number of runs on that line whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`; that figure is derived rather than stored, so recomputing it advances neither the line's `version` nor its `updated_at`, and a filling line is versioned only by a service-status change. No changeover buffer applies to packaging.

Starting a run moves it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING` and re-checks two things at the moment of the start: the configuration must still be `ACTIVE` and the line must still be `AVAILABLE` — in that order, configuration activity sitting at rung 15 and line availability at rung 16, so a run whose configuration and line have both gone bad reports `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE`. Completing a running run moves it `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, records the `produced_units` actually filled from the request — an integer of at least zero, a run that filled nothing being a legitimate completion — and recomputes the line's `scheduled_run_count`, which decreases by one. Cancelling moves a run `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `RUNNING → CANCELLED`, records the supplied `reason` into `cancel_reason`, and likewise recomputes the line's `scheduled_run_count` downwards by one. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. The same cancellation, with `cancel_reason` set to `BATCH_QUARANTINED`, is driven by the system as link 2 of cascade B whenever the batch being packed enters quarantine, and link 3 of that cascade is exactly the `scheduled_run_count` recomputation just described.

### 3.8. The Commercial Desk: Orders, Lines and the Pricing Waterfall

A `SalesRep` opens an order for a buying account, naming the `customer_id`, a `delivery_mode` of either `BULK_DELIVERY` or `PACKAGED`, and an optional `takeback_linked` flag that defaults to false and states whether used-oil credit may ever be drawn against this order. The order opens in `DRAFT` with `subtotal_cents`, `total_volume_l`, `freight_surcharge_cents`, `pre_credit_total_cents`, `takeback_credit_applied_cents` and `total_cents` all at 0, and `hold_reason` null.

Lines are added to a draft order, each naming the `package_configuration_id` being bought and a `quantity_units` count greater than zero — packaged units for a packed format, litre-units for bulk. Each line records the `order_id` that owns it and opens with status `OPEN`. The referenced configuration must be `ACTIVE`, or the line is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE`. Adding a line prices that line at once and rolls the result straight into the owning order: the order's `subtotal_cents` and `total_volume_l` are recomputed over its `OPEN` lines there and then, and the order's own `version` advances by 1. The remaining money figures are not touched at this point — `freight_surcharge_cents`, `pre_credit_total_cents`, `takeback_credit_applied_cents` and `total_cents` all stay at 0 on a `DRAFT` order until confirmation runs the waterfall. The call returns the created line, not the order.

Pricing a line proceeds from three ingredients. The **base rate**, in cents per litre, is fixed by the configuration's `package_type`: 320 for `BULK`, 380 for `IBC_1000L`, 425 for `DRUM_205L`, 505 for `PAIL_20L` and 640 for `CASE_12X1L` — the smaller the pack, the more the filling costs. The **tier discount** is fixed by the account's `contract_tier`: 12.0 percent for `TIER_A`, 7.5 percent for `TIER_B` and no discount at all for `TIER_C`. The **private-label uplift** is 4.5 percent where the configuration's `private_label` flag is true, and zero where it is false. Multiply the base rate by one minus the tier discount divided by one hundred, then by one plus the uplift divided by one hundred; that product is the line's effective rate per litre and it is **never rounded**.

From that rate three figures follow. The line's `volume_l` is `quantity_units` multiplied by the configuration's `fill_volume_l`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The line's `line_total_cents` is that same already-rounded two-decimal `volume_l` multiplied by the effective rate, rounded half up to whole cents — the volume is rounded first and the money figure is derived from the rounded volume, not independently from the unrounded product. The line's `unit_price_cents` is the configuration's `fill_volume_l` multiplied by the same effective rate, rounded half up to whole cents — a display figure for one unit. The two money figures are deliberately not factors of one another: `line_total_cents` is never `quantity_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`, and the two will legitimately disagree on most lines. The rounding order at order level is pinned in the same way: round each line first, then add. The order's `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `line_total_cents` over the lines whose status is `OPEN`, taken in ascending `created_at` order, and is never obtained by summing unrounded line values. The order's `total_volume_l` is the sum of `volume_l` over those same `OPEN` lines, rounded half up to two decimal places. Lines in `HOLD` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing to either total.

Confirmation runs the full waterfall and is where the order becomes a commitment. It moves the order `DRAFT → CONFIRMED` and requires at least one line in `OPEN` status, every `OPEN` line referencing a configuration that is still `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE` — and, where the order is take-back linked, the account's `takeback_agreement_status` being `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TAKEBACK_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE`. The four money figures look mutually dependent, and the evaluation order below is the only correct one.

**Step one** recomputes every line and produces `subtotal_cents` by the round-per-line-then-sum discipline above, along with `total_volume_l`. **Step two** produces `freight_surcharge_cents`, and the branch is chosen by `delivery_mode`. For a `BULK_DELIVERY` order, freight is waived entirely once `total_volume_l` reaches 4,000 litres — a volume of exactly 4,000 qualifies — and below that it is a minimum freight charge of 48,000 cents multiplied by one minus the ratio of `total_volume_l` to 4,000, rounded half up to whole cents; a tanker that is half full therefore pays half the minimum. For a `PACKAGED` order, freight is a three-step tier keyed on `subtotal_cents` **before** freight and **before** any credit: a subtotal of 1,000,000 cents or more carries no freight at all, a subtotal of at least 500,000 cents but below 1,000,000 carries 22,500 cents, and anything below 500,000 cents carries 39,500 cents. Both thresholds are inclusive at the lower edge. **Step three** sets `pre_credit_total_cents` to `subtotal_cents` plus `freight_surcharge_cents`. **Step four** settles `takeback_credit_applied_cents`, which is zero outright when the order is not take-back linked; where it is linked, compute the cap as 15 percent of `pre_credit_total_cents` with the fractional part discarded — a floor, never a round — and apply the smaller of the account's `takeback_credit_balance_cents` and that cap. The cap is always a share of `pre_credit_total_cents` and never of `total_cents`. **Step five** sets `total_cents` to `pre_credit_total_cents` minus `takeback_credit_applied_cents`. Confirmation performs steps one to three and step five; drawing credit performs steps four and five; voiding resets step four to zero and re-runs step five.

The credit-limit gate closes the confirmation: `pre_credit_total_cents` must not exceed the account's `credit_limit_cents`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Note carefully that the ceiling is tested against the pre-credit figure, so an account cannot buy its way under its own limit by drawing take-back credit.

A confirmed order can be put on hold by a `SalesRep`, supplying a `reason` that is recorded into `hold_reason` and moving the order `CONFIRMED → HOLD`. The same `CONFIRMED → HOLD` hop is driven by the system as link 4 of cascade A, with `hold_reason` set to `CLAIM_BLOCKED_LINE`, whenever an order line on a confirmed order is put on hold because the configuration it quotes stopped being `ACTIVE`. Line-level holds are always system-driven: an `OPEN` line moves `OPEN → HOLD` exactly when the configuration it references has become `CLAIM_BLOCKED` or `SUPERSEDED`.

Resuming reverses both levels. It moves the order `HOLD → CONFIRMED` and requires every line on the order whose status is `OPEN` or `HOLD` to reference a configuration that is `ACTIVE` again, otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success every line in `HOLD` moves `HOLD → OPEN`, `hold_reason` is cleared back to null, and the pricing waterfall is recomputed over the lines that were `OPEN` or `HOLD` at the moment of the resume — steps one to three and then step five, exactly the set confirmation runs, so step four is not re-run and any `takeback_credit_applied_cents` already standing on the order is carried through unchanged into the new `total_cents`. Recomputing from step one also means the credit-limit gate applies to a resume exactly as it applies to a confirmation, and a resume whose recomputed `pre_credit_total_cents` exceeds `credit_limit_cents` is likewise refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Invoicing moves a confirmed order `CONFIRMED → INVOICED` and is permitted only while `total_cents` is greater than zero; an order fully covered by take-back credit cannot be invoiced. `INVOICED` is terminal.

Voiding is reachable from `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED` and `HOLD`, always with a `reason`, and `VOID` is terminal. Every line on the order in `OPEN` or `HOLD` moves to `CANCELLED`. Where the order being voided was `CONFIRMED` or `HOLD`, the void is also the compensating action for a credit draw: `takeback_credit_applied_cents` is reset to 0, `total_cents` is recomputed as `pre_credit_total_cents`, the account's `takeback_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the account's `version` is advanced by 1. Voiding a `DRAFT` order cancels its `OPEN` lines and touches no money, because a draft order was never carrying an applied credit in the first place.

### 3.9. Accounts, Used-Oil Take-Back and the Credit Ledger

A buying account is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `name` giving the legal or trading name, a `customer_type` from `DISTRIBUTOR`, `FLEET`, `PRIVATE_LABEL` and `INDUSTRIAL`, a `contract_tier` from `TIER_A`, `TIER_B` and `TIER_C` — which selects both the pricing discount of §3.8 and the credit variant below — and a `credit_limit_cents` of at least zero, capping the `pre_credit_total_cents` the account may carry on any single confirmed order. An optional `takeback_agreement_status` may be supplied and defaults to `NONE`; the other permitted values are `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`. On registration `takeback_credit_balance_cents` is initialised to 0 and `last_credit_event_at` is null until the first credit event on the account. Either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `SalesRep` may later set the agreement in force by supplying an `agreement_status`. Any of the three values may be written from any of the three, and setting the agreement to the value it already carries is accepted rather than refused — it is a plain write, and it advances the account's `version` like any other.

A **take-back collection** is scheduled against an account by a `SalesRep` or by the account itself acting as `Customer`, naming the `customer_id`, an optional `order_id` when the pickup is piggy-backed onto a delivery, and an optional `scheduled_volume_l` giving the litres the customer expects to hand over — a documentation figure that enters no calculation. Scheduling requires the account's `takeback_agreement_status` to be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_TAKEBACK_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE`. The collection opens `SCHEDULED` with `collected_volume_l`, `water_pct`, `sediment_pct`, `credit_variant` and `reject_reason` all null and `credit_cents` at 0.

Recording the pickup moves the collection `SCHEDULED → COLLECTED` and writes the `collected_volume_l` actually taken — a number of at least zero, a wasted trip being recordable as a pickup of nothing; it too requires the account's agreement to be `ACTIVE`. A `QCTechnician` may then assay the load, supplying `water_pct` and `sediment_pct`. Contamination is capped at a combined 25 percent: where `water_pct` plus `sediment_pct` is at most 25, the collection moves `COLLECTED → ASSAYED` and both figures are stored; where the sum exceeds 25, the collection moves `COLLECTED → REJECTED` instead, both figures are still stored, `reject_reason` is set to `CONTAMINATION_ABOVE_LIMIT`, `credit_cents` stays at 0 and `credit_variant` stays null. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Granting the credit is a `SalesRep` action reachable from two originating states, and which one it comes from changes the arithmetic. From `COLLECTED` — that is, with no assay on file — the variant is always `FLAT_RATE`. From `ASSAYED`, the variant is `FLAT_RATE` when the account's `contract_tier` is `TIER_A`, and `ASSAY_ADJUSTED` when the tier is `TIER_B` or `TIER_C`. There is no other branch and no default. The chosen variant is recorded in `credit_variant`.

The `FLAT_RATE` variant pays a flat 18 cents per collected litre: multiply `collected_volume_l` by 18 and round half up to whole cents. It ignores `water_pct` and `sediment_pct` entirely, so a dirty load is paid exactly as much as a clean one. The `ASSAY_ADJUSTED` variant pays a higher headline 25 cents per litre but discounts for contamination: compute a net factor of one minus `water_pct` divided by one hundred minus `sediment_pct` divided by one hundred, multiply `collected_volume_l` by 25 and by that net factor, round half up to whole cents, and then take the greater of the result and zero so the credit is never negative. The two variants can land either side of one another depending on how dirty the load was. The computed figure is written to `credit_cents` and the collection moves to `CREDITED`, which is terminal.

The account's ledger is never incremented in place. `takeback_credit_balance_cents` is always recomputed from scratch as the sum of `credit_cents` over that account's collections whose status is `CREDITED`, minus the sum of `takeback_credit_applied_cents` over that account's orders whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `HOLD` or `INVOICED`. Collections in `SCHEDULED`, `COLLECTED`, `ASSAYED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to the first sum, and orders in `DRAFT` or `VOID` contribute nothing to the second. Every recomputation — on a credit grant, on a credit draw, and on a void — also stamps the account's `version` upward by 1, and a grant or a draw additionally stamps `last_credit_event_at` from the logical clock.

Drawing the credit against an order is the final piece, and it is guarded on three fronts. The order must be `CONFIRMED`, or the draw is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even though the draw changes no status field. The request must carry `expected_customer_version` matching the account's current `version`, or the draw is refused with `ERR_STALE_CUSTOMER_VERSION` as described in §2.8. And the order must be take-back linked with a positive balance on the account: a draw against an order whose `takeback_linked` is false, or against an account whose `takeback_credit_balance_cents` is 0, is refused with `ERR_NO_TAKEBACK_CREDIT`. A successful draw runs steps four and five of the waterfall, **overwriting** `takeback_credit_applied_cents` with the freshly computed figure rather than adding to whatever stood there, writing `total_cents` from it, then recomputes the account's `takeback_credit_balance_cents`, stamps `last_credit_event_at`, and advances both the account's and the order's `version`. Because the balance is a recomputation rather than a decrement, and because a voided order drops out of the applied sum, an apply-then-void sequence restores the ledger to the cent.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

The catalogue below lists every error identifier the business logic of this API may return together with its HTTP status code. Three transport-level identifiers sit outside it because they belong to no rung of the ladder: a request to a path that matches no endpoint at all answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a request using a method the matched path does not offer answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, and an unhandled server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500. All three use the same error envelope as the catalogued identifiers. The conditions that trigger each one are deliberately not repeated here — they are stated in the narrative of §2 and §3, at the point in the workflow where each check is applied, and the evaluation order among them is fixed by the rejection ladder of §2.6.
