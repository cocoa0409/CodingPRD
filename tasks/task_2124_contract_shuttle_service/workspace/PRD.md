# Contract Shuttle Service API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Operators who run contracted shuttle fleets — the airport loop at a harbourfront hotel, the inter-quad circulator on a university campus, the park-and-ride shuttle for a corporate headquarters — live at the intersection of two very different disciplines. On one side sits an operations problem: routes and headways have to be cut against a demand forecast, vehicles and drivers have to be blocked out with legal layovers and duty caps, ADA-accessible equipment has to be where a wheelchair user asked for it, and a bus that goes into the shop at eight in the morning has to be pulled off every trip it was holding. On the other side sits a commercial problem: the client signed a service-level agreement, the operator owes penalties and missed-trip credits when it is not met, ridership has to be counted and reported per period, and when the term runs out someone has to negotiate a rate escalation against last period's actual performance.

Today those two disciplines are typically served by two disconnected systems, and the seam between them is where money leaks. A dispatcher cancels a trip in the scheduling tool and nobody raises the credit memo. A period report is submitted from a spreadsheet whose billable hours were rounded per trip instead of per period, producing cents that do not reconcile. A renewal is priced off a report that was still under dispute. This API exists to close that seam by making the operational record and the financial record the same record: every trip carries both an operational status and a settlement status, every missed trip that was the operator's fault mints a credit against the client's balance automatically, and every renewal rate is keyed to a frozen, approved report rather than to a live query.

This document specifies that API in full. It defines the contract, route, vehicle, driver, trip, ridership, on-demand request, maintenance, reporting, credit and renewal resources; the state machines that govern each of them; the calculations that turn minutes and boardings into cents; and the validation ladder that every request is screened through. Readers should treat §2 as the invariant contract that holds everywhere in the system, and §3 as the business narrative from which the request and response payloads of §4 must be reconstructed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide rules that hold for every endpoint, every resource, and every request, regardless of which business workflow is being exercised.

### 2.1. Operating Personas and Access Control

Every request to the API must be authenticated by two mandatory HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or is present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rejection with HTTP 401 applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the five roles the system recognises.

Those five roles are `ContractAdmin`, `ClientLiaison`, `OpsPlanner`, `FleetManager`, and `DriverUser`. Each endpoint declares which of them may invoke it — the authoritative list is the fourth column of the table in §4 — and a caller whose role is not on an endpoint's list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. Read operations are deliberately wide: every one of the five roles may issue every GET in the catalogue, because dispatchers, fleet managers, drivers and the client's own liaison all need to see the same operational picture. Write operations are narrow and are described role by role below.

A `ContractAdmin` owns the commercial relationship. That persona registers contracts, activates and terminates them, approves service period reports, voids credit memos, and drives renewal negotiation from the operator's side of the table. A `ClientLiaison` speaks for the client site: raising on-demand ride requests, opening and submitting reporting periods, disputing a settlement the client disagrees with, raising credit memos, and countering a renewal offer. An `OpsPlanner` owns the schedule: designing routes and headways, publishing and re-cutting the day's trips, registering drivers, binding vehicles and drivers to trips and releasing them again, matching on-demand requests to trips, and declaring trips missed. A `FleetManager` owns the physical assets: registering vehicles, applying and stripping client livery, and raising and closing shop orders. A `DriverUser` is a person behind the wheel, who starts and completes trips and serves the on-demand riders who were matched onto them. Opening and submitting a reporting period is shared between `ClientLiaison` and `ContractAdmin`; appending a ridership tally is shared between `OpsPlanner` and `DriverUser`; moving the logical clock is available to all five roles.

The relationship between `X-User-ID` and persisted data differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractAdmin`, `ClientLiaison`, `OpsPlanner`, or `FleetManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a service period report, or as `recorded_by_user_id` on a ridership row — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DriverUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Driver` row, and ownership checks compare the resource's driver foreign key to `X-User-ID` directly.

That ownership check has its own rejection. Whenever the caller's role is `DriverUser`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the `driver_id` of the trip being acted upon; for serving an on-demand request, it must equal the `driver_id` of the trip named by the request's `matched_trip_id`. A `DriverUser` who fails this test is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ASSIGNED` and HTTP 403. This ownership rule guards exactly four operations: starting a trip, completing a trip, serving an on-demand request, and appending a ridership count row. The ridership append is shared with `OpsPlanner`, and an `OpsPlanner` caller is not subject to the ownership test at all — the check is conditioned on the caller's role being `DriverUser`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Collection Filters

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query-string parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` sets how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400. Every list response repeats `limit` and `offset` in the `meta` object alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection says otherwise, list endpoints return rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Three collections override that default. The trip collection is ordered by `service_date` ascending, then `scheduled_departure_min` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, so a day's board reads top to bottom in departure order. The ridership rows of a single trip are ordered by `sequence_no` ascending, which is the same order in which they were appended. The maintenance orders raised against a single vehicle are ordered newest first, by `created_at` descending, because the shop cares about the most recent visit.

Collections also accept optional equality filters, and a filter that is omitted simply does not narrow the result. Contracts may be filtered by `status`, by `predecessor_contract_id`, and by `site_type`. Routes may be filtered by `contract_id` and `status`. Vehicles may be filtered by `branding_contract_id`, `status`, and `is_ada_accessible`. Trips accept the widest filter set: `route_id`, `contract_id`, `vehicle_id`, `driver_id`, `service_date`, `dispatch_status`, and `settlement_status`. A trip's ridership rows may be filtered by `source`. On-demand requests may be filtered by `contract_id`, `matched_trip_id`, `status`, and `service_date`. A vehicle's maintenance orders may be filtered by `status`. Service period reports may be filtered by `contract_id` and `status`. Credit memos may be filtered by `contract_id`, `trip_id`, `applied_report_id`, `status`, and `reason`. Renewal offers may be filtered by `contract_id`, `basis_report_id`, `successor_contract_id`, and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Audit Trail

Every resource the API persists is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix; a contract id and a trip id are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must never parse them. Alongside `id`, resources carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation. Crucially, "mutation" here includes mutations the caller did not directly request: when a cascade recomputes a derived counter on a parent, that parent's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if the parent had been written directly. Contracts, routes, vehicles, drivers, trips, on-demand requests, maintenance orders, service period reports, credit memos and renewal offers all behave this way.

One resource is deliberately different. A ridership count row is an append-only observation and is never edited after it is written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither should appear in its response payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Service Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical UTC clock which, before any client has moved it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and which never falls back to real time under any circumstance. Two rules in this system are keyed to that clock: whether a renewal offer falls inside its contract's renewal window, and whether a newly raised shop order is a same-day order that must displace trips immediately or a future-dated order that merely blocks assignment inside its window.

Any of the five roles may move the clock through a test-driving capability whose payload carries a single field, `to_iso8601`, holding the instant the clock should be set to. The response body is not a domain resource but a small object reporting `current_utc`, the new instant, and `current_service_date`, the UTC calendar date of that instant in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Moving the clock has one side effect: because a driver's committed duty is always measured against the clock's current UTC date, `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` is recomputed for every driver in the system against the new date. A driver who was fully booked yesterday and has nothing scheduled today therefore reads zero committed duty minutes the moment the clock rolls forward.

All calendar dates in the system — `term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `service_date`, `published_service_date`, `period_start_date`, `period_end_date` — are UTC calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. All times of day are integer minutes after local midnight in the range 0 through 1439 inclusive.

### 2.6. The Request Screening Ladder

Every request is screened through the same ordered ladder of checks, and the ladder is evaluated strictly in the order given below. The first check that fails determines the response, and no later check is evaluated. This ordering is a contract in its own right: when two conditions are simultaneously violated, the earlier rung is the one the client sees.

1. **Header presence.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must both be present and non-empty, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognition.** `X-User-Role` must name one of the five declared roles, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Role authorisation.** The caller's role must appear in the endpoint's authorised list, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
4. **Driver ownership.** When the caller's role is `DriverUser`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `driver_id` of the trip being acted on — and for serving an on-demand request, the `driver_id` of the trip named by `matched_trip_id`. This rung guards trip start, trip complete, on-demand serve, and ridership append. Failure yields `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ASSIGNED` (403).
5. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint, every required body field must be present, every value must be of the declared type, every enum value must be a member of its declared set, every integer must fall inside its declared range, every date must parse as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and every minute-of-day value must lie between 0 and 1439 inclusive. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Pagination shape.** On every list endpoint, `limit` must be an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` must be an integer of at least 0. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
7. **Path existence.** On every endpoint that takes an identifier in its URL, each identifier is resolved left to right, and the first one that names no existing row rejects the request with HTTP 404 carrying the error for that resource kind: `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MAINTENANCE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMO_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`. A request for the ridership rows of a trip that does not exist therefore fails as `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` before anything about ridership is considered.
8. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body — `contract_id`, `route_id`, `vehicle_id`, `driver_id`, `trip_id`, `branding_contract_id`, `predecessor_contract_id` — must resolve to an existing row, yielding HTTP 404 with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. This rung guards contract registration, route design, vehicle registration, vehicle branding, trip assignment, shop-order creation, on-demand request creation, on-demand matching, ridership append, report creation, credit memo creation, and renewal offer creation.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where an endpoint carries an expected parent version, that value must equal the parent's current `version`. This rung guards renewal offer creation and route republish, yielding `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` (412). §2.8 specifies the mechanism.
10. **Own-state precondition.** The target resource must be in a state from which the requested operation is declared. On every state-changing endpoint this rung yields HTTP 409 with the error matching the resource and operation: `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_ASSIGNED`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_IN_SERVICE`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_MATCHED`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_MEMO_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_COUNTERED`, or `ERR_MAINTENANCE_ORDER_NOT_OPEN`.
11. **Cross-entity state precondition.** Some operations require a second resource to be in a particular state. Assigning a trip requires the named vehicle's `status` to be `AVAILABLE`, failing as `ERR_VEHICLE_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Approving a report requires the contract's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`, and publishing or republishing a route requires the same, failing as `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Matching an on-demand request requires the resolved trip's route `status` to be `PUBLISHED`, failing as `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
12. **Segregation of duties.** Approving a report requires the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from the report's `submitted_by_user_id`, failing as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
13. **Maintenance-window safety**, on trip assignment: `ERR_VEHICLE_IN_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW` (422).
14. **ADA driver certification**, on trip assignment: `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ADA_CERTIFIED` (422).
15. **ADA vehicle requirement**, on an on-demand match that names an explicit `trip_id`: `ERR_ADA_VEHICLE_REQUIRED` (422).
16. **Branded-fleet ADA floor**, on vehicle branding and on vehicle registration when `branding_contract_id` is supplied: `ERR_ADA_FLEET_SHORTFALL` (422).
17. **Fleet code uniqueness**, on vehicle registration: `ERR_FLEET_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409).
18. **Vehicle block conflict**, on trip assignment: `ERR_VEHICLE_BLOCK_CONFLICT` (409).
19. **Driver block conflict**, on trip assignment: `ERR_DRIVER_BLOCK_CONFLICT` (409).
20. **Driver duty cap**, on trip assignment: `ERR_DRIVER_DUTY_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Seat availability**, on an on-demand match that names an explicit `trip_id`: `ERR_TRIP_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
22. **Service date inside term**, on route publish, route republish, and on-demand request creation: `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM` (422).
23. **Reporting period inside term**, on report creation: `ERR_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM` (422).
24. **Reporting period overlap**, on report creation: `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
25. **Eligible route exists**, on on-demand request creation: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROUTE` (422).
26. **Renewal window open**, on renewal offer creation: `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
27. **Explicit trip departure**, on an on-demand match that names an explicit `trip_id`: `ERR_TRIP_DEPARTURE_TOO_EARLY` (422).
28. **Vehicle branding match**, on trip assignment: `ERR_VEHICLE_BRANDING_MISMATCH` (422).
29. **Trip contract match**, on credit memo creation and on an on-demand match that names an explicit `trip_id`: `ERR_TRIP_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` (422).
30. **Credit memo cap**, on credit memo creation: `ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422).
31. **Counter rate bounds**, on countering a renewal offer: `ERR_COUNTER_RATE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (422).

Rungs 13 through 31 are described in full, with their exact conditions, in the workflow subsections of §3. The point of listing them here in order is that their relative precedence is fixed: a trip assignment that simultaneously names an unavailable vehicle, sits inside a shop window, uses a non-certified driver for an ADA bus, conflicts with another block, and blows the duty cap is rejected as `ERR_VEHICLE_UNAVAILABLE`, because rung 11 precedes all of them. Likewise, an explicit on-demand match against a trip that is both non-ADA and full is rejected as `ERR_ADA_VEHICLE_REQUIRED`, because rung 15 precedes rung 21.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

A large part of this system's behaviour lives in cascades — one write that legitimately changes several rows. The governing principle is that **every derived counter is a full recomputation from its source rows, never an incremental delta applied to a stored figure.** When a trip is released from a vehicle, the vehicle's committed minutes are not decremented; they are recomputed from scratch by scanning the trip table. This is what makes the compensating pairs in this system exactly conserving.

The derived fields that behave this way are: `credit_balance_cents`, `branded_vehicle_count` and `branded_ada_vehicle_count` on a contract; `required_vehicle_count` and `ada_required_count` on a route; `committed_block_minutes` on a vehicle; `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on a driver; `counted_boardings`, `counted_ada_boardings` and `matched_party_seats` on a trip; `displaced_trip_count` on a maintenance order; and the whole settlement block on a service period report. Each of these is defined precisely in §3 alongside the workflow that moves it.

When a cascade touches a row, that row is mutated in the sense of §2.4: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one. A caller who terminates a contract will therefore observe not only the contract's version moving, but the versions of every route, trip and on-demand request the termination swept up. A caller who mints a credit memo will observe the owning contract's version move, because the memo changed the contract's outstanding credit balance.

Two derived counters on a contract deserve their definitions here, because they are read by a validation rather than by a workflow. `branded_vehicle_count` is the number of vehicle rows whose `branding_contract_id` equals the contract's `id` and whose `status` is either `AVAILABLE` or `IN_MAINTENANCE`; retired vehicles are excluded. `branded_ada_vehicle_count` is the number within that same set whose `is_ada_accessible` is true.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Contracts and Routes

Two write operations in this system are destructive in a way that a stale client cannot detect on its own, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent entity's `version`. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no meaning.

The first guard covers **renewal offer creation**. The request body must carry `expected_contract_version`, a required field holding the `version` of the target `ServiceContract` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version` at the moment of the write, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This guard exists because creating a second offer against a contract is legitimate product behaviour on its own terms — it supersedes the standing offer — so the only thing that distinguishes an intentional supersede from a write made against a stale view of the contract is the version the caller carries.

The second guard covers **route republish**. The request body must carry `expected_route_version`, a required field holding the `version` of the target `Route` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the route's current `version` at the moment of the write, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Republishing looks idempotent but is destructive: it cancels the day's `SCHEDULED` and `ASSIGNED` trips and regenerates them from a new headway, so a caller working from a stale headway or a stale trip list would silently discard work that another writer had already committed.

In both cases the version check sits at rung 9 of the ladder in §2.6 — after the referenced rows have been proven to exist, but before any state precondition, business rule or cascade is evaluated.

### 2.9. Minutes, Money and Rounding Discipline

Durations, times of day, distances and money are all integers in this system. A time of day is an integer count of minutes after local midnight, from 0 through 1439 inclusive. A duration is an integer count of minutes. An odometer reading is an integer count of kilometres. Money is always an integer count of cents; there is no floating-point currency anywhere in the API.

Percentages are the only decimal quantities, and they carry two decimal places: `escalation_index_pct`, `sla_on_time_threshold_pct`, `ada_min_accessible_pct`, `on_time_pct`, `penalty_tier_pct`, `volume_pct`, `clawback_pct` and `applied_escalation_pct`. The single non-percentage decimal is `billable_service_hours`, which also carries two decimal places.

Wherever a value must be reduced to fewer digits, the system rounds half up: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. This applies to `billable_service_hours` at two decimal places, to `on_time_pct` at two decimal places, and to every cent amount that is derived by multiplication or division — `gross_amount_cents`, `sla_penalty_cents`, and `proposed_rate_cents_per_service_hour` — each of which is rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

Where a formula calls for a ceiling or a floor, that ceiling or floor is taken on the exact rational value of the expression, never on a pre-rounded intermediate. Computing the ceiling of a loop time divided by a headway must not first evaluate the quotient as a limited-precision decimal and then round it; the exact quotient decides.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties in Settlement

Money leaving the operator's pocket is never authorised by the same person who computed it. Every submission of a service period report stamps the caller's `X-User-ID` into the report's `submitted_by_user_id`, overwriting whatever a previous submission had stored there. Every approval stamps the caller's `X-User-ID` into `approved_by_user_id`. Approval requires the two to differ: if the approving `X-User-ID` equals the report's current `submitted_by_user_id`, the approval is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

This holds even when both actions are performed under a role that is permitted to do both. A `ContractAdmin` may open and submit a reporting period, and a `ContractAdmin` is the only role that may approve one — but a `ContractAdmin` who submitted a particular report may not approve that same report. A second `ContractAdmin`, or a report that was submitted by a `ClientLiaison`, clears the check. Note that because each submit overwrites `submitted_by_user_id`, it is the most recent submitter who is locked out of approval, not every historical submitter.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Client Site

Everything in this system hangs off a **service contract**, the commercial agreement between the shuttle operator and one client site. A `ContractAdmin` registers one by supplying the site's `client_name`, between 1 and 120 characters, and its `site_type`, which is one of `HOTEL`, `CAMPUS`, or `CORPORATE`. The commercial terms follow: `base_rate_cents_per_service_hour` is the contracted price in cents for one billable service hour and must be at least 1; `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` bound the calendar dates the contract covers, and the end date may not precede the start date. Four policy parameters complete the registration. `escalation_index_pct` is the base annual escalation index, a percentage between 0.00 and 10.00 with two decimals, which later drives renewal pricing. `sla_on_time_threshold_pct`, between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals, is the on-time percentage the operator promises for each reporting period. `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip`, an integer of at least 0 cents, does double duty as both the flat penalty charged per missed trip and the amount of the credit memo a missed trip mints. `ada_min_accessible_pct`, between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals, is the minimum share of the client's branded fleet that must be wheelchair accessible. A registration may optionally carry `predecessor_contract_id`, which is null on an original contract and is otherwise the id of the contract this one succeeds.

A newly registered contract lands in `DRAFT` with `credit_balance_cents`, `branded_vehicle_count` and `branded_ada_vehicle_count` all initialised to 0. Nothing operational may hang off it yet. Activation moves it `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE`, and is available only from `DRAFT`; attempting it against a contract in any other state is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`. Activation has no cascade of its own — it simply refreshes `updated_at` and increments the contract's `version` by one — but it is the gate that opens route publication, on-demand requests, report approval and renewal.

The contract's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_PENDING`, and then into one of two terminal states, `RENEWED` or `TERMINATED`. The path through `RENEWAL_PENDING` is a genuine cycle: a contract can enter it, come back to `ACTIVE` on a declined offer, and enter it again within the same renewal window. §3.9 covers that loop.

Termination is the blunt exit and is permitted from either `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`; from any other state it is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The `ContractAdmin` supplies a `termination_reason` in the payload, and the contract moves to `TERMINATED` while a four-link cascade unwinds everything hanging off it. First, every route belonging to the contract whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, or `DEGRADED` moves to `RETIRED`. Second, because those routes retired, every trip on them whose `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `CANCELLED`, has its `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` cleared, and triggers a recomputation of `committed_block_minutes` on each released vehicle and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on each released driver. Third, because those trips were cancelled, every on-demand request of the contract whose `status` is `PENDING` or `MATCHED` moves to `EXPIRED` with its `matched_trip_id` cleared — and where a request had been `MATCHED`, the trip it was riding has its `matched_party_seats` recomputed. Fourth, when the contract was in `RENEWAL_PENDING`, the standing renewal offer — the one whose `status` is `OPEN` or `COUNTERED` — moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The contract's own `version` increments by one, as does the version of every row the cascade touched.

Throughout its life the contract carries a running client credit position in `credit_balance_cents`. That figure is the sum of `amount_cents` over every credit memo of this contract whose `status` is `ISSUED`; memos that have been applied to a report or voided are excluded, so the balance always reads as outstanding, not-yet-settled credit. It is recomputed in full — never adjusted by a delta — every time a memo is minted, voided, or applied.

### 3.2. Route Design, Fleet Sizing and the Daily Cut

An `OpsPlanner` designs a **route** against an existing contract by supplying `contract_id`, a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and five numbers that describe the service pattern. `loop_minutes`, from 1 to 1440, is how long one full loop takes from departure back to the stop it started at. `headway_minutes`, from 1 to 720, is the planned gap between consecutive departures. `service_window_start_min` and `service_window_end_min`, each a minute of day from 0 to 1439, bound the first and last departures of the day, and the end may not precede the start. `demand_forecast_pax_per_hour`, an integer of at least 0, is the forecast boardings per hour. `reference_seat_capacity`, an integer of at least 1, is the seat capacity the planner assumes when a trip has no vehicle bound to it yet. The route is born in `DRAFT` with `published_service_date` null and no trips.

Designing a route immediately sizes the fleet it needs, and that sizing is recomputed again on every publish and every republish. Two independent requirements are calculated and the larger wins. The **headway requirement** is the loop time divided by the headway, rounded up to the next whole vehicle: a 60-minute loop on a 20-minute headway needs three vehicles, and a 61-minute loop on the same headway needs four. The **demand requirement** takes the forecast boardings per hour, multiplies it by the loop time in minutes, divides that product by the product of 60 and the reference seat capacity, and rounds the result up to the next whole vehicle. `required_vehicle_count` is whichever of those two whole numbers is greater. From it, `ada_required_count` is derived by multiplying `required_vehicle_count` by the owning contract's `ada_min_accessible_pct`, dividing by 100, and rounding up to the next whole vehicle. All three ceilings are taken on the exact rational value of their expressions, not on a decimal that was rounded first, and only the route's own fields and the contract's accessibility floor participate — no vehicle row and no trip row is consulted.

Publishing cuts the day's schedule. The `OpsPlanner` supplies a single `service_date` in the payload, and the route moves `DRAFT` → `PUBLISHED`; a route in any other state is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_DRAFT`. The owning contract must be `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`, failing which the publish is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the supplied `service_date` must fall inside the closed range from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` inclusive, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`. On success the route's `published_service_date` is set to that date, the fleet requirement is recomputed, the route's `version` increments by one, and the day's trips are generated.

Trip generation is fully determined by the route's window and headway. The number of departures is found by subtracting `service_window_start_min` from `service_window_end_min`, dividing that span by `headway_minutes`, taking the floor of the exact quotient, and adding one. Departures are then emitted in ascending order, indexed from zero: the departure at index zero is at `service_window_start_min`, and each subsequent departure is one headway later than the one before it. Each generated trip records `scheduled_departure_min` at its departure minute and `scheduled_arrival_min` at that departure plus the route's `loop_minutes` as it stands at generation time. Each trip also records `route_id`, the `contract_id` copied down from the route and never changed afterwards, and the `service_date` from the payload. Trips are persisted in ascending departure order, so `created_at` ascends with departure. Every generated trip begins with `dispatch_status` `SCHEDULED` and `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, with `vehicle_id`, `driver_id`, `actual_departure_min`, `actual_completion_min` and `missed_reason` all null, and with `counted_boardings`, `counted_ada_boardings` and `matched_party_seats` at 0.

Re-cutting the day is a separate and deliberately guarded operation. Republishing accepts three fields — a `service_date`, a replacement `headway_minutes`, and `expected_route_version` — and is permitted only when the route's `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `DEGRADED`; from anywhere else it is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. As with publish, the contract must be `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the `service_date` must lie inside the contract's closed term range (`ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`). The version guard of §2.8 applies: a mismatched `expected_route_version` fails with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before any of the destructive work below begins.

Republishing then runs a cancel-and-regenerate cascade. Every trip of this route on the supplied `service_date` whose `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `CANCELLED` and has its `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` cleared; trips already `IN_SERVICE`, `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` are left entirely untouched and are never duplicated. Because those trips released their crews and buses, `committed_block_minutes` is recomputed on each affected vehicle and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on each affected driver. Because those trips lost their riders, every on-demand request whose `matched_trip_id` pointed at a cancelled trip returns from `MATCHED` to `PENDING` with `matched_trip_id` cleared, its `promised_wait_minutes` recomputed, and the trip's `matched_party_seats` recomputed. The route's stored `headway_minutes` is then replaced by the payload's value, the day's trips are regenerated from index zero using the new headway exactly as described above, `required_vehicle_count` and `ada_required_count` are recomputed, and the route's `version` increments by one.

Republish is also the recovery leg of the route cycle. A route that had fallen to `DEGRADED` returns `DEGRADED` → `PUBLISHED` on a successful republish, running the identical cancel-and-regenerate cascade. A route falls to `DEGRADED` in only one way: the maintenance displacement cascade of §3.3 pulls buses off its trips, and afterwards the number of distinct vehicles still bound to that route's trips on the affected `service_date` with `dispatch_status` of `ASSIGNED` or `IN_SERVICE` is strictly fewer than the route's `required_vehicle_count`. That degradation applies only to a route currently in `PUBLISHED`, and it increments the route's `version` by one.

`RETIRED` is the route's only terminal state. A `DRAFT` route retires when its owning contract is terminated. A `PUBLISHED` or `DEGRADED` route retires when its owning contract is terminated or when an accepted renewal closes that contract out. In every case retirement cancels every trip of the route whose `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED` and increments the route's `version` by one.

### 3.3. The Fleet: Registration, Client Livery and the Shop

A `FleetManager` registers a **vehicle** with an operator-wide unique `fleet_code` of 1 to 40 characters, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1, an `is_ada_accessible` flag that is true when the bus has a wheelchair lift and securement, an `odometer_km` reading of at least 0 kilometres, and a `service_interval_km` of at least 1 kilometre recording the preventive maintenance interval the shop works to. If the supplied `fleet_code` is already carried by another vehicle row, the registration is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_CODE_DUPLICATE` and HTTP 409. A new vehicle enters the fleet with `status` `AVAILABLE` and `committed_block_minutes` at 0.

Registration may optionally supply `branding_contract_id`, wrapping the bus in a client's livery from day one, and a bus can be re-wrapped or stripped at any point afterwards through a dedicated branding operation that takes `branding_contract_id` as its only field. Both paths recompute `branded_vehicle_count` and `branded_ada_vehicle_count` on every contract affected — the contract being wrapped into and, on a re-wrap, the contract being wrapped out of — and the branding operation increments the vehicle's `version` by one.

Livery is not free of obligation, because the client's accessibility floor is measured against the branded fleet rather than the whole fleet. Whenever a vehicle is being wrapped into a contract's livery — whether at registration or through a later re-wrap — the system evaluates the resulting fleet composition **after** the change takes effect: it multiplies the contract's resulting `branded_ada_vehicle_count` by 100, divides by its resulting `branded_vehicle_count`, and rounds half up to two decimal places. That percentage must be greater than or equal to the contract's `ada_min_accessible_pct`; a result exactly equal to the floor passes. A wrap that would push the branded ADA share below the floor is rejected with `ERR_ADA_FLEET_SHORTFALL` and HTTP 422, and nothing is written. The floor is evaluated against the contract named by `branding_contract_id`, so stripping a bus of its livery by supplying a null value has no contract to test against.

Taking a bus into the shop is done by raising a **maintenance order**. The `FleetManager` supplies the `vehicle_id`, the `kind` of visit — `PREVENTIVE`, `CORRECTIVE`, or `BRANDING_WRAP` — the `service_date` the visit occupies, and the `window_start_min` and `window_end_min` bounding the shop window, where the end may not precede the start. The order is created in `status` `OPEN` with `outcome` null, `displaced_trip_count` at 0, and `odometer_at_open` set to a snapshot of the vehicle's `odometer_km` taken at that moment.

What happens next depends entirely on the date. When the order's `service_date` equals the logical clock's current UTC date and the vehicle's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, the vehicle moves `AVAILABLE` → `IN_MAINTENANCE` immediately and a four-link displacement cascade runs. The order row itself is the first link. The second link moves the vehicle. The third link displaces work: every trip whose `vehicle_id` is this vehicle, whose `service_date` equals the order's `service_date`, whose `dispatch_status` is `ASSIGNED`, and whose closed interval from `scheduled_departure_min` to `scheduled_arrival_min` intersects the closed shop window from `window_start_min` to `window_end_min`, is pushed back from `ASSIGNED` to `SCHEDULED` with its `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` cleared; the order's `displaced_trip_count` is set to the number of trips displaced. The fourth link releases riders: for each displaced trip, every on-demand request whose `matched_trip_id` named that trip and whose `status` is `MATCHED` returns to `PENDING` with `matched_trip_id` cleared and `promised_wait_minutes` recomputed. Beyond those links, `committed_block_minutes` is recomputed on the vehicle and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on every driver freed, and the vehicle's `version` increments by one.

The cascade has one further branch, and it reaches sideways into the route model. Because the displaced trips left their route's assigned set, each affected route whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` is re-examined: if the number of distinct vehicles still bound to that route's trips on that `service_date` with `dispatch_status` of `ASSIGNED` or `IN_SERVICE` has fallen strictly below the route's `required_vehicle_count`, the route drops to `DEGRADED`. Recovering it takes a republish, as §3.2 describes.

A future-dated order behaves quite differently: nothing is displaced, the vehicle stays `AVAILABLE`, and the order simply sits there as a booking that blocks assignment inside its window, as §3.4 explains.

Closing a shop order is a branching fork — the same operation produces two entirely different outcomes according to the `outcome` field in the payload. Closing is permitted only while the order's `status` is `OPEN`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_ORDER_NOT_OPEN`. On either branch the order moves `OPEN` → `CLOSED`, its `outcome` is stored, and its `version` increments by one. With `outcome` `RETURN_TO_SERVICE`, a vehicle currently `IN_MAINTENANCE` moves back to `AVAILABLE` and its `version` increments by one. With `outcome` `RETIRE`, a vehicle currently `IN_MAINTENANCE` moves to `RETIRED` — terminal, permanently out of the fleet — its `branding_contract_id` is cleared, and if it had been branded, `branded_vehicle_count` and `branded_ada_vehicle_count` are recomputed on the contract it was wrapped for; its `version` increments by one. When the order being closed was future-dated and the vehicle is therefore still `AVAILABLE`, neither vehicle transition fires and only the order's `status` and `outcome` change.

A vehicle's `committed_block_minutes` is the operator's measure of how much of that bus's day is already spoken for, and it is recomputed in full — never by delta — on assignment, release, missed-trip declaration, shop-order creation, route republish and contract termination. It is the sum, over every trip pointing at that vehicle whose `dispatch_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED` across all service dates, of that trip's scheduled duration plus a fixed twelve-minute layover buffer; a trip's scheduled duration is its `scheduled_arrival_min` minus its `scheduled_departure_min`. Trips that are `SCHEDULED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. Because the figure is recomputed from the trip table rather than adjusted, releasing a trip restores the vehicle to exactly the value it carried before that trip was bound to it.

### 3.4. Drivers, Blocks and the Dispatch Board

An `OpsPlanner` registers a **driver** with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `license_class` that is either `CDL_B` or `CDL_C`, an `ada_certified` flag that is true when the driver is certified to operate wheelchair-lift equipment, and a `max_duty_minutes_per_day` between 1 and 1440 that is the driver's hard daily driving-duty cap. A newly registered driver starts with `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` at 0.

That counter is a live picture of today only. It is the sum, over every trip whose `driver_id` is this driver, whose `service_date` equals the logical clock's current UTC calendar date, and whose `dispatch_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED`, of that trip's `scheduled_arrival_min` minus its `scheduled_departure_min`. Unlike the vehicle's committed block, a driver's duty carries no layover buffer at all — duty is measured by wheels-turning minutes only. It is recomputed on assignment, release, missed-trip declaration, shop-order creation, route republish, contract termination, and whenever the logical clock is moved.

Binding a bus and a driver to a trip is the central dispatch action. The `OpsPlanner` supplies `vehicle_id` and `driver_id`, and the trip moves `SCHEDULED` → `ASSIGNED`; a trip in any other dispatch state is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The trip's route must itself still be `PUBLISHED` or `DEGRADED` — in practice a retired route has already cancelled its trips, so this is normally satisfied by the trip's own `SCHEDULED` status, and a violation surfaces as `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The named vehicle's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`; a bus that is `IN_MAINTENANCE` or `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_UNAVAILABLE` and HTTP 409, and that rejection precedes every safety and scheduling check below.

Six further checks then run, in this fixed order:

- **The shop window.** If any `OPEN` maintenance order exists for the named vehicle on the trip's `service_date` whose closed window from `window_start_min` to `window_end_min` intersects the trip's closed interval from `scheduled_departure_min` to `scheduled_arrival_min`, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_IN_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW` and HTTP 422. This is how a future-dated shop order protects itself without taking the bus out of service today.
- **ADA certification.** When the named vehicle's `is_ada_accessible` is true, the named driver's `ada_certified` must also be true, failing which the assignment is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ADA_CERTIFIED` and HTTP 422. A driver who is certified may of course drive a non-accessible bus.
- **The vehicle's block.** No other trip already assigned to this vehicle on this `service_date` may overlap the candidate's buffered block. A trip's buffered block is the half-open interval that starts at its `scheduled_departure_min` and ends at its `scheduled_arrival_min` plus the twelve-minute layover buffer, with the end point excluded. Because the end point is excluded, two trips need a gap of at least twelve minutes between one arrival and the next departure, and a gap of exactly twelve minutes passes. An overlap is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_BLOCK_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409.
- **The driver's block.** No other trip already assigned to this driver on the same `service_date` may overlap the candidate's unbuffered interval, which runs from `scheduled_departure_min` up to but not including `scheduled_arrival_min`. Drivers carry no layover buffer, so touching endpoints — one trip's arrival exactly equal to the next trip's departure — are permitted. An overlap is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_BLOCK_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409.
- **The duty cap.** The driver's total assigned duty minutes on the candidate trip's own `service_date`, counting the candidate trip itself, must be less than or equal to that driver's `max_duty_minutes_per_day`; a total exactly equal to the cap passes. Exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_DUTY_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. Note carefully that this check is evaluated for the candidate trip's service date, which is not necessarily the logical clock's current date that `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` reports.
- **Livery discipline.** The named vehicle's `branding_contract_id` must be null or must equal the trip's `contract_id`; a bus wearing one client's livery may not run another client's route. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_BRANDING_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422.

A successful assignment writes `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` onto the trip, recomputes `committed_block_minutes` on the vehicle and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on the driver, and increments the trip's `version` by one.

Releasing a trip is the exact compensating action. It is permitted only from `ASSIGNED`, moving the trip back `ASSIGNED` → `SCHEDULED`; from any other dispatch state it is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The release clears `vehicle_id` and `driver_id`, recomputes `committed_block_minutes` on the released vehicle — which, because the recomputation is a full scan rather than a delta, restores it to precisely its pre-assignment value — and recomputes `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` on the released driver. It also unwinds the riders: every on-demand request whose `matched_trip_id` was this trip and whose `status` is `MATCHED` returns to `PENDING` with `matched_trip_id` cleared and `promised_wait_minutes` recomputed, and the trip's `matched_party_seats` is recomputed. The trip's `version` increments by one.

Once the day begins, the trip belongs to its driver. A `DriverUser` whose `X-User-ID` equals the trip's `driver_id` pulls out by supplying `actual_departure_min`, moving the trip `ASSIGNED` → `IN_SERVICE`; from any other dispatch state the request fails with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_ASSIGNED`, and a driver acting on someone else's trip fails with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The departure minute is stored and read later by the on-time test in §3.8, and the trip's `version` increments by one.

Closing the loop moves the trip `IN_SERVICE` → `COMPLETED`; from any other dispatch state the request fails with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. The same driver supplies four fields: `actual_completion_min`, which must be at least the trip's recorded `actual_departure_min` and is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it is earlier, together with the driver's own tally of `boarded`, `alighted` and `ada_boardings`. Completion stores `actual_completion_min`, appends a ridership row carrying those three counts with `source` `DRIVER_TALLY` and the next `sequence_no` for that trip, recomputes `counted_boardings` and `counted_ada_boardings`, and increments the trip's `version` by one.

The trip's dispatch axis has three terminal states — `COMPLETED`, `MISSED`, and `CANCELLED`. Cancellation is never a client action: a trip in `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `CANCELLED` only through the route republish, route retirement, or contract termination cascades, and where the trip had been `ASSIGNED`, the cancellation additionally clears `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` and recomputes `committed_block_minutes` and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` before incrementing the trip's `version` by one.

### 3.5. On-Demand Riders: Promising a Wait and Delivering It

Contracted shuttle service is never purely fixed-route. A guest at the hotel desk or a staff member on campus calls in a ride, and a `ClientLiaison` logs it as an **on-demand request** carrying the `contract_id` the rider travels under, the `service_date` of travel, a `requested_pickup_min` giving the earliest acceptable pickup as a minute of day, a `party_size` of at least one seat, and a `requires_ada` flag that is true when the party needs a wheelchair-accessible bus. The `service_date` must lie inside the contract's closed term range, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`.

The moment a request is logged, the system quotes the rider a wait, and this **promised** quote is computed once and then frozen forever. It looks only at routes: the candidates are the routes of the same contract whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose `published_service_date` equals the request's `service_date`, and whose service window covers the requested pickup — that is, whose `service_window_start_min` is less than or equal to `requested_pickup_min` and whose `service_window_end_min` is greater than or equal to it. Candidates are ranked by `headway_minutes` ascending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first one is chosen. If there are no candidates at all, the request is not created: it is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROUTE` and HTTP 422. Otherwise the chosen route's headway is frozen onto the request as `promised_headway_minutes`, and `promised_wait_minutes` is set to half that headway rounded up to a whole minute, plus a fixed three-minute boarding lead. A request on a 15-minute headway is therefore promised eleven minutes: eight, being half of fifteen rounded up, plus three.

Note what the promised quote deliberately does not see. It never reads a trip row, so it is blind to displacement, to missed trips and to actual departure times. A new request begins in `PENDING` with `matched_trip_id`, `actual_wait_minutes` and `wait_overage_minutes` all null.

Matching is an `OpsPlanner` action on a request that is still `PENDING`; from any other state it is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`. The payload optionally carries `trip_id`, and the two forms behave very differently.

When `trip_id` is omitted, the system runs its selector over the day's board. A trip is eligible when its `contract_id` equals the request's `contract_id`, its `service_date` equals the request's `service_date`, its `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED`, its `scheduled_departure_min` is greater than or equal to the request's `requested_pickup_min`, its route's `status` is `PUBLISHED`, and it has enough room. Room is measured as remaining seats: the trip's seat basis — the bound vehicle's `seat_capacity` when the trip has a `vehicle_id`, and otherwise the route's `reference_seat_capacity` — minus the trip's current `matched_party_seats`; that remainder must be greater than or equal to the request's `party_size`. When the request's `requires_ada` is true, eligibility additionally demands that the trip already has a `vehicle_id` and that the bound vehicle's `is_ada_accessible` is true; when `requires_ada` is false, no vehicle condition applies at all. Trips that are `IN_SERVICE`, `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` are never candidates. Eligible trips are ranked by `scheduled_departure_min` ascending, then by their route's `headway_minutes` ascending, then by their own `created_at` ascending, and the first is taken. No random property, and in particular no identifier, participates in eligibility, ranking or tie-breaking.

If the selector's candidate pool is empty, matching does not fail — it decides. The request moves `PENDING` → `DENIED` with its `version` incremented by one, and the call succeeds with HTTP 200 returning the denied request. This empty-pool fallback exists only for the selector form.

When `trip_id` is supplied explicitly, the selector is skipped, but every eligibility condition above is still enforced against that single named trip, and a failure raises a specific error instead of quietly denying the request. A trip belonging to a different contract than the request fails with `ERR_TRIP_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` (422). A trip whose route is not `PUBLISHED` fails with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). A request with `requires_ada` true pointed at a trip that has no vehicle bound, or whose bound vehicle is not accessible, fails with `ERR_ADA_VEHICLE_REQUIRED` (422). A trip whose `scheduled_departure_min` is earlier than the request's `requested_pickup_min`, or whose `service_date` differs from the request's, fails with `ERR_TRIP_DEPARTURE_TOO_EARLY` (422). A trip whose remaining seats are fewer than the request's `party_size` fails with `ERR_TRIP_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Where several of these are violated at once, the screening order of §2.6 decides which the caller sees: the ADA check precedes the seat check, which precedes the departure check, which precedes the contract-match check.

A successful match, by either form, sets `matched_trip_id`, moves the request `PENDING` → `MATCHED`, recomputes the trip's `matched_party_seats`, and increments the request's `version` by one. A trip's `matched_party_seats` is the sum of `party_size` over every on-demand request whose `matched_trip_id` names that trip and whose `status` is `MATCHED` or `SERVED`; requests that were pushed back to `PENDING`, denied or expired contribute nothing.

Serving the rider is the driver's action and is the only point at which the **realized** wait is computed. The `DriverUser` whose `X-User-ID` equals the `driver_id` of the trip named by `matched_trip_id` supplies `actual_pickup_min`; anyone else is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The request must be `MATCHED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_MATCHED`, and the matched trip's `dispatch_status` must be `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_IN_SERVICE`.

The realized calculation reads only the request's own frozen figures and the observed pickup minute; it never re-reads the route. `actual_wait_minutes` is the observed `actual_pickup_min` minus the rider's `requested_pickup_min`, floored at zero so an early pickup records a wait of zero rather than a negative number. `wait_overage_minutes` is `actual_wait_minutes` minus the frozen `promised_wait_minutes`, likewise floored at zero. For the same request the promised and realized figures routinely disagree, and only the realized side can create money.

When `wait_overage_minutes` is strictly greater than the two-minute wait grace, the service level has been breached and a credit memo is minted automatically against the client: `contract_id` from the request, `trip_id` set to the request's `matched_trip_id`, `reason` `WAIT_SLA_BREACH`, `status` `ISSUED`, and `amount_cents` set to whichever is smaller — the overage minutes multiplied by 150 cents per minute, or the contract's `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip`, which acts as a per-incident cap. An overage of exactly two minutes mints nothing; three minutes mints a memo. Because the memo became `ISSUED`, the contract's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the contract's `version` increments by one. The request itself moves `MATCHED` → `SERVED` with its `version` incremented by one.

A request that never reaches the bus has three other endings, all of them driven by cascades rather than by a direct call. A `MATCHED` request returns to `PENDING`, with `matched_trip_id` cleared, `promised_wait_minutes` recomputed and the trip's `matched_party_seats` recomputed, whenever its trip is released, displaced by a shop order, or cancelled by a republish. A `MATCHED` request moves to `DENIED`, with `matched_trip_id` cleared, when its trip is declared missed. And a `PENDING` or `MATCHED` request moves to `EXPIRED` when the owning contract is terminated, with `matched_trip_id` cleared and the trip's `matched_party_seats` recomputed where the request had been matched. `SERVED`, `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal, and every one of these transitions increments the request's `version` by one.

### 3.6. Counting Ridership

Boardings are the currency of client reporting, and this system deliberately accepts more than one account of them. Both an `OpsPlanner` and a `DriverUser` may append a **ridership count** row against a trip by supplying `trip_id`, the number `boarded` (at least 0), the number `alighted` (at least 0), the subset `ada_boardings` that used the wheelchair lift, which must be between 0 and `boarded` inclusive, and the `source` of the number, which is either `DRIVER_TALLY` or `APC_SENSOR`. When the caller is a `DriverUser`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the trip's `driver_id`, otherwise the append is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Completing a trip also appends a row automatically, always with `source` `DRIVER_TALLY`, carrying the tally the driver supplied at completion.

Ridership rows are append-only observations. Each is stamped with `recorded_by_user_id`, holding the `X-User-ID` of whoever appended it, and with `sequence_no`, a per-trip monotonic counter that starts at 1 for a trip's first row and increases by exactly one for each row appended to that same trip afterwards. As §2.4 notes, these rows carry `id` and `created_at` only — no `updated_at` and no `version`.

From those rows the system projects a single authoritative count onto the trip. Automatic passenger counters outrank human tallies unconditionally: if any row for the trip has `source` `APC_SENSOR`, the one among them with the greatest `sequence_no` is chosen. Only when there is no sensor row at all does the system fall back to the `DRIVER_TALLY` row with the greatest `sequence_no`. The trip's `counted_boardings` is then the chosen row's `boarded` and its `counted_ada_boardings` is that same row's `ada_boardings`; when the trip has no ridership rows whatsoever, both read 0. A single `APC_SENSOR` row therefore outranks any number of driver tallies regardless of the order in which they were appended, and because `sequence_no` is the sole ranking key, no timestamp or identifier tie-break is ever needed.

### 3.7. Missed Trips, Credit Memos and the Client's Balance

A trip carries two statuses that move independently of one another. `dispatch_status` is the operational axis described in §3.4; `settlement_status` is the financial axis, which begins at `UNBILLED` and ends at either `BILLED` or `WAIVED`. A trip that has been completed operationally stays `UNBILLED` financially until a report is approved, and a trip that has been credited can be walked back to `UNBILLED` if the credit is reversed. The two axes never constrain each other directly; they are joined only through the specific rules below.

Declaring a trip missed is an `OpsPlanner` action, permitted while the trip's `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ASSIGNED` and rejected from anywhere else with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The payload carries a single `reason`, which is one of `OPERATOR_FAULT`, `CLIENT_REQUEST`, or `WEATHER`. The trip moves to `MISSED`, its `missed_reason` records the supplied value, and where the trip had been `ASSIGNED` its `vehicle_id` and `driver_id` are cleared with `committed_block_minutes` and `assigned_duty_minutes_current_date` recomputed on the released bus and driver. Every on-demand request whose `matched_trip_id` named this trip and whose `status` is `MATCHED` moves to `DENIED` with `matched_trip_id` cleared. The trip's `version` increments by one.

The `reason` then chooses one of three financial branches, and this is where the operational axis reaches across to the settlement axis. With `OPERATOR_FAULT`, the trip's `settlement_status` moves `UNBILLED` → `CREDITED`, and because it did, a credit memo is minted automatically: `contract_id` taken from the trip's `contract_id`, `trip_id` set to the trip, `reason` `MISSED_TRIP`, `status` `ISSUED`, and `amount_cents` set to the contract's `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip`. Because that memo became `ISSUED`, the contract's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the contract's `version` increments by one. With `CLIENT_REQUEST`, the trip's `settlement_status` moves `UNBILLED` → `WAIVED`: no memo is minted, `credit_balance_cents` is unchanged, and — as §3.8 explains — the trip leaves both the on-time denominator and the missed-trip count entirely. With `WEATHER`, `settlement_status` is left exactly as it was; a weather-missed trip remains `UNBILLED` and still counts against the operator's on-time percentage.

Credits can also be raised by hand. A `ClientLiaison` issues a **credit memo** by supplying `contract_id`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, a `reason` of `MISSED_TRIP`, `WAIT_SLA_BREACH`, or `GOODWILL`, and optionally a `trip_id` naming the trip that caused it — a memo without a trip is a `GOODWILL` gesture that belongs to no service period. Where `trip_id` is supplied, that trip's `contract_id` must equal the `contract_id` in the body, failing which the memo is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. The amount is capped commercially: `amount_cents` must be at least 1 and at most three times the contract's `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip`, and an amount below 1 or above that ceiling is rejected with `ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP` and HTTP 422. A newly raised memo has `status` `ISSUED`, `applied_report_id` null and `voided_reason` null, recomputes the contract's `credit_balance_cents`, and increments the contract's `version` by one.

Voiding is the compensating action of every credit-issuing path, whether the memo was minted by a missed trip, by a wait-SLA breach, or raised by hand. A `ContractAdmin` supplies a `voided_reason`; the memo must currently be `ISSUED`, and a memo that is already `APPLIED` or `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_MEMO_NOT_ISSUED`. The memo moves to `VOIDED`, the contract's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed — returning, because it is a full recomputation, to exactly its pre-issue value — and the contract's `version` increments by one. When the voided memo names a trip and its `reason` is `MISSED_TRIP`, the void additionally walks the trip's financial axis back: `settlement_status` moves `CREDITED` → `UNBILLED` and the trip's `version` increments by one. A memo whose `reason` is `WAIT_SLA_BREACH` or `GOODWILL` does not move any trip's settlement status when voided.

### 3.8. Period Settlement: Submit, Dispute, Approve

Client reporting is organised into non-overlapping **service period reports**. Either a `ClientLiaison` or a `ContractAdmin` opens one by supplying `contract_id`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`. The start may not fall after the end, and both endpoints must lie inside the contract's closed term range; a violation of either condition is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM` and HTTP 422. The new period must also not overlap any existing report of the same contract — two closed date ranges overlap when the new start is on or before the existing end and the existing start is on or before the new end — and an overlap is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409. The report opens in `DRAFT` with `settlement_variant` `NONE`, `revision_round` at 0, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `dispute_reason` all null, `effective_rate_cents_per_service_hour` at 0, and every settlement figure at 0.

Submitting is permitted from `DRAFT` or from `DISPUTED`; from `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` it is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. Either a `ClientLiaison` or a `ContractAdmin` may submit. Submission freezes the commercial rate by copying the contract's current `base_rate_cents_per_service_hour` into the report's `effective_rate_cents_per_service_hour`, overwriting whatever a previous submission had frozen there; computes the entire settlement block using the **provisional** variant of the three settlement calculations below and sets `settlement_variant` to `PROVISIONAL`; stamps the caller's `X-User-ID` into `submitted_by_user_id`, again overwriting whatever a previous submission stored; and increments the report's `version` by one. When the submission arrives from `DISPUTED` rather than from `DRAFT`, it additionally increments `revision_round` by one — a report submitted, disputed and resubmitted therefore reads `revision_round` 1.

Disputing is the client's lever and belongs to the `ClientLiaison` alone. It is permitted only from `SUBMITTED`, and from anywhere else is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The payload carries `dispute_reason` as free text, which is stored; the report moves `SUBMITTED` → `DISPUTED`, `settlement_variant` is reset to `NONE`, and every settlement figure is zeroed — `scheduled_trip_count`, `completed_trip_count`, `missed_trip_count`, `waived_trip_count`, `countable_trip_count`, `on_time_trip_count`, `on_time_pct`, `total_boardings`, `total_ada_boardings`, `total_billable_minutes`, `billable_service_hours`, `gross_amount_cents`, `penalty_tier_pct`, `sla_penalty_cents`, `missed_trip_penalty_cents`, `penalty_amount_cents`, `credit_amount_cents` and `net_amount_cents` all return to 0 — so that the next submission is forced to recompute rather than inheriting stale figures. The report's `version` increments by one. The submit-dispute-submit loop may run as many laps as the parties need, and `revision_round` counts them.

Approval is a `ContractAdmin` action and is the report's terminal transition. It is permitted only from `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTED`); it requires the contract's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`); and it enforces the segregation rule of §2.10, rejecting an approver whose `X-User-ID` equals the report's `submitted_by_user_id` with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval recomputes the entire settlement block using the **final** variant and sets `settlement_variant` to `FINAL`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, and moves the report `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` with its `version` incremented by one. Two cascades follow. Once `credit_amount_cents` has been computed and stored, every credit memo of this contract whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose trip's `service_date` lies inside the period moves to `APPLIED` with `applied_report_id` set to this report; because those memos left `ISSUED`, the contract's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the contract's `version` increments by one. Separately, every trip in the final input set whose `settlement_status` is still `UNBILLED` moves `UNBILLED` → `BILLED`, each with its own `version` incremented by one — a trip qualifies when its `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED`, its `settlement_status` is `UNBILLED`, and its `service_date` lies inside the period's closed date range.

**Gross billing.** The billable population depends on the variant. The provisional set is every trip whose `contract_id` matches the report's, whose `service_date` lies inside the closed range from `period_start_date` to `period_end_date`, and whose `dispatch_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED` — it bills the schedule, so trips that were never assigned and never ran still count at their planned duration. The final set applies the same contract and date filter but admits only trips whose `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED` — it bills the operation. Both variants exclude `MISSED` and `CANCELLED` trips entirely. Each trip in the set contributes an integer count of billable minutes: under the provisional variant, its `scheduled_arrival_min` minus its `scheduled_departure_min`; under the final variant, its `actual_completion_min` minus its `actual_departure_min`. Those per-trip minutes are never rounded. They are summed into `total_billable_minutes`, and only then, exactly once, is a rounding applied: `billable_service_hours` is the period total divided by 60 and rounded half up to two decimal places. `gross_amount_cents` is `billable_service_hours` multiplied by the report's frozen `effective_rate_cents_per_service_hour`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The order is pinned: sum first, then round. Rounding each trip's hours to two decimals and then summing is explicitly not this system's rule and produces different cents.

**Service-level penalty.** The on-time population is deliberately variant-independent — it is identical under both variants, so only the gross amount moves the penalty between them. The countable population is every trip of the contract whose `service_date` lies inside the closed period range, whose `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED` or `MISSED`, and whose `settlement_status` is anything other than `WAIVED`; its size is `countable_trip_count`. Within that population, `on_time_trip_count` counts the trips that are `COMPLETED` and whose lateness — `actual_departure_min` minus `scheduled_departure_min` — is at most five minutes; a departure delay of exactly five minutes is on time, and six minutes is late. `on_time_pct` is 100.00 when `countable_trip_count` is 0, and otherwise is 100 multiplied by `on_time_trip_count`, divided by `countable_trip_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The shortfall against the promise is the contract's `sla_on_time_threshold_pct` minus `on_time_pct`, and it selects `penalty_tier_pct` from four bands: 0 when the shortfall is zero or negative, 2 when the shortfall is greater than zero and at most 5.00 percentage points, 5 when the shortfall is greater than 5.00 and at most 15.00 percentage points, and 10 when the shortfall exceeds 15.00 percentage points. A shortfall of exactly zero therefore attracts no penalty at all. `sla_penalty_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` multiplied by `penalty_tier_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Separately, `missed_trip_count` counts the period's trips whose `dispatch_status` is `MISSED` and whose `settlement_status` is not `WAIVED`, while `waived_trip_count` counts those whose `dispatch_status` is `MISSED` and whose `settlement_status` is `WAIVED`. `missed_trip_penalty_cents` is `missed_trip_count` multiplied by the contract's `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip`, and `penalty_amount_cents` is `sla_penalty_cents` plus `missed_trip_penalty_cents`.

**Net settlement.** Evaluation order is pinned and single-pass: the gross amount first, then the penalty amount which reads the gross, then the credit amount which reads neither, and finally the net which reads all three. The floor applied at the last step is never fed back into the earlier steps, which is what makes the apparent circularity — a net that depends on a penalty, a penalty that depends on a gross, and a zero net that would otherwise re-open the tier choice — well defined. `credit_amount_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every credit memo of this contract whose `status` is `ISSUED`, whose `trip_id` is not null, and whose trip's `service_date` falls inside the closed period range; `GOODWILL` memos carrying a null `trip_id` never belong to a period and are therefore never settled by an approval. `net_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` minus `penalty_amount_cents` minus `credit_amount_cents`, clamped at a floor of exactly 0 so it is never negative. Alongside the money, the report records ridership and counts: `total_boardings` is the sum of `counted_boardings` over the settlement input set, `total_ada_boardings` the sum of `counted_ada_boardings` over the same set, `scheduled_trip_count` the size of that set, and `completed_trip_count` the number of the period's trips whose `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED`. Under the provisional variant the qualifying memos are read but left `ISSUED`; under the final variant they are flipped to `APPLIED`, and only after `net_amount_cents` has been stored. For identical underlying data the two variants therefore report different `total_billable_minutes`, different `billable_service_hours`, different `gross_amount_cents` and different `net_amount_cents`, which is the whole point of provisional versus final settlement.

### 3.9. Renewal Negotiation and Rate Escalation

As a contract nears the end of its term, the operator opens a **renewal offer**. A `ContractAdmin` supplies `contract_id`, a `proposed_term_months` between 1 and 60 whole months, and `expected_contract_version`. The renewal window is the sixty days that close on the contract's `term_end_date`: the logical clock's current UTC date must lie inside the closed range that begins sixty days before `term_end_date` and ends on `term_end_date`, and the opening day itself passes. A clock sitting outside that window is rejected with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` and HTTP 422. The version guard of §2.8 applies here too: a mismatched `expected_contract_version` fails with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before the contract or any standing offer is touched.

Opening an offer against a contract that is `ACTIVE` moves it `ACTIVE` → `RENEWAL_PENDING`. Opening a further offer against a contract already in `RENEWAL_PENDING` leaves it in `RENEWAL_PENDING`, and in either case any standing offer for that contract whose `status` is `OPEN` or `COUNTERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — a terminal state — with its own `version` incremented by one. The contract's `version` increments by one on every offer creation. The new offer is born `OPEN` with `round_index` at 1, `counter_rate_cents_per_service_hour`, `agreed_rate_cents_per_service_hour` and `successor_contract_id` all null.

The proposed rate is derived at creation time from the contract's own escalation index adjusted by the client's recent performance, and the performance signal is read from a **frozen** report rather than a live query. The basis is the single service period report of this contract whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `period_end_date` is the greatest, with ties broken by the greatest `created_at`; reports that are still `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` are invisible to this calculation. `basis_report_id` records the chosen report, or null when the contract has no approved report at all.

Two adjustments are computed from that basis. `volume_pct` rewards ridership: it is 0.0 when there is no basis report, 0.0 when the basis report's `total_boardings` is below 2,000, 1.5 when `total_boardings` is at least 2,000 but below 5,000, and 3.0 when `total_boardings` is 5,000 or more. `clawback_pct` punishes underperformance: it is 0.0 when there is no basis report, 2.0 when the basis report's `on_time_pct` is strictly below the contract's `sla_on_time_threshold_pct`, and 0.0 otherwise. The raw escalation is the contract's `escalation_index_pct` plus `volume_pct` minus `clawback_pct`. That raw figure is then clamped into the band from 0.0 to 6.0 inclusive — anything below 0.0 becomes 0.0 and anything above 6.0 becomes 6.0 — and the clamped result is stored as `applied_escalation_pct`. Finally, `proposed_rate_cents_per_service_hour` is the contract's `base_rate_cents_per_service_hour` multiplied by one plus `applied_escalation_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Because the escalation is keyed on a frozen approved report, whose own `effective_rate_cents_per_service_hour` was itself snapshotted at submission time, the new rate never feeds back into the basis report and the apparent circularity of "rate depends on report, report depends on rate" is broken at the freeze.

The client answers through a counter. A `ClientLiaison` supplies `counter_rate_cents_per_service_hour`, which must sit between the contract's current `base_rate_cents_per_service_hour` and the offer's `proposed_rate_cents_per_service_hour` inclusive at both ends; a value below the base rate or above the proposed rate is rejected with `ERR_COUNTER_RATE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` and HTTP 422. Countering is permitted only while the offer is `OPEN`, and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`; the contract must be in `RENEWAL_PENDING`. The counter rate is stored, the offer moves `OPEN` → `COUNTERED`, and its `version` increments by one.

The operator then responds, and this is where the negotiation either loops or ends. A `ContractAdmin` supplies a single `decision`, one of `REOPEN`, `ACCEPT`, or `DECLINE`. Responding is permitted only while the offer is `COUNTERED`, and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_COUNTERED`.

A `REOPEN` decision is the return leg of the negotiation cycle: the offer moves `COUNTERED` → `OPEN`, `round_index` increments by one, `counter_rate_cents_per_service_hour` is cleared, and the offer's `version` increments by one. The contract stays in `RENEWAL_PENDING`, and the client may counter again — each lap advancing `round_index`.

A `DECLINE` decision requires the contract to be in `RENEWAL_PENDING`. The offer moves `COUNTERED` → `DECLINED`, terminal, and the contract cycles back `RENEWAL_PENDING` → `ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented by one; the offer's `version` also increments by one. The contract may be offered again later inside the same renewal window.

An `ACCEPT` decision likewise requires the contract to be in `RENEWAL_PENDING`, and it fires a three-link renewal cascade. First, the offer moves `COUNTERED` → `ACCEPTED`, terminal, and `agreed_rate_cents_per_service_hour` is set to the offer's `counter_rate_cents_per_service_hour`. Second, because the offer became `ACCEPTED`, the contract moves `RENEWAL_PENDING` → `RENEWED`, which is terminal, with its `version` incremented by one. Third, because the contract became `RENEWED`, a successor contract is minted in `DRAFT`: its `predecessor_contract_id` is the id of the contract just renewed, its `base_rate_cents_per_service_hour` is the offer's `agreed_rate_cents_per_service_hour`, its `term_start_date` is the day after the renewed contract's `term_end_date`, and its `term_end_date` is that new start date advanced by `proposed_term_months` whole months and then pulled back by one day — a twelve-month renewal starting on 2027-01-01 therefore ends on 2027-12-31. Every other input field — `client_name`, `site_type`, `escalation_index_pct`, `sla_on_time_threshold_pct`, `penalty_rate_cents_per_missed_trip` and `ada_min_accessible_pct` — is copied from the renewed contract, and the successor starts with `credit_balance_cents`, `branded_vehicle_count` and `branded_ada_vehicle_count` at 0. The offer's `successor_contract_id` is written back with the new contract's id, and the offer's `version` increments by one. Because the successor is `DRAFT`, it must be activated before anything can be published against it.
