# Fountain Beverage Service Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A bottler that places self-serve fountain equipment into restaurants, cinemas and convenience stores carries an unusual mix of obligations at once. The equipment is frequently placed free-on-loan against a volume commitment, so the bottler is simultaneously a landlord of hardware, a syrup wholesaler, a preventive-maintenance contractor and a compliance auditor for water quality and sanitation. Today those four concerns live in four disconnected spreadsheets: the contract team tracks committed gallons, the demand planner guesses cartridge consumption from last month's invoices, the service dispatcher phones vans to find out whether a pump assembly is still on board, and the quality team discovers an overdue sanitation cycle only when a customer complains about off-ratio product.

This document specifies the **Fountain Beverage Service Network API**, a single back-office service that unifies those workstreams behind one contract. Every collection is mounted under the top-level path `/fountain-network`. The service holds the outlet master data and its equipment placements, ingests pour-volume telemetry from each dispenser, forecasts cartridge and bag-in-box demand per outlet, allocates a finite national supply pool onto replenishment routes, schedules and grades preventive maintenance including brix/ratio calibration and water-filter changes, dispatches service calls against a van's rolling parts inventory, and finally settles volume shortfalls against the placement agreement — either as a per-gallon fee or as an equipment recall.

Two properties dominate the design and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **compliance gates commerce**: an outlet under a sanitation hold or carrying overdue maintenance is progressively cut off from deliveries, and committed supply is handed back to the national pool rather than stranded on a van. Second, **quantities are conserved**: units of consumable and units of service part move between named buckets and are never minted or destroyed, so every ledger in the system can be reconciled arithmetically at any instant.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Access Control

Every request to the API — without exception, including reads and including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This is the very first check performed on any request.

The six roles are `NetworkAdmin`, `SupplyPlanner`, `RouteDriver`, `ServiceTechnician`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `OutletManager`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `NetworkAdmin` is the bottler's operations administrator. It masters outlets, placement agreements, dispenser equipment, consumable SKUs and van loadouts, and it runs the volume-shortfall assessment.
- A `SupplyPlanner` is the demand planner. It opens stock positions, runs consumption forecasts, allocates the national pool and plans, dispatches, completes and cancels replenishment routes.
- A `RouteDriver` executes a dispatched route stop by stop: arriving, serving and skipping.
- A `ServiceTechnician` works service calls off a van and completes maintenance and calibration tasks; it also places and releases equipment service holds.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` owns sanitation compliance holds, the preventive-maintenance schedule, due evaluation, waivers and the recall decision.
- An `OutletManager` manages exactly one customer outlet and may act only on that outlet's own resources.

Once authentication succeeds, the second check is authorization: if the acting `X-User-Role` is not among the roles listed for the target endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to data.** For five of the six roles the identifier is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `SupplyPlanner`, `RouteDriver`, `ServiceTechnician` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever an audit or segregation-of-duties rule requires a name — as `last_hold_placed_by_user_id` on an outlet, as `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id` and `recall_decided_by_user_id` on a placement agreement, as `completed_by_user_id` and `waived_by_user_id` on a maintenance task, and as `assigned_tech_user_id` on a service call — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The sixth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `OutletManager`, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `Outlet`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an outlet therefore compare that resource's outlet reference to `X-User-ID`: the outlet itself for the brand-mix report, the `outlet_id` of a stock position when acknowledging an alert, and the outlet of the service call's dispenser unit when opening or cancelling a service call. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_OUTLET_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` and HTTP 403. The manager's own identifier is also what gets stored in `alert_acknowledged_by_user_id` when an alert is acknowledged.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` below 1 or above 100 is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, delivery window, promotional window, telemetry window and reporting period in this system is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**, never against wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real time under any circumstance.

An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock: the caller supplies the target instant as `to_iso8601` and the server responds with the new instant in a body of the shape `{ current_utc: datetime }`. Setting the clock mutates no entity — no task is re-evaluated, no forecast is refreshed, no route is skipped as a side effect. What changes is the answer that every subsequent due-evaluation, forecast run, allocation, dispatch, term closure and report gives. This capability is open to `NetworkAdmin`, `SupplyPlanner`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ServiceTechnician` and `RouteDriver`; an `OutletManager` may not move the clock.

Timestamps that the server writes itself — `completed_at` on a maintenance task, for instance — are taken from the logical clock at the moment of the write.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply it.

Ten of the eleven resource kinds — outlets, placement agreements, dispenser units, supply items, outlet supply positions, replenishment routes, route stops, maintenance tasks, service calls and van part stocks — additionally carry the full audit triple:

- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the resource was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of the most recent modification of the resource.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource.

Telemetry readings are the single exception. A telemetry reading is an immutable event: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. A reading is never edited, never deleted and never superseded.

The `version` counter is not decorative. Whenever a rule in this document says that a derived field is rewritten — an outlet's `service_posture`, a supply item's `reserved_pool_units`, a route's `committed_units` — that rewrite is a mutation of the owning resource: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by one. When one request mutates several resources, each mutated resource independently advances its own `version` by one; the counters of untouched resources stand still.

### 2.6. Request Validation Order

Because most operations in this network are guarded by several rules at once, the order in which those rules fire is part of the contract: a caller who violates two rules must receive a predictable error. Checks are evaluated in the following priority order, and the first failure terminates the request. Nothing is persisted when a request fails at any rung of this ladder.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's role list, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body parses, every required input field is present, every enum-valued field carries a declared member, numeric fields respect their declared ranges (no negative units, ounces, gallons or capacities), and any supplied `limit` lies between 1 and 100 inclusive. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the request path must resolve to an existing row, validated strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides the error. The 404 code is the one belonging to the missing resource kind: `ERR_OUTLET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPENSER_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MAINTENANCE_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VAN_PART_STOCK_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TELEMETRY_READING_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that carries a path identifier.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign reference named in the body — `outlet_id`, `agreement_id`, `dispenser_unit_id`, `supply_item_id`, and every member of `supply_position_ids` — must resolve, otherwise the corresponding 404 code above is returned. This rung applies to agreement creation, dispenser-unit creation, telemetry ingestion, supply-position creation, route-stop creation, stop allocation, maintenance-task creation and service-call creation.
6. **Outlet ownership.** When the acting role is `OutletManager`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `id` of the outlet the target resource resolves to, otherwise `ERR_OUTLET_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (403). This rung guards exactly four operations: `GET /fountain-network/outlets/{id}/brand-mix-report`, `POST /fountain-network/supply-positions/{id}/acknowledge-alert`, `POST /fountain-network/service-calls` and `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/cancel`.
7. **Outlet stale-version guard.** `expected_outlet_version` must equal the stored `version` of the outlet, otherwise `ERR_STALE_OUTLET_VERSION` (412). This rung guards `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements`.
8. **Route stale-version guard.** `expected_route_version` must equal the stored `version` of the replenishment route, otherwise `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` (412). This rung guards `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops`, `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/cancel`.
9. **State legality.** The target resource must currently sit in a state from which the requested transition is declared in §3. Every unmatched precondition that is expressed as a state or counter condition on the target — the outlet must be `ACTIVE` to take a compliance hold or an equipment placement decision, an outlet may hold only one open compliance hold at a time, a reinstated outlet must have no open hold, an outlet may only be closed once every one of its dispensers is `RETURNED`, an agreement must sit in the declared from-state, an agreement may only be activated once at least one dispenser references it, a route must be `DISPATCHED` before one of its stops can be arrived at, a route must have at least one stop and its committed units must not exceed its capacity before dispatch, a route may only be completed once every stop is terminal, and a service call may only be dispatched from `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` — is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
10. **Serviceability of the unit.** A service call may only be opened or dispatched while its dispenser unit's `status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `SERVICE_HOLD`; a `RECALL_STAGED` or `RETURNED` unit yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_SERVICEABLE` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls` and `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/dispatch`.
11. **Dispatch holder.** Arriving on site, resolving and escalating must be performed by the technician holding the current dispatch, that is `X-User-ID` equal to `assigned_tech_user_id`; otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` (403). Guards `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/arrive-on-site`, `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/resolve` and `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/escalate`.
12. **Escalation ceiling.** A call whose `escalation_count` has already reached 2 may not be escalated again: `ERR_ESCALATION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/escalate`.
13. **Frozen posture.** The outlet's `service_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_OUTLET_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409). This rung guards two operations: `POST /fountain-network/route-stops/{id}/allocations` and `POST /fountain-network/route-stops/{id}/serve`.
14. **Calibration spec.** No dispenser unit at the stop's outlet may carry `calibration_status` equal to `OUT_OF_SPEC`, otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_OUT_OF_SPEC` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/route-stops/{id}/serve`.
15. **Sanitation.** No maintenance task of the unit whose `task_type` is `SANITATION` may be in `OVERDUE`, otherwise `ERR_SANITATION_OVERDUE` (409), on the `RELEASE` action of `POST /fountain-network/dispenser-units/{id}/service-hold`.
16. **Assessment/decision separation.** The officer recording the recall decision must not be the administrator who ran the assessment, that is `X-User-ID` must differ from `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id`; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements/{id}/recall-decision`.
17. **Hold/waiver separation.** The officer waiving a maintenance task must not be the officer recorded in the outlet's `last_hold_placed_by_user_id`; otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/maintenance-tasks/{id}/waive`.
18. **Placement coherence.** The submitted `outlet_id` must equal the `outlet_id` of the submitted `agreement_id`, otherwise `ERR_OUTLET_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/dispenser-units`.
19. **Region coherence.** The stop outlet's `region_code` must equal the route's `depot_region_code`, otherwise `ERR_REGION_MISMATCH` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops`.
20. **No future events.** `reading_at` on a telemetry reading and `reported_at` on a service call must be at or before the logical clock; a later instant yields `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/telemetry-readings` and `POST /fountain-network/service-calls`.
21. **Well-ordered intervals.** Every submitted interval must have its end bound strictly after its start bound: `term_end_date` after `term_start_date`, `window_end` after `window_start`, `scheduled_window_end` after `scheduled_window_start`, `promo_window_end` after `promo_window_start`, and `period_end_date` after `period_start_date`. A violation yields `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). This rung guards `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements`, `POST /fountain-network/supply-items`, `PATCH /fountain-network/supply-items/{id}`, `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops`, `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements/{id}/assess-shortfall`.
22. **Term elapsed.** The logical clock must be at or past the agreement's `term_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements/{id}/close-term`.
23. **Stop window clash.** The new delivery window must not overlap another `PENDING` or `ARRIVED` stop of the same route under the half-open, zero-buffer rule of §2.9, otherwise `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops`.
24. **Van window clash.** The requested van window must not collide with another `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE` call on the same van under the closed-interval, 30-minute-buffer rule of §2.9, otherwise `ERR_VAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/dispatch`.
25. **Part on the van.** When `part_sku` is supplied, a van stock row must exist for that van and that part, otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_ON_VAN` (404) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/dispatch`.
26. **Part availability.** The selected van stock row must hold at least as many `on_van_units` as the requested `part_units`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VAN_PARTS` (409) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/dispatch`.
27. **Consumption within reservation.** `consumed_part_units` must not exceed the call's `reserved_part_units`, otherwise `ERR_CONSUMED_EXCEEDS_RESERVED` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/service-calls/{id}/resolve`.
28. **Gallon interval applicability.** On `POST /fountain-network/maintenance-tasks`, `gallon_interval` must be present when `task_type` is `FILTER_CHANGE` — its absence is `ERR_GALLON_INTERVAL_REQUIRED` (422) — and must be absent for every other `task_type`, its presence being `ERR_GALLON_INTERVAL_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422).
29. **Brix measurement.** Completing a `BRIX_CALIBRATION` task without `measured_brix_ratio` is `ERR_BRIX_MEASUREMENT_REQUIRED` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/maintenance-tasks/{id}/complete`.
30. **Uniqueness.** An outlet's `account_code`, a dispenser's `serial_number`, a supply item's `sku_code`, a route's `route_code`, the outlet-and-SKU pair behind a supply position, the van-and-part pair behind a van stock row, and a stop's `sequence_index` within its route are each unique. Collisions return `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCOUNT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROUTE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPLY_POSITION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VAN_PART` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SEQUENCE` respectively, all with HTTP 409. This rung guards `POST /fountain-network/outlets`, `POST /fountain-network/dispenser-units`, `POST /fountain-network/supply-items`, `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes`, `POST /fountain-network/supply-positions`, `POST /fountain-network/van-part-stocks` and `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops`.
31. **Allocation eligibility.** At least one submitted supply position must survive the eligibility filter of the allocation waterfall, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_POSITIONS` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/route-stops/{id}/allocations`.
32. **Pool sufficiency.** For each eligible position, the SKU's unreserved pool — its `national_allocation_units` less its `reserved_pool_units` — must be at least one `case_pack_units`, otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (409) on the same endpoint.
33. **Capacity sufficiency.** For each eligible position, the route's remaining capacity — its `capacity_units` less its `committed_units` — must be at least one `case_pack_units`, otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) on the same endpoint.
34. **Something to assess.** The shortfall assessment must yield a fee greater than zero cents, otherwise `ERR_NO_SHORTFALL_TO_ASSESS` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements/{id}/assess-shortfall`.
35. **Recall permissibility.** A `RECALL` outcome requires the agreement's `free_on_loan` to be true, otherwise `ERR_RECALL_NOT_PERMITTED` (422) on `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements/{id}/recall-decision`.
36. **Recall exposure floor.** A `RECALL` outcome requires `recall_exposure_cents` to be at least 450000, otherwise `ERR_RECALL_EXPOSURE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422) on the same endpoint.

One further guard sits outside this ladder because it belongs to a single field edit: lowering a supply item's `national_allocation_units` below its current `reserved_pool_units` is refused with `ERR_POOL_BELOW_RESERVED` (422).

### 2.7. Quantities, Precision and Unit Conversions

Three conversion constants recur throughout the network and are stated once here.

- **Ounces to gallons.** One gallon of finished beverage is 128 fluid ounces. Telemetry is reported in ounces; commitments and equipment counters are expressed in gallons.
- **Months in service.** For equipment-age purposes, one month is 30 days, and only whole months count: the number of months a dispenser has been in service is the elapsed time since its `installed_at` divided by 30 days and then truncated downward to a whole number.
- **A contract year holds four calendar quarters**, namely January through March, April through June, July through September, and October through December.

Rounding is never left to chance. Wherever a stored quantity is described as rounded to two decimal places — `forecast_units`, `assessed_shortfall_gallons`, `lifetime_dispensed_gallons`, `effective_gallon_interval` — the rounding method is round half up, meaning a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Cent amounts (`assessed_fee_cents`, `recall_exposure_cents`, `shortfall_fee_cents_per_gallon`, `equipment_value_cents`) are whole integers of cents, also rounded half up where a rounding step is prescribed. Unit counts (`on_hand_units`, `requested_units`, `allocated_units`, `committed_units`, `delivered_units`, `on_van_units`, `reserved_units`, `consumed_units`, `initial_units`, `restocked_units`, `reserved_part_units`, `consumed_part_units`, `part_units`, `national_allocation_units`, `reserved_pool_units`, `case_pack_units`, `capacity_units`) are always integers; where a fractional requirement must become an integer count of units to order, it is rounded **upward** to the next whole unit, and where it must become a whole number of shippable cases it is truncated **downward**. Percentage shares in the brand-mix report are carried to one decimal place. Severity scores, escalation counts and both outlet counters are integers throughout.

Two rounding orders are pinned deliberately and must not be rearranged. The shortfall fee rounds **per quarter first and sums the rounded cents afterwards** (§3.9). The equipment exposure rounds **per dispenser unit first and sums the rounded cents afterwards** (§3.9). Any implementation that sums unrounded intermediates and rounds once at the end will produce a different total whenever two or more components carry a half-cent residue, and is wrong.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Contract and Route Writes

Two write paths in this network can silently destroy work that the client never observed, and both are therefore protected by an explicit optimistic-concurrency field. The mechanism is a comparison of a client-supplied expected version against the stored `version` of a parent resource, evaluated on one request at a time; it is not a lock, and it is not about requests arriving at the same moment.

**Agreement creation against an outlet.** `POST /fountain-network/placement-agreements` requires `expected_outlet_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the `version` of the target outlet as the client last read it. Creating a new agreement legitimately supersedes any agreement of the same outlet that is still in `DRAFT` — that older agreement is moved to `EXPIRED` — so a writer holding an outdated view of the outlet would discard paperwork it never saw. If `expected_outlet_version` does not equal the outlet's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OUTLET_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no agreement is created.

**Stop creation, dispatch and cancellation against a route.** `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/stops` requires `expected_route_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the route's `version` as last read by the client. Adding a stop is legal from `PLANNED` no matter how many stops already exist, so no state precondition can catch a writer whose window plan was computed against an earlier stop set; the version comparison is the only signal that the route's stop list has moved on. A mismatch against the route's current stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no stop is created. The same mandatory `expected_route_version` field and the same `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` rejection at HTTP 412 apply to `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /fountain-network/replenishment-routes/{id}/cancel`.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. In particular, route completion, stop allocation, stop arrival, stop service and stop skipping take no expected-version input.

### 2.9. Interval Arithmetic: Two Deliberately Different Rules

The network reasons about two kinds of time window, and they do **not** use the same arithmetic. Confusing them is the most common integration mistake.

**Delivery windows on a route are half-open with no buffer.** A stop's window runs from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive. A newly proposed stop conflicts with an existing stop of the same route when that existing stop is in `PENDING` or `ARRIVED` status and, simultaneously, the existing stop's start lies strictly before the new stop's end and the new stop's start lies strictly before the existing stop's end. Because both comparisons are strict, two windows that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — never conflict. Stops already in `SERVED` or `SKIPPED` no longer occupy the van's day and are ignored by this test. A conflict is `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

**Van service windows are closed and padded on both sides by a 30-minute travel buffer.** A dispatch proposes `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end`, and both bounds are inclusive. The test first widens the proposed window by 30 minutes at each end: the padded lower bound is 30 minutes before the proposed start and the padded upper bound is 30 minutes after the proposed end. The dispatch then conflicts with any other service call — excluding the call being dispatched itself — that names the same `van_code`, sits in `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE` status, and whose own `scheduled_window_start` is at or before the padded upper bound while the padded lower bound is at or before that call's `scheduled_window_end`. Both comparisons are non-strict. The practical consequence is worth spelling out: a window that starts exactly 30 minutes after another one ends **does** conflict, while a window starting 31 minutes after does not. Calls in `OPEN`, `ESCALATED`, `RESOLVED` or `CANCELLED` hold no van time and are ignored. A conflict is `ERR_VAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Telemetry and reporting windows follow the delivery-window convention: they are half-open, anchored on the logical clock, and include their earlier bound while excluding the logical clock instant itself. Promotional availability windows are likewise half-open: a SKU is available when the logical clock is at or after `promo_window_start` and strictly before `promo_window_end`. An agreement's term is half-open in the same way, with `term_start_date` inclusive and `term_end_date` exclusive. Assessment periods submitted as `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` are half-open too.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Compliance Propagation

Nothing in this network is allowed to be half-consistent. A single operator action routinely walks several hops of consequence, and every hop is mandatory, immediate and part of the same request.

The canonical cascade is the **posture-to-delivery** chain. An outlet's `service_posture` is a derived compliance gate, never written directly by a client. It is recomputed from the outlet's own two counters — `open_compliance_hold_count` and `overdue_task_count` — immediately after either counter changes, by a rule evaluated top to bottom in which the first matching clause wins: if the outlet has at least one open compliance hold **or** at least three overdue maintenance tasks the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if it has at least one overdue maintenance task the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Because the frozen clause is tested first, an outlet under a compliance hold is `FROZEN` even when it has zero overdue tasks.

When that recomputation lands on `FROZEN`, the chain continues: every route stop of that outlet still in `PENDING` becomes `SKIPPED`, each such skip releases the consumable units committed to it, and each release adjusts the SKU's reserved pool and the route's committed total. That is three hops deep from a single hold placement, and all three land before the response is returned.

Releases are always the same four movements, applied to every outlet supply position whose `planned_route_stop_id` points at the stop being released: the position's `allocated_units` returns to 0, its `planned_route_stop_id` returns to null, the referenced supply item's `reserved_pool_units` is reduced by the released amount, and the owning route's `committed_units` is reduced by the released amount.

The reverse-direction propagation matters just as much. A maintenance task that enters `OVERDUE` raises its outlet's `overdue_task_count` by one and immediately triggers the posture recomputation; a task that leaves `OVERDUE` for any reason — completed, waived by an officer, or waived automatically because the equipment was returned — lowers that counter by one and triggers the same recomputation. Equipment lifecycle propagates upward as well: when the last dispenser of an agreement is confirmed returned, the agreement terminates, and if that leaves the outlet with no agreement in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SHORTFALL_REVIEW` or `RECALL_PENDING`, the outlet itself is suspended, which again skips its pending stops and releases their allocations — four hops from one confirmation.

Every resource touched anywhere along a cascade is a mutated resource: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by one, exactly once per request.

### 2.11. Conserved Ledgers

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and the conservation identities below must hold after **every** operation that touches them — not merely at the end of a happy path.

**The national consumable pool.** For every supply item, `reserved_pool_units` equals the sum of `allocated_units` across all outlet supply positions that reference that item. Allocation raises both the item's `reserved_pool_units` and the route's `committed_units` by the granted amount. A release lowers both by the released amount and returns the position to zero allocated units with a null stop link. A delivery is the asymmetric case and the asymmetry is intentional: serving a stop moves the allocated units into the position's `on_hand_units` and lowers the item's `reserved_pool_units`, but it does **not** lower the route's `committed_units`, because that commitment was consumed by the delivery rather than returned to the pool. A supply item's `national_allocation_units` is never changed by allocation or release; across a full allocate-then-release cycle its `reserved_pool_units` returns to exactly its pre-allocation value, which holds precisely because grants are always whole multiples of `case_pack_units`.

**The van parts inventory.** For every van part stock row, the sum of `on_van_units`, `reserved_units` and `consumed_units` equals the sum of `initial_units` and `restocked_units`. Units move between the three live buckets and are never minted or destroyed: a dispatch reservation moves units from `on_van_units` into `reserved_units`; an escalation or a cancellation moves the whole held reservation back from `reserved_units` into `on_van_units`; a resolution clears the reservation, credits the fitted quantity to `consumed_units` and returns the unfitted remainder to `on_van_units`; and a restock raises `on_van_units` and `restocked_units` together, which is the only operation that raises the conserved total. `initial_units` is fixed at creation from the units first loaded onto the van and never changes thereafter.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this network are too consequential to be made by the person who set them up, so the API enforces separation on `X-User-ID`.

The first separation is between assessment and consequence. The `NetworkAdmin` who runs a volume-shortfall assessment has their identifier recorded in `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id`. The `ComplianceOfficer` who later records the recall decision has their identifier recorded in `recall_decided_by_user_id`, and their `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored assessor identifier. An attempt by the same identifier to do both is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The second separation is between raising a compliance concern and forgiving it. When a `ComplianceOfficer` places a compliance hold on an outlet, their identifier is captured in `last_hold_placed_by_user_id`, and — importantly — releasing the hold does **not** clear that field; it survives as an audit trail precisely so this check can still be made afterwards. A `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the outlet's stored `last_hold_placed_by_user_id` may not waive any maintenance task belonging to that outlet's equipment; the attempt is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Outlet and Governing Its Commercial Standing

The **Outlet** is the customer location that hosts the dispensers, and it is the root of almost every other record in the network. A `NetworkAdmin` registers one by supplying its trading `name`, an operator-assigned `account_code` and the `region_code` of the depot that will serve it. Two further inputs are optional and both carry defaults that materially affect later arithmetic. `throughput_tier` is the volume band used by the projected forecast variant and must be one of `SMALL`, `MEDIUM` or `LARGE`; when it is omitted the outlet is recorded as `MEDIUM`. `water_hardness_gpg` is the feed-water hardness in grains per gallon, accepted from 0 through 60 inclusive, and defaults to 10 when omitted — a value that is not arbitrary, since 10 grains per gallon is exactly the baseline at which water-filter intervals are left untouched.

A freshly registered outlet opens for business immediately: its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its derived `service_posture` is `OPEN`, both of its compliance counters — `open_compliance_hold_count` and `overdue_task_count` — stand at 0, its `last_hold_placed_by_user_id` is null, and its `version` is 1. Registration answers with 201. The `account_code` must not already belong to another outlet; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCOUNT_CODE`. Reading a single outlet returns its counters and derived posture alongside its input fields, and the outlet list can be narrowed by `region_code`, by `status` and by `service_posture` in addition to the usual `limit` and `offset`.

Commercial standing moves along three named actions, all reserved to a `NetworkAdmin` and all submitted as an `action` value with an optional `reason_code` for the audit trail. `SUSPEND` drives the outlet `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. `REINSTATE` drives it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, and is legal only while no compliance hold is open — a suspended outlet that still carries an open hold cannot be reinstated, and the attempt is refused as an illegal transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `CLOSE` drives the outlet to `CLOSED` from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and it is permitted only once every dispenser unit at that outlet has reached `RETURNED`; `CLOSED` is terminal and nothing leaves it. Each of these three actions increments the outlet's `version` by one.

Suspension is not a quiet flag. Whenever an outlet moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, every route stop of that outlet still sitting in `PENDING` **or** `ARRIVED` is driven to `SKIPPED` and stamped with `skip_reason_code` `OUTLET_CLOSED`, and every one of those skipped stops releases the consumable it was holding exactly as described in §2.10. Reinstatement is deliberately asymmetric: moving `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` recomputes the posture and bumps the version, but it does **not** resurrect the stops that were skipped, and it does not re-reserve the units that were released. Suspension can also arrive without an operator: when the last live placement agreement of an outlet terminates, leaving no agreement of that outlet in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SHORTFALL_REVIEW` or `RECALL_PENDING`, the server itself drives the outlet `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, applying precisely the same stop-skip and allocation-release cascade and the same single version increment as the operator action.

The **sanitation compliance hold** is owned exclusively by a `ComplianceOfficer` and is submitted as an `action` of either `PLACE` or `RELEASE` together with a mandatory `hold_reason_code`. Both actions leave the outlet's `status` at `ACTIVE` — the hold is a compliance dimension, not a commercial one — and both require the outlet to be `ACTIVE` to begin with. `PLACE` additionally requires that no hold is currently open, that is `open_compliance_hold_count` standing at 0; placing a second hold on top of an open one is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Placing sets `open_compliance_hold_count` to 1, records the acting `X-User-ID` in `last_hold_placed_by_user_id`, recomputes `service_posture`, which necessarily lands on `FROZEN`, drives the outlet's `PENDING` stops to `SKIPPED` with `skip_reason_code` `POSTURE_FROZEN`, releases each of those stops' allocations back to the pool and the route, and increments the outlet's `version` by one. `RELEASE` requires `open_compliance_hold_count` to be exactly 1, returns it to 0, **retains** `last_hold_placed_by_user_id` at its current value as the audit trail that powers the waiver separation of §2.12, recomputes `service_posture` — which will settle on `FROZEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN` purely according to how many overdue tasks the outlet still carries — and increments `version` by one. Releasing a hold does not undo any skip and does not restore any released allocation.

### 3.2. Placement Agreements: Drafting, Activation and Term Closure

Equipment is never placed casually; it is placed under a **PlacementAgreement**, the contract that fixes how much finished beverage the outlet promises to pour and what happens if it does not. A `NetworkAdmin` drafts one against a payload naming the `outlet_id`, the `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` bounding the contract term, the `annual_commitment_gallons` the outlet promises per contract year, and the `equipment_value_cents` representing the undepreciated placement value in cents of **one** dispenser unit under this agreement. `annual_commitment_gallons` must be greater than zero and `equipment_value_cents` must be greater than zero. Two commercial terms are optional: `free_on_loan`, which defaults to true and states that the equipment is on loan and may therefore be recalled if volume disappoints, and `shortfall_fee_cents_per_gallon`, which defaults to 50 and must be zero or greater. Drafting also demands `expected_outlet_version` and is guarded by the stale-outlet rule of §2.8. Creation answers with 201, the agreement's `status` is `DRAFT` and its `version` is 1; its assessment outputs start empty, with `assessed_shortfall_gallons` and `assessed_fee_cents` at 0, `recall_exposure_cents` at 0, `exposure_variant` recorded as `BOOK_VALUE`, `assessment_period_start` and `assessment_period_end` null, and both `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id` and `recall_decided_by_user_id` null.

Drafting has one further consequence that is easy to miss and is the entire reason the write is version-guarded: any **other** agreement of the same outlet that is still in `DRAFT` is superseded by the new one and moves `DRAFT → EXPIRED`, with its own `version` advancing by one. The outlet's `version` is also incremented by one by the creation. An outlet therefore never carries two competing drafts, and a client working from a stale outlet view can never quietly discard a draft it has not seen.

Activation moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is available to a `NetworkAdmin` with no request body. It requires three things at once: the agreement must actually be in `DRAFT`, the owning outlet's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and at least one dispenser unit must already reference this agreement. An agreement with no equipment recorded against it cannot be activated, and the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Activation increments the agreement's `version` by one.

Term closure is the peaceful ending. A `NetworkAdmin` closes the term of an `ACTIVE` agreement, moving it `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and incrementing its `version` by one, but only once the logical clock has reached or passed the agreement's `term_end_date`; attempting it earlier is `ERR_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED`. Both `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED` are terminal states of an agreement: nothing transitions out of either. The unpeaceful ending — `ACTIVE → SHORTFALL_REVIEW`, then either back to `ACTIVE` or on to `RECALL_PENDING` and finally `TERMINATED` — is the subject of §3.9. Agreements can be listed and filtered by `outlet_id` and by `status`, which is how an operator walks an outlet's whole contract history.

### 3.3. Dispenser Equipment: Placement, Service Holds and the Calibration Dimension

A **DispenserUnit** is one physical machine on an outlet's counter. A `NetworkAdmin` records a placement by naming the `outlet_id` where the machine physically sits, the `agreement_id` that covers it, the manufacturer `serial_number`, the equipment `model_code` such as `FREESTYLE_9100`, and the `installed_at` timestamp from which the machine's age is measured for exposure purposes. The `serial_number` must be unique across the whole fleet, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` on a collision, and the submitted `outlet_id` must match the `outlet_id` recorded on the submitted agreement, with `ERR_OUTLET_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` when the two disagree. A newly recorded unit answers 201 with `status` `IN_SERVICE`, `calibration_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `lifetime_dispensed_gallons` at 0, `gallons_at_last_filter_change` at 0, `brix_ratio_last_measured` null and `version` 1. Units can be listed filtered by `outlet_id`, by `agreement_id`, by `status` and by `calibration_status`.

Every dispenser carries **two independent state dimensions**, and this independence is a first-class rule rather than an implementation detail. The service dimension, `status`, takes one of `IN_SERVICE`, `SERVICE_HOLD`, `RECALL_STAGED` or `RETURNED`. The brix/ratio dimension, `calibration_status`, takes one of `UNVERIFIED`, `IN_SPEC` or `OUT_OF_SPEC`. The two never constrain each other: a machine may perfectly legitimately be in `SERVICE_HOLD` and `IN_SPEC` at the same instant, and each dimension advances only through its own triggers.

On the service dimension, a `ServiceTechnician` submits an `action` of `PLACE` or `RELEASE`. `PLACE` moves the unit `IN_SERVICE → SERVICE_HOLD`; `RELEASE` moves it `SERVICE_HOLD → IN_SERVICE`, but is blocked while any maintenance task of that unit whose `task_type` is `SANITATION` sits in `OVERDUE`, in which case the release is refused with `ERR_SANITATION_OVERDUE`. Either action increments the unit's `version` by one. A service hold is a maintenance state, not an accounting one: telemetry ingestion continues for a `SERVICE_HOLD` unit and forecasting continues to count its pours.

The remaining two service-dimension moves are not operator actions on the unit at all. When the agreement covering a unit enters `RECALL_PENDING`, the server drives every unit of that agreement whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` **or** `SERVICE_HOLD` to `RECALL_STAGED`, incrementing each unit's `version` by one. A `NetworkAdmin` then confirms physical return, moving the unit `RECALL_STAGED → RETURNED`, which is terminal — and this single confirmation is the deepest cascade in the system. Confirming a return does all of the following in one request: every maintenance task of that unit currently in `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `OVERDUE` is driven to `WAIVED` with its `waiver_reason` set to the literal string `EQUIPMENT_RETURNED`, and each such task that was leaving `OVERDUE` lowers the outlet's `overdue_task_count` by one and triggers a posture recomputation; every service call of that unit currently in `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `ESCALATED` is driven to `CANCELLED`, returning any van parts it was holding to the van; and if that confirmation leaves every unit of the agreement `RETURNED`, the agreement moves `RECALL_PENDING → TERMINATED`, which may in turn suspend the outlet and skip its pending stops as described in §2.10 and §3.1. The unit's own `version` advances by one.

On the calibration dimension, movement has exactly one trigger: the completion of a maintenance task whose `task_type` is `BRIX_CALIBRATION`, performed by a `ServiceTechnician` who submits the `measured_brix_ratio` observed at the machine. The measurement is graded against a target syrup-to-water ratio of 5.00 with a tolerance of 0.15: the absolute difference between the measured ratio and 5.00 is compared against 0.15, and a difference at or below the tolerance grades the task's `calibration_outcome` as `PASS` while any larger difference grades it `FAIL`. The boundary is inclusive on the passing side — a measured ratio of exactly 5.15 or exactly 4.85 still passes, whereas 5.16 fails. A `PASS` sets the unit's `calibration_status` to `IN_SPEC` and a `FAIL` sets it to `OUT_OF_SPEC`; in both cases the measured value is written to the unit's `brix_ratio_last_measured` and the unit's `version` advances by one. The declared movements on this dimension are therefore `UNVERIFIED → IN_SPEC` and `UNVERIFIED → OUT_OF_SPEC` on a machine that has never been verified, `IN_SPEC → OUT_OF_SPEC` when a previously good machine drifts out, and `OUT_OF_SPEC → IN_SPEC` when a re-calibration brings it back — the last two forming a cycle that a machine may travel any number of times. Only that single submitted measurement participates in the grading; no other task and no telemetry reading influences it. A unit sitting in `OUT_OF_SPEC` blocks delivery service at its outlet entirely until it is brought back to `IN_SPEC`, as §3.7 sets out.

### 3.4. Telemetry Ingestion and the Gallon Counters

Pour volume arrives as a stream of **TelemetryReading** events, each summarising one window of consumption for one SKU on one machine. Either a `SupplyPlanner` or a `ServiceTechnician` may ingest a reading, naming the `dispenser_unit_id` that poured, the `supply_item_id` whose consumption is being reported, the `dispensed_ounces` of finished beverage in the window, and the `reading_at` timestamp that marks the **end** of the reported window. Two optional inputs shape interpretation: `window_days`, the length in days of the window the reading summarises, which defaults to 7 and is accepted from 1 through 90 inclusive; and `source`, which is either `TELEMETRY` or `MANUAL_AUDIT` and defaults to `TELEMETRY`. `dispensed_ounces` must be zero or greater, and `reading_at` must be at or before the logical clock — a reading from the future is refused with `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. Ingestion answers with 201. A reading is an immutable event with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and both variants of `source` count equally in every calculation that consumes readings; a manual audit is not second-class evidence. Readings can be listed filtered by `dispenser_unit_id` and by `supply_item_id`, and bounded in time by `reading_at_from` and `reading_at_to`.

Each ingestion refreshes the receiving machine's `lifetime_dispensed_gallons`, which is defined as the total ounces ever reported for that unit divided by 128 and then rounded half up to two decimal places. That counter is the machine's odometer, and it is read together with `gallons_at_last_filter_change` — the odometer value captured the last time a filter change was completed on that unit — to decide whether a water filter has run past its useful volume. Ingesting a reading therefore re-evaluates every maintenance task of that unit that is still `SCHEDULED` and carries a `task_type` of `FILTER_CHANGE`, applying the breach rule of §3.5. A gallon breach drives such a task straight to `OVERDUE` with `breach_kind` `GALLON`, skipping the courtesy grace band entirely, which raises the outlet's `overdue_task_count` by one and forces a posture recomputation; and if that posture recomputation lands on `FROZEN`, the outlet's `PENDING` route stops are skipped with `skip_reason_code` `POSTURE_FROZEN` and their allocations are released back to the pool and the route. One reading can therefore reach four hops out: reading, task, outlet posture, route stop and pool. The receiving unit's own `version` increments by one on every ingestion.

### 3.5. Preventive Maintenance: Scheduling, Due Evaluation, Completion and Waivers

A `ComplianceOfficer` owns the maintenance schedule. A **MaintenanceTask** is raised against a `dispenser_unit_id` with a `task_type` of `PREVENTIVE`, `BRIX_CALIBRATION`, `SANITATION` or `FILTER_CHANGE`, and a calendar `due_at` deadline. One optional input, `gallon_interval`, expresses the nominal gallons-since-last-change that also trips the task, and it is tied strictly to one task type: it is required when `task_type` is `FILTER_CHANGE`, and its absence there is `ERR_GALLON_INTERVAL_REQUIRED`; it must be absent for every other task type, and its presence there is `ERR_GALLON_INTERVAL_NOT_APPLICABLE`. When supplied, `gallon_interval` must be greater than zero. Creation answers 201 with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `breach_kind` null, `completed_at`, `completed_by_user_id`, `calibration_outcome`, `waiver_reason` and `waived_by_user_id` all null, and `version` 1. Tasks can be listed filtered by `dispenser_unit_id`, by `task_type` and by `status`.

Hard water eats filters faster, so the nominal interval is **derated once, at creation, and stored**. The stored `effective_gallon_interval` is the submitted `gallon_interval` multiplied by a factor of one minus two percent for every grain per gallon by which the outlet's `water_hardness_gpg` exceeds the baseline of 10, where hardness at or below the baseline contributes nothing to the derate, and the result is rounded half up to two decimal places. Concretely, a 3,000-gallon nominal interval at an outlet whose feed water measures 20 grains per gallon is derated by twenty percent — ten grains above baseline at two percent each — and is stored as 2400.00 gallons. When `gallon_interval` is null, `effective_gallon_interval` is null as well, and the task can only ever be tripped by the calendar.

Due evaluation is an explicit act by a `ComplianceOfficer` on one task, and it is declared only from `SCHEDULED` and from `DUE`; requesting it on a task already in `OVERDUE`, `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The evaluation weighs two limits, and the volume limit is weighed **first** and outranks any calendar reasoning, because water quality is a compliance matter rather than a calendar courtesy:

- If the task has a non-null `effective_gallon_interval` and the machine's gallons since its last filter change — that is `lifetime_dispensed_gallons` less `gallons_at_last_filter_change` — have reached or exceeded that effective interval, the task's `breach_kind` becomes `GALLON` and it goes straight to `OVERDUE`, with no grace band. A machine sitting exactly at the effective interval has breached it.
- Otherwise, if the logical clock is at or past the task's `due_at` plus a 72-hour grace band, the `breach_kind` becomes `DATE` and the task goes to `OVERDUE`.
- Otherwise, if the logical clock is at or past `due_at` but still inside that three-day grace band, the `breach_kind` becomes `DATE` and the task moves to `DUE`. A clock sitting exactly at `due_at` yields `DUE`.
- Otherwise nothing has been breached: `breach_kind` stays null, the task's `status` is left exactly where it was, and because nothing was mutated the task's `version` does not advance.

The transitions this produces are `SCHEDULED → DUE`, `SCHEDULED → OVERDUE` and `DUE → OVERDUE`, and each of them writes the `breach_kind` that justified it and increments the task's `version` by one. Both moves that land in `OVERDUE` additionally raise the owning outlet's `overdue_task_count` by one and recompute `service_posture`, and where that recomputation lands on `FROZEN` the outlet's `PENDING` stops are skipped and their allocations released as in §2.10. The same `SCHEDULED → OVERDUE` move with `breach_kind` `GALLON` also arises without any operator, driven by telemetry ingestion as described in §3.4.

Completion belongs to the `ServiceTechnician`, who may complete a task from `SCHEDULED`, from `DUE` or from `OVERDUE`, provided the machine has not already reached `RETURNED`. Completion writes `completed_at` from the logical clock and `completed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the task to the terminal `COMPLETED` state and increments its `version` by one. Two task types do extra work on completion. A `FILTER_CHANGE` completion resets the volume baseline by copying the machine's current `lifetime_dispensed_gallons` into its `gallons_at_last_filter_change`, so the next interval is measured from here. A `BRIX_CALIBRATION` completion requires `measured_brix_ratio` in the request — its absence is `ERR_BRIX_MEASUREMENT_REQUIRED` — and writes the task's `calibration_outcome` together with the machine's `brix_ratio_last_measured` and `calibration_status` as graded in §3.3; `measured_brix_ratio` is ignored on every other task type. Completing a task that was in `OVERDUE` also lowers the outlet's `overdue_task_count` by one and recomputes `service_posture`, which is how an outlet works its way back from `FROZEN` to `RESTRICTED` and eventually to `OPEN`.

Waivers are the compliance escape hatch and are deliberately narrower. A `ComplianceOfficer` may waive a task from `DUE` or from `OVERDUE` — never from `SCHEDULED`, and never once it is terminal — by supplying a free-text `waiver_reason`. The waiver records that reason and the acting `X-User-ID` in `waived_by_user_id`, moves the task to the terminal `WAIVED` state and increments its `version` by one; waiving a task that was `OVERDUE` also decrements the outlet's `overdue_task_count` and recomputes `service_posture`. The officer doing the waiving must not be the officer recorded in the outlet's `last_hold_placed_by_user_id`, per §2.12, and a violation is `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION`. A third route into `WAIVED` needs no officer at all: when a machine is confirmed returned, each of its tasks in `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `OVERDUE` is waived by the server with `waiver_reason` set to `EQUIPMENT_RETURNED`, each one leaving `OVERDUE` decrementing the outlet's counter and triggering the posture recomputation, and each one advancing its own `version` by one.

### 3.6. The SKU Catalogue, Stock Positions and Demand Forecasting

Consumables are catalogued as **SupplyItem** rows, registered by a `NetworkAdmin`. Each carries a `sku_code`, unique across the catalogue with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE` on a collision; a `brand_name`, which is the grouping label the brand-mix report reports against; a `supply_form` of either `CARTRIDGE` or `BAG_IN_BOX`; a `case_pack_units` count of at least 1, which is the indivisible shippable case size; and an `ounces_per_unit` figure greater than zero stating how many finished-beverage ounces one unit yields, which is the bridge that turns telemetry ounces into orderable units. Three optional inputs describe limited-time flavours: `promotional`, which defaults to false; and `promo_window_start` and `promo_window_end`, both null by default and meaningful only for promotional SKUs. A final optional input, `national_allocation_units`, defaults to 0, must be zero or greater, and is the finite national pool of units the network may commit to routes. Registration answers 201 with `reserved_pool_units` at 0 and `version` 1; `reserved_pool_units` is server-maintained, never below zero and never above `national_allocation_units`.

A `NetworkAdmin` may later adjust a SKU, submitting any of `national_allocation_units`, `promo_window_start` and `promo_window_end`. The adjustment increments the SKU's `version` by one. One guard applies: `national_allocation_units` may never be lowered below the SKU's current `reserved_pool_units`, since that would leave more units committed to routes than the pool contains, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_POOL_BELOW_RESERVED`.

An **OutletSupplyPosition** is the stock position for exactly one SKU at exactly one outlet, opened by a `SupplyPlanner` against an `outlet_id` and a `supply_item_id`, with an optional `on_hand_units` count of units physically at the outlet that defaults to 0 and may not be negative. The outlet-and-SKU pair is unique, so a second position for the same combination is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPLY_POSITION`. A newly opened position answers 201 with `forecast_variant` recorded as `TRAILING_ACTUAL`, `forecast_units` at 0, `requested_units` at 0, `allocated_units` at 0, `planned_route_stop_id` null, `alert_level` `NONE`, `promo_suppressed` false, `alert_acknowledged_by_user_id` null and `version` 1. Positions can be listed filtered by `outlet_id`, by `supply_item_id`, by `planned_route_stop_id` and by `alert_level`.

Forecasting is a `SupplyPlanner` action on one position, and it always requires two inputs: a `variant` of either `TRAILING_ACTUAL` or `THROUGHPUT_PROJECTED`, and a `window_days` count giving the length of the trailing telemetry window to measure. The run proceeds in five ordered steps.

First, the trailing consumption is measured. The run sums `dispensed_ounces` over every telemetry reading that reports the position's own SKU, was produced by a dispenser unit belonging to the position's outlet whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `SERVICE_HOLD`, and whose `reading_at` falls in the half-open window that begins `window_days` days before the logical clock and ends at the logical clock exclusive. Units in `RECALL_STAGED` or `RETURNED` contribute nothing. That sum divided by the number of window days gives a daily ounce rate, held exactly and never rounded at this stage.

Second, the horizon is projected across 14 days. Under `TRAILING_ACTUAL` the projection is simply the daily rate multiplied by 14, and the outlet's `throughput_tier` is ignored entirely. Under `THROUGHPUT_PROJECTED` the same fourteen-day projection is additionally multiplied by the tier factor of the outlet's `throughput_tier`: a `SMALL` outlet uses 1.00, a `MEDIUM` outlet 1.15 and a `LARGE` outlet 1.30 — so from identical telemetry a large outlet forecasts thirty percent more units than a small one.

Third, the projection becomes a net requirement. The projected ounces are divided by the SKU's `ounces_per_unit` to give a raw unit requirement, the position's current `on_hand_units` are subtracted, any negative result is clamped up to zero, and the outcome is rounded half up to two decimal places and stored as `forecast_units`.

Fourth, promotional suppression is applied **after** the third step and overwrites its result. When the SKU is `promotional` and the logical clock does **not** sit inside the promotional window — that is, the clock is before `promo_window_start` or at or after `promo_window_end` — then `forecast_units` is overwritten with 0.00 and `promo_suppressed` is set to true. In every other case, including a non-promotional SKU and a promotional SKU inside its window, `promo_suppressed` is set to false and the computed `forecast_units` stands. `requested_units` is then the `forecast_units` value rounded **upward** to the next whole unit, so a suppressed position requests nothing.

Fifth, the alert band is set from days of cover on hand. If the measured daily ounce rate is zero the position is treated as having unlimited cover. Otherwise the days of cover are the position's `on_hand_units` multiplied by the SKU's `ounces_per_unit` and divided by the daily ounce rate, compared without rounding. The resulting `alert_level` is `CRITICAL` when the position has zero `on_hand_units` **or** its cover is under 3 days, `LOW` when the cover is under 7 days without meeting the critical test, and `NONE` otherwise. A run writes `forecast_variant`, `forecast_units`, `requested_units`, `alert_level` and `promo_suppressed`, clears `alert_acknowledged_by_user_id` back to null so that every fresh alert must be acknowledged afresh, and increments the position's `version` by one.

An `OutletManager` acknowledges the current low-supply alert on their own outlet's position, which records the acting `X-User-ID` in `alert_acknowledged_by_user_id` and increments the position's `version` by one. Because acknowledgement is an outlet-scoped act, the ownership rule of §2.1 applies and a manager reaching for another outlet's position receives `ERR_OUTLET_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.

### 3.7. Replenishment Routes: Planning, Allocation and Stop Execution

A `SupplyPlanner` plans a **ReplenishmentRoute** out of one depot for one day and one van, supplying a `route_code` that must be unique across all routes — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROUTE_CODE` — together with the `depot_region_code` the route serves, the `service_date` on which it runs expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC midnight instant, the `van_code` of the van that will run it, and a `capacity_units` figure of at least 1 giving the total consumable units the van can carry. Planning answers 201 with `status` `PLANNED`, `committed_units` at 0, `delivered_units` at 0 and `version` 1. Thereafter `committed_units` always equals the sum of `allocated_units` across the positions linked to this route's stops and may never exceed `capacity_units`, while `delivered_units` is the sum of `delivered_units` over the route's stops that reached `SERVED`.

Stops are added to a `PLANNED` route one at a time. The planner supplies the `outlet_id` to be visited, the `sequence_index` giving visit order within the route, and the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the delivery window, along with the mandatory `expected_route_version` of §2.8. Three coherence rules bite here. The `sequence_index` must be at least 1 and must not already be used by another stop of the same route, a collision being `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SEQUENCE`. The visited outlet's `region_code` must equal the route's `depot_region_code`, a mismatch being `ERR_REGION_MISMATCH` — a van out of the wrong depot is never dispatched to an outlet it does not serve. And the new window must not overlap another `PENDING` or `ARRIVED` stop of the same route under the half-open, zero-buffer arithmetic of §2.9, an overlap being `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A created stop answers 201 with `status` `PENDING`, `delivered_units` at 0, `skip_reason_code` null and `version` 1, and the owning route's `version` also increments by one. Stops can be listed filtered by `route_id`, by `outlet_id` and by `status`.

**The allocation waterfall** is where the finite national pool meets outlet demand, and it is the most order-sensitive rule in the system. A `SupplyPlanner` submits a list of `supply_position_ids` against one stop. The submitted positions are first filtered for eligibility: a position qualifies only if its `outlet_id` is the stop's own outlet, its `alert_level` is `LOW` or `CRITICAL`, its `planned_route_stop_id` is currently null, its `promo_suppressed` flag is false, and its `requested_units` is at least 1. Positions that fail any of those tests — belonging to another outlet, already linked to a stop, promo-suppressed, sitting at `alert_level` `NONE`, or requesting nothing — are silently dropped from consideration rather than treated as errors. If nothing survives the filter, the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_POSITIONS`.

The survivors are then processed in a pinned order: all `CRITICAL` positions before all `LOW` ones; within the same alert band, the larger `requested_units` first; and where those still tie, the older position first by `created_at` ascending. Each position in that order is served as follows. The SKU's unreserved pool is its `national_allocation_units` less its `reserved_pool_units`, and the route's remaining capacity is its `capacity_units` less its `committed_units`, both read at the moment this position is reached, so earlier grants in the same request have already consumed what they took. Before any grant is written for the position, two hard stops are checked in this order: if the unreserved pool is smaller than one `case_pack_units` the whole request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, and if the remaining route capacity is smaller than one `case_pack_units` the whole request is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Either refusal aborts the entire request and leaves no grant from it persisted.

Where both hard stops pass, the grant is computed by taking the smallest of three numbers — the position's `requested_units`, the unreserved pool and the remaining route capacity — and then truncating that cap downward to a whole multiple of the SKU's `case_pack_units`. Partial cases are never shipped: a cap of 5 against a case pack of 4 grants 4, and a cap of 3 against a case pack of 4 grants 0. A positive grant writes the position's `allocated_units` and points its `planned_route_stop_id` at this stop, and raises both the SKU's `reserved_pool_units` and the route's `committed_units` by the granted amount. A grant of zero changes nothing: the position keeps `allocated_units` at 0 and `planned_route_stop_id` null. Each mutated position, supply item and route advances its own `version` by one. Allocation is refused outright with `ERR_OUTLET_POSTURE_FROZEN` when the stop's outlet is `FROZEN`, checked before the waterfall is ever entered. The reply to a successful run is not the stop but a summary object carrying `route_stop_id`, `allocated_position_count` giving the number of positions that actually received a positive grant, `total_allocated_units` giving the sum of all grants in this run, and `granted_units_by_position`, an array pairing each granted position with the units it received.

Dispatch releases a planned route to the driver. It requires `expected_route_version`, and it requires that the route has at least one stop and that its `committed_units` do not exceed its `capacity_units`; the move is `PLANNED → DISPATCHED` and the route's `version` advances by one. From there the day belongs to the `RouteDriver`. Arriving at a stop moves it `PENDING → ARRIVED` and is legal only while the owning route is `DISPATCHED`. Serving a stop moves it `ARRIVED → SERVED` and is the moment stock actually changes hands: for every position pointing at this stop, the position's `allocated_units` are added to its `on_hand_units`, then `allocated_units` returns to 0 and `planned_route_stop_id` to null; the stop's `delivered_units` is written as the total units handed over; the SKU's `reserved_pool_units` is reduced per SKU by what was delivered, because that pool commitment has now been consumed rather than returned; and the route's `committed_units` is deliberately **not** reduced. Each affected position then has its `alert_level` recomputed by re-running the days-of-cover test of §3.6 against its new `on_hand_units`, using the same trailing daily consumption rate that the position's most recent forecast run measured, while `forecast_units`, `requested_units`, `forecast_variant` and `promo_suppressed` keep their stored values. Since a delivery can only raise `on_hand_units`, the recomputed band can only move toward `NONE`. Every row touched advances its own `version` by one.

Serving carries two safety gates that fire before anything moves. If the outlet's `service_posture` is `FROZEN`, service is refused with `ERR_OUTLET_POSTURE_FROZEN`. If any dispenser unit at that outlet carries `calibration_status` `OUT_OF_SPEC`, service is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_OUT_OF_SPEC` — the network will not top up a machine that is pouring off-ratio product.

Skipping is the other terminal branch, and it is a fully specified outcome rather than an absence of one. A `RouteDriver` may skip a stop from `PENDING` or from `ARRIVED`, supplying the `skip_reason_code`, which must be one of `OUTLET_CLOSED`, `POSTURE_FROZEN` or `ACCESS_DENIED`. Either skip writes that reason, releases every position pointing at the stop through the four release movements of §2.10 — allocated units to 0, stop link to null, the SKU's `reserved_pool_units` down, the route's `committed_units` down — and advances the `version` of every mutated row by one. The server also skips `PENDING` stops on its own initiative, and the reason it stamps identifies the cause: `POSTURE_FROZEN` when the outlet's posture became `FROZEN`, `OUTLET_CLOSED` when the outlet was suspended, and `ACCESS_DENIED` when the route was cancelled. Both `SERVED` and `SKIPPED` are terminal; a stop never leaves either.

A route ends one of two ways. Completion moves it `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`, takes no request body and no expected-version field, and is permitted only once every stop of the route has reached `SERVED` or `SKIPPED`; it recomputes the route's `delivered_units` as the sum of `delivered_units` over its `SERVED` stops and advances the route's `version` by one. Cancellation moves it to `CANCELLED` from either `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED`, requires `expected_route_version` and accepts an optional `reason_code`; it drives every stop of the route in `PENDING` or `ARRIVED` to `SKIPPED` with `skip_reason_code` `ACCESS_DENIED`, releases each of those stops' positions restoring both the SKUs' `reserved_pool_units` and the route's `committed_units`, and advances the route's `version` by one. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.8. Service Calls, Van Dispatch and the Parts Ledger

When a machine misbehaves, either the `OutletManager` who runs the location or a `NetworkAdmin` opens a **ServiceCall** against the `dispenser_unit_id` at fault, stating when the outlet noticed it with `reported_at` and what it observed with a `symptom_code` of `NO_POUR`, `OFF_RATIO`, `LEAK`, `CO2_LOW` or `ICE_FAULT`. `reported_at` must be at or before the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. The machine must be serviceable, meaning its `status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `SERVICE_HOLD`; a call against a `RECALL_STAGED` or `RETURNED` machine is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. An `OutletManager` may only open a call for a machine at their own outlet, per §2.1. Creation answers 201 with `status` `OPEN`, `van_code`, `scheduled_window_start`, `scheduled_window_end`, `assigned_tech_user_id`, `reserved_part_stock_id` and `resolution_code` all null, `reserved_part_units`, `consumed_part_units` and `escalation_count` all 0, and `version` 1. Calls can be listed filtered by `dispenser_unit_id`, by `reserved_part_stock_id`, by `status` and by `severity`.

**Severity is graded once, at creation, and never recomputed.** The grade starts from the symptom's own weight: `NO_POUR` is worth 60 points, `LEAK` 55, `OFF_RATIO` 40, `CO2_LOW` 30 and `ICE_FAULT` 25. Three surcharges are then added, all read from state as stored at the moment of the request. The outlet's compliance posture adds nothing when `OPEN`, 15 points when `RESTRICTED` and 30 points when `FROZEN`. The outlet's overdue maintenance adds 5 points for each task counted in `overdue_task_count`, but that component is capped at 20 points, so a fifth overdue task adds nothing further. A machine whose `calibration_status` is `OUT_OF_SPEC` adds a further 10 points. The sum of the base weight and the three surcharges is then capped at 100 and stored as `severity_score`. The `severity` band follows from the score with inclusive lower bounds: a score of 85 or more is `CRITICAL`, a score of 60 or more but under 85 is `URGENT`, and anything below 60 is `ROUTINE` — a score of exactly 85 is `CRITICAL` and a score of exactly 60 is `URGENT`. All arithmetic here is on whole integers.

Dispatch is a `ServiceTechnician` action and is declared from two states: `OPEN → DISPATCHED` for a fresh call, and `ESCALATED → DISPATCHED` for a call handed back for another attempt, which is legal while `escalation_count` is at most 2. The technician supplies the `van_code` that will attend and the `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` bounding the on-site window, and may optionally reserve a part off that van by naming a `part_sku` together with the `part_units` needed. The requested window must clear the closed-interval, 30-minute-buffered van-collision test of §2.9 against other `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE` calls on the same van, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_VAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Where a part is requested, the van stock row for that van and that part must exist — otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_ON_VAN` — and must carry at least `part_units` in `on_van_units`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VAN_PARTS`. A successful dispatch writes `van_code`, both window bounds and `assigned_tech_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, overwriting whatever a previous dispatch had left there when the call is being re-dispatched out of `ESCALATED`. Where a part was requested it also moves `part_units` out of the row's `on_van_units` and into its `reserved_units`, and records the row's identifier in the call's `reserved_part_stock_id` and the held quantity in `reserved_part_units`. The call's `version` and any mutated van stock row's `version` each advance by one.

Only the technician holding the dispatch may drive the call further: arriving on site, resolving and escalating all require `X-User-ID` to equal `assigned_tech_user_id`, and any other technician is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Arrival moves the call `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE` with no body and advances its `version` by one.

Resolution moves the call `ON_SITE → RESOLVED`, which is terminal. The technician supplies the `resolution_code`, one of `PART_REPLACED`, `CALIBRATED`, `CLEANED` or `NO_FAULT_FOUND`, and optionally the `consumed_part_units` actually fitted, which may not exceed the `reserved_part_units` the call is holding — an excess is `ERR_CONSUMED_EXCEEDS_RESERVED`. Settlement of the van row then follows the ledger of §2.11 exactly: the whole held reservation leaves `reserved_units`, the fitted quantity is added to `consumed_units`, and the difference between what was held and what was fitted returns to `on_van_units`. The call records its `consumed_part_units` and `resolution_code`, returns `reserved_part_units` to 0 and `reserved_part_stock_id` to null, and both the call's and the van row's `version` advance by one.

Escalation is the loop back. A technician may escalate `ON_SITE → ESCALATED` only while `escalation_count` is strictly below 2, and an attempt once it has reached 2 is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_LIMIT_REACHED`; a call can therefore travel the `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE → ESCALATED → DISPATCHED` cycle at most twice. The technician supplies an `escalation_reason_code`. Escalation fully reverses the parts reservation as a compensating movement — every unit held in `reserved_units` for this call returns to `on_van_units`, and the call's `reserved_part_units` returns to 0 with `reserved_part_stock_id` set to null — raises `escalation_count` by one, clears `van_code`, `scheduled_window_start`, `scheduled_window_end` and `assigned_tech_user_id` so the call is free for re-dispatch, and advances both the call's and the van row's `version` by one.

Cancellation is available to the `OutletManager` who owns the outlet or to a `NetworkAdmin`, from `OPEN` or from `DISPATCHED`, and drives the call to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Where a reservation is being held — which can only be the case from `DISPATCHED` — cancellation applies exactly the same compensating release as escalation, returning every reserved unit to `on_van_units`. The call's `version` advances by one. Calls are also cancelled without any operator when their machine is confirmed returned, as §3.3 describes.

The van side of this story is a **VanPartStock** row, created by a `NetworkAdmin` who names the `van_code`, the `part_sku` such as `PUMP_ASSY_12`, and the `on_van_units` first loaded. The van-and-part pair is unique, so a duplicate load is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VAN_PART`. Creation answers 201 recording `initial_units` equal to the units supplied — a fixed baseline that never changes again — with `reserved_units`, `consumed_units` and `restocked_units` all 0 and `version` 1; none of the four counters may ever go negative. A `NetworkAdmin` restocks a row by supplying a count of `units`, which raises both `on_van_units` and `restocked_units` by that amount and advances the row's `version` by one; restocking is the only operation that raises the row's conserved total. Van rows can be listed filtered by `van_code` and by `part_sku`.

### 3.9. Volume Shortfall, Equipment Exposure, the Recall Decision and Brand Mix

The commercial reckoning happens quarter by quarter. A `NetworkAdmin` runs a **shortfall assessment** on an `ACTIVE` agreement, supplying the half-open assessment period as `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` and, optionally, the `exposure_variant` to value the equipment under, which defaults to `BOOK_VALUE`. The run drives the agreement `ACTIVE → SHORTFALL_REVIEW`.

The fee arithmetic works on calendar quarters, and a quarter enters the assessment when the quarter's own start instant falls inside the half-open request period. The quarterly commitment is the agreement's `annual_commitment_gallons` divided by four, held exactly and **never** rounded. Then, for each assessed quarter taken in chronological order of its start:

- The quarter's dispensed volume is the sum of `dispensed_ounces` over every telemetry reading of every dispenser unit whose `agreement_id` is this agreement, whose `reading_at` is at or after the quarter's start and strictly before the quarter's end, divided by 128 to convert ounces to gallons. Readings from units that have since moved to `RECALL_STAGED` or even `RETURNED` count in full — the volume was poured, and history is not rewritten by the fate of the hardware.
- The quarter's shortfall in gallons is the quarterly commitment less that dispensed volume, clamped up to zero when the outlet over-poured.
- The quarter's fee is that shortfall multiplied by the agreement's `shortfall_fee_cents_per_gallon` and **rounded half up to whole cents at this point**.

The agreement's `assessed_shortfall_gallons` is then the sum of the per-quarter shortfall gallons, rounded half up to two decimal places, and its `assessed_fee_cents` is the sum of the **already-rounded** per-quarter cent amounts. That rounding order is pinned: summing unrounded quarterly shortfalls and rounding the product once at the end is wrong and yields a different total whenever two or more quarters carry a half-cent residue. `assessed_shortfall_gallons` is a reported figure only and is never the basis on which `assessed_fee_cents` is computed. If the assessment produces a fee of zero cents there is nothing to review, and the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_SHORTFALL_TO_ASSESS` — the agreement stays `ACTIVE`.

The same run also values the equipment, because a fee is not the only available remedy. The exposure is summed over the agreement's dispenser units whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, `SERVICE_HOLD` or `RECALL_STAGED`; units already `RETURNED` contribute nothing. Each contributing unit's whole months in service are counted as the elapsed time from its `installed_at` to the logical clock divided by 30 days and truncated downward. Under the `BOOK_VALUE` variant the unit's exposure is the agreement's `equipment_value_cents` multiplied by one minus one percent per whole month in service, with that multiplier clamped up to zero so a very old machine is worth nothing rather than a negative amount. Under the `REPLACEMENT_RISK` variant the unit's exposure is instead `equipment_value_cents` multiplied by one plus one half of one percent per whole month in service, so age escalates rather than erodes the figure. Either way each unit's exposure is rounded half up to whole cents **before** the units are summed, and the total is stored as the agreement's `recall_exposure_cents`. The consequence of the two variants is deliberate: the same fleet can clear the recall floor under `REPLACEMENT_RISK` while falling short of it under `BOOK_VALUE`.

A successful assessment writes `assessed_shortfall_gallons`, `assessed_fee_cents`, `recall_exposure_cents`, the `exposure_variant` actually used, `assessment_period_start` and `assessment_period_end` from the submitted period bounds, and `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and advances the agreement's `version` by one.

The **recall decision** then belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer`, who submits an `outcome` of either `FEE_ONLY` or `RECALL` on an agreement sitting in `SHORTFALL_REVIEW`. Either way the officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `shortfall_assessed_by_user_id`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and either way `recall_decided_by_user_id` is written and the agreement's `version` advances by one.

`FEE_ONLY` returns the agreement `SHORTFALL_REVIEW → ACTIVE` and leaves `assessed_fee_cents` standing as the amount payable — the cycle `ACTIVE → SHORTFALL_REVIEW → ACTIVE` may therefore be travelled once per assessment period. `RECALL` is the harder outcome and carries two further preconditions. The equipment must actually be recallable, meaning the agreement's `free_on_loan` is true; equipment the outlet purchased outright cannot be taken back and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RECALL_NOT_PERMITTED`. And the exposure must be worth the trouble: `recall_exposure_cents` must be at least 450000 — four thousand five hundred dollars in cents — with anything below that floor refused as `ERR_RECALL_EXPOSURE_BELOW_FLOOR`. Where both hold, the agreement moves `SHORTFALL_REVIEW → RECALL_PENDING` and every dispenser unit of that agreement whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `SERVICE_HOLD` is driven to `RECALL_STAGED`, each advancing its own `version` by one. From there the recall plays out through the physical returns of §3.3: as each machine is confirmed returned, and once the last one is, the agreement moves `RECALL_PENDING → TERMINATED` and the outlet-suspension cascade of §2.10 follows.

**Brand mix reporting** closes the loop back to the customer conversation. Available to a `NetworkAdmin`, a `SupplyPlanner` or the `OutletManager` of the outlet in question, this computed read mutates nothing and reports over a fixed half-open 30-day period that ends at the logical clock. A reading qualifies for the report when its `reading_at` falls inside that period and it was produced by a dispenser unit of this outlet whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, `SERVICE_HOLD` or `RECALL_STAGED`; readings from `RETURNED` units are excluded. The response carries `outlet_id`, the `period_start` and `period_end` bounding the window, the `total_ounces` poured across all qualifying readings, the array of `lines`, the `top_brand_name` and the array of `promo_available_skus`.

One line is emitted for each supply item that has at least one qualifying reading, and each line carries that SKU's `supply_item_id`, `sku_code` and `brand_name`, its own `total_ounces` summed over its qualifying readings, and its `share_pct`. Shares are apportioned to one decimal place by the largest-remainder method so that the emitted shares total exactly 100.0. For each line, the exact share is its own ounces divided by the report's `total_ounces` and multiplied by 100; that exact share is first truncated downward to a whole tenth of a percent, and the amount discarded by the truncation is the line's remainder. The number of leftover tenths still to be distributed is 100.0 minus the sum of all truncated shares, expressed in tenths and rounded to a whole number of tenths. Those leftover tenths are then awarded, one tenth each, to the lines with the largest remainders, ties being broken in favour of the supply item with the earlier `created_at`. Each line's `share_pct` is its truncated share plus any tenth it was awarded.

The `top_brand_name` is the `brand_name` of the line with the largest `total_ounces`, ties again broken in favour of the earlier `created_at` of the supply item. `promo_available_skus` lists the `sku_code` of every supply item that is `promotional`, whose promotional window currently contains the logical clock — at or after `promo_window_start` and strictly before `promo_window_end` — and only while the outlet's `service_posture` is not `FROZEN`, ordered by the supply item's `created_at` ascending; a frozen outlet is offered no promotional flavours at all. Where the period contains no qualifying volume at all, `total_ounces` is 0, `lines` is an empty array with no `share_pct` emitted anywhere, and `top_brand_name` is null.
