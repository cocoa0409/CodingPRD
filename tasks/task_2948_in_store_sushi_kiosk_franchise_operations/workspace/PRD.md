# Sushi Kiosk Franchise Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Hissho-style in-store sushi is a franchise business that lives inside somebody else's building. A grocery banner licenses a slice of its floor and a stretch of its refrigerated case to the franchisor; the franchisor places a trained chef behind that case, produces sushi on-site every morning against the store's shopper traffic, sells it through the host store's own registers, and settles a revenue share at the end of each period. Two obligations run in opposite directions and both have to be provable after the fact: the host store owes dedicated square footage and case frontage, and the franchisor owes documented food safety on a product that is assembled from acidified rice and raw fish a few feet from the checkout lanes.

This service is the operational and compliance system of record for that arrangement. It carries the license agreement with its revenue-share and markdown terms, chef training and certification, the daily production plan derived from store traffic, the acidification and time/temperature control record for every rice batch, HACCP variance documentation when a batch falls outside limits but inside a documentable band, raw-fish sourcing with parasite-destruction and cold-chain records, discard and markdown handling for unsold packs, the point-of-sale feed from the host registers, the settlement statement built on top of it, and the food safety audit lifecycle that can end in a kiosk being closed. Every one of those records is written by a different party, and the system's job is to keep them honest about each other.

The design pressure that shapes almost every rule below is that a compliance failure and a commercial failure must never be able to hide one another. A rice batch that failed its time/temperature control cannot be packed no matter how favourable the settlement math looks; a kiosk locked down by a critical audit finding keeps producing zero packs even while its license agreement is perfectly active; and a discard caused by the franchisee's own compliance failure appears in the settlement statement's transparency total without ever reducing what the host store owes. The API is mounted under `/kiosk-ops` and is consumed by four distinct parties whose powers are deliberately disjoint.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request except the logical-clock endpoint carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it acts in. If either header is absent, or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined — before the path parameters are resolved, before the body is parsed. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the four role names below, or is a valid role that is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The four roles are `FranchisorOps`, `HostStoreManager`, `FranchiseChef` and `QualityAuditor`, spelled exactly that way.

`FranchisorOps` is Hissho franchisor operations staff and owns the commercial surface: host store registration, license agreements and their whole lifecycle, chef registration, training and placement, kiosk provisioning and lifecycle, POS feed adjudication, settlement statements, plan reconciliation and pack-lot voids. It is not a food-safety authority: it cannot certify a chef, decide a HACCP variance, release or destroy a fish lot, or run an audit.

`HostStoreManager` is the manager of the grocery or convenience store hosting the kiosk. It attests the dedicated space and case frontage, drives the POS integration, submits the daily sales extract, acknowledges, disputes and pays settlement statements, records corrective action against audit findings, and requests reverification.

`FranchiseChef` is the placed franchise sushi chef and owns everything that touches product: releasing and locking the production plan, mixing rice and recording acidification, filing HACCP variance documentation, receiving fish, recording freezing regimes and cold-chain readings, building pack lots, marking them down and discarding them.

`QualityAuditor` is the independent food-safety and quality authority. It issues and revokes chef certifications, decides and revokes HACCP rice variances, releases or destroys fish lots, and runs the entire audit lifecycle up to kiosk closure. It deliberately holds no commercial powers: it cannot register stores, write agreements, submit feeds, or touch settlement money.

Identity binding differs by role, and it is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `HostStoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HostStore`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `HostStore` compare the resource's host-store foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A manager may act only on its own store's agreements, kiosks, plans, pack lots, feeds, statements and audits.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FranchiseChef`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Chef`; ownership checks compare the chef's own `assigned_kiosk_id` against the kiosk the target resource is reachable from. A chef may act only on the kiosk they are placed at, and on the plans, rice batches, fish lots and pack lots belonging to it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FranchisorOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where an audit trail demands it — as `created_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on a settlement statement, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a discard event it records — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `QualityAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `opened_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a food safety audit, as `variance_reviewed_by_user_id` on a rice batch whose variance it decided, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a discard event it records, but it is never compared to any entity's `id` and never itself an input to a business decision.

An entity-backed actor that reaches outside its own scope is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403): a `HostStoreManager` acting on another store's agreement, feed or statement, or a `FranchiseChef` acting on a kiosk other than the one they are assigned to. Ownership is a scoping check rather than a business rule, and it fires before any state or business validation on the target.

Segregation of duties is enforced on top of role authorization, and it compares `X-User-ID` values rather than roles. The user who issues a settlement statement must differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded on the draft, and the auditor who verifies an audit must differ from the `opened_by_user_id` recorded when the audit was started; either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). The third separation — that a chef may never adjudicate the HACCP variance they filed — is structural rather than identity-based: filing is a `FranchiseChef` capability and deciding is a `QualityAuditor` capability, the two role sets are disjoint, and a chef attempting the decision is therefore rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated with two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, so a caller walking `offset` forward sees records in the order the system created them; where two rows share a `created_at`, the tie is broken by `id` ascending so that paging remains stable.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as true when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit those four fields. Filter query parameters are additive: supplying two of them narrows the result to rows matching both, and supplying none returns everything the caller is authorized to see.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` that is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation, and a `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two record types are pure append-only evidence and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and their payloads must not contain those keys: `ColdChainReading` (a temperature reading of a fish lot) and `DiscardEvent` (a record of packs destroyed). Once written, neither can be amended; a correction is expressed by writing another record, never by editing the first.

The `version` field is load-bearing in two ways. It is the value a caller echoes back in the optimistic-concurrency fields described in §2.7, and it is the signal that a cascade touched a row the caller was not directly writing: when suspending a kiosk voids a production plan, the plan's `version` and `updated_at` both move even though the caller never named the plan. Any of a row's computed aggregates being recalculated counts as a mutation of that row for this purpose.

Calendar dates — `production_date`, `business_date`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `scheduled_for`, `attested_on`, `commissioned_on` and `last_attested_on` — are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component. Every other temporal field is a full ISO 8601 UTC instant.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Its Two Sweeps

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Shelf life, certification expiry, acidification hold windows, freeze durations, audit due dates and markdown pricing all read a single server-side **logical clock**, and an administrative capability sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant. Before the first such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is mandatory and must never fall back to real time. The clock capability is the one endpoint in the catalogue that requires no authentication headers at all: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` are not evaluated for it, and any of the four roles — or none — may call it.

Advancing the clock is not a passive act. It runs two sweeps, in this order, before it returns. First, every pack lot that is still `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` and whose `shelf_expiry_at` is at or before the new instant is discarded, each one appending exactly one discard event whose `reason` is `SHELF_LIFE_EXPIRED`. Second, every certification whose `expires_at` is at or before the new instant moves from `VALID` to `EXPIRED`, and any chef thereby left without both mandatory certifications is suspended. Because both sweeps change risk inputs, the affected kiosks then have their risk total recomputed and their sanitation posture re-derived. A caller who advances the clock past a shelf expiry and a certification expiry in one step must see all of it settled in the same call.

### 2.6. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error comes back, and which one is fully determined. The ladder below is evaluated strictly in order, and the first violation found wins; a lower-numbered rung always pre-empts a higher-numbered one, even when both would carry HTTP 422.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This precedes path-parameter existence, so a request with no headers against a non-existent resource is a 401, not a 404.
2. **Role authorization.** The role is one of the four declared names and is listed for the endpoint, or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). This also precedes existence: a `FranchiseChef` calling a `FranchisorOps`-only endpoint with a fabricated id receives 403, never 404.
3. **Ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, the acting identity owns the target, or `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (403).
4. **Existence.** Every id the request references resolves. Path parameters are checked first, left to right along the URL, and then request-body foreign keys in the order the body fields are declared for that endpoint. The order is observable: a call carrying two unresolvable ids returns the error belonging to the leftmost one. The applicable code is the entity-specific one — `ERR_HOST_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KIOSK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHEF_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOURCING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AUDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` — each at HTTP 404. A nested record that exists but hangs off a different parent than the one named in the path is treated as not found: a certification that does not belong to the path's chef yields `ERR_CHEF_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, and a finding that does not belong to the path's audit yields `ERR_AUDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Schema.** Required fields present, types correct, enum values inside their declared set, numeric range constraints respected, ISO 8601 instants well formed, and every start/end pair strictly ordered with the start before the end. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Schema runs *after* existence, deliberately, so that a malformed body aimed at a non-existent parent still reports the 404.
6. **State.** The target is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, or the entity-specific state error at HTTP 409: `ERR_LICENSE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_KIOSK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CHEF_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RICE_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FISH_LOT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PACK_LOT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_POS_FEED_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_FINDING_INVALID_STATE`. A target already in a terminal state is always a state error, never a business error.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** The echoed parent version matches, or the rule's stale-version error at HTTP 412 (§2.7). OCC sits between state and business validation: a stale version carried by a body that is also business-invalid returns the 412.
8. **Food safety.** The safety gates described throughout §3 — a certified chef with unexpired mandatory certifications, an approved rice batch behind a pack lot, a released fish lot with parasite destruction satisfied and no unresolved cold-chain breach, a kiosk whose sanitation posture is not `LOCKDOWN`, and a documented variance inside the documentable pH and hold-time band. All are HTTP 422. Safety outranks every other business category: nothing that could put unsafe product on a shelf may be masked by a referential or fiscal complaint.
9. **Temporal windows.** Rice hold windows that must not overlap, production dates that must fall inside the agreement term, settlement periods that must not overlap another live statement, and audits that must not start early. All HTTP 422.
10. **Referential coherence.** Cross-aggregate rules: a pack lot's rice batch and fish lot must resolve to the same kiosk, a POS feed line must name a pack lot of the feed's own host store, and a plan may only be reconciled once every feed for its date is terminal. All HTTP 422.
11. **Capacity and cardinality.** Aggregate limits that need the referenced rows already resolved: pack count against rice yield, live packs against the kiosk's daily cap and the host store's case capacity, one live POS feed per store and date, every finding resolved before reverification, and only minor findings waivable. All HTTP 422.
12. **Fiscal gates.** Money rules: a statement may not be issued with no reconciled sales in its period, a payment must equal the net amount due exactly, a fish allocation may not exceed the source lot's remaining mass, and the segregation-of-duties comparisons on statement issue and audit verification. All HTTP 422.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth stating outright, because they are the cases where a plausible implementation goes wrong. First, **state beats fiscal**: issuing a settlement statement that is `VOID` *and* has zero reconciled feeds in its period returns `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (rung 6, HTTP 409), never `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_SALES` (rung 12, HTTP 422). Second, **safety beats referential**: building a pack lot whose rice batch is `REJECTED` *and* whose fish lot belongs to a different kiosk returns `ERR_RICE_BATCH_NOT_APPROVED` (rung 8), never `ERR_CROSS_KIOSK_REFERENCE` (rung 10) — both are HTTP 422, so only the `error_id` distinguishes them.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two creates in this system silently retire an existing record, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent whose terms they depend on. In each case the echoed field is a required, non-optional part of the request body — omitting it is a schema violation, not a free pass.

Creating a production plan requires `expected_kiosk_version`, carrying the `version` of the target kiosk as the client last observed it. If the kiosk's current `version` differs, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_KIOSK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new draft plan legitimately supersedes any prior draft plan for the same kiosk and production date: without the check, a writer holding an outdated view of the kiosk would void a plan it had never seen. The check forces it to re-read the kiosk first. A successful create increments the kiosk's `version` by 1, which is exactly what invalidates every other client's stale view.

Creating a settlement statement requires `expected_license_agreement_version`, carrying the `version` of the target license agreement as the client last observed it. If the agreement's current `version` differs, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new draft statement supersedes a prior draft for the same agreement and period, and every money field on it is derived from the agreement's `revenue_share_model` and space obligations, so a stale writer would compute a statement against superseded terms. A successful create increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Adding one elsewhere, or making either of these two optional, is wrong.

### 2.8. Money, Mass and Rounding

All money in this system is integer cents, and there are no fractional-cent fields anywhere. Wherever a monetary result is derived from a rate or a percentage, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the rounding is applied once per named money field — never to the intermediate products inside a formula. In the tiered revenue share of §3.8 that distinction is observable: the three tier products are summed at full precision and the sum is rounded once.

Non-monetary quantities each carry their own precision, and they do not all round the same way. Rice mass in a production plan (`planned_rice_kg`) is 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Fish mass consumed by a pack lot (`consumed_fish_kg`) and the mass remaining in a source lot (`remaining_kg`) are 3 decimal places, rounded half up. Forecast variance (`forecast_variance_pct`) is a signed value at 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Pack counts, risk points, audit scores and minute counts are integers. Where a ceiling or a capacity is derived by multiplication — packs per linear foot of case, packs per certified chef, packs per kilogram of rice — the result is always rounded *down* to a whole unit, because a fraction of a pack cannot be produced. Freeze duration is measured in whole hours with any fractional hour discarded. Temperature comparisons are made at 1 decimal place; pH comparisons at 2 decimal places.

Cold-chain breach minutes are the one place where rounding *order* is load-bearing rather than cosmetic, and §3.6 spells it out: each interval is rounded to a whole minute before the intervals are summed, not after.

### 2.9. Binding Numbers and Advisory Numbers

Two calculations in this system produce two answers on the same inputs, and only one of them is money. The license agreement names which variant binds — `revenue_share_model` selects the settlement base, and `markdown_policy` selects the markdown price — and the system computes the other variant anyway and stores it beside the binding one for franchisor reporting.

On a settlement statement, `binding_share_cents` is the tiered share computed on the base named by the agreement, `advisory_share_cents` is the same tier ladder run over the other model's base, and `variance_cents` is the binding figure minus the advisory figure. Only `binding_share_cents` flows into credits, into `net_due_cents`, and into the agreement's lifetime accrual. On a pack lot, `markdown_price_cents` is the price computed by the policy the agreement names, `advisory_markdown_price_cents` is the other variant on the same inputs, and `markdown_variant_applied` records which policy bound. Only `markdown_price_cents` ever values a pack for a markdown sale or a discard; the advisory number never touches money.

Because the advisory figure is computed and not merely echoed, the variance can be either sign, and both variants must be implemented in full even though only one is billed.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system reach far beyond the record the caller named, and the reach is specified rather than incidental. The recurring shape is a four-link chain that begins when a kiosk stops operating: the kiosk's live production plans are voided; the rice batches of those voided plans that are not already rejected or consumed are rejected; the pack lots built from those plans' batches that are still on the shelf or marked down are discarded, each appending exactly one discard event; and every draft settlement statement covering the discard date recomputes its discard figures. That chain is reached from three different places — an operator suspending a kiosk, an operator decommissioning one, and an audit escalating to closure — and it must behave identically in all three.

Two properties of the chain are easy to get wrong. The third link fires on the *pack lots of the plans*, whether or not the intervening rice batch was itself rejectable: a batch already `CONSUMED` by the pack lot is untouched by the second link, but its pack lots are still pulled off the shelf by the third. And the fourth link is asymmetric: a lockdown-driven discard raises a draft statement's `period_discard_value_cents` while leaving `discard_credit_cents` and `net_due_cents` exactly where they were, because such a discard is not credit-eligible.

Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been written directly. Reverts follow the same discipline: when a cascade or a revocation removes risk points from a kiosk, the removal is exact, so that a kiosk driven into lockdown and then cleared returns to the posture band its remaining risk actually warrants.

### 2.11. Nested Paths and Filtered Reads

Four record types live underneath a parent in the URL rather than at the top level: a chef's certifications, a fish lot's cold-chain readings, a pack lot's discard events, and an audit's findings. For those, the path is validated left to right — the parent must resolve before the child is looked up, and the child must actually belong to that parent. The parent's identifier is taken from the path and never from the body: `chef_id` on a certification, `fish_lot_id` on a cold-chain reading, `pack_lot_id` on a discard event and `audit_id` on a finding are all set by the server from the URL, and a body attempting to supply them does not override the path. Two of those children are themselves addressed by a second path segment when they are acted on individually — a certification being revoked is named by its own `certification_id` beneath its chef, and a finding being corrected or waived is named by its own `finding_id` beneath its audit — and the left-to-right rule applies to those pairs too: a request naming a non-existent chef and a non-existent certification reports the missing chef, and one naming an existing chef but a certification belonging to a different chef reports the missing certification.

These nested collections matter more than a convenience read, because cascade-created records appear nowhere else. A discard event appended by a lockdown cascade, a temperature-abuse cascade or the shelf-expiry sweep is discoverable only through the owning pack lot's discard-event list, and the per-interval breach attribution of a cold-chain reading is discoverable only through the owning fish lot's reading list. Both lists are paginated and ordered as §2.3 requires; the reading list, being ordered by `created_at` ascending, is also in `recorded_at` ascending order because readings may only be appended forward in time.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Host Store and Licensing Its Floor Space

A franchisor operations user registers the host store first, describing it with a display `name`, the retail `banner_chain` that owns it, the banner-assigned `store_number`, and a `state_code` that must be exactly two uppercase letters. Three physical facts are captured at the same time and never move afterwards: `weekly_traffic_count`, the measured weekly shopper traffic (which is what a planner later divides down into the daily `traffic_forecast` used for production), `sushi_space_sqft`, the square footage the store declares it will dedicate to the kiosk, and the case dimensions — `refrigerated_case_linear_ft`, the linear feet of refrigerated case given over to sushi packs, and `refrigerated_case_capacity_packs`, the physical pack capacity of that case, which is shared by every kiosk of the same host store. Traffic and square footage may be zero; both case figures must be strictly greater than zero. A newly registered store starts with `pos_integration_status` of `NOT_CONNECTED`, `pos_terminal_count` of 0, `space_compliance_state` of `UNVERIFIED`, and `attested_sushi_space_sqft`, `attested_case_linear_ft` and `last_attested_on` all null. Stores can be read one at a time or listed with the list narrowed by `banner_chain`, by `pos_integration_status` or by `space_compliance_state`.

The store manager owns two ongoing obligations against that registration. The first is the register link: the manager sets `pos_integration_status` to `NOT_CONNECTED`, `CONNECTED` or `SUSPENDED` and reports how many lanes are wired to the kiosk in `pos_terminal_count`. A store whose integration is anything other than `CONNECTED` cannot have a sales feed accepted — the attempt is refused with `ERR_POS_NOT_CONNECTED` — and cannot have an agreement activated or a kiosk commissioned.

The second obligation is periodic **space attestation**: the manager measures what is actually dedicated to sushi and reports `attested_sushi_space_sqft`, `attested_case_linear_ft` and the calendar date `attested_on`, which the system stores as `last_attested_on`. Each attestation re-derives compliance. The shortfall against a license is the agreement's `min_space_sqft_obligation` minus the store's attested square footage, floored at zero, so an attestation that meets or beats the obligation produces a shortfall of zero; before any attestation has been filed the declared `sushi_space_sqft` stands in for the attested figure. The store's `space_compliance_state` is `UNVERIFIED` while `last_attested_on` is null; it is `COMPLIANT` when the shortfall is zero *and* the attested case frontage is greater than or equal to the agreement's `min_case_linear_ft_obligation`; otherwise it is `DEFICIENT`. Both obligations are inclusive — attesting exactly the obligated figures is compliant. The result of that derivation is written back to the store as `space_compliance_state` and to each of the store's active agreements as `space_shortfall_sqft`.

The license agreement itself is drafted by franchisor operations against a `host_store_id` and carries the whole commercial bargain: `revenue_share_model`, either `GROSS_SALES` or `NET_OF_MARKDOWN`, which selects the binding settlement base; `markdown_policy`, either `PRORATED_REMAINING_LIFE` or `FLAT_STEP`, which selects the binding markdown price for packs produced under the agreement; `min_space_sqft_obligation` and `min_case_linear_ft_obligation`, both strictly positive; `space_credit_cents_per_sqft`, the cents credited back to the store per square foot of attested shortfall on a settlement statement, which may be zero; and the term dates `effective_from` and `effective_to`. A drafted agreement begins in `DRAFT` with `lifetime_share_accrued_cents` at 0, `active_kiosk_count` at 0, `space_shortfall_sqft` at 0 and `suspension_reason` null. Agreements can be listed with the list narrowed by `host_store_id` or by `status`.

Activation is where the space obligation first bites. Moving an agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE` requires the host store's `pos_integration_status` to be `CONNECTED`, and it requires the store to have attested at least once: an `UNVERIFIED` store is refused with `ERR_SPACE_OBLIGATION_DEFICIENT`. A `DEFICIENT` store, by contrast, *can* have its agreement activated — the shortfall is priced into settlement as a credit rather than used to block operations, which is the whole point of `space_credit_cents_per_sqft`. Activation recomputes `space_shortfall_sqft` from the store's current attestation, re-derives the store's `space_compliance_state` against this agreement's obligations, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

An active agreement can be suspended and brought back any number of times. Suspension takes a `suspension_reason`, records it, and moves the agreement `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`; every kiosk of that agreement that is currently operating is suspended too, each one running the full kiosk-suspension cascade of §2.10, `active_kiosk_count` is set to 0, and the agreement's `version` increments. Coming back the other way — `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, reachable either through the activate action or through the dedicated reinstate action, both of which again require `CONNECTED` point-of-sale integration — clears `suspension_reason` and increments `version`, but deliberately does **not** revive the kiosks: each suspended kiosk needs its own reinstatement.

Termination is final. It takes a `termination_reason`, is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and is blocked while any settlement statement of the agreement is in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED` — unfinished money must be resolved before the licence ends, and an attempt made while such a statement exists is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_INVALID_STATE`. On success every kiosk of the agreement that is in `PROVISIONING`, `OPERATING` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `DECOMMISSIONED`, `active_kiosk_count` becomes 0, `version` increments, and the agreement sits in `TERMINATED` permanently; any later attempt to act on it is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_INVALID_STATE`, as is any transition attempted from a state where it is not declared. Throughout its life the agreement carries `lifetime_share_accrued_cents`, a conserved ledger that at all times equals the sum of `binding_share_cents` over the statements of this agreement whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `PAID`, and `active_kiosk_count`, the count of its kiosks whose `operational_state` is `OPERATING`.

### 3.2. Training, Certifying and Placing Franchise Chefs

A chef enters the system as a bare `full_name` and nothing else: `CANDIDATE` status, `training_hours_logged` of 0, `valid_cert_count` of 0, `assigned_kiosk_id` null, and both `food_handler_cert_expires_at` and `haccp_cert_expires_at` null. Franchisor operations then logs training against them, supplying `hours` and the `curriculum_code` covered; each entry adds its `hours` to the cumulative `training_hours_logged`. The first entry promotes the chef `CANDIDATE → TRAINEE`; every later entry is a self-loop `TRAINEE → TRAINEE` that simply accumulates more hours, so there is no limit on how many training entries a chef may have. Logging training against a chef who is neither a candidate nor a trainee is rejected with `ERR_CHEF_INVALID_STATE`. Chefs can be listed with the list narrowed by `assigned_kiosk_id` or by `status`.

Certifications are issued only by the quality auditor, one per call, against the chef named in the path — the certification's `chef_id` is taken from that path and never from the body. The body carries the `cert_type`, which is one of `FOOD_HANDLER`, `HACCP_RICE_ACIDIFICATION` or `PARASITE_DESTRUCTION_VERIFIER`; the `exam_score` out of 100, which must be at least 0 and at most 100; the `issued_at` instant; and `valid_days`, a strictly positive validity window. The system computes `expires_at` as `issued_at` advanced by `valid_days` whole days, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, stores the row with `status` `VALID` and `revocation_reason` null, and then recomputes the owning chef's `valid_cert_count` (the number of the chef's certifications whose `status` is `VALID`), `food_handler_cert_expires_at` (the `expires_at` of the chef's valid `FOOD_HANDLER` certification with the latest expiry, or null if there is none) and `haccp_cert_expires_at` (the same for `HACCP_RICE_ACIDIFICATION`). The chef's own certification history — including rows that have since become `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` — is readable as a list narrowed by `cert_type` or `status`.

Certification readiness is re-evaluated after every issuance and after every clock movement, and it is what drives the chef's `status`. A chef whose `training_hours_logged` is at least 24 hours **and** who holds a valid `FOOD_HANDLER` certification whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock **and** a valid `HACCP_RICE_ACIDIFICATION` certification whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock becomes `CERTIFIED`; exactly 24 hours is enough. A chef with any training hours at all but not both mandatory certifications is `TRAINEE`; a chef with no training hours is `CANDIDATE`. The third certification type, `PARASITE_DESTRUCTION_VERIFIER`, is real and countable in `valid_cert_count` but is not one of the two mandatory certifications. When a chef becomes `CERTIFIED` — whether the promotion is `TRAINEE → CERTIFIED` or the recertification edge `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`, which may be traversed any number of times — and they are already placed at a kiosk, that kiosk's `certified_chef_count` is recomputed upward.

Losing a mandatory certification is the one path out of `CERTIFIED`, and it does not walk back down the training ladder. A quality auditor may revoke a certification with a `revocation_reason`, moving it `VALID → REVOKED`; a certification that is already `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` cannot be revoked again and yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INVALID_STATE`. Revocation recomputes `valid_cert_count` and the two expiry fields, and if the lost certification was a `FOOD_HANDLER` or `HACCP_RICE_ACIDIFICATION` and the chef was `CERTIFIED`, the chef moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` and the assigned kiosk's `certified_chef_count` is decremented and that kiosk's risk inputs recomputed. Expiry through the logical clock has exactly the same effect on the chef, moving the certification `VALID → EXPIRED` first. In neither case is the kiosk itself suspended: it keeps operating, and the consequence surfaces later as a smaller staffing ceiling on the next production plan.

Placement is a franchisor-operations act: it sets the chef's `assigned_kiosk_id` to the supplied kiosk and, when `as_lead_chef` is true, also writes that chef into the kiosk's `lead_chef_id`. If the placed chef is `CERTIFIED`, the kiosk's `certified_chef_count` is incremented at the same time. A chef still in `CANDIDATE` may not be placed, and the attempt returns `ERR_CHEF_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Commissioning a Kiosk, and Its Two Independent Postures

A kiosk is provisioned under an agreement by supplying `license_agreement_id`, an operator-facing `kiosk_code` that must be unique within that agreement, and `case_footprint_sqft`, the strictly positive square footage this particular kiosk occupies inside the store's sushi space. It starts life in `operational_state` `PROVISIONING` with `sanitation_posture` `CLEAR`, `risk_points` 0, `certified_chef_count` 0, `daily_pack_cap` 0, `open_critical_finding_count` 0, `consecutive_clean_audits` 0, and `lead_chef_id`, `commissioned_on` and `suspension_reason` all null. Kiosks can be listed with the list narrowed by `license_agreement_id`, `lead_chef_id`, `operational_state` or `sanitation_posture`.

Commissioning is the gate between a provisioned shell and a producing kiosk, and it has six conditions. The kiosk must still be in `PROVISIONING`; its agreement must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_INVALID_STATE`; the host store's `pos_integration_status` must be `CONNECTED`, or `ERR_POS_NOT_CONNECTED`; the host store's `space_compliance_state` must be `COMPLIANT`, or `ERR_SPACE_OBLIGATION_DEFICIENT` — this is the one operation for which a deficient store is genuinely blocking rather than merely priced; the chef named by `lead_chef_id` must have `status` `CERTIFIED` and must actually be assigned to this kiosk, or `ERR_CHEF_NOT_CERTIFIED`; and that chef's `haccp_cert_expires_at` must be strictly after the current logical clock, or `ERR_HACCP_CERT_EXPIRED`. The call supplies the calendar date `commissioned_on`. On success the kiosk moves `PROVISIONING → OPERATING`, records `lead_chef_id` and `commissioned_on`, recomputes `certified_chef_count`, re-derives `sanitation_posture` from its risk total (which on a fresh kiosk is `CLEAR`), increments the agreement's `active_kiosk_count` by 1, and freezes `daily_pack_cap` — computed once, at this moment, as the host store's `refrigerated_case_linear_ft` multiplied by nine packs per linear foot, rounded down to a whole pack. Because it is frozen, later changes to the case frontage do not move an already-commissioned kiosk's cap.

Suspending an operating kiosk takes a `suspension_reason`, is available to franchisor operations and to the quality auditor, and drives the four-link cascade of §2.10 in full: the kiosk's plans in `DRAFT`, `RELEASED` or `LOCKED` move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `KIOSK_SUSPENDED`; the rice batches of those voided plans that are not already `REJECTED` or `CONSUMED` move to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `PLAN_VOIDED`; every pack lot whose rice batch belongs to one of those plans and whose `status` is `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` moves to `DISCARDED` with exactly one appended discard event whose `reason` is `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` and whose `discarded_count` equals that lot's `unsold_count` at the moment of the discard; and every draft settlement statement of the kiosk's agreement recomputes `period_discard_value_cents`, `discard_credit_cents` and `net_due_cents`, of which only the first actually moves because a lockdown discard is not credit-eligible. The agreement's `active_kiosk_count` is decremented by 1. What suspension does *not* do is touch `sanitation_posture`: the two dimensions move independently, and a manually suspended kiosk keeps whatever posture its risk total warrants.

Reinstatement is the mirror edge, traversable as often as the kiosk cycles: `SUSPENDED → OPERATING` requires the agreement to be `ACTIVE`, the kiosk's `sanitation_posture` to be anything other than `LOCKDOWN`, no audit of this kiosk to be sitting in `IN_PROGRESS`, `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PENDING` or `REVERIFICATION`, and the chef named by `lead_chef_id` to still be `CERTIFIED`. It clears `suspension_reason`, recomputes `certified_chef_count`, re-derives `sanitation_posture` and increments `active_kiosk_count` by 1. Plans that a suspension voided are never revived — production resumes only with new plans.

Decommissioning takes a `decommission_reason` and is reachable from `PROVISIONING`, `OPERATING` or `SUSPENDED`, but only while no pack lot of the kiosk is still `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN`: live product must be sold, discarded or voided first. From `PROVISIONING` nothing cascades, because a provisioning kiosk owns no plans; from the other two states the same four cascade links run over whatever plans and batches are still non-terminal. Decommissioning also unassigns every chef whose `assigned_kiosk_id` is this kiosk and clears `lead_chef_id`, and it sets the agreement's `active_kiosk_count` to a fresh recount of that agreement's kiosks that are still `OPERATING`. `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal; anything attempted against a decommissioned kiosk, and any transition attempted from a state where it is not declared — commissioning an already-operating kiosk, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_KIOSK_INVALID_STATE`.

Running alongside `operational_state`, and never constraining it, is the kiosk's food-safety dimension: `sanitation_posture`, one of `CLEAR`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKDOWN`. It is never set by a caller. It is re-derived by the system from `risk_points` and `open_critical_finding_count` after every event that changes a risk input — an audit submitted or verified, a finding corrected or waived, a rice variance approved or revoked, a fish lot quarantined for a cold-chain breach, or a clock advance that expires certifications and discards packs. The risk total itself is a weighted count: one point for each of the kiosk's audit findings of severity `MINOR` whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CORRECTED`, four points for each such `MAJOR` finding, ten points for each such `CRITICAL` finding, three points for each of the kiosk's rice batches that is `VARIANCE_APPROVED` or that was consumed while carrying an approved variance, six points for each of the kiosk's fish lots currently `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `COLD_CHAIN_BREACH`, minus two points for every one of the kiosk's `consecutive_clean_audits`. The sum is then clamped into the range 0 through 40 inclusive, and that clamped value is what `risk_points` holds. Findings in `CORRECTED` still carry their full weight — only `VERIFIED` and `WAIVED` findings drop out of the total.

The posture band is read off the clamped total, with one override taking precedence over the arithmetic. If `open_critical_finding_count` is greater than zero the posture is `LOCKDOWN`, whatever the total says. Otherwise a total of at least 30 is `LOCKDOWN`, a total of at least 18 but below 30 is `RESTRICTED`, a total of at least 8 but below 18 is `WATCH`, and anything below 8 is `CLEAR`; each band is inclusive at its lower bound, so exactly 8 is `WATCH` and exactly 18 is `RESTRICTED`. Because the ceiling clamps before the thresholds are read, a kiosk cannot be pushed so far past lockdown that clearing its findings fails to bring it back. The ladder is deliberately not monotone: the critical-finding override takes a kiosk straight from `CLEAR → LOCKDOWN`, skipping the `WATCH` and `RESTRICTED` bands entirely, while the ordinary escalation path walks `CLEAR → WATCH`, `WATCH → RESTRICTED` and `RESTRICTED → LOCKDOWN` one band at a time as points accumulate. Recovery is equally free: as findings are verified or waived a kiosk may travel `LOCKDOWN → WATCH`, `LOCKDOWN → CLEAR`, `RESTRICTED → CLEAR` or `WATCH → CLEAR` in a single re-derivation, and every recovery edge additionally requires `open_critical_finding_count` to be back to zero. Entering `LOCKDOWN` has two immediate consequences beyond gating: production planning at that kiosk is refused with `ERR_KIOSK_POSTURE_LOCKDOWN`, and every plan of the kiosk still in `DRAFT` or `RELEASED` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `POSTURE_LOCKDOWN`. What lockdown never does is change `operational_state` — a locked-down kiosk stays `OPERATING` until somebody explicitly suspends it.

Because the posture arithmetic drives so much, it is separately readable. A computed read returns the kiosk's current risk decomposition as an object with `kiosk_id`, the per-severity counts of findings still carrying weight as `minor_open`, `major_open` and `critical_open`, the `approved_variance_count`, the `quarantined_lot_count`, the `clean_audit_credit` that was subtracted, `raw_risk_points` as the total before clamping, `risk_points` as the clamped total, `sanitation_posture` as the band it lands in, and `posture_forced_by_critical` as a boolean saying whether the critical-finding override rather than the arithmetic decided the band.

### 3.4. Planning the Day's Production Against Store Traffic

A production plan is created for one kiosk and one `production_date` by a franchise chef or by franchisor operations, carrying the planner's `traffic_forecast` for that date (which may be zero but never negative) and the `expected_kiosk_version` described in §2.7. The `production_date` must fall inside the agreement's closed term, from `effective_from` through `effective_to` inclusive; a date outside it is refused with `ERR_PLAN_DATE_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT`. Plans can be listed with the list narrowed by `kiosk_id`, `production_date`, `status` or `superseded_plan_id`.

The pack target is the minimum of three independent ceilings, and the plan records which one bound. The **demand** ceiling is the forecast traffic multiplied by an attach rate of 0.018 — eighteen packs per thousand forecast shoppers — rounded down to a whole pack. The **case capacity** ceiling is the smaller of the host store's `refrigerated_case_capacity_packs` and the frontage-implied capacity of nine packs per linear foot of `refrigerated_case_linear_ft`, rounded down. The **staffing** ceiling is the kiosk's `certified_chef_count` multiplied by 200 packs per certified chef; only chefs whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` count, which is why a revoked certification quietly lowers tomorrow's plan. `binding_cap_source` records which ceiling produced the minimum, and when two or more ceilings tie at the minimum value the tie is broken in the fixed precedence `DEMAND`, then `CASE_CAPACITY`, then `STAFFING` — the first of those three whose own value equals the minimum is the one recorded.

Only after the minimum has been taken does the sanitation posture attenuate it. `posture_factor_applied` is 1.00 when the kiosk is `CLEAR`, 0.85 when it is `WATCH`, 0.50 when it is `RESTRICTED` and 0.00 when it is `LOCKDOWN`; `planned_pack_count` is the binding ceiling multiplied by that factor and rounded down to a whole pack. The order matters and is not interchangeable: attenuating each candidate ceiling before taking the minimum would sometimes change *which* ceiling binds, and `binding_cap_source` must reflect the unattenuated comparison. From the pack target the plan derives `planned_rice_kg`, the pack count multiplied by 0.12 kilograms of cooked rice per pack — 120 grams — rounded half up to two decimal places. Both figures are frozen at create time and never recomputed.

The lockdown factor of 0.00 exists for completeness rather than as a working path, because planning at a locked-down kiosk is refused outright with `ERR_KIOSK_POSTURE_LOCKDOWN`; and even a plan whose `planned_pack_count` came out at zero for some other reason can never be released, since release requires a strictly positive pack count.

Creating a plan is also a supersede: any prior plan for the same `kiosk_id` and the same `production_date` that is still in `DRAFT` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `SUPERSEDED`, and the new plan records that prior plan's identifier in its own `superseded_plan_id`. The new plan increments the kiosk's `version` by 1, which is precisely what makes every other client's `expected_kiosk_version` stale.

From `DRAFT` the plan advances `DRAFT → RELEASED` at a chef's hand, and only while the kiosk's `operational_state` is `OPERATING`, its `sanitation_posture` is not `LOCKDOWN`, and `planned_pack_count` is greater than zero; a locked-down kiosk yields `ERR_KIOSK_POSTURE_LOCKDOWN`. A released plan is the only plan rice batches may be created against for that kiosk and date. `RELEASED → LOCKED` closes the plan to further rice batches and requires at least one of the plan's rice batches to have reached `APPROVED`, `VARIANCE_APPROVED` or `CONSUMED` — a plan nobody actually produced against cannot be locked. `LOCKED → RECONCILED` is a franchisor-operations act and requires every point-of-sale feed of the kiosk's host store whose `business_date` equals the plan's `production_date` to have reached a terminal state, either `RECONCILED` or `REJECTED`; a feed still `SUBMITTED` blocks the plan with `ERR_PERIOD_FEED_NOT_RECONCILED`.

`actual_pack_count` is not a reconciliation-time discovery. It is the sum of `pack_count` over the pack lots whose rice batch belongs to this plan and whose `status` is `ON_SHELF`, `MARKED_DOWN` or `SOLD_OUT`, with `DISCARDED` and `VOIDED` lots excluded, and it is maintained live from the moment packs are built — which is exactly why a discard cascade changes a figure that was already computed. Reconciliation recomputes it once more and then sets `forecast_variance_pct`: when `planned_pack_count` is zero the variance is 0.00; otherwise it is the actual count minus the planned count, multiplied by 100, divided by the planned count, and rounded half up to two decimal places, signed, so a negative value means the kiosk under-produced against its traffic forecast. Before reconciliation `forecast_variance_pct` is null.

Voiding a plan is available from `DRAFT`, `RELEASED` or `LOCKED` and takes a `void_reason`, which is recorded verbatim on the manual path and set to `SUPERSEDED`, `KIOSK_SUSPENDED` or `POSTURE_LOCKDOWN` when the void is cascade-driven. Whichever path drove it, the void rejects every rice batch of the plan that is not already `REJECTED` or `CONSUMED` with `rejection_reason` `PLAN_VOIDED`, discards every pack lot of the plan's batches that is still `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` with a discard event whose `reason` is `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` and which is therefore not credit-eligible, and recomputes `actual_pack_count` after those discards. `RECONCILED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal; acting on either, or attempting a transition the plan's current state does not declare — releasing an already-released plan, for example — returns `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Sushi Rice: Acidification, Time/Temperature Control and the HACCP Variance

Rice is the most regulated thing the kiosk makes, because acidified rice held at ambient temperature is only safe inside a narrow envelope of pH, time and temperature. A batch is started by a franchise chef against a released plan, supplying `production_plan_id`, the `chef_id` of the chef who mixed it, the strictly positive `rice_kg` of cooked rice and `vinegar_liters` of seasoned vinegar added during mixing, and the two ends of the ambient hold window, `hold_start_at` and `hold_end_at`. The plan must be `RELEASED` — starting a batch against a plan in any other state returns `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE` — and the mixing chef must be `CERTIFIED`, or the batch is refused with `ERR_CHEF_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Hold windows are treated as half-open, running from `hold_start_at` up to but not including `hold_end_at`, and no two non-rejected batches of the same kiosk may overlap: a batch that begins at exactly the instant another ends is accepted, while any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_ACIDIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The batch starts in `MIXING` and immediately computes `max_pack_yield`, the ceiling on how many packs may ever be drawn from it, as `rice_kg` divided by 0.12 kilograms of rice per pack, rounded down to a whole pack. Batches are readable individually or as a list narrowed by `production_plan_id`, `chef_id`, `variance_submitted_by_chef_id` or `status`.

The chef then records the acidification reading, supplying `measured_ph`, the `ph_measured_at` instant at which the pH was taken, and `max_temp_c`, the maximum ambient temperature in Celsius observed during the hold. This closes the mixing step and moves the batch `MIXING → ACIDIFYING`.

Verification of the time and temperature control record is the branch point of the whole rice lifecycle, and it is a read-only judgement in the sense that it takes no body at all. It first computes `exposure_minutes` as the whole number of minutes spanned by the hold window from `hold_start_at` to `hold_end_at`, both endpoints included in the span, so a hold that runs exactly four hours yields exactly 240. Three parameters are then tested, each inclusively: the pH passes when `measured_ph` is at most 4.20, so exactly 4.20 passes; the time passes when `exposure_minutes` is at most 240 minutes, so exactly 240 passes; and the temperature passes when `max_temp_c` is at most 21.0 degrees Celsius, so exactly 21.0 passes.

- When all three pass, the batch moves `ACIDIFYING → APPROVED`. `exposure_minutes` is recorded and no risk points at all are added to the kiosk on this branch.
- When the pH is the *only* failing parameter and it is still at most 4.60 — the documentable ceiling — with both time and temperature in spec, the batch moves `ACIDIFYING → VARIANCE_REQUIRED`. `exposure_minutes` is recorded, and the batch may not be packed until a variance has been both documented and approved.
- When the time is the *only* failing parameter and the overrun is modest — `exposure_minutes` at most 300, which is the 240-minute limit plus a further hour — with pH and temperature both in spec, the batch again moves `ACIDIFYING → VARIANCE_REQUIRED` on the same terms.
- In every other case the batch moves `ACIDIFYING → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `TTC_HARD_FAIL`: a pH above the documentable ceiling, any temperature excursion at all, or more than one failing parameter simultaneously. This branch is not documentable — there is no variance path out of `REJECTED`, and `REJECTED` is terminal for a rice batch.

Filing the variance is a `FranchiseChef` act and it is gated on the chef's own paperwork: the filing chef must hold a `VALID` `HACCP_RICE_ACIDIFICATION` certification, and a chef without one is refused with `ERR_HACCP_CERT_EXPIRED`. The filing carries `documented_ph`, the `documented_hold_minutes` of ambient hold being documented, and a `corrective_narrative` describing what was done about it; the system records those three plus `variance_submitted_by_chef_id` from the acting chef and moves the batch `VARIANCE_REQUIRED → VARIANCE_PENDING`. Filing against a batch in any other state — an `APPROVED` batch, for instance — is `ERR_RICE_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`.

Adjudication belongs exclusively to the quality auditor, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with a `review_note`, and whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `variance_reviewed_by_user_id` for the audit trail on either outcome. Approval additionally requires the documented figures to stay inside the documentable band: `documented_ph` must be at most 4.60 and `documented_hold_minutes` must be at most 240, and a filing that breaches either is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_PH_ABOVE_CEILING`. An approval moves the batch `VARIANCE_PENDING → VARIANCE_APPROVED` and adds three risk points to the kiosk, after which its `sanitation_posture` is re-derived — documented deviations are tolerated but they are not free. A rejection moves the batch `VARIANCE_PENDING → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `VARIANCE_DENIED`, and no pack lot can ever be built from it.

Both `APPROVED` and `VARIANCE_APPROVED` are packable, and building a pack lot from either moves the batch to `CONSUMED`: a batch is spent by exactly one pack lot, and a second attempt against the same batch is refused because the batch is no longer approved. Long after that, a quality auditor may reach back into a consumed batch and revoke the variance it shipped on, supplying a `revocation_rationale`. That revert edge is available only when the batch is `CONSUMED` and only when it actually carries variance documentation, which is to say `documented_ph` is not null; revoking a variance on a batch that is not consumed returns `ERR_RICE_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. It moves the batch `CONSUMED → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `VARIANCE_REVOKED`, pulls every pack lot of that batch still `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` to `DISCARDED` with exactly one appended discard event per lot whose `reason` is `VARIANCE_REJECTED` and whose `discarded_count` equals that lot's `unsold_count` — discards which are pointedly *not* settlement-credit-eligible, because they arise from the franchisee's own compliance failure — makes the owning plan recompute `actual_pack_count` because those lots left the shelf, and removes the three variance risk points from the kiosk before re-deriving its posture. Batches also reach `REJECTED` by cascade: when a plan is voided, every one of its batches not already `REJECTED` or `CONSUMED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `PLAN_VOIDED`, and when the batch caught by that cascade was `APPROVED` its pack lots are discarded with `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` events in the same sweep.

### 3.6. Raw Fish: Sourcing, Parasite Destruction and the Cold Chain

A fish lot is received at a kiosk by the franchise chef, who records `kiosk_id`, the `species` — one of `TUNA_YELLOWFIN`, `SALMON_ATLANTIC`, `HAMACHI` or `SHRIMP_COOKED` — the `supplier_code` printed on the approved-supplier lot tag, the `harvest_method` claimed on the supplier certificate as either `WILD` or `AQUACULTURE_EXEMPT`, the strictly positive `net_weight_kg` at receiving, the `received_at` instant, and `receiving_temp_c`, the product temperature in Celsius measured at receiving. The lot begins in `RECEIVED` with `allocated_kg` 0, `cold_chain_breach_minutes` 0, `quarantine_reason` null and the whole freezing record — `destruction_method`, `freeze_start_at`, `freeze_end_at`, `freeze_min_temp_c`, `freeze_effective_hours` and `attested_by_chef_id` — still null. That receiving temperature is not merely descriptive: together with `received_at` it is the implicit first point of the lot's cold-chain series. Lots are readable individually or as a list narrowed by `kiosk_id`, `attested_by_chef_id`, `species` or `status`.

Whether the lot needs parasite destruction at all is decided at receiving, before any freeze arithmetic is attempted. A lot whose `harvest_method` is `AQUACULTURE_EXEMPT`, or whose `species` is `TUNA_YELLOWFIN` or `SHRIMP_COOKED`, has `parasite_destruction_required` false and `parasite_destruction_compliant` true by definition — vacuously satisfied, no freezing record needed. Every other lot has `parasite_destruction_required` true and `parasite_destruction_compliant` false until a satisfactory record is filed.

Filing that record means declaring a freezing regime and the data achieved under it: `destruction_method`, one of `FREEZE_MINUS20_7DAYS`, `FREEZE_MINUS35_15HRS` or `FREEZE_MINUS35_THEN_MINUS20_24HRS`; the `freeze_start_at` and `freeze_end_at` instants; `freeze_min_temp_c`, the lowest product temperature attained; and `attested_by_chef_id`, the chef attesting the record. `freeze_effective_hours` is the elapsed time between the two instants expressed in whole hours with any fractional hour discarded. The declared method then selects **both** thresholds, and the achieved data must satisfy the regime that was actually declared rather than merely some regime: `FREEZE_MINUS35_15HRS` demands a minimum temperature at or below −35.0 degrees Celsius held for at least 15 hours; `FREEZE_MINUS35_THEN_MINUS20_24HRS` demands the same −35.0 degrees but for at least 24 hours; and `FREEZE_MINUS20_7DAYS` demands a minimum temperature at or below −20.0 degrees Celsius held for at least 168 hours, which is the seven days its name refers to. Both comparisons are inclusive — exactly −20.0 degrees for exactly 168 hours under the seven-day regime is compliant, while 167 hours is not — and declaring the fifteen-hour method and then holding −22 degrees for two hundred hours is *not* compliant, because −22 never reaches the −35 the declared method claims. The outcome is written to `parasite_destruction_compliant`.

The cold chain is tracked by appending immutable readings to the lot, each carrying a `recorded_at` instant and the `temp_c` measured then. Readings are processed in `recorded_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the lot's own `received_at` with `receiving_temp_c` is the origin of the series. For each consecutive pair of points the system computes the gap in whole minutes and stores it on the later reading as `interval_minutes_from_previous`, which is null for the very first appended reading. That interval then contributes to the breach total under a three-way rule, with 4.4 degrees Celsius as the cold limit and a temperature exactly equal to 4.4 counting as compliant: if the temperature at *both* ends of the interval is above 4.4 the whole gap is attributed, since the product was out of temperature throughout; if exactly *one* end is above 4.4 the midpoint rule attributes half the gap, rounded half up to a whole minute; and if neither end is above 4.4 nothing is attributed. The attribution is stored on the reading as `breach_minutes_attributed`, which is 0 for the first reading, and the lot's `cold_chain_breach_minutes` becomes the sum of the per-interval attributions, echoed onto the reading as `lot_breach_minutes_after` so the running total is auditable point by point. The rounding order here is load-bearing: each interval is rounded to a whole minute *before* the intervals are summed, and summing the raw halves to round once at the end gives a different answer whenever two or more half-attributed intervals have odd gaps. Cold-chain readings are append-only evidence and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Crossing the limit quarantines the lot. A total strictly greater than 30 minutes moves the lot to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `COLD_CHAIN_BREACH` and adds six risk points to the kiosk before re-deriving its posture; a total of exactly 30 minutes does not quarantine. Quarantine is reachable from `RECEIVED` and from `RELEASED`, and the two branches differ in reach: a lot quarantined out of `RECEIVED` has produced nothing yet, whereas a lot quarantined out of `RELEASED` also drags its product off the shelf — every pack lot built from it that is `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` moves to `DISCARDED` with a discard event whose `reason` is `TEMP_ABUSE`, and because those lots left the shelf each affected plan recomputes `actual_pack_count`. Both branches add the same six points.

Release is the quality auditor's decision and it is where sourcing meets safety. Releasing a lot out of `RECEIVED` requires that parasite destruction is either not required or satisfied, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PARASITE_DESTRUCTION_INCOMPLETE`, and requires the accumulated `cold_chain_breach_minutes` to be at most 30, or `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BREACH_BLOCKS_RELEASE`. Releasing a lot out of `QUARANTINED` requires the same parasite-destruction condition plus an explicit `release_rationale` in the body — the auditor must say on the record why a breached lot is going back into production; without that rationale the release is refused with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BREACH_BLOCKS_RELEASE`. Release sets `remaining_kg` to `net_weight_kg` minus `allocated_kg`, clears `quarantine_reason`, and makes the lot eligible as a pack-lot fish source. Critically, release does **not** reset the accumulated breach minutes, so a single further breaching interval re-quarantines the lot immediately; the `RELEASED → QUARANTINED → RELEASED` cycle may repeat any number of times.

Destroying a lot takes a `destruction_rationale`, is available from `RECEIVED` or `QUARANTINED`, and sets `remaining_kg` to 0. `DESTROYED` is terminal: releasing a destroyed lot, or appending a cold-chain reading to one, returns `ERR_FISH_LOT_INVALID_STATE`, as does any other transition the lot's current state does not declare. The remaining two edges are mass-driven rather than operator-driven. `allocated_kg` is the sum of `consumed_fish_kg` over the lot's pack lots that are not `VOIDED`, and `remaining_kg` is `net_weight_kg` minus `allocated_kg` rounded half up to three decimal places, conserved against the net weight at all times. When an allocation drives `remaining_kg` to exactly zero the lot moves `RELEASED → CONSUMED` and is no longer selectable as a source; when a pack lot is voided and its mass is handed back, a lot in `CONSUMED` returns to `RELEASED` provided the restored `remaining_kg` is greater than zero. The net drift across an allocate-then-void round trip must be exactly zero.

### 3.7. Building, Marking Down and Discarding Retail Packs

A pack lot is the join of rice and fish into saleable product, and building one is the single most heavily gated write in the system. The franchise chef supplies `rice_batch_id`, `fish_lot_id`, the `sku_code` of the pack SKU, a strictly positive `pack_count`, a strictly positive `list_price_cents` full shelf price per pack, and the `produced_at` instant the packs were placed in the case. There is no `kiosk_id` in the body: the kiosk is inferred from the sources, which is precisely why they must agree. The gates, evaluated in the priority order of §2.6 so that a body failing several of them reports the highest-ranked complaint, are these.

- The rice batch must be `APPROVED` or `VARIANCE_APPROVED`; anything else, including `REJECTED`, `MIXING`, `ACIDIFYING`, `VARIANCE_REQUIRED`, `VARIANCE_PENDING` or an already-`CONSUMED` batch, is `ERR_RICE_BATCH_NOT_APPROVED`.
- The fish lot must be `RELEASED`; `RECEIVED`, `QUARANTINED`, `CONSUMED` and `DESTROYED` all yield `ERR_FISH_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`.
- The kiosk's `sanitation_posture` must not be `LOCKDOWN`, or the build is refused with `ERR_KIOSK_POSTURE_LOCKDOWN`. `CLEAR`, `WATCH` and `RESTRICTED` do not block a build; only `LOCKDOWN` does.
- The rice batch and the fish lot must resolve to the same kiosk — the batch through its plan, the lot directly — or the build is refused with `ERR_CROSS_KIOSK_REFERENCE`. Because safety outranks referential coherence, a body that is *both* backed by a rejected batch *and* crosses kiosks reports `ERR_RICE_BATCH_NOT_APPROVED`; both are 422, so only the error identifier distinguishes them.
- `pack_count` must not exceed the batch's `max_pack_yield`; equality is allowed and one pack more is `ERR_RICE_YIELD_EXCEEDED`.
- The packs live against the plan must not exceed the plan's `planned_pack_count`, and the kiosk's live pack total must not exceed its frozen `daily_pack_cap`; either overrun is `ERR_PACK_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
- The aggregate live pack count across every kiosk of the host store must not exceed the refrigerated case capacity implied by the attested frontage, which is `attested_case_linear_ft` multiplied by the same nine packs per linear foot and rounded down to a whole pack; exceeding it is `ERR_CASE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This is the store-wide sibling of the kiosk-level cap, and a single kiosk inside its own cap can still breach it.
- The fish mass the build would consume must not exceed the source lot's `remaining_kg`; equality is allowed, and asking for one gram more is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISH_INVENTORY`.

A lot that clears all of that starts in `ON_SHELF` with `sold_count` 0, `discarded_count` 0 and `unsold_count` equal to `pack_count`, both markdown prices and `markdown_variant_applied` still null. Its `shelf_expiry_at` is `produced_at` plus 720 minutes, the twelve hours a case-held sushi pack is allowed. Its `consumed_fish_kg` is `pack_count` multiplied by 0.045 kilograms of fish per pack, rounded half up to three decimal places, and that mass is immediately debited from the source lot as described in §3.6. The owning plan recomputes `actual_pack_count` in the same breath, so a plan's actual figure is live from the moment packs are built rather than deferred to reconciliation. Lots are readable individually — the single read returns both markdown prices and the full sold/discarded/unsold decomposition — or as a list narrowed by `rice_batch_id`, `fish_lot_id`, `sku_code` or `status`.

Marking down a lot is the chef's response to a slow afternoon. It requires the lot to be `ON_SHELF`, requires `unsold_count` to be greater than zero, and requires the logical clock to be strictly before `shelf_expiry_at` — a markdown at or after expiry is refused with `ERR_PACK_SHELF_LIFE_EXPIRED`, and marking down a lot that is already `MARKED_DOWN`, or is `SOLD_OUT`, `DISCARDED` or `VOIDED`, is `ERR_PACK_LOT_INVALID_STATE`. The transition takes no body and computes both markdown variants from the same inputs. First, `remaining_minutes` is the whole number of minutes from the logical clock to `shelf_expiry_at`, clamped into the range zero to 720. Then `floor_price_cents` is 30 percent of `list_price_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — the absolute floor no markdown may go beneath. The prorated variant is `list_price_cents` scaled by `remaining_minutes` divided by 720, rounded half up to whole cents, then raised to the floor if it fell below it. The flat-step variant is 50 percent of `list_price_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, then likewise raised to the floor, which for any positive list price it never is. The binding number is the variant named by `markdown_policy` on the license agreement reached from the lot through its rice batch, that batch's plan, the plan's kiosk and the kiosk's agreement; it is written to `markdown_price_cents`, the other variant is written to `advisory_markdown_price_cents`, and `markdown_variant_applied` records which policy was binding. The two variants cross over during the shelf life: with more than half the window left the prorated price is the higher of the two, and late in the window, pinned to the 30 percent floor, it is the lower. Only `markdown_price_cents` values packs for discard and for markdown sales; the advisory number never touches money.

Discarding disposes of whatever remains unsold. The actor — a franchise chef or a quality auditor — supplies only a `reason`, and the transition is available from `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` while `unsold_count` is greater than zero. Exactly one discard event is appended, never more: its `discarded_count` is the lot's `unsold_count` at that instant, its `unit_value_cents` is `markdown_price_cents` when the lot was `MARKED_DOWN` and `list_price_cents` otherwise, and its `discard_value_cents` is the product of the two. `settlement_credit_eligible` is true when the `reason` is `SHELF_LIFE_EXPIRED` or `TEMP_ABUSE` — ordinary spoilage and cold-chain loss, which the franchisor shares in — and false when it is `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` or `VARIANCE_REJECTED`, which arise from the franchisee's own compliance failure. Eligibility is a policy consequence of the reason alone and never of the discard's size. The event records `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting header for the audit trail and nothing else; it is immutable, so it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. The lot moves to `DISCARDED` with `discarded_count` set and `unsold_count` driven to zero, the owning plan recomputes `actual_pack_count`, and every `DRAFT` settlement statement whose period covers the discard date recomputes `period_discard_value_cents` and — only when the event was credit-eligible — `discard_credit_cents` and `net_due_cents` too. Discard events of a lot are listable, optionally narrowed by `reason`, and that nested list is the only place cascade-created events ever surface.

Selling is not an operator action on the lot at all: reconciled register lines drive `sold_count` up and `unsold_count` down, and a lot whose `unsold_count` reaches zero that way moves to `SOLD_OUT` from either `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN`. Voiding, by contrast, is a franchisor correction for packs that should never have been built: it takes a `void_reason`, is available only from `ON_SHELF`, and only while `sold_count` is exactly zero — a lot that has sold even one pack cannot be voided, and neither can a marked-down one. The void hands `consumed_fish_kg` back to the source lot exactly, decreasing its `allocated_kg` and increasing its `remaining_kg` by that precise figure and returning a `CONSUMED` source to `RELEASED`, and the owning plan recomputes `actual_pack_count`. `SOLD_OUT`, `DISCARDED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal. Throughout every one of these paths the conservation invariant holds without exception: `pack_count` always equals `sold_count` plus `discarded_count` plus `unsold_count`.

### 3.8. Register Feeds, Reconciliation and the Revenue-Share Settlement

Money enters the system through the host store's registers. The host store manager submits one feed per store and business date, carrying `host_store_id`, the `business_date` the totals belong to as a calendar date, `gross_sales_cents` for all sushi sales including markdown sales, `markdown_sales_cents` for the portion rung at a marked-down price, `full_price_units` and `markdown_units` as the unit split, and `lines`, an array of per-pack-lot register lines each holding a `pack_lot_id`, the `units_sold` against that lot and a `was_markdown` flag. The three money and unit totals may be zero, and `markdown_sales_cents` may not exceed `gross_sales_cents`. `lines` may be empty, because some host registers cannot attribute units to a lot; an empty array is a legitimate feed that still carries store-level totals. Only one feed may exist per store and date unless the earlier one was rejected: a second submission while a `SUBMITTED` or `RECONCILED` feed already covers that date is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEED_FOR_DATE`, while a `REJECTED` feed leaves the date open for resubmission. A feed starts in `SUBMITTED` with `reconciled_unit_total` 0 and `rejection_reason` null, and feeds are readable individually — including their lines — or as a list narrowed by `host_store_id`, `business_date` or `status`.

Reconciliation is the franchisor's acceptance of the extract and it is the moment register lines touch inventory. It takes no body and requires the feed to be `SUBMITTED`; a feed already reconciled or rejected cannot be either again, and the second attempt is `ERR_POS_FEED_INVALID_STATE`. It requires the host store's `pos_integration_status` to be `CONNECTED` — a store that never completed integration, or whose integration is disconnected, gets `ERR_POS_NOT_CONNECTED`. Every `pack_lot_id` in `lines` must resolve to a pack lot produced at a kiosk of this feed's own host store, and a line naming another store's product is `ERR_CROSS_KIOSK_REFERENCE`, the same identifier the pack-lot build uses for its own coherence failure. For every line the `units_sold` must not exceed that lot's current `unsold_count`. Reconciliation then sets `reconciled_unit_total` to the sum of `units_sold` across the lines, increments each referenced lot's `sold_count` and decrements its `unsold_count` by the same amount, moves any lot whose `unsold_count` reaches zero to `SOLD_OUT`, and makes the feed's store-level totals settleable. Rejecting a feed instead takes a `rejection_reason`, is likewise available only from `SUBMITTED`, and records that reason. Both `RECONCILED` and `REJECTED` are terminal. The asymmetry between them matters twice over: a rejected feed contributes exactly zero to any settlement, yet it still counts as a terminal feed for the purposes of the plan-reconciliation precondition in §3.4, so a bad register day does not strand its production plan forever.

Settling a period is a franchisor draft followed by a formal exchange with the store. The draft carries `license_agreement_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` calendar dates, and `expected_license_agreement_version`, the agreement version the caller last observed; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` per §2.7, and a successful create increments the agreement's `version`. Settlement periods are closed intervals on both ends, so two periods that merely share a single endpoint date do overlap, and a period overlapping any non-`VOID` statement of the same agreement is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A prior `DRAFT` statement for the same agreement and identical period is not an overlap but a supersede: it moves to `VOID` with a `void_reason` recorded, and the new statement records its identifier in `superseded_statement_id`. The new statement starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 1, `created_by_user_id` taken from the acting header, and `issued_by_user_id`, `dispute_reason`, `revision_note`, `void_reason`, `paid_amount_cents` and `paid_at` all null. Statements are readable individually or as a list narrowed by `license_agreement_id`, `status` or `superseded_statement_id`.

Every money field on the statement is derived, and the derivation runs in a pinned order that cannot be rearranged without changing the answer.

1. **Assemble the sales inputs.** Only feeds of the agreement's host store whose `status` is `RECONCILED` and whose `business_date` falls inside the closed period contribute. `gross_sales_cents` on the statement is the sum of the feeds' `gross_sales_cents` and `markdown_sales_cents` the sum of theirs; `SUBMITTED` and `REJECTED` feeds contribute nothing at all.
2. **Choose the settlement base.** Under the `GROSS_SALES` model `settlement_base_cents` is the full `gross_sales_cents`; under `NET_OF_MARKDOWN` it is `gross_sales_cents` minus `markdown_sales_cents`.
3. **Run the marginal tier ladder on that base.** The rates are marginal, not average: the first 2,000,000 cents of base earn 12 percent, the portion from 2,000,000 up to 5,000,000 cents earns 14 percent, and everything above 5,000,000 cents earns 16 percent. The three tier products are summed and only then rounded half up to whole cents — rounding each tier separately is wrong. The result is `binding_share_cents`. The identical ladder is then run a second time over the *other* model's base, and that result is `advisory_share_cents`; `variance_cents` is `binding_share_cents` minus `advisory_share_cents`, which is positive when the binding model is `GROSS_SALES` and negative when it is `NET_OF_MARKDOWN`. `binding_variant` records which model was binding. Only the binding number is billed; the advisory number is reporting.
4. **Compute the space-obligation credit.** The raw credit is the store's `space_shortfall_sqft` multiplied by the agreement's `space_credit_cents_per_sqft`. Its cap is 25 percent of `binding_share_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `space_shortfall_credit_cents` is the smaller of the two. A store attesting its full obligation has a zero shortfall and therefore a zero credit.
5. **Compute the discard credit.** `period_discard_value_cents` is the sum of `discard_value_cents` over *every* discard event whose date falls inside the period, eligible or not; it is disclosure only and is never netted against anything. The credit itself is computed from the eligible subset alone: 40 percent of the total `discard_value_cents` of events whose `settlement_credit_eligible` is true, rounded half up, capped at 10 percent of `binding_share_cents` also rounded half up, and `discard_credit_cents` is the smaller. An `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` or `VARIANCE_REJECTED` discard therefore raises `period_discard_value_cents` while leaving `discard_credit_cents` and `net_due_cents` untouched — an observable difference that distinguishes a compliance-driven loss from ordinary spoilage.
6. **Net the amount due.** `net_due_cents` is `binding_share_cents` less both credits, floored at zero, so credits can zero a bill but never turn it into a payment owed to the store. Both caps are keyed on `binding_share_cents`, the share *before* any credit is applied; keying the discard cap on the already-reduced amount produces a different figure and is wrong.

Issuing the statement freezes all of that. It requires the statement to be `DRAFT`, requires at least one `RECONCILED` feed inside the closed period — a period with no settleable sales is refused with `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_SALES` — and requires the issuing `X-User-ID` to differ from `created_by_user_id`, since the person who drafted a bill may not be the person who sends it; a self-issue is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Issue records `issued_by_user_id` and adds `binding_share_cents` to the agreement's `lifetime_share_accrued_cents`. Because state outranks fiscal checks, issuing a statement that is both already `VOID` *and* has no reconciled feeds in its period reports `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE` at 409, never the 422 fiscal complaint.

From `ISSUED` the host store manager either acknowledges or disputes, and ownership is enforced on both: a manager may only act on statements of their own store. Acknowledgement is a bare accept with no side effects beyond the state change. A dispute records `dispute_reason` and subtracts `binding_share_cents` back out of `lifetime_share_accrued_cents`, so a contested bill stops counting as accrued revenue while it is contested. The franchisor answers a dispute by revising, which returns the statement to `DRAFT`, increments `revision_round` by one, records `revision_note`, clears `issued_by_user_id` and recomputes every money field from the currently reconciled feeds and discard events — a revision genuinely re-derives, so feeds reconciled since the original draft are picked up. That `DRAFT → ISSUED → DISPUTED → DRAFT` cycle is bounded: a revision is only permitted while `revision_round` is below 3, and once it has reached that bound a further revision is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Revising a statement that is not `DISPUTED` is a state error, not a bound error.

Payment closes the statement from `ACKNOWLEDGED` only, and it takes `paid_amount_cents` together with the `paid_at` instant of remittance. The amount must equal `net_due_cents` exactly — under- and over-payment are equally refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, and a statement whose net due is zero must be paid with exactly zero. Paying an `ISSUED` statement that was never acknowledged, or a `DISPUTED` one, is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding is the franchisor's escape hatch and takes a `void_reason`; it is reachable from `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED`, but never from `PAID`, which along with `VOID` is terminal. When the voided statement had reached `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` — and so had booked an accrual that was not already reversed by a dispute — its `binding_share_cents` is subtracted from `lifetime_share_accrued_cents`, and the requirement is exactness: after any sequence of issue, dispute, revise, re-issue and void, the agreement's lifetime accrual must return to precisely the value it held before the statement was ever issued, with no drift.

### 3.9. Food Safety Audits, Corrective Action and Escalation to Closure

Audits are how the franchisor's risk picture of a kiosk gets refreshed, and they are the one workflow that can force a kiosk shut. A quality auditor schedules one against a `kiosk_id` for a `scheduled_for` calendar date. The audit starts in `SCHEDULED` with `round_count` 0, `critical_finding_count` 0, `open_finding_count` 0, a `score` of 100, no findings, and `opened_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id` and `closure_outcome` all null. Audits are readable individually — the single read shows the round count, score and closure outcome — or as a list narrowed by `kiosk_id` or `status`.

Starting the audit opens it for finding entry, requires the logical clock to be at or after `scheduled_for` — starting early is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_YET_DUE`, while starting exactly on the scheduled date is allowed — and records `opened_by_user_id` from the acting header. That recorded identity is what the later segregation-of-duties check compares against, so it matters who starts an audit, not merely who schedules it. Findings may only be appended while the audit is `IN_PROGRESS`; appending to a `SCHEDULED` or already-submitted audit is `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`.

Each finding carries a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, a `finding_code` from the standard checklist, and a `description` narrative. Its `audit_id` comes from the path and never from the body. Severity alone fixes the finding's `risk_points`: one point for `MINOR`, four for `MAJOR`, ten for `CRITICAL`. The finding starts `OPEN`, increments the audit's `open_finding_count`, and when it is `CRITICAL` also increments the kiosk's `open_critical_finding_count`, which is the field behind the posture override of §3.3. Findings are listable per audit, optionally narrowed by `severity` or `status`.

Submitting the audit takes no body and computes three fields from every finding recorded, regardless of each one's status: `critical_finding_count` is the number with `CRITICAL` severity, `open_finding_count` is the number still `OPEN`, and `score` is 100 less two points per `MINOR`, eight per `MAJOR` and twenty per `CRITICAL`, floored at zero. Note that the score's deduction weights are deliberately *not* the same numbers as a finding's `risk_points`: the score is a hundred-point compliance grade with 2/8/20 weights, while the kiosk's risk total uses the 1/4/10 weights, and confusing the two produces two wrong numbers at once. The score is frozen at submit and later corrections never raise it. Submission then branches on whether anything was found at all.

- With zero findings the audit goes straight from `IN_PROGRESS` to `CLOSED` with `closure_outcome` `PASS`, skipping the corrective-action branch entirely. The kiosk's `consecutive_clean_audits` increments by one, which subtracts a further two points from its risk inputs, and its `sanitation_posture` is re-derived — a clean audit is the only thing that pushes a kiosk's risk *down* on its own.
- With at least one finding the audit moves `IN_PROGRESS → CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PENDING`. The severity-weighted finding points join the kiosk's risk inputs, `consecutive_clean_audits` resets to zero — so a single finding forfeits the whole accumulated clean-audit credit, not one audit's worth of it — and the posture is re-derived. If any `CRITICAL` finding was recorded the posture is forced to `LOCKDOWN` irrespective of the risk total, which is the non-monotone override of §3.3 and can take a kiosk from `CLEAR` to `LOCKDOWN` in one step.

Remediation is the store's job. The host store manager records a `correction_note` against an individual finding, moving it `OPEN → CORRECTED` and decrementing the audit's `open_finding_count`; this is only available while the finding is `OPEN` and the audit is `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PENDING`, and a finding that is already `CORRECTED`, `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED` yields `ERR_FINDING_INVALID_STATE`. Crucially, correcting a finding does *not* remove its `risk_points` from the kiosk: a `CORRECTED` finding still counts as risk until a reverification actually passes, so a store cannot lift its own posture by filing paperwork. The quality auditor may instead waive a finding outright with a `waiver_rationale`, which moves it `OPEN → WAIVED`, decrements `open_finding_count`, and *does* remove its `risk_points` from the kiosk with an immediate posture re-derivation. Waivers are restricted to trivia: only `MINOR` findings are waivable, and attempting to waive a `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` finding is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_WAIVABLE`. `VERIFIED` and `WAIVED` are terminal for a finding.

Once nothing is left `OPEN` the host store manager requests reverification. Every finding of the audit must be `CORRECTED` or `WAIVED`; a single still-`OPEN` finding blocks the request with `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_BLOCK_REVERIFICATION`. The request moves the audit `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PENDING → REVERIFICATION` and increments `round_count` by one, so the first request makes it 1.

Verification is the quality auditor's judgement and takes an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` plus a `verification_note`. It is available only from `REVERIFICATION`, and the verifying `X-User-ID` must differ from `opened_by_user_id` — the auditor who opened an audit may never be the one who signs off its remediation, and a self-verification is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The three outcomes are these.

- `PASS` moves every `CORRECTED` finding of the audit to `VERIFIED` and removes each one's `risk_points` from the kiosk, decrementing `open_critical_finding_count` for each `CRITICAL` finding cleared. The audit moves to `CLOSED` with `closure_outcome` `PASS_AFTER_CORRECTION` — distinguishable from a first-time clean `PASS` — and records `verified_by_user_id`. The kiosk's posture is then re-derived from the reduced total, which is how a kiosk in `LOCKDOWN` with no remaining open critical findings returns to `RESTRICTED`, `WATCH` or `CLEAR`. A `PASS` does not touch `consecutive_clean_audits`: passing after correction earns no clean-audit credit.
- `FAIL` while `round_count` is still below the two-round corrective budget returns every `CORRECTED` finding to `OPEN`, clearing each one's `correction_note` and re-incrementing `open_finding_count`, re-applies their `risk_points` to the kiosk and re-derives the posture, and returns the audit to `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PENDING`. This is the corrective cycle, and it may run exactly once: with `round_count` starting at 0, the first request makes it 1, a failure at 1 sends the audit back for another round, and the second request makes it 2.
- `FAIL` when `round_count` has reached 2 is the end of the road. The audit moves to `ESCALATED_CLOSURE` with `closure_outcome` `ESCALATED` and `verified_by_user_id` recorded, and it drives the full four-link cascade of §2.10: the audited kiosk moves `OPERATING → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `AUDIT_ESCALATION` and — uniquely among the ways a kiosk gets suspended — `sanitation_posture` forced to `LOCKDOWN`; its plans in `DRAFT`, `RELEASED` or `LOCKED` move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `KIOSK_SUSPENDED`; every pack lot of those plans' rice batches that is `ON_SHELF` or `MARKED_DOWN` moves to `DISCARDED` with a discard event whose `reason` is `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` while those batches not already `REJECTED` or `CONSUMED` move to `REJECTED`; and every `DRAFT` settlement statement covering the discard date recomputes `period_discard_value_cents`, `discard_credit_cents` and `net_due_cents` — with the last two in fact unchanged, because an `AUDIT_LOCKDOWN` discard is not credit-eligible. The asymmetry is the point: the cascade reaches all four links, but the money moves at only three of them.

`CLOSED` and `ESCALATED_CLOSURE` are both terminal. Requesting reverification of an escalated audit is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, the same identifier the settlement revision bound uses; verifying an audit that is not in `REVERIFICATION`, or submitting one that was never started, is `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`; and because state is checked before every business rule, a terminal-state target always reports the state error rather than a business complaint.
