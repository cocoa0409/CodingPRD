# Residence Life & RA Staffing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university residence life office runs two operations that look separate on an org chart and are hopelessly entangled in practice. One is a housing operation: halls, floors, rooms, beds, the students who occupy them, the inspections that record what condition a room was left in, and the bills that follow. The other is a staffing operation: recruiting resident assistants out of the student body, training and certifying them, and rostering them onto an on-call duty rotation that has to be covered every night of the term. The entanglement is the point. A resident assistant is also a resident. The student who files a room-change request may be the same person whose conduct case is pending. A hall that closes for a burst pipe simultaneously voids break-housing stays, forces relocations, freezes floor programming budgets and cancels scheduled inspections. Today all of this is coordinated by email threads, a shared spreadsheet per hall, and the institutional memory of whichever professional staff member happens to be on duty.

This document specifies the API that replaces that coordination layer. The system is mounted under `/residence-life` and is organised around fourteen resource types that form a graph rather than a tree: a housing axis running from residence halls down through floors and rooms to the residents who occupy beds, a staffing axis running from an RA application through the RA record to individual duty shifts, and four case types — incident reports and their conduct cases, room inspections and their damage charges, room-change cases, and break-housing requests — that bind the two axes together. Two resources carry two independent lifecycle dimensions at once: an RA has an employment standing and a training standing that advance separately, and a room-change case has a mediation track and a placement track that advance separately. Much of the difficulty in this domain lies in knowing which dimension a given rule reads.

Three commitments shape everything that follows. First, **every operation is auditable**: each state change is attributable to a named actor and every derived figure — a damage assessment, a duty-load score, a floor's remaining programming budget — is reproducible from the records that fed it. Second, **money and beds are conserved quantities**: a compensating action such as waiving a charge, cancelling an approved program or reverting a completed room move must restore the affected balance to exactly the value it held before the forward action, never to an approximation of it. Third, **time is a server-controlled input, not an ambient fact**: every deadline, window, depreciation year and rotation lookahead reads a logical clock the operator can set, so the same request sequence produces the same figures on any day of the year.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, empty, or `X-User-Role` names something outside the role set below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-formed caller whose role is not among those permitted for the endpoint receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Exactly five roles exist:

- **`ResidenceDirector`** — professional residence life staff. The director owns the housing inventory, RA hiring decisions, the duty rotation, incident triage, roommate mediation and the floor programming budget. A director may neither adjudicate conduct nor bill damage.
- **`ResidentAssistant`** — student staff assigned to a floor. An RA files incident reports, inspects rooms, proposes floor programs and works the duty rotation.
- **`ConductOfficer`** — the student conduct office. Conduct officers schedule and decide conduct cases, and nothing else beyond reading.
- **`BursarOfficer`** — the student accounts office. The bursar owns the damage ledger: billing, appeal resolution, settlement and waiver. A bursar may not inspect rooms or triage incidents.
- **`Student`** — a resident. Students apply for RA positions, open room-change cases, appeal damage charges and request break housing.

Two of these roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those the value of `X-User-ID` is not decorative. When `X-User-Role` is `ResidentAssistant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RA` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an RA compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — specifically `IncidentReport.filed_by_ra_id`, `RoomInspection.inspector_ra_id` and `DutyShift.assigned_ra_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student` record, and ownership is checked against `RAApplication.applicant_student_id`, `DamageCharge.student_id` and `RoomChangeCase.student_id`. A mismatch on any of these is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Where an operation reads the acting RA's own record rather than comparing a stored foreign key — filing an incident report, scheduling an inspection and proposing a program — an `X-User-ID` that names no `RA` row is refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). An ownership comparison whose owning foreign key is still null is skipped rather than failed, leaving the state check to decide.

The other three roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ResidenceDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for it — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an application under review, as `routed_by_user_id` on a report sent to conduct, as `decided_by_user_id` on a break-housing decision, and as `opened_by_user_id` on a room-change case — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ConductOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `scheduled_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a conduct case and compared only against *itself* across those two fields, never against an entity id. When `X-User-Role` is `BursarOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server may record but does not compare to any entity's `id`.

Ownership enforcement applies to a specific, closed list of operations: submitting an RA application, responding to an offer on one, appealing a damage charge, submitting an incident report, recording an inspection completion, requesting a duty swap, and the check-in and complete outcomes on a duty shift. Elsewhere, role membership alone governs access.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. An optional `reason` or `note` accepted by an action is validated as a non-empty string when it is supplied, but it is persisted and echoed back only where §3 names the field it is stored into.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a value of either that is not an integer at all, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Collections are always sorted by `created_at` ascending, so paging through a collection with a stable dataset visits every row exactly once. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before the page window was taken, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, which combine conjunctively with each other and with pagination. Halls may be filtered by `posture`. Floors accept `building_id` and `programming_compliance_status`. Rooms accept `floor_id` and `status`. Residents accept `current_room_id`, `conduct_standing` and `housing_status`. RA applications accept `applicant_student_id`, `created_ra_id`, `status` and `term_code`. RA records accept `student_id`, `application_id`, `assigned_floor_id`, `employment_status` and `certification_status`. Duty shifts accept `floor_id`, `assigned_ra_id`, `swap_requested_ra_id` and `status`. Incident reports accept `filed_by_ra_id`, `room_id`, `subject_student_id`, `conduct_case_id` and `status`. Conduct cases accept `incident_report_id`, `respondent_student_id` and `status`. Room inspections accept `room_id`, `inspector_ra_id`, `responsible_student_id`, `damage_charge_id` and `status`. Damage charges accept `inspection_id`, `student_id` and `status`. Room-change cases accept `student_id`, `roommate_student_id`, `from_room_id`, `to_room_id`, `reverted_from_room_id`, `superseded_case_id`, `placement_status` and `mediation_status`. Program events accept `floor_id`, `organizer_ra_id`, `category` and `status`. Break-housing requests accept `student_id`, `room_id`, `break_code` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource in this system carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply one, and an `id` string reveals nothing about the resource type it names. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written; `created_at` and `updated_at` are stamped from the server's wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.5, which governs business evaluations alone.

All fourteen resource types additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly one on each subsequent mutation. There are no version-less resources in this domain: `Building`, `Floor`, `Room`, `Student`, `RAApplication`, `RA`, `DutyShift`, `IncidentReport`, `ConductCase`, `RoomInspection`, `DamageCharge`, `RoomChangeCase`, `ProgramEvent` and `BreakHousingRequest` all expose the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

The version counter is deliberately sensitive to indirect change. When an operation on one resource cascades into another — a hall closure that takes rooms offline, a conduct sanction that revokes a break stay, a placement completion that shifts bed counts between two rooms — every resource actually written by that cascade has its own `version` incremented and its own `updated_at` refreshed. A resource whose stored values are recomputed to the same numbers is not considered written.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this API reads the wall clock for business purposes. Duty-shift windows, conduct hearing deadlines, damage-charge appeal deadlines, the depreciation year applied to a damaged room, the twenty-eight-day rotation lookahead, break-stay date comparisons and the term boundary all read a server-held logical clock. Before any operator sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which is also the term start instant used by the backward-looking duty-load figure. An administrative capability lets a `ResidenceDirector` move the clock to a supplied instant, passed as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. Moving the clock mutates no entity — it changes only what subsequent window, deadline and depreciation evaluations see.

The clock may be moved forwards or backwards. Implementations must not memoise a derived figure that reads the clock; the depreciation rate on a damage charge, for instance, is computed from the clock at the moment of billing, and re-billing after the clock moved would legitimately produce a different rate.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that names a missing resource determines the error. A request naming a floor inside a hall that does not exist reports the hall's absence, never the floor's. Each resource type has its own not-found code: `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLOOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDUCT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_CHANGE_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BREAK_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

The same codes cover references that arrive in a request body rather than in the path. Any of `floor_id`, `room_id`, `from_room_id`, `student_id`, `ra_id`, `replacement_ra_id`, `to_room_id`, `roommate_student_id`, `applicant_student_id` and `responsible_student_id` that fails to resolve to an existing row produces the not-found code for the type it should have named, also HTTP 404. Body-level resolution happens after the request has been checked for shape, so a payload that is both malformed and points at a missing row reports the malformation.

### 2.7. Order of Evaluation

Because a single request can fail several ways at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in ascending priority and the first failure wins; later checks never run.

1. **Headers present.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of the five declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permitted.** The caller's role must be listed for the endpoint, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Ownership.** For the entity-backed roles acting on their own resources, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning foreign key, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence.** Every resource named by a path segment must exist, checked left to right.
5. **Request shape.** Every required input field must be present; enum-typed fields must name a member of their declared set; integers must fall inside their declared ranges; datetimes must parse as ISO 8601 and dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`; a duty shift's `end_at` must be strictly later than its `start_at` with a duration of at most 24 hours; a break stay's `end_date` must be on or after its `start_date` with a span of at most 45 days; and pagination arguments must satisfy the bounds in §2.3. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. **Body reference existence.** Foreign keys supplied in the body must resolve, per §2.6.
7. **Stale-view rejection.** Where a version expectation is required, it must match — see §2.8.
8. **State legality.** The target resource must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, otherwise the state code for that resource type (HTTP 409).
9. **Round caps.** Cyclic workflows must not exceed their permitted number of rounds.
10. **Cross-entity gates**, in this order: hall posture, then target-room availability, then the RA safety checks (certification before employment standing), then segregation of duties, self-report prohibition, judicial hold and probation, then break-housing eligibility.
11. **Temporal windows** — duty coverage conflict first, then the shift clock window, then the hearing window, then the appeal window.
12. **Aggregate and uniqueness constraints** — break-housing capacity, uniqueness of hall codes, resident emails, floor numbers, room numbers and applications; then the auto-fill candidate pool; then the consolidation preconditions.
13. **Referential coherence** — floor coherence between an RA and the shift, room or program they touch, then the room-change origin coherence.
14. **Fiscal constraints** — programming budget allocation and availability at approval, then the reimbursement overrun check.

This ladder matters most where two failures could plausibly describe the same request. A duty assignment naming an RA who is neither certified nor free of a conflicting shift reports `ERR_RA_NOT_CERTIFIED`, because the certification check sits above the coverage-conflict check. An assignment naming an uncertified, inactive RA reports `ERR_RA_NOT_CERTIFIED` rather than `ERR_RA_NOT_ACTIVE`, because certification is checked immediately before employment standing. A placement approval for a student who is both on judicial hold and targeting a room in a closed hall reports `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN`, because the posture gate sits above the student-condition gates.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection

Two creation operations write into a shared parent whose recent history the client must have observed. Both use an explicit version expectation rather than any timing-based mechanism.

Scheduling a room inspection requires `expected_room_version` as a mandatory field of the request, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Room`. If that value does not equal the room's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because scheduling an inspection supersedes any prior scheduled inspection of the same room and type: a client whose view of the room predates another writer's change would silently discard an inspection it never saw.

Opening a room-change case requires `expected_student_version` as a mandatory field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Student`. A mismatch with the student's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and a successful open advances the student's own `version` by one, exactly as scheduling an inspection advances the room's. The reasoning is the same: opening a case supersedes and cancels the student's prior requested case, and a client working from a stale view of the student would cancel a case it never observed.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version expectation. Requests to those endpoints neither require nor honour one.

### 2.9. Money, Rounding and Ledger Discipline

All monetary quantities in this system are integer counts of cents; there are no fractional-cent balances anywhere. Wherever a rate is applied to produce a monetary figure, the result is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up — a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero.

**Where the rounding happens is as much a part of the specification as the rate itself.** Two calculations apply a rate line by line, and in both the rounding is applied to each line before the lines are summed; rounding the sum instead of the lines produces a different total and is incorrect. The single non-monetary rounding in the system is the hall vacancy rate, which is carried to four decimal places, also round-half-up.

Three actions in this domain are **compensating**: waiving a damage charge that has already been billed, cancelling a program event whose budget was already encumbered, and reverting a room move that has already completed. Each must restore the affected balance to exactly the value it held before the corresponding forward action — the identical number of cents, the identical bed count — never a freshly-estimated figure. Where a forward action added a computed amount, the compensating action removes that same stored amount rather than recomputing it from current inputs.

### 2.10. Parallel Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Two resource types track two lifecycles at once, and the operations that drive them are strictly segregated. An `RA` carries `employment_status` — one of `PROVISIONAL`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `SEPARATED` — alongside `certification_status`, one of `UNCERTIFIED`, `IN_TRAINING`, `CERTIFIED`, `LAPSED`. An action on one dimension never writes the other: suspending an RA leaves their certification exactly as it was, and letting a certification lapse leaves their employment exactly as it was. It is entirely legitimate for an RA to be `ACTIVE` while `UNCERTIFIED`, or `CERTIFIED` while `SUSPENDED`.

A `RoomChangeCase` behaves the same way, carrying `mediation_status` — `NOT_REQUIRED`, `INTAKE`, `SESSION_SCHEDULED`, `AGREEMENT_REACHED`, `IMPASSE` — alongside `placement_status`, one of `NONE`, `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED`, `DENIED`, `CANCELLED`, `REVERTED`. Mediation actions never write `placement_status` and placement actions never write `mediation_status`. Precondition reads, however, do cross: a placement request on a conflict-origin case reads the mediation dimension before it will proceed.

Cascades are executed to completion within the request that triggers them, and every resource they touch is visible in its post-cascade state to the very next read. There is no deferred or eventually-consistent settlement anywhere in this API. Where a cascade is described in §3 as a numbered chain of links, each link fires for every row the previous link produced, and each link's writes bump `version` and `updated_at` on the rows it wrote.

### 2.11. Hall Posture as a System-Wide Gate

Each residence hall carries a `posture` that is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `EMERGENCY_CLOSED`, and it gates operations across the whole graph beneath it. Six families of operation require the owning hall to be `OPEN`: placing a resident into a room, deciding a room-change placement, completing a room-change placement, deciding a break-housing request, driving break-housing occupancy, and running a consolidation. Attempting any of these while the hall is `RESTRICTED` or `EMERGENCY_CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The gate reads the hall that owns the affected room or floor, which for a room means walking from the room to its floor to its hall.

`RESTRICTED` is the intermediate posture: it stops new placements into the hall's rooms but touches no existing occupancy, no break stay and no programming budget. `EMERGENCY_CLOSED` is the disruptive one, and its cascade is described in §3.9.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Housing Inventory

Everything begins with a residence hall. A `ResidenceDirector` creates one by supplying its display `name`, a short `code` and the `campus_zone` it sits in, optionally declaring `break_housing_enabled` — a boolean that defaults to false and determines whether the hall will accept break-housing requests at all. The `code` is an uppercase alphanumeric string of two to eight characters and must be unique across all halls; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUILDING_CODE`. The `campus_zone` is one of `NORTH`, `SOUTH` or `WEST`. A newly created hall opens at `posture` `OPEN` with `closure_reason` and `last_posture_change_at` both null, a `bed_capacity` of 0 and an `assigned_bed_count` of 0.

Those last two figures are maintained by the system and never supplied. A hall's `bed_capacity` is the sum of the bed capacities of every room on every floor of that hall, counted regardless of what status those rooms are in — an offline room still contributes its beds to the hall's physical capacity. Its `assigned_bed_count` is the number of residents whose current room lies somewhere in that hall, and it is the conserved quantity that the placement-revert and consolidation invariants are stated against.

Floors are created inside a hall by naming the `building_id` and the `floor_number`, an ordinal between 1 and 30 that must be unique within its hall; a duplicate draws `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLOOR_NUMBER`. The creator may also set `required_programs_per_term`, an integer between 1 and 12 that defaults to 4 and states how many programs the floor must actually deliver in a term to be considered compliant. A fresh floor starts with all three of its programming buckets at zero — `programming_budget_cents`, `programming_encumbered_cents` and `programming_spent_cents` — with `programming_available_cents` therefore also zero, `programming_frozen` false, `held_program_count` zero, `programming_compliance_status` at `PENDING`, `uncovered_shift_count` zero, `coverage_status` at `NORMAL`, and `bed_capacity` and `occupied_beds` both zero. The floor's `bed_capacity` is the sum of the bed capacities of its rooms and its `occupied_beds` the sum of their occupancies.

Rooms are created on a floor by naming the `floor_id`, a `room_number` unique among that floor's rooms, a `bed_capacity` between 1 and 4, and a `last_renovation_year` between 1950 and 2100 — the year that later drives damage depreciation. A duplicate room label on the same floor is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_NUMBER`. The new room is `AVAILABLE` with `occupied_beds` at zero, `pre_closure_status` null, `condition_score` at 100 and `break_hold_count` at zero, and both the parent floor's and the hall's `bed_capacity` are rebuilt to include it. A room's `condition_score` always mirrors the score recorded by its most recent completed inspection — most recent by completion instant, and where two share a completion instant, by the later creation instant — and reads 100 while the room has never been inspected. Its `break_hold_count` is the number of break-housing requests against that room that are either approved or checked in.

Residents are created with a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across all residents (a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_EMAIL`), a `class_year` of `FIRST_YEAR`, `SOPHOMORE`, `JUNIOR` or `SENIOR`, and optionally the bursar-verified `financial_hardship_flag`, which defaults to false and later unlocks the damage relief branch. A new resident is unhoused and in good standing: `current_room_id` null, `housing_status` `UNASSIGNED`, `conduct_standing` `GOOD`, `conduct_points` zero, `judicial_hold` false, `break_housing_eligible` true and `damage_balance_cents` zero.

Initial occupancy is set by a director through a room-assignment operation carrying an `action` of either `PLACE` or `VACATE`. A `PLACE` also carries the `room_id` of the destination. Placement requires the target room to be `AVAILABLE` with `occupied_beds` strictly below its `bed_capacity`, and the owning hall to be `OPEN`; a room that is `OFFLINE` or `CONSOLIDATED` draws `ERR_ROOM_NOT_AVAILABLE` and a room already at its bed capacity draws `ERR_ROOM_AT_CAPACITY`. On success the resident's `current_room_id` is set and `housing_status` becomes `ASSIGNED`, the room's `occupied_beds` rises by one and the room moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED`, and both the floor's `occupied_beds` and the hall's `assigned_bed_count` are recomputed. A `PLACE` naming a resident who already occupies a room is not refused: the resident is moved, and the room they left is re-synchronised, falling back to `AVAILABLE` when the departure leaves it empty. A `VACATE` reverses this: `current_room_id` returns to null, `housing_status` returns to `UNASSIGNED`, the room's `occupied_beds` falls by one, and when the departing resident was the room's only occupant — that is, when the room held exactly one occupied bed — the room moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE`. Floor and hall aggregates are recomputed on this path too. A `VACATE` naming a resident who occupies no room is accepted and simply changes nothing.

A director can also take a room out of service directly, outside any hall-wide event, by supplying a `target_status` of `OFFLINE` or `AVAILABLE` together with an optional `reason`. Taking a room offline is permitted from either `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` — a room already `OFFLINE`, or one that is `CONSOLIDATED`, is refused with `ERR_ROOM_NOT_AVAILABLE` — and on this director-driven path `pre_closure_status` is explicitly left null — the room will not be restored automatically later, because there is no hall reopening to restore it. Going offline from either `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` also revokes every approved or checked-in break stay for that room — stamping `revocation_cause` `EMERGENCY_CLOSURE` and opening for each affected resident the same `EMERGENCY_RELOCATION` room-change case at `placement_status` `REQUESTED`, with `opened_by_user_id` `system` and the resident's `housing_status` set to `RELOCATION_PENDING` — and cancels every scheduled inspection of it, exactly as the hall-wide cascade does. Returning a room to `AVAILABLE` on the director-driven path requires the room to currently be `OFFLINE`, any other status drawing `ERR_ROOM_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and to have no occupants at all — `occupied_beds` must be zero, a still-occupied room drawing `ERR_ROOM_AT_CAPACITY` — and clears `pre_closure_status`. A room that has reached `CONSOLIDATED` is terminal and no status action returns it to service.

Programming money enters a floor through a budget allocation carrying an `amount_cents` — a required integer of zero or more, with no upper bound — and the `term_code` it belongs to, a required non-empty string that is validated and then recorded nowhere on the floor. The allocated cents are added to `programming_budget_cents`, and because they are neither encumbered nor spent, they land entirely in the available bucket. Allocation is purely additive; it never retires an existing encumbrance.

### 3.2. Recruiting the Resident Assistant Corps

An RA application belongs to one applicant and one hiring term. It is opened by naming the `applicant_student_id`, the `term_code` — a four-digit year followed by `FA`, `SP` or `SU`, as in `2026FA` — and an `essay_ref` handle pointing at the applicant's essay packet. A given applicant may hold only one application per term; a second attempt at the same pairing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION`. The application is born in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at zero, `interview_score`, `revision_notes`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `created_ra_id` all null. Both the applicant themselves and a director may open the application, but only the applicant may move it forward from there.

Submission takes the application from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, optionally replacing `essay_ref` on the way, and requires that the essay reference be non-empty — an empty one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and that the caller be the applicant named on the record. The submit action stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock instant. A director then opens review by recording an `interview_score` — a number from 0 to 100 carried to one decimal place — which takes the application `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing director's identifier.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the director issues one of three decisions. `REQUEST_REVISION` sends the application back to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_count` by one and stores the supplied `revision_notes`; it is permitted only while `revision_count` is strictly below the two-round revision cap, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. `OFFER` moves the application to `OFFERED` and is permitted only once an `interview_score` has actually been recorded; a missing score is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. `REJECT` moves it to `REJECTED`, which is terminal. An application in `REVISION_REQUESTED` returns to `SUBMITTED` when its applicant submits again, closing the cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; the second submit re-stamps `submitted_at`.

The applicant closes the loop with a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`. `DECLINE` moves the application to `DECLINED` and ends the matter. `ACCEPT` moves it to `ACCEPTED` and synthesizes the staffing record: a new `RA` row is written whose `student_id` is the applicant, whose `application_id` points back at this application, and which starts with `employment_status` `PROVISIONAL` and `certification_status` `UNCERTIFIED`, `assigned_floor_id` null, `training_credits_earned` at zero, an empty `completed_module_codes` list, `certified_at` null, `duty_points_balance` and `missed_shift_count` at zero, and `separation_reason` null. The application records the new record's identifier as `created_ra_id`. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` and `REJECTED` are the three terminal states of the application machine, and any submit, review, decision or response attempted against a state from which it is not declared is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. Training, Certification and Employment — Two Dimensions, One Person

A newly hired RA can do nothing until a director assigns them to the floor they will staff, which writes `assigned_floor_id` from a supplied `floor_id` that must resolve. That assignment is accepted from any employment or certification standing and may be repeated to move the RA to another floor. That assignment is a prerequisite for the employment dimension advancing at all: activating an RA — moving `employment_status` from `PROVISIONAL` to `ACTIVE` — is refused with `ERR_RA_STATE_INVALID` while `assigned_floor_id` is still null, since an RA with no floor cannot cover a rotation, inspect a room or propose a program.

The training dimension runs on its own track. Starting training moves `certification_status` from `UNCERTIFIED` to `IN_TRAINING`, and the same action moves a `LAPSED` RA back to `IN_TRAINING`, which is what closes the recertification cycle `CERTIFIED → LAPSED → IN_TRAINING → CERTIFIED`. Individual modules are logged one at a time by a director supplying a `module_code`, which must be one of `CRISIS`, `TITLE_IX`, `FIRE_SAFETY`, `MEDIATION`, `DIVERSITY` or `PROGRAMMING`, and the module is only loggable while the RA is `IN_TRAINING`; logging against any other certification state is `ERR_RA_STATE_INVALID`. The code is appended to `completed_module_codes` and the module's credit weight is added to `training_credits_earned`. **Credits are never supplied by the caller** — the catalogue is fixed at five credits for `CRISIS`, four for `TITLE_IX`, two for `FIRE_SAFETY`, three for `MEDIATION`, one for `DIVERSITY` and two for `PROGRAMMING`. Because a module already present in `completed_module_codes` would double-count its credits, logging a repeat is refused with `ERR_MODULE_ALREADY_COMPLETED`.

Certifying an RA takes `certification_status` from `IN_TRAINING` to `CERTIFIED` and stamps `certified_at` with the logical clock. Two independent conditions gate it. The RA must have accumulated at least twelve training credits; the comparison is inclusive, so exactly twelve certifies, and anything below draws `ERR_TRAINING_CREDITS_INSUFFICIENT`. The RA must also have completed all three mandatory modules — `CRISIS`, `TITLE_IX` and `FIRE_SAFETY` — and a missing one draws `ERR_MANDATORY_MODULE_MISSING`. Because the three mandatory modules together carry only eleven credits, every certification requires at least one elective on top of them. The credit check is evaluated before the mandatory-module check, so an RA short on both reports the insufficient-credit code. Letting a certification lapse moves `CERTIFIED → LAPSED`; it explicitly does **not** disturb shifts the RA already holds, and only new assignments and swap approvals are then refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

Employment moves independently through `ACTIVATE`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` and `SEPARATE`. Suspension takes an `ACTIVE` RA to `SUSPENDED` and reinstatement brings them back; neither writes the certification dimension. Separation is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, records the supplied `reason` as `separation_reason`, and is terminal — `SEPARATED` admits no further employment action, and an attempt draws `ERR_RA_STATE_INVALID`.

Separation is the point where the staffing axis reaches out and touches the rotation, in a chain of three links. First, every duty shift held by the separating RA that is still in the future — that is, whose `start_at` is strictly later than the logical clock — and whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `SWAP_PENDING` returns to `OPEN`, with both `assigned_ra_id` and `swap_requested_ra_id` cleared. Shifts already `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `VOID` are untouched, as are shifts whose start has already passed. Second, every floor that owned one of those re-opened shifts has its `uncovered_shift_count` recomputed as the number of shifts on that floor that are `OPEN` and whose `start_at` is at or after the logical clock. Third, any floor whose recomputed count reaches two or more flips `coverage_status` to `CRITICAL`; below two it reads `NORMAL`. This threshold is the floor-wide rule and applies wherever `uncovered_shift_count` is recomputed, not only on separation: a floor reads `CRITICAL` exactly when its `uncovered_shift_count` is two or more and `NORMAL` otherwise. Each of these three links writes real rows, so each affected shift and each affected floor has its own `version` incremented.

### 3.4. Covering the Duty Rotation

A duty shift is an on-call block belonging to one floor. A director creates it by naming the `floor_id`, a `shift_type` of `WEEKDAY`, `WEEKEND` or `BREAK`, and the half-open coverage interval `start_at` (inclusive) and `end_at` (exclusive). The end must be strictly later than the start and the block may not exceed 24 hours. The shift's `points_value` is derived from its type and never supplied: one point for a `WEEKDAY` block, three for a `WEEKEND` block and five for a `BREAK` block. The new shift is `OPEN` with `assigned_ra_id`, `swap_requested_ra_id`, `checked_in_at` and `completed_at` all null and `filled_by_selector` false, and the floor's `uncovered_shift_count` and `coverage_status` are refreshed to reflect it.

Staffing an `OPEN` shift takes it to `ASSIGNED` and can happen two ways. A director may name the RA directly by supplying `ra_id`; or a director may let the server choose. Either way the same six conditions hold: the shift must be `OPEN`; the named RA must have `employment_status` `ACTIVE`; the named RA must have `certification_status` `CERTIFIED`; the named RA's `assigned_floor_id` must equal the shift's `floor_id`, and a mismatch is `ERR_FLOOR_MISMATCH`; the RA must have no conflicting shift under the rest rule below; and the shift's `start_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock, since a block already under way cannot be newly staffed — a late assignment is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`. On success `assigned_ra_id` is written and the floor's `uncovered_shift_count` and `coverage_status` are recomputed. The server-chosen path additionally sets `filled_by_selector` to true; the directly-named path leaves it false.

**The rest rule.** Duty shifts are half-open intervals: a block runs from its `start_at` inclusive to its `end_at` exclusive, so a block ending at 08:00 and one starting at 08:00 do not themselves overlap. Overlap alone is not the whole test, though. A candidate block conflicts with any block already held by the same RA whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `SWAP_PENDING`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED` when the two intervals intersect, **or** when the clear gap between them is strictly less than eight hours. A gap of exactly eight hours is permitted; a gap of seven hours fifty-nine minutes is not, and back-to-back blocks with no gap at all are refused. Any such collision reports `ERR_DUTY_COVERAGE_CONFLICT`. `MISSED`, `VOID` and `OPEN` blocks never participate in the conflict test.

**Server-chosen assignment.** When a director asks the server to fill an unstaffed block, the candidate pool is every RA whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`, whose `assigned_floor_id` equals the shift's floor, who is not named in the optional `exclude_ra_ids` list supplied with the request, and who has no conflicting block under the rest rule above. Candidates are ranked by their forward-looking commitment load, and **the lowest score wins**. Ties are broken in a fixed order: first the candidate with the fewer `missed_shift_count`; then the candidate with the lower `duty_points_balance`; then the candidate created earliest. The record identifier is never a tie-breaker. When the pool comes out empty, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RA` (HTTP 422), the block stays `OPEN`, and the floor's `uncovered_shift_count` is left exactly as it was.

**Duty load, in two readings.** An RA's workload is reported two ways, and the two numbers legitimately differ for the same RA against the same shift table. The **term-to-date** figure looks backwards: it sums, over the RA's shifts whose status is `COMPLETED` or `MISSED` and whose `start_at` falls at or after the term start instant of `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and strictly before the logical clock, that shift's `points_value` — except that a `MISSED` shift contributes double its points value as a reliability penalty. The **forward-28** figure looks ahead: it sums the plain `points_value`, with no penalty multiplier, over the RA's shifts whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `SWAP_PENDING` or `CHECKED_IN` and whose `start_at` falls at or after the logical clock and strictly before the instant 28 days later. That window is half-open — a block starting exactly at the current instant is counted, a block starting exactly 28 days out is not. The two status sets are disjoint, so no single shift can feed both figures. A read of one RA's duty load returns `term_to_date_score`, `forward_28_score`, the RA's `missed_shift_count` and `upcoming_shift_count`, the number of shifts feeding the forward window. The forward-28 figure is also the ranking key used by server-chosen assignment.

**Swapping.** The RA holding an `ASSIGNED` block may propose a replacement, moving it to `SWAP_PENDING`. The request carries an `action` of `REQUEST` together with the `replacement_ra_id` being proposed, requires the caller to be the block's current holder acting as a `ResidentAssistant`, requires the proposed replacement to be `CERTIFIED` and then `ACTIVE`, to be free of a conflicting block under the rest rule and to staff the shift's own floor, and requires the block to still be in the future. The proposal is recorded in `swap_requested_ra_id`. A director — and only a director, an RA attempting either half being refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, just as a director attempting `REQUEST` is — then closes the cycle with an `action` of `APPROVE` or `WITHDRAW`, both of which return the block to `ASSIGNED` and clear `swap_requested_ra_id`. `APPROVE` installs the replacement as `assigned_ra_id`, and before it does so the replacement is put through exactly the same active, certified, floor-coherence and rest-gap checks the original assignment passed. `WITHDRAW` leaves `assigned_ra_id` untouched, keeping the original holder on the block. This is the swap cycle `ASSIGNED → SWAP_PENDING → ASSIGNED`.

**Closing out.** Four outcome actions close a block. `CHECK_IN` moves it `ASSIGNED → CHECKED_IN`, requires the caller to be the assigned RA, and requires the logical clock to sit at or after `start_at` and strictly before `end_at`; it stamps `checked_in_at`. `COMPLETE` moves it `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED`, again by the assigned RA, and requires the clock to have reached `end_at`; it stamps `completed_at` and adds the block's `points_value` to the RA's `duty_points_balance`. `MARK_MISSED` is a director action available from `ASSIGNED` once the clock stands at or after `end_at`, and it increments the RA's `missed_shift_count`. `VOID` is a director action available from two states with two very different meanings: voiding an `OPEN` block simply retires it and refreshes the floor's `uncovered_shift_count` and `coverage_status`, while voiding a `COMPLETED` block is a compensating reversal that subtracts exactly the same `points_value` that completion added, leaving the RA's `duty_points_balance` at its pre-completion figure with no drift. `VOID` alone reads no clock window at all. Any outcome action issued outside its clock window is `ERR_SHIFT_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`, and any action issued from a status where it is not declared is `ERR_SHIFT_STATE_INVALID`. `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` and `VOID` are the machine's terminal states.

### 3.5. Incidents, Triage and Conduct Adjudication

An RA who witnesses a policy violation drafts a report about it. The draft names the `room_id` where it happened, the `subject_student_id` the report concerns, the `occurred_at` instant, a reporter-assessed `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, the `policy_code` allegedly breached — one of `ALCOHOL`, `NOISE`, `GUEST`, `DAMAGE` or `SAFETY` — and a free-text `narrative`. The filing RA is taken from the caller's identity and stored as `filed_by_ra_id`; it is never supplied in the body. Of the two body references, `room_id` is resolved before `subject_student_id`. Only an RA whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` may file, and an inactive filer is refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_ACTIVE`. An RA may not report on themselves: if `subject_student_id` names the RA's own resident record, the request is refused with `ERR_SELF_REPORT_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422). The report is created in `DRAFT` with `return_count` at zero and `submitted_at`, `routed_by_user_id` and `conduct_case_id` all null.

Submission moves the report `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, may replace the `narrative` on the way, requires the caller to be the RA who filed it, and requires the effective narrative — the supplied one, or the stored one when the body omits it — to run to at least ten characters, a shorter one drawing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the state check is reached. The same action also serves the return cycle, moving a report `RETURNED_FOR_DETAIL → SUBMITTED` under identical conditions and re-stamping `submitted_at`.

A director triages a `SUBMITTED` report by choosing a `disposition`, with an optional `note`. The fork has three branches, and their effects are deliberately disjoint. `RETURN` sends the report back to `RETURNED_FOR_DETAIL` and increments `return_count`; it is available only while `return_count` is strictly below the two-round cap, and a third return draws `ERR_RETURN_LIMIT_REACHED`. `CLOSE_NO_ACTION` moves the report to `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, and it is precisely characterised by what it does *not* do: **no** conduct case is created, `conduct_case_id` stays null, and the subject resident's `judicial_hold` is left exactly as it was. `ROUTE` moves the report to `ROUTED_TO_CONDUCT` and does three things: it creates a conduct case whose `incident_report_id` points back at this report, whose `respondent_student_id` is copied from the report's `subject_student_id`, whose `severity` is copied from the report's `severity`, whose status is `OPEN` and whose `opened_at` is the current logical clock instant; it records the new case's identifier as `conduct_case_id` and the triaging director as `routed_by_user_id`; and it sets the respondent's `judicial_hold` to true. A triage disposition issued against a report that is not `SUBMITTED` is `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Conduct adjudication is deliberately two-handed. A `ConductOfficer` schedules the hearing on an `OPEN` case by supplying `hearing_at`, which moves the case to `HEARING_SCHEDULED` and records the scheduling officer as `scheduled_by_user_id`. The instant must fall strictly after the logical clock and no later than ten days after the case's `opened_at`; an instant at or before the clock, or beyond that ten-day window, is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`. A *different* conduct officer then decides the case with an `outcome` of `SANCTION` or `DISMISS`, recording themselves as `decided_by_user_id`. **The deciding officer's identifier must differ from `scheduled_by_user_id`**; the same officer attempting both halves is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Scheduling a hearing on a case that is not `OPEN`, or deciding a case that is not `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, is `ERR_CONDUCT_CASE_STATE_INVALID`.

**Sanction points.** When the outcome is `SANCTION`, the case first counts its respondent's prior record: `prior_sanctioned_count` is the number of *other* conduct cases naming the same respondent whose status is already `SANCTIONED`, evaluated at the moment of decision — so the order in which a respondent's cases are decided changes the arithmetic. The base figure comes from the case's own `severity`: two points for `MINOR`, five for `MODERATE`, nine for `SEVERE`. A repeat multiplier is then formed as one plus one half for each prior sanctioned case, capped at two and a half; a respondent with no priors carries a multiplier of exactly one, one prior gives one and a half, two priors give two, and three or more priors are all held at the cap of two and a half. The case's `sanction_points` is the base figure multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half-up to a whole number of points.

The sanction then fires a four-link cascade. **Link one:** the respondent's `conduct_points` is increased by the newly computed `sanction_points`; the running total is always the sum of `sanction_points` over that respondent's `SANCTIONED` cases, and dismissed or still-open cases never contribute. **Link two:** the respondent's `conduct_standing` is re-derived from that total — `SUSPENDED` once `conduct_points` reaches 20 or more, otherwise `PROBATION` once it reaches 12 or more, otherwise `GOOD`. **Link three:** a respondent who has just landed in `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` has `break_housing_eligible` set to false, and every room-change case of theirs whose `placement_status` is `REQUESTED` is moved to `DENIED`. **Link four:** that loss of break-housing eligibility moves every break-housing request of theirs whose status is `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` to `REVOKED`, stamping `revocation_cause` as `CONDUCT_INELIGIBLE`. Alongside the cascade, the originating report bubbles up from `ROUTED_TO_CONDUCT` to `CLOSED_ADJUDICATED`, and the respondent's `judicial_hold` returns to false once no other case of theirs remains `OPEN` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`. The case records `outcome` as `SANCTION`.

A `DISMISS` outcome is the deliberately narrow sibling. The case's `sanction_points` is set to zero, its `prior_sanctioned_count` stays at the zero it was created with, and the respondent's `conduct_points` is left completely unchanged, so no standing re-derivation, no eligibility change, no room-change denial and no break-stay revocation follow. The originating report still bubbles up from `ROUTED_TO_CONDUCT` to `CLOSED_ADJUDICATED`, the respondent's `judicial_hold` still returns to false once no other live case remains, and the case records `outcome` as `DISMISS`. `SANCTIONED` and `DISMISSED` are both terminal.

### 3.6. Room Condition, Damage Assessment and the Bursar Ledger

Inspections are scheduled by the RA who will carry them out. The request names the `room_id`, an `inspection_type` of `CHECK_IN`, `CHECK_OUT` or `HEALTH_SAFETY`, the `scheduled_for` instant, and the `expected_room_version` described in §2.8. The inspecting RA is taken from the caller and stored as `inspector_ra_id`; the RA must be `ACTIVE`, and an inactive scheduler is refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_ACTIVE`. The room's floor must also be the RA's own `assigned_floor_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FLOOR_MISMATCH`. The `scheduled_for` instant need only parse; it is not compared to the logical clock. Scheduling is also a supersession: any inspection of the same room and the same `inspection_type` still sitting in `SCHEDULED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the new one. The new inspection is `SCHEDULED` with `condition_score` null, an empty `damage_lines` array, and `responsible_student_id`, `damage_charge_id` and `completed_at` all null. Because the supersession makes the room's recent history load-bearing, this is one of the two operations guarded by a version expectation, and the act of scheduling advances the room's own `version`.

Completing the walk-through moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. The caller must be the inspecting RA named on the record, and that RA must be both `CERTIFIED` and `ACTIVE` and still assigned to the room's floor — an uncertified inspector is refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_CERTIFIED`, an inactive one with `ERR_RA_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the certification check is evaluated first. The request supplies a `condition_score` between 0 and 100, a `damage_lines` array, and optionally a `responsible_student_id`. Each entry in `damage_lines` is a pairing of a `component_code` — one of `WALL`, `DOOR`, `CARPET`, `FURNITURE` or `WINDOW` — with a `quantity` between 1 and 10. Completion stamps `completed_at`, copies the supplied score onto the room's own `condition_score`, and, when `damage_lines` is non-empty, synthesizes a damage charge in `PENDING` whose `student_id` is the supplied `responsible_student_id`, or the room's sole occupant when none was supplied, or null when the room has no single occupant to fall back on — the charge is created either way, and the resolved identifier is written back onto the inspection's own `responsible_student_id`; the new charge's identifier is recorded on the inspection as `damage_charge_id`. An empty `damage_lines` array creates no charge and leaves `damage_charge_id` null. A completion attempted against an inspection that is not `SCHEDULED` is `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`. `COMPLETED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal; the `CANCELLED` state is written only by the system, when the inspected room goes offline.

**The provisional figure.** The charge created at completion carries `provisional_subtotal_cents`, computed from the frozen `damage_lines` alone. Each line contributes the catalogue list price of its component multiplied by its quantity, and the line contributions are summed. The catalogue is fixed in whole cents: a `WALL` line is 7,500 cents each, a `DOOR` 22,000, a `CARPET` 18,000, a `FURNITURE` item 13,500 and a `WINDOW` 26,000. The provisional figure applies **no** depreciation, **no** administrative fee and **no** relief; it is the undiscounted list total the resident sees before the bursar reviews anything, and it is strictly larger than the final assessed figure whenever the room's depreciation rate is above zero. The charge's `assessed_subtotal_cents`, `depreciation_rate`, `admin_fee_cents`, `hardship_relief_cents`, `final_amount_cents`, `billed_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `appeal_reason` and `appeal_outcome` all remain null while the charge is `PENDING`.

**Billing.** A `BursarOfficer` bills a `PENDING` charge, optionally attaching a `note`, and the charge moves to `BILLED`. Billing is refused for a resident whose `conduct_standing` is `SUSPENDED`, with `ERR_STUDENT_ON_PROBATION`. Billing computes the depreciated figure first. The room's age in years is the calendar year of the logical clock at billing time minus the room's `last_renovation_year`, floored at zero for a room renovated in the current year or later. The `depreciation_rate` is six one-hundredths for each such year, capped at one half — so a room eight years past renovation depreciates at forty-eight hundredths, and a room nine or more years past renovation is held at fifty hundredths. **The rounding order is pinned:** each damage line is separately multiplied by its catalogue price and quantity, reduced by the depreciation rate, and rounded half-up to whole cents; only then are the rounded lines summed into `assessed_subtotal_cents`. Summing the un-rounded lines and rounding once at the end produces a different number of cents and is incorrect.

**Settlement arithmetic.** The settlement layer runs immediately after, in a pinned six-step order that reads as circular if the bases are swapped. Step one takes the gross figure to be `assessed_subtotal_cents`. Step two selects an administrative rate from that gross figure alone: zero for a gross below 5,000 cents, five one-hundredths for a gross from 5,000 cents up to but not including 20,000 cents, and one tenth for a gross of 20,000 cents or more. Step three computes `admin_fee_cents` as the gross figure multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to whole cents. Step four forms the total before relief as the gross figure plus `admin_fee_cents`. Step five computes `hardship_relief_cents`: it is zero when the resident's `financial_hardship_flag` is false, and otherwise the smaller of 5,000 cents and one quarter of the total-before-relief figure, that quarter being rounded half-up to whole cents. Step six sets `final_amount_cents` to the total before relief less `hardship_relief_cents`, floored at zero. **The administrative tier is chosen from the gross figure and never from the total before relief; the relief base is the total before relief and never the gross figure.** Only the charge's own subtotal participates — a resident's other charges never influence the tier decision.

Billing then stamps `billed_at` with the logical clock and sets `appeal_deadline_at` to fourteen days after it, and the resident's `damage_balance_cents` is recomputed. That balance is the sum of `final_amount_cents` over the resident's charges whose status is `BILLED` or `APPEALED`; a `PENDING`, `SETTLED`, `WAIVED` or `VOID` charge contributes nothing.

**Appeal.** The billed resident — and only the billed resident — may appeal, supplying a `reason` that is stored as `appeal_reason`. The charge moves `BILLED → APPEALED` and the resident's balance still includes it. An appeal is permitted only once per charge: a charge whose `appeal_outcome` is already `ADJUSTED` or `UPHELD` refuses a second appeal with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. The appeal must also arrive at or before `appeal_deadline_at` on the logical clock; strictly later is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

The bursar resolves the appeal with an `action` of `ADJUST` or `UPHOLD`, and either way the charge returns to `BILLED`, closing the cycle `BILLED → APPEALED → BILLED`. `ADJUST` carries `adjusted_subtotal_cents`, which must be present and must fall between zero and the existing `assessed_subtotal_cents` inclusive, a breach of either bound being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the adjusted figure replaces `assessed_subtotal_cents`, and `admin_fee_cents`, `hardship_relief_cents` and `final_amount_cents` are then recomputed by the same six-step settlement from that new subtotal. `UPHOLD` leaves every amount exactly as it stood. `ADJUST` records `appeal_outcome` as `ADJUSTED` and `UPHOLD` records it as `UPHELD`. Both re-stamp `billed_at` to the current instant and push `appeal_deadline_at` out to fourteen days after it, and both recompute the resident's `damage_balance_cents`.

**Closing the charge.** A `SETTLE` action moves a `BILLED` charge to `SETTLED`, recording payment; the charge stops contributing to the resident's `damage_balance_cents`, which is recomputed accordingly. A `WAIVE` action means two different things depending on where the charge stands. On a `BILLED` charge it moves to `WAIVED` and is the compensating action of the ledger: the exact `final_amount_cents` that billing added is removed, restoring the resident's balance to precisely its pre-billing value with no drift. On a charge that was never billed at all — one still sitting in `PENDING` — the same `WAIVE` action moves it to `VOID`, and because the charge never contributed to the resident's balance, **no ledger movement occurs at all**. Both closing actions accept an optional `note`. `SETTLED`, `WAIVED` and `VOID` are terminal, and any billing, appeal, resolution or settlement action issued from a status where it is not declared is `ERR_CHARGE_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.7. Roommate Mediation and Moving a Resident

A room-change case is opened either by the resident who would move or by a director on their behalf. The request names the `student_id`, the `origin`, the `from_room_id` the resident currently occupies, and the `expected_student_version` described in §2.8; a `roommate_student_id` is supplied only when the case concerns a conflict with another resident. The `origin` is one of `SELF_REQUEST`, `ROOMMATE_CONFLICT` or `EMERGENCY_RELOCATION`, and `roommate_student_id` is null for the first and third of those by convention rather than by enforcement — the server stores whatever roommate the caller supplies, whichever origin was declared. The declared `from_room_id` must equal the resident's actual `current_room_id`, and the eventual target must not be the room they already occupy; either breach is `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_IN_ROOM` (HTTP 422). The opening actor is recorded as `opened_by_user_id`, and the case begins with `placement_status` at `NONE`, `to_room_id` and `reverted_from_room_id` null, `mediation_round` at zero and `session_at` null.

The origin decides where the mediation dimension starts. A `ROOMMATE_CONFLICT` case is born already in `INTAKE`, because a conflict always needs handling; a `SELF_REQUEST` or `EMERGENCY_RELOCATION` case is born in `NOT_REQUIRED`. Opening a case also supersedes the resident's own history: any earlier room-change case for the same resident still sitting at `placement_status` `REQUESTED` is moved to `CANCELLED` — every one of them, where more than one somehow stands — and the new case records the most recently created of those cancelled cases as `superseded_case_id`, leaving that field null when there were none. This supersession is exactly why opening a case carries a version expectation on the resident.

**The mediation track.** A director drives mediation with an `action` of `OPEN_INTAKE`, `SCHEDULE_SESSION` or `RECORD_SESSION`, and none of these actions ever writes `placement_status`. `OPEN_INTAKE` moves `NOT_REQUIRED → INTAKE` and requires the case to actually name a `roommate_student_id`; used on a case with no roommate, it is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_STATE_INVALID`. A conflict-origin case, already born in `INTAKE`, has no need of it. `SCHEDULE_SESSION` carries a `session_at` instant that must be strictly later than the logical clock — an instant at or before it is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_STATE_INVALID`, evaluated after both the state check and the round cap — and moves the case to `SESSION_SCHEDULED` from either `INTAKE` or `IMPASSE` — the second of those closing the cycle `SESSION_SCHEDULED → IMPASSE → SESSION_SCHEDULED`. It is permitted only while `mediation_round` is strictly below the three-round cap; a fourth session draws `ERR_MEDIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. `RECORD_SESSION` carries an `outcome` of `AGREEMENT` or `IMPASSE`; both increment `mediation_round` by one, and they move the case from `SESSION_SCHEDULED` to `AGREEMENT_REACHED` and `IMPASSE` respectively. `AGREEMENT_REACHED` is terminal on the mediation track. A mediation action issued from a state where it is not declared is `ERR_MEDIATION_STATE_INVALID`.

**The placement track.** Naming a destination moves `placement_status` from `NONE` to `REQUESTED` and stores the supplied `to_room_id`; this may be done by the resident themselves or by a director, and it never writes `mediation_status`. Four conditions apply. The target room must either be `AVAILABLE`, or `OCCUPIED` with `occupied_beds` still below `bed_capacity` — an `OFFLINE` or `CONSOLIDATED` target is `ERR_ROOM_NOT_AVAILABLE` and a full target is `ERR_ROOM_AT_CAPACITY`. The resident's `conduct_standing` must not be `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended resident is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_ON_PROBATION`. The target must differ from the origin room. And crucially, **when the case's `origin` is `ROOMMATE_CONFLICT`, the mediation track must already have reached either `AGREEMENT_REACHED` or `IMPASSE`** — a conflict case that has not been through mediation cannot request a placement, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. This is the one point where the placement track *reads* the mediation track, and that read runs first — immediately after the `NONE` state check and ahead of the target-room, conduct-standing and origin-room conditions listed above.

A director then approves or denies a `REQUESTED` placement with a `decision` and an optional `reason`. `DENY` moves the case to `DENIED`. `APPROVE` moves it to `APPROVED` and re-checks four things at decision time rather than trusting the request-time snapshot: the hall owning `to_room_id` must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN`; the target room must still be `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` with a free bed; the resident's `conduct_standing` must be exactly `GOOD`, so a resident who has drifted onto `PROBATION` since requesting is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_ON_PROBATION`; and the resident's `judicial_hold` must be false, a live conduct case blocking the move with `ERR_JUDICIAL_HOLD`. The `DENIED` state is also written by the system, as link three of the conduct sanction cascade.

Executing the approved move, optionally with a `note`, is where beds actually change hands. The case first records `reverted_from_room_id` as the current `from_room_id`, preserving the exact origin so a reversal can restore it. The resident's `current_room_id` becomes `to_room_id` and their `housing_status` becomes `ASSIGNED`. The origin room's `occupied_beds` falls by one and the room moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE` if that leaves it empty; the target room's `occupied_beds` rises by one and the room moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED` if it was previously empty. Completion re-checks that the target still has a free bed and that its hall is still `OPEN`. **The hall's `assigned_bed_count` is unchanged by the move**, because a transfer neither creates nor destroys an occupied bed; only floor-level `occupied_beds` figures shift when the two rooms sit on different floors.

Reverting a `COMPLETED` move is the compensating action of the housing ledger. It requires a `reason`, and it requires the room named by `reverted_from_room_id` to still be usable — not `CONSOLIDATED`, which draws `ERR_ROOM_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and with a free bed, a full origin room drawing `ERR_ROOM_AT_CAPACITY`. An origin room that has merely gone `OFFLINE` does not block the reversal, and neither does a hall that is no longer `OPEN`. It sets the resident's `current_room_id` back to `reverted_from_room_id` and their `housing_status` to `ASSIGNED`, decrements the target room's `occupied_beds` and increments the origin room's, and moves the case to `REVERTED`. Every room touched by the forward move returns to the exact `occupied_beds` figure it held before completion, and the hall's `assigned_bed_count` is identical end to end. `COMPLETED`, `DENIED`, `CANCELLED` and `REVERTED` are the placement track's terminal states, and a placement action issued from a state where it is not declared is `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Floor Programming and the Three-Bucket Budget

Every floor's programming money lives in three buckets that always partition the same total. `programming_budget_cents` is everything ever allocated to the floor for the term. `programming_encumbered_cents` is the portion locked by programs that have been approved but not yet reimbursed. `programming_spent_cents` is the portion actually disbursed through reimbursements. `programming_available_cents` is what remains: the budget less the encumbered portion less the spent portion. That identity holds after every operation described here, and it is the invariant against which every bucket movement should be checked.

An RA proposes a program by naming the `floor_id`, a `title`, a `category` of `SOCIAL`, `EDUCATIONAL` or `WELLNESS`, the `scheduled_for` instant, and a `requested_amount_cents` between 0 and 500,000. The organizing RA is taken from the caller and stored as `organizer_ra_id`, must be `ACTIVE` — an inactive proposer is refused with `ERR_RA_NOT_ACTIVE` — and must actually staff the floor in question, since a proposal for another floor is `ERR_FLOOR_MISMATCH`. The event is created in `PROPOSED` with `encumbered_amount_cents` at zero, `attendance_count` null, an empty `receipt_lines` array, `reimbursed_amount_cents` null and `decision_reason` null.

A director decides a `PROPOSED` event with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `reason`, which is stored as `decision_reason`. `DENY` moves the event to `DENIED` and moves no money at all. `APPROVE` moves it to `APPROVED` and encumbers the ask: the event's `encumbered_amount_cents` is set to its `requested_amount_cents`, the floor's `programming_encumbered_cents` grows by that amount and `programming_available_cents` is recomputed. Three conditions gate approval. The floor must not be frozen — `programming_frozen` must be false, which it is unless the hall is under emergency closure, and a frozen floor refuses the approval with `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN`. The floor must have money allocated at all: a `programming_budget_cents` of zero is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_NOT_ALLOCATED`. And the ask must fit inside `programming_available_cents`; an ask that exceeds it is `ERR_FLOOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The budget-allocation check is evaluated before the availability check, so a floor with no budget at all reports the allocation code.

Once the scheduled instant has arrived on the logical clock, an RA or a director records the outcome. `HOLD` moves the event `APPROVED → HELD`, records the supplied `attendance_count` as the headcount, and refreshes the floor's compliance figures; attempting to hold before `scheduled_for` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. `CANCEL` is the compensating action, available from either `APPROVED` or `HELD`: it moves the event to `CANCELLED`, subtracts exactly the stored `encumbered_amount_cents` from the floor's `programming_encumbered_cents`, resets the event's `encumbered_amount_cents` to zero, and recomputes `programming_available_cents` back to precisely its pre-approval value. Cancelling from `HELD` additionally refreshes the floor's `held_program_count` and compliance status, since the event stops counting as delivered.

**Floor compliance.** A floor's `held_program_count` is the number of its events whose status is `HELD` or `REIMBURSED`; proposed, approved, denied and cancelled events never count. The floor's `programming_compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when that count reaches `required_programs_per_term` — the comparison is inclusive, so exactly the required number qualifies — **and** the qualifying set contains at least one `EDUCATIONAL` event and at least one `WELLNESS` event. Failing that, the status is `DEFICIENT` when the floor's `programming_frozen` is true, and `PENDING` otherwise. The status is re-derived whenever an event on the floor reaches `HELD`, `REIMBURSED` or `CANCELLED`, and whenever the frozen flag itself changes. A read of one floor's programming summary reports `budget_cents`, `encumbered_cents`, `spent_cents` and `available_cents` mirroring the three buckets and the remainder, together with `held_program_count`, `compliance_status`, and the two booleans `has_educational` and `has_wellness` that show which half of the category mix is satisfied.

**Reimbursement.** A director reimburses a `HELD` event from itemised receipts supplied as `receipt_lines`, each a required non-negative integer `amount_cents` paired with a `line_category` of `FOOD`, `SUPPLIES` or `HONORARIUM`. Reimbursement against an event whose status is not `HELD` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_HELD`, and the array must not be empty. Each line is reimbursed at its category's rate — three quarters for `FOOD`, nine tenths for `SUPPLIES` and the full amount for `HONORARIUM` — and, as elsewhere in this system, **the rate is applied to each line and that line is rounded half-up to whole cents before the lines are summed** into `reimbursed_amount_cents`. Grouping lines by category and rounding once per category, or summing first and rounding at the end, produces a different figure and is wrong.

There is no cap on the reimbursement relative to the original ask. An under-spend returns the difference to the floor; an over-spend draws the excess from it. What is checked is the overrun: the amount by which `reimbursed_amount_cents` exceeds the event's `requested_amount_cents`, or zero when it does not exceed it at all, must fit inside the floor's `programming_available_cents` as it stands *before* this event's own encumbrance is released — that encumbrance still counts against the floor while the check runs — and an overrun that does not fit is `ERR_FLOOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On success the event moves `HELD → REIMBURSED` and the buckets move in a fixed order: the floor's `programming_encumbered_cents` is reduced by the event's stored `encumbered_amount_cents`, which is then zeroed; the floor's `programming_spent_cents` grows by `reimbursed_amount_cents`; and `programming_available_cents` is recomputed as the budget less the encumbered portion less the spent portion. `REIMBURSED`, `DENIED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and any decision or outcome action issued from a state where it is not declared is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Break Housing, Occupancy Census, Consolidation and Emergency Closure

**Break stays.** A resident, or a director acting for them, requests to stay over a break by naming the `student_id`, the `room_id`, a `break_code` of `THANKSGIVING`, `WINTER` or `SPRING`, the inclusive `start_date` and `end_date` as calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `reason`. The end date must be on or after the start date and the two dates may not lie more than 45 days apart — the span is that difference, so a 45-day gap is accepted and a 46-day gap is not. The request is created in `REQUESTED` with `decided_by_user_id`, `revocation_cause` and `checked_in_at` null.

A director decides it with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `reason`, recording themselves as `decided_by_user_id`. `DENY` moves the request to `DENIED` and does nothing further. `APPROVE` moves it to `APPROVED` and passes four gates, in this order: the hall's `posture` must be `OPEN`; the hall owning the room must have `break_housing_enabled` true, and a hall that does not accept break stays refuses with `ERR_BREAK_HOUSING_DISABLED`; the resident's `break_housing_eligible` must be true, and a resident who lost it to a conduct sanction is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_ON_PROBATION`; and the stay must fit the room's beds under the capacity projection below. Approval refreshes the room's `break_hold_count`.

**Break stays are closed date intervals — both endpoints are occupied.** Two stays on the same room collide when the first starts on or before the second ends and the second starts on or before the first ends, which means a stay ending on 2026-12-24 and a stay starting on 2026-12-24 *do* collide, because they share that date. This is deliberately unlike the half-open duty-shift interval of §3.4. Collision alone is not disqualifying, though: the projection is an aggregate bed count, not a pairwise exclusion. The server counts the room's other stays whose status is `APPROVED` or `CHECKED_IN` and which collide with the candidate's dates, adds one for the candidate itself, and approves while that projected total is at most the room's `bed_capacity`. A projection exceeding the room's beds is refused with `ERR_BREAK_HOUSING_CAPACITY`. Stays that are `REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED` or `CLOSED` never occupy a bed and never enter the count.

An RA or a director then works the stay's occupancy with an `action` of `CHECK_IN` or `CLOSE` and an optional `note`. `CHECK_IN` moves an `APPROVED` stay to `CHECKED_IN`, requires the hall to be `OPEN`, requires the date portion of the logical clock to fall on or after `start_date` and on or before `end_date` — a clock outside that span is refused with `ERR_BREAK_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` rather than with any window code — and stamps `checked_in_at`. `CLOSE` moves a `CHECKED_IN` stay to `CLOSED`, requires the hall to be `OPEN` as well, requires the date portion of the clock to be strictly past `end_date` — again `ERR_BREAK_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` when it is not — and refreshes the room's `break_hold_count`. `DENIED`, `REVOKED` and `CLOSED` are terminal; an action against a state where it is not declared is `ERR_BREAK_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. `REVOKED` is never reached by an operator action — it is written only by the two cascades, carrying `revocation_cause` of `EMERGENCY_CLOSURE` or `CONDUCT_INELIGIBLE`.

**The census.** A read of one hall's occupancy census reports five figures over every room whose floor belongs to that hall, regardless of room status: `total_beds` is the sum of every room's `bed_capacity`, including rooms that are offline or consolidated; `occupied_beds` is the sum of every room's `occupied_beds`; `offline_room_count` is the number of rooms whose status is `OFFLINE` or `CONSOLIDATED`; `underfilled_room_count` is the number of rooms that are `OCCUPIED` with at least one occupant but fewer occupants than beds; and `vacancy_rate` is the unoccupied share of total beds — total beds less occupied beds, divided by total beds — rounded half-up to four decimal places, and reported as zero when the hall has no beds at all.

**Consolidation.** Late in a term a director may pack a hall's residents into fewer rooms. The operation takes a `mode` of `PLAN` or `EXECUTE`: the plan mode reports the moves it would make without touching anything, and the execute mode applies them. Three preconditions run before any planning. The hall must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN`. The census must show at least one occupied bed; a hall with nobody in it draws `ERR_CENSUS_EMPTY` (HTTP 422). And the census `vacancy_rate` must be at least twenty-five hundredths — a hall that is too full to be worth consolidating draws `ERR_CONSOLIDATION_NOT_WARRANTED`.

The plan itself is a waterfall. **Source rooms** are those that are `OCCUPIED` with at least one occupant and strictly fewer occupants than beds; a room filled to its `bed_capacity` is never a source. Sources are taken emptiest first — ordered by ascending `occupied_beds`, then by descending `bed_capacity`, then by earliest creation — so the fewest residents move. **Target rooms** are those that are `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` and still have a free bed, excluding any source already emptied by this run, and they are taken fullest first — ordered by descending `occupied_beds`, then by descending `bed_capacity`, then by earliest creation — so vacancies concentrate rather than scatter. Working through the sources in order, each source's occupants are taken in ascending order of the resident's creation instant, and each is placed into the first target that still has a free bed and is not the source room itself. **Target occupancy is updated after every single placement**, so the ordering is load-bearing and a target that fills up during the run drops out for the placements that follow. A source whose occupants cannot all be placed is skipped whole — partial evacuation is never planned. Neither room identifiers nor resident identifiers ever influence the plan; ties are broken by creation instant alone. An empty source set is **not** an error: the run succeeds and simply reports no moves.

The response carries the `mode` it ran in, the `planned_move_count` of resident-and-target pairs the waterfall produced, the `executed_move_count` — equal to the planned count under `EXECUTE` and zero under `PLAN` — the `vacated_room_ids` of the sources fully emptied, the `moved_student_ids` in the order they were placed, the `occupied_beds_after` figure and the `vacancy_rate_before` the run started from. Under `EXECUTE` every planned move is applied — each resident's `current_room_id` is rewritten, their `housing_status` set to `ASSIGNED`, and both rooms' `occupied_beds` adjusted — and each fully emptied source then moves `OCCUPIED → CONSOLIDATED`, which is terminal. The hall's `assigned_bed_count` is unchanged by the run, because every move is a transfer and never a creation.

**Hall posture and the closure cascade.** A director drives a hall's posture by supplying a `target_posture` and, where the move demands one, a `reason`. Four moves are declared. `OPEN → RESTRICTED` and `RESTRICTED → OPEN` are the mild pair: the first stores the supplied `reason` as `closure_reason` and stamps `last_posture_change_at` with the logical clock, and thereafter new placements into the hall's rooms are refused with `ERR_BUILDING_NOT_OPEN`; the second clears `closure_reason` back to null and re-stamps `last_posture_change_at`. Neither touches a room, a stay or a budget. `EMERGENCY_CLOSED` may be entered from either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and both paths require a non-empty `reason`, a missing one being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and run the identical cascade below. `EMERGENCY_CLOSED → OPEN` reopens the hall. **`EMERGENCY_CLOSED → RESTRICTED` is not a declared move** and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POSTURE_TRANSITION`, as is any other undeclared pairing.

Closing a hall runs a four-link chain plus a fan-out. **Link one:** every room in the hall whose status is `AVAILABLE` or `OCCUPIED` records its current status into `pre_closure_status` and moves to `OFFLINE`. **Link two:** each room that just went offline revokes every break-housing stay against it whose status is `APPROVED` or `CHECKED_IN`, moving those stays to `REVOKED` with `revocation_cause` set to `EMERGENCY_CLOSURE`. **Link three:** each stay that just entered `REVOKED` opens a new room-change case for its resident, with `origin` `EMERGENCY_RELOCATION`, `from_room_id` set to the resident's `current_room_id`, `mediation_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, `placement_status` already at `REQUESTED`, and `opened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal string `system`. **Link four:** each resident with a case opened by link three has their `housing_status` set to `RELOCATION_PENDING`. The **fan-out** runs alongside: every floor in the hall sets `programming_frozen` to true and re-derives its `programming_compliance_status`, and every inspection still `SCHEDULED` on a room that just went offline is moved to `CANCELLED`. Finally the hall stores the supplied `reason` as `closure_reason` and stamps `last_posture_change_at`.

Reopening from `EMERGENCY_CLOSED` is a partial restoration, and the asymmetry is intentional. Every room in the hall that is `OFFLINE` with a non-null `pre_closure_status` returns to exactly that stored status — a room that was `AVAILABLE` before the closure becomes `AVAILABLE` again and a room that was `OCCUPIED` becomes `OCCUPIED` again — and `pre_closure_status` is then cleared back to null. Rooms taken offline by a director outside the closure carry no `pre_closure_status` and are therefore left offline. Every floor sets `programming_frozen` back to false and re-derives its compliance status. **Revoked break-housing stays are not restored**, and neither are cancelled inspections or the relocation cases the closure opened; those must be worked through on their own tracks. The hall clears `closure_reason` back to null.
