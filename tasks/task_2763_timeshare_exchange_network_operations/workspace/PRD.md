# Timeshare Exchange Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A vacation exchange network sits between two groups of people who never meet: owners who cannot use their week this year, and owners who want somebody else's week instead. The network's whole value is the promise that a banked week is worth a fair, predictable number of trading power points, that the points are honoured when they are spent, and that nobody is quietly charged twice for the same holiday. Today that promise is administered by hand — spreadsheets that value a deposit one way at intake and a different way at settlement, waitlists that are re-sorted by whoever happens to be looking at them, and cancellation refunds argued case by case. This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets.

The system under specification manages the full operational surface of the network: affiliating resorts and recording their season calendars and quality ratings, enrolling members onto tiered fee schedules, valuing banked weeks into trading power, matching exchange requests against banked inventory through an operator-driven run, confirming and completing stays, cancelling and reinstating them, renting out inventory nobody wanted as cash getaway offers, and adjudicating member disputes about valuations and resort quality. Every movement of trading power and every movement of wallet cash is written to an append-only ledger, and the two member balances the network reports are defined as the sums of that ledger — there is no second source of truth.

Two properties dominate the design and should be read as constraints on every requirement that follows. First, the network is **valuation-honest**: a week is priced twice, once when it is banked and once when the network actually commits it, and the difference is settled back to the depositing owner rather than absorbed silently. Second, the network is **checkpoint-driven**: nothing expires, lapses, matches, completes or goes on probation because wall-clock time passed. All of that happens when an operator executes a matching run against the server's logical clock, in one pinned step order, so that every observer of the system can replay how a given state was reached. The API is mounted under `/exchange-network`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Membership Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the four recognised roles, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but the endpoint being called does not list it among its authorised roles, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The four roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in the header, are `Member`, `ResortManager`, `NetworkOperator` and `DisputeAnalyst`.

Two of the four roles are backed by persisted records. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a member compare the resource's member reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Member` caller may act only where `X-User-ID` equals the target member's own `id`, the `member_id` of the deposit being extended, withdrawn or banked, the `member_id` of the exchange request being funded, declined or cancelled, the `member_id` of the exchange request behind the confirmation being created, cancelled or reinstated, the `member_id` of the dispute being answered or withdrawn, or the `member_id` supplied in a getaway booking body; and a member filing a quality review must own the exchange request behind the cited confirmation. Any breach of these ownership rules is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ResortManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resort`; the manager may add season bands only for that same resort — the `resort_id` it supplies must equal `X-User-ID` — and may read the deposit-obligation compliance report only for that resort, the `resort_id` path parameter again having to equal `X-User-ID`. A breach is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESORT_MANAGER` (HTTP 403).

The other two roles are operational identities with no persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record or log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DisputeAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque analyst handle that is never looked up. Because `Member` and `ResortManager` identities *are* looked up, a request in one of those two roles whose `X-User-ID` matches no existing `Member` row (respectively no existing `Resort` row) fails with `ERR_ACTING_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); operator and analyst identities are never subjected to that lookup.

The role model is one of peers rather than a hierarchy. A `Member` banks weeks, files and funds exchange requests, confirms, cancels and reinstates exchanges, buys deposit extensions, books getaway offers, files quality reviews, files disputes, answers information requests and withdraws its own disputes; it reads the whole inventory market — every resort, tier, deposit, request, confirmation, getaway offer and season band — because the market is public, but it writes only its own rows. It may never create or affiliate a resort, edit a resort's demand rating or unit capacity, create season bands, create tiers or other members, drive any enrollment status, execute a matching run, set the clock, withdraw a getaway listing, list the whole member roster, or take any dispute review action. A `ResortManager` maintains one resort's season calendar and reads that resort's compliance report and the matching runs that affect it; it may not set demand ratings or quality ratings, affiliate or suspend a resort, create or read the member list, create deposits, file or resolve disputes, run a matching run, set the clock, or read another resort's compliance report. A `NetworkOperator` creates tiers, resorts and members, grants and revokes affiliation, sets demand and capacity, drives member enrollment and tier changes, records dues payments on a member's behalf, may cancel a confirmation on a member's behalf, executes matching runs, withdraws getaway listings and controls the logical clock — but is never a counterparty and never an adjudicator, so it may not file an exchange request, confirm an exchange, book a getaway, file a quality review, file a dispute, or take any dispute review action. A `DisputeAnalyst` reads members, deposits, confirmations, reviews, ledger entries and matching runs in order to judge, and is the only role that can move a dispute through review, information requests and resolution; it can never file, answer or withdraw a dispute, cannot create or edit resorts, tiers, members, deposits, requests or confirmations, cannot run a matching run and cannot set the clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1 and may not be higher than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; the matching-run collection is instead ordered by `run_sequence` ascending, and the ledger collection is ordered by `created_at` ascending as the natural posting order of the ledger. The `meta` block of every list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Path Resolution

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carried as a plain string with no type prefix, and every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was written. All twelve record families in this system — membership tiers, resorts, season bands, members, deposits, exchange requests, confirmations, matching runs, getaway offers, disputes, resort quality reviews and ledger entries — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There is no record family in this specification that omits `updated_at` or `version`. Client-supplied values for `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` are never honoured.

Path parameters are resolved left to right, in the order the identifiers appear in the URL, and the first identifier that matches no row ends the request with that family's 404: `tier_id` yields `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `resort_id` yields `ERR_RESORT_NOT_FOUND`, `band_id` yields `ERR_SEASON_BAND_NOT_FOUND`, `review_id` yields `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `member_id` yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `entry_id` yields `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `deposit_id` yields `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `request_id` yields `ERR_EXCHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `confirmation_id` yields `ERR_CONFIRMATION_NOT_FOUND`, `run_id` yields `ERR_MATCHING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `offer_id` yields `ERR_GETAWAY_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` and `dispute_id` yields `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied inside a request body or a query string are resolved the same way, in the field order declared for that operation's input, and raise the same family-specific 404 — a body naming an unknown `tier_id`, `resort_id`, `member_id`, `deposit_id`, `exchange_request_id` or `confirmation_id` fails with `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CONFIRMATION_NOT_FOUND` respectively.

Mutation is always visible. Any operation that changes a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, and this applies to rows changed as a cascade just as much as to the row the caller named: when a matching run expires a deposit, both the deposit and the resort whose committed inventory counters moved are mutated rows in this sense, and each advances its own `version` independently.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Operations-Run Checkpoint

Time in this system is explicit. The server holds a logical clock whose default value is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every temporal comparison in this specification — deposit expiry, match holds, cancellation windows, reinstatement windows, dues arrears, lead times — is evaluated against that clock rather than against real elapsed time. An operator-only test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and reports the resulting `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. Moving the clock has no consequences of its own: no deposit expires, no request is swept, no confirmation completes and no resort changes its affiliation state merely because the clock advanced past a threshold.

Those consequences are applied only by a matching run, the network's single operations checkpoint. A run stamps `as_of_utc` from the logical clock at the instant the run is accepted, executes a fixed sequence of seven steps described in §3.5, freezes a set of counters describing what it did, and only then reports itself as `COMPLETED`. Because the step order is pinned and every step reads the state left by its predecessors, the same starting state and the same clock always produce the same run outcome.

### 2.6. Arithmetic, Rounding and Money Conventions

All cash amounts in this system are integer numbers of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the API surface, and no cash value is ever rounded to two decimal places because none is ever expressed in units of currency. All trading power values are integers. The only non-integer values the API exposes are a resort's `quality_score` and a review's `composite_score`, both carried to exactly 2 decimal places in the range 1.00 to 10.00, and a season band's `season_multiplier`, which is one of the four fixed factors listed in §3.1.

Rounding is stated per calculation and each statement is normative — several calculations in this domain deliberately round at different points, and a single shared rounding helper applied to every intermediate product will produce wrong answers. Where a calculation says "rounded half up", ties round away from zero to the larger magnitude. Where a calculation says "rounded down" or "truncated", the fractional part is discarded toward zero. Where a calculation says "rounded up", any fractional part advances the integer by one. The trading-power valuation of a week rounds half up at exactly two named points; the getaway price rounds half up exactly once at the very end; the power budget and the deposit-first credit round down; a cancellation haircut rounds up; and the quality score rounds half up to two decimals both per review and again on the mean of reviews. No other rounding occurs.

### 2.7. The Two-Ledger Principle

Members hold two balances and neither is authored directly. `trading_power_balance` is the member's spendable trading power and `wallet_credit_cents` is the member's spendable cash credit, and both are defined as sums over the append-only ledger: a member's `trading_power_balance` always equals the sum of `power_delta` across every ledger entry whose `member_id` is that member, and their `wallet_credit_cents` always equals the sum of `cash_delta_cents` across those same entries. Both balances are constrained to be at least 0 at all times. Every entry records its `member_id`, an `entry_type` naming which declared movement produced it, the signed `power_delta` it applies to trading power (0 for a pure cash movement), the signed `cash_delta_cents` it applies to the wallet (0 for a pure power movement), a `related_entity_type` and `related_entity_id` identifying the row the movement was raised against — identity only, never an input to any decision — and a human-readable `description`.

The `entry_type` vocabulary is closed. Power-side movements are `DEPOSIT_CREDIT`, `DEPOSIT_WITHDRAWAL_REVERSAL`, `DEPOSIT_EXPIRY_FORFEIT`, `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT`, `EXCHANGE_DEBIT`, `EXCHANGE_RESTORATION`, `RESTORATION_HAIRCUT`, `GETAWAY_LISTING_FORFEIT` and `DISPUTE_POWER_GRANT`. Cash-side movements are `DUES_PAYMENT`, `EXCHANGE_CHARGE`, `EXCHANGE_CHARGE_REFUND`, `CANCELLATION_FEE`, `REINSTATEMENT_FEE`, `EXTENSION_FEE` and `GETAWAY_BOOKING_PAYMENT`. The `related_entity_type` vocabulary is likewise closed: `DEPOSIT`, `EXCHANGE_REQUEST`, `CONFIRMATION`, `GETAWAY_OFFER`, `DISPUTE` and `MEMBER`.

Because a balance may never go negative, every debit-style power movement is clamped. Any forfeit, haircut or reversal whose magnitude exceeds the owner's current `trading_power_balance` is clamped so the resulting balance lands on exactly 0, and the ledger entry records the *clamped* `power_delta`, not the notional one. This keeps the identity between balance and ledger sum exact rather than approximately true. Cash debits are never clamped; instead they are refused in advance by the wallet-sufficiency check described in §2.10.

### 2.8. Derived Counters and Inventory Conservation

Four derived quantities are reconciled continuously against the rows that define them, and an implementation that lets any of them drift is wrong even if every individual endpoint appears to work. The two balance identities of §2.7 are the first two. The third concerns resort occupancy: a resort's `deposits_committed_count` always equals the number of deposit rows for that resort whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED`, `RESERVED` or `USED`, and its `committed_nights` always equals eight nights multiplied by that same count. The fourth concerns member workload: a member's `active_request_count` always equals the number of that member's exchange requests whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MATCHED`.

The eight-night figure is not arbitrary. A banked week occupies seven stay nights plus one turnover night, and the resulting occupancy interval runs from `check_in_date` inclusive to `occupancy_end_date` exclusive, where `occupancy_end_date` is eight days after `check_in_date`. Two deposits of the same resort and the same `unit_size` conflict when their intervals share at least one calendar day; because the interval is half-open, a week checking in exactly eight days after another does not conflict with it. Capacity is aggregate rather than per-deposit: for any single day, the number of deposits of one `unit_size` whose interval covers that day, counting only deposits whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED`, `RESERVED` or `USED`, may not exceed that resort's `units_per_size`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Contended Records

Three write operations mutate a parent record whose state a client must have observed before writing, and each of them requires an optimistic-concurrency token as a non-optional body field. In all three cases the server compares the submitted token to the parent row's current `version`; when the two are unequal the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all changes; when they are equal the write proceeds and increments the parent's `version` by 1, so replaying the same token after a successful write is itself rejected. No other endpoint in this specification accepts or requires a version field.

`POST /exchange-network/deposits/{deposit_id}/extensions` requires `expected_deposit_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Deposit`. On mismatch the rejection is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412 and `extension_months_purchased`, `expires_at`, the member's `wallet_credit_cents` and the ledger are all left untouched. This matters because buying three months and then three more is a legitimate repeated operation: a caller holding a `version` read from before the first purchase must be told its read is stale rather than silently granted a second three months.

`POST /exchange-network/resorts/{resort_id}/quality-reviews` requires `expected_resort_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Resort`. On mismatch the rejection is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412, no review row is created, and `review_count`, `quality_score` and `quality_rating` keep their previous values. A resort accumulates many reviews over its life, so a `version` read before an earlier review landed no longer describes the rating state the new review would be folded into; on success the resort's `version` advances because the review changes `review_count` and may change both `quality_score` and `quality_rating`.

`POST /exchange-network/members/{member_id}/dues-payments` requires `expected_member_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Member`. On mismatch the rejection is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412 and `enrollment_status`, `dues_paid_through` and `wallet_credit_cents` keep their previous values with no `DUES_PAYMENT` ledger entry written. Dues are paid every year, and a lapsed member pays again to be reinstated, so a stale token must never be allowed to double-credit a wallet against an already-settled year.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

Validation is globally ordered, not per-endpoint ordered. When one request violates several rules, the response carries the error of the *earliest* rule in the following sequence and no other. The sequence runs authentication first, then existence, then state, then business safety, then temporal rules, then referential rules, then structural constraints, then fiscal rules.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` is one of `Member`, `ResortManager`, `NetworkOperator`, `DisputeAnalyst`, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. `X-User-Role` appears among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. Body and query shapes are satisfied, on every endpoint: required fields present, enum fields inside their declared value sets, integers inside their declared ranges, dates formatted as `YYYY-MM-DD`, season-band day strings formatted as two digits of month and two digits of day separated by a hyphen, instants formatted as ISO 8601 UTC, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. The acting identity exists, on every endpoint: a `Member` caller's `X-User-ID` identifies an existing member and a `ResortManager` caller's `X-User-ID` identifies an existing resort, while `NetworkOperator` and `DisputeAnalyst` are opaque and not looked up — otherwise `ERR_ACTING_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. Every path parameter identifies an existing row, on every endpoint carrying one, checked left to right — otherwise the family-specific 404 listed in §2.4.
7. Every foreign key in a body or query string identifies an existing row, on every endpoint accepting one, checked in the declared input field order — otherwise the family-specific 404 listed in §2.4.
8. A `Member` caller acts only on rows it owns — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This guards sixteen operations: recording a dues payment, banking a deposit, buying deposit extensions, withdrawing a deposit, filing an exchange request, attaching funding to a request, declining a match, cancelling a request, creating a confirmation, cancelling a confirmation, reinstating a confirmation, booking a getaway offer, filing a quality review, filing a dispute, answering an information request and withdrawing a dispute.
9. A `ResortManager` caller acts only on its own resort — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESORT_MANAGER` (403). This guards two operations: creating a season band and reading a resort's compliance report.
10. On the three operations that require an optimistic-concurrency token — deposit extension purchase, quality-review filing and dues-payment recording — the submitted `expected_deposit_version`, `expected_resort_version` or `expected_member_version` equals the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
11. The target row's current state has an outgoing transition to the requested state under the operation being invoked — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). This covers every state-changing operation, including a `to_status` that is unreachable on a resort, member or dispute status change, cancelling a confirmation that is not `CONFIRMED`, reinstating a confirmation that is not `CANCELLED`, declining a request that is not `MATCHED`, cancelling a request whose status is already terminal, booking or withdrawing a getaway offer that is not `LISTED`, withdrawing a deposit whose `deposit_status` is not `BANKED`, extending a deposit whose `deposit_status` is not `BANKED`, attaching funding to a request that is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `WAITLISTED`, reviewing a dispute that is not `OPEN`, requesting information on a dispute that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, responding to a dispute that is not `INFO_REQUESTED`, resolving a dispute that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, and affiliating a resort that is not `PENDING_AFFILIATION`.
12. The acting or referenced member's `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This guards seven operations: banking a deposit, filing an exchange request, attaching funding to a request, creating a confirmation, reinstating a confirmation, booking a getaway offer and filing a dispute.
13. A deposit named as funding for a request, or as the deposit behind a reinstatement, has `deposit_status` `BANKED` and `rental_status` `NOT_LISTED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). This guards three operations: filing an exchange request with a funding deposit, attaching funding to an existing request, and reinstating a confirmation. A deposit merely *cited* as the subject of a `TRADING_POWER` dispute is exempt from this check.
14. A cited confirmation's `status` is `COMPLETED` — otherwise `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED` (409). This guards two operations: filing a quality review, and filing a `RESORT_QUALITY` dispute whose `confirmation_id` is the proof of stay.
15. The resort involved has `affiliation_status` `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` — otherwise `ERR_RESORT_NOT_AFFILIATED` (409). `PENDING_AFFILIATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` all reject. This guards two operations: banking a deposit against that resort, and filing an exchange request against `requested_resort_id`.
16. The deposit involved has not passed its expiry: its `expires_at` is later than the current logical clock instant — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXPIRED` (409). This guards four operations: buying deposit extensions, withdrawing a deposit, filing an exchange request with a funding deposit, and resolving a dispute in a way that would grant power against the cited deposit.
17. A season band's `end_month_day` does not sort before its `start_month_day`, comparing the two day strings as text — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422), on season band creation.
18. An exchange request's `window_end_date` is not earlier than its `window_start_date`, and the span between them in whole days is at most 60 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422), on exchange request creation.
19. A deposit's `check_in_date` is at least 60 days after the date of the current logical clock instant — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422), on deposit creation.
20. A reinstatement happens while the current logical clock instant is no later than 14 days after the predecessor's `cancelled_at` — otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), on confirmation reinstatement.
21. The evaluated date falls inside exactly one season band of the relevant resort, matched on month and day — otherwise `ERR_NO_SEASON_BAND_FOR_DATE` (422). This guards two operations: banking a deposit, where the evaluated date is `check_in_date`, and filing an exchange request, where the evaluated date is `window_start_date` against `requested_resort_id`.
22. A dispute's subject references match its `subject_type` — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (422), on dispute filing. `TRADING_POWER` requires `deposit_id` present with `resort_id` and `confirmation_id` both null; `RESORT_QUALITY` requires `resort_id` and `confirmation_id` both present with `deposit_id` null; and for `RESORT_QUALITY` the deposit awarded by the cited confirmation must belong to the cited resort.
23. A new season band shares no calendar day with an existing band of the same resort — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_BAND_OVERLAP` (409), on season band creation. Adjacent bands are accepted.
24. A new deposit pushes no single day of its occupancy interval above the resort's `units_per_size` capacity for its `unit_size` — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_INVENTORY_CONFLICT` (409), on deposit creation.
25. A member's `active_request_count` is strictly less than that member's tier `max_active_requests` — otherwise `ERR_ACTIVE_REQUEST_LIMIT` (409), on exchange request creation.
26. A deposit's `extension_months_purchased` plus the submitted `months` is at most 12 — otherwise `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409), on deposit extension purchase.
27. A dispute's `round_count` is strictly less than 2 before another information request is issued — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` (409), on dispute information requests.
28. At most one quality review exists per `confirmation_id` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUALITY_REVIEW` (409), on quality review filing.
29. A resort has at least one season band before it can be affiliated — otherwise `ERR_AFFILIATION_REQUIRES_SEASON_BANDS` (409), on resort affiliation.
30. The member booking a getaway offer is not the owner of the underlying deposit — otherwise `ERR_GETAWAY_SELF_BOOKING` (409), on getaway booking.
31. A dues payment's `amount_cents` is at least the member's tier `annual_dues_cents` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DUES_PAYMENT` (422), on dues-payment recording.
32. The paying member's `wallet_credit_cents` is at least the amount about to be debited — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT` (402). This guards five operations, each with its own amount: `quoted_charge_cents` on exchange request creation, the levied `cancellation_fee_cents` on confirmation cancellation, the successor's `reinstatement_fee_cents` on reinstatement, the tier's `extension_fee_cents_per_month` multiplied by the submitted `months` on a deposit extension, and `list_price_cents` on a getaway booking.
33. The confirming member's `trading_power_balance` is at least the awarded deposit's `settlement_power` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRADING_POWER` (402). This guards two operations: creating a confirmation, and reinstating one, where the same check is repeated against the `settlement_power` about to be re-debited.

Two worked precedence cases make the ordering concrete. A lapsed member who submits an exchange request with an empty wallet violates both the enrollment-state rule at position 12 and the wallet-sufficiency rule at position 32; the response is `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` with HTTP 409, never `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT`. A deposit filed against a suspended resort for a date no season band covers violates both the affiliation rule at position 15 and the season-band rule at position 21; the response is `ERR_RESORT_NOT_AFFILIATED` with HTTP 409, never `ERR_NO_SEASON_BAND_FOR_DATE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Resort Into the Network

A resort enters the network as a prospect. A `NetworkOperator` registers it with its trade `name` (between 1 and 120 characters), its geographic `region_code` (between 2 and 8 characters, for example a string like US-CO), its inventory obligation `required_annual_deposit_units` expressed as a whole number of weeks and at least 1, and its `initial_quality_rating`, which must be one of `GOLD_CROWN`, `SILVER_CROWN`, `STANDARD` or `HOSPITALITY`. Two further profile figures may be supplied and otherwise take defaults: `units_per_size`, the aggregate nightly capacity per unit size — how many banked weeks of one unit size may cover the same night — defaults to 2 and must be at least 1; and `demand_index`, the operator-assigned demand rating that feeds the demand factor of every trading-power valuation, defaults to 3 and must be between 1 and 5 inclusive. A freshly registered resort has `affiliation_status` `PENDING_AFFILIATION`, a null `affiliated_on`, a null `quality_score`, a `review_count` of 0, `complaint_strikes` of 0, `downgrade_notches` of 0, and both `deposits_committed_count` and `committed_nights` at 0. While `quality_score` is null the resort's effective `quality_rating` is simply its `initial_quality_rating`.

Before a prospect can be admitted it must publish a season calendar, because without one no week banked against it can be valued. A season band is created by the operator, or by the `ResortManager` of that same resort, and carries the `resort_id` it belongs to, a `band_code` of `PLATINUM`, `GOLD`, `SILVER` or `BRONZE`, and the two ends of the covered period as `start_month_day` and `end_month_day`, each written as a two-digit month and a two-digit day joined by a hyphen. The band covers a closed interval, both endpoints included, interpreted without reference to any particular year, so a band running from 12-20 to 12-31 covers both of those days and every day between. A band whose `end_month_day` sorts before its `start_month_day` as text is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` — no band may wrap around the end of the year. A band that shares even one calendar day with an existing band of the same resort is rejected with `ERR_SEASON_BAND_OVERLAP`, while bands that merely touch are accepted: one ending 06-30 and the next starting 07-01 do not conflict. Each band also exposes `season_multiplier`, the denormalized season factor for its `band_code`, so that the valuation factor is readable without a lookup: 1.50 for `PLATINUM`, 1.25 for `GOLD`, 1.00 for `SILVER` and 0.70 for `BRONZE`.

Granting affiliation moves the resort `PENDING_AFFILIATION` → `ACTIVE`. The grant is available only to an operator and is refused with `ERR_AFFILIATION_REQUIRES_SEASON_BANDS` if no season band exists for that resort yet. On success two things are stamped: `affiliated_on` is set to the current logical clock instant, anchoring the resort's affiliation year, and `quality_rating` is set to `initial_quality_rating`.

Thereafter an operator drives the resort's lifecycle by naming a `to_status`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reason`. From `ACTIVE` the resort may be moved to `PROBATION` — an operator-initiated move carries no additional condition — or to `SUSPENDED`. From `PROBATION` the resort may return to `ACTIVE`, but only when the authoritative deposit-obligation shortfall described below is exactly 0; a resort still short of its obligation cannot be rehabilitated by decree. `PROBATION` may also lead to `SUSPENDED` or straight to `TERMINATED`. From `SUSPENDED` the resort may return to `ACTIVE` or be moved to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal and has no outgoing transitions. Any `to_status` that is not reachable from the resort's current state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Suspension has real inventory consequences: while a resort is `SUSPENDED` no week may be banked against it, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RESORT_NOT_AFFILIATED` — as it is for a resort still `PENDING_AFFILIATION` or already `TERMINATED`. Only `ACTIVE` and `PROBATION` resorts accept new inventory.

The operator-controlled profile stays editable after affiliation. `demand_index`, `units_per_size` and `required_annual_deposit_units` may each be updated independently by an operator, and any field omitted from the update keeps its current value. Raising or lowering `demand_index` changes what future weeks are worth, but it never rewrites a week that has already been banked: an existing deposit's `awarded_power` was frozen at its own creation and is not recomputed by a profile edit.

A resort's obligation is measured against its affiliation year, and the measurement is reported through a compliance view available to the operator and to that resort's own manager. The view names the `resort_id`, the `affiliation_year_start` — the calendar date of `affiliated_on` — and the `affiliation_year_end`, which is 365 days after that start and is **exclusive**, so the last day inside the year is the day before it. It reports `obligation_units`, the resort's `required_annual_deposit_units`, alongside the resort's live `deposits_committed_count` and `committed_nights`. It then reports the same obligation measured two different ways. For both measurements, only deposits of that resort whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED`, `RESERVED` or `USED` are counted, and only those whose relevant date falls inside the half-open affiliation year — on or after the start, strictly before the end. The first measurement, reported as `counted_by_stay_year` with its shortfall as `shortfall_by_stay_year`, keys on each deposit's `check_in_date` and answers how many weeks of stayable inventory the resort actually put into the year. The second, reported as `counted_by_deposit_date` with its shortfall as `shortfall_by_deposit_date`, keys on the calendar date each deposit row was created and answers how many deposit transactions the resort filed during the year, regardless of when the stay falls. Each shortfall is the obligation minus the count, floored at 0 so a resort that over-delivers reports a shortfall of 0 rather than a negative number.

The two measurements diverge for any week filed inside the year for a stay outside it, or filed outside the year for a stay inside it, and the divergence is deliberate: a resort can be compliant by deposit date and simultaneously short by stay year. **Only the stay-year figure has authority.** It is the number the matching run consults when it decides whether to put a resort on probation, and it is the number that must be 0 for a resort to be lifted from `PROBATION` back to `ACTIVE`. The deposit-date figure exists purely for operator reconciliation and may never drive a state change.

### 3.2. Membership Tiers, Enrollment and Annual Dues

Everything a member pays and every window a member enjoys comes from their tier. A `NetworkOperator` defines a tier with a `tier_code` of `BASIC`, `PLUS` or `ELITE` — unique across all tiers — and the ten-figure fee schedule attached to it: `annual_dues_cents`, the dues withheld from each dues payment before the remainder becomes wallet credit; `base_exchange_fee_cents`, the flat base of every exchange charge; `priority_surcharge_cents`, a flat surcharge added to the exchange charge subtotal in return for the tier's priority window; `priority_window_days`, how many days ahead of a matching run's as-of date this tier may see candidate inventory, at least 1; `deposit_first_bonus_pct`, the percentage uplift the tier grants to a deposit-first request's power budget, between 0 and 100 inclusive; `max_active_requests`, the ceiling on the member's simultaneously live exchange requests, at least 1; `cancellation_fee_cents`, levied when one of this tier's confirmations is cancelled; `reinstatement_fee_cents`, levied when a cancelled confirmation is reinstated; and `extension_fee_cents_per_month`, charged per extension month bought on a deposit. All nine monetary and count figures other than the percentage must be at least 0.

Enrollment is operator-driven. A member is created with an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all members, a `full_name` between 1 and 120 characters, the `tier_id` of the tier whose schedule applies, and optionally the `home_resort_id` of the resort behind the member's underlying ownership week — an optional field that defaults to null and is purely descriptive. A new member starts with `enrollment_status` `PENDING_PAYMENT`, a null `dues_paid_through`, a `trading_power_balance` of 0, a `wallet_credit_cents` of 0 and an `active_request_count` of 0. A `PENDING_PAYMENT` member cannot bank weeks, file requests, confirm exchanges, book getaways or file disputes: all of those require `ACTIVE` enrollment and otherwise fail with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Payment activates the enrollment. A dues payment — recorded by the member itself or by an operator on the member's behalf — carries `amount_cents`, the `paid_through_date` (a `YYYY-MM-DD` date) through which dues are now settled, and the concurrency token `expected_member_version` discussed in §2.9. The payment must be at least the tier's `annual_dues_cents`, or it is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DUES_PAYMENT`. When accepted from a `PENDING_PAYMENT` member the enrollment moves `PENDING_PAYMENT` → `ACTIVE`; `dues_paid_through` is set to the submitted `paid_through_date`; and a `DUES_PAYMENT` ledger entry is written whose `cash_delta_cents` is the submitted `amount_cents` minus the tier's `annual_dues_cents`, so the excess over dues — and only the excess — becomes spendable wallet credit. A member who pays exactly the dues receives a ledger entry of zero cash movement.

A lapsed member is reinstated the same way, with one extra condition. The move `LAPSED` → `ACTIVE` requires both that `amount_cents` is at least the tier's `annual_dues_cents` and that the submitted `paid_through_date` is not earlier than the calendar date of the current logical clock instant — paying through a date already in the past does not bring a member back. The side effects are identical: `dues_paid_through` is refreshed to the submitted `paid_through_date` and a `DUES_PAYMENT` entry records the excess as wallet credit. The reverse edge, `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED`, is never triggered by a payment or by an operator; it happens only inside a matching run, as described in §3.5.

Operators also drive enrollment directly by naming a `to_status`, optionally with a `reason`. A member may be suspended from either `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, moving to `SUSPENDED`. The return edge `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` is guarded: `dues_paid_through` must not be earlier than the calendar date of the current logical clock instant, so a member whose dues have run out cannot be un-suspended into a paid-up state. From either `LAPSED` or `SUSPENDED` a member may be moved to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal; there is no path from `ACTIVE` straight to `CANCELLED`, and no path out of `CANCELLED` at all. Cancellation is not quiet: every exchange request of that member whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MATCHED` is moved to `CANCELLED`, and any deposit a cancelled request was holding is released from `RESERVED` back to `BANKED` so that the inventory returns to the market. Since `active_request_count` is defined as the count of that member's `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED` and `MATCHED` requests, the cascade necessarily leaves it at 0.

A member may be moved onto a different tier at any time by an operator supplying the new `tier_id`. The change is forward-looking only: it governs requests created after the change, and any exchange charge already quoted on an existing request is not repriced. A member whose tier changes between filing a request and confirming it is still charged what the earlier tier quoted, but a cancellation fee levied later is drawn from whichever tier the member holds at the moment of cancellation.

### 3.3. Banking a Week and What It Is Worth

Banking is the act that creates inventory. A member — or an operator acting for one — banks a week by naming the depositing `member_id`, the `resort_id` whose week is being surrendered, the `unit_size` of that week, which must be one of `STUDIO`, `ONE_BR`, `TWO_BR` or `THREE_BR`, and the `check_in_date` of the first night, written as `YYYY-MM-DD`. The depositing member must be `ACTIVE` and the resort must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. Weeks cannot be banked at the last minute: a `check_in_date` fewer than 60 days after the calendar date of the current logical clock instant is too late to bank and is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, while a `check_in_date` exactly 60 days out is accepted. The date must also fall inside exactly one season band of that resort, matched on month and day; a date covered by no band is refused with `ERR_NO_SEASON_BAND_FOR_DATE`. Finally, the resort must have room: if any single day of the new week's occupancy interval would exceed the resort's `units_per_size` for that `unit_size`, counting only weeks whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED`, `RESERVED` or `USED`, the bank is refused with `ERR_UNIT_INVENTORY_CONFLICT`.

An accepted deposit is stamped with several derived facts. Its `season_band_code` is the `band_code` of the unique band that covers `check_in_date`. Its `occupancy_end_date` is eight days after `check_in_date` and is the exclusive end of the occupancy interval described in §2.8. Its `expires_at` is computed from `check_in_date` taken at midnight UTC and pushed out by the deposit lifetime of 730 days — two years of shelf life — plus 30 days for each extension month the deposit has bought, which is 0 at creation. Its `deposit_status` opens at `BANKED` and its `rental_status` opens at `NOT_LISTED`; these two dimensions live on the same row and advance through entirely different triggers. Its `settlement_power` is null, its `restoration_haircut_power` is 0, its `extension_months_purchased` is 0, and its `remaining_power` is its `awarded_power` minus its `restoration_haircut_power` — at creation, simply the full awarded figure. The resort's `deposits_committed_count` rises by 1 and its `committed_nights` rises by eight nights. The owner is credited the deposit's `awarded_power` through a `DEPOSIT_CREDIT` ledger entry raised against the `DEPOSIT` family.

**Valuing a week.** The trading power of a week is built from five factors: unit size, season, resort demand, resort quality and lead time. The unit-size base is 30 points for a `STUDIO`, 45 for a `ONE_BR`, 60 for a `TWO_BR` and 80 for a `THREE_BR`. The season factor is the `season_multiplier` of the covering band — 1.50, 1.25, 1.00 or 0.70 for `PLATINUM`, `GOLD`, `SILVER` and `BRONZE` respectively. The demand factor is one plus one tenth for each step the resort's `demand_index` stands above 3, and one minus one tenth for each step it stands below 3, so an index of 5 gives 1.20 and an index of 1 gives 0.80. The quality bonus is a flat point adjustment taken from the resort's effective `quality_rating`: plus 6 points for `GOLD_CROWN`, plus 3 for `SILVER_CROWN`, nothing for `STANDARD`, and minus 4 for `HOSPITALITY`. The lead factor is chosen from the number of whole days between the evaluated date and the calendar date of the as-of instant, a signed figure that goes negative once the evaluated date is in the past: scanning four bands from the top, the first band whose threshold is at most that lead time wins — 1.20 at 270 days or more, 1.10 at 180 days or more, 1.00 at 90 days or more, and 0.85 as the catch-all for anything less, including negative lead times.

The order in which those five factors are combined is normative, and rounding happens at exactly two points and nowhere else. First, multiply the unit-size base by the season factor and by the demand factor, and round that product half up to a whole number. Second, add the quality bonus to that already-rounded whole number; the intermediate result may legitimately be negative for a low-quality, low-demand studio in a bronze week. Third, multiply that sum by the lead factor and round half up to a whole number again. Fourth, take the larger of 1 and that result, so trading power never falls below a floor of 1 point. The quality bonus is added *between* the two roundings and is therefore never scaled by the season or demand factors. Collapsing the two roundings into one — adding the bonus to the unrounded product and rounding only after applying the lead factor — is not the rule and produces different integers. Consider a `STUDIO` in a `PLATINUM` band at a resort with `demand_index` 4 and `quality_rating` `GOLD_CROWN`, evaluated 185 days ahead: the first step is 30 multiplied by 1.50 and by 1.10, giving 49.5, which rounds half up to 50; adding the gold-crown bonus of 6 gives 56; multiplying by the lead factor 1.10 gives 61.6, which rounds half up to 62; and the floor leaves it at 62. The single-rounding shortcut would have produced 61. Implementations must produce 62.

That one formula is evaluated in two distinct variants, against two different instants and two different snapshots of the resort profile. The **at-deposit** variant produces `awarded_power`: its as-of instant is the logical clock instant at which the bank was accepted, its evaluated date is the deposit's `check_in_date`, and it reads the resort's `demand_index` and `quality_rating` as they stood at that moment. It is written once at creation and never recomputed — the single exception being an upheld trading-power dispute, which adds a granted adjustment to it. The **at-settlement** variant produces `settlement_power`: its as-of instant is the `as_of_utc` of the matching run that reserved or getaway-listed the deposit, its evaluated date is again `check_in_date`, and it reads the resort's `demand_index` and `quality_rating` as they stand at that run. It is written once, by the first run that reserves or getaway-lists the deposit, and later runs never overwrite it. The two figures diverge whenever the resort's demand or quality moved between banking and settlement, or whenever the shrinking lead time crossed one of the four lead-factor thresholds. `awarded_power` is what the depositing owner was credited; `settlement_power` is what the exchanging member is charged; the gap between them is reconciled in §3.6.

A read-only valuation view explains a deposit's arithmetic to whoever asks. It reports the `deposit_id`, the `season_band_code` that was resolved, the `unit_size_base` that was used, the `season_multiplier` and the `demand_multiplier` that were applied, the `quality_bonus` that was added, the `awarded_lead_time_days` behind the at-deposit valuation together with the resulting `awarded_power`, the `settlement_lead_time_days` behind the at-settlement valuation together with the resulting `settlement_power`, and the deposit's current `remaining_power`. Where `settlement_power` has not been frozen yet, the settlement side of the view reflects that absence.

**Buying more shelf life.** A deposit's owner may buy extension months while the deposit is still `BANKED` and still unexpired, submitting the number of `months` wanted alongside the `expected_deposit_version` token of §2.9. The purchase is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` when the deposit's existing `extension_months_purchased` plus the submitted `months` would exceed 12 months, and refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXPIRED` when the deposit's `expires_at` is not later than the current logical clock instant. The cash cost is the tier's `extension_fee_cents_per_month` multiplied by the submitted `months`, drawn from the member's `wallet_credit_cents` — insufficient credit is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT` — and posted as an `EXTENSION_FEE` ledger entry. On success `extension_months_purchased` accumulates the purchased months and `expires_at` moves out by 30 days for each month now held, measured from the same `check_in_date` midnight baseline.

**Taking a week back.** An owner may withdraw a banked week, moving it `BANKED` → `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. Withdrawal requires that the week is not currently on the getaway market — its `rental_status` must be `NOT_LISTED` — and that it has not expired, an expired week being refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXPIRED`. On success the deposit's `remaining_power` is reversed out of the owner's balance through a `DEPOSIT_WITHDRAWAL_REVERSAL` ledger entry, and the resort's `deposits_committed_count` falls by 1 while its `committed_nights` falls by eight nights. Because a withdrawal, an expiry and a getaway listing all forfeit `remaining_power` rather than `awarded_power`, an owner who has already suffered a restoration haircut loses only what is left.

### 3.4. Filing an Exchange Request: Budgets, Charges and the Deposit-First Advantage

A member asks for somebody else's week by filing an exchange request naming its own `member_id`, the `requested_resort_id` it wants to stay at, the `requested_unit_size` it wants — candidate weeks must match that size exactly — and the two ends of its acceptable arrival window as `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, both inclusive. It may additionally name a `deposit_id`, and whether it does or not is the single most consequential choice in the request.

The window must be coherent and compact: `window_end_date` may not be earlier than `window_start_date`, and the span between them in whole days may not exceed 60 days, either breach being refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The requesting member must be `ACTIVE`, the requested resort must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` — otherwise `ERR_RESORT_NOT_AFFILIATED` — and `window_start_date` must fall inside exactly one season band of the requested resort, or the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_SEASON_BAND_FOR_DATE`. The member must also have room in its tier: when `active_request_count` has already reached the tier's `max_active_requests` the filing is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_REQUEST_LIMIT`, so the limit is a strict one — a member on a tier permitting three live requests may hold three, not four. On acceptance the request opens with `status` `SUBMITTED`, a `charge_refunded_cents` of 0, a `decline_count` of 0, a `priority_score` of 0, a null `matched_deposit_id`, a null `matched_hold_expires_at` and a null `waitlisted_at`, and the member's `active_request_count` rises by 1.

**Funding mode and the power budget.** `funding_mode` is decided once, at creation, and never changes afterwards. A request created with a `deposit_id` in its body is `DEPOSIT_FIRST`; a request created without one is `REQUEST_FIRST`. A named funding deposit must be owned by the requesting member, must be `BANKED` with `rental_status` `NOT_LISTED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` — and must not have passed its `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXPIRED`. The funding deposit's `awarded_power` becomes the raw material of `power_budget`, the affordability ceiling the matching pass compares candidate weeks against. For a `DEPOSIT_FIRST` request the budget is the funding deposit's `awarded_power` multiplied by one hundred plus the tier's `deposit_first_bonus_pct`, divided by one hundred, with any fraction discarded — a tier granting a 10 percent deposit-first bonus turns 62 awarded points into a budget of 68. For a `REQUEST_FIRST` request the budget is the funding deposit's `awarded_power` multiplied by one hundred and divided by one hundred and ten, again with any fraction discarded, which is a 10 percent power surcharge for having asked before banking: the same 62 awarded points yield a budget of 56. While a request has no funding deposit at all its `power_budget` is 0.

A `REQUEST_FIRST` request may attach a funding deposit later by supplying a `deposit_id`, and that attachment is only available while the request is still `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED`; a member must be `ACTIVE` to do it, and the deposit must satisfy the same availability rule, failing with `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` otherwise. Attaching a deposit recomputes `power_budget` from that deposit's `awarded_power` — but `funding_mode` stays `REQUEST_FIRST`, so the surcharged, shrunken budget applies. This asymmetry is intentional network policy: banking first is strictly better than asking first for the identical week, both in budget and, as §3.5 shows, in queue position.

**Quoting the charge.** `required_power_estimate` is written at creation by evaluating the week-valuation formula of §3.3 for the *requested* week rather than a banked one: the unit size is `requested_unit_size`, the evaluated date is `window_start_date`, the resort is `requested_resort_id`, and the as-of instant is the logical clock instant at which the request was created. The cash charge follows from the gap between that estimate and the budget. First take the power gap, which is the `required_power_estimate` minus the `power_budget`, or 0 when the budget already covers the estimate — never a negative number. Multiply the gap by 350 cents per point of shortfall to get the shortfall charge. Add the tier's `base_exchange_fee_cents`, that shortfall charge, and the tier's `priority_surcharge_cents` together to get the subtotal. Note that a `REQUEST_FIRST` request filed with no deposit at all has a budget of 0, so its entire `required_power_estimate` is charged as shortfall.

Two bands are then selected, and **both are keyed on that pre-credit subtotal**. The deposit-first credit band, scanned from the top, gives 0 percent for a subtotal of 9999 cents or less, 5 percent for a subtotal of 19999 cents or less, and 10 percent for anything above that; the credit itself is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage and divided by one hundred with any fraction discarded, and it is granted only to a `DEPOSIT_FIRST` request — a `REQUEST_FIRST` request receives a credit of 0 regardless of its subtotal. The processing-fee band, also scanned from the top, adds 1200 cents for a subtotal of 14999 cents or less, 2000 cents for a subtotal of 29999 cents or less, and 2900 cents above that. The `quoted_charge_cents` is the subtotal, minus the credit, plus the processing fee. Neither band may be keyed on the final quoted charge or on the subtotal after the credit has been taken off: a subtotal of 30400 cents on a `DEPOSIT_FIRST` request earns a 10 percent credit of 3040 cents and a processing fee of 2900 cents because 30400 exceeds 29999, for a total of 30260 cents. Keying the processing fee on the post-credit 27360 would wrongly yield 2000 cents and a total of 29360. Implementations must produce 30260.

The quoted charge is debited at creation as an `EXCHANGE_CHARGE` ledger entry whose `cash_delta_cents` is the negative of `quoted_charge_cents`, raised against the `EXCHANGE_REQUEST` family, and a member whose `wallet_credit_cents` cannot cover it is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT` before anything is written. The charge is refunded exactly once, as a single `EXCHANGE_CHARGE_REFUND` entry, whenever the request leaves the pipeline unfulfilled — that is, on reaching `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED` or `DENIED` — in the amount `quoted_charge_cents` minus whatever `charge_refunded_cents` already records, with `charge_refunded_cents` accumulating what has been returned. A request that reaches `CONFIRMED` is never refunded: the member got its holiday.

**Walking away early.** A member may cancel its own request outright while the request is `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `MATCHED`, and the destination is `CANCELLED`, which is terminal; cancelling a request already in a terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Cancellation from any of those three states refunds the unrefunded portion of the exchange charge as described above and lowers the member's `active_request_count` by 1. Cancelling from `MATCHED` does one thing more: the week the request was holding is released from `RESERVED` back to `BANKED` so it can be offered to somebody else, and the request's `matched_deposit_id` and `matched_hold_expires_at` are cleared.

### 3.5. The Operations Run: Seven Pinned Steps

Nothing in this network drifts forward on its own. A `NetworkOperator` executes an operations run — the matching run — and every sweep, expiry, match and probation decision happens inside that single request. The operator may attach a free-text `note` for the audit trail. The run is stamped with a `run_sequence` equal to one more than the highest sequence already recorded, or 1 when no run exists yet, and with `as_of_utc`, the logical clock instant at which the run was accepted; every comparison below is made against that `as_of_utc`. The run opens as `PENDING` and moves `PENDING` → `COMPLETED` at the end of the pinned step sequence, at which point all eleven counter fields are frozen at their post-sweep values and the completed run is returned to the caller. Runs are read back individually or listed in `run_sequence` ascending order, and their counters are the operator's record of what the checkpoint did.

The seven steps run in exactly the order given, and each step reads the state its predecessors left behind. Ordering *within* a step is also pinned, so that a run over the same starting state always produces the same outcome.

**Step 0 — dues lapse.** Every member whose `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `dues_paid_through` is earlier than the calendar date of `as_of_utc` moves `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Each such member raises `lapsed_member_count` by 1. A member whose `dues_paid_through` falls exactly on the run's date is still paid up and does not lapse.

**Step 1 — deposit expiry.** Every deposit whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED` and whose `expires_at` is not later than `as_of_utc` moves `BANKED` → `EXPIRED`, processed in `expires_at` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending. Each expiry forfeits the deposit's `remaining_power` from its owner through a `DEPOSIT_EXPIRY_FORFEIT` ledger entry — clamped, as always, so the owner's balance lands no lower than 0 — lowers the resort's `deposits_committed_count` by 1 and its `committed_nights` by eight nights, and moves any `LISTED` getaway offer of that deposit to `EXPIRED`. Each expiry raises `expired_deposit_count` by 1. A deposit whose `expires_at` falls exactly on `as_of_utc` does expire, because the comparison is "not later than".

**Step 2 — request expiry and denial.** Requests are examined in `created_at` ascending order, and for each request the three checks below are applied in this order. First, a `MATCHED` request whose `matched_hold_expires_at` is not later than `as_of_utc` moves `MATCHED` → `EXPIRED` and releases its held week from `RESERVED` back to `BANKED`, clearing `matched_deposit_id` and `matched_hold_expires_at`. Second, a `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` request whose `window_end_date` is earlier than the calendar date of `as_of_utc` moves to `EXPIRED`, having waited out its own arrival window. Third, a `WAITLISTED` request whose `funding_mode` is `DEPOSIT_FIRST` and whose funding deposit's `deposit_status` is neither `BANKED` nor `RESERVED` moves to `DENIED`, because the week that was supposed to pay for the exchange has left the pool. Every request leaving the pipeline in this step is refunded `quoted_charge_cents` minus `charge_refunded_cents` through a single `EXCHANGE_CHARGE_REFUND` entry. Expiries raise `expired_request_count`; denials raise `denied_request_count`.

**Step 3 — the matching pass.** Every request whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` after the previous steps has its `priority_score` recomputed and stored, and the surviving requests are then processed in `priority_score` descending order, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. A request's `id` is never a tie-break input.

The score is assembled from a tier weight, a funding bonus, a tenure reward and two penalties. The tier weight contributes nothing for `BASIC`, 20 points for `PLUS` and 40 points for `ELITE`. A `DEPOSIT_FIRST` request earns a further 15 points; a `REQUEST_FIRST` request earns nothing there. Tenure is counted in whole years: take the number of days between the calendar date the member was created and the calendar date of `as_of_utc`, divide by 365 and discard the fraction, award 1 point per whole year, and cap the tenure contribution at 10 points, so a member of eleven years' standing scores the same tenure as one of ten. Two penalties are then subtracted: 5 points for every decline the request has already recorded in `decline_count`, and a flat 25 points when the member's `dues_paid_through` is earlier than the request's `window_start_date` — a member asking for a week its dues do not yet reach is pushed down the queue. The result is floored at 0, so no request can carry a negative score.

For each request in turn the run builds a candidate pool of weeks it could take. A deposit is a candidate only when all nine of the following hold: its `resort_id` equals the request's `requested_resort_id`; its `unit_size` equals the request's `requested_unit_size`; its `deposit_status` is `BANKED`; its `rental_status` is `NOT_LISTED`; its `member_id` is not the requesting member's, so nobody is matched to their own week; its `expires_at` is later than `as_of_utc`; its `check_in_date` falls between the request's `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` with both endpoints included; the number of whole days from the calendar date of `as_of_utc` to that `check_in_date` is at most the requesting member's tier `priority_window_days`, which is how a tier's visibility horizon is enforced; and the deposit's `settlement_power` for this run is at most the request's `power_budget`. Because trading power is floored at 1, a request that still has no funding deposit — and therefore a `power_budget` of 0 — can never assemble a non-empty pool.

When the pool is non-empty the request moves `SUBMITTED` → `MATCHED`, or `WAITLISTED` → `MATCHED` if it had been waiting from an earlier run. The winning week is the top-ranked candidate under the ranking `settlement_power` descending, then `check_in_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; the deposit's `id` is never a ranking input. Matching sets the request's `matched_deposit_id` to the winner, moves that week `BANKED` → `RESERVED`, freezes the week's `settlement_power` using the at-settlement variant of §3.3 if it is not frozen already, sets `matched_hold_expires_at` to 48 hours — two days — after `as_of_utc`, and raises `matched_count` by 1. A week reserved by an earlier request inside this same pass is no longer `BANKED` and is therefore not a candidate for any later request in the pass, which is what makes the priority ordering meaningful. When the pool is empty the request instead moves `SUBMITTED` → `WAITLISTED` (a request already `WAITLISTED` simply stays there), stamps `waitlisted_at` with `as_of_utc` on first entry, leaves `matched_deposit_id` null, touches no deposit at all, and raises `waitlisted_count` by 1.

**Step 4 — confirmation completion.** Every confirmation whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `awarded_check_in_date` plus seven stay nights is not later than the calendar date of `as_of_utc` moves `CONFIRMED` → `COMPLETED`, processed in `awarded_check_in_date` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending, raising `completed_confirmation_count` by 1 each time. Completion is what unlocks the post-stay quality review of §3.8 and the resort-quality dispute of §3.9: until the stay is `COMPLETED`, both are refused with `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED`.

**Step 5 — getaway listing.** Inventory nobody wanted is pushed onto the cash market before it can rot. Every deposit whose `deposit_status` is `BANKED`, whose `rental_status` is `NOT_LISTED`, and whose expiry is close — the number of whole days from the calendar date of `as_of_utc` to the calendar date of its `expires_at` being at most 45 days — freezes its `settlement_power` if that is still null, moves `NOT_LISTED` → `LISTED_GETAWAY`, forfeits its `remaining_power` from its owner through a `GETAWAY_LISTING_FORFEIT` ledger entry, and gains a getaway offer with `status` `LISTED`, priced as described in §3.7, whose `created_by_run_id` names this run. Each listing raises `getaway_listed_count` by 1. The sweep is processed in `expires_at` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending.

**Step 6 — resort probation.** Only resorts that lost at least one week to step 1 of *this* run are evaluated, in `created_at` ascending order. For each, the authoritative stay-year shortfall of §3.1 is recomputed, and when that shortfall is at least 1 and the resort's `affiliation_status` is `ACTIVE`, the resort moves `ACTIVE` → `PROBATION` and `probation_resort_count` rises by 1. Resorts untouched by step 1 are not evaluated at all, so a run that expires nothing puts nobody on probation, however far behind its obligation a resort may be.

### 3.6. Confirming, Declining, Completing, Cancelling and Reinstating

Being matched is an offer, not a booking. The member behind a `MATCHED` request has 48 hours of logical-clock time to accept it, and acceptance means creating a confirmation that names the `exchange_request_id` being confirmed. The member must be `ACTIVE`, must own the request, and the request's `matched_hold_expires_at` must still be later than the current logical clock instant — a hold that has run out cannot be converted, and the request will be swept to `EXPIRED` by the next run's step 2. The confirming member's `trading_power_balance` must be at least the awarded week's `settlement_power`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRADING_POWER`.

Confirmation is where the cross-aggregate binding happens: one member's request is bound to another member's week. The request moves `MATCHED` → `CONFIRMED` and the member's `active_request_count` falls by 1. A confirmation row is created with `status` `CONFIRMED`, its `awarded_deposit_id` copied from the request's `matched_deposit_id`, its `awarded_check_in_date` copied from that week's `check_in_date` — the date that later anchors both the completion sweep and the cancellation-window arithmetic — and its `power_debited` set to that week's `settlement_power`. The week itself moves `RESERVED` → `USED`. Two ledger entries may be written. The first is unconditional: an `EXCHANGE_DEBIT` taking `settlement_power` from the confirming member. The second is the settlement reconciliation, and it exists because the week was valued twice.

**Reconciling the two valuations.** The settlement delta is the week's `settlement_power` minus its `awarded_power`. When that delta is not zero, a `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry is written against the **depositing owner** — the week's `member_id`, never the confirming member — with a `power_delta` equal to the delta, a `related_entity_type` of `DEPOSIT` and a `related_entity_id` naming the week. When the delta is exactly zero, no ledger row is written at all. The adjustment is posted exactly once, inside the confirmation, alongside the exchange debit; a getaway listing never posts a settlement adjustment. If the delta is negative and large enough to drive the depositing owner's `trading_power_balance` below 0, the recorded `power_delta` is clamped to the negative of that owner's current balance, so the balance lands on exactly 0 and the ledger identity of §2.7 stays exact. The effect of the whole mechanism is that the network's power supply and demand stay equal: whatever the exchanging member paid over or under what the owner was originally credited flows to the owner, and the exchanging member is never touched by the correction.

**Declining an offer.** A member may refuse the week it was offered instead of confirming it, provided the request is `MATCHED` with a `matched_deposit_id` set. Declining moves the request `MATCHED` → `WAITLISTED`, releases the held week from `RESERVED` back to `BANKED`, clears `matched_deposit_id` and `matched_hold_expires_at`, stamps `waitlisted_at` with the current logical clock instant, and raises `decline_count` by 1 — which lowers `priority_score` by 5 points at every subsequent run. The loop `MATCHED` → `WAITLISTED` → `MATCHED` may run repeatedly, and re-entry into `MATCHED` is not equivalent to the first entry: the request now carries a higher `decline_count`, ranks lower, and may well be offered a different week. Declining does not refund any part of the exchange charge, because the request is still live. A request that has not been offered anything cannot be declined, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. One condition governs every release of a held week back to `BANKED`, whether the release comes from a decline, from the cancellation of the holding request, or from the operations run reaping an elapsed hold: a week may only be released while no confirmation naming it as its `awarded_deposit_id` is still in `CONFIRMED`. Once a hold has been converted into a live confirmation the week has left `RESERVED` for `USED`, and only cancelling that confirmation can return it.

**Cancelling a confirmed exchange.** A confirmation may be cancelled by its own member or by an operator on that member's behalf, and only from `CONFIRMED`; a confirmation already `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `REINSTATED` cannot be cancelled again and yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Cancellation moves `CONFIRMED` → `CANCELLED`, stamps `cancelled_at` with the current logical clock instant, moves the awarded week `USED` → `BANKED`, and levies the cancellation charge from the member's wallet as a `CANCELLATION_FEE` ledger entry, with insufficient wallet credit refused in advance by `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT`.

What the member gets back, and what it costs, turns on one hard day count. Take the number of whole days from the calendar date of the current logical clock instant to the confirmation's `awarded_check_in_date`. When that count is at least 30 days, the cancellation is a full restoration: `restoration_variant` is recorded as `FULL_RESTORE`, `restored_power` equals the confirmation's `power_debited` and is credited back to the cancelling member through an `EXCHANGE_RESTORATION` ledger entry, the levied `cancellation_fee_cents` is exactly the member's tier `cancellation_fee_cents`, and the week is left otherwise untouched: its `restoration_haircut_power` is unchanged so its `remaining_power` is unchanged, its `expires_at` is unchanged, its `rental_status` is unchanged and no getaway offer is created. When that count is under 30 days — including a count of zero or a negative one, for a cancellation on or after the arrival date — the cancellation is prorated: `restoration_variant` is recorded as `PRORATED_RESTORE`, the cancelling member is still credited `restored_power` equal to `power_debited` through an `EXCHANGE_RESTORATION` entry, but the depositing owner is debited a haircut equal to the week's `awarded_power` multiplied by 25 percent and rounded *up* to a whole point, posted as a `RESTORATION_HAIRCUT` ledger entry; the week's `restoration_haircut_power` rises by that haircut and its `remaining_power` is recomputed as `awarded_power` minus the accumulated haircut; its `expires_at` is still unchanged; its `rental_status` moves `NOT_LISTED` → `LISTED_GETAWAY` and a getaway offer is created for it, priced as in §3.7 with no `created_by_run_id` because no run listed it; because that move to `LISTED_GETAWAY` is the same edge a run-driven listing takes, it carries the same consequence, so whatever `remaining_power` survives the haircut is forfeited from the depositing owner through a `GETAWAY_LISTING_FORFEIT` ledger entry; and the levied `cancellation_fee_cents` is the tier's `cancellation_fee_cents` plus a late-cancellation surcharge of 7500 cents. The threshold is inclusive on the full-restore side: a cancellation exactly 30 days before the awarded arrival date is `FULL_RESTORE`, and one day later is `PRORATED_RESTORE`.

**Changing one's mind about a cancellation.** A cancelled confirmation may be reinstated by its member within 14 days: the current logical clock instant must be no later than 14 days after `cancelled_at`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The member must be `ACTIVE`, and the awarded week must still be available — its `deposit_status` `BANKED` and its `rental_status` `NOT_LISTED`, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, which is exactly what a prorated cancellation's getaway listing makes impossible until the listing is pulled. Reinstatement moves the old confirmation `CANCELLED` → `REINSTATED`, a terminal state, and creates a **successor** confirmation with `status` `CONFIRMED` whose `reinstated_from_confirmation_id` names the predecessor row. The successor's `reinstatement_fee_cents` is the member's tier `reinstatement_fee_cents` and is levied from the wallet as a `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` ledger entry. The awarded week's `settlement_power` is re-debited from the member — the same `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRADING_POWER` check applies to that re-debit — and the week moves `BANKED` → `USED` once more. Confirmations may be listed by the predecessor they succeed, so an operator can follow a cancellation-and-reinstatement chain end to end.

### 3.7. Unmatched Inventory: Getaway Listings, Pricing and Rentals

Some weeks never find a match, and a week that expires unsold is a total loss to everybody. The getaway market is the network's salvage channel: the week is rented to a member for cash instead of being exchanged for trading power. Offers arise in exactly two ways. Most are created by step 5 of an operations run, and those carry a `created_by_run_id` naming the run that listed them. The rest are created by a prorated cancellation, which lists the returned week immediately; those carry a null `created_by_run_id`, because no run listed them. Either way the offer names the `deposit_id` of the week behind it, opens with `status` `LISTED`, and carries a null `booked_by_member_id` and a null `booked_at` until somebody rents it.

Pricing combines three independent factors into one expression that is rounded exactly once and then clamped into a fixed corridor. Start from the raw value of the week: its `settlement_power` multiplied by 900 cents per power point. Apply the resort's demand: the demand premium is 5 percentage points for each step the resort's `demand_index` stands above 3 at the listing instant, and minus 5 percentage points for each step below, so the raw value is multiplied by one hundred plus that premium and divided by one hundred. Keep that adjusted value at full precision — do not round it. Then apply urgency, recorded on the offer as `urgency_discount_pct`, chosen from the number of whole days between the calendar date of the listing instant and the week's `check_in_date`: scanning four bands from the top, the first whose threshold is at most that day count wins, giving no discount at 120 days or more, 10 percent at 60 days or more, 20 percent at 30 days or more, and 35 percent as the catch-all below that. Multiply the adjusted value by one hundred minus the discount percentage and divide by one hundred, and round that result half up to a whole number of cents — this is the only rounding in the whole calculation, and it deliberately differs from the twice-rounding week valuation of §3.3; the two calculations must not share a rounding helper that rounds every intermediate product. Finally clamp: apply the floor of 29900 cents first and then the ceiling of 249900 cents, so a computed price below the floor becomes exactly 29900 and one above the ceiling becomes exactly 249900, and store the result as `list_price_cents`. Pricing always has a settlement figure to work from: a week listed by step 5 has its `settlement_power` frozen by that same run before pricing, and a week listed by a prorated cancellation already carries the `settlement_power` written by the run that reserved it.

Renting is a member action. The booking member supplies its own `member_id`, must be `ACTIVE`, and must not be the owner of the underlying week — a self-booking is refused with `ERR_GETAWAY_SELF_BOOKING` — and its `wallet_credit_cents` must cover `list_price_cents`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_WALLET_CREDIT`. Only a `LISTED` offer can be booked; anything else yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On success the offer moves `LISTED` → `BOOKED`, which is terminal, stamping `booked_by_member_id` with the renter and `booked_at` with the current logical clock instant; the week's `rental_status` moves `LISTED_GETAWAY` → `RENTED`, also terminal; and `list_price_cents` is debited from the renter through a `GETAWAY_BOOKING_PAYMENT` ledger entry raised against the `GETAWAY_OFFER` family. No trading power changes hands in a getaway rental — the week's power was already forfeited when it was listed.

An operator may pull a listing back off the market while it is still `LISTED`, moving the offer `LISTED` → `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. Withdrawal returns the week's `rental_status` from `LISTED_GETAWAY` to `NOT_LISTED` and re-credits the week's `remaining_power` to the depositing owner through a `DEPOSIT_CREDIT` ledger entry, so a week pulled back becomes exchangeable again and its owner is made whole for the listing forfeit. The remaining path out of `LISTED` is `LISTED` → `EXPIRED`, driven not by any operator action but by step 1 of an operations run: when the underlying week itself moves `BANKED` → `EXPIRED` in that run, its `LISTED` offer moves to `EXPIRED` alongside it, with no further ledger consequence of its own.

The exchange pipeline and the getaway pipeline are two independent state dimensions on the same week, and they never write each other's field. The triggers that move `deposit_status` never touch `rental_status`, and the triggers that move `rental_status` never touch `deposit_status`. They communicate only through guards: a week can be reserved by a matching run or withdrawn by its owner only while its `rental_status` is `NOT_LISTED`, and a week can be listed on the getaway market only while its `deposit_status` is `BANKED`.

### 3.8. Post-Stay Reviews and the Quality Ladder

Once a stay is `COMPLETED`, the member who took it may rate the resort exactly once. The review is filed against the resort named in the path — recorded on the review as `resort_id`, which must be the resort of the week the confirmation awarded — and cites the `confirmation_id` that proves the stay. That confirmation must be owned by the reviewing member and must be `COMPLETED`, an incomplete stay being refused with `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED`, and at most one review may exist per `confirmation_id`, a second attempt being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUALITY_REVIEW`. The member rates three dimensions, each an integer from 1 to 10 inclusive: `cleanliness_score`, `service_score` and `amenities_score`. Because the review changes the resort's rating state, the filing also requires the `expected_resort_version` token of §2.9.

Each review carries its own `composite_score`: the cleanliness rating weighted at 0.40, plus the service rating weighted at 0.35, plus the amenities rating weighted at 0.25, rounded half up to two decimal places. A perfect ten in every dimension therefore composites to 10.00 and a straight one to 1.00.

The resort's own figures are recomputed after every review. `review_count` is the number of that resort's reviews. The scoring window is that resort's reviews ordered by `created_at` descending, taking the first eight; when the review sitting at the eighth position ties on `created_at` with reviews beyond it, every tied review is included as well, so the window may legitimately hold more than eight rows. While `review_count` is under 2 the resort's `quality_score` stays null and the rating derived from scores is simply the resort's `initial_quality_rating` — a single glowing or scathing review does not move a resort's band. From the second review onward, `quality_score` is the mean of the window's `composite_score` values — their sum divided by the number of reviews in the window — rounded half up to two decimal places. Note carefully that the mean is taken over the *already-rounded* per-review composites, never over their raw weighted sums. The score then selects a band, scanning from the top: a `quality_score` of at least 8.50 gives `GOLD_CROWN`, at least 7.00 gives `SILVER_CROWN`, at least 5.00 gives `STANDARD`, and anything below 5.00 gives `HOSPITALITY`.

That score-derived band is not necessarily what the network publishes. The four ratings form a ladder running `GOLD_CROWN`, `SILVER_CROWN`, `STANDARD`, `HOSPITALITY` from best to worst, and the resort's effective `quality_rating` is the score-derived band shifted down that ladder by its `downgrade_notches`, clamped at the bottom rung: a resort scoring into `GOLD_CROWN` with two notches publishes `STANDARD`, and one with three notches publishes `HOSPITALITY` and cannot fall further. `downgrade_notches` itself ranges from 0 to 3 and is only ever raised by upheld quality complaints, as §3.9 describes. Because `quality_rating` feeds the quality bonus of the week-valuation formula, a downgraded resort's future weeks are worth measurably less trading power — a `GOLD_CROWN` resort that slides to `STANDARD` loses the 6-point bonus entirely, and one that slides to `HOSPITALITY` turns it into a 4-point penalty. Reviews themselves are readable individually and listable, filterable by the resort or the confirmation they concern.

### 3.9. Disputes Over Valuations and Resort Quality

Members have two grievances worth adjudicating, and they follow one pipeline. A dispute is filed by an `ACTIVE` member naming its own `member_id`, a `subject_type` of either `TRADING_POWER` or `RESORT_QUALITY`, and a `narrative` between 1 and 2000 characters setting out the complaint. The subject references must match the type exactly. A `TRADING_POWER` dispute names the `deposit_id` of the week whose valuation is challenged and must leave `resort_id` and `confirmation_id` both null; the cited week must belong to the filing member, but it is deliberately exempt from the availability check that other operations impose, so a member may dispute the valuation of a week that has already been reserved or used. A `RESORT_QUALITY` dispute names both the `resort_id` complained about and the `confirmation_id` that proves the member stayed there, and must leave `deposit_id` null; the week awarded by that confirmation must belong to the cited resort, and the confirmation must be `COMPLETED` or the filing is refused with `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED`. Any other combination of subject references is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. A new dispute opens with `status` `OPEN`, a `round_count` of 0, a `granted_power_adjustment` of 0 and a null `resolution_note`.

Adjudication belongs exclusively to the `DisputeAnalyst`, and the filing member is never the adjudicator of its own complaint. The analyst takes an `OPEN` dispute into review, moving it `OPEN` → `UNDER_REVIEW`. From there the analyst may ask the member for more, submitting a `question`; that moves the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` and raises `round_count` by 1, but only while `round_count` is strictly below 2 — the third attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT`. The filing member replies with an `answer`, moving the dispute `INFO_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW`. The cycle `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` may therefore run at most twice, and re-entry into `UNDER_REVIEW` is not equivalent to the first entry because `round_count` has advanced and the analyst's remaining questions have been consumed.

Resolution ends the dispute. The analyst supplies an `outcome` of either `UPHELD` or `DENIED` together with a `resolution_note` recording the rationale, and for a trading-power grievance may supply a `granted_power_adjustment`. A `DENIED` outcome moves the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW` → `RESOLVED_DENIED`, a terminal state, and changes nothing else in the system beyond recording the note. An `UPHELD` outcome moves the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW` → `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, also terminal, and its consequences depend on the grievance family.

Upholding a `TRADING_POWER` dispute requires that the cited week is still economically live: its `deposit_status` must be `BANKED`, `RESERVED` or `USED`, and it must not have passed its `expires_at`; a week that is `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`, or one whose expiry instant has passed, is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXPIRED`. When the grant proceeds, `granted_power_adjustment` is added both to the week's `awarded_power` and to its `remaining_power` — the one and only circumstance in which a frozen `awarded_power` is ever revised — and the same number of points is credited to the member through a `DISPUTE_POWER_GRANT` ledger entry.

Upholding a `RESORT_QUALITY` dispute strikes the resort rather than paying the member. The cited resort's `complaint_strikes` rises by 1. When that counter reaches 2, three things happen together: `complaint_strikes` resets to 0, `downgrade_notches` rises by 1, and the resort's `quality_rating` is recomputed by the ladder rule of §3.8 with the new notch count applied — after which the resort moves `ACTIVE` → `PROBATION`. A first upheld complaint therefore leaves the resort's rating and affiliation untouched and merely arms the counter; the second one downgrades and sidelines it. A resort already on `PROBATION` or in any other state is not moved by the cascade, but the strike and any resulting notch are recorded all the same.

A member may abandon its own dispute at any point before resolution: a dispute that is `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` may be withdrawn by its filer, moving it to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. The analyst can never withdraw a dispute, just as it can never file one or answer its own information request. Once a dispute is `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `RESOLVED_DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` it accepts no further action, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
