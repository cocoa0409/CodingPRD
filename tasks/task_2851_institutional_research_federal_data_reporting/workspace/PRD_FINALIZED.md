# Institutional Research & Federal Data Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every university institutional research (IR) office lives twice a year through the same compressed ordeal: a census date arrives, extracts are pulled from a dozen local systems that were never designed to agree with one another, and a small team has weeks to turn those extracts into federal survey components that will be audited, ranked, litigated and quoted for a decade. The office's real problem is not arithmetic — it is *governance*. Which extract was the reported figure drawn from? Who prepared it, who certified it, and were those two people different? When a registrar approves a late allowable exclusion, which published graduation rate silently becomes wrong, and who is accountable for restating it? This API exists to make those questions answerable by construction rather than by institutional memory.

The system models one institution-year of federal reporting as a **reporting cycle** whose governance posture gates every downstream write. Census extracts are registered against the cycle as immutable **frozen files** occupying non-overlapping coverage windows; survey components are prepared from a named extract, machine-checked against prior-year values, explained, adjudicated, certified by someone other than the preparer, and finally transmitted. In parallel, the office maintains a mapping layer between local system field paths and federal data-element definitions, tracks graduation and retention cohorts with their allowable exclusions, publishes internal dashboard figures under formal restated-data change control, resubmits selected metrics to state, consortium and ranking recipients with a reconciliation tolerance, and screens external data requests for small-cell disclosure risk before any number leaves the building.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **the same underlying number is legitimately different in two places**: a graduation rate computed for the federal collection removes approved allowable exclusions from both numerator and denominator, while the rate a ranking survey demands ignores exclusions entirely — both are stored, and neither is a rounding of the other. Second, **corrections are first-class events, not repairs**: reopening a frozen cycle, decertifying a component, or approving a late exclusion each cascade deterministically into every figure derived from them, leaving an auditable trail of pending restatements rather than quietly rewriting history. The API is mounted under the `/ir` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold everywhere: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, clock semantics, posture gating, cascade discipline, concurrency control, rounding, and the order in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must identify its actor through two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, carrying the acting operator's identifier, and `X-User-Role`, carrying that operator's institutional capacity. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. The five roles are `IRAnalyst`, `IRDirector`, `RegistrarOfficer`, `DataSteward` and `DisclosureOfficer`. Each endpoint declares the roles permitted to call it (see §4); a syntactically valid actor whose role is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five capacities divide the work as follows. An `IRAnalyst` assembles survey components, reports metrics, explains prior-year variances, proposes element mappings, drafts external submissions, reconciles them, publishes dashboard figures and files restatement requests. An `IRDirector` is the approving authority: certifying and federally submitting components, adjudicating edit checks and exclusion claims, deciding restatements, overriding held variances, transmitting external submissions, withdrawing publications, and freezing or reopening the cycle. A `RegistrarOfficer` owns the census snapshot and the student roster: opening cycles, registering census extracts, locking the census, defining and locking cohorts, recording tracked outcomes and filing allowable-exclusion claims. A `DataSteward` owns the federal definition library and the verdict on local-to-federal element mappings. A `DisclosureOfficer` intakes data requests, builds the crosstab, runs disclosure avoidance and closes the request, and is the only role permitted to write disclosure artefacts. Read access is broad — every role may read cycles, extracts, components, metrics, edit checks, cohorts, members, definitions, mappings, submissions, publications and restatements — with one deliberate narrowing: data-request and disclosure-cell reads are visible only to `DisclosureOfficer` and `IRDirector`.

`X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** in this system. It is not the primary key of any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any resource's `id`. This applies uniformly to all five roles: when `X-User-Role` is `IRAnalyst`, `IRDirector`, `RegistrarOfficer`, `DataSteward` or `DisclosureOfficer`, the supplied `X-User-ID` is an opaque string that the server *records* on the rows the actor touches but never resolves to an entity. The recording sites are: `prepared_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a survey component, `explained_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an edit check, `exclusion_claimed_by_user_id` and `exclusion_reviewed_by_user_id` on a cohort member, `validated_by_user_id` on an element mapping, `overridden_by_user_id` on an external submission, `published_by_user_id` on a dashboard publication, `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a restatement request, and `screened_by_user_id` on a data request. Because these fields hold opaque identifiers rather than foreign keys, the only comparisons the system performs against them are string equality checks between the acting `X-User-ID` and a previously recorded actor — which is exactly how segregation of duties is enforced (§2.10).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Deterministic List Ordering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` states how many items to skip and defaults to 0. Both values are echoed in the response envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count` (the number of items matching the filter, ignoring pagination) and `has_more` (true when items remain beyond the current window). No other pagination mechanism exists.

Unless a list endpoint says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four listings override that default because their domain has a stronger natural order, and the override is part of the contract: reported metrics are listed by `metric_key` ascending; edit checks are listed by `sequence_no` ascending, which reproduces the exact order in which a preparation run raised them; dashboard publications are listed by `effective_from` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending as a tie-break; and crosstab cells are listed by `dimension_group` ascending and then by `dimension_key` ascending.

Every list endpoint additionally accepts a set of equality filters drawn from the resource's own attributes, and filters compose conjunctively. Census extracts filter on `cycle_id`, `domain`, `status` and `supersedes_frozen_file_id`. Survey components filter on `cycle_id`, `frozen_file_id`, `prep_status` and `edit_status`. Reported metrics filter on `component_id` and `metric_key`. Edit checks filter on `component_id`, `status` and `severity`. Cohorts filter on `cycle_id`, `cohort_type` and `status`; cohort members filter on `cohort_id`, `exclusion_status` and `outcome`. Federal definitions filter on `survey_component`, `effective_cycle_code` and `required`. Element mappings filter on `cycle_id`, `definition_id`, `status` and `local_system_code`. External submissions filter on `cycle_id`, `component_id`, `target_type` and `status`. Publications filter on `component_id`, `metric_key` and `publication_status`; restatement requests filter on `publication_id`, `status` and `origin`. Data requests filter on `cycle_id`, `status` and `requester_type`; crosstab cells filter on `request_id`, `dimension_group` and `suppression_state`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Immutable Definition Library

Almost every resource in this system carries four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by a client.
- **`created_at`** (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) at which the row was created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent modification, in the same format.
- **`version`** (integer): starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade or a recomputation.

There is exactly one exception, and it is deliberate. A `FederalDefinition` is configuration, not a workflow artefact: it is registered once for a survey component and a cycle code and is never updated or deleted. Federal definitions therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — **both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a federal definition's representation**, and no endpoint exists to modify one.

Recomputation counts as mutation. When a reporting cycle's `active_record_count` and `frozen_file_count` are rewritten because an extract was registered, when a cohort's counts and rate variants are rewritten because a member's exclusion decision changed, when a data request's screening counters are rewritten, or when a survey component's `edit_status` is re-derived because one of its edit checks moved — in each case the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1, even though no client sent a payload naming that row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, the Census Date and the Collection Deadline

All temporal rules in this system read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, and a dedicated test-driving capability sets it: a caller in any of the five roles supplies a `to_iso8601` instant and the server answers with the clock's new reading in a `current_utc` field. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until the first set call it must report that instant and must never silently fall back to real wall-clock time. Setting the clock mutates no business row.

Two cycle-level instants govern the calendar of a collection. `census_date` is an ISO 8601 calendar date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) naming the official snapshot day; the census snapshot may not be sealed until the logical clock's date has reached it, and equality passes — a lock attempted on the census date itself is legal, while a lock attempted on any strictly earlier date is refused with `ERR_CENSUS_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). `collection_deadline` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant (three fractional digits are accepted) after which the institution may no longer bind itself to a reported figure. It must be strictly after midnight UTC on the census date. Three acts are deadline-bound: certifying a survey component, federally submitting a certified component, and transmitting an external submission to its recipient. In each, the clock must be at or before `collection_deadline` — equality passes, and a clock strictly after the deadline yields `ERR_COLLECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422).

Every timestamp the server writes itself — `census_locked_at`, `frozen_at`, `snapshot_taken_at`, `submitted_at`, `locked_at`, `transmitted_at`, `screened_at` — is taken from the logical clock at the moment of the transition, not from wall-clock time.

### 2.6. Snapshot Posture: How the Cycle Gates Everything Beneath It

A reporting cycle's `snapshot_posture` is the master gate of the system. It takes one of five values — `OPEN`, `CENSUS_LOCKED`, `FROZEN`, `REOPENED`, `ARCHIVED` — beginning at `OPEN` on creation, and `ARCHIVED` is terminal. Rather than repeating the gate on every workflow, the specification states it once here in four bands, and each band names the error a violation carries.

**Pre-freeze work is refused once the governing cycle is `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED`**, with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Twelve operations sit in this band: registering a census extract, creating a survey component, adding a reported metric, correcting a reported metric, preparing a component, certifying a component, defining a cohort, adding a cohort member, patching a member's outcome, proposing an element mapping, validating an element mapping, and revising a rejected element mapping.

**A census extract may not be registered while the cycle is `CENSUS_LOCKED`.** The snapshot is sealed at that point and stays sealed until the cycle is reopened, so extract registration requires a posture of `OPEN` or `REOPENED`; an attempt while `CENSUS_LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_CENSUS_LOCKED` (HTTP 409).

**Post-freeze work requires a posture of `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED`**, and anything else yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Five operations sit in this band: publishing a dashboard figure, transmitting an external submission, intaking a data request, screening a data request, and releasing a screened data request. The logic is that internal dashboards and external recipients may only ever see numbers drawn from a frozen file.

**Every remaining posture mismatch reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`** (HTTP 409). That covers component work attempted while the cycle is still `OPEN`; a cycle transition fired from the wrong posture (a census lock attempted from `CENSUS_LOCKED` or `FROZEN`, a freeze attempted from `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, a reopen attempted from anything other than `FROZEN`); and any mutation of any child of an `ARCHIVED` cycle. Twelve operations declare this band explicitly: census-lock, freeze, reopen, component creation, metric creation, prepare, certify, submit, revert-to-draft, external-submission creation, cohort lock, exclusion claim and exclusion review.

### 2.7. Hierarchical Consistency and Cascade Discipline

Corrections in this domain are never local. Because a published dashboard figure is derived from a certified component, which is derived from a census extract, undoing any link in that chain must visibly invalidate everything downstream. The system therefore models three deterministic multi-level cascades, and each level is triggered *by the level above it*, not by the original request:

1. **Reopening a frozen cycle** moves the cycle to `REOPENED` (level 1); every `CERTIFIED` survey component of that cycle reverts to `DRAFT` and has its edit checks voided (level 2); every non-`WITHDRAWN` dashboard publication of each reverted component is visited and set to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` (level 3); and every `SUBMITTED` restatement request beneath each visited publication becomes `VOIDED` (level 4).
2. **Reverting a component to draft** — whether from `PREPARED` or from `CERTIFIED` — voids that component's edit checks (level 1), then when the component was `CERTIFIED` it flips its non-`WITHDRAWN` publications to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` (level 2) and voids their `SUBMITTED` restatement requests (level 3).
3. **Approving or reversing an allowable exclusion on a locked cohort** recomputes the cohort's counts and both rate variants (level 2 of the member's own decision), then flips matching `PUBLISHED` dashboard figures to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` (level 3), then supersedes their pending restatement requests and files a fresh system-authored one carrying the recomputed rate (level 4).

Three properties of cascades hold universally. **Ordering is deterministic**: wherever a cascade visits a set of sibling rows, it visits them in `created_at` ascending order, except for edit checks voided by a component reversion, which are visited in `sequence_no` ascending order. **Visiting is versioning**: every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — and this holds even when the visit does not change the row's state value, so a publication already sitting at `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` that is re-visited by a cascade still advances its `version`. **Terminal rows are immune**: a `WITHDRAWN` publication is never visited by any cascade, and a `SUBMITTED` survey component is never reverted by a cycle reopen, because federal transmission is irreversible.

The reverse direction — bubbling up — also exists. A parent's posture may change because of the aggregate state of its children: when the last survey component of a `FROZEN` cycle reaches `SUBMITTED`, the cycle itself moves `FROZEN → ARCHIVED` with its `version` incremented, without any client requesting a change on the cycle.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Governed Parents

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy governance state that the calling client never observed, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against their parent resource's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional input** — omitting it is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not a licence to skip the check.

Registering a census extract under a reporting cycle requires `expected_cycle_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of that reporting cycle as the client last read it. Registration legitimately supersedes an incumbent extract that holds the identical domain and coverage window, so a client whose view of the cycle predates an already-registered replacement would discard governance state it never saw. If `expected_cycle_version` does not equal the reporting cycle's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

Filing a restatement request against a dashboard publication requires `expected_publication_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of that publication as the client last read it. Filing supersedes every other `SUBMITTED` request on the same publication and captures `baseline_value` from the publication's current `published_value`, so a stale view would measure materiality against a baseline the client never observed. If `expected_publication_version` does not equal the publication's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. In particular, approving or rejecting a restatement, certifying a component, locking a cohort and screening a data request all proceed without any client-supplied version.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the precision depends on what is being measured. Three precisions are in use, and mixing them is a defect:

- **One decimal place** applies to every percentage that describes reported data or its coverage: a metric's `variance_pct`, a component's `mapping_coverage_pct`, a cohort's `federal_rate_pct` and `ranking_rate_pct`, the divergence figure `rate_divergence_pp`, and the accumulated `net_applied_delta` on a dashboard publication.
- **Two decimal places** apply to every percentage that drives a governance verdict: a restatement's `delta_pct`, and an external submission's `raw_delta_pct` and `effective_delta_pct`. The allowance and tolerance figures those comparisons use — `allowance_pct` and `tolerance_pct` — are exact two-decimal constants.
- **Whole integers, no rounding** apply to counts (`record_count`, `active_record_count`, `frozen_file_count`, `initial_count`, `exclusion_count`, `adjusted_count`, `completers_count`, `retained_count`, `fatal_open_count`, `cell_count`, `suppressed_cell_count`, `released_cell_count`, `raw_count`, `released_count`, `restatement_count`, `sequence_no`, `explanation_round`, `revision_round`, `reopen_count`). Monetary amounts are carried in whole cents: `core_expense_per_fte_cents` is an integer number of cents, rounded to whole cents per functional category before summation as described in §3.3.

Percentages and monetary values are returned as JSON numbers, never as strings. Where a derived figure is undefined — a variance against an absent or zero prior year, a rate over a zero denominator, a restatement delta against a zero baseline — the field is `null` rather than 0, and the distinction between `null` and 0 is load-bearing throughout.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The office's control framework rests on one rule: no operator may approve their own work. The system enforces it by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the opaque identifier recorded by the preceding step, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Four operations carry the check. Certifying a survey component compares the actor against `prepared_by_user_id`. Adjudicating an edit check compares the actor against `explained_by_user_id`. Approving a restatement request and rejecting a restatement request both compare the actor against `requested_by_user_id`.

One case is exempt by construction. When a restatement request is created by a cascade rather than by a person, its `requested_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM` and its `origin` is `CASCADE`. Because no operator's `X-User-ID` is the literal `SYSTEM`, a cascade-authored request can never collide with its approver, and any `IRDirector` may decide it.

### 2.11. Interval Geometry: Two Windows, Two Different Rules

The system holds two kinds of occupancy interval, and they use deliberately different endpoint semantics. Confusing them produces exactly the wrong answer on adjacency, so the distinction is stated here once.

A census extract's coverage window, spanning `coverage_start_date` to `coverage_end_date`, is a **closed** interval of whole calendar dates: both endpoints are occupied. An extract that ends on 2026-10-15 covers 2026-10-15, so a second extract for the same domain starting on 2026-10-15 double-counts that day and is an overlap. Adjacency therefore collides: `[2026-09-01, 2026-10-15]` and `[2026-10-15, 2026-11-30]` conflict, while a one-day gap — `[…, 2026-10-14]` followed by `[2026-10-15, …]` — is accepted.

A dashboard publication's effective window, spanning `effective_from` to `effective_to`, is a **half-open** instant interval: the start is occupied and the end is not. A dashboard figure stops applying at the instant its successor begins, so the two may share that instant. Adjacency is therefore allowed: `[2026-11-01T00:00:00Z, 2026-12-01T00:00:00Z)` and `[2026-12-01T00:00:00Z, …)` do not overlap. An omitted `effective_to` means the window is open-ended and extends to positive infinity — the figure is currently effective.

### 2.12. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Requests that address a nested resource are resolved left to right. Each identifier in the path is looked up in turn, and the first identifier that names no existing row decides the 404 — the server does not attempt to resolve identifiers further right. The thirteen path identifiers map to their own error codes: `{cycle_id}` to `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `{frozen_file_id}` to `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `{component_id}` to `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `{metric_id}` to `ERR_METRIC_NOT_FOUND`, `{edit_check_id}` to `ERR_EDIT_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `{cohort_id}` to `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FOUND`, `{member_id}` to `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `{mapping_id}` to `ERR_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND`, `{submission_id}` to `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `{publication_id}` to `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `{restatement_id}` to `ERR_RESTATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `{request_id}` to `ERR_DATA_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, and `{cell_id}` to `ERR_CELL_NOT_FOUND`. All thirteen are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved the same way and produce the same codes: an unresolvable `cycle_id` gives `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `frozen_file_id` gives `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `component_id` gives `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `definition_id` gives `ERR_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`. Crucially, **a row that exists but hangs under a different parent is treated as not found**: a `frozen_file_id` belonging to another cycle, or a `component_id` whose own `cycle_id` differs from the `cycle_id` supplied in the same body, both yield 404 rather than a conflict. This body-level resolution applies to component creation, element-mapping creation, external-submission creation, publication creation, and census-extract registration.

Because several rules can be violated by the same request, the order of evaluation is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in strict ascending priority and **the first violated rule decides the response; no later rule is evaluated**:

1. Authentication headers present and the role recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. The acting role appears on the called endpoint's permitted list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. Request-body schema: every field the endpoint marks required is present, JSON types match, enum fields carry a declared member, calendar dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`, instants are ISO 8601 UTC, counts and cent amounts are non-negative integers, and declared free-text length bounds hold — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Two length checks are deliberately *not* handled here and surface much later: `explanation_text` at priority 30 and `rejection_reason` at priority 31.
4. Path identifiers resolve, left to right — the thirteen 404 codes above.
5. Body foreign keys resolve, including the wrong-parent rule — the four 404 codes above.
6. Pre-freeze work against a `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` cycle — `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` (409).
7. Extract registration against a `CENSUS_LOCKED` cycle — `ERR_CENSUS_LOCKED` (409).
8. Post-freeze work against a cycle that is neither `FROZEN` nor `ARCHIVED` — `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN` (409).
9. Any remaining cycle-posture mismatch — `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` (409).
10. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION` (412).
11. The target row's own state admits the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EDIT_CHECK_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXCLUSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MAPPING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PUBLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RESTATEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DATA_REQUEST_STATE`, or `ERR_COHORT_LOCKED` for any roster write against a locked cohort (all 409). This band covers every action endpoint that drives a state transition, plus adding a cohort member, patching a member, adding a reported metric, correcting a reported metric, and adding a crosstab cell.
12. Cross-entity state preconditions — `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_SUPERSEDED`, `ERR_EDIT_CHECKS_UNRESOLVED`, `ERR_COMPONENTS_NOT_READY`, `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT`, `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EXCLUSION_CLAIM`, `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (all 409), on prepare, certify, freeze, census-lock, cohort lock and reconcile respectively.
13. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
14. Residual small-cell disclosure risk on release — `ERR_SMALL_CELL_DISCLOSURE` (422).
15. A waiver attempted on a `FATAL` edit check — `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).
16. A mapping's `value_map` targets a code outside the federal `value_domain` — `ERR_VALUE_DOMAIN_VIOLATION` (422).
17. A `metric_key` outside its component's survey domain — `ERR_METRIC_DOMAIN_MISMATCH` (422).
18. An `exclusion_code` outside the closed federal list — `ERR_INVALID_EXCLUSION_CODE` (422).
19. Reconciliation finds no federal counterpart metric — `ERR_FEDERAL_VALUE_UNAVAILABLE` (422).
20. The federal counterpart's `reported_value` is zero — `ERR_ZERO_BASE_RECONCILIATION` (422).
21. The clock has not reached the census date — `ERR_CENSUS_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (422).
22. The clock is past the collection deadline — `ERR_COLLECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (422).
23. An invalid coverage window — `ERR_INVALID_COVERAGE_WINDOW` (422).
24. An invalid effective window — `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` (422).
25. Coverage windows overlap — `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
26. Effective windows overlap — `ERR_PUBLICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
27. Scoped uniqueness — the nine `ERR_DUPLICATE_…` codes (409).
28. A material restatement approved without a public notice reference — `ERR_PUBLIC_NOTICE_REQUIRED` (422).
29. Denominator and input sufficiency — `ERR_EMPTY_COHORT`, `ERR_EMPTY_COHORT_DENOMINATOR`, `ERR_NO_METRICS`, `ERR_NO_CELLS_TO_SCREEN` (422).
30. `explanation_text` too short — `ERR_EXPLANATION_TOO_SHORT` (422).
31. A negative decision without its reason — `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED` (422).

One consequence deserves emphasis because it is easy to get backwards: because posture gating (priorities 6 through 9) precedes the target row's own state check (priority 11), a request that is wrong in both ways reports the *cycle* problem. Likewise, because segregation of duties (priority 13) precedes the waiver rule (priority 15), a director who explained a check and then tries to waive it as `FATAL` sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, not `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED`. And because the schema check at priority 3 precedes both, an explanation shorter than 20 characters reaches priority 30 only when the rest of the payload is well formed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Collection Cycle and Sealing the Census Snapshot

A `RegistrarOfficer` opens the year's work by creating a **reporting cycle**. The payload carries a `cycle_code` — the human-readable identifier such as `IPEDS-2027`, between 1 and 32 characters, and unique across all cycles under case-sensitive comparison, with a collision reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_CODE` — together with the `collection_year` the cycle reports on (an integer at least 2000 and at most 2100), the `census_date`, and the `collection_deadline`, which must fall strictly after midnight UTC on the census date. A newly opened cycle begins at `snapshot_posture` `OPEN`, with `reopen_count` at 0, `active_record_count` at 0, `frozen_file_count` at 0, `census_locked_at`, `frozen_at` and `reopen_reason` all null, and `version` 1.

Census extracts are then registered against the cycle, one per source domain per coverage window. Each **frozen file** declares its `domain` — one of `ENROLLMENT`, `COMPLETIONS`, `FINANCE` or `HR` — a `record_count` of at least 0, the closed coverage window bounded by `coverage_start_date` and `coverage_end_date`, an extract fingerprint `checksum` of 8 to 64 characters, and the concurrency token `expected_cycle_version` described in §2.8. The server records the owning `cycle_id`, sets `snapshot_taken_at` from the logical clock, starts the row at `status` `ACTIVE` with `version` 1, and leaves `supersedes_frozen_file_id` null unless the registration replaced an incumbent. The window itself is validated before anything else about it: `coverage_start_date` must be on or before `coverage_end_date`, and both dates must fall inside the cycle's reporting year — the closed span running from 1 July of the year before `collection_year` through 30 June of `collection_year` itself. A violation of either condition is `ERR_INVALID_COVERAGE_WINDOW`.

Registration then resolves the new window against the extracts already on file. The candidate peers are the extracts of the *same* cycle and the *same* `domain` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED`; superseded extracts are invisible to this scan, so a window once vacated by a supersession may be reused freely. If exactly one peer holds precisely the same `coverage_start_date` **and** precisely the same `coverage_end_date`, the registration is understood as a corrected extract rather than a conflict: that incumbent moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented, the new row's `supersedes_frozen_file_id` is set by the server to the incumbent's `id` (never declared by the client), and the incumbent drops out of the remaining overlap comparison. Consider a scenario where an enrollment extract covering 1 September through 15 October is re-pulled after a coding error is found: re-registering the identical domain and identical window is the sanctioned correction path, and it fires the `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` transition. The identical-window set can never hold more than one row, because the overlap scan below already forbids a second occupant of that window. Every remaining peer is then compared for intersection: if the new window's start falls on or before a peer's end **and** that peer's start falls on or before the new window's end, the registration is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Per §2.11 a shared endpoint date is an intersection, so a peer ending on the new extract's start date is a conflict. When an extract has been superseded, a survey component still pointing at it can no longer be prepared, and the attempt reports `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_SUPERSEDED`.

Two derived ledgers on the cycle are rewritten every time an extract is registered and every time any extract's `status` changes. `active_record_count` is the sum of `record_count` over the cycle's extracts whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED`, with an empty set yielding 0 — so a superseded extract's records leave the total at the very moment its replacement is created, and the replacement's records enter it. `frozen_file_count` is the number of the cycle's extracts whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`. Both are integers and neither is rounded.

Sealing the snapshot is the `RegistrarOfficer`'s decision. The census lock drives `OPEN → CENSUS_LOCKED`, and — after a correction round — also `REOPENED → CENSUS_LOCKED`; both variants of the transition impose the same two substantive preconditions. The logical clock's date must have reached `census_date`, with equality passing and any earlier date giving `ERR_CENSUS_DATE_NOT_REACHED`; and at least one extract of the cycle must currently be `ACTIVE`, failing which the lock reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT`. In the reopened case that second precondition carries real weight: it means a *fresh* extract must have been registered since the reopen, because the reopen leaves every existing extract sitting at `LOCKED`. The lock then sets `snapshot_posture` to `CENSUS_LOCKED`, writes `census_locked_at` from the logical clock, increments the cycle's `version`, walks the cycle's `ACTIVE` extracts in `created_at` ascending order flipping each one `ACTIVE → LOCKED` with its own `version` incremented, and recomputes `active_record_count` and `frozen_file_count` — the record total is unchanged in value, because locked extracts remain inside the sum.

Superseding a `LOCKED` extract is the one path that requires a reopened cycle. While the cycle is `REOPENED`, registering an identical `domain` and identical coverage window against a `LOCKED` incumbent drives `LOCKED → SUPERSEDED` on that incumbent, with the same version bump, the same self-referential link written on the newcomer, and the same recomputation of the cycle's record ledger.

### 3.2. The Federal Definition Library and Local-to-Federal Element Mapping

Before any component can claim full coverage, a `DataSteward` must populate the **federal definition library**. Each definition is immutable configuration: it names a `definition_code` of 3 to 64 characters (for example `EF_TOTAL_HEADCOUNT`), the `survey_component` it belongs to (`ENROLLMENT`, `COMPLETIONS`, `FINANCE` or `HR`), the closed `value_domain` of federal codes it accepts as an array of 1 to 40 strings each 1 to 32 characters long, a `required` flag that defaults to true, a `unit_of_measure` of 1 to 32 characters such as `HEADCOUNT`, `USD_CENTS` or `MONTHS`, and an `effective_cycle_code` of 1 to 32 characters. That last field is matched against a reporting cycle's `cycle_code` by plain string equality rather than by foreign key, which is what lets one definition library serve several institution-years. The pair of `definition_code` and `effective_cycle_code` must be unique; a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEFINITION`. As noted in §2.4, a federal definition exposes only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no way to be changed once written.

An `IRAnalyst` bridges the local systems to that library by proposing **element mappings**. A proposal carries the `cycle_id` it is authored for, the `definition_id` it targets, the `local_system_code` the source field lives in (`SIS`, `HR`, `FIN` or `ADV`), the `local_field_path` being mapped as 3 to 120 characters (for example `SIS.STUDENT.ETHNICITY`), a `transform_expression` of 1 to 500 characters describing the transformation applied before reporting (for example `UPPER(TRIM(x))`), and a `value_map` of 1 to 40 entries translating local codes to federal codes. New proposals start at `status` `PROPOSED` with `revision_round` 0, `validated_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` null, and `version` 1. The triple of `cycle_id`, `definition_id` and `local_system_code` must be unique among mappings whose `status` is anything other than `REJECTED`, so a rejected proposal does not block a fresh attempt on the same field; a collision against a live row reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_MAPPING`.

The `DataSteward` renders the verdict, and the two branches of that single decision are deliberately asymmetric. An approval drives `PROPOSED → VALIDATED`: it requires the mapping to be `PROPOSED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MAPPING_STATE`), requires the governing cycle's posture to be neither `FROZEN` nor `ARCHIVED` (otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`), and requires **every value of the `value_map` to appear in the target definition's `value_domain`**, failing which the approval is refused with `ERR_VALUE_DOMAIN_VIOLATION`. On success the steward's opaque identifier is written to `validated_by_user_id`, `rejection_reason` is cleared to null, and `version` is incremented; `VALIDATED` is terminal for the mapping. A rejection drives `PROPOSED → REJECTED`: it likewise requires the mapping to be `PROPOSED`, and it additionally demands a `rejection_reason` of 10 to 500 characters, absent which the verdict is refused with `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`. On success the reason is stored, `validated_by_user_id` records the steward, and `version` is incremented.

A rejected mapping is not dead. The authoring analyst may revise it, driving `REJECTED → PROPOSED`: the request supplies a replacement `transform_expression` and a replacement `value_map`, both of which overwrite the stored values; `rejection_reason` is cleared to null; `revision_round` increases by 1, counting the negotiation rounds; and `version` is incremented. A revision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MAPPING_STATE` unless the mapping is `REJECTED`, with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` when the cycle's posture is `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED`, and with `ERR_VALUE_DOMAIN_VIOLATION` when any value of the *new* `value_map` falls outside the target definition's `value_domain`.

Coverage is the payoff of all this bookkeeping, and it is measured only at preparation time. A component's `mapping_coverage_pct` is computed by first assembling the set of *required* definitions for that component: the definitions whose `survey_component` equals the component's `component_code`, whose `required` flag is true, and whose `effective_cycle_code` equals the parent cycle's `cycle_code`. When that set is empty the convention is that nothing required is fully covered, and `mapping_coverage_pct` is 100.0. Otherwise a definition counts as covered when at least one element mapping exists whose `definition_id` names it, whose `cycle_id` names the component's cycle, and whose `status` is `VALIDATED` — proposals and rejections never count, and `local_system_code` is ignored entirely, so any single validated mapping covers its definition no matter which local system it came from. The percentage is the covered count divided by the required count, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to one decimal place. Because the downstream test in §3.3 asks whether coverage is strictly below 100.0, a coverage of 99.9 raises the unmapped-elements flag while 100.0 does not. Note also the timing: validating a mapping does not push a new coverage figure onto any component — it changes what the *next* preparation of any component of that cycle will compute.

### 3.3. Assembling a Survey Component: Reported Metrics, Per-FTE Expense and Preparation

An `IRAnalyst` opens a **survey component** — the preparation workspace for one survey of one cycle — by naming the `cycle_id`, the `frozen_file_id` of the census extract the component will be prepared from, and the `component_code` identifying the survey (`ENROLLMENT`, `COMPLETIONS`, `FINANCE` or `HR`). The referenced extract must belong to that same cycle and its `domain` must equal the requested `component_code`; an extract from another cycle or from another domain is treated as unresolvable and reports `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_NOT_FOUND`. Only one component per survey may exist per cycle, so a second attempt at the same pair of `cycle_id` and `component_code` reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT`. Component creation additionally requires the cycle to be `CENSUS_LOCKED` or `REOPENED`; attempting it while the cycle is still `OPEN` reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, and attempting it after the freeze reports `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`. A new component starts with `prep_status` `DRAFT`, `edit_status` `NOT_RUN`, `fatal_open_count` 0, `mapping_coverage_pct` and `core_expense_per_fte_cents` null, `prepared_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `certification_statement` and `submitted_at` all null, and `version` 1.

Reported figures are then attached to the component one at a time, and **only while the component is `DRAFT`** — a metric write against a component in any other `prep_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`. Each **component metric** carries a `metric_key`, the `reported_value` submitted to the federal collection (at least 0), an optional `prior_year_value` (at least 0 when supplied, and null when no prior-year figure exists), and the `source_field_path` of 3 to 120 characters naming the local-system field the number was extracted from, such as `SIS.STUDENT.HEADCOUNT_FALL`. The server records the owning `component_id` and starts the row with `restated` false, `restatement_note` and `variance_pct` null, and `version` 1. The pair of `component_id` and `metric_key` must be unique, with a collision reporting `ERR_DUPLICATE_METRIC_KEY`.

A `metric_key` is only meaningful inside its own survey domain, and the permitted sets are closed. An `ENROLLMENT` component accepts `TOTAL_HEADCOUNT` and `FTE_ENROLLMENT`. A `COMPLETIONS` component accepts `TOTAL_COMPLETIONS` and `CIP_PROGRAM_COUNT`. A `FINANCE` component accepts `INSTRUCTION_EXPENSES_CENTS`, `ACADEMIC_SUPPORT_EXPENSES_CENTS`, `STUDENT_SERVICES_EXPENSES_CENTS` and `FTE_ENROLLMENT`. An `HR` component accepts `INSTRUCTIONAL_STAFF_HEADCOUNT` and `INSTRUCTIONAL_CONTRACT_MONTHS`. `FTE_ENROLLMENT` appears in two sets on purpose: the per-FTE expense figure below needs it on the `FINANCE` component, not merely on the enrollment one. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_METRIC_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`, and the same rule governs the `metric_key` an external submission names against its source component (§3.8).

A misreported figure is corrected in place while the component is still `DRAFT`. The correction supplies a replacement `reported_value` and an optional `restatement_note` explaining the change; the stored value is overwritten, `restated` is set to true, `restatement_note` is overwritten with the supplied note — and overwritten with null when the correction omits `restatement_note` or sends it as null, so a later correction that carries no note clears whatever note an earlier one left standing — that metric's `variance_pct` is recomputed, and `version` is incremented. Corrections outside `DRAFT` are refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`, and corrections after the cycle freeze are refused with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.

**Prior-year variance.** For a single metric row in isolation — no other row participates — the variance is derived from that row alone. When `prior_year_value` is null, `variance_pct` is null. When `prior_year_value` is exactly 0, `variance_pct` is also null, because a percentage change from a zero base is undefined; this zero-base case is not ignored, it is escalated separately below. Otherwise `variance_pct` is the absolute difference between `reported_value` and `prior_year_value`, divided by `prior_year_value`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to one decimal place. Two thresholds read that figure, and both are inclusive: a variance of at least 10.0 percent is a warning-level concern, and a variance of at least 25.0 percent is a fatal one. Exactly 10.0 is a warning, exactly 25.0 is fatal, and 9.9 raises nothing at all.

**Per-FTE core expense.** Finance components additionally publish `core_expense_per_fte_cents`, and its evaluation order is pinned. The calculation reads exactly four of the component's metrics: the reported values of `INSTRUCTION_EXPENSES_CENTS`, `ACADEMIC_SUPPORT_EXPENSES_CENTS`, `STUDENT_SERVICES_EXPENSES_CENTS` and `FTE_ENROLLMENT`. If any of those four metrics is absent from the component, or if the FTE enrollment figure is exactly 0, the result is null and no per-FTE figure is published. Otherwise **each functional category is divided by the FTE enrollment and rounded half up to whole cents first, and the three whole-cent results are then added together**. An exact half-cent remainder rounds up: 500007 cents of instruction expense over 6 FTE gives 83334.5, which becomes 83335. The alternative order — adding the three categories and dividing the total once — is explicitly not used, and it differs by one or two cents whenever the three per-category remainders together pass one half. Round-then-sum is normative; sum-then-round is a wrong answer. The figure is null for every component whose `component_code` is not `FINANCE`, and null on any component before its first preparation.

**Preparation.** Running preparation is the analyst's act of committing the workspace to review, and it drives `DRAFT → PREPARED`. Four preconditions guard it: the component must be `DRAFT`; the parent cycle's posture must be `CENSUS_LOCKED` or `REOPENED`, because the census-date snapshot rule permits preparation only from a census-locked extract, and anything else reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`; the referenced extract's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED`, and a `SUPERSEDED` extract reports `ERR_FROZEN_FILE_SUPERSEDED`; and at least one component metric must exist, failing which the run reports `ERR_NO_METRICS`. A successful run writes the acting operator's identifier into `prepared_by_user_id`, sets `prep_status` to `PREPARED`, increments `version`, recomputes `mapping_coverage_pct`, recomputes `core_expense_per_fte_cents` for finance components (writing null for all others), recomputes every metric's `variance_pct`, raises the edit checks described next, re-derives `edit_status`, and recomputes `fatal_open_count`.

**Edit-check generation** is fully deterministic. The component's metrics are walked in `metric_key` ascending order — ties are impossible because the key is unique per component — and a cascade of five branches is evaluated top-down for each metric, stopping at the first branch that matches, so **at most one check is raised per metric**:

1. When the metric has a `prior_year_value` that is not null, that prior value is exactly 0, and `reported_value` is strictly greater than 0, a zero-base check is raised with `check_code` formed as `PY_ZERO_BASE_` followed by the metric's key (for example `PY_ZERO_BASE_FTE_ENROLLMENT`), `severity` `FATAL`, and its `variance_pct` left null.
2. Otherwise, when the metric's `variance_pct` is not null and is at least 25.0, a variance check is raised with `check_code` formed as `PY_VAR_` followed by the metric's key (for example `PY_VAR_TOTAL_HEADCOUNT`), `severity` `FATAL`, and the metric's `variance_pct` carried onto the check.
3. Otherwise, when the metric's `variance_pct` is not null and is at least 10.0, the same `PY_VAR_` check code is raised but with `severity` `WARNING`, again carrying the metric's `variance_pct`.
4. Otherwise, when the metric has no `prior_year_value` at all and its `reported_value` is exactly 0, a check is raised with `check_code` formed as `ZERO_NO_BASE_` followed by the metric's key, `severity` `WARNING`, and `variance_pct` null.
5. Otherwise nothing is raised for that metric.

After the per-metric walk, and always last, one component-level check is appended when and only when `mapping_coverage_pct` is strictly below 100.0: `check_code` `UNMAPPED_ELEMENTS`, `severity` `FATAL`, and `metric_key` the literal string `ALL` rather than a real key, with `observed_value`, `prior_year_value` and `variance_pct` all null. Every check raised in a run receives a `sequence_no` numbered from 1 upward in the order of creation, pinning both the creation order and the listing order; starts at `status` `OPEN`; captures `observed_value` from the raising metric's `reported_value` and `prior_year_value` from that metric's prior-year figure; and records the raising metric's key in its own `metric_key`. A check also carries `explanation_text`, `prior_year_comparison_note`, `explained_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudication_note`, all null at creation, and an `explanation_round` counter starting at 0.

### 3.4. Edit-Check Resolution: Explanation, Adjudication and the Rejection Loop

An **edit check** is a machine-raised flag that must be talked down before the component can be certified, and its lifecycle deliberately requires two different people. The analyst supplies the narrative; the director decides whether the narrative is good enough.

Explaining a check drives `OPEN → EXPLAINED`, and — after a rejection — also `REJECTED → EXPLAINED`. Both variants require the parent component to be sitting at `prep_status` `PREPARED`, and a check on a component in any other preparation state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`; a check whose own `status` is neither `OPEN` nor `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EDIT_CHECK_STATE`. The payload carries `explanation_text`, the analyst's account of the flagged value, which must be at least 20 characters after trimming and at most 2000 — anything shorter is refused with `ERR_EXPLANATION_TOO_SHORT` — and `prior_year_comparison_note`, the narrative comparing the value to the prior year, which must be between 10 and 2000 characters, with a violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success both texts are stored (replacing whatever a previous round left there), `explained_by_user_id` records the acting analyst, `explanation_round` increases by 1 — counting every round including re-explanations after a rejection — and the check's `version` is incremented. The parent component's `edit_status` is then re-derived and the component's own `version` is incremented.

Adjudicating a check is the `IRDirector`'s call and branches three ways on a single `decision` field, optionally accompanied by an `adjudication_note`. All three branches require the check to be `EXPLAINED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EDIT_CHECK_STATE`) and all three require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `explained_by_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). A decision of `ACCEPT` drives `EXPLAINED → ACCEPTED`, storing the note, writing `adjudicated_by_user_id`, incrementing the check's `version`, then re-deriving the parent's `edit_status`, recomputing the parent's `fatal_open_count` and incrementing the parent's `version`. A decision of `WAIVE` drives `EXPLAINED → WAIVED` and is the divergent branch: it clears the check *without* accepting the explanation, and it is legal only when the check's `severity` is `WARNING` — a waiver aimed at a `FATAL` check is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED`. It writes `adjudicated_by_user_id`, increments the check's `version`, and re-derives the parent's `edit_status` with the parent's `version` incremented. A decision of `REJECT` drives `EXPLAINED → REJECTED` and additionally demands an `adjudication_note` of 10 to 500 characters, absent which the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it stores the note, writes `adjudicated_by_user_id`, increments the check's `version`, and pushes the parent's `edit_status` back to `OPEN_EDITS` with the parent's `fatal_open_count` recomputed and the parent's `version` incremented. `ACCEPTED` and `WAIVED` are terminal states for a check; `REJECTED` is not, and it is the doorway back into the explanation loop.

The component's second state dimension, `edit_status`, is never set by a client. It is a **pure function of the component's edit checks**, re-derived after every check creation, explanation, adjudication and voiding, and also on preparation and on reversion to draft. The derivation evaluates four branches in this normative order: if the component has no checks at all, or every check it has is `VOIDED`, the status is `NOT_RUN`; otherwise if any check is `OPEN` or `REJECTED`, the status is `OPEN_EDITS`; otherwise if any check is `EXPLAINED`, the status is `EXPLAINED`; otherwise the status is `CLEARED`. Voided checks never block the `CLEARED` verdict — they simply drop out of consideration once at least one live check remains. One special case is pinned against the first branch: a preparation run that raised **zero** checks yields `CLEARED`, not `NOT_RUN`, because the checks genuinely ran and genuinely found nothing. Alongside the derivation, `fatal_open_count` is recomputed as the number of the component's checks whose `severity` is `FATAL` and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `EXPLAINED` or `REJECTED`.

Read as a machine, the second dimension moves through eight transitions. A preparation run that raised at least one check drives `NOT_RUN → OPEN_EDITS`; a run that raised none, because every metric passed and coverage stood at exactly 100.0, drives `NOT_RUN → CLEARED`. Once the last check sitting at `OPEN` or `REJECTED` has been explained and at least one check is `EXPLAINED`, the component moves `OPEN_EDITS → EXPLAINED`. Once every check of the component is `ACCEPTED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`, it moves `EXPLAINED → CLEARED`. A single rejection is enough to drive `EXPLAINED → OPEN_EDITS`, which is the rejection loop: the component falls back out of `EXPLAINED` and must be re-explained before it can be certified, and the dimension cycles `EXPLAINED → OPEN_EDITS → EXPLAINED` once per negotiation round. Finally, when a reversion to draft has voided every check, the component returns to `NOT_RUN` from whichever of `OPEN_EDITS`, `CLEARED` or `EXPLAINED` it was in — `OPEN_EDITS → NOT_RUN`, `CLEARED → NOT_RUN` and `EXPLAINED → NOT_RUN` are three separate transitions with identical semantics.

Voiding is the system's own act, never a client's. When a component is reverted to draft, whether directly by a director or indirectly by a cycle reopen, every one of its checks whose `status` is `OPEN`, `EXPLAINED`, `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` moves to `VOIDED` with its `version` incremented — `OPEN → VOIDED`, `EXPLAINED → VOIDED`, `REJECTED → VOIDED`, `ACCEPTED → VOIDED` and `WAIVED → VOIDED` are five source states with one destination, and the checks are walked in `sequence_no` ascending order. `VOIDED` is terminal.

### 3.5. Certification, Federal Submission, Freezing, Reopening and the Reversion Loop

**Certification** is the `IRDirector`'s attestation that a prepared component is fit to send, and it drives `PREPARED → CERTIFIED`. Six preconditions apply, in the order established by the global precedence list. The component must be `PREPARED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`. The parent cycle's posture must be `CENSUS_LOCKED` or `REOPENED`; a cycle already `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` reports `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`, and a cycle still `OPEN` reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. The component's `edit_status` must be exactly `CLEARED`, so every raised check must have reached `ACCEPTED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`; anything else reports `ERR_EDIT_CHECKS_UNRESOLVED`, and note that this gate is what makes the two state dimensions meet — `prep_status` and `edit_status` evolve independently everywhere except here. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The logical clock must be at or before the cycle's `collection_deadline`, with equality passing, otherwise `ERR_COLLECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Finally a `certification_statement` of 20 to 1000 characters must be supplied. On success the component moves to `CERTIFIED`, `certified_by_user_id` records the director, the statement is stored verbatim, and `version` is incremented.

**Federal submission** drives `CERTIFIED → SUBMITTED` and is the irreversible act. It requires the component to be `CERTIFIED` (else `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`), the parent cycle's posture to be exactly `FROZEN` (else `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` — a component cannot be sent to the federal collection before its cycle is frozen, and an `ARCHIVED` cycle is likewise refused), and the logical clock to be at or before `collection_deadline` (else `ERR_COLLECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`). On success `prep_status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` is incremented. `SUBMITTED` is terminal for a component: no reversion, no reopen cascade and no decertification can move it again. The submission also bubbles upward — when the last component of the cycle reaches `SUBMITTED`, so that **every** component of that cycle is `SUBMITTED`, the system itself drives the cycle `FROZEN → ARCHIVED` and increments the cycle's `version`. Nobody requests archival; it is derived from the aggregate state of the children at submit time.

**Reversion to draft** is the director's undo, exposed as a single operation that requires a `decertify_reason` of 10 to 500 characters and behaves differently depending on where the component stands. From `PREPARED`, reversion is shallow: `prep_status` returns to `DRAFT`, `prepared_by_user_id` is cleared to null, `version` is incremented, every edit check of the component sitting at `OPEN`, `EXPLAINED`, `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` is voided in `sequence_no` ascending order, `edit_status` is forced to `NOT_RUN` and `fatal_open_count` to 0. This closes the preparation loop `DRAFT → PREPARED → DRAFT`, so a corrected metric can be re-prepared and freshly re-flagged; each lap raises a brand-new set of checks whose `sequence_no` again starts at 1, while the previous lap's checks remain visible as `VOIDED` history.

From `CERTIFIED`, the same operation is a **decertification** driving `CERTIFIED → DRAFT`, and it cascades three levels deep. The first level does everything the shallow reversion does and additionally clears `certified_by_user_id` and `certification_statement` to null. The second level, caused by the first, visits every dashboard publication of that component whose `publication_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, in `created_at` ascending order, and sets each to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` with its `version` incremented — a published figure whose source component has lost its certification is no longer trustworthy and is marked as awaiting restatement. The third level, caused by each such visit, voids every restatement request of that visited publication whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, because the pending proposal was measured against a baseline that has just been invalidated. Decertification additionally requires the parent cycle not to be `ARCHIVED`, reporting `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` if it is; a component in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_STATE`.

**Freezing the cycle** is the `IRDirector`'s act of closing the collection and opening publication, and it drives `CENSUS_LOCKED → FROZEN`. The cycle must be `CENSUS_LOCKED`; firing it from `OPEN` or `REOPENED` reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. At least one survey component must exist for the cycle, and every component of the cycle must be `CERTIFIED`; either failure reports `ERR_COMPONENTS_NOT_READY`, so an empty cycle and a cycle with one straggling `DRAFT` component are refused by the same error. On success `snapshot_posture` becomes `FROZEN`, `frozen_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` is incremented. The freeze also carries one cascade into the cohort world: every cohort definition of the cycle whose `status` is `DRAFT` **and** whose `adjusted_count` is at least 1 is locked — `status` becomes `LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped, both rate variants are recomputed, and `version` is incremented — processed in `created_at` ascending order. Cohorts whose `adjusted_count` is exactly 0 are deliberately **skipped** and left `DRAFT`, because no rate is definable over a zero denominator; they must be corrected and locked explicitly afterwards. This is the one place where a cascade is selective rather than universal.

**Reopening** drives `FROZEN → REOPENED` and is the deepest cascade in the system, four levels of consequence from a single director action. It requires the cycle to be exactly `FROZEN` — a `REOPENED`, `CENSUS_LOCKED`, `OPEN` or `ARCHIVED` cycle reports `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` — and a `reopen_reason` of 10 to 500 characters, absent or malformed which reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Level one sets `snapshot_posture` to `REOPENED`, increments `reopen_count`, stores the reason in `reopen_reason` and increments the cycle's `version`. Level two, caused by level one, reverts every survey component of the cycle whose `prep_status` is `CERTIFIED`: `prep_status` becomes `DRAFT`, `certified_by_user_id` and `certification_statement` are cleared, `edit_status` is forced to `NOT_RUN`, every edit check of that component at `OPEN`, `EXPLAINED`, `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` is voided, and the component's `version` is incremented, with components processed in `created_at` ascending order. Components already `SUBMITTED` are **not** reverted, because federal transmission is irreversible; components in `DRAFT` or `PREPARED` are untouched by this level because they were never certified. Level three, caused by each level-two reversion, visits every dashboard publication of that reverted component whose `publication_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, in `created_at` ascending order, and sets it to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING`: rows at `PUBLISHED` or `RESTATED` change status, and rows already at `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` keep it, but every visited row's `version` is still incremented because being visited is itself an event. Level four, caused by each level-three visit, voids every restatement request of that publication whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`. Throughout the reopen, every census extract of the cycle keeps its `status` of `LOCKED` — none is superseded automatically — but a fresh extract carrying an identical `domain` and coverage window may now be registered to supersede one, which is exactly how the loop `FROZEN → REOPENED → CENSUS_LOCKED → FROZEN` is closed. `reopen_count` records how many laps were run.

Two paths lead out of `FROZEN` and only one leads out of `ARCHIVED` — none. `ARCHIVED` is terminal, and any mutation of any child of an archived cycle is refused, with post-freeze read-side work and the disclosure pipeline being the sole exceptions that treat `ARCHIVED` as equivalent to `FROZEN`.

### 3.6. Cohorts, Tracked Outcomes, Allowable Exclusions and the Two Rate Variants

A **cohort definition** is a fixed group of entering students whose outcome is tracked to produce a graduation or retention rate. A `RegistrarOfficer` defines one against a cycle by supplying the `cohort_type` — `GRAD_RATE_150` for completion within 150 percent of normal time, or `RETENTION_FT` for full-time retention — the `entry_term` of 3 to 32 characters naming the term the cohort entered, and the `publication_metric_key` of 3 to 64 characters binding the cohort to the dashboard metric its federal rate publishes to. The triple of `cycle_id`, `cohort_type` and `entry_term` must be unique, so a second cohort of the same type for the same term reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_COHORT`. A new cohort starts at `status` `DRAFT`, with all five headcounts — `initial_count`, `exclusion_count`, `adjusted_count`, `completers_count` and `retained_count` — at 0, both `federal_rate_pct` and `ranking_rate_pct` null, `locked_at` null, and `version` 1. Cohort creation is refused once the cycle is `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.

**Roster membership** is editable only while the cohort is `DRAFT`. Adding a member supplies the `student_local_id` of 3 to 32 characters, the `entry_status` of `FULL_TIME` or `PART_TIME`, and optionally the `outcome`, which defaults to `STILL_ENROLLED`. The identifier must be unique within the cohort, so a repeat reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER`. The member is created with `exclusion_status` `NONE`, `exclusion_code`, `exclusion_evidence_note`, `exclusion_claimed_by_user_id`, `exclusion_reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` all null, and `version` 1; the parent cohort's counts and both rate variants are then recomputed and the cohort's `version` is incremented. Recording a tracked outcome later replaces `outcome` with one of `COMPLETED_150`, `STILL_ENROLLED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT` or `NO_LONGER_ENROLLED`, increments the member's `version`, and recomputes the cohort's `completers_count`, `retained_count` and both rate variants with the cohort's `version` incremented. Both of these roster writes, and a second attempt to lock, are refused on a `LOCKED` cohort with `ERR_COHORT_LOCKED`; both are refused once the cycle is `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.

**The five headcounts** are always recomputed from scratch over every member of the cohort, never adjusted by deltas. `initial_count` is the number of members with no filter at all. `exclusion_count` is the number whose `exclusion_status` is `APPROVED`. `adjusted_count` is `initial_count` minus `exclusion_count`. `completers_count` is the number whose `outcome` is `COMPLETED_150`. `retained_count` is the number whose `outcome` is `COMPLETED_150` or `STILL_ENROLLED`, so a completer is also counted as retained. A conservation identity holds after every membership creation and every exclusion decision: `adjusted_count` plus `exclusion_count` always equals `initial_count`. Because the recomputation is total rather than incremental, no drift can accumulate: an exclusion that is approved, refiled and then denied restores `adjusted_count`, `exclusion_count`, `federal_rate_pct` and `ranking_rate_pct` to the exact values they held before the approval.

**Two rate variants are computed from the same roster, and they differ in what they do with approved exclusions.** They are not two roundings of one number; they are two different fractions.

- The **federal adjusted rate**, exposed as `federal_rate_pct`, removes approved exclusions from *both* sides. Its numerator counts members whose `exclusion_status` is not `APPROVED` and whose `outcome` is `COMPLETED_150` for a `GRAD_RATE_150` cohort, or whose `outcome` is `COMPLETED_150` or `STILL_ENROLLED` for a `RETENTION_FT` cohort. Its denominator is `adjusted_count`. When `adjusted_count` is 0 the rate is null; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to one decimal place. This is the figure reported to the federal collection and published on the internal dashboard.
- The **ranking gross rate**, exposed as `ranking_rate_pct`, applies no exclusion filter at all, because ranking and consortium surveys forbid exclusion adjustments. Its numerator is `completers_count` for a `GRAD_RATE_150` cohort and `retained_count` for a `RETENTION_FT` cohort — note that these counts include members whose exclusion was approved. Its denominator is `initial_count`. When `initial_count` is 0 the rate is null; otherwise the same percentage and the same one-decimal round half up apply. Whenever `exclusion_count` is above 0 and the excluded students are non-completers, this rate is strictly lower than the federal one.

The divergence between them, `rate_divergence_pp`, is reported in percentage points as the federal rate minus the ranking rate, rounded half up to one decimal place. It is computed from **the two already-rounded variants**, not re-derived from the underlying counts, so it is exactly the difference of the two published figures. It is null whenever either variant is null. A computed read exposes both variants side by side together with `rate_divergence_pp` and the five headcounts they were derived from.

**Locking the cohort** freezes the roster and pins both rates. The `RegistrarOfficer` who locks it must satisfy five preconditions: the cohort must be `DRAFT` (else `ERR_COHORT_LOCKED`), the parent cycle must not be `ARCHIVED` (else `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`), `initial_count` must be at least 1 (else `ERR_EMPTY_COHORT`), no member of the cohort may sit at `exclusion_status` `CLAIMED` (else `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EXCLUSION_CLAIM`), and `adjusted_count` must be at least 1 (else `ERR_EMPTY_COHORT_DENOMINATOR`). The last two conditions are distinct failures with distinct errors: a cohort of ten students, all of whose exclusions were approved, has a non-zero `initial_count` and a zero `adjusted_count`, so it passes the emptiness test and fails the denominator test. On success the cohort moves `DRAFT → LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped from the logical clock, both rate variants are recomputed and `version` is incremented. `LOCKED` is terminal. The cycle freeze reaches the same destination through its own cascade, with one difference already noted: the cascade silently skips cohorts whose `adjusted_count` is 0 rather than failing.

**Allowable exclusions** are the mechanism by which a student is removed from the federal denominator, and their claim-and-review loop is deliberately open even after the roster is locked, because a late allowable exclusion is precisely what makes a published rate wrong. Filing a claim requires an `exclusion_code` drawn from the closed federal list — `DECEASED`, `PERMANENTLY_DISABLED`, `ARMED_FORCES`, `FOREIGN_AID_SERVICE` or `CHURCH_MISSION`, anything else reporting `ERR_INVALID_EXCLUSION_CODE` — together with an `exclusion_evidence_note` of 10 to 500 characters. It drives `NONE → CLAIMED` for a first filing and `DENIED → CLAIMED` for a refiling with new evidence, in which case the code and the note are replaced. Both variants store `exclusion_claimed_by_user_id` and increment the member's `version`, both require the parent cohort's cycle not to be `ARCHIVED`, and a member in any other exclusion state reports `ERR_INVALID_EXCLUSION_STATE`. Filing a claim on a `LOCKED` cohort is explicitly permitted.

Reviewing a claim is the `IRDirector`'s call on a `decision` field of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, with an optional `review_note`, and it drives three transitions. `CLAIMED → APPROVED` on an approval, `CLAIMED → DENIED` on a denial, and `APPROVED → DENIED` on a denial of an already-approved exclusion, which is the compensating action that reverses an approval. Every variant writes `exclusion_reviewed_by_user_id`, stores the note when supplied, increments the member's `version`, and recomputes the parent cohort's `exclusion_count`, `adjusted_count` and both rate variants with the cohort's `version` incremented. The three differ in what actually moves: an approval adds the member to `exclusion_count` and removes it from `adjusted_count`; a denial of a claim moves neither count, because a merely `CLAIMED` member had never entered `exclusion_count`; and a denial of an approval decreases `exclusion_count` by one and increases `adjusted_count` by one, restoring both rates to their pre-approval values exactly.

**The cascade into published rates fires only when the cohort is `LOCKED`.** When an exclusion decision changes `federal_rate_pct` on a locked cohort, the system reaches into the dashboard: every dashboard publication whose `metric_key` equals the cohort's `publication_metric_key`, whose parent component belongs to the same cycle as the cohort, and whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, is moved to `RESTATEMENT_PENDING`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with one `version` increment per publication for the whole visit. Then, for each publication so flipped, every restatement request beneath it whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, and a fresh restatement request is filed by the system itself: `origin` `CASCADE`, `requested_by_user_id` the literal string `SYSTEM`, `proposed_value` the cohort's newly computed `federal_rate_pct`, `baseline_value` the publication's current `published_value`, `justification` the fixed text `Cascade restatement: cohort exclusion decision applied`, `status` `SUBMITTED`, and `delta_pct` and `public_notice_required` derived by the materiality rule of §3.7. The denial of an approval fires the identical cascade, carrying the restored rate rather than the adjusted one. A decision on a `DRAFT` cohort recomputes the counts and rates and stops there — nothing is published yet, so nothing needs restating.

### 3.7. Dashboard Publication and Restated-Data Change Control

A **dashboard publication** is one metric value shown on the institution's public-facing dashboard over a stated period of validity. An `IRAnalyst` publishes one by supplying the `component_id` of the certified component the figure comes from, the `metric_key` it is published under, the `published_value`, the `effective_from` instant and optionally the `effective_to` instant; omitting `effective_to` means the figure is currently effective with no end. Publication requires the governing cycle's `snapshot_posture` to be `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED`, because nothing may be published from a file still open to correction, and a cycle in any earlier posture reports `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN`. When `effective_to` is supplied it must be strictly after `effective_from`, since a zero-length half-open window would publish nothing at all; a violation reports `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW`. The new window must not overlap the window of any peer publication of the same `component_id` and the same `metric_key` whose `publication_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, per the half-open geometry of §2.11; an overlap reports `ERR_PUBLICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Withdrawn publications are excluded from that occupancy set, so withdrawing a figure frees its window for reuse.

The row is created at `publication_status` `PUBLISHED`, with `original_value` set equal to the supplied `published_value` and never written again, `net_applied_delta` at 0, `restatement_count` at 0, `published_by_user_id` recording the publishing analyst, `withdrawal_reason` null and `version` 1. Together these four numeric fields form a small **restatement ledger** on every publication, and one invariant links them: `published_value` always equals `original_value` plus `net_applied_delta`, rounded half up to one decimal place. `original_value` is the figure the institution first stood behind, `net_applied_delta` is the accumulated signed movement of every applied restatement, and `restatement_count` is how many were applied. Because the delta is signed, approving a restatement that returns the figure to its original value drives `net_applied_delta` to exactly 0.0 and `published_value` back to `original_value`, while `restatement_count` keeps climbing — the audit trail is never rewound.

**Filing a restatement request** is how a published figure is challenged, and it is guarded by optimistic concurrency: the request carries the mandatory `expected_publication_version`, and a mismatch against the publication's current `version` reports `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION`. Beyond that, the analyst supplies the `proposed_value` and a `justification` of 20 to 1000 characters. The created request starts at `status` `SUBMITTED` and `origin` `ANALYST`, captures `baseline_value` from the publication's current `published_value`, records `requested_by_user_id`, has `approved_by_user_id`, `public_notice_reference` and `rejection_reason` null, and `version` 1. Filing it moves the publication itself: `PUBLISHED → RESTATEMENT_PENDING` for a figure never yet restated, and `RESTATED → RESTATEMENT_PENDING` for one that has been, with the publication's `version` incremented either way. A `WITHDRAWN` publication never accepts a restatement and is never visited by a cascade, reporting `ERR_INVALID_PUBLICATION_STATE`. Filing also supersedes the competition: every *other* restatement request of the same publication still sitting at `SUBMITTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one request is ever live on a publication.

**Materiality** is decided at filing time and never recomputed. The baseline is the publication's `published_value` at that moment — which for a second restatement is the value the first one left behind, not `original_value`. When the baseline is exactly 0, `delta_pct` is null, because a percentage change from zero is undefined, and `public_notice_required` is true whenever the `proposed_value` is anything other than 0. Otherwise `delta_pct` is the absolute difference between `proposed_value` and the baseline, divided by the absolute baseline, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `public_notice_required` is true when `delta_pct` is at least 2.00. The comparison is inclusive, so a delta of exactly 2.00 requires a public notice. Note the baseline asymmetry this creates: moving a figure from 250 to 275 is a delta of 10.00 and material, while moving it back from 275 to 250 is a delta of 9.09 and not.

**Approving a restatement** drives the request `SUBMITTED → APPLIED` and the publication `RESTATEMENT_PENDING → RESTATED`. It requires the request to be `SUBMITTED` (else `ERR_INVALID_RESTATEMENT_STATE`), its parent publication to be `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` (else `ERR_INVALID_PUBLICATION_STATE`), the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `requested_by_user_id` (else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — a request whose requester is the literal `SYSTEM` can never collide), and, when `public_notice_required` is true, a `public_notice_reference` of 3 to 120 characters, absent which the approval reports `ERR_PUBLIC_NOTICE_REQUIRED`. The reference is optional on an immaterial restatement. On success the request records `approved_by_user_id`, stores the reference, and increments its `version`; the publication sets `published_value` to the request's `proposed_value`, increases `net_applied_delta` by the difference between `proposed_value` and `baseline_value` rounded half up to one decimal place, increments `restatement_count` by one, moves to `RESTATED`, and increments its own `version`. The ledger invariant is re-checked afterwards.

**Rejecting a restatement** drives the request `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and requires a `rejection_reason` of 10 to 500 characters, absent which it reports `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`, together with the same segregation-of-duties separation from `requested_by_user_id`. The request stores the reason, records the deciding director in `approved_by_user_id`, and increments its `version`. The publication's own destination then depends on its history, and this is the compensation branch: once no other request of that publication remains at `SUBMITTED`, a publication whose `restatement_count` is exactly 0 returns to `PUBLISHED`, restoring the state it held before the request was ever filed, while a publication whose `restatement_count` is at least 1 returns to `RESTATED`, because a previously applied restatement still stands. Either way the publication's `version` is incremented and neither `published_value` nor `net_applied_delta` moves: a rejected restatement contributes nothing to the ledger and does not increment `restatement_count`.

The publication dimension is therefore a genuine loop. `PUBLISHED → RESTATEMENT_PENDING → RESTATED → RESTATEMENT_PENDING` can be run any number of times, which is how a compensating restatement walks a figure back to its original value, and a rejection drives either `RESTATEMENT_PENDING → PUBLISHED` or `RESTATEMENT_PENDING → RESTATED`. Three of the four states — `PUBLISHED`, `RESTATEMENT_PENDING` and `RESTATED` — accept **withdrawal** by an `IRDirector` — `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, `RESTATEMENT_PENDING → WITHDRAWN` and `RESTATED → WITHDRAWN` — which requires a `withdrawal_reason` of 10 to 500 characters, stores it, moves `publication_status` to `WITHDRAWN`, increments `version`, and voids every restatement request of that publication still sitting at `SUBMITTED`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, and a withdrawal attempt against an already-withdrawn publication reports `ERR_INVALID_PUBLICATION_STATE`.

Restatement requests do not only come from analysts. The cohort exclusion cascade of §3.6 and the decertification and reopen cascades of §3.5 both reach into this area, and the four terminal states of a request record which fate befell it: `APPLIED` for an approval, `REJECTED` for a rejection, `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` when a newer request displaced it on the same publication, and `SUBMITTED → VOIDED` when the ground beneath it was removed — its publication's source component was decertified, directly or by a cycle reopen, or the publication itself was withdrawn. Requests filed by a cascade are distinguishable from analyst filings by their `origin` of `CASCADE` and their `requested_by_user_id` of `SYSTEM`, and they are approved and rejected through exactly the same operations, with the same materiality flag and the same public-notice obligation, by a director who is never blocked by the segregation-of-duties check.

### 3.8. External Survey Reconciliation, Allowances and Transmission

The same institutional figures are also sent to state agencies, consortia and commercial ranking publishers, and those recipients must not receive numbers that silently contradict the federal filing. An **external submission** records one metric sent to one recipient and holds the arithmetic of the comparison. An `IRAnalyst` creates one by supplying the `cycle_id`, the `component_id` of the source component the federal figure will be read from, the `target_type` of `STATE`, `CONSORTIUM` or `RANKING`, the `target_name` of 3 to 120 characters naming the recipient, the `metric_key` of 3 to 64 characters, and the `reported_value` actually sent externally, which must be at least 0. The source component must belong to the supplied cycle, or it is treated as not found. The named `metric_key` is gated against the source component's own survey domain by exactly the rule of §3.3, so an `HR` component cannot back an enrollment figure, and a violation reports `ERR_METRIC_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`. Creation additionally requires the cycle not to be `ARCHIVED`, reporting `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. The triple of `cycle_id`, `target_name` and `metric_key` must be unique among rows whose `status` is not `TRANSMITTED`, so a second draft to the same recipient for the same metric reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION` while a figure already transmitted leaves the slot free for a corrected resubmission. The row starts at `status` `DRAFT` with all five reconciliation fields — `federal_value`, `raw_delta_pct`, `allowance_pct`, `effective_delta_pct` and `tolerance_pct` — null, `override_reason`, `overridden_by_user_id`, `transmission_reference` and `transmitted_at` null, and `version` 1.

**Reconciliation** is the analyst's act of measuring the external figure against the federal one, and it is a single forward pass of five steps whose order is normative.

1. The federal figure is read: `federal_value` is the `reported_value` of the component metric on the referenced component whose `metric_key` equals the submission's `metric_key`. Then `raw_delta_pct` is the absolute difference between the submission's `reported_value` and `federal_value`, divided by `federal_value`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places.
2. The **allowance tier** is selected from `raw_delta_pct` and from nothing else. A raw delta below 1.00 earns an `allowance_pct` of 0.00; a raw delta below 5.00 earns 0.50; anything at or above 5.00 earns 1.00. The tier bounds are strict, so a raw delta of exactly 1.00 falls into the middle band and a raw delta of exactly 5.00 falls into the top band. The allowance exists because recipients accept small definitional drift between a state's counting rules and the federal ones, and larger gaps earn a larger, not smaller, benefit of the doubt.
3. The reduced gap is computed: `effective_delta_pct` is `raw_delta_pct` minus `allowance_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places, floored at 0.00 so it is never negative.
4. The recipient tolerance is looked up from `target_type`: `tolerance_pct` is 0.50 for a `STATE` recipient, 1.00 for a `CONSORTIUM` and 3.00 for a `RANKING` publisher.
5. The verdict is taken by comparing the reduced gap against the tolerance, and the comparison is inclusive: when `effective_delta_pct` is less than or equal to `tolerance_pct` the submission is reconciled, and otherwise it is held.

The evaluation order matters and must not be iterated. The dependency only looks circular — the tolerance comparison consumes a value whose allowance came from a tier of that same value — and a single pass in the order 1 through 5 makes it well defined. **Re-tiering the reduced gap is a wrong answer.** A raw delta of 1.40 earns an allowance of 0.50 and an effective delta of 0.90 in a single pass; feeding 0.90 back into the tier table would put it in the lowest band, produce an allowance of 0.00, and yield an effective delta of 1.40 instead — a different number, and for a `CONSORTIUM` recipient a different verdict. The tier is derived once, from the unadjusted `raw_delta_pct`, and never re-derived.

Reconciliation is therefore one operation with two divergent destinations chosen purely by step 5: `DRAFT → RECONCILED` when the reduced gap sits within tolerance, and `DRAFT → VARIANCE_HOLD` when it does not. Both branches write all five reconciliation fields and increment `version`; they differ only in the `status` written. Both require the submission to be `DRAFT`, reporting `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE` otherwise, and both require the referenced component's `prep_status` to be `CERTIFIED` or `SUBMITTED`, because an uncertified component has no authoritative federal value to compare against, reporting `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Both require the matching component metric to exist on that component, reporting `ERR_FEDERAL_VALUE_UNAVAILABLE` when it does not. And the arithmetic requires a non-zero denominator: when the matching metric's `reported_value` is exactly 0, the percentage gap is undefined and the reconciliation is refused with `ERR_ZERO_BASE_RECONCILIATION` rather than producing a null or an infinity.

A held variance is not a dead end. The `IRDirector` may **override** it, driving `VARIANCE_HOLD → RECONCILED`, on supplying an `override_reason` of 20 to 500 characters, absent or malformed which reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The override stores the reason, records `overridden_by_user_id`, and increments `version`, and it deliberately **does not recompute** the five reconciliation fields: the variance that was measured is preserved exactly as recorded, so the audit trail shows both that the figure was out of tolerance and that a named director accepted it anyway. An override attempted against a submission that is not `VARIANCE_HOLD` reports `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`.

**Transmission** drives `RECONCILED → TRANSMITTED` and is terminal. It is the director's act, requires the submission to be `RECONCILED` (else `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`), requires the cycle's `snapshot_posture` to be `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` (else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN`), requires the logical clock to be at or before the cycle's `collection_deadline` with equality passing (else `ERR_COLLECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`), and requires a `transmission_reference` of 3 to 64 characters identifying the recipient's acknowledgement. On success `status` becomes `TRANSMITTED`, the reference is stored, `transmitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` is incremented. A submission that reached `VARIANCE_HOLD` can never be transmitted directly — it must pass through an override first — which is the whole point of the hold.

### 3.9. Data Requests and Statistical Disclosure Avoidance

The last area of the system serves outside parties who ask for institutional figures, and its concern is the opposite of every other area's: not accuracy but the protection of individual students hiding inside small counts. A **data request** is opened against a cycle by a `DisclosureOfficer` who supplies the `requester_org` of 3 to 120 characters, the `requester_type` of `INTERNAL`, `EXTERNAL` or `MEDIA`, the `requested_metric_key` of 3 to 64 characters, optionally the `avoidance_method` of `SMALL_CELL_SUPPRESSION` or `CONTROLLED_ROUNDING` which defaults to `SMALL_CELL_SUPPRESSION`, and optionally the `min_cell_size` which defaults to 5 and must be between 2 and 10 inclusive. Opening a request requires the cycle's `snapshot_posture` to be `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED`, reporting `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN` otherwise, because only settled figures may be disclosed. The row starts at `status` `RECEIVED`, with `cell_count`, `suppressed_cell_count` and `released_cell_count` at 0, `residual_disclosure_risk` false, `refusal_reason`, `screened_at`, `screened_by_user_id` and `release_note` null, and `version` 1.

The figures themselves arrive as **disclosure cells**, one per crosstab cell of the requested breakdown. Each cell supplies the `dimension_group` of 2 to 64 characters naming the crosstab dimension, such as a race-and-ethnicity or a program-level breakdown, the `dimension_key` of 1 to 64 characters naming the category within that dimension, and the `raw_count` of at least 0 — the true, unprotected count. The triple of `request_id`, `dimension_group` and `dimension_key` must be unique, so a repeated category within a dimension reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CELL`. Cells may be added only while the request is `RECEIVED`; once it has been screened the crosstab is fixed, and a later addition reports `ERR_INVALID_DATA_REQUEST_STATE`. Each new cell starts at `suppression_state` `PENDING` with `released_count` null and `version` 1, and the parent request's `cell_count` is recomputed with the request's `version` incremented.

**Screening** is one operation with two divergent destinations, and the branch is chosen before any cell is touched. Both branches require the request to be `RECEIVED` (else `ERR_INVALID_DATA_REQUEST_STATE`), the cycle to be `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` (else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN`), and `cell_count` to be at least 1 (else `ERR_NO_CELLS_TO_SCREEN`). The test is the sum of `raw_count` across every cell of the request. When that total is below the request's `min_cell_size`, the whole crosstab is too small to protect by any means, and the request is refused: `RECEIVED → REFUSED`, with `refusal_reason` set to the literal `AGGREGATE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `screened_at` stamped, `screened_by_user_id` recorded and `version` incremented. **No cell is mutated on this branch**: every cell keeps `suppression_state` `PENDING` and `released_count` null, and nothing is computed at all. When the total is at or above `min_cell_size`, the request proceeds `RECEIVED → SCREENED`: every cell of the request is assigned a `suppression_state` and a `released_count` by the avoidance algorithm below, processed in `dimension_group` ascending then `dimension_key` ascending order with each cell's `version` incremented, after which `suppressed_cell_count`, `released_cell_count` and `residual_disclosure_risk` are recomputed, `screened_at` is stamped, `screened_by_user_id` is recorded and the request's `version` is incremented.

**Small-cell suppression** — the default method — runs in two passes over the request's cells.

The first pass is a cell-by-cell primary suppression, walking the cells in `dimension_group` ascending then `dimension_key` ascending order. A cell whose `raw_count` is strictly greater than 0 **and** strictly less than `min_cell_size` is a small cell: its `suppression_state` becomes `PRIMARY_SUPPRESSED` and its `released_count` is set to null. Every other cell — one whose `raw_count` is 0, and one whose `raw_count` is at or above `min_cell_size` — becomes `RELEASED` with `released_count` set equal to its `raw_count`. A `raw_count` of exactly 0 is a **structural zero**, not a small count: it reveals nothing about any individual because there is nobody in the category, so it is always released untouched.

The second pass is complementary suppression, and it is evaluated per `dimension_group`, taking the groups in ascending order. Within a group, the primaries are the cells the first pass suppressed, and the candidates are the cells the first pass released whose `raw_count` is strictly greater than 0 — a structural zero is never a complementary candidate, because suppressing it would protect nothing. When the group contains **exactly one** primary, that primary is exposed: anyone who knows the group total can subtract the released cells and recover the suppressed count, so a second cell must be suppressed to break the arithmetic. If the group has at least one candidate, the victim is the candidate with the smallest `raw_count`, with ties broken by `dimension_key` ascending; its `suppression_state` becomes `COMPLEMENTARY_SUPPRESSED` and its `released_count` is set to null. If the group has no candidate at all, no second suppression is possible, and the request's `residual_disclosure_risk` is set to true to record that the release remains re-identifiable. When the group contains **no** primary there is nothing to protect and nothing is done. When it contains **two or more** primaries, the primaries already mask each other — no single subtraction recovers either — and again nothing is done. The middle case is the only one that acts, and the two edge cases are deliberately silent.

**Controlled rounding** — the alternative method — is far simpler and takes a different shape entirely. There is no notion of a primary, no per-group second pass and no victim. Every cell of the request becomes `RELEASED`, and its `released_count` is its `raw_count` divided by 5, rounded half up to a whole number, multiplied by 5. A raw count of 3 is released as 5, 12 as 10, 20 as 20, 45 as 45, and 0 as 0. Because no cell is ever suppressed, `residual_disclosure_risk` is always false under this method. The two methods share only their tail: `released_cell_count` and `suppressed_cell_count` are recomputed the same way afterwards, as the number of cells whose `suppression_state` is `RELEASED` and the number whose state is `PRIMARY_SUPPRESSED` or `COMPLEMENTARY_SUPPRESSED` respectively.

**Release** is the final act and is again one operation with two destinations, chosen by what survived screening. It requires the request to be `SCREENED` (else `ERR_INVALID_DATA_REQUEST_STATE`) and the cycle to be `FROZEN` or `ARCHIVED` (else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FROZEN`), and it accepts an optional `release_note`. When `released_cell_count` is at least 1 the request moves `SCREENED → RELEASED`; when `released_cell_count` is exactly 0, so that suppression consumed the entire crosstab, it moves `SCREENED → SUPPRESSED_RELEASE` instead, which records that the request was answered with nothing disclosable. Both branches store the note, increment `version`, and are terminal, as is `REFUSED`. Both branches also carry one shared safety gate: a request whose `residual_disclosure_risk` is true may not be released at all when its `requester_type` is `EXTERNAL` or `MEDIA`, because the lone primary-suppressed cell that had no complementary candidate is recoverable by subtraction, and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_SMALL_CELL_DISCLOSURE`. The same residual risk is tolerated for an `INTERNAL` requester, who is already inside the institution's confidentiality perimeter. Since `residual_disclosure_risk` is only ever raised by the small-cell method, a controlled-rounding request never meets this gate.

The screening verdict itself is readable per cell, so a recipient can see exactly which categories were withheld: each disclosure cell exposes its `dimension_group`, `dimension_key`, `raw_count`, `suppression_state` and `released_count`, and the three terminal verdicts are reached only by a screening run and never revisited afterwards: `PENDING → RELEASED`, `PENDING → PRIMARY_SUPPRESSED` and `PENDING → COMPLEMENTARY_SUPPRESSED` are the only transitions a cell ever makes.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/ir` prefix and follows the response envelope of §2.2, the role model of §2.1 and the validation precedence of §2.12. Endpoints that create a new resource return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful call, including every state-transition action and every list, returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource, the requested resource, or the array of resources in `data`.
