# Interscholastic Athletics Eligibility API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state high school athletic association sits at the intersection of two very different failure modes. On one side, an ineligible athlete who takes the field costs the member school every contest that athlete appeared in, and the association's credibility with the schools that played by the rules. On the other, an eligible athlete kept off the field by a lost consent form, an unposted grading-period record, or a bus that was never approved loses a season that cannot be given back. Today those decisions are made by fax, spreadsheet and phone call across hundreds of member schools, and no two regions apply the same order of reasoning to the same facts.

This document specifies the association's Athletics Eligibility and Scheduling service, a single HTTP API mounted under `/athletics` that owns the whole chain: member-school registration and coach background screening, athlete enrolment and grading-period records, the eligibility ruling itself, physical and consent form clearance, participation fees and hardship waivers, the season calendar, contest scheduling with certified officials, bus and instructional-release-time coordination, injury reporting with a graduated return-to-play protocol, and finally the playoff bracket. Every one of those surfaces is a place where a school and the association can disagree, so the service is written as a rules engine with a fixed order of reasoning rather than as a set of independent CRUD screens.

Two design commitments run through everything below. First, **segregation of duties**: the principal who supplies a fact is never the principal who rules on it — the registrar who posts a transcript cannot adjudicate the eligibility case built from it, and the medical officer who files an injury report cannot be the one who clears the athlete to return. Second, **conservation**: instructional minutes and fee cents are tracked as ledgers with explicit compensating actions, so that an approve/cancel pair or a pay/refund pair returns the school's totals to exactly where they started. Determinism matters more than convenience here; where two readings of a rule are defensible, this document pins one of them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the seven roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `AssociationAdmin`, `SchoolAthleticDirector`, `Registrar`, `EligibilityReviewer`, `MedicalOfficer`, `OfficialsCoordinator`, and `Official`. Spelling is exact and case-sensitive.

- **`AssociationAdmin`** is the state association's operations staff: the calendar, sanctions, seeding, background-check adjudication, waiver decisions, refunds, transport approval, forfeits, postponements, and the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `AssociationAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it in narrative notes, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and an `AssociationAdmin` is never subject to an ownership check.
- **`SchoolAthleticDirector`** is the athletic director acting for one member school. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolAthleticDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `School` compare the resource's governing school reference to `X-User-ID`.
- **`Registrar`** is the school records officer, who posts grading-period academic records and nothing else. When `X-User-Role` is `Registrar`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `School`, and the same ownership comparison applies.
- **`EligibilityReviewer`** is the association's eligibility officer, who runs the ladder and promotes cases. When `X-User-Role` is `EligibilityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`, but it does compare it to the operator identifier previously stored on a `Student` as `academic_record_submitted_by` for the segregation-of-duties rule.
- **`MedicalOfficer`** is a licensed physician or athletic trainer. When `X-User-Role` is `MedicalOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `reported_by_user_id` on an `InjuryReport` it files and as `cleared_by_user_id` on a clearance it issues, and compares those two recorded values to each other, but never to an entity's `id`.
- **`OfficialsCoordinator`** is the association assignor who offers games to certified officials. When `X-User-Role` is `OfficialsCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- **`Official`** is a certified game official acting for themselves. When `X-User-Role` is `Official`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Official`; ownership checks compare an assignment's `official_id` to `X-User-ID`.

For the two entity-backed families, the ownership comparison is resolved per resource, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `SchoolAthleticDirector` or `Registrar` must present the governing school's identifier: `home_school_id` for contest writes, `requesting_school_id` for transport writes, the owning `school_id` of the `Student` for student and eligibility writes, and the `school_id` on the credential for credential creation. An `Official` must present the `official_id` recorded on the assignment being answered. This ownership screen guards exactly fourteen write operations — creating a student, posting that student's academic record, creating a staff credential, opening an eligibility case, filing a clearance document on a case, appealing a case, paying a case's fee, requesting a fee waiver on a case, creating a contest, confirming a contest, recording a contest result, creating a transport request, cancelling a transport request, and responding to an officiating offer. Contest creation is shared between `SchoolAthleticDirector` and `AssociationAdmin`; the admin is exempt from the home-school comparison, the director is not.

Read operations are universally permitted: every listed `GET` endpoint accepts all seven roles. Write operations are narrowly held, and §4 records the authorized role or roles for each one.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer in the inclusive range 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400) before any domain work is attempted.

Unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four listings override that default and are called out where they belong: a school's coaching credentials come back by `created_at` descending (newest first); a season's drawn bracket comes back by `seed_number` ascending; contests come back by `starts_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; transport requests come back by `depart_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; and injury reports come back by `reported_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Because the logical clock stands still between administrator moves, many rows are minted with an identical `created_at`; wherever `created_at` is the ordering key or the declared tie-break, rows sharing an instant are ordered by insertion sequence, the earliest-inserted row first (and last, on the one descending listing). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Version Counter

Every persisted resource is minted with an `id` that is a server-generated UUID carrying no type prefix — clients must never construct one — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. The exceptions are the two subordinate record types. A `ClearanceDocument` carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because a filed form is never edited. A `PlayoffSeed` likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`: seeds are minted once by a seeding run and never mutated. All ten other resource families — `School`, `Student`, `StaffCredential`, `SportSeason`, `EligibilityCase`, `Contest`, `Official`, `OfficialAssignment`, `TransportRequest`, and `InjuryReport` — carry the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Because several operations mutate a parent as a side effect of touching a child, the version counter is not merely local bookkeeping. Where a rule below says that a cascade "bumps the school's version", it means precisely that the affected `School` row's `version` advances by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as if the school had been edited directly.

### 2.5. The Association Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads real wall-clock time. Every date and time rule — age at the cutoff, sit-out arithmetic, document freshness, credential lapse, return-to-play intervals, departure-in-the-past checks, result-before-tipoff checks — reads a server-held **logical clock**. At startup the logical clock stands at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that instant until an administrator moves it. There is no fallback to system time under any circumstance.

An administrator moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The response reports the new `current_utc`, along with `expired_credential_count` — the number of coaching credentials that this move pushed into the `EXPIRED` state — and `expired_document_count` — the number of clearance documents whose freshness, recomputed against the new instant, now reads `EXPIRED`, counted over every clearance document in the service rather than only those the move newly expired. Moving the clock is therefore not a passive operation: it runs the credential-expiry checkpoint described in §3.2, and that checkpoint can itself reopen a school's coaching gap. The checkpoint only ever moves credentials out of `CLEARED`: moving the clock backwards never returns an `EXPIRED` credential to `CLEARED` and never removes a sport from a gap list.

Where a rule compares a stored ISO 8601 **date** against the clock, the comparison uses the clock instant's UTC calendar date. Where a rule compares against a stored **instant**, the comparison is instant-to-instant.

### 2.6. Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first failing check determines the response. This ordering is authoritative: where a workflow narrative in §3 lists several conditions, that narrative describes what must hold, not the sequence in which the conditions are tested.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint role list.** The acting role appears among the endpoint's authorized roles — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Resource ownership.** For the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` matches the governing entity as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to the fourteen write operations enumerated in §2.1. This tier outranks the two existence tiers below it only where the governing identifier is supplied directly in the request body — `school_id` on student and credential creation, `home_school_id` on contest creation, `requesting_school_id` on transport creation. Where the governing school or official can be reached only by first resolving another identifier — any path-parameterised write, and eligibility-case creation, whose school is discovered through the named `student_id` — that identifier is necessarily resolved first, so a missing row reports its own 404 (`ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSPORT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`) ahead of `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a row, validated left to right, so that an outer identifier is reported before an inner one. The rejection is 404 with the code naming the missing kind: `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSPORT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INJURY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign reference supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row — otherwise 404 with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_OFFICIAL_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to student creation, staff-credential creation, eligibility-case creation, contest creation, officiating offers, transport-request creation, and injury-report filing.
6. **Body shape.** Required body fields present, enum values drawn from their declared sets, numbers inside their declared ranges, ISO 8601 fields parseable, and `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at` — likewise `new_ends_at` strictly after `new_starts_at` on a postponement — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
7. **Pagination shape.** `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
8. **Season version lock.** `expected_sport_season_version` matches the season's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` (412). Applies to contest creation.
9. **Contest version lock.** `expected_contest_version` matches the contest's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONTEST_VERSION` (412). Applies to officiating offers.
10. **State admissibility.** The target row's current state admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) on any transition endpoint that has no more specific state error. At this same tier sit the specific state gates: `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVATABLE` (409) when activation is attempted on a case whose eligibility status is not `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE`; `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) when an appeal is attempted on a case that has already appealed twice; `ERR_ALREADY_RESPONDED` (409) when an official answers an assignment whose status is not `OFFERED`; `ERR_RTP_STAGE_CEILING` (409) when a non-recurrence advance is attempted at return-to-play stage 5 and `ERR_RTP_STAGE_INCOMPLETE` (409) when a return-to-play clearance is attempted below stage 5; and `ERR_CREDENTIAL_ALREADY_LAPSED` (409) when a background-check adjudication would clear a credential whose effective expiry date is on or before the clock's UTC date.
11. **Open contests before seeding.** Every contest of the season has reached `PLAYED` or `FORFEITED` — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_HAS_OPEN_CONTESTS` (409) on the seeding run.
12. **Season posture.** The governing season is in a status that admits the operation: `PRACTICE_OPEN` or `REGULAR_SEASON` for eligibility evaluation, and `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS` for contest confirmation and for officiating offers — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` (409).
13. **Contest posture for downstream work.** A contest must be in `SCHEDULED` or `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` when an official responds — otherwise `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_STAFFABLE` (409) — and in `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` when a transport request is approved — otherwise `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_SCHEDULED_FOR_TRANSPORT` (409).
14. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the principal recorded on the record being adjudicated — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to eligibility evaluation, where the comparison is against `academic_record_submitted_by`, and to injury clearance, where it is against `reported_by_user_id`.
15. **School sanction.** The governing school's `sanction_status` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_SANCTIONED` (409). Applies to contest confirmation and to case activation.
16. **Head-coach credential.** The student's school holds at least one cleared, unexpired head-coach credential for the season's sport — otherwise `ERR_COACH_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (409). Applies to case activation.
17. **Academic record present.** The student's `academic_record_submitted_at` is not null — otherwise `ERR_ACADEMIC_RECORD_MISSING` (409). Applies to eligibility evaluation.
18. **Medical hold.** No case of the injured athlete may be left eligible while an injury report of that athlete stands at `REPORTED` or `RTP_IN_PROGRESS`, and a fresh case for such an athlete cannot be activated — otherwise `ERR_ATHLETE_MEDICALLY_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to case activation.
19. **Season window.** A contest's start date falls on or after the season's `first_contest_date` and on or before its `season_end_date` — otherwise `ERR_CONTEST_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW` (409). Applies to contest creation.
20. **Venue turnover.** The home venue admits the new contest — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_TURNOVER_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to contest creation.
21. **Official double-booking.** The official's buffered commitments admit the new offer — otherwise `ERR_OFFICIAL_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409). Applies to officiating offers.
22. **Return-to-play intervals.** The applicable rest, inter-stage, or total-duration floor has elapsed on the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_RTP_INTERVAL_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Applies to stage advancement and to injury clearance.
23. **Instants in the past.** A computed departure must be strictly after the clock (`ERR_TRANSPORT_DEPARTURE_IN_PAST`, 409); a posted result must not precede the contest's `starts_at` (`ERR_RESULT_BEFORE_TIPOFF`, 409); a postponement's `new_starts_at` must be strictly after the clock (`ERR_CONTEST_TIME_IN_PAST`, 409). These apply to transport-request creation, result recording, and postponement respectively.
24. **Opponent sanity.** `home_school_id` differs from `away_school_id` — otherwise `ERR_SAME_SCHOOL_CONTEST` (409) — and both schools' `classification` equals the season's `classification` — otherwise `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to contest creation.
25. **Contest load.** The season limit and the rolling-window density admit the new contest, for the home school first and then the away school — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_CONTEST_LIMIT` (409) or `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_DENSITY` (409). Applies to contest creation.
26. **Officiating quorum.** `accepted_official_count` has reached the season's `required_officials` — otherwise `ERR_OFFICIALS_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to contest confirmation.
27. **Referential sanity on closing operations.** The seeding snapshot holds at least one contest — otherwise `ERR_SEEDING_EMPTY_SNAPSHOT` (409) — and a forfeit's `forfeiting_school_id` names one of the contest's two schools — otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_IN_CONTEST` (409).
28. **One case per athlete per season.** No eligibility case already exists for the same `student_id` and `sport_season_id` pair — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELIGIBILITY_CASE` (409). Applies to eligibility-case creation.
29. **Capacity ceilings.** The bracket holds at least four seeded schools before the playoffs open (`ERR_BRACKET_TOO_SMALL`, 409); the computed release minutes do not exceed the school's `max_release_minutes_per_request` (`ERR_RELEASE_TIME_EXCEEDED`, 409); the fleet admits the trip (`ERR_FLEET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409). These apply to season advancement, transport-request creation, and transport approval respectively.
30. **Fee settled.** The case's `fee_outstanding_cents` is zero — otherwise `ERR_FEE_OUTSTANDING` (409). Applies to case activation.
31. **Payment amount.** `amount_cents` is strictly above zero and no greater than the case's `fee_outstanding_cents` — otherwise `ERR_FEE_OVERPAYMENT` (HTTP 422). Applies to fee payments.
32. **Refundable balance.** `fee_paid_cents` minus `fee_refunded_cents` is strictly above zero — otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REFUND` (HTTP 422). Applies to fee refunds.

A practical consequence worth stating: on case activation, a case that is not `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` reports `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVATABLE` before the sanction, coach-credential, medical-hold and fee gates are ever consulted, and among those four, sanction is reported first, then the coaching gap, then the medical hold, then the outstanding fee. On eligibility evaluation, the season posture is reported before the segregation-of-duties bar, which is reported before the missing academic record.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Calendar and Contest Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the parent legitimately accepts many children and a second writer working from a stale view would otherwise silently succeed.

Creating a contest requires `expected_sport_season_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SportSeason`. If that value does not equal the season's current `version` — because another writer has since added a contest or advanced the calendar — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is persisted. The client is expected to re-read the season, observe the new `scheduled_contest_count` and `status`, and decide again.

Offering a game to an official requires `expected_contest_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Contest`. If that value does not equal the contest's current `version` — because another offer has been minted or an acceptance has moved `accepted_official_count` — the offer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTEST_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.8. Arithmetic Discipline: Cents, Percentages, and Ratings

Money is held exclusively in whole cents as integers; there are no fractional-cent balances anywhere in the service. Wherever a monetary figure is produced by multiplying by a rate, discount, or multiplier, the product is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** — a value ending in exactly one half rounds away from zero. Where a monetary calculation produces several addends, each addend is rounded to whole cents independently before they are summed; the sum itself is never re-rounded.

Grade point averages are carried to three decimal places, round half up, and the comparison that decides the academic rung uses the rounded value rather than an unrounded intermediate. Win percentages, opponent averages, and power ratings are carried to four decimal places, round half up, and every comparison in the bracket's tie-break chain reads the rounded stored values, never an unrounded intermediate.

### 2.9. Conserved Ledgers and Compensating Actions

Two quantities on the `School` row are ledgers rather than free-standing counters, and each has a forward action and an exact compensating action.

`release_minutes_used` must at all times equal the sum of `release_minutes` over that school's transport requests whose status is `APPROVED` or `COMPLETED`. Approving a trip adds that trip's `release_minutes`; cancelling a previously approved trip subtracts exactly the same figure, as do all three cascade paths that cancel approved trips. Completing a trip changes nothing, because a completed trip remains inside the conserved sum. After any approve-then-cancel pair on the same trip, the school's `release_minutes_used` returns to precisely its pre-approval value.

`fee_collected_cents` must at all times equal the sum, over every eligibility case belonging to a student of that school, of `fee_paid_cents` minus `fee_refunded_cents`. Recording a payment adds the paid cents; refunding subtracts exactly what was refunded. A waiver never touches `fee_collected_cents` — waived money was never collected. At the case level, `fee_outstanding_cents` must at all times equal `fee_amount_cents` less `fee_paid_cents`, less `fee_waived_cents`, plus `fee_refunded_cents`. After a pay-then-refund pair, the school's `fee_collected_cents` returns to its pre-payment value and the case's `fee_outstanding_cents` returns to `fee_amount_cents` less `fee_waived_cents`.

### 2.10. Derived Postures and Cascade Discipline

Three fields on `School` are never written directly by a client and are always recomputed from the rows beneath them: `sanction_status`, derived from `forfeit_count`; `credential_gap_sports`, the list of sports for which the school currently holds no cleared head-coach credential; and the two ledgers above. `forfeit_count` itself is the count of contests recording that school as `forfeiting_school_id`.

Two named cascades run through the system, and both are all-or-nothing within a single request. **Cascade A** begins when a cleared coaching credential is revoked and runs coach gap → contest hold → transport cancellation → release-minute restitution. **Cascade B** begins when a contest is forfeited and runs forfeit tally → sanction posture → provisional-eligibility revocation. Each link fires only because the preceding link fired; §3.2 and §3.9 spell out both chains link by link. Whenever a cascade touches a parent row, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes exactly as for a direct edit.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Season Calendar and the Gates It Imposes

Everything the association does hangs off a season. An `AssociationAdmin` opens one by declaring the `sport` it contests — one of `FOOTBALL`, `BASKETBALL`, `TRACK`, or `SOCCER` — the `classification` band it serves, which is one of `C1A`, `C2A`, `C3A`, `C4A`, or `C5A`, the `school_year` label it belongs to (for example `2026-2027`), and the `sport_tier` that fixes its fee and stipend economics, one of `TIER_A`, `TIER_B`, or `TIER_C`. Six dates come with it: `practice_start_date`, `first_contest_date`, `season_end_date`, `age_cutoff_date`, `residency_deadline`, and `grading_lock_date`. A `contest_limit` of at least 1 caps how many games any one school may hold in the season, and an optional `required_officials`, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 2, sets the officiating quorum a game must reach. A newly opened season stands at `PLANNED`, with `scheduled_contest_count`, `seeded_school_count`, and `unseeded_school_count` all initialised to 0.

Calendar movement is explicit rather than implicit: the administrator names the `to_status` they intend, and each accepted move increments the season's `version` by 1. From `PLANNED` the season may move to `PRACTICE_OPEN`, but only once the logical clock's UTC date has reached or passed `practice_start_date`. From `PRACTICE_OPEN` it may move to `REGULAR_SEASON`, but only once the clock's UTC date has reached or passed `first_contest_date`. `REGULAR_SEASON → SEEDING_LOCKED` is not an ordinary advance at all — it is the outcome of the seeding run described in §3.9, and it cannot be requested as a status change. From `SEEDING_LOCKED` the season may open its bracket by moving to `PLAYOFFS`, and that move requires the drawn bracket to hold at least four schools: a `seeded_school_count` below 4 is rejected with `ERR_BRACKET_TOO_SMALL`. From `PLAYOFFS` the season may reach `COMPLETED`, but only once the clock's UTC date has reached or passed `season_end_date`. A season in `REGULAR_SEASON` may also be written off entirely by moving to `ABANDONED`, which carries no precondition at all. `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal; no further movement is possible from either. Any requested move that is not one of the transitions just described — including a premature move whose date floor has not yet been reached on the logical clock, and including a move a season requests to the status it already holds — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a `to_status` that is not one of the seven declared season statuses at all is a body-shape failure and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead.

The calendar is not just a label; it is a gate on three downstream families of work. Eligibility evaluation is admitted only while the governing season stands at `PRACTICE_OPEN` or `REGULAR_SEASON`. Contest confirmation and officiating offers are admitted only while it stands at `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`. In every one of those three cases, a season in the wrong posture yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. Scheduling a contest, by contrast, is not gated on the season's status but on the season's date window, which §3.7 covers.

### 3.2. Member Schools, Coaching Credentials, and the Credential Gap

A member school enters the register with a `name`, a `classification` drawn from the same five bands the seasons use, and a `region_code` such as `R-NORTH`. Five operating parameters may be supplied and otherwise take association defaults: `school_day_end_utc_hhmm`, the 24-hour UTC wall time at which the instructional day ends, written as exactly `HH:MM` with an hour of 00 through 23 and a minute of 00 through 59 — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and defaulting to `20:30`; `bus_fleet_size`, at least 1, defaulting to 4 buses; `max_release_minutes_per_request`, at least 0, defaulting to 240 minutes — four instructional hours — as the ceiling on what a single trip may consume; `fee_cap_cents`, at least 0, defaulting to 20000 cents, which is the 200-dollar ceiling on the participation fees one student may be assessed across a single school year; and `venue_concurrent_capacity`, at least 1, defaulting to 1, being how many home contests the school's facilities can host over one instant. A fresh school begins in `GOOD_STANDING` with `forfeit_count`, `release_minutes_used`, and `fee_collected_cents` all at 0 and `credential_gap_sports` empty.

Coaching staff are registered by the school's athletic director, who supplies the employing `school_id`, the `coach_name`, the `sport` the credential covers, a `role_title` of either `HEAD_COACH` or `ASSISTANT_COACH`, and the `certification_expires_on` date on which the coaching certification lapses. Every new credential begins at `BACKGROUND_PENDING` with no `background_check_completed_on` recorded. Only a head-coach credential in the `CLEARED` state closes a coaching gap: `credential_gap_sports` lists those sports for which the school currently holds zero credentials with `role_title` `HEAD_COACH` and status `CLEARED` **and for which some head-coach credential has been filed** — the list is a materialised record, not a derivation over all four sports, so a school that has never filed a head-coach credential for a sport carries an empty list rather than that sport, and a fresh school's list is empty. Filing a pending head-coach credential for a sport the school cannot yet staff is precisely what puts that sport into the list: the sport is appended and the school's `version` increments by 1. Filing a head-coach credential for a sport the school already staffs with a `CLEARED` head coach, filing one for a sport already sitting in the list, and filing an assistant-coach credential all leave the list and the school's `version` untouched.

Adjudication is the association's business, not the school's. The administrator supplies a `result` and may attach the vendor's `background_check_reference`. A `result` of `CLEARED` on a pending credential moves it `BACKGROUND_PENDING → CLEARED`, stamps `background_check_completed_on` with the logical clock's UTC date, and — when the `role_title` is `HEAD_COACH` and that `sport` is actually sitting in the school's `credential_gap_sports` — removes it from that list and increments the school's `version` by 1; where the sport is not in the list, the school row is not written at all and its `version` does not move. Clearance is refused outright if the certification has already lapsed: when the clock's UTC date is at or after `certification_expires_on`, the answer is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_ALREADY_LAPSED`. A `renewed_certification_expires_on` supplied alongside a `CLEARED` result on a still-pending credential is honoured in the same way it is on an expired one: it overwrites `certification_expires_on` and it is the renewed date, not the originally filed one, that the lapse test reads. A `result` of `DENIED` moves the credential `BACKGROUND_PENDING → DENIED`, stamps `background_check_completed_on`, and touches nothing on the school; `DENIED` is terminal. `background_check_reference` is written only when supplied — omitting it leaves whatever was already recorded. Every other combination of current status and result — any adjudication of a `CLEARED`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED` credential, and a `DENIED` result against an `EXPIRED` one — has no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Credentials lapse on their own. Whenever an administrator moves the logical clock, the service runs a checkpoint over every credential standing at `CLEARED`: any whose `certification_expires_on` is on or before the new clock's UTC date moves `CLEARED → EXPIRED`, and where such a credential was a `HEAD_COACH` credential and the school is left holding zero `CLEARED` head-coach credentials for that sport, the sport is appended to `credential_gap_sports` and the school's `version` increments by 1. The count of credentials moved this way is what the clock response reports as `expired_credential_count`. The checkpoint stops there: links 2 through 4 of Cascade A belong to revocation alone, so a gap opened by lapse holds no contest, cancels no trip and returns no release minutes.

Re-credentialing closes the loop. An expired credential is brought back by adjudicating it again with a `result` of `CLEARED` together with a `renewed_certification_expires_on`; that date must be strictly after the logical clock's UTC date, and if it is not, the answer is again `ERR_CREDENTIAL_ALREADY_LAPSED`. On success the credential's `certification_expires_on` is overwritten with the renewed date, the credential moves `EXPIRED → CLEARED`, and — for a `HEAD_COACH` credential whose sport is sitting in `credential_gap_sports` — the sport leaves that list and the school's `version` increments by 1. The pair `CLEARED → EXPIRED → CLEARED` is therefore a genuine cycle a coach may run through many times across a career.

**Cascade A.** Revocation is the destructive path, and it is deliberately loud. An administrator may revoke a `CLEARED` credential, optionally attaching a `revocation_note`, and the effects run in four strictly caused links. *Link 1:* the credential moves `CLEARED → REVOKED`, and where the `role_title` was `HEAD_COACH` and the school is left with zero `CLEARED` head-coach credentials for that sport, the sport is appended to `credential_gap_sports` and the school's `version` increments by 1. `REVOKED` is terminal — a revoked credential can never be re-cleared. *Link 2, fired only because link 1 opened a coaching gap:* every contest whose season contests that sport, whose `home_school_id` is this school, whose status is `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose `starts_at` is still in the future relative to the logical clock, reverts to `SCHEDULED` with `hold_reason` set to `COACH_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` and `accepted_official_count` reset to 0; every officiating assignment on those contests standing at `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with its `stipend_cents` cleared back to null, and for each formerly `ACCEPTED` one, that official's `accepted_assignment_count` is decremented by 1. *Link 3, fired only because those contests left the staffed states:* every transport request whose `contest_id` names one of them and whose status is `APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_reason` `CONTEST_HOLD_CASCADE`. *Link 4, fired only because those trips were cancelled:* each trip's `requesting_school_id` has its `release_minutes_used` decremented by exactly that trip's `release_minutes`, restoring the conservation rule of §2.9.

A school's credentials can be listed on demand, filtered by `sport` and by `status`, and — unusually for this service — that listing arrives newest first, because the question a reviewer actually asks is "what is the most recent screening on file".

### 3.3. Athletes, Academic Records, and Case Intake

An athletic director enrols an athlete against their own school by supplying the `school_id`, the athlete's `full_name`, a `birth_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and a `grade_level` that must be an integer from 9 through 12 inclusive. Six further facts are optional at enrolment, and each takes the default stated with it: `previous_school_id`, the prior member school when the athlete transferred, which is null for a non-transfer; `residency_verified`, defaulting to false; `residency_verified_on`, the ISO 8601 date on which in-attendance-zone residency was verified, which is null whenever `residency_verified` is false; `transfer_date`, the ISO 8601 date the athlete enrolled at the current school after leaving the previous one, null for non-transfers; `hardship_waiver_granted`, an association-granted waiver defaulting to false; and `free_reduced_lunch`, defaulting to false. Note that `previous_school_id` points back into the school register, so the transfer relation is a school-to-school edge running through the athlete.

Academic facts are deliberately not writable at enrolment. Any `gpa_prior_period`, `gpa_current_period`, `credits_prior_period`, or `credits_current_period` value appearing in an enrolment body is ignored: a fresh athlete row carries `gpa_prior_period` null, `gpa_current_period` null, `credits_prior_period` 0, `credits_current_period` 0, and both `academic_record_submitted_by` and `academic_record_submitted_at` null.

Those four academic figures reach the athlete only through the `Registrar`, who posts the grading-period record for a student of their own school. `gpa_prior_period` is the grade point average for the most recently completed grading period and `gpa_current_period` the average for the in-progress one; both are numbers from 0.000 through 4.000 inclusive, carried to three decimal places. `credits_prior_period` and `credits_current_period` are the credit hours carried in those two periods and must each be an integer of at least 0. All four figures are mandatory on every posting; a posting overwrites all four. Posting the record stamps `academic_record_submitted_by` with the registrar's `X-User-ID` and `academic_record_submitted_at` with the logical clock instant, and increments the athlete's `version` by 1; it answers 200, not 201, because it edits an existing athlete rather than creating anything. The stamp is what makes the segregation-of-duties rule enforceable later, and it is also the sole thing that distinguishes an athlete who can be evaluated from one who cannot: evaluating a case whose athlete has a null `academic_record_submitted_at` is refused with `ERR_ACADEMIC_RECORD_MISSING`. Athletes can be listed and filtered both by their current `school_id` and by the `previous_school_id` they transferred away from, which is how a receiving school audits its own incoming transfer cohort.

**Opening a case.** An athletic director opens an eligibility case by naming the `student_id` and the `sport_season_id`. At most one case may exist for a given athlete-and-season pair; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELIGIBILITY_CASE`. A new case begins at `INTAKE` on its eligibility axis, with `ineligibility_reason`, `gpa_variant_applied`, `effective_gpa`, `provisional_revoked_at`, and `activated_at` all null, and with `appeal_count` and `evaluation_count` both 0.

**Assessing the participation fee.** Case creation is also the single moment at which the participation fee is priced, and the order of operations is pinned because two defensible readings of the same words produce different money. The starting figure is the season's tier base: 12345 cents for `TIER_A`, 8975 cents for `TIER_B`, and 6499 cents for `TIER_C`. The service then counts the athlete's **prior cases** — the athlete's other eligibility cases whose season carries the same `school_year`, whose `fee_status` is anything other than `REFUNDED`, and which were created strictly before the case being opened, taken in `created_at` ascending order. Cases in other school years and refunded cases are excluded from that count entirely. A multi-sport discount follows from that count, capped at two: no discount for a first case, a twenty-five percent discount when exactly one prior case is counted, and a forty percent discount when two or more are counted. Applying the discount to the tier base and rounding half up to whole cents produces the line item — this is the first rounding. If the athlete carries `free_reduced_lunch`, that line item is then halved and rounded half up to whole cents again — this is the second rounding, taken on the already-rounded line item, never folded into the first. Collapsing the two roundings into a single multiplication is wrong and is known to produce a different persisted figure: for a `TIER_A` season with exactly one prior case and free-or-reduced lunch, the pinned order gives 9259 cents after the first rounding and 4630 cents after the second, while the collapsed reading would give 4629 cents.

The family cap is applied last and is exact integer arithmetic that is never rounded. Headroom is the school's `fee_cap_cents` minus the sum of `fee_amount_cents` over exactly the prior cases counted above; `fee_amount_cents` for the new case is the line item or that headroom, whichever is smaller, floored at 0 so it can never be negative. `fee_outstanding_cents` is initialised to the same figure. Where the arithmetic lands on 0 — because the cap is already exhausted, or the school's cap is itself 0 — the case is initialised straight to `PAID` on its fee axis with `fee_paid_cents` 0 and `fee_outstanding_cents` 0; otherwise it is initialised to `ASSESSED`. `fee_waived_cents` and `fee_refunded_cents` both start at 0.

### 3.4. Clearance Forms and the Five-Rung Eligibility Ladder

Three paper forms stand between an athlete and the field, and all three are filed against the open case rather than against the athlete: a `PHYSICAL`, a `PARENT_CONSENT`, and a `CONCUSSION_ACK`. Either the school's athletic director or a `MedicalOfficer` may file one, supplying the `doc_type`, the `issued_on` date the form was signed, the `expires_on` date after which it no longer clears, and the `signer_name` of the physician or guardian who signed it. A form may be filed against a case standing in any eligibility status and in any fee status — nothing about the case's posture gates the filing — and no relation is required between `issued_on` and `expires_on`, so a form may be filed already expired. A filed form is immutable — it records its parent case as `eligibility_case_id` and carries an `id` and a `created_at`, and nothing else in the way of bookkeeping. Its `status` is not stored but derived on every read: `VALID` when `expires_on` is on or after the logical clock's UTC date, and `EXPIRED` otherwise. Listing a case's forms, optionally narrowed by `doc_type`, therefore reports freshness as of the moment of the read, and the count of forms that read `EXPIRED` under a newly set clock is what the clock response returns as `expired_document_count`.

**The ladder.** An `EligibilityReviewer` rules on a case by running an evaluation, and the ruling is the output of a five-rung ladder with strict precedence. The rungs are evaluated in exactly this order — age, then residency, then transfer, then documents, then academics — and evaluation **stops at the first failure**. The reported `ineligibility_reason` names that first failure and nothing else, which means a case failing three rungs is indistinguishable, in what it reports, from a case failing only the earliest of the three.

*Rung one, age.* The athlete's whole completed years of age are measured from `birth_date` to the season's `age_cutoff_date`, truncating toward zero, so an athlete born on the cutoff date's month and day is exactly that many years old on that date. An athlete who is 19 or older on the cutoff fails with `AGE_LIMIT`; 18 and under passes.

*Rung two, residency.* The rung fails with `RESIDENCY_UNVERIFIED` in any of three situations: `residency_verified` is false, or `residency_verified_on` is null, or `residency_verified_on` falls strictly after the season's `residency_deadline`. Verification on the deadline itself is in time.

*Rung three, transfer.* Non-transfers skip this rung entirely — if `previous_school_id` is null there is nothing to serve. So do transfers holding a `hardship_waiver_granted` of true. For every other transfer, the sit-out is measured as the number of whole calendar days between `transfer_date` and the season's `first_contest_date`, computed over UTC dates counting the start date and not counting the end date; a transfer carrying no `transfer_date` at all has served nothing and fails the rung outright with `TRANSFER_SITOUT`. Fewer than 365 such days and the rung fails with `TRANSFER_SITOUT`; 365 or more clears it.

*Rung four, documents.* Each of the three form types is examined in turn, and the case must hold, for every one of them, at least one filed form whose `expires_on` is on or after the logical clock's UTC date. Freshness here is decided purely by that date comparison — there is no stored flag to consult and no filtering on any status. If any one of the three types has no such form, the rung fails with `DOCS_INCOMPLETE`.

*Rung five, academics.* The athlete's effective grade point average must be at least 2.000. Below that, the rung fails with `ACADEMIC_GPA`.

If no rung fails, `ineligibility_reason` is set to null.

**Which grade point average.** The academic figure the last rung compares is itself computed on every evaluation, before the rung is reached, and it comes in two variants selected by the logical clock against the season's `grading_lock_date`. While the clock's UTC date is strictly before `grading_lock_date`, the `PROVISIONAL` variant applies: `effective_gpa` is `gpa_prior_period` rounded half up to three decimal places, and the in-progress period's grades and credits are ignored entirely even when both are populated. Once the clock's UTC date reaches or passes `grading_lock_date`, the `FINAL` variant applies: the denominator is `credits_prior_period` plus `credits_current_period`; if that denominator is zero, `effective_gpa` is 0.000; otherwise the numerator is `gpa_prior_period` multiplied by `credits_prior_period` plus `gpa_current_period` multiplied by `credits_current_period`, and `effective_gpa` is that numerator divided by that denominator, rounded half up to three decimal places. The chosen variant is recorded on the case as `gpa_variant_applied`, taking the value `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`. Because the variant switches on a date rather than on data, a strong prior period can be pulled below the 2.000 floor by a weaker, higher-credit current period, and the very same athlete row flips from clearing the academic rung to failing it with no change to any stored fact. The inputs to this computation are exactly those four academic fields as last posted by the registrar; an athlete who has never had a record posted cannot be evaluated at all, so the fields are never read while unset.

**What an evaluation writes.** An evaluation may be run on a case standing at `INTAKE`, at `UNDER_REVIEW`, or at `UNDER_APPEAL`; from any other eligibility status the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Three conditions guard it in every case: the governing season must stand at `PRACTICE_OPEN` or `REGULAR_SEASON` or the answer is `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`; the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the athlete's `academic_record_submitted_by` or the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and `academic_record_submitted_at` must not be null or the answer is `ERR_ACADEMIC_RECORD_MISSING`. Where a clean ladder run returns no failing rung, the case moves to `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` and `ineligibility_reason` is set to null. Where the ladder returns a failing rung, the case moves to `INELIGIBLE` and `ineligibility_reason` records that first failing rung, which is one of `AGE_LIMIT`, `RESIDENCY_UNVERIFIED`, `TRANSFER_SITOUT`, `DOCS_INCOMPLETE`, or `ACADEMIC_GPA`. Both outcomes are symmetric in everything else they write: both set `gpa_variant_applied` and `effective_gpa` from the variant computation — including on rungs that fail before academics is ever reached — and both increment `evaluation_count` by 1. The three starting states behave identically: `INTAKE → PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE`, `UNDER_REVIEW → PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE`, and `UNDER_APPEAL → PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` carry the same guards and the same writes, as do `INTAKE → INELIGIBLE`, `UNDER_REVIEW → INELIGIBLE`, and `UNDER_APPEAL → INELIGIBLE`.

Note what the ladder does **not** read. Contests, fees, coach credentials and injuries are not inputs to it. The coaching-credential gate and the fee gate live on activation, which §3.6 covers, and they are deliberately kept off the ladder so that an athlete's own record is judged separately from their school's readiness to field them.

### 3.5. The Fee Axis: Payment, Waiver, and Refund

An eligibility case carries two state dimensions on one row, and they are genuinely independent. The eligibility axis is `status`; the money axis is `fee_status`, whose values are `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID`, `WAIVER_REQUESTED`, `WAIVED`, `WAIVER_DENIED`, and `REFUNDED`. Neither dimension's transitions read the other's current value, with exactly one exception: the promotion of a case from `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` to `ELIGIBLE` reads the money side, and §3.6 describes how. Money movement never changes `status`, and an eligibility ruling never changes `fee_status`.

Payments are recorded by the school's athletic director against the case, one `amount_cents` at a time. The amount is a whole number of cents — a non-integer `amount_cents` is a body-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before any money state is consulted — and it must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than the case's current `fee_outstanding_cents`; anything outside that half-open range — zero, negative, or more than is owed — is refused with `ERR_FEE_OVERPAYMENT` and HTTP 422, which is the only 422 in the fee flow besides refunds. A payment that is strictly less than the outstanding balance moves the money axis `ASSESSED → PARTIALLY_PAID`. A payment that exactly equals the outstanding balance settles the case: from `ASSESSED` it moves straight to `PAID`, from `PARTIALLY_PAID` it moves to `PAID`, and from `WAIVER_DENIED` — a school that asked for relief, was told no, and then simply paid — it also moves to `PAID`. Every one of those four payment edges does the same three things: it increases `fee_paid_cents` by the amount received, recomputes `fee_outstanding_cents` (which lands at exactly 0 on the three settling edges), and increases the school's `fee_collected_cents` by the same amount received. A payment attempted from any other money state has no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

There is a fifth way to reach `PAID`, and it is not a payment at all: a case whose assessed `fee_amount_cents` came out at 0 is initialised directly to `PAID` at creation, with `fee_paid_cents` 0 and `fee_outstanding_cents` 0. This matters more than it looks, as the refund rule below shows.

Hardship relief runs on its own branch. The athletic director requests a waiver of the outstanding balance by supplying a `waiver_reason`; this is admitted from `ASSESSED` and from `PARTIALLY_PAID`, in both cases only while `fee_outstanding_cents` is above 0 — which is always true from `ASSESSED`, since a zero-fee case never sits there. Either way the money axis moves to `WAIVER_REQUESTED`. The association then rules on the request with a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`. A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves `WAIVER_REQUESTED → WAIVED`: `fee_waived_cents` is set to whatever `fee_outstanding_cents` stood at in that moment, `fee_outstanding_cents` drops to 0, and the school's `fee_collected_cents` is left completely untouched, because waived money was never collected. A `decision` of `DENY` moves `WAIVER_REQUESTED → WAIVER_DENIED` and changes no figure at all, leaving the balance owed exactly as it was.

Refunds are the compensating action for payment and are the association's call, taken with a `refund_reason`. A refund is declared only from `PAID`, and it returns everything collected: `fee_refunded_cents` is set equal to `fee_paid_cents`, `fee_outstanding_cents` is recomputed as `fee_amount_cents` less `fee_paid_cents`, less `fee_waived_cents`, plus `fee_refunded_cents`, the school's `fee_collected_cents` is decremented by the refunded cents, and the money axis moves `PAID → REFUNDED`. One fiscal guard sits in front of all of that: `fee_paid_cents` minus `fee_refunded_cents` must be strictly above 0, and if it is not, the answer is `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REFUND` with HTTP 422. A zero-fee case is precisely the interesting case here — it sits at `PAID` with nothing ever collected, so it passes the state gate and is turned away by the fiscal guard rather than by a state error.

### 3.6. Appeal, Activation, and the Medical Hold

A school that disagrees with an ineligibility ruling appeals it. The athletic director may attach an `appeal_note`, and the case moves `INELIGIBLE → UNDER_APPEAL` with `appeal_count` incremented by 1. Appeals are capped at two per case: an appeal attempted on a case whose `appeal_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. Because a re-evaluation from `UNDER_APPEAL` may itself land on `INELIGIBLE`, the pair `INELIGIBLE → UNDER_APPEAL → INELIGIBLE` is a real cycle, and the appeal cap is the only thing that terminates it.

Clearing the ladder is necessary but not sufficient. A `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` case becomes fully `ELIGIBLE` only when an `EligibilityReviewer` activates it, and activation is where the athlete's own record meets their school's readiness. Four gates apply, and §2.6 fixes the order in which they are reported. A case that is not standing at `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` is turned away first, with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`. The athlete's school must not be `SUSPENDED` on its `sanction_status`, or the answer is `ERR_SCHOOL_SANCTIONED`. The school must hold at least one coaching credential whose `sport` equals the season's `sport`, whose `role_title` is `HEAD_COACH`, whose status is `CLEARED`, and whose `certification_expires_on` falls strictly after the logical clock's UTC date; if it holds none, the answer is `ERR_COACH_NOT_CREDENTIALED`. The athlete must not be under a live medical hold — no injury report of theirs may stand at `REPORTED` or `RTP_IN_PROGRESS` — or the answer is `ERR_ATHLETE_MEDICALLY_SUSPENDED`. Finally the money must be settled: `fee_outstanding_cents` must be exactly 0, or the answer is `ERR_FEE_OUTSTANDING`. When all four clear, the case moves `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE → ELIGIBLE` and `activated_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant.

Activation is not permanent. Two forces can take it away again. The first is a school sanction: when a forfeit pushes the athlete's school from `GOOD_STANDING` into `PROBATION`, every case of that school's students standing at `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` is knocked back to `UNDER_REVIEW` with `provisional_revoked_at` stamped, which is described as link three of Cascade B in §3.9. The second is injury.

**Filing an injury.** A `MedicalOfficer` files a report naming the injured `student_id`, an `injury_type` of `CONCUSSION`, `FRACTURE`, `SPRAIN`, or `OTHER`, optionally the `contest_id` during which the injury occurred — null for a practice injury — and optionally an `injury_note`. Filing stamps `reported_at` with the logical clock instant and `reported_by_user_id` with the filing officer's `X-User-ID`, opens the report at `REPORTED` with `rtp_stage` 0 and `recurrence_count` 0, and immediately moves every one of that athlete's cases standing at `ELIGIBLE` to `MEDICALLY_SUSPENDED`.

**The graduated protocol.** Return to play is a five-stage ladder walked one step at a time, and the intervals are measured on the logical clock. Every advance carries an optional `symptom_recurrence` flag, defaulting to false, and an optional `stage_note`. The first advance, taken with `symptom_recurrence` false, moves the report `REPORTED → RTP_IN_PROGRESS`, sets `rtp_stage` to 1 and stamps `last_stage_advanced_at`, and requires that the clock has reached or passed 48 hours — two full days of initial rest — after `reported_at`; earlier than that and the answer is `ERR_RTP_INTERVAL_NOT_ELAPSED`. Each subsequent advance with `symptom_recurrence` false keeps the report at `RTP_IN_PROGRESS`, increments `rtp_stage` by 1 and re-stamps `last_stage_advanced_at`, and requires two things: `rtp_stage` must be strictly below 5, or the answer is `ERR_RTP_STAGE_CEILING`, and the clock must have reached or passed 24 hours after `last_stage_advanced_at`, or the answer is again `ERR_RTP_INTERVAL_NOT_ELAPSED`. Each of these interval comparisons admits the boundary exactly: an advance at precisely `reported_at` plus 48 hours, or at precisely `last_stage_advanced_at` plus 24 hours, is accepted, while one second earlier is not. A recurrence-flagged advance from `REPORTED` has no declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Symptom recurrence resets the ladder without any waiting period at all. An advance from `RTP_IN_PROGRESS` with `symptom_recurrence` true requires no elapsed interval whatsoever: `rtp_stage` snaps back to 1, `recurrence_count` increments by 1, and `last_stage_advanced_at` is re-stamped, so the 24-hour inter-stage ladder restarts from there. What does *not* restart is the total-duration floor, which is still measured from the original `reported_at`.

**Clearance.** Closing a report requires a `disposition` and permits a `clearance_note`, and it is barred to the officer who filed it: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the report's `reported_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on both dispositions. A `disposition` of `RETURN_TO_PLAY` is declared only from `RTP_IN_PROGRESS` and demands that `rtp_stage` has reached exactly 5 — anything lower, including a report still at `REPORTED` with stage 0, is refused with `ERR_RTP_STAGE_INCOMPLETE` — and that the logical clock has reached or passed 144 hours, six full days, after `reported_at`, failing which the answer is `ERR_RTP_INTERVAL_NOT_ELAPSED`. On success the report moves to `CLEARED`, `cleared_by_user_id` records the clearing officer and `cleared_at` the clock instant, and every case of that athlete standing at `MEDICALLY_SUSPENDED` returns to `ELIGIBLE` — but only when no other injury report of that same athlete is still standing at `REPORTED` or `RTP_IN_PROGRESS`. An athlete with two live injuries stays suspended until the second is closed too. A `disposition` of `SEASON_ENDED` is declared from both `REPORTED` and `RTP_IN_PROGRESS`, carries no stage or interval requirement whatsoever, moves the report to `SEASON_ENDED`, stamps `cleared_by_user_id` and `cleared_at` identically, and deliberately leaves every `MEDICALLY_SUSPENDED` case of the athlete exactly where it is. `CLEARED` and `SEASON_ENDED` are both terminal.

### 3.7. Scheduling Contests and Staffing Them with Officials

A contest joins two member schools over one season, so scheduling is where the calendar, the venue, and the schools' workloads all have to agree at once. Either the home school's athletic director or an `AssociationAdmin` may schedule one, naming the `sport_season_id` the game belongs to, the `home_school_id` that owns the venue and pays the home side's release time, the `away_school_id` visiting, the `starts_at` and `ends_at` instants in ISO 8601 UTC, the `travel_miles` the assigned officials will cover one way (which must be at least 0 and is what drives their mileage money), and optionally `is_playoff`, which defaults to false. `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at`. The `expected_sport_season_version` field described in §2.7 is mandatory. A newly scheduled contest opens at `SCHEDULED` with `accepted_official_count` 0, `home_score` and `away_score` null, `forfeiting_school_id` null, `hold_reason` null, and `result_recorded_at` null; the season's `scheduled_contest_count` increments by 1 and its `version` increments by 1.

Four substantive screens stand between the request and the row, and §2.6 fixes the order. **The season window** comes first: the UTC calendar date of `starts_at` must fall on or after the season's `first_contest_date` and on or before its `season_end_date`, or the answer is `ERR_CONTEST_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`.

**The venue turnover check** comes second and has a geometry all its own — the buffer is asymmetric, extended forward only, and applied to the game that is already on the books rather than to the one being added. The service looks at every contest already scheduled at the same home venue, in any status and across every season, and counts a contest as occupying the venue when the new game's `starts_at` falls strictly before that booked game's `ends_at` plus one hour of turnover, and the booked game's `starts_at` falls strictly before the new game's `ends_at`. If the number of such occupants plus the new game would exceed the home school's `venue_concurrent_capacity`, the answer is `ERR_VENUE_TURNOVER_CONFLICT`. A venue with a capacity of 2 therefore admits a second simultaneous contest and rejects the third. Note the shape this produces, and note that it is not symmetric. There is no leading turnover in front of a booked game: a new game that ends at or before the booked game's `starts_at` is admitted however tightly it lands against it — ending exactly when the booked game begins, or five minutes before it, or anywhere else inside the hour that precedes it, all clear the check, because the hour of turnover is added only to the booked game's `ends_at` and never subtracted from its `starts_at`. The rejection runs the other way only: a new game whose `starts_at` falls anywhere inside the booked game's 60-minute trailing turnover — that is, strictly before `ends_at` plus one hour — is counted as an occupant.

**Opponent sanity** comes third: `home_school_id` must differ from `away_school_id`, or the answer is `ERR_SAME_SCHOOL_CONTEST`, and both schools' `classification` must equal the season's `classification`, or the answer is `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH`. Only schools in the same enrolment band may meet.

**The load check** comes last and is run twice, first for the home school and then for the away school, with both of its tests applied to a school before the next school is considered. For the school under test, the service gathers every contest of this same season naming that school on either side — in any status at all, since a contest has no cancelled state and played and forfeited games still consume capacity. If adding this game would push that count strictly above the season's `contest_limit`, the answer is `ERR_SEASON_CONTEST_LIMIT`; a school sitting at exactly one below its limit may still be scheduled. Then, among those same games, the service counts how many start inside a rolling window running from seven days before the new game's `starts_at` up to seven days after it, where the lower bound is included and the upper bound is not — a game starting exactly seven days before is inside the window, one starting exactly seven days after is outside it. If three or more already sit in that window, the answer is `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_DENSITY`.

**Officials.** The association registers each certified official with a `full_name`, a `certification_level` of `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2`, or `LEVEL_3`, and a `home_region_code`; their `accepted_assignment_count` starts at 0 and thereafter tracks how many assignments they currently hold in the accepted state. An `OfficialsCoordinator` offers a game to an official by naming the `official_id`, together with the mandatory `expected_contest_version` of §2.7. Offers are admitted only while the governing season stands at `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`, and otherwise draw `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. Nothing gates an offer on the contest's own status: a game already `CONFIRMED`, `PLAYED` or `FORFEITED` still accepts a fresh offer, because the staffability screen of §2.6 belongs to the official's response rather than to the offer.

Before the assignment row is minted, the official's calendar is checked, and this geometry is deliberately unlike the venue's. Each contest an official is committed to is widened by a ninety-minute travel buffer on **both** ends, and the resulting window is half-open — the lower bound counts, the upper bound does not. The official's commitments are every assignment of theirs standing at `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`, across every season and including any they already hold on this very contest — so a second offer of the same game to an official whose first offer is still `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` collides with itself and draws `ERR_OFFICIAL_DOUBLE_BOOKED`; assignments that have been `DECLINED` or `WITHDRAWN` free the time back up. Two buffered windows collide only when they overlap with positive measure, meaning each one's start falls strictly before the other's end. A collision is refused with `ERR_OFFICIAL_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Because the comparison is strict on both sides, a second contest whose buffered window begins exactly when the first buffered window ends is admitted — the opposite outcome from the touching case in the bus-fleet rule of §3.8. An accepted offer opens at `OFFERED` with `stipend_cents` and `responded_at` both null, and the parent contest's `version` increments by 1.

The official themselves answers, and only they may: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the assignment's `official_id`. The `response` is either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`. An assignment that has already been answered — anything other than `OFFERED` — draws `ERR_ALREADY_RESPONDED`, and an assignment whose contest has moved out of `SCHEDULED` or `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` draws `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_STAFFABLE`. A `DECLINE` moves the assignment `OFFERED → DECLINED` and stamps `responded_at` with the logical clock instant, leaving `stipend_cents` null and leaving the contest row entirely untouched — a decline writes nothing to the contest and does not move its `version`. An `ACCEPT` moves it `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_at`, locks the travel stipend into `stipend_cents`, increments the contest's `accepted_official_count` by 1 and the official's `accepted_assignment_count` by 1, and — when that count reaches the season's `required_officials` — promotes the contest `SCHEDULED → OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` and clears its `hold_reason` back to null.

**What the stipend is worth.** The stipend has four addends, each rounded to whole cents on its own before they are added together. The certification base is 4000 cents for a `LEVEL_1` official, 6000 for `LEVEL_2`, and 8500 for `LEVEL_3`. That base is multiplied by the season's tier multiplier — 1.25 for `TIER_A`, 1.00 for `TIER_B`, 0.85 for `TIER_C` — and the product rounded half up to whole cents; this rounding happens **before** anything else is added. The mileage addend takes the contest's `travel_miles` capped at 120 miles, multiplies the capped figure by 62 cents per mile, and rounds half up. A night addend of a flat 1500 cents applies when the UTC hour of the contest's `starts_at` is 23 or later, and is 0 otherwise. A playoff addend applies only when `is_playoff` is true, and it is the larger of 2500 cents and twenty percent of the certification base rounded half up; since twenty percent of the three declared bases comes to 800, 1200, and 1700 cents respectively, every certification level in practice takes the 2500-cent floor — the floor, not the percentage, is what makes this branch non-proportional. When `is_playoff` is false the playoff addend is 0. The stipend is the sum of those four rounded addends, and it is fixed at the moment of acceptance rather than recomputed later.

**Confirmation.** Once the officiating quorum is met, the home school's athletic director confirms the game. Confirmation is declared from `SCHEDULED` as well as from `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED`, and that is intentional: it means an understaffed contest reaches the substantive quorum check rather than being turned away by a generic state error. Three conditions apply. The governing season must stand at `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`, or the answer is `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. The home school's `sanction_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the answer is `ERR_SCHOOL_SANCTIONED`. And `accepted_official_count` must have reached the season's `required_officials`, or the answer is `ERR_OFFICIALS_INCOMPLETE`. On success the contest moves to `CONFIRMED` and its `hold_reason` is cleared to null.

Staffing can also run backwards. When a withdrawal drops a contest's `accepted_official_count` below the season's `required_officials`, the contest falls `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED → SCHEDULED` and its `hold_reason` is set to `COACH_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` — the cycle `SCHEDULED → OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED → SCHEDULED` that Cascade A produces. Contests can be listed and filtered by `sport_season_id`, by `home_school_id`, by `away_school_id`, by `forfeiting_school_id`, and by `status`; officiating assignments can be listed and filtered by `contest_id`, by `official_id`, and by `status`.

### 3.8. Transportation and Instructional Release Time

Getting a team to a game costs a school two scarce things: buses, and classroom minutes. The service prices both. An athletic director requests transport for their own school by naming the `contest_id` the trip serves, the `requesting_school_id` whose fleet and instructional minutes are consumed, the one-way `travel_minutes` (at least 0), and optionally `bus_count`, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 1 bus.

Three figures are computed at that moment. `depart_at` is the contest's `starts_at` moved earlier by the travel time plus a 75-minute pre-game buffer. `return_at` is the contest's `ends_at` moved later by the travel time plus a 30-minute post-game buffer. `release_minutes` — the instructional time actually lost — is the number of whole minutes from `depart_at` until the end of the school's instructional day, where that end is the instant on `depart_at`'s own UTC calendar date at the requesting school's `school_day_end_utc_hhmm`. The minute count truncates toward zero and is floored at 0, so a bus leaving at or after the end of the school day costs exactly 0 release minutes rather than a negative figure — and an after-hours trip like that therefore leaves the school's `release_minutes_used` untouched both when it is approved and when it is later cancelled.

Two rejections guard the request, and per §2.6 the temporal one is reported first: a computed `depart_at` that falls at or before the logical clock is refused with `ERR_TRANSPORT_DEPARTURE_IN_PAST`, and a computed `release_minutes` strictly greater than the school's `max_release_minutes_per_request` is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_TIME_EXCEEDED`. Nothing else is read here — no other trip is consulted, because fleet capacity is a question for approval, not for request. A surviving request opens at `REQUESTED` with `cancelled_reason` null, and the school's `release_minutes_used` is deliberately left unchanged: an unapproved trip commits nothing.

Approval belongs to the association and is declared only from `REQUESTED`; a trip already `APPROVED`, `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` is turned away with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` before the contest is even looked at. The parent contest must be standing at `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED`, or the answer is `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_SCHEDULED_FOR_TRANSPORT` — a trip to a game that has not yet been staffed, or one that has already been played, is not approvable. Then the fleet is checked, and here the interval geometry is **closed on both ends**, so two trips that merely touch do collide. The service gathers the requesting school's trips standing at `APPROVED` or `COMPLETED` — trips at `REQUESTED` and `CANCELLED` hold no buses — and keeps those that overlap this trip's window, where two trips fail to overlap only when one's `return_at` falls strictly before the other's `depart_at` or one's `depart_at` falls strictly after the other's `return_at`. If the total `bus_count` across the overlapping trips plus this trip's own `bus_count` would exceed the school's `bus_fleet_size`, the answer is `ERR_FLEET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the comparison is strictly greater-than, so a school may commit exactly its whole fleet over a single instant. On approval the trip moves `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and the school's `release_minutes_used` increases by exactly that trip's `release_minutes`.

Cancellation is the compensating action and belongs to the requesting school, whose director may attach a `cancel_note`. A trip cancelled from `REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_reason` `SCHOOL_REQUEST` and leaves `release_minutes_used` alone, because that trip never contributed to it. A trip cancelled from `APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with the same `SCHOOL_REQUEST` reason and decrements the school's `release_minutes_used` by exactly that trip's `release_minutes`, so an approve-then-cancel pair leaves the ledger precisely where it started.

Two other paths close a trip out. When the parent contest's result is recorded, every trip on it standing at `APPROVED` moves to `COMPLETED`, and `release_minutes_used` is left unchanged — a completed trip stays inside the conserved sum. When a contest is held, postponed, or forfeited, the cascades of §3.2 and §3.9 cancel its trips with `cancelled_reason` `CONTEST_HOLD_CASCADE`; a trip cancelled that way from `APPROVED` returns its release minutes to the school, while one cancelled from `REQUESTED` has nothing to return. `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are both terminal. Trips can be listed and filtered by `contest_id`, by `requesting_school_id`, and by `status`.

### 3.9. Closing Out a Season: Results, Postponements, Forfeits, and the Bracket

**Recording a result.** The home school's athletic director closes a `CONFIRMED` contest by posting `home_score` and `away_score`, each a mandatory integer of at least 0; equal scores are accepted and record a drawn contest. The logical clock must have reached or passed the contest's `starts_at`; a score posted before then is refused with `ERR_RESULT_BEFORE_TIPOFF`. On success the two scores are written, `result_recorded_at` is stamped with the clock instant, the contest moves `CONFIRMED → PLAYED`, and every trip on it standing at `APPROVED` moves to `COMPLETED` with the school's `release_minutes_used` untouched. `PLAYED` is terminal.

**Postponing.** The association may move a `CONFIRMED` contest to a new date, supplying `new_starts_at`, `new_ends_at`, and optionally a `postpone_note`. The `new_starts_at` must be strictly after the logical clock, or the answer is `ERR_CONTEST_TIME_IN_PAST`. A postponement rewrites the contest's `starts_at` and `ends_at` from the two supplied instants, drops it `CONFIRMED → SCHEDULED` with `hold_reason` set to `ADMIN_POSTPONE` and `accepted_official_count` reset to 0, moves every assignment on it standing at `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN` while decrementing the relevant official's `accepted_assignment_count` by 1 for each formerly accepted one, and cancels every trip on it standing at `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` with `cancelled_reason` `CONTEST_HOLD_CASCADE`, returning each formerly approved trip's `release_minutes` to its school's `release_minutes_used`. The whole slate of officials and buses is therefore rebuilt from scratch after a postponement.

**Cascade B.** A forfeit is charged by the association against a contest standing at `SCHEDULED`, `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED`, or `CONFIRMED` — all three edges behave identically — by naming the `forfeiting_school_id` and optionally a `forfeit_note`. That school must be one of the contest's two, or the answer is `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_IN_CONTEST`. *Link 1:* the contest moves to `FORFEITED`, `forfeiting_school_id` is recorded, the non-forfeiting side is credited a score of 1 and the forfeiting side a score of 0, `result_recorded_at` is stamped with the clock instant, and the forfeiting school's `forfeit_count` increments by 1. *Link 2, fired only because link 1 moved the tally:* the school's sanction posture is re-derived — a `forfeit_count` that has reached 2 while still below 4 moves the school `GOOD_STANDING → PROBATION`, and a `forfeit_count` that has reached 4 moves it `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`. *Link 3, fired only because link 2 put the school on probation:* every eligibility case whose athlete belongs to that school and which is standing at `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE` is knocked back `PROVISIONAL_ELIGIBLE → UNDER_REVIEW` with `provisional_revoked_at` stamped at the clock instant. It is the `GOOD_STANDING → PROBATION` edge alone that fires this link: a forfeit that leaves an already-`PROBATION` school on probation, and the later forfeit that carries it `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, revoke nothing and stamp nothing. Independently of those three links, every trip on the forfeited contest standing at `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` is cancelled with `cancelled_reason` `CONTEST_HOLD_CASCADE`, and each formerly approved trip returns its `release_minutes` to its school. `FORFEITED` is terminal for the contest, and `SUSPENDED` is terminal for the school: from then on, every confirmation naming that school as the home side and every activation of one of its athletes' cases is refused with `ERR_SCHOOL_SANCTIONED`.

**Seeding the bracket.** Once every contest of a season has reached `PLAYED` or `FORFEITED`, the association draws the bracket, and this is the only way a season reaches `SEEDING_LOCKED`. The run is declared only from `REGULAR_SEASON`; requested against a season in any other status it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, reported ahead of both of the guards that follow. Two guards apply: if any contest of the season is still at `SCHEDULED`, `OFFICIALS_ASSIGNED`, or `CONFIRMED`, the answer is `ERR_SEASON_HAS_OPEN_CONTESTS`, and if the season has no completed contest at all, the answer is `ERR_SEEDING_EMPTY_SNAPSHOT`.

The run has a pinned evaluation order, because the dependency looks circular — a school's rating depends on its opponents' records, which are drawn from the same body of games — and the order below is what breaks the circle. Later steps never feed earlier ones.

1. **Freeze the snapshot.** The snapshot is exactly the contests of this season standing at `PLAYED` or `FORFEITED`. Nothing outside it is ever read.
2. **Tally each school.** For every school holding at least one contest in the snapshot, count `wins` (the higher score, or the awarded side of a forfeit), `losses` (the lower score, or being the recorded `forfeiting_school_id`), and `forfeit_losses` (the subset of losses charged as forfeits). A drawn contest — equal scores — counts as neither a win nor a loss for either side, yet still puts both schools into the tally and so into the bracket; a school whose every snapshot contest was drawn therefore lands on zero wins and zero losses and takes the zero-denominator reading of step 3.
3. **Compute and freeze win percentages.** `raw_win_pct` is `wins` divided by the sum of `wins` and `losses`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0000 when that sum is zero. This table is frozen at this point and is what every later step reads.
4. **Compute strength of schedule.** `opponent_avg_win_pct` is the arithmetic mean of the frozen `raw_win_pct` values of the distinct schools this school met in the snapshot, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0000 when that opponent set is empty. Opponents whose `sanction_status` is `SUSPENDED` at seeding time are excluded from the opponent set — though the games against them still count in this school's own `wins` and `losses`. This step reads only the frozen step-3 table, never a rating computed in step 5 and never a value recomputed after any seed has been assigned.
5. **Blend.** `power_rating` is 65 percent of `raw_win_pct` plus 35 percent of `opponent_avg_win_pct`, rounded half up to four decimal places.
6. **Order and break ties.** Schools are ordered by `power_rating` descending. Where two schools are level, the following criteria are applied in order, stopping at the first that separates the pair: higher `raw_win_pct`; then head-to-head, meaning that if the two met in the snapshot, the winner of their latest meeting by `starts_at` ranks higher (for a forfeited meeting the awarded side is the winner, and where two meetings share the same `starts_at` the later-created of them is the one read), and if they never met — or if that latest meeting was itself a draw, which has no winner — this criterion separates nothing and the chain falls through to the next rung; then fewer `forfeit_losses`; then higher `opponent_avg_win_pct`; then, as the terminal rung, the `created_at` of the school row itself read **ascending** — the school whose row was created earlier ranks higher and takes the lower `seed_number`, and where two school rows carry the identical `created_at` the earlier-inserted row ranks higher. Because this last rung always separates two distinct school rows, the chain never runs out of criteria. Each seed records in `tiebreak_trace` which criterion separated it from the row immediately above it — `RAW_WIN_PCT`, `HEAD_TO_HEAD`, `FORFEIT_LOSSES`, `OPP_AVG_WIN_PCT`, or `CREATED_AT` — while a pair separated by the `power_rating` comparison itself records `POWER_RATING`. The top-ranked row records `POWER_RATING` by convention, having nothing above it.
7. **Number the seeds.** `seed_number` runs 1 upward in the resulting order.
8. **Lock the season.** The season moves `REGULAR_SEASON → SEEDING_LOCKED`.

Each seeded school gets exactly one bracket row, carrying the `sport_season_id`, the `school_id`, and all of the figures above. The season's `seeded_school_count` is set to the number of rows minted; its `unseeded_school_count` is set to the number of schools whose `classification` equals the season's and which hold zero contests in the frozen snapshot — those schools receive no bracket row at all, which is the empty-collection edge of the run. Because seeds are frozen artefacts rather than living records, they carry only an `id` and a `created_at`. Bracket rows can be read back per season, ordered by `seed_number` ascending, or per school across every season the school has been seeded in.

One nuance concerning the rating that a school sees before the bracket is drawn: while a season is still standing at `REGULAR_SEASON`, the in-season reading of a school's `power_rating` ignores strength of schedule entirely and is simply its `raw_win_pct` rounded half up to four decimal places. The blended figure is only what the seeding run writes. Two schools with identical 0.5000 raw records therefore receive different blended ratings, and the bracket order can invert what the in-season reading suggested. Recomputing opponents' win percentages after step 5 — the naive fixed-point reading — yields different ratings and a different bracket, and is wrong.
