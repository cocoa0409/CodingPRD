# Ecommerce Photo Studio Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A large retailer's in-house content studio is the narrow bridge between merchandising and the web site: nothing goes on sale until the photography exists, is colour-accurate, is retouched to standard, is named to contract, and has landed in the content management system. Today that bridge is coordinated through spreadsheets and a shared calendar. Physical samples arrive from vendors in unlabelled boxes and are reconciled to style numbers by eye; shoot days are booked on stations without anyone checking whether the station's teardown buffer or its daily minute budget still has room; models are called to set before anyone confirms that a signed usage-rights release actually covers the window and the intended usage breadth; and post-production turnaround is reported from memory, which means the studio cannot answer the only question finance actually asks — is it cheaper to shoot this style in-house or to send it to an outsourced studio?

This document specifies the Studio Operations API, a single system of record for that pipeline, mounted under `/studio`. It covers six connected concerns: vendor sample custody and style-number matching, station capacity and shoot scheduling, talent booking with usage-rights and release documentation, shot-list definition against per-category content requirements, on-set colour accuracy and consistency control, and the post-production retouch queue with its service-level measurement and CMS delivery. Every rule below is enforced server-side; the studio's operators are deliberately given no way to work around a capacity ceiling, a lapsed calibration, a missing release, or a segregation-of-duties boundary.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, the system is **temporally driven**: almost every meaningful rule — release validity, station calibration, sample return lateness, retouch turnaround, queue urgency — is evaluated against a single server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the whole pipeline can be driven deterministically. Second, the system is **ledger-honest**: denormalised counters (outstanding samples, committed station minutes, booked talent minutes, committed talent cost, delivered assets, live assets, assigned retouch load) must at every instant agree with a full recount of their source rows, and cost figures are posted once and never silently re-derived.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Users, Roles and Studio Access Control

Every request to every endpoint carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty, and surrounding whitespace is otherwise trimmed from both header values before they are used, so a recognised role name padded with spaces is accepted. The same code and status are returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven roles this system recognises. Once the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection becomes `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These three checks run before anything else, in exactly that order: header presence, then role recognition, then endpoint authorisation.

The seven roles are `StudioAdmin`, `SampleCoordinator`, `Producer`, `Photographer`, `Retoucher`, `QCReviewer` and `ContentOps`. They divide the pipeline into disjoint slices, and the division is deliberate — the studio's control environment depends on no single operator owning two adjacent stages.

A `StudioAdmin` configures the studio's reference data — vendors, per-category content profiles, capture stations, bookable talent, production providers, styles and usage-rights releases — countersigns releases, and drives the logical clock. A `SampleCoordinator` owns the physical sample pipeline end to end: pre-advice, receipt, style-number matching and vendor coordination, custody moves, returns and loss write-offs. A `Producer` plans capacity and crews the floor: booking sessions, freezing and reopening call sheets, cancelling, holding and confirming talent, and defining, assigning and waiving shot-list items. A `Photographer` is the person behind the camera and is the only role that may call a session to order, submit its colour reading, ingest captured files, or wrap it. A `Retoucher` is a post-production operator who pulls work from the shared queue and hands it to quality control. A `QCReviewer` is the quality gatekeeper who approves or rejects submitted retouch work. A `ContentOps` operator owns the post-production queue itself and CMS publication: queueing ingested assets to a provider and delivering approved assets.

Two of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not a free-form operator string but a primary key. When `X-User-Role` is `Photographer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TalentProfile`; ownership checks compare that value against the `talent_id` of the `TalentBooking` rows on the target session, and the acting photographer is admitted only if one of those bookings has `role_on_set` equal to `PHOTOGRAPHER` and `booking_state` equal to `CONFIRMED`. When `X-User-Role` is `Retoucher`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `TalentProfile`; that profile must exist and its `talent_type` must be `RETOUCHER` to claim work, and a task may be submitted only when `X-User-ID` equals the task's `assigned_retoucher_id`.

The other five roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `StudioAdmin`, `SampleCoordinator`, `Producer` or `ContentOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server accepts it, may record it, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. `QCReviewer` is also opaque in the sense that no `QCReviewer` row exists anywhere in the data model, but its identifier is not inert — it is compared, as a plain string, against the `assigned_retoucher_id` of the task under review, and a review whose `X-User-ID` equals that value is refused. That is the studio's hard segregation-of-duties gate: nobody signs off their own retouching.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`: the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits, minted freshly for every response, error responses included.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the system read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical studio clock of §2.4, so setting the clock does not move it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0, and has no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — the value is never silently clamped. The same rejection applies when a list endpoint's enum-typed filter parameter carries a value that is not a declared member of that enumeration. A boolean-typed filter parameter — the shot-list `required` flag — accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, matched case-insensitively after trimming, and a date-typed filter parameter — `scheduled_date` — accepts only a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date; anything else is the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Identifier-typed filter parameters are compared as opaque strings and are never checked for existence, so a filter naming a row that does not exist yields an empty page rather than a not-found error.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. There is no client-selectable sort. Because the logical clock makes exact `created_at` ties routine, rows sharing a `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which they were created, oldest insertion first; a row's `id` never participates in any ordering. For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective offset actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination was applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. The Logical Studio Clock

The studio runs on a server-side logical clock rather than on wall-clock time. Before any operator sets it, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `StudioAdmin` may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, which echoes back an object containing the new `current_utc` rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. The clock may be set to any instant, including one earlier than its current reading; the system never refuses a backwards move.

Every datetime the system writes as a side effect is read from that clock at the moment of the write, not from the host's system time: a sample's `received_at` and `returned_at`, a release's `signed_at`, a session's `started_at`, `wrapped_at` and `color_measured_at`, a retouch task's `claimed_at`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at` and `rework_started_at`, and an asset's `delivered_at`. Every temporal rule reads the same clock: release validity windows, station calibration expiry, sample return lateness, retouch turnaround and queue urgency, and the "today" window of the clock-dated capacity counters.

Advancing the clock has one direct side effect of its own. Usage-rights releases expire lazily: whenever the clock is set, every `ReleaseDocument` whose `doc_state` is `PENDING` or `SIGNED` and whose `valid_until` is at or before the new clock instant is moved to `EXPIRED`. The same lazy evaluation applies whenever a release is read or evaluated — a document whose `valid_until` has been reached is `EXPIRED` from that instant onward and stops covering any booking confirmation, even if no clock call has been made since. Wherever that lazy transition fires it is a real mutation of the document, including when it fires during a read: the document's `version` increments by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed to the clock instant that expired it, so the very first read after the deadline is the read that persists the change.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Immutable Content Profile

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Instants supplied by clients are parsed more liberally than they are rendered: the seconds component and the fractional part are both optional, a `T` or a single space may separate the date from the time, a trailing `Z` or a `±HH:MM` (or `±HHMM`) offset is honoured and normalised to UTC, and a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. A value that does not parse under those rules — or that names an impossible calendar date — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row suffers as the downstream consequence of somebody else's write — a vendor whose sample was received, a style whose content state was recomputed by a cascade, a session that was auto-unlocked. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions.

`AssetProfile` is the single exception. A content requirement template is immutable once registered: there is no update endpoint for it, and the resource carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an `AssetProfile` payload.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two create operations attach a child row to a parent whose surrounding context the client must have observed, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`.

A `POST /studio/talent-bookings` request — creating a `TalentBooking` — requires a non-optional `expected_session_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ShootSession`. If that value does not equal the session's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. A session legitimately accepts many bookings, so nothing about the session's state forbids a second one; the hazard the check closes is that the crew composition, the talent-minute ledger and the value of `committed_talent_cost_cents` the producer was reasoning about have already moved on since the producer last read them.

A `POST /studio/shot-list-items` request — creating a `ShotListItem` — requires a non-optional `expected_style_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Style`. If that value does not equal the style's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STYLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Again a style legitimately accumulates many shot-list items; the hazard is that `required_item_count`, `content_state` and the angle coverage the producer was working from are stale.

Both fields are declared as integers of at least 0, so a negative value — like a fractional or non-integer one — fails the request-shape check of §2.7 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than the concurrency check. No other endpoint takes a version field. Every other write is accepted or refused purely on its own state preconditions and business rules.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

Because many endpoints carry several checks that could all fire on the same request, the order of evaluation is normative — it determines which single `error_id` the caller actually sees. Checks are applied in the following strict sequence, and the first failure terminates the request.

1. **Authentication and authorisation.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` both present and non-empty, then `X-User-Role` recognised as one of the seven roles — both failures return `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Then the role must be authorised for the endpoint, failing with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
2. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint, the body must carry every required field with the declared type; enum-typed fields must hold a declared member; string-typed fields must respect the length bounds and the character set declared for them, so a `style_number`, `colorway_code`, `declared_style_number`, `vendor_code`, `provider_code`, `station_code` or `barcode` carrying a character its declaration does not allow — a hyphen inside a field declared uppercase alphanumeric, for instance — is rejected at this step rather than being stored or judged by a later rule; arrays declared non-empty must be non-empty; numeric fields must respect their declared ranges. The presence of `expected_session_version` on booking creation and of `expected_style_version` on shot-list-item creation is part of this check, not of the concurrency check that follows later. An explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field: for an optional field the declared default applies, and for a required field it fails just as leaving the field out does. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected; an absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint carrying no required field accepts it; and a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object, or that is not valid JSON at all, is rejected — except on the seven action endpoints that declare no body field whatsoever (sample receipt, session lock, unlock, start and wrap, booking confirmation, and the retouch claim), which never inspect the request body at all and therefore ignore whatever it carries, malformed or not. Type checking is strict rather than coercive: a boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is declared, an integer-declared field refuses a fractional value, and a numeric string is not a number. On every list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` must be in range and enum-typed filters must hold declared members. Any failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
3. **Existence of path identifiers**, resolved left to right along the URL. A miss returns the code belonging to the resource that failed to resolve: `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` for the configuration resources.
4. **Existence of body foreign keys.** A missing `Vendor`, `Station`, `TalentProfile`, `StudioProvider`, `AssetProfile` or `ReleaseDocument` reference all raise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `Style`, `ShootSession`, `ShotListItem` or `Asset` reference raises that entity's own not-found code. This applies to style creation, sample pre-advice, sample matching, session creation, booking creation, shot-list-item creation, shot-list-item assignment, release creation, asset ingest and retouch-task creation. Where one endpoint carries two body references that would report different codes, they are resolved in the order the narrative names them: `session_id` before `talent_id` on booking creation, and `asset_id` before `provider_id` on retouch-task creation.
5. **Actor identity gates**, in this order: the photographer session gate (`ERR_NOT_BOOKED_ON_SESSION`), the assigned-retoucher gate on submission (`ERR_TASK_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ACTOR`), the segregation-of-duties gate on quality-control decisions (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and the retoucher-type gate on claiming (`ERR_TALENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`).
6. **State preconditions**, in this order: sample custody legality (`ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY`), session must be `BOOKED` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_BOOKED`), session must be `LOCKED` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_LOCKED`), session must be `SHOOTING` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_SHOOTING`), session must not already be terminal (`ERR_SESSION_TERMINAL`), booking state legality (`ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`), shot-list-item state legality (`ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`), retouch-task state legality (`ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_INVALID_STATE`), asset state legality (`ERR_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED`), release signability (`ERR_RELEASE_INVALID_STATE`), and the one-reading-per-session rule (`ERR_COLOR_ALREADY_MEASURED`).
7. **Optimistic concurrency**: `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_STYLE_VERSION`.
8. **Business rules**, in this exact order: station calibration currency (`ERR_STATION_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`), the session's colour verdict (`ERR_COLOR_CHECK_FAILED`), cross-session colour drift (`ERR_COLOR_CONSISTENCY_DRIFT`), vendor coordination hold (`ERR_VENDOR_COORDINATION_HOLD`), style-number uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_STYLE_NUMBER`), code uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), shot-list-item uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SHOT_ITEM`), category-to-profile agreement (`ERR_CATEGORY_PROFILE_MISMATCH`), colorway parentage integrity (`ERR_INVALID_BASE_STYLE`), interval sanity (`ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`), station occupation overlap (`ERR_STATION_OVERLAP`), talent occupation overlap (`ERR_TALENT_OVERLAP`), on-set role against talent type (`ERR_TALENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`), station type against asset kind (`ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`), declared style number against the style (`ERR_STYLE_NUMBER_MISMATCH`), sample matched before custody advance (`ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_MATCHED`), sample physically present at lock (`ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_IN_STUDIO`), a signed release on file (`ERR_RELEASE_NOT_ON_FILE`), that release's scope sufficiency (`ERR_RELEASE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT`), asset naming (`ERR_ASSET_NAMING_VIOLATION`), asset metadata completeness (`ERR_ASSET_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`), no live retouch task already on the asset (`ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_EXISTS`), a non-empty required call sheet at lock (`ERR_SHOT_LIST_EMPTY`), station daily capacity (`ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), session talent budget (`ERR_SESSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`), and finally a non-empty claim pool (`ERR_NO_CLAIMABLE_TASK`).

### 2.8. Minutes, Cents, Hours and Rounding

All money in this system is expressed in whole cents as integers; there is no fractional-cent representation anywhere. Wherever a monetary figure is derived from a rate and a duration, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where a charge is composed of more than one leg — a regular-time leg and an overtime leg, or a station leg and a talent leg — each leg is rounded to whole cents **before** the legs are added; the sum of two rounded legs is normative and summing the unrounded products first is not permitted.

All durations that describe occupation are whole minutes: `planned_minutes`, `actual_minutes`, `booked_minutes`, `regular_minutes`, `overtime_minutes`, `committed_minutes_today`, `booked_minutes_today`, `estimated_minutes`, `daily_capacity_minutes`, `buffer_minutes`, `max_daily_minutes` and `minimum_call_hours` expressed in hours. A minute count derived from two instants — `planned_minutes`, `actual_minutes`, `booked_minutes` — is the elapsed seconds divided by 60 with any part-minute remainder discarded, never rounded up. All durations that describe service level are hours carried to two decimal places, rounded half up: `turnaround_hours_gross`, `turnaround_hours_net` and `rework_hours_total`. The queue's `priority_score` and the colour composite `color_composite_delta_e` are likewise carried to two decimal places, rounded half up. Two decimal places describe how these figures — and the configured `quality_score` and `color_tolerance_delta_e` — are *rendered*: where a client supplies finer precision the value is stored and compared at full precision, and only the rendering is rounded half up. Sample lateness, `return_days_late`, is a whole number of days obtained by rounding up any positive overrun.

### 2.9. Ledgers, Counters and Cascades

Seven denormalised counters exist so that operators can see load without scanning. Each has an exact definition, and an incremental update must at all times agree with a full recount from the definition:

- A vendor's `outstanding_sample_count` is the number of that vendor's samples whose `custody_state` is `RECEIVED`, `IN_STUDIO`, `ON_SET` or `RETURN_PENDING`. Samples still `EXPECTED`, already `RETURNED`, or written off as `LOST` never count as outstanding.
- A vendor's `late_return_count` rises by one each time one of its samples is returned after its `due_return_at`, and by one each time one of its samples is reported lost.
- A style's `delivered_asset_count` is the number of assets belonging to that style's shot-list items whose `asset_state` is `DELIVERED`.
- A style's `required_item_count` is the number of that style's shot-list items whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is anything other than `WAIVED`.
- A shot-list item's `live_asset_count` is the number of that item's assets whose `asset_state` is anything other than `REJECTED`.
- A provider's `assigned_asset_count` is the number of that provider's retouch tasks whose `task_state` is `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `REWORK`.
- A station's `committed_minutes_today` is the sum of `planned_minutes` over that station's sessions whose `session_state` is `BOOKED`, `LOCKED`, `SHOOTING` or `WRAPPED` and whose `scheduled_start` falls on the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock. A talent's `booked_minutes_today` is the sum of `booked_minutes` over that talent's bookings whose `booking_state` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `call_time` falls on that same clock date. A session's `committed_talent_cost_cents` is the sum of `charge_cents` over its bookings whose `booking_state` is `CONFIRMED` or `RELEASED`. Cancelled sessions and cancelled or released bookings are excluded from the two minute counters; released bookings do remain inside `committed_talent_cost_cents`. Day windows are inclusive of the day's first instant and exclusive of the next day's.

One subtlety about the clock-dated counters is load-bearing. `committed_minutes_today` is denormalised against the clock's date, but the station **capacity validation** is evaluated against the UTC date of the candidate session's own `scheduled_start`. A session may therefore be scheduled for any date at all; only the visible counter is clock-dated.

Cascades in this system are synchronous and immediate: by the time a write returns, every downstream consequence has already been applied and every affected row's `version` and `updated_at` have already moved. Two cascades run four levels deep — the write-off of a lost sample, and the delivery of the last asset a style was waiting on — and both are described in full in the workflow sections below. Where a cascade credits or debits a parent, that parent's `version` increments once per cascade, not once per touched child, except where the workflow section describing that cascade says otherwise.

### 2.10. Flat Addressing and Filtered Discovery

There is no URL nesting in this API: every resource is addressed flatly by its own identifier, and relationships are discovered by filtering list endpoints on foreign keys rather than by walking a path hierarchy. A trailing slash is accepted on any path as equivalent to the same path without it. Where a path does carry an identifier, it is validated left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error returned. The path variables in play are `vendor_id` for a vendor, `profile_id` for an asset profile, `station_id` for a station, `talent_id` for a talent profile, `provider_id` for a studio provider, `style_id` for a style, `sample_id` for a sample, `release_id` for a release document, `session_id` for a shoot session, `booking_id` for a talent booking, `item_id` for a shot-list item, `asset_id` for an asset and `task_id` for a retouch task.

Filters are exact-match and combine conjunctively. Styles may be filtered by `vendor_id`, `asset_profile_id`, `base_style_id`, `category`, `content_state` and `priority_tier`. Samples may be filtered by `vendor_id`, `matched_style_id`, `custody_state` and `match_state` — the two state axes filter independently. Releases may be filtered by `talent_id`, `doc_state` and `scope`. Sessions may be filtered by `station_id`, `provider_id`, `color_measured_by_talent_id`, `session_state` and `scheduled_date`, the last of which selects sessions whose `scheduled_start` falls on the supplied UTC calendar date. Bookings may be filtered by `session_id`, `talent_id`, `release_document_id`, `booking_state` and `role_on_set`. Shot-list items may be filtered by `style_id`, `session_id`, `item_state`, `asset_kind` and `required`. Assets may be filtered by `shot_list_item_id`, `session_id`, `captured_by_talent_id` and `asset_state`. Retouch tasks may be filtered by `asset_id`, `provider_id`, `assigned_retoucher_id`, `task_state` and `sla_state`.

The enumerations these filters accept are fixed. A merchandise `category` is one of `APPAREL_TOP`, `APPAREL_BOTTOM`, `FOOTWEAR`, `ACCESSORY` or `HOME`. An angle code is one of `FRONT`, `BACK`, `SIDE`, `FLAT`, `DETAIL` or `VIDEO_360`. An asset kind is one of `STILL_ANGLE`, `DETAIL`, `ON_MODEL` or `VIDEO`. A station type is `MODEL`, `MANNEQUIN` or `STILL_LIFE`. A talent type is `MODEL`, `STYLIST`, `PHOTOGRAPHER` or `RETOUCHER`. A provider kind is `IN_HOUSE` or `OUTSOURCED`. A usage scope is `ECOM_ONLY`, `ECOM_PLUS_SOCIAL` or `FULL_BUYOUT`, and a `territory` is `US` or `GLOBAL`. A merchandising `priority_tier` is `A`, `B` or `C`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Studio: Reference Data and Content Requirements

Before any content can be produced, a `StudioAdmin` populates five kinds of reference record. Each is registered once and each carries constraints that the rest of the pipeline then leans on.

A **vendor** is the merchandise supplier that ships physical samples. Registration takes a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters that must still be non-empty after surrounding whitespace is trimmed — the 1-to-120 bound is measured on the value exactly as supplied, and the name is stored and rendered verbatim rather than trimmed — and a `vendor_code` of between 2 and 12 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and digits, which must be unique across all vendors — a repeat raises `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Two optional values complete the record: `sample_sla_days`, the number of days after receipt by which a sample must be back with this vendor, which defaults to 14 and must be at least 1 and at most 120; and `contact_email`, the coordination mailbox used for sample chase-ups, which defaults to null and, when supplied, may be at most 320 characters and is not checked for address syntax of any kind. A newly registered vendor starts with `outstanding_sample_count` at 0, `late_return_count` at 0 and `coordination_hold` false.

An **asset profile** is the immutable per-category content contract: it declares what a style of that category owes, how its files must be named, and what metadata every file must carry. Its `category` is one of the five merchandise categories and must be unique across all asset profiles, so a second profile for the same category is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. `required_angles` is a non-empty array whose members are angle codes and which lists the angles that must exist as required shot-list items for a style of this category. `required_detail_shots` is the minimum count of `DETAIL`-kind items expected and must be at least 0; `min_on_model_shots` is the minimum count of `ON_MODEL`-kind items expected and must likewise be at least 0; `video_required` is a boolean saying whether a `VIDEO`-kind item is expected. `naming_pattern` is the file-name template and must contain the three tokens `{style_number}`, `{angle_code}` and `{seq}`; its expansion is exactly the style's style number, then an underscore, then the item's angle code, then an underscore, then the frame's sequence number written with leading zeros to a width of three digits. `required_metadata_keys` is a non-empty array of strings — none of which may itself be empty or consist only of whitespace — naming the metadata keys every ingested file of this category must carry with a non-empty string value.

A **station** is a physical capture position, and its geometry of occupation depends on what kind of position it is. `station_code` is 2 to 16 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and must be unique across stations, again guarded by `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. `station_type` is `MODEL`, `MANNEQUIN` or `STILL_LIFE`. `daily_capacity_minutes` is the number of bookable minutes per UTC calendar day on that position and must be at least 1 and at most 1440 — a full day. `buffer_minutes` is the mandatory teardown gap enforced **after** each session's scheduled end on that position; it defaults to 0 and may not exceed 240 minutes, four hours. `max_concurrent_sessions` says how many sessions may occupy overlapping intervals there; it defaults to 1 and may not exceed 8, which is what makes a still-life bench able to host several simultaneous tabletop setups while a model bay stays exclusive. `calibrated_until` is the instant at which the position's colour calibration lapses and defaults to null, meaning the position has never been calibrated. A new station starts with `committed_minutes_today` at 0.

A **talent profile** is a bookable person, and the same record type serves models, stylists, photographers and retouchers. `full_name` is 1 to 120 characters, measured as supplied and carrying no additional non-whitespace test of the kind a vendor's `name` carries, and `talent_type` is one of the four talent types. `agency_name` is optional, may be at most 200 characters, and defaults to null for freelancers. Rates are `hourly_rate_cents`, the regular hourly rate, and `day_rate_cents`, the standard-day cap, both of which must be at least 0. `minimum_call_hours` is the minimum billable hours per booking regardless of how short the actual window is; it defaults to 4 and may be anywhere from 0 to 12. `max_daily_minutes` is how many minutes per calendar date this person is billable at the regular rate before overtime applies; it defaults to 480 — an eight-hour day — and must be at least 60 and at most 1440. On registration `booked_minutes_today` is 0, and the system computes `requires_release` as true exactly when `talent_type` is `MODEL` and false for every other talent type; that flag is what drives the usage-rights gate at booking confirmation. Photographers and retouchers authenticate as this row: the `id` of the talent profile is the value those operators put in `X-User-ID`.

A **studio provider** is a production source — either the retailer's own studio or an outsourced studio and retouch house. `provider_code` is 2 to 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters, unique across providers, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` on collision. `provider_kind` is `IN_HOUSE` or `OUTSOURCED`. `station_hour_rate_cents` is the fully loaded studio floor rate per hour and must be at least 0; `retouch_rate_cents_per_asset` is the flat retouch price this provider charges per asset and must be at least 0; `sla_hours` is the base post-production turnaround service level in hours and must be at least 1; `quality_score` is the audited retouch quality score carried to two decimals, at least 0 and at most 100. `style_quote_cents` is the all-in per-style quote used in the outsourced comparison; it defaults to 0, must be at least 0, and must be exactly 0 when `provider_kind` is `IN_HOUSE` — an in-house source does not quote, and a non-zero quote on one is refused as a request-shape failure with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, ahead of the code-uniqueness check. `capacity_assets_per_day` is the daily retouch throughput ceiling; it defaults to 40 and must be at least 1. A new provider starts with `assigned_asset_count` at 0.

### 3.2. Styles, Colorways and the Compound Content State

A **style** is the unit of merchandise that owes e-commerce content. A `StudioAdmin` registers it with a `style_number` of 4 to 16 uppercase alphanumeric characters, which must be unique across every style — case-sensitively and after trimming, surrounding whitespace being stripped before the length bounds, the character set and the uniqueness test are judged, so that the trimmed value is what is stored and rendered — with a collision refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_STYLE_NUMBER`. The style declares its `category`, the supplying vendor via `vendor_id`, and the content contract it is held to via `asset_profile_id`. The style's own `category` must equal the `category` of the referenced asset profile; a disagreement is `ERR_CATEGORY_PROFILE_MISMATCH`. Merchandising priority arrives as `priority_tier`, defaulting to `C`.

Colorways are modelled as styles that point at a parent. Supplying `base_style_id` makes the new row a colorway of the referenced style, and `colorway_code` — 2 to 8 uppercase alphanumeric characters — must then be present. The two fields are strictly paired: a `colorway_code` without a `base_style_id`, or a `base_style_id` without a `colorway_code`, is rejected. Parentage is one level deep only, so the referenced base style must itself have no `base_style_id`; a colorway of a colorway is refused. And the pair of parent and code must be unique, so the same colorway code cannot appear twice under one base style. All three of these failures surface as `ERR_INVALID_BASE_STYLE`. A base style that does not resolve at all is `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND`, while an unresolvable `vendor_id` or `asset_profile_id` is `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`; the three body references are resolved in the order `vendor_id`, then `asset_profile_id`, then `base_style_id`, so a request whose vendor and base style are both unresolvable reports `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. A freshly registered style begins with `content_state` at `NOT_STARTED`, `required_item_count` at 0, `allocated_cost_cents` at 0 and `delivered_asset_count` at 0.

What makes a style unusual is that its `content_state` is **compound**: the operator never sets it directly and no endpoint exists to change it. Every transition on the style's content axis is a system consequence of something happening to one of its samples, shot-list items, retouch tasks or assets. The seven states are `NOT_STARTED`, `SAMPLE_READY`, `SCHEDULED`, `SHOT`, `IN_POST`, `DELIVERED` and `BLOCKED`, and `DELIVERED` is terminal.

The forward path opens when physical goods arrive. When a matched sample is moved onto the studio rack, a style sitting in either `NOT_STARTED` or `BLOCKED` advances to `SAMPLE_READY`. Note carefully that recovery from `BLOCKED` restores only the style: `BLOCKED → SAMPLE_READY` does **not** auto-restore the shot-list items that were blocked alongside it, and a `Producer` must re-create or waive those items by hand. From `SAMPLE_READY` the style moves to `SCHEDULED` the moment one of its required shot-list items is put on a call sheet. From `SCHEDULED` it moves to `SHOT` once every one of its shot-list items whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is not `WAIVED` has been captured. `SCHEDULED` and `SHOT` form a genuine loop: if the last live asset behind a captured item is thrown out, that item falls back to being merely assigned, and the style reverts from `SHOT` to `SCHEDULED`. From `SHOT` the style moves to `IN_POST` as soon as the first retouch task for any of its assets is claimed out of the queue. From `IN_POST` it reaches `DELIVERED` when every required non-waived item of the style owns at least one asset in `DELIVERED` state. Both of those completion tests are judged over a non-empty set: a style that owns no required non-waived shot-list item at all satisfies neither vacuously, so it never advances to `SHOT` or to `DELIVERED` on that basis.

`BLOCKED` is entered from `NOT_STARTED`, from `SAMPLE_READY` or from `SCHEDULED`, and only ever as a consequence of a sample being written off: the style blocks when, after the write-off, no sample remains matched to it whose `match_state` is `MATCHED` and whose `custody_state` is `IN_STUDIO` or `ON_SET`. A style already `SHOT`, `IN_POST` or `DELIVERED` never blocks — the physical goods no longer gate anything.

### 3.3. Sample Custody: Intake, Matching, Return and Loss

A physical **sample** travels on two entirely independent state axes. `custody_state` answers "where is the box?" and runs `EXPECTED`, `RECEIVED`, `IN_STUDIO`, `ON_SET`, `RETURN_PENDING`, and then terminally either `RETURNED` or `LOST`. `match_state` answers "do we know what this box is?" and runs `UNMATCHED`, `MISMATCH_HOLD` and `MATCHED`. Neither axis waits for the other except where a rule explicitly names the other, so a box can sit `RECEIVED` while still `UNMATCHED`, and it can be reconciled while parked on the rack.

A `SampleCoordinator` pre-advises an inbound box from the vendor's packing slip, supplying a `barcode` of 4 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, which must be unique across all samples — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — plus the shipping `vendor_id` and the `declared_style_number` printed on the slip, itself 4 to 16 uppercase alphanumeric characters. A pre-advised sample starts `EXPECTED` and `UNMATCHED` with `matched_style_id` null, `return_days_late` at 0, and `received_at`, `due_return_at`, `returned_at` and `condition_note` all null.

Receiving the box books it into the dock, moving custody `EXPECTED → RECEIVED`. Two things must hold. The sample must actually still be `EXPECTED`; any other custody state makes the receipt an undeclared transition, refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY`. And the shipping vendor must not be on coordination hold — a vendor whose `coordination_hold` is true has its receipts refused outright with `ERR_VENDOR_COORDINATION_HOLD`. On success the system stamps `received_at` from the logical clock, derives `due_return_at` by adding the vendor's `sample_sla_days` to `received_at` as exact instant arithmetic (so a 14-day service level received at 09:15 is due back at 09:15 fourteen days later, not at midnight), raises the vendor's `outstanding_sample_count` by one, and increments the vendor's `version` by one.

Matching reconciles the box against a style. The coordinator supplies the `style_id` believed to be correct, and may optionally supply `mismatch_resolution`. When `mismatch_resolution` is omitted, the reconciliation is strict: the referenced style's `style_number` must equal the sample's `declared_style_number`, and any disagreement is refused with `ERR_STYLE_NUMBER_MISMATCH`, leaving the sample untouched. A clean match sets `matched_style_id` to the supplied style and moves the match axis `UNMATCHED → MATCHED`. When the numbers disagree and the coordinator passes `HOLD`, the box is quarantined instead: the match axis moves `UNMATCHED → MISMATCH_HOLD` and `matched_style_id` deliberately stays null while the vendor is chased. When the coordinator passes `ACCEPT_SUBSTITUTE`, a vendor-coordinated substitution is accepted regardless of whether the numbers agree: the axis moves `UNMATCHED → MATCHED` and `matched_style_id` is set to the supplied style. A quarantined box is not stuck — `MISMATCH_HOLD → MATCHED` is available either by supplying a style whose `style_number` does equal the sample's `declared_style_number`, or by supplying `ACCEPT_SUBSTITUTE`; either way `matched_style_id` is set. Matching of any kind is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY` once custody has reached `RETURNED` or `LOST`. Short of that, matching is repeatable rather than once-only: a sample in any match state, including one already `MATCHED`, may be matched again against any style, and the outcome is decided purely by the rule above — so `HOLD` against a disagreeing style knocks an already-matched box back to `MISMATCH_HOLD` and clears `matched_style_id` to null again. Matching never has a consequence beyond the sample row itself, so re-pointing a box at a different style neither advances nor disturbs either style's `content_state`.

Custody moves along the rack are requested by naming the target in `to_state`. Four destinations are legal, and each is only reachable from specific origins; `to_state` is nevertheless declared over the whole seven-member custody enumeration, so naming `EXPECTED`, `RETURNED` or `LOST` is a well-formed request that fails as an undeclared transition rather than as a shape error. `RECEIVED → IN_STUDIO` puts the box on the rack, and `IN_STUDIO → ON_SET` walks it to the floor; both of these require that the sample's `match_state` is already `MATCHED`, because the studio refuses to put unidentified goods into production — a move to either destination while the sample is `UNMATCHED` or in `MISMATCH_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_MATCHED`. `ON_SET → IN_STUDIO` walks it back to the rack and carries no match requirement, which makes the custody axis cyclic between the rack and the floor. Finally the return queue is reachable from three origins — `RECEIVED → RETURN_PENDING`, `IN_STUDIO → RETURN_PENDING` and `ON_SET → RETURN_PENDING` — and also arrives as a system consequence of the style's content being fully delivered. Any origin-destination pair not in that list is an undeclared transition and is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY`. Only one of these moves has a consequence beyond the sample itself: on `RECEIVED → IN_STUDIO`, if the matched style's `content_state` is either `NOT_STARTED` or `BLOCKED`, that style advances to `SAMPLE_READY` and its `version` increments by one.

Returning the box completes the loop and settles lateness. The sample must be `RETURN_PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY`. The coordinator may attach a free-text `condition_note` recording the state the goods came back in. Custody moves `RETURN_PENDING → RETURNED`, `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and lateness is computed: the overrun is the whole number of seconds from `due_return_at` to `returned_at`, and `return_days_late` is 0 whenever that overrun is zero or negative, and otherwise the overrun divided by the 86,400 seconds in a day and rounded **up** to the next whole day. The boundary is deliberately generous by exactly one instant: returning at precisely `due_return_at` yields 0 days late, while returning one second later yields a full day late. The vendor's `outstanding_sample_count` then falls by one; if `return_days_late` is greater than 0 the vendor also takes a strike, raising `late_return_count` by one; and once `late_return_count` reaches 3 strikes the vendor's `coordination_hold` flips to true, which is what stops any further receipts from that vendor. The vendor's `version` increments by one.

Writing a box off as lost is the sharpest action in the sample pipeline, and it triggers the deepest cascade in the system. A `SampleCoordinator` may write off a sample that is `RECEIVED`, `IN_STUDIO`, `ON_SET` or `RETURN_PENDING`, optionally recording a `reason`; a sample that is `EXPECTED`, already `RETURNED` or already `LOST` is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INVALID_CUSTODY`. Custody moves to `LOST`, which is terminal, and `match_state` is deliberately left exactly as it was — the axes are independent, and a lost box is still a box we knew the identity of.

Four levels of consequence then run, each caused by the level above it. At the first level the vendor pays for it: `outstanding_sample_count` falls by one, `late_return_count` rises by one — a loss counts as a strike exactly as a late return does — the third strike flips `coordination_hold` to true, and the vendor's `version` increments by one. At the second level, if the write-off leaves no sample at all still matched to that style with `match_state` of `MATCHED` and `custody_state` of either `IN_STUDIO` or `ON_SET`, and the style's `content_state` is currently `NOT_STARTED`, `SAMPLE_READY` or `SCHEDULED`, then the style's `content_state` becomes `BLOCKED` and its `version` increments by one. At the third level, caused by that block, every shot-list item of that style whose `item_state` is `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED` becomes `BLOCKED` as well. At the fourth level, caused by those items being blocked, each shoot session that had been holding one of them is re-examined: if that session is left with no shot-list item at all whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is `ASSIGNED` or `CAPTURED`, then the session auto-unlocks from `LOCKED` back to `BOOKED` and its `version` increments by one. The whole chain is applied synchronously before the write-off returns.

### 3.4. Booking the Floor: Station Occupation and Daily Capacity

A **shoot session** is a booked interval of occupation on exactly one station, produced by exactly one studio provider. A `Producer` books it by naming the `station_id` to occupy and the `provider_id` who runs the floor and therefore supplies the hourly rate the session will later be costed at, the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` of the interval, the `required_usage_scope` that every model on the session must hold rights for, and the `budget_cap_cents` ceiling that talent commitments on this session may not breach. Three optional values may accompany it: a human `session_label` for the call sheet, at most 200 characters and defaulting to null, the `color_chart_code` naming the reference chart used for the on-set colour check, 1 to 64 characters and defaulting to `XRITE_CC24`, and `color_tolerance_delta_e`, the maximum acceptable composite reading for this session, which defaults to 2.00 and must be at least 0.10 and at most 10.00 carried to two decimals.

The interval must be sane before anything else about it is considered. `scheduled_end` must fall strictly after `scheduled_start`, and both must land on the same UTC calendar date — a session may not straddle midnight. Either failure is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

Occupation geometry comes next, and it is where the station's physical character matters. Take the station's `buffer_minutes` as the mandatory teardown gap and its `max_concurrent_sessions` as the concurrency ceiling. Every existing session on that same station whose `session_state` is `BOOKED`, `LOCKED`, `SHOOTING` or `WRAPPED` occupies a **buffer-extended** window: it runs from its `scheduled_start` inclusive up to its `scheduled_end` plus the buffer, exclusive. Cancelled sessions occupy nothing. The candidate's own window is extended the same way. The system counts how many of those existing occupations the candidate's extended window intersects, and rejects the booking with `ERR_STATION_OVERLAP` when that count reaches or exceeds the concurrency ceiling. On an exclusive model bay, whose ceiling is one, a single intersection is already fatal; on a still-life bench with a ceiling of four, the candidate is admitted while three existing occupations overlap it and refused when a fourth does. Because the windows are half-open, a session starting at exactly the instant a previous session's buffer runs out does not intersect it.

Daily capacity is checked last among the scheduling rules. The system sums `planned_minutes` over the station's sessions that are not cancelled and whose `scheduled_start` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the candidate's, adds the candidate's own `planned_minutes`, and requires the total to be no greater than the station's `daily_capacity_minutes`. Landing exactly on the ceiling is permitted; only a strictly greater total is refused, with `ERR_STATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A station configured for 600 bookable minutes — ten hours — therefore accepts a fourth two-and-a-half-hour session on a day already carrying seven and a half hours, and refuses a fourth three-hour one.

On success the session is created in `session_state` `BOOKED`. Its `planned_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, `committed_talent_cost_cents` starts at 0, `color_check_state` starts at `NOT_MEASURED` with `color_patch_deltas` an empty array and `color_composite_delta_e`, `color_measured_at` and `color_measured_by_talent_id` all null, and `started_at`, `wrapped_at` and `actual_minutes` are null. If the session's `scheduled_start` happens to fall on the current logical clock's UTC date, the station's `committed_minutes_today` rises by the session's `planned_minutes`.

Cancellation is available while the session is still `BOOKED` or `LOCKED`, optionally with a `reason`; a session that has already started shooting, or that has wrapped or been cancelled, cannot be cancelled and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SESSION_TERMINAL`. Cancellation from either origin runs the identical chain of consequences. At the first level the station's capacity is released: the cancelled session stops counting toward `committed_minutes_today`, which being a recount needs no write of its own — the station row is never rewritten by any session's lifecycle, so the station's `version` and `updated_at` do not move. At the second level, caused by that cancellation, every talent booking on the session whose `booking_state` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and the session's `committed_talent_cost_cents` is reset to 0. At the third level, caused by those booking cancellations, each affected talent's `booked_minutes_today` is reduced by exactly that booking's `booked_minutes`, so the minute ledger returns to precisely where it stood before the hold was placed; a talent's `version` increments once for each of their own bookings the cancellation reverses, so a person holding two separate windows on the one cancelled session takes two increments rather than one. Separately, every shot-list item that had been assigned to the session reverts from `ASSIGNED` to `PLANNED` and has its `session_id` cleared, so the work returns to the backlog rather than disappearing with the session.

### 3.5. Crewing the Session: Holds, Charges and Usage Rights

Crewing happens in two steps, and the split is deliberate: a hold reserves a person's time and prices the work, while confirmation is the moment the studio takes on both the fiscal commitment and the usage-rights obligation.

A `Producer` places a hold by naming the `session_id` being crewed, the `talent_id` being booked, the `role_on_set` that person will fill, the `call_time` at which they are due and the `wrap_time` at which they are released, together with the `expected_session_version` demanded by the concurrency rule in §2.6. The session must currently be `BOOKED`; a hold placed against a session that has already been locked, has started shooting, or has wrapped or been cancelled is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BOOKED`. The requested `role_on_set` is one of `MODEL`, `STYLIST` or `PHOTOGRAPHER` and must equal the referenced person's `talent_type`; asking a stylist to fill a photographer slot is refused with `ERR_TALENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The window must be sane and must sit inside its session: `wrap_time` strictly after `call_time`, `call_time` at or after the session's `scheduled_start`, and `wrap_time` at or before the session's `scheduled_end`. Any of those failures is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

People cannot be in two places at once. The candidate hold is refused with `ERR_TALENT_OVERLAP` when the same person already has a booking whose `booking_state` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` that genuinely overlaps it — that is, when the candidate's `call_time` falls strictly before the existing booking's `wrap_time` **and** the existing booking's `call_time` falls strictly before the candidate's `wrap_time`. Unlike station occupation, talent occupation carries no buffer at all, and exact adjacency is explicitly permitted: a model wrapping one session at 13:00 may be called to another at exactly 13:00.

The charge is computed at hold time, frozen onto the row, and never recomputed afterwards — not at confirmation, not at wrap, not at cancellation. It is built in four steps, and the step order is normative. First, `booked_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between `call_time` and `wrap_time`, and the billable quantity is the larger of that figure and the person's `minimum_call_hours` converted to minutes — a four-hour minimum call means a 90-minute booking is billed as 240 minutes. Second, the regular-time headroom is established: the system sums `booked_minutes` across the **other** bookings of the same person whose `booking_state` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `call_time` falls on the same UTC calendar date as this booking's `call_time`, excluding this booking itself and excluding every booking that is `CANCELLED` or `RELEASED` and every booking on another date; the headroom is the person's `max_daily_minutes` less that prior total, floored at zero. `regular_minutes` is then the smaller of the billable quantity and that headroom, and `overtime_minutes` is whatever billable quantity is left over. Third, the two legs are priced separately and each is rounded to a whole cent before they are added: the regular leg is the person's `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by `regular_minutes` divided by 60, rounded half up; the overtime leg is `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by one and a half — the studio's overtime premium — then multiplied by `overtime_minutes` divided by 60, and rounded half up. Their sum is the uncapped figure. Fourth and last, the standard-day cap is applied, and it applies **only** to a booking that produced no overtime at all: when `overtime_minutes` is zero, `charge_cents` is the smaller of the uncapped figure and the person's `day_rate_cents`; the moment even one overtime minute exists the cap is void and the uncapped figure stands in full. The cap never feeds back into the regular/overtime split.

A successful hold lands in `booking_state` `HELD` with `usage_rights_scope` and `release_document_id` both null, raises the booked person's `booked_minutes_today` by `booked_minutes` and increments that person's `version` by one, and increments the parent session's `version` by one. Note that a held booking contributes nothing to `committed_talent_cost_cents` — money is only committed at confirmation.

Usage rights are documented ahead of the shoot. A `StudioAdmin` records a **release document** against a person by supplying the `talent_id` granting rights, the `scope` of the grant, the `territory` it covers, and the `valid_from` and `valid_until` bounds of its window. The window is inclusive at `valid_from` and exclusive at `valid_until`, and `valid_until` must fall strictly after `valid_from`, or the record is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`; an unresolvable `talent_id` is `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. A new release lands in `doc_state` `PENDING` with `signed_at` null — except that the creation response is itself a read of the document, so a release recorded with a `valid_until` already at or before the current clock has the lazy expiry of §2.4 fire on the spot and is returned already `EXPIRED` at `version` 2. Countersigning moves it `PENDING → SIGNED` and stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock, but only while the document is still `PENDING` and the clock is strictly before `valid_until`; a document already `SIGNED`, already `EXPIRED`, or whose `valid_until` has been reached is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_INVALID_STATE`. Expiry is never an operator action: from either `PENDING` or `SIGNED`, a document whose `valid_until` is at or before the current logical clock is `EXPIRED`, and from that instant it stops covering any booking confirmation.

Confirming a hold is where all of that converges, and the checks run in a fixed order so that the operator always sees the most fundamental obstacle first. The booking itself must be `HELD` — confirming something already confirmed, released or cancelled is `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. The parent session must still be `BOOKED` or `LOCKED`; a session that has already wrapped or been cancelled refuses the confirmation with `ERR_SESSION_TERMINAL`. Then, and only for a person whose `requires_release` is true — which is to say only for models — the studio looks for a covering release: a document belonging to that same `talent_id`, in `doc_state` `SIGNED`, whose `valid_from` is at or before the booking's `call_time` and whose `valid_until` is strictly after the booking's `wrap_time`. That search opens by applying §2.4's lazy expiry across every release document in the store rather than only to this talent's, so confirming a model can be the write that persists an unrelated document's expiry; a booking for a person who needs no release never runs that sweep and never touches a release at all. Partial coverage is worthless; the document must span the whole booked window. If nothing qualifies the confirmation is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_ON_FILE`. When several documents qualify the choice is not arbitrary: candidates are ranked by usage breadth from widest to narrowest — `FULL_BUYOUT` outranks `ECOM_PLUS_SOCIAL`, which outranks `ECOM_ONLY` — then by the later `valid_until` first, and finally by the earlier `created_at` first — and, should two candidates share a `created_at` as well, by the one recorded first — and the first candidate under that ranking is the one selected. A document's `territory` plays no part in either the eligibility test or the ranking. The selected document's `scope` must then be at least as broad as the session's `required_usage_scope` under that same ordering; a narrower grant is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT`, so a model holding only `ECOM_ONLY` rights cannot be confirmed onto a session that requires `ECOM_PLUS_SOCIAL`. Last of the confirmation checks is fiscal: the session's current `committed_talent_cost_cents` plus this booking's frozen `charge_cents` must be no greater than the session's `budget_cap_cents`. Landing exactly on the cap is allowed; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_SESSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

A successful confirmation moves the booking `HELD → CONFIRMED`. For a model booking it pins the provenance of the rights by writing the selected document's identifier into `release_document_id` and copying its `scope` into `usage_rights_scope`; for a stylist or photographer booking both of those fields remain null, because no release is required and therefore none is pinned. The session's `committed_talent_cost_cents` then rises by the booking's `charge_cents` and the session's `version` increments by one.

A confirmed booking leaves `CONFIRMED` in only two ways. When the session wraps, every booking on it that is `CONFIRMED` at that instant moves to `RELEASED` — the rights have now been exercised — and a released booking keeps counting toward `committed_talent_cost_cents`, because the money was genuinely spent. Alternatively a `Producer` may cancel a booking that is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, optionally recording a `reason`; a booking already `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation reverses exactly the ledgers the booking posted and nothing more: the person's `booked_minutes_today` falls by that booking's `booked_minutes` and their `version` increments by one, and when the booking being cancelled was `CONFIRMED` the session's `committed_talent_cost_cents` falls by that booking's `charge_cents` and the session's `version` increments by one. A booking cancelled straight out of `HELD` never touched the money ledger, so it does not adjust it.

### 3.6. Defining and Assigning the Shot List

A **shot-list item** is one shot a style owes, and it doubles as the unit of cost allocation. A `Producer` defines one against a style by supplying the `style_id`, the `asset_kind` describing what kind of capture it is, the `angle_code` describing the camera angle or framing, the `estimated_minutes` used for shoot-day planning — which must be at least 1 and at most 480 — and the `expected_style_version` demanded by the concurrency rule in §2.6. The `required` flag is optional and defaults to true, marking the item as something the style's content is genuinely incomplete without. Within one style, the combination of style, `asset_kind` and `angle_code` must be unique among items that have not been waived; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SHOT_ITEM`, while waived items are ignored by that uniqueness test so a waived shot can be re-created. A new item lands in `item_state` `PLANNED` with `session_id` null, `live_asset_count` 0 and `allocated_cost_cents` 0, and if its `required` flag is true the parent style's `required_item_count` rises by one; either way the style's `version` increments by one.

The five item states are `PLANNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `CAPTURED`, `BLOCKED` and `WAIVED`, the last of which is terminal. Assignment puts a planned item onto a session's call sheet by naming the `session_id`, and it is the only operator-driven way to leave `PLANNED` toward production. The item must be `PLANNED`; an item that is already assigned, captured, blocked or waived is refused with `ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`. The target session must still be `BOOKED`, because a locked call sheet is frozen — assignment to a `LOCKED`, `SHOOTING`, `WRAPPED` or `CANCELLED` session is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BOOKED`. Under §2.7 the session's state is judged before the item's own, so an already-assigned item aimed at a locked session reports `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BOOKED` rather than `ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`.

Then the physical question: can that station actually shoot that kind of capture? The rule is specific per asset kind. An `ON_MODEL` item requires a station whose `station_type` is `MODEL`. A `STILL_ANGLE` or `DETAIL` item requires a station whose `station_type` is either `MANNEQUIN` or `STILL_LIFE` — a model bay is the wrong instrument for a flat lay. A `VIDEO` item requires a station whose `station_type` is either `MODEL` or `STILL_LIFE`, which means a mannequin position cannot carry video at all. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`. On success the item moves `PLANNED → ASSIGNED` with its `session_id` set, and if the item's `required` flag is true and the parent style's `content_state` is `SAMPLE_READY`, that style advances to `SCHEDULED` and its `version` increments by one.

Two further moves out of `ASSIGNED` are system consequences rather than operator actions. Cancelling the holding session reverts the item `ASSIGNED → PLANNED` and clears its `session_id`, as described in §3.4. Ingesting the first non-rejected file against the item advances it `ASSIGNED → CAPTURED`, which happens precisely when its `live_asset_count` rises from 0 to 1; at that moment, if every shot-list item of the style whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is not `WAIVED` is now `CAPTURED`, the style advances to `SHOT` and its `version` increments by one. Capture is revocable, so the axis is genuinely cyclic: when a quality-control rejection finally throws out the last live file behind a captured item — its `live_asset_count` falling from 1 back to 0 — the item reverts `CAPTURED → ASSIGNED` and, if the style was `SHOT`, the style reverts to `SCHEDULED`. Either event rewrites the item row whether or not its transition actually fires: every ingest against the item, and every rejection that finally throws one of its files out, increments the item's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, so a second file ingested against an already-`CAPTURED` item still bumps it while leaving `item_state` alone.

Items also arrive at `BLOCKED` only as a consequence of a sample write-off: an item that is `PLANNED` or `ASSIGNED` becomes `BLOCKED` when its style's `content_state` has just become `BLOCKED`, and an item blocked out of `ASSIGNED` may take its holding session's lock with it, as the fourth level of that cascade describes. Unlike a session cancellation, blocking leaves the item's `session_id` exactly as it stood, so an item blocked out of `ASSIGNED` goes on reporting — and goes on being found by a `session_id` filter for — the session that was holding it. Blocked items are never automatically restored, even when a replacement sample later brings the style back to `SAMPLE_READY`.

Waiving is the producer's escape hatch. An item that is `PLANNED` or `BLOCKED` may be waived, optionally with a `reason`, moving it to the terminal `WAIVED` state so that it no longer holds up the style's content completion; an item that is `ASSIGNED`, `CAPTURED` or already `WAIVED` cannot be waived and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`. When the waived item's `required` flag was true, the style's `required_item_count` falls by one, and the style's `version` increments by one; when it was false the style is not rewritten at all. Waiving re-evaluates nothing else on the style: its `content_state` is left exactly where it was, so waiving away the last outstanding required shot never advances a style to `SHOT` or `DELIVERED`, and waiving the items of a `BLOCKED` style never unblocks it. A waived item also keeps whatever `session_id` it was carrying.

### 3.7. Shoot Day: Freezing the Call Sheet, Colour Accuracy, Capture and Wrap

Freezing the call sheet is the producer's last planning act. The session must still be `BOOKED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BOOKED`. Two substantive conditions then apply, and their order matters. First, the studio verifies that the physical goods are in the building: for every style referenced by a shot-list item on this session whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is `ASSIGNED`, there must exist at least one sample matched to that style — `matched_style_id` pointing at it, `match_state` of `MATCHED`, and `custody_state` of either `IN_STUDIO` or `ON_SET`. A style on the call sheet with no such sample refuses the freeze with `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_IN_STUDIO`. Second, the call sheet must not be empty: at least one shot-list item whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is `ASSIGNED` must reference the session, failing which the freeze is refused with `ERR_SHOT_LIST_EMPTY`. Because the sample check ranges only over the required assigned items, a session with none of them passes that check vacuously and surfaces the empty call sheet instead. On success the session moves `BOOKED → LOCKED` and further shot-list assignment to it is rejected.

Reopening reverses it. `LOCKED → BOOKED` restores assignment to the call sheet, and is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_LOCKED` if the session is not currently locked. The same reversion also happens without any operator asking for it, as the fourth level of the sample write-off cascade: a session stripped of every required assigned or captured item auto-unlocks.

From here the session belongs to the photographer, and only to one specific photographer. Every on-set action — calling the session to order, submitting the colour reading, ingesting captures, and wrapping — requires that the acting `X-User-ID` is the `id` of a talent profile holding a booking on that very session whose `role_on_set` is `PHOTOGRAPHER` and whose `booking_state` is `CONFIRMED`. A photographer with only a `HELD` booking, a photographer booked on a different session, or any other talent profile is refused with `ERR_NOT_BOOKED_ON_SESSION`, and that identity gate is evaluated before the session's own state is considered. Calling the session to order then moves it `LOCKED → SHOOTING` and stamps `started_at` from the logical clock; a session that is not `LOCKED` refuses with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_LOCKED`.

Colour accuracy is measured exactly once per session, while it is `SHOOTING`. The photographer submits `patch_deltas`, a non-empty array of per-patch delta-E readings taken off the session's reference chart; each member must be a number, but the individual readings carry no declared bounds of their own. A session that is not `SHOOTING` refuses with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SHOOTING`, and a session whose `color_check_state` has already moved off `NOT_MEASURED` to either `PASS` or `FAIL` refuses a second reading with `ERR_COLOR_ALREADY_MEASURED`.

The composite reading weights the average patch against the worst patch, and the weighting depends on what kind of station the session occupies. Take the mean of all submitted patch readings and, separately, the largest of them. On a station whose `station_type` is `MODEL`, the composite is three tenths of the mean plus seven tenths of the largest, rounded half up to two decimals — skin-tone accuracy is punished far more by one badly-off patch than by a mildly-off average. On a `MANNEQUIN` or `STILL_LIFE` station the two contributions are weighted equally, one half each, likewise rounded half up to two decimals. The verdict is then inclusive: `color_check_state` becomes `PASS` when the composite is less than or equal to the session's `color_tolerance_delta_e`, and `FAIL` only when it is strictly greater. A composite landing exactly on the tolerance passes.

Consistency across sessions is enforced separately, and it does not merely record a verdict — it refuses the reading outright. The system looks for a baseline: among sessions on the **same** station whose `color_check_state` is `PASS` and whose `scheduled_start` falls strictly earlier than this session's `scheduled_start`, it ranks by the later `scheduled_start` first and then by the earlier `created_at` first, and takes the first row. If no such baseline exists the check passes and nothing further is done. If a baseline does exist and the absolute difference between the new composite and that baseline's `color_composite_delta_e` is strictly greater than three quarters of a delta-E unit, the measurement is rejected with `ERR_COLOR_CONSISTENCY_DRIFT` and **nothing at all is persisted** — no composite, no verdict, no timestamp — so the session remains `NOT_MEASURED` and may be measured again. A difference of exactly three quarters is within tolerance and is accepted.

When the reading is accepted, `color_patch_deltas` records the submitted array verbatim, `color_composite_delta_e` records the composite, `color_check_state` records the verdict, `color_measured_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `color_measured_by_talent_id` records the acting photographer.

Ingesting a capture creates an **asset**. The photographer supplies the `shot_list_item_id` the file satisfies, the `file_name` under which it will be delivered — 1 to 300 characters — the `sequence_number` giving the frame's position within that shot — at least 1 and at most 999 — and `metadata_tags`, a required key/value JSON object of tagging metadata which is stored and rendered verbatim, any keys beyond those the profile demands included. The checks run in a fixed order. The item must be on a call sheet at all before any of them can be judged: an item whose `session_id` is still null offers no session for the photographer gate to test, and the ingest is refused with `ERR_NOT_BOOKED_ON_SESSION`. The item's session must be `SHOOTING` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_SHOOTING`) and the item itself must be `ASSIGNED` or `CAPTURED` (`ERR_SHOT_ITEM_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`). The station the session occupies must be currently calibrated: its `calibrated_until` must be non-null and must fall strictly after the current logical clock, since a station that has never been calibrated or whose calibration has lapsed cannot certify colour — either case is `ERR_STATION_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`. The session's `color_check_state` must not be `FAIL`; a failed colour check bars all ingest with `ERR_COLOR_CHECK_FAILED`, while a session still `NOT_MEASURED` is permitted to ingest. The `file_name` must be unique across all assets, with a collision refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The `file_name` must then equal the expansion of the naming pattern belonging to the style's asset profile: the style's `style_number`, an underscore, the item's `angle_code`, an underscore, and the `sequence_number` written with leading zeros to three digits — so frame 7 of the front angle of style `TS10442` is `TS10442_FRONT_007`. Anything else is `ERR_ASSET_NAMING_VIOLATION`. Finally `metadata_tags` must contain every key listed in that asset profile's `required_metadata_keys`, each mapped to a non-empty string; a missing key or an empty value is `ERR_ASSET_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`.

An accepted ingest lands the asset in `asset_state` `INGESTED`, copies the item's `session_id` onto the asset's own `session_id`, and records the acting photographer as `captured_by_talent_id`. The item's `live_asset_count` rises by one and, when it rises from 0 to 1, the item advances `ASSIGNED → CAPTURED`; if that capture completes the style's required non-waived set, the style advances to `SHOT` and its `version` increments by one. The asset's `cms_asset_key` and `delivered_at` both start null.

Wrapping ends the shoot and is where the in-house cost ledger is written. The session must be `SHOOTING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SHOOTING`, and the actor must clear the same photographer gate. The session moves `SHOOTING → WRAPPED`, `wrapped_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `actual_minutes` becomes the whole number of minutes between `started_at` and `wrapped_at`. Every booking on the session still in `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`; one still merely `HELD` is left `HELD`. No talent row is rewritten by the wrap: each released person's `booked_minutes_today` falls because the release drops the booking out of the recount, but their `version` and `updated_at` do not move.

Cost allocation then runs, and its rounding order is normative. The station leg is the producing provider's `station_hour_rate_cents` multiplied by the session's `actual_minutes` divided by 60, rounded half up to a whole cent. The talent leg is the plain sum of `charge_cents` over the session's bookings whose `booking_state` is `CONFIRMED` or `RELEASED`; held and cancelled bookings contribute nothing. The two legs are added to give the session total. The system then collects every shot-list item on this session whose `item_state` is `CAPTURED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending — never by identifier — and counts them. If that count is zero nothing is posted at all and the session total is absorbed as studio overhead; items that are merely `ASSIGNED`, or `BLOCKED`, or `WAIVED` never receive an allocation. Otherwise each item's even share is the session total divided by the count and rounded **down** to a whole cent, and the leftover cents — the session total less the even share multiplied by the count, a figure strictly less than the count — are distributed one cent each to the earliest-created items in that same ascending order. Each item's resulting share is written to its `allocated_cost_cents`, and then, item by item, that same figure is added to the item's style's `allocated_cost_cents`; the style's `version` increments by one for each style credited. Deriving a style's share by rounding a session-wide proportion instead is not permitted, because it yields a different number of cents.

### 3.8. Post-Production: Queueing, Claiming, Quality Control and Turnaround

A `ContentOps` operator queues a captured file for post-production by creating a **retouch task**, naming the `asset_id` to be worked and the `provider_id` that will do the work and therefore supplies both the service level and the throughput ceiling. The asset must be `INGESTED` — a file already in retouch, awaiting quality control, approved, rejected or delivered is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED` — and the asset must not already carry a live task, meaning no existing task for it whose `task_state` is `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `REWORK` or `APPROVED`; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_EXISTS`. Note that an `ABANDONED` task does not block a new one.

The effective service level is pinned onto the task at creation and never moves afterwards. It starts from the provider's `sla_hours`, and when the style behind the asset carries `priority_tier` `A` the studio halves it and rounds **up** to a whole hour — so a provider quoting a 25-hour turnaround owes 13 hours on a tier-`A` style and the full 25 on tier `B` or `C`. That figure is written to the task's own `sla_hours`, and `due_at` becomes the asset's `created_at` plus that many hours. A new task lands in `task_state` `QUEUED` with `assigned_retoucher_id` null, `rework_count` at 0, `rework_hours_total` at 0.00, `rework_started_at` null, `claimed_at`, `submitted_at` and `approved_at` null, `turnaround_hours_gross` and `turnaround_hours_net` null, and `sla_state` at `IN_SLA`. The asset itself moves `INGESTED → IN_RETOUCH`, the provider's `assigned_asset_count` rises by one, and the provider's `version` increments by one.

Retouchers do not pick their work; the queue picks for them. A `Retoucher` asks for the next task and supplies no identifier at all — the server selects it. The acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to a talent profile whose `talent_type` is `RETOUCHER`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_TALENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Eligibility for the candidate pool has two conditions: the task's `task_state` must be `QUEUED`, and the task's provider must still have headroom, meaning that provider's `assigned_asset_count` is no greater than its `capacity_assets_per_day`. Tasks that are `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `REWORK`, `APPROVED` or `ABANDONED` are never candidates. If the pool is empty — nothing queued at all, or every queued task's provider is at capacity — the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CLAIMABLE_TASK`.

Every candidate's `priority_score` is recomputed from scratch on each claim request; it is never a stored figure that ages in place. The same holds for the figure a task payload reports: `priority_score` is computed at read time against the clock as it then stands, so advancing the clock changes the value a read returns without touching the task's `version` or `updated_at`. Four terms compose it. The merchandising weight comes from the style behind the asset: 100 points for `priority_tier` `A`, 60 for `B`, 30 for `C`. Urgency rewards waiting: it is two points for every hour the task has existed — the elapsed hours between the task's `created_at` and the current logical clock, multiplied by two and rounded half up to two decimals — but it is capped at 50.00 points, which is to say urgency stops accumulating after the task has waited 25 hours. A rework penalty subtracts 15 points for each rejection loop the task has been through, counting at most two loops, so a task rejected twice carries a 30-point penalty and a further rejection adds nothing. And a scope bonus adds 20 points when the shot behind the asset has `asset_kind` `ON_MODEL`, because on-model imagery is what the site actually sells with, and nothing otherwise. The four terms are combined — weight plus urgency, less the rework penalty, plus the scope bonus — and the result is rounded half up to two decimals.

Candidates are ranked by `priority_score` from highest to lowest; ties are broken by the earlier `due_at` first, and any remaining tie by the earlier `created_at` first. The `created_at` ordering settles it in practice, and should two tasks share a `created_at` as well the one created first wins; a task's identifier never participates in the ranking. The winning task moves `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS`, its `assigned_retoucher_id` is set to the claiming `X-User-ID`, and `claimed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. If the style behind the asset is currently `SHOT`, this first claim moves it to `IN_POST` and increments its `version` by one.

Handing work to quality control is available from `IN_PROGRESS` and from `REWORK`, optionally with a `note`. Only the assigned retoucher may do it: a submission whose `X-User-ID` differs from the task's `assigned_retoucher_id` is refused with `ERR_TASK_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ACTOR`, and that ownership check precedes the state check. A task in any other state is refused with `ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. The task moves to `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. When the submission is leaving `REWORK` specifically, the time the file spent waiting is banked first: the hours between `rework_started_at` and the current clock, carried to two decimals, are **added** to the running `rework_hours_total`, and `rework_started_at` is then cleared. The asset moves `IN_RETOUCH → QC_PENDING`.

A `QCReviewer` then rules on it with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally attaching a `note`. The reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the task's `assigned_retoucher_id`; equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that check precedes the state check. Only a `SUBMITTED` task can be ruled on; anything else is `ERR_RETOUCH_TASK_INVALID_STATE`.

Approval moves the task `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, which is terminal, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock, moves the asset `QC_PENDING → APPROVED`, and lowers the provider's `assigned_asset_count` by one. Approval is also the moment turnaround is measured, in two variants against the same instants. The gross variant, `turnaround_hours_gross`, is the elapsed hours from the asset's `created_at` to `approved_at`, rounded half up to two decimals; it is the vendor-facing scorecard, on which the retailer's clock never stops, and it therefore counts every hour the file sat waiting in rework. The net variant, `turnaround_hours_net`, is that gross figure less the accumulated `rework_hours_total`, again rounded half up to two decimals; it is the provider-facing scorecard used in the outsourced comparison, and it deducts producer-side waiting and nothing else. The service-level verdict is derived from the **gross** variant only, and never from the net one, which is exactly why a task can be `BREACHED` on gross while sitting comfortably inside the service level on net: `sla_state` becomes `BREACHED` when the gross figure is strictly greater than the task's pinned `sla_hours`, otherwise `AT_RISK` when the gross figure is strictly greater than four fifths of that `sla_hours`, and otherwise `IN_SLA`. A task pinned at 20 hours is therefore `IN_SLA` at exactly 16.00 gross hours, `AT_RISK` at 16.01, still `AT_RISK` at exactly 20.00, and `BREACHED` at 20.01.

Rejection has two outcomes, and which one applies depends on how many loops the task has already been through. While the task's `rework_count` is below two, rejection recycles the work: the task moves `SUBMITTED → REWORK`, `rework_count` rises by one, `rework_started_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the asset moves `QC_PENDING → IN_RETOUCH` for the same retoucher to fix — whose `assigned_retoucher_id`, along with the `claimed_at` and the `submitted_at` of the loop just refused, is left standing rather than cleared, so the next submission simply overwrites `submitted_at`. Once `rework_count` has already reached two, the studio gives up: the task moves `SUBMITTED → ABANDONED`, which is terminal, the asset moves `QC_PENDING → REJECTED`, which is likewise terminal, and the provider's `assigned_asset_count` falls by one. Abandonment is not an approval, so no turnaround is measured on the way out: `approved_at`, `turnaround_hours_gross` and `turnaround_hours_net` all stay null and `sla_state` stays at the `IN_SLA` it was created with, however long the file actually took. The rejection also propagates downward — the shot-list item's `live_asset_count` falls by one and, if that leaves it at 0, the item reverts `CAPTURED → ASSIGNED` and the style reverts `SHOT → SCHEDULED`, putting the shot back on the floor's to-do list.

### 3.9. Delivery into the CMS and the Cost-per-Style Comparison

Publication is a `ContentOps` action on an asset that has cleared quality control. The asset must be `APPROVED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED`. An optional `cms_collection` hint may accompany the request. Four levels of consequence follow, each caused by the one above it.

At the first level the asset moves `APPROVED → DELIVERED`, `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `cms_asset_key` is minted as the style's `category`, a slash, the style's `style_number`, a slash, and the asset's `file_name` — so a front shot of an apparel top is filed as `APPAREL_TOP/TS10442/TS10442_FRONT_007`. At the second level, caused by that delivery, the style's `delivered_asset_count` rises by one and the style's `version` increments by one. At the third level, caused by that delivery having covered the last outstanding shot, the system checks whether every shot-list item of the style whose `required` flag is true and whose `item_state` is not `WAIVED` now owns at least one asset in `DELIVERED` state; when that holds and the style is `IN_POST`, the style advances `IN_POST → DELIVERED`, which is terminal for its content axis. At the fourth level, caused by the style reaching `DELIVERED`, every sample matched to that style whose `custody_state` is `IN_STUDIO` or `ON_SET` is moved to `RETURN_PENDING` — the goods are no longer needed and the return clock, already ticking since `due_return_at` was set, now has somewhere to go. Each such sample's `version` increments by one; no vendor row is rewritten, because a box sitting in the return queue is still outstanding and no vendor counter has moved.

Finance and merchandising both need to know what a style actually cost, and they need two different answers to that question at the same instant. A computed cost summary therefore reports **two variants evaluated against the same database snapshot**, and its response carries nine members: the `style_id` it describes, `provisional_cost_cents`, `final_cost_cents`, `provisional_cost_per_asset_cents`, `final_cost_per_asset_cents`, `outsourced_quote_cents`, `outsourced_provider_id`, `variance_cents` and `recommended_source`.

The **provisional** variant is a forward-looking projection for a producer whose sessions may still be `BOOKED`, `LOCKED`, `SHOOTING` or already `WRAPPED`. It starts at zero and walks every session that holds at least one shot-list item of this style and whose `session_state` is not `CANCELLED`. For each such session it projects a total: the session's provider's `station_hour_rate_cents` multiplied by the session's `planned_minutes` divided by 60 and rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the sum of `charge_cents` over that session's bookings whose `booking_state` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RELEASED`. It then takes that session's shot-list items whose `item_state` is `ASSIGNED` or `CAPTURED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and skips the session entirely if there are none. The projected total is divided across those items with exactly the same arithmetic the wrap-time allocation uses: an even share rounded down to a whole cent, with the leftover cents distributed one each to the earliest-created items. Only the shares landing on items belonging to this style are accumulated into `provisional_cost_cents`.

The **final** variant is the finance-facing actual, and it is not recomputed at all: `final_cost_cents` is simply the style's persisted `allocated_cost_cents`, which is to say the sum of the per-item allocations already posted at each session wrap. Because those postings used `actual_minutes` rather than `planned_minutes`, counted only `CONFIRMED` and `RELEASED` bookings rather than including `HELD` ones, and divided across `CAPTURED` items only rather than across assigned-and-captured ones, the two variants diverge in **both** directions. A style that captured fewer shots than were planned for it absorbs a larger share of a smaller session total, so the final figure can land above or below the provisional one; and a style whose sessions have not wrapped yet reports a provisional figure against a final figure of zero.

Per-asset figures normalise each variant by its own denominator. The provisional denominator is the count of this style's shot-list items whose `item_state` is `ASSIGNED` or `CAPTURED`; the final denominator is the count of those whose `item_state` is `CAPTURED`. Each variant's cost is divided by its denominator, or by 1 when that denominator is zero, and rounded half up to a whole cent, giving `provisional_cost_per_asset_cents` and `final_cost_per_asset_cents` respectively.

The outsourced comparison closes the summary. Eligible providers are those whose `provider_kind` is `OUTSOURCED` and whose `capacity_assets_per_day` is at least the style's `required_item_count` — a retouch house that cannot absorb the style's whole required shot list in a day is not a real alternative. Eligible providers are ranked by the lower `style_quote_cents` first, then by the higher `quality_score` first, then by the earlier `created_at` first, and the first provider under that ranking supplies both `outsourced_quote_cents`, taken from its `style_quote_cents`, and `outsourced_provider_id`. When no provider is eligible, `outsourced_quote_cents` and `outsourced_provider_id` are null. `variance_cents` is the final cost less the outsourced quote, and is null whenever there is no quote. `recommended_source` is the string `IN_HOUSE` when there is no quote at all or when the final cost is less than or equal to the quote, and the string `OUTSOURCED` only when the final cost is strictly greater than the quote — a tie always favours keeping the work in the building.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint is mounted under the `/studio` prefix. Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful call — reads, state transitions, and the logical-clock capability — returns HTTP 200. Transition and action endpoints return the full updated resource, not a fragment. The optional free-text values several actions accept are validated but not kept: a `reason` on a session cancellation, a booking cancellation, an item waive or a sample write-off (at most 2000 characters), a `note` on a retouch submission or a quality-control decision (at most 1000 characters) and a `cms_collection` hint on delivery (at most 200 characters) must each be a string if present, but none is persisted and none appears on any resource payload. A sample's `condition_note`, at most 2000 characters, is the one free-text value that is stored and rendered.
