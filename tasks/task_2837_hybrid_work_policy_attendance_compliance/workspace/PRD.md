# Hybrid Work Attendance Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Three years after the return-to-office debate began, the corporate workplace program office still cannot answer the two questions the executive committee keeps asking: *are people actually coming in as often as our policy says they should*, and *are we applying that policy evenly*. Badge readers, network-presence telemetry and manager attestations all exist, but they live in separate systems, they disagree with each other, and nobody agrees on whether a badge swipe alone proves a day in the office. Meanwhile accommodation requests are adjudicated over email, non-compliance conversations happen inconsistently, remote-work relocations are approved without anyone checking the tax or data-security consequences, and floor space is allocated on the basis of last year's headcount rather than this quarter's expectations.

This document specifies the Hybrid Work Attendance Compliance service: a single system of record for hybrid-work policy that classifies role eligibility, measures attendance from corroborated presence signals, adjudicates exceptions through a documented interactive process, allocates neighborhoods and desks against declared expectations, walks non-compliance up a formal escalation ladder, gates remote-work locations behind parallel tax and data-security reviews, and periodically recalibrates the whole policy against measured collaboration and equity outcomes.

The system is deliberately opinionated about determinism. Every derived number — a credited office day, a compliance pressure score, a seat entitlement, a chargeback total, an equity spread — has exactly one correct value given the same stored rows, because those numbers end up in performance conversations and in regulator-facing audit files. Two properties follow from that requirement and shape almost every rule below: all time reasoning resolves against an explicit server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, and every accumulation, ranking and rounding step has a pinned order.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that user acts under. The single exception is the logical-clock endpoint `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which is a test-driving affordance and requires no authenticated user. On every other endpoint, a missing or empty `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Employee`, `PeopleManager`, `HRBusinessPartner`, `AccommodationSpecialist`, `WorkplaceProgramOffice`, `ComplianceAuditor` and `SystemIntegration`. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles authorized to call it; a caller whose `X-User-Role` is not on that list is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This role check runs **before** any resource is looked up, so an unauthorized caller who names a resource id that does not exist receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) and never a 404 — the API does not leak existence to callers who could not have acted anyway.

`Employee` is the only role whose identity is backed by a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare that resource's `employee_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That ownership gate guards exactly five operations: reading one employee (`GET /employees/{employee_id}`), filing an exception request (`POST /exception-requests`), filing a remote-location request (`POST /remote-location-requests`), reading one attendance week (`GET /attendance-weeks/{week_id}`) and reading one exception request (`GET /exception-requests/{request_id}`). An `Employee` caller may also respond to an information round on their own request and read their own remote-location request.

The other six roles — `PeopleManager`, `HRBusinessPartner`, `AccommodationSpecialist`, `WorkplaceProgramOffice`, `ComplianceAuditor` and `SystemIntegration` — carry opaque identities. For each of them, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a workflow step demands attribution (as `created_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a classification, `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an exception request, `last_action_by_user_id` on an escalation case, and `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a recalibration review) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Two `Employee` attributes, `manager_user_id` and `hr_partner_user_id`, hold opaque operator identifiers of exactly this kind: they name the `PeopleManager` who owns that employee's approvals and the `HRBusinessPartner` assigned to them, and they are compared against the acting `X-User-ID` for the segregation-of-duties and manager-ownership rules described in §2.11.

The division of labour between the opaque roles is intentional. `WorkplaceProgramOffice` owns the policy cycle, the space model and the recalibration workflow, but cannot register employees or amend attendance. `HRBusinessPartner` owns the employee record, week amendment and the discretionary hold, but cannot record an eligibility decision — that belongs to `PeopleManager` — and cannot decide a relocation. `AccommodationSpecialist` runs the interactive process and is the only role permitted to adjudicate an accommodation-flagged exception. `ComplianceAuditor` records the data-security review dimension and may countersign a recalibration review, and is read-only everywhere else. `SystemIntegration` is the badge and network telemetry feed: it opens attendance weeks, appends signals, finalizes weeks and reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, must be at least 1 and at most 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A value outside either range is a request-shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain after the returned window. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

Unless a collection states otherwise, rows are returned in `created_at` ascending order. Several collections override that default because their business ordering matters more than their insertion ordering: policy cycles are returned newest first, by `created_at` descending; teams are ordered by `team_code` ascending; employees by `employee_number` ascending; eligibility classifications by `effective_from` descending and then `created_at` descending, which is the same ranking the current-class selector uses; attendance weeks by `week_start_date` ascending; compliance signals by `observed_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; neighborhoods by `neighborhood_code` ascending; and desk assignments by `start_date` ascending and then `desk_index` ascending. Exception requests, remote-location requests, seat allocations, escalation cases and recalibration reviews all use the `created_at` ascending default — and for exception requests and seat allocations that order is not cosmetic, because it is also the pinned accumulation order for relief allocation and for chargeback totalling respectively.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every stored resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Ids never carry a type prefix, are never supplied by the client, and are never used as a ranking or tie-breaking input by any calculation in this specification — where a tie-break is needed, `created_at` is the tie-breaker.

Twelve of the thirteen resource types also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, drawn from the logical clock described in §2.6. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations a cascade performs rather than the caller. Whenever a field on a row changes, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant and its `version` advances — this applies equally to a row the caller addressed directly and to a parent or sibling row a cascade touched.

`ComplianceSignal` is the exception. A compliance signal is an immutable observation: once appended it is never edited, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its response payload.

### 2.5. Addressing Resources: Flat Collections and Path Parameter Resolution

The API has no nested collection paths. Every entity is exposed as a peer collection at the API root, and relationships travel in payloads and query filters rather than in URL segments — a seat allocation names its `neighborhood_id` and `team_id` in its creation payload rather than hanging off a neighborhood's URL. Twelve path parameters exist across the surface: `cycle_id`, `team_id`, `employee_id`, `classification_id`, `week_id`, `signal_id`, `request_id`, `neighborhood_id`, `allocation_id`, `assignment_id`, `case_id` and `review_id`.

Path parameters and payload foreign keys are resolved left to right, path segments before body fields, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Each entity has its own not-found code, all at HTTP 404: `ERR_POLICY_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTENDANCE_WEEK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SIGNAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEIGHBORHOOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESK_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCALATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RECALIBRATION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Note that `request_id` is shared by two unrelated collections: an unresolvable exception-request id yields `ERR_EXCEPTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` while an unresolvable remote-location-request id yields `ERR_RELOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Because the role check of §2.1 runs first, none of these 404s is ever reachable by a caller who was not authorized in the first place.

Two collections are reachable only through discovery rather than through creation. Compliance signals are appended to a week but are read back through the signals collection filtered by `attendance_week_id`, and escalation cases are never created by any client — they are opened by system cascade — so listing them, optionally filtered by `employee_id`, `prior_case_id` or `stage`, is their sole discovery channel.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Calendar Conventions

The server holds a logical current UTC instant and every temporal comparison in this specification resolves against it: attendance-week date containment, seat-allocation window coverage, desk changeover buffers, exception-relief windows, and every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `finalized_at` and `vacated_at` value written. The clock starts fixed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which falls in ISO week `2026-W31`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A caller of any role may rebind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; that call returns 200, mutates no entity, and triggers no recomputation — it only changes what "now" means for subsequent requests.

Calendar dates are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. ISO week labels are strings of the form `YYYY-Www`, four digits, a literal `W`, then two digits; a label that does not match that shape is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Week ordinals are comparable: one ISO week label is earlier than another when its ordinal position in the ISO calendar is lower.

Two interval conventions coexist, and confusing them is the single most common implementation error in this domain. **Seat-allocation week windows are half-open**: the window spans every ISO week from `window_start_week` inclusive up to but excluding `window_end_week`. Two allocations that merely touch — one ending at `2026-W41`, the next starting at `2026-W41` — therefore do not overlap and never contend for the same capacity. **Date intervals are closed**: an exception request's relief window, a desk assignment's occupancy, and an attendance week's seven-date span all include both endpoints. An attendance week covers exactly the seven calendar dates beginning at `week_start_date` and ending six days later.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Monetary Amounts

Every non-integer derived quantity in this system is carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded half up — that is, a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. This applies to `non_compliance_pressure`, `utilization_pct`, `aggregate_compliance_rate`, `collaboration_index`, `equity_variance_pct` and to each per-band and per-team sub-score that feeds them. Where a formula specifies more than one rounding step, the number of steps and their positions are part of the contract: rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different, wrong answer.

Money is always an integer count of cents and is never represented as a decimal or a string. `line_chargeback_cents`, `total_space_chargeback_cents` and `tax_nexus_registration_cost_cents` are integers throughout. Seat and day counts are integers; where a formula divides to produce one, the quotient is rounded **up** to the next whole number, and where it divides to produce risk points from a cost, the quotient is truncated **down** to a whole number. Enumerated results — an eligibility class, an escalation stage, a decision reason — are not rounded at all.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Parent Records

Three write operations reach across an aggregate boundary in a way that can silently discard a decision the client never saw, and each is guarded by an optimistic-concurrency field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent record. These three fields are required inputs, not optional ones; omitting one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422), and supplying one that does not match the parent's current `version` is a precondition failure at HTTP 412.

`POST /eligibility-classifications` must carry `expected_employee_version`, equal to the current `version` of the `Employee` named by `employee_id`. When another writer has already created a classification that legitimately supersedes the employee's current `APPROVED` class, the employee's `version` has advanced and the current writer's picture of the employee is stale; the create is then rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (412).

`POST /seat-allocations` must carry `expected_neighborhood_version`, equal to the current `version` of the `Neighborhood` named by `neighborhood_id`. A second allocation on the same zone is perfectly legitimate product behaviour, so the parent's `version` is the only deterministic guard against a client reserving seats against a capacity picture that has since moved; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NEIGHBORHOOD_VERSION` (412).

`POST /recalibration-reviews/{review_id}/decision` must carry `expected_policy_cycle_version`, equal to the current `version` of the parent `PolicyCycle`. Adopting a review rewrites every team's days-in-office expectation in the cycle, so a second adoption legitimately supersedes the first and the second decider's view of the cycle baseline is stale; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_CYCLE_VERSION` (412).

No other endpoint takes a version field, and none of these three is checked on any endpoint other than the one named above.

### 2.9. Cycle Posture as a Cycle-Wide Write Gate

Every entity in this system is reachable, through the entity graph, from exactly one `PolicyCycle`, and that cycle's `posture` gates writes across the whole subtree. A cycle in `OPEN` posture accepts every cycle-scoped write. A cycle in `RECALIBRATING` posture keeps attendance measurement and space bookkeeping running, closes the eligibility-classification approval path, and is the only posture in which a recalibration review may be measured or adopted. A cycle in `LOCKED` posture accepts no mutation at all — `LOCKED` is terminal, and nothing brings a cycle back out of it.

The `LOCKED` refusal is `ERR_CYCLE_LOCKED` (HTTP 409) and it guards fifteen operations: `POST /teams`, `POST /employees`, `POST /eligibility-classifications`, `POST /attendance-weeks`, `POST /attendance-weeks/{week_id}/signals`, `POST /attendance-weeks/{week_id}/finalize`, `POST /attendance-weeks/{week_id}/amend`, `POST /exception-requests`, `POST /neighborhoods`, `POST /seat-allocations`, `POST /seat-allocations/{allocation_id}/commit`, `POST /desk-assignments`, `POST /desk-assignments/auto`, `POST /recalibration-reviews` and `POST /recalibration-reviews/{review_id}/submit`. The same code is returned when `POST /eligibility-classifications/{classification_id}/submit` or `POST /eligibility-classifications/{classification_id}/decision` is called against a cycle whose posture is anything other than `OPEN`, because those two steps require an open cycle by definition.

Recalibration works the other way around. `POST /recalibration-reviews/{review_id}/submit` and `POST /recalibration-reviews/{review_id}/decision` both require the parent cycle's posture to be exactly `RECALIBRATING`, and refuse with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_RECALIBRATING` (HTTP 409) otherwise. Because submit also sits in the fifteen-endpoint locked list at a higher precedence band and the decision endpoint does not, a submit attempted inside a `LOCKED` cycle reports `ERR_CYCLE_LOCKED` while a decision attempted inside a `LOCKED` cycle reports `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_RECALIBRATING`.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are grouped into precedence bands and evaluated from the top of this list downwards; the first band that produces a violation supplies the response, and later bands are never reached.

1. **Authentication** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, ahead of every existence check.
3. **Existence** — the thirteen not-found codes of §2.5.
4. **Ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` for an `Employee` caller reaching outside their own row.
5. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. **Request shape** — missing required fields, undeclared enum members and out-of-range numbers, all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Interval well-formedness** — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` for an empty or inverted declared interval.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the three stale-version codes of §2.8.
9. **Cycle posture** — `ERR_CYCLE_LOCKED`.
10. **Lifecycle state preconditions** — the "wrong state for this step" family: `ERR_INVALID_POSTURE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_EMPLOYEE_STATUS_TRANSITION`, `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_WEEK_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_WEEK_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_RECORDED`, `ERR_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_RELOCATION_DECISION_FINAL`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_RESERVED`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ESCALATION_AT_FLOOR`, `ERR_ESCALATION_AT_CEILING`, `ERR_ESCALATION_TERMINAL`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT` and `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED`.
11. **Cross-entity state** — `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_RECALIBRATING`, then the relocation prerequisites `ERR_NO_APPROVED_CLASSIFICATION` and `ERR_CLASS_NOT_REMOTE_ELIGIBLE`.
12. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_LABEL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEIGHBORHOOD_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_EMAIL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_LABEL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE_WEEK` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIGNAL`.
13. **Cross-entity configuration gates** — `ERR_SIGNAL_OUTSIDE_WEEK`, `ERR_TEAM_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_SPECIALIST_REQUIRED`, `ERR_INTERACTIVE_PROCESS_PENDING` and `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_PROHIBITED`.
14. **Aggregate capacity** — `ERR_NEIGHBORHOOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_SEAT_ENTITLEMENT_EXCEEDED`.
15. **Temporal overlap** — `ERR_DESK_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT` and `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
16. **Policy ceilings** — `ERR_RELOCATION_RISK_CEILING`, `ERR_TAX_NEXUS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_BELOW_DAYS_FLOOR`, `ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DESK`.
17. **Governance gate** — `ERR_EQUITY_FLAG_BLOCKS_ADOPTION`, the lowest-precedence check in the entire system.

Six precedence interactions are worth calling out explicitly, because each of them is a case where an implementer's instinct disagrees with the specification. A classification create that is both stale and aimed at a `SEPARATED` employee reports `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (412), because concurrency outranks the employee-active gate. A seat-allocation create whose window is inverted *and* whose seats exceed the team's entitlement reports `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422), because interval well-formedness outranks aggregate capacity, and one that is merely stale as well as over-entitlement reports `ERR_STALE_NEIGHBORHOOD_VERSION` (412). A replayed signal whose `dedupe_key` already exists on the week *and* whose `observed_date` falls outside the week reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIGNAL` (409), since uniqueness outranks the containment gate — though both sit below the editability check, so a replay onto an already-`FINALIZED` week reports `ERR_WEEK_NOT_OPEN` (409). An employee payload duplicating both `employee_number` and `work_email` reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER`. An approval of a `RESTRICTED`-tier relocation into a prohibited jurisdiction reports `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_PROHIBITED` (409) even when its final risk also breaches the approval ceiling. And an adoption that would both push a team under the days floor and carries a raised equity flag reports `ERR_BELOW_DAYS_FLOOR` (409).

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Manager Ownership

Four independent identity rules run across this system, and they exist because every one of them was, at some point, a real audit finding.

The user who drafts an eligibility classification may not be the user who records its manager decision: when the acting `X-User-ID` on `POST /eligibility-classifications/{classification_id}/decision` equals the classification's stored `created_by_user_id`, the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The same code and status guard the recalibration decision: when the acting `X-User-ID` on `POST /recalibration-reviews/{review_id}/decision` equals the review's `submitted_by_user_id`, the decision is refused. A `ComplianceAuditor` is deliberately permitted to countersign a review the program office submitted, which is exactly why the check compares user identity rather than role.

Manager ownership is the mirror image. A classification decision is only legal when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the target employee's `manager_user_id` — the approving manager must be the employee's own manager. De-escalating an escalation case is likewise reserved to the employee's own `PeopleManager`, comparing `X-User-ID` against `manager_user_id`. Escalating a case up the ladder inverts the test: the acting `HRBusinessPartner` must **not** be the employee's `manager_user_id`, so that no single person can both own an employee and sign their formal action.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Policy Cycle and Enrolling Teams

Everything in this system hangs off a **policy cycle** — the half-yearly or annual period over which one version of the hybrid-work policy is in force. A `WorkplaceProgramOffice` operator opens one by supplying a `cycle_label` of 1 to 40 characters that no other cycle already uses (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_LABEL`), the inclusive calendar dates `effective_from` and `effective_to` where the end date must fall strictly after the start date, and the four policy dials described below. A new cycle starts in `OPEN` posture with `enrolled_team_count` at 0, `aggregate_compliance_rate` at 0.00 and `formal_action_total` at 0.

The first dial is `days_in_office_floor`, an integer from 0 through 5 that no team in the cycle may declare an expectation below. The second and third are the documentation weights `badge_weight` and `network_weight`, each a value from 0.00 through 1.00 carried to two decimals, which record how much evidentiary weight the policy communication attributes to badge data versus network data; the two must add up to exactly 1.00 or the cycle is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both weights are recorded for audit and for the published policy — neither is consumed by the credited-days arithmetic of §3.4, which treats every qualifying signal source as equally probative. The fourth and fifth dials are the measurement targets: `collaboration_target_score`, a value from 0.00 through 100.00 that a recalibration's collaboration index must reach, and `equity_variance_threshold_pct`, a percentage-point spread from 0.00 through 100.00 above which the equity flag is raised. Any of these five values outside its declared range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Posture moves are a small, deliberate state machine driven by a `target_posture` value and an optional `note`. From `OPEN` the cycle may move to `RECALIBRATING` (opening a recalibration round) or straight to `LOCKED`; from `RECALIBRATING` it may return to `OPEN` (closing the round) or move to `LOCKED`. `LOCKED` is terminal. Any request naming a target that is not reachable from the current posture — including one naming the posture the cycle is already in, and any target at all out of `LOCKED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POSTURE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Reaching `LOCKED`, from either predecessor posture, does three things beyond setting `posture`: every neighborhood in the cycle recomputes its `allocation_state`, which becomes `FROZEN` because the parent posture is now `LOCKED`; every attendance week of every employee on a team in the cycle whose `status` is `FINALIZED` transitions to `LOCKED`; and the cycle's own `version` advances by one. The two non-terminal moves, `OPEN → RECALIBRATING` and `RECALIBRATING → OPEN`, change only `posture` and advance the cycle's `version`.

A team is enrolled by naming its `policy_cycle_id` and a `team_code` of 1 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase alphanumerics and hyphens, unique among the teams of that cycle (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_CODE`). The team declares `expected_days_in_office_per_week`, an integer from 0 through 5 which must be at least the cycle's `days_in_office_floor`; a lower value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It also declares `core_collaboration_days`, an array of between zero and five distinct weekday codes drawn from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU` and `FRI` on which the team is expected to co-locate — published in the policy communication and not consumed by any calculation — and `headcount_planned`, an integer from 1 through 5000. Finally, the optional boolean `signal_corroboration_required` selects the evidence standard applied to this team's employees and defaults to false. Enrolling a team increments the cycle's `enrolled_team_count` by one. A team's `escalated_headcount` and `formal_action_count` both begin at 0.

**Seat entitlement** is derived from the team declaration in a strictly pinned five-step order, and the order matters because the dependency only looks circular. Step one: `expected_daily_attendance` is the planned headcount multiplied by the expected days in office, divided by the five working days of a week, then rounded **up** to a whole number. Step two: `base_entitlement` is set equal to `expected_daily_attendance`. Step three: a buffer percentage is selected from three tiers, evaluated in declared order and keyed on `base_entitlement` alone — never on the eventual total — where a base of at most 10 takes 20 percent, a base of at most 25 takes 12 percent, and any larger base takes 6 percent. Step four: `buffer_seats_entitled` is `base_entitlement` multiplied by that percentage, rounded **up** to a whole number. Step five: `entitled_seats` is `base_entitlement` plus `buffer_seats_entitled`, and it is the per-week ceiling on how many seats the team may hold. Consider a team with 25 planned heads and a five-day expectation: the base is 25, so the pinned order selects the 12 percent tier, giving a buffer of 3 and `entitled_seats` of 28. Keying the tier off the 28-seat total instead would select 6 percent and yield 27, which is wrong. All four derived fields are recomputed whenever `headcount_planned` or `expected_days_in_office_per_week` changes — at enrolment, and again when a recalibration is adopted.

### 3.2. Registering Employees and Moving Them Through Employment Status

An `HRBusinessPartner` registers an employee against a `team_id`, supplying an `employee_number` of 1 to 24 characters that is globally unique (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER`), a `work_email` that must contain an at-sign and is likewise globally unique (`ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_EMAIL`), the IANA `home_timezone` recorded for policy communication and used in no calculation, the `tenure_start_date` on which employment began, and a `demographic_band` of `BAND_A`, `BAND_B`, `BAND_C` or `BAND_D` used solely by the equity monitoring of §3.9. The payload also names the two operator identifiers `manager_user_id` and `hr_partner_user_id`, and may carry the optional boolean `disability_accommodation_on_file`, which defaults to false and which — when true — forces the interactive process onto every exception request that employee ever files, whatever its category.

A freshly registered employee starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `current_eligibility_class` at the `ONSITE_REQUIRED` policy default, `compliance_state` `COMPLIANT`, `escalation_stage` `NONE`, and `cumulative_shortfall_days`, `consecutive_breach_weeks`, `approved_relief_days_total` and `non_compliance_pressure` all at zero. Registration also recomputes the parent cycle's `aggregate_compliance_rate`, which is the count of `ACTIVE` employees on teams of that cycle whose `compliance_state` is `COMPLIANT`, divided by the total count of `ACTIVE` employees on those teams, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimals; when no `ACTIVE` employee exists on the cycle's teams at all the rate is exactly 0.00 rather than an error. That rate is refreshed every time any employee's `compliance_state` or `status` changes anywhere in the cycle.

Employment status is a three-state machine driven by a `target_status` value and an optional `note`, and only an `HRBusinessPartner` may drive it. `ACTIVE` and `ON_LEAVE` cycle freely in both directions; both may move to `SEPARATED`, which is terminal. A target that is not reachable from the current status — including the current status itself and any target out of `SEPARATED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EMPLOYEE_STATUS_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Moving to `ON_LEAVE` or back to `ACTIVE` changes `status`, advances the employee's `version`, and recomputes the cycle's `aggregate_compliance_rate` because the `ACTIVE` denominator has changed size. An employee on leave still has their attendance weeks settled — the finalize step accepts an employee whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE` — but they leave both the compliance-rate population and the equity population, which count `ACTIVE` employees only.

Separation runs a wider cascade, identically from `ACTIVE` and from `ON_LEAVE`. Beyond setting `status` to `SEPARATED` and advancing the employee's `version`, every desk assignment of that employee whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` transitions to `VACATED` with `vacated_at` stamped at the current logical instant; the employee's escalation case, if one exists whose `stage` is neither `CURED` nor `CLOSED_SEPARATION`, transitions to `CLOSED_SEPARATION`, which sets the employee's `escalation_stage` to `NONE` and decrements the team's `escalated_headcount` by one, and — when the case was sitting in `WRITTEN_WARNING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` or `REFERRED` — also decrements the team's `formal_action_count` by one and therefore the cycle's `formal_action_total`; and the cycle's `aggregate_compliance_rate` is recomputed with the employee dropped from the `ACTIVE` population.

Because a separated employee is no longer a participant, six creation paths refuse to open new work for anyone whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`, returning `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409): drafting an eligibility classification, opening an attendance week, filing an exception request, filing a remote-location request, and both the manual and the automatic desk-assignment paths.

### 3.3. Classifying Role Eligibility

Every role in the company is eventually classified as one of three eligibility classes — `FULLY_REMOTE`, `HYBRID` or `ONSITE_REQUIRED` — and until a classification has been approved the employee is treated as `ONSITE_REQUIRED`. That default is a policy decision, not a placeholder: an unclassified role is presumed to need its people on site.

A `PeopleManager` or an `HRBusinessPartner` drafts a classification by naming the `employee_id`, the `requested_class`, a `justification_code` drawn from `ROLE_DESIGN`, `EQUIPMENT_BOUND`, `CUSTOMER_ONSITE`, `TALENT_RETENTION` and `ACCOMMODATION_LINKED`, the `effective_from` date on which the class takes effect once approved, and the stale-version guard `expected_employee_version` described in §2.8. An optional `decision_note` may be supplied. The new row lands in `DRAFT` state with `created_by_user_id` captured from the acting `X-User-ID` and `decided_by_user_id` left null. At the same moment the server sets `supersedes_classification_id` to the `id` of the employee's current `APPROVED` classification — the one the current-class selector below would pick — or to null when the employee has none. A successful draft also advances the parent employee's `version` by one, which is precisely why the next writer's `expected_employee_version` will no longer match.

Routing a draft to the approving manager moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_MANAGER`. That step requires the row to still be in `DRAFT`, or it is refused with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409); it requires the employee to still be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ACTIVE`; and it requires the parent cycle to be `OPEN`, as §2.9 sets out. Only `posture` and the classification's own `version` move.

The decision step belongs exclusively to `PeopleManager`, and to one particular manager: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the target employee's `manager_user_id`, and a manager who does not own the employee is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The row must be in `PENDING_MANAGER`, or the call is refused with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_PENDING` (409) — which is also what a second decision on an already-`APPROVED` row receives. The acting user must not be the `created_by_user_id` who drafted it, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). The payload carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REVISE`, plus an optional `decision_note`.

A `REVISE` decision sends the row back along the revert edge `PENDING_MANAGER → DRAFT`, storing `decided_by_user_id` and the supplied `decision_note` and advancing `version`; the draft may then be resubmitted, so `DRAFT → PENDING_MANAGER → DRAFT` is a legitimate loop with no bound on its length. An `APPROVE` decision moves the row `PENDING_MANAGER → APPROVED`, stores `decided_by_user_id`, and sets off four further effects: the classification named by `supersedes_classification_id`, when it is not null, transitions `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` and its own `version` advances; the employee's `current_eligibility_class` is recomputed; the employee's `non_compliance_pressure`, `compliance_state` and `escalation_stage` are recomputed, because the severity multiplier that scales shortfall into pressure is keyed on the eligibility class and has just changed; and the classification's `version` advances.

The **current eligibility class** is selected as follows. Consider only that employee's classifications whose `state` is `APPROVED`; rows in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_MANAGER` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded outright. If no such row exists, the class is `ONSITE_REQUIRED`. Otherwise rank the surviving rows by `effective_from` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` descending, and take the `requested_class` of the first row. The tie-break is `created_at` and never `id`, so two classifications sharing an `effective_from` resolve in favour of the later-created one.

### 3.4. Measuring Presence: Attendance Weeks and Compliance Signals

Attendance is accounted one ISO week at a time. Either the telemetry feed (`SystemIntegration`), an `HRBusinessPartner` or a `PeopleManager` opens a week for an employee by supplying the `employee_id`, the `iso_week` label and the `week_start_date`, which must be the Monday of that ISO week — a mismatch, or an `iso_week` that does not match the four-digit / `W` / two-digit shape, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). At most one week may exist per employee and `iso_week` pair; a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE_WEEK` (409). The week opens in `status` `OPEN` with `required_days`, `credited_days`, `relief_days_credited` and `shortfall_days` all at 0, `variant_applied`, `amendment_reason` and `finalized_at` all null.

Presence evidence arrives as immutable **compliance signals** appended to the week. The telemetry feed and a `PeopleManager` may append them; each append names a `signal_source` of `BADGE`, `NETWORK` or `MANUAL_ATTEST`, the `site_code` of the building or campus where presence was observed (1 to 16 characters), the `observed_date`, the `minutes_present` observed on site as an integer from 0 through 1440, and a `dedupe_key` of 1 to 64 characters that the upstream feed uses as its idempotency handle. Signals may only be appended while the week is still editable — that is, while its `status` is `OPEN` or `AMENDED`; an append onto a `FINALIZED` or `LOCKED` week is refused with `ERR_WEEK_NOT_OPEN` (409). A `dedupe_key` already present on that week is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIGNAL` (409), and an `observed_date` falling outside the week's seven dates is `ERR_SIGNAL_OUTSIDE_WEEK` (422). Appending a signal moves no derived field whatsoever: signals are pure evidence, and nothing is credited until the week is settled.

**Required days** are snapshotted at each settlement from the employee's class at that moment. A `FULLY_REMOTE` employee is required to be in the office 0 days. A `HYBRID` employee is required for exactly their team's `expected_days_in_office_per_week`. An `ONSITE_REQUIRED` employee is required for all five working days.

**Credited days** are counted date by date across the week's seven dates, against a minute floor of four hours — a badge or network observation must record at least 240 minutes present to count, while a manual attestation counts on the strength of its existence alone and its `minutes_present` is ignored entirely. For a given date, badge evidence is satisfied when at least one signal on that date has source `BADGE` and at least 240 minutes present; network evidence is satisfied the same way with source `NETWORK`; and attestation evidence is satisfied when at least one signal on that date has source `MANUAL_ATTEST`, whatever its minutes. Only signals attached to this very week participate — there is no carryover from an adjacent week — and their insertion order is irrelevant.

Which of the two evidence standards applies is taken from the team's `signal_corroboration_required` flag at settlement time and recorded on the week as `variant_applied`. Under the `SIGNAL_UNION` standard, used when the team does not require corroboration, a date counts when **any** of badge, network or attestation evidence is satisfied, so a badge-only day counts. Under the `SIGNAL_CORROBORATED` standard, used when the team does require it, a date counts only when badge **and** network evidence are **both** satisfied on that date, or an attestation exists for it. For the same signal set, the corroborated standard therefore yields strictly fewer credited days than the union standard whenever any date carries exactly one automated source. The count of qualifying dates is finally capped at 5, so a week with weekend presence never credits more than five days.

### 3.5. Settling a Week and Walking the Non-Compliance Ladder

Settlement is the single most consequential operation in the system, because it is the point at which evidence becomes a compliance judgement. The telemetry feed, an `HRBusinessPartner` or a `PeopleManager` settles a week, moving it `OPEN → FINALIZED` the first time and `AMENDED → FINALIZED` on any later pass. The week must be editable — `OPEN` or `AMENDED` — or the call is refused with `ERR_WEEK_NOT_OPEN` (409), which is also what a second settlement of an already-`FINALIZED` week receives. The employee must be `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, so a separated employee's weeks can no longer be settled and the attempt is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The parent cycle's posture must be `OPEN` or `RECALIBRATING`; a `LOCKED` cycle refuses with `ERR_CYCLE_LOCKED` (409). No payload is required.

What follows is a chain of six links, each caused by the previous one rather than by the call itself, and an implementation that stops after the first link will look correct on the week and wrong everywhere else. **First**, the week's own accounting is written: `status` becomes `FINALIZED`, `finalized_at` is stamped at the current logical instant, `required_days` is snapshotted, `variant_applied` records `SIGNAL_CORROBORATED` when the team requires corroboration and `SIGNAL_UNION` otherwise, `credited_days` is counted under that standard, `relief_days_credited` is applied from approved exceptions, and `shortfall_days` becomes the required days minus the credited days minus the credited relief, floored at zero so it never goes negative. **Second**, because this week has entered the settled set, the employee's `cumulative_shortfall_days`, `consecutive_breach_weeks`, `non_compliance_pressure` and `compliance_state` are all recomputed. **Third**, if that recomputation left the employee in `BREACH` and no escalation case for them is currently open, a case is opened. **Fourth**, the open case's stage is driven from the recomputed pressure. **Fifth**, a stage that has entered the formal-action band changes the team's `formal_action_count`. **Sixth**, because the team counter changed, the cycle's `formal_action_total` and `aggregate_compliance_rate` are recomputed. The week's `version` advances by one, and so does the `version` of every other row the chain touched. Re-settlement after an amendment replays this identical chain against whatever signals and approved exceptions exist at that later moment.

The employee's rolled-up counters are defined against settled weeks only. `cumulative_shortfall_days` is the sum of `shortfall_days` across that employee's weeks whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `LOCKED`. `consecutive_breach_weeks` is the length of the current breach streak: walk that same settled set from the most recent `week_start_date` backwards, counting each week whose `shortfall_days` is greater than zero, and stop at the first week whose `shortfall_days` is zero. Weeks in `OPEN` or `AMENDED` status are absent from the settled set entirely — they contribute nothing to the sum and are simply skipped over by the streak walk rather than terminating it.

**Non-compliance pressure** combines three inputs in a pinned order. Take the raw shortfall — the same sum as `cumulative_shortfall_days` — and multiply it by the severity multiplier for the employee's current eligibility class: 0.0 for `FULLY_REMOTE`, 1.0 for `HYBRID`, and 1.5 for `ONSITE_REQUIRED`. To that product add the breach streak multiplied by a consecutive-week weight of 1.0. Round the result half up to two decimals. Finally clamp it to a ceiling of 12.00, so no employee's pressure exceeds twelve however long the shortfall runs. Multiply first, then add the streak term, then round, then clamp — any other order can produce a different number. A `FULLY_REMOTE` employee therefore accumulates pressure from the streak term alone, because their multiplier is zero and their required days are zero in the first place.

`compliance_state` follows directly from the pressure: `COMPLIANT` when the pressure is exactly 0.00, `AT_RISK` when it is above 0.00 but strictly below 2.00, and `BREACH` at 2.00 or above.

The **escalation ladder** has four live rungs, `COACHING`, `WRITTEN_WARNING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` and `REFERRED`, and two terminal outcomes, `CURED` and `CLOSED_SEPARATION`. Cases are never created by a client. When a cascade leaves an employee in `BREACH` and no case of theirs sits outside the two terminal stages, the server opens one at `COACHING`, records `opened_pressure` as the employee's pressure at that instant, sets `discretionary_hold` to false, and sets `prior_case_id` to the `id` of that employee's most recently created `CURED` case — ordered by `created_at` descending — or null when this is their first case. Opening a case mirrors `COACHING` into the employee's `escalation_stage` and increments the team's `escalated_headcount` by one.

Thereafter, every cascade that touches the employee's pressure sets the open case's `stage` from the pressure directly, provided `discretionary_hold` is false: a pressure of 10.00 or more yields `REFERRED`, 7.00 or more yields `PERFORMANCE_PLAN`, 4.00 or more yields `WRITTEN_WARNING`, 2.00 or more yields `COACHING`, and anything below 2.00 yields `CURED`. Every comparison is inclusive at two decimal places, so a pressure of exactly 4.00 yields `WRITTEN_WARNING` while 3.99 yields `COACHING`. Because the stage is assigned in one step rather than walked, a single settlement, amendment or exception cascade can move a case more than one rung at once — `COACHING` straight to `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` when the recomputed pressure reaches 7.00, or `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` straight to `CURED` when it falls under 2.00. A multi-rung move is the composition of the rungs it passes through, and its counter effects are computed **once** from band entry and exit, never once per rung skipped.

The two team counters are what those band crossings maintain. `escalated_headcount` is the number of that team's employees holding a case whose `stage` is `COACHING`, `WRITTEN_WARNING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` or `REFERRED`; `formal_action_count` is the number holding a case in the narrower formal-action band of `WRITTEN_WARNING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` or `REFERRED`. So `COACHING → WRITTEN_WARNING` increments `formal_action_count` by one because the case entered the band, and the cycle's `formal_action_total` — the sum of `formal_action_count` across the cycle's teams — is recomputed. `WRITTEN_WARNING → PERFORMANCE_PLAN` and `PERFORMANCE_PLAN → REFERRED` leave both counters untouched, because the case was already inside the band. Walking back down, `WRITTEN_WARNING → COACHING` decrements `formal_action_count` and refreshes `formal_action_total`, while `PERFORMANCE_PLAN → WRITTEN_WARNING` and `REFERRED → PERFORMANCE_PLAN` change neither. Reaching `CURED` from `COACHING` sets `escalation_stage` back to `NONE`, decrements `escalated_headcount` by one, and refreshes the cycle's `aggregate_compliance_rate`; reaching `CURED` from `WRITTEN_WARNING` does all of that **and** decrements `formal_action_count`, refreshing `formal_action_total` as well. A later breach opens a brand-new case whose `prior_case_id` points back at the cured one, which is how the self-referential case history is threaded. Reaching `CLOSED_SEPARATION`, which only ever happens because the employee separated, sets `escalation_stage` to `NONE` and decrements `escalated_headcount`; from `WRITTEN_WARNING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN` or `REFERRED` it additionally decrements `formal_action_count` and refreshes `formal_action_total`, while from `COACHING` it does not.

Two manual levers sit on top of the automatic ladder, and both take an `action_note`. Escalating a case one rung — `COACHING → WRITTEN_WARNING`, `WRITTEN_WARNING → PERFORMANCE_PLAN` or `PERFORMANCE_PLAN → REFERRED` — is reserved to an `HRBusinessPartner` who is **not** the employee's `manager_user_id`; a manager attempting to sign their own report's formal action is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), and a `PeopleManager` attempting the step at all is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Escalation is also blocked while the employee has any exception request in `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` status whose interactive process is required, refused with `ERR_INTERACTIVE_PROCESS_PENDING` (409) — an accommodation conversation in flight suspends formal action. A manual escalation sets `discretionary_hold` to true, records `last_action_by_user_id` and the supplied `action_note`, mirrors the new stage into `escalation_stage`, and maintains the two counters exactly as the automatic path does. De-escalating one rung — `WRITTEN_WARNING → COACHING`, `PERFORMANCE_PLAN → WRITTEN_WARNING` or `REFERRED → PERFORMANCE_PLAN` — is reserved to the employee's own `PeopleManager`, comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against `manager_user_id`, and anyone else is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403); it clears `discretionary_hold` back to false and records `last_action_by_user_id` and `action_note`. Unlike the cascade, both manual levers always move exactly one rung. De-escalating a case already at `COACHING` is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_AT_FLOOR` (409) — the only exit from the bottom rung is a cure driven by the relief cascade — escalating a case already at `REFERRED` is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_AT_CEILING` (409), and either lever applied to a case in `CURED` or `CLOSED_SEPARATION` is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_TERMINAL` (409).

The **discretionary hold** is the third lever and belongs exclusively to `HRBusinessPartner`. It carries the boolean `discretionary_hold` and an `action_note`, and it sets the flag to the supplied value. While the flag is true, the pressure-derived stage assignment is skipped entirely for that case and cascades leave `stage` untouched — but pressure itself keeps accumulating on the employee, so clearing the hold later exposes the case to whatever stage the accumulated pressure now implies. Setting or clearing the hold changes no stage and no roll-up on that call; it records `last_action_by_user_id` and nothing else.

**Amendment** is the compensating action for a settlement. Only an `HRBusinessPartner` may reopen a week, moving it `FINALIZED → AMENDED` with a supplied `amendment_reason`; a week in any other status is refused with `ERR_WEEK_NOT_FINALIZED` (409), and the parent cycle must again be `OPEN` or `RECALIBRATING`. Reopening zeroes the week's `credited_days`, `relief_days_credited` and `shortfall_days`, so the week stops contributing to the employee's totals while it sits amended. The employee's `cumulative_shortfall_days`, `consecutive_breach_weeks`, `non_compliance_pressure` and `compliance_state` are then recomputed downward, and the employee's open case — provided `discretionary_hold` is false — has its `stage` reset from the new pressure; when that value is `CURED` the case becomes `CURED` and `escalated_headcount`, `formal_action_count` and `formal_action_total` all decrement accordingly. Because `FINALIZED → AMENDED → FINALIZED` is a cycle, a week may be corrected and re-settled any number of times.

Finally, when a cycle is locked, every `FINALIZED` week in it transitions to `LOCKED` and advances its `version`. A locked week is still counted by `cumulative_shortfall_days` and still feeds the streak walk — locking freezes the record, it does not erase it.

### 3.6. Attendance Exceptions and the Interactive Process

Not every missed office day is a compliance failure. An employee undergoing treatment, caring for a dependent, or working under a disability accommodation is entitled to relief, and the process by which that relief is granted has to be documented well enough to survive a discrimination claim.

An `Employee` may file a request for themselves — and only for themselves — while a `PeopleManager` or an `HRBusinessPartner` may file on anyone's behalf. The payload names the `employee_id`, a `category` of `MEDICAL`, `CAREGIVING` or `DISABILITY_ACCOMMODATION`, the `requested_relief_days` as an integer from 1 through 5 representing days-in-office relief per affected week, the inclusive `start_date` and `end_date` of the relief window, and a `rationale` of 1 to 1000 characters. An end date earlier than the start date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). The request opens in `SUBMITTED` status with `granted_relief_days` and `info_round_count` at 0 and `info_needed`, `response_note`, `closure_reason` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` all null. An employee may not hold two live requests of the same `category` whose closed date windows overlap: a live request being one in `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` status, and an overlap being any pair of closed intervals sharing at least one date. Such a collision is `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Requests in `DENIED`, `REVOKED` or `WITHDRAWN` status are dead and never conflict, so a revoked window may be re-requested.

At creation the server computes `interactive_process_required`, which is true when the `category` is `MEDICAL` or `DISABILITY_ACCOMMODATION`, and also true — whatever the category, `CAREGIVING` included — when the employee's `disability_accommodation_on_file` flag is set. Two consequences follow, and both are the point of the flag. First, only an `AccommodationSpecialist` may adjudicate such a request; an `HRBusinessPartner` attempting it is refused with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_SPECIALIST_REQUIRED` (409), and the same restriction applies to opening an information round on it. Second, while such a request is still live in `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, formal escalation of that employee is suspended, as §3.5 describes.

The interactive process itself is the `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` loop. An `AccommodationSpecialist` or an `HRBusinessPartner` opens a round by supplying `info_needed` — the text of what is being asked for — which moves the request to `INFO_REQUESTED`; the request must be `SUBMITTED` for this, and any other status, `INFO_REQUESTED` included, is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` (409). The employee (for their own request), their manager or an `HRBusinessPartner` then replies with a `response_note`, which returns the request to `SUBMITTED` and increments `info_round_count` by one; a reply against a request whose status is not `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED` (409). The loop is unbounded — an accommodation conversation may take as many rounds as it takes, and `info_round_count` records how many completed.

Adjudication closes a live request and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `WITHDRAW`, plus `granted_relief_days` (required for an approval) and `closure_reason` (recorded on a denial or a withdrawal). A request whose status is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` (409). A denial and an approval both require the request to be `SUBMITTED`, whereas a withdrawal is legal from `SUBMITTED` and from `INFO_REQUESTED` alike. Granted relief may never exceed what the employee asked for: a `granted_relief_days` above the stored `requested_relief_days` is refused with `ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` (409). And where the interactive process is required, an approval may not be recorded until at least one information round has completed — an approval while `info_round_count` is still 0 is refused with `ERR_INTERACTIVE_PROCESS_PENDING` (409). An approval is also refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when another `APPROVED` request of the same employee and category already covers an intersecting closed window.

A denial sets `status` to `DENIED`, stores `closure_reason` and `adjudicated_by_user_id`, leaves `granted_relief_days` at 0, and moves nothing downstream. A withdrawal sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN` and stores `closure_reason`, and likewise moves nothing downstream. An approval, by contrast, sets off a five-link chain. **First**, `status` becomes `APPROVED`, `granted_relief_days` takes the supplied value, and `adjudicated_by_user_id` records the acting user. **Second**, because the request is now approved, the employee's `approved_relief_days_total` — the sum of `granted_relief_days` across their `APPROVED` requests — is recomputed. **Third**, because the employee's approved relief changed, every one of that employee's attendance weeks whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `LOCKED` and whose seven-date window intersects the relief window recomputes its `relief_days_credited` and its `shortfall_days`, processed in `week_start_date` ascending order. **Fourth**, because those weeks' shortfalls changed, the employee's `cumulative_shortfall_days`, `consecutive_breach_weeks`, `non_compliance_pressure` and `compliance_state` are recomputed. **Fifth**, because the pressure changed, the employee's open case — provided `discretionary_hold` is false — has its `stage` reset from the new pressure, and a case leaving the formal-action band decrements the team's `formal_action_count` and therefore the cycle's `formal_action_total`.

**Credited relief** for one week is a waterfall over the employee's approved requests. Collect the requests whose closed relief window intersects any of the week's seven dates, and order them by `created_at` ascending — that order is pinned, and it is why the exception collection is listed in the same order. Walk them in that order, keeping a running total. For each request, count how many of the week's dates fall on a Monday through Friday **and** inside that request's inclusive window; that count is the request's business-day overlap for this week. Add to the running total the smaller of the request's `granted_relief_days` and that business-day overlap. When every request has been walked, the week's `relief_days_credited` is the smaller of the running total and the week's `required_days` — the system never credits more relief than the week actually demanded. Only `APPROVED` requests participate; `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED` and `WITHDRAWN` rows are excluded.

**Revocation** is the compensating action for an approval and reverses it exactly. A request must be `APPROVED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_APPROVED` (409), which is also what a second revocation receives. The caller supplies a `closure_reason`. The request's `status` becomes `REVOKED` and its `granted_relief_days` returns to 0; the employee's `approved_relief_days_total` is recomputed, undoing the second link of the approval chain; the same set of `FINALIZED` and `LOCKED` weeks recomputes `relief_days_credited` and `shortfall_days` in `week_start_date` ascending order; the employee's `cumulative_shortfall_days`, `consecutive_breach_weeks`, `non_compliance_pressure` and `compliance_state` are recomputed; and the open case's `stage` — again only when `discretionary_hold` is false — is reset from the restored pressure, so a case re-entering the formal-action band re-increments `formal_action_count` and `formal_action_total`. Every conserved quantity returns to its pre-approval value with no drift: that is the contract revocation is held to.

### 3.7. Remote Work Locations: Two Reviews That Run in Parallel

Letting someone work from a different jurisdiction is not a seating decision — it creates tax nexus, may demand a professional licence the company does not hold, and can put regulated data on a network nobody has assessed. This system therefore holds a relocation request open across **two independent review dimensions** that advance on the same row, in either order, and joins them before any decision may be taken.

An `Employee` filing for themselves, a `PeopleManager` or an `HRBusinessPartner` names the `employee_id`, the `jurisdiction_code` of the proposed location — `US_CA`, `US_TX`, `US_NY`, `IE`, `IN` or `BR` — the `data_classification_tier` naming the most sensitive data the role handles (`PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED`), and the `proposed_start_date` on which the arrangement would begin. Two prerequisites are checked against the employee's classification record: an employee holding no `APPROVED` classification at all is refused with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_CLASSIFICATION` (409), and an employee whose `current_eligibility_class` is `ONSITE_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_CLASS_NOT_REMOTE_ELIGIBLE` (409) — you cannot relocate a role that policy says must be on site.

A new request lands in `SUBMITTED` status with both `tax_review_status` and `security_review_status` at `PENDING`, with `licensing_gap_count`, `control_gap_count` and `tax_nexus_registration_cost_cents` at 0, with `final_risk_score` at 0 and `decision_reason` null, and with `preliminary_risk_score` scored once and never recomputed thereafter.

**Risk scoring** starts from a base built from the two creation inputs. Jurisdiction contributes 6 points for `US_TX`, 10 for `US_CA`, 12 for `US_NY`, 18 for `IE`, 26 for `IN` and 30 for `BR`. Data tier contributes 0 points for `PUBLIC`, 8 for `INTERNAL`, 18 for `CONFIDENTIAL` and 30 for `RESTRICTED`. The base is the sum of those two contributions. The **preliminary** variant is that base alone, clamped to a ceiling of 100 points, and it deliberately ignores licensing, control and nexus-cost penalties because none of them has been verified yet. The **final** variant, scored only at the join, adds three capped penalties on top of the same base: a licensing penalty of 7 points for each licensing gap found, capped at 21 points; a control penalty of 9 points for each data-security control gap found, capped at 27 points; and a nexus penalty of one point for every whole 250,000 cents of estimated registration cost — that is, one point per 2,500 dollars, with any remainder discarded rather than rounded — capped at 12 points. The sum of base and all three penalties is then clamped to the same 100-point ceiling. For identical creation inputs the final score is therefore never below the preliminary score, and the two differ whenever any gap count or nexus cost is non-zero. Only the final score is ever compared against the approval ceiling.

The **tax and licensing dimension** is recorded by an `HRBusinessPartner` or the `WorkplaceProgramOffice`, supplying a `review_outcome` of `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED`, the `licensing_gap_count` as an integer from 0 through 20, and the `tax_nexus_registration_cost_cents` as an integer from 0 through 100,000,000. A recorded cost above the per-request budget of 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — is refused with `ERR_TAX_NEXUS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409). Recording this dimension sets `tax_review_status` from the outcome, stores both figures, moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` if it has not moved already, and leaves `security_review_status` completely untouched.

The **data-security dimension** is recorded by a `ComplianceAuditor` or the `WorkplaceProgramOffice`, supplying a `review_outcome` of `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` and the `control_gap_count` as an integer from 0 through 20. It sets `security_review_status` from the outcome, stores that figure, moves `status` from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` if it has not moved already, and leaves `tax_review_status` completely untouched. Whichever dimension is written first is the fork point that moves the request into `UNDER_REVIEW`; the second one leaves `status` at `UNDER_REVIEW` unchanged, which is a genuine self-loop on the overall state while the two dimensions evolve independently. Each dimension may be recorded exactly once: an attempt against a dimension that has already left `PENDING`, or against a request that has already reached `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409). Every recording advances the request's `version`.

The join belongs exclusively to the `WorkplaceProgramOffice`, which supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`. A request that has already reached the terminal `APPROVED` or `DENIED` status accepts no further decision and is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_DECISION_FINAL` (409). A request with either dimension still at `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE` (409) — the fork must join before anyone decides. An approval of a `RESTRICTED`-tier request is only permitted into the three approved residency jurisdictions `US_CA`, `US_TX` and `US_NY`; any other jurisdiction is refused with `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_PROHIBITED` (409). And an approval whose final risk score reaches the approval ceiling of 60 points is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_RISK_CEILING` (409) — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 60 is a refusal and 59 is approvable. On that particular refusal the request stays `UNDER_REVIEW`, and `decision_reason` is not written at all.

`final_risk_score` is written on both branches of a decision that is actually taken. An approval where both dimensions are `CLEARED` sets `status` to `APPROVED` and `decision_reason` to `BOTH_REVIEWS_CLEARED`. A denial — and equally an approval where either dimension is `BLOCKED` — sets `status` to `DENIED`, with `decision_reason` recorded as `REVIEW_BLOCKED` when either dimension is `BLOCKED` and as `RISK_CEILING_REACHED` otherwise.

### 3.8. Space: Neighborhoods, Seat Allocations, Desks and Chargeback

The space model exists so that expected attendance and physical seats can be reconciled honestly. It has three layers: a **neighborhood** is a zone of the floor plan; a **seat allocation** reserves a slice of that zone for a team over a run of ISO weeks; and a **desk assignment** puts a named employee at a numbered desk over a run of calendar dates.

A `WorkplaceProgramOffice` operator creates a neighborhood under a `policy_cycle_id`, designed around an `anchor_team_id`, with a `neighborhood_code` of 1 to 24 characters unique within the cycle (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEIGHBORHOOD_CODE`) and a `site_code` of 1 to 16 characters. It declares `seat_count` as an integer from 1 through 500, `buffer_seats` as an integer of at least 0 that must be strictly smaller than `seat_count` so that usable capacity is positive, and the contiguous desk index band `desk_index_from` and `desk_index_to`. The band must be well formed and exactly the right width: `desk_index_from` at least 1, `desk_index_to` at least `desk_index_from`, and the count of indices from one to the other inclusive equal to `seat_count`. An inverted band, a band narrower or wider than the seat count, or a buffer at least as large as the seat count is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A new zone starts with `committed_seats` and `peak_committed_seats` at 0, `utilization_pct` at 0.00, and `allocation_state` at `OPEN` — or already `FROZEN`, when the parent cycle is `LOCKED`.

Seat allocations are reserved by naming the `neighborhood_id`, the `team_id`, the `allocated_seats` as an integer from 1 through 500, the half-open week window `window_start_week` and `window_end_week`, and the stale-version guard `expected_neighborhood_version` of §2.8. The window must be non-empty in ISO-week ordinal terms — `window_end_week` strictly later than `window_start_week` — and both labels well formed, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). Team and zone must belong to the same policy cycle, or the request is refused with `ERR_TEAM_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH` (409). And the team's entitlement is enforced week by week: for every ISO week in the requested window, the sum of `allocated_seats` over that team's non-`RELEASED` allocations covering that week, plus the requested seats, must not exceed the team's `entitled_seats`; a breach in any single week is refused with `ERR_SEAT_ENTITLEMENT_EXCEEDED` (409). A successful reservation lands in `RESERVED` status with `release_reason` null, `line_chargeback_cents` at 0, and `weeks_in_window` set to the number of ISO weeks in the half-open interval — the ordinal of `window_end_week` minus the ordinal of `window_start_week`. It also advances the parent neighborhood's `version` by one.

Committing an allocation is what actually consumes capacity, and only a `COMMITTED` allocation counts anywhere. Commit requires `RESERVED` status: an allocation already `COMMITTED` is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_RESERVED` (409), while an allocation that has been `RELEASED` — a terminal status neither trigger can ever apply to again — is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL` (409). The zone must not be `FROZEN`. The capacity gate is aggregate, per week, and half-open: for every ISO week in this allocation's window, the sum of `allocated_seats` over the **other** `COMMITTED` allocations on the same zone whose own half-open windows cover that week, plus this allocation's seats, must not exceed the zone's usable capacity of `seat_count` minus `buffer_seats`. Landing exactly on capacity is permitted; exceeding it in even one week is refused with `ERR_NEIGHBORHOOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Each individual allocation may sit comfortably inside capacity — it is only the overlapping sum that breaches it. A successful commit sets `status` to `COMMITTED`, writes the allocation's chargeback line, and recomputes the zone's four derived fields.

Releasing is the compensating action, and it is legal from both `RESERVED` and `COMMITTED`; only a `RELEASED` allocation refuses, with `ERR_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL`. The caller supplies a `release_reason`. Releasing a merely reserved allocation sets `status` to `RELEASED` and records the reason. Releasing a committed one does that and three things more: `line_chargeback_cents` returns to 0, every desk assignment on that allocation whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` transitions to `VACATED` with `vacated_at` stamped at the current logical instant, and the zone's derived fields are recomputed downward. The zone's `committed_seats` returns to exactly what it was before the commit — that conservation is the guarantee release is held to.

**Zone load** is computed over the cycle's weeks. Usable capacity is `seat_count` minus `buffer_seats`. The cycle's weeks are every ISO week whose Monday falls inside the cycle's inclusive `effective_from` through `effective_to` range. The load of a given week is the sum of `allocated_seats` over the zone's `COMMITTED` allocations whose half-open window covers that week — that is, whose `window_start_week` ordinal is at most the week's ordinal and whose `window_end_week` ordinal is strictly greater than it. `peak_committed_seats` is the largest such weekly load anywhere in the cycle window, or 0 when the cycle window contains no weeks at all. `committed_seats` is the load of the ISO week the logical clock currently sits in, or 0 when that week falls outside the cycle window. `utilization_pct` is 100 multiplied by `peak_committed_seats`, divided by usable capacity, rounded half up to two decimals. And `allocation_state` is `FROZEN` whenever the parent cycle's posture is `LOCKED`; otherwise `TIGHT` when `utilization_pct` has reached 90.00; otherwise `OPEN`. `RESERVED` and `RELEASED` allocations contribute nothing to any week's load.

**Chargeback** is computed in two steps whose rounding order changes the answer, so the order is pinned. Step one runs on each commit and writes that allocation's `line_chargeback_cents`. Take the base seat rate of 4,333 cents per seat per week, multiply by `allocated_seats`, multiply by `weeks_in_window`, then apply the team's volume discount as a basis-point factor keyed on its `expected_days_in_office_per_week`: a five-day team pays the full 10,000 basis points, a four-day team 9,500, a three-day team 9,000, and a team at two, one or zero days 8,500. Divide that basis-point product by 10,000 and round the result half up to a whole number of cents. Step two, described in §3.9, sums the already-rounded lines and never re-rounds. To see why that matters, consider two lines: a four-day team with 5 seats over a 2-week window computes 4,333 times 5 times 2 times 9,500, divided by 10,000, which is 41,163.5 and rounds to 41,164; and a two-day team with 2 seats over a 5-week window computes 4,333 times 2 times 5 times 8,500, divided by 10,000, which is 36,830.5 and rounds to 36,831. The pinned total is 77,995. Summing the unrounded values first would give 77,994.0 and a total of 77,994, which is wrong.

A desk is assigned manually by a `WorkplaceProgramOffice` operator or a `PeopleManager`, naming the `seat_allocation_id`, the `employee_id`, the `desk_index`, and the inclusive occupancy dates `start_date` and `end_date`. An end date earlier than the start date is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). The employee must be on the allocation's team and the `desk_index` must fall inside the zone's declared band, or the request is refused with `ERR_TEAM_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH` (409). Every ISO week touched by the closed occupancy interval must fall inside the allocation's half-open week window; a request whose `end_date` lands in the exact week named by `window_end_week` is **outside** the window, because that bound is exclusive, and is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (409). A successful assignment starts in `ASSIGNED` status with `assignment_mode` `MANUAL` and `vacated_at` null.

Desks are exclusive per occupant and carry a one-day sanitation changeover, which makes their overlap rule deliberately different from the aggregate, half-open seat-allocation window. Against any existing `ASSIGNED` assignment on the same zone and the same `desk_index`, a new closed interval is rejected when the new start date is no later than the existing end date plus one day **and** the existing start date is no later than the new end date plus one day. Back-to-back assignments that merely abut therefore still conflict, and so do assignments separated by a single clear day; the refusal is `ERR_DESK_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT` (409). `VACATED` assignments never conflict with anything.

The automatic path lets the server choose. The caller supplies only the `employee_id`, `start_date` and `end_date`, and the server picks both the zone and the desk under a pinned selector. Eligibility first: consider the `COMMITTED` allocations of the employee's own team whose half-open week window contains **every** ISO week touched by the requested closed date range, and keep only those whose zone's `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `TIGHT` — a `FROZEN` zone and any `RESERVED` or `RELEASED` allocation are excluded. Then, for each surviving allocation and each desk index in its zone's band, the pair is a candidate only when no `ASSIGNED` assignment on that zone and index fails the one-day changeover predicate above against the requested dates. If the candidate pool is empty the call is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DESK` (409). Otherwise the candidates are ranked: zones whose `anchor_team_id` is the employee's own team come first; among those, lower `utilization_pct` comes first; among those, lower `desk_index` comes first; remaining ties break on the zone's `created_at` ascending and then the allocation's `created_at` ascending. The winner becomes the assignment's `seat_allocation_id` and `desk_index`, and the row is created in `ASSIGNED` status with `assignment_mode` `AUTO`. No random value and no entity `id` participates in the ranking, so the same world always yields the same desk.

Vacating a desk requires `ASSIGNED` status — a second vacate, or a vacate of an already-`VACATED` row, is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). It sets `status` to `VACATED`, stamps `vacated_at` at the current logical instant, and immediately makes that `desk_index` available again to the automatic selector for overlapping date ranges, subject to the changeover buffer. The same `ASSIGNED → VACATED` move happens without any caller when the parent allocation is released or the occupant separates.

### 3.9. Recalibration: Measuring the Cycle and Changing the Expectation

A hybrid-work policy is only defensible if the organisation periodically re-examines whether the expectation it set is working, what it costs, and whether it lands evenly across the workforce. That is what a **recalibration review** is: a `WorkplaceProgramOffice` drafts a proposed change to the days-in-office expectation, freezes a measurement snapshot behind it, and then a second person adopts or rejects it.

A review is drafted against a `policy_cycle_id` with a `review_label` of 1 to 40 characters unique within that cycle — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_LABEL` (409) — and a `proposed_days_in_office_delta`, a signed integer from -2 through 2 naming the change that would be applied to every team on adoption. A new review lands in `DRAFT` state with `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` null, `decision_reason` null, `collaboration_index` and `equity_variance_pct` at 0.00, `total_space_chargeback_cents` and `qualifying_band_count` at 0, and `meets_collaboration_target` and `equity_flag` both false. Every one of those measurement fields stays at its zero value until the submit step; a `DRAFT` review carries no measurements at all.

Submitting the review is what freezes the snapshot, and it requires `DRAFT` state — a second submit, or a submit against a `SUBMITTED`, `ADOPTED` or `REJECTED` review, is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT` (409). It also requires the parent cycle to be in `RECALIBRATING` posture, per §2.9. A successful submit sets `state` to `SUBMITTED`, records `submitted_by_user_id` as the calling `X-User-ID`, and writes four measurements.

The **collaboration index** measures how much of the demanded office presence the organisation actually achieved, weighted by team size, and its rounding order is pinned because pooling instead of averaging gives a different number. Consider only the cycle's teams that have at least one settled attendance week — a week whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `LOCKED` — for at least one of their employees; teams with no settled week are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator entirely, and if that leaves no teams the index is exactly 0.00 rather than a division by zero. Process the surviving teams in `team_code` ascending order for a deterministic accumulation. For each team, sum `credited_days` across all settled weeks of all its employees to get the credited total, and sum `required_days` across the same weeks to get the demanded total. The team's sub-score is 100 multiplied by the credited total, divided by the demanded total or by 1 if that total is zero, **rounded half up to two decimals right there**. Then take the headcount-weighted mean of those already-rounded sub-scores: multiply each sub-score by its team's `headcount_planned`, sum those products, divide by the sum of the same teams' `headcount_planned`, and round half up to two decimals once more. Two teams with sub-scores of 40.00 at 25 planned heads and 66.67 at 12 planned heads yield 48.65; pooling the same underlying 4 credited days out of 8 demanded across both teams instead would yield 50.00, which is wrong. `meets_collaboration_target` is then set to true when the index has reached the cycle's `collaboration_target_score`, and false otherwise.

The **space chargeback total** is step two of the calculation whose step one is described in §3.8. Take every seat allocation whose `status` is `COMMITTED` on any neighborhood belonging to this policy cycle, order them by `created_at` ascending, and sum their `line_chargeback_cents` exactly as those lines were already rounded. The sum is written to `total_space_chargeback_cents` and is **never** re-rounded — no second rounding step exists anywhere in this calculation. `RESERVED` and `RELEASED` allocations contribute nothing, because their `line_chargeback_cents` is 0.

The **equity spread** asks whether compliance outcomes differ across demographic bands, and it applies small-cell suppression so that a band of one person cannot be reported on. The population is every employee on a team of this cycle whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; `ON_LEAVE` and `SEPARATED` employees are excluded from both the count and the compliant count. For each of the four bands `BAND_A`, `BAND_B`, `BAND_C` and `BAND_D`, count the population in that band; a band holding fewer than 2 such employees is dropped entirely and contributes to nothing. For each surviving band, count how many of its members have `compliance_state` `COMPLIANT`, and the band's rate is 100 multiplied by that compliant count, divided by the band's population count, rounded half up to two decimals. `qualifying_band_count` is the number of surviving bands, considered in band-name ascending order. When fewer than 2 bands qualify, `equity_variance_pct` is exactly 0.00 — not null and not an error — because a spread between fewer than two points is meaningless. Otherwise `equity_variance_pct` is the highest qualifying band rate minus the lowest, rounded half up to two decimals. `equity_flag` is set to true only when at least 2 bands qualified **and** the spread is strictly greater than the cycle's `equity_variance_threshold_pct`; a spread exactly equal to the threshold does not raise the flag.

The decision step is open to the `WorkplaceProgramOffice` and to a `ComplianceAuditor`, carries a `decision` of `ADOPT` or `REJECT`, an optional `decision_reason`, and the `expected_policy_cycle_version` guard of §2.8. It requires `SUBMITTED` state — a `DRAFT` review, or a second decision on an `ADOPTED` or `REJECTED` one, is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED` (409) — and it requires the parent cycle to still be in `RECALIBRATING` posture. The caller must not be the person recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`; that self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Two substantive gates then apply only to an adoption, and their relative order is fixed: an adoption whose delta would drive any team's `expected_days_in_office_per_week` below the cycle's `days_in_office_floor` is refused with `ERR_BELOW_DAYS_FLOOR` (409), and an adoption of a review whose `equity_flag` is true is refused with `ERR_EQUITY_FLAG_BLOCKS_ADOPTION` (409). Because the equity gate sits at the very bottom of the precedence order of §2.10, a review that breaches both reports the days-floor error.

The two branches diverge in exactly one respect and are otherwise identical, and both must be implemented with equal care. On `ADOPT`, `state` becomes `ADOPTED` and every team in the cycle has its `expected_days_in_office_per_week` shifted by `proposed_days_in_office_delta` and then clamped into the range 0 through 5 — a team already at 5 receiving a delta of +1 stays at 5, and a team at 0 receiving -1 stays at 0 — with the teams processed in `team_code` ascending order, and each shifted team immediately re-running the five-step seat-entitlement derivation of §3.1 so that `expected_daily_attendance`, `base_entitlement`, `buffer_seats_entitled` and `entitled_seats` all reflect the new expectation. On `REJECT`, `state` becomes `REJECTED` and not a single team is touched: no expectation moves and no entitlement is recomputed. Everything else is common to both branches — `decided_by_user_id` is recorded as the calling `X-User-ID`, `decision_reason` is stored if supplied, the parent cycle's `posture` returns to `OPEN`, and the cycle's `version` advances by one. Closing the recalibration window is therefore not conditional on the outcome: a rejected review reopens the cycle for ordinary operations exactly as an adopted one does. Note that adoption changes team entitlements without touching any existing seat allocation, so an allocation that was legal when reserved may exceed the team's reduced entitlement afterwards; the entitlement gate is enforced at reservation time and no retroactive sweep occurs.
