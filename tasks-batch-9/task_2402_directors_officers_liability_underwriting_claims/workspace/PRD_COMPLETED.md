# Directors And Officers Liability API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Management liability is the line of business where underwriting judgment and claims judgment are least separable. A director-and-officer risk is priced from what a company discloses about its own financial controls and board structure, from the litigation already circling it, and from the sector it operates in — and then, years later, the same file has to answer whether a particular lawsuit was first made inside a claims-made window, whose defense bills the insurer will advance, and how much of a settlement the insurer will fund when the insured refuses to sign. Today this work is carried out across spreadsheets, broker emails and wording redlines, and the numbers that matter — the entity-securities capacity already committed to one insured, the share of a tower written on difference-in-conditions terms, the retention still unabsorbed on a live claim — exist only in somebody's head.

This document specifies the API for a management liability insurer that runs the whole file in one place: intake of an insured and its disclosures, a repeatable risk assessment, quotation with an auditable premium build-up, exclusion wording negotiated round by round between the underwriter and the insured's risk manager, binding into a policy with Side A, Side B and Side C insuring agreements, participation in an excess tower, and then the claims side — notice under a claims-made policy, circumstance reporting with relation-back, defense cost advancement allocated between covered and uncovered matters, settlement consent with a hammer clause, and run-off cover when the insured is acquired.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, every derived amount is reproducible: the order in which loads, credits, caps and roundings are applied is fixed, so two readers of this document arrive at the same cent. Second, time is explicit and server-controlled: no rule reads the wall clock. Deadlines, recency windows and expiries are all measured against a logical clock that an operator sets, and nothing expires or activates on its own until an operator asks the system to advance its epoch.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API and is assumed, without repetition, by the workflow narratives in section 3. The system is mounted under the `/dno` path prefix.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user is acting. Four roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Underwriter`, `RiskManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. If either header is absent or is the empty string, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those four strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint (see the table in section 4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The four roles divide the work by segregation of duties. An `Underwriter` is the insurer's underwriting authority: it registers insureds, files their disclosure packages and litigation history, opens submissions, runs the risk assessment, issues quotes, opens exclusion wording for negotiation, subscribes tower layers and seals the tower. A `RiskManager` represents the insured organisation: it negotiates wording from the buyer's side, binds an accepted quote, pays premium, gives notice of claims and circumstances, submits defense counsel invoices and answers settlement recommendations. A `ClaimsAdjuster` handles the claim itself: maturing circumstance reports, recording coverage decisions, allocating and advancing defense invoices, recommending settlements and funding resolutions. A `ComplianceOfficer` administers the portfolio: maintaining an insured's control posture and capacity cap, cancelling policies, issuing run-off endorsements, advancing the epoch and setting the logical clock. Every read (`GET`) endpoint is open to all four roles.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they determine what `X-User-ID` is compared against:

- When `X-User-Role` is `RiskManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Insured`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Insured` compare the resource's owning insured to `X-User-ID`: for a quote, the owning insured is reached through the quote's submission; for an exclusion term, through the term's quote and then that quote's submission; for a policy, through the policy's own `insured_id`; for a claim or a circumstance report, through its policy; for a settlement proposal, through its claim and then that claim's policy. A `RiskManager` acting on a resource owned by a different insured is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — a quote carries `issued_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of the underwriter who issued it, kept for audit only — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on a claim whose coverage decision that adjuster made, and as `recommended_by_user_id` on a settlement proposal that adjuster recommended, and it is compared only against those two recorded values — never against an entity `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server neither records it on any resource nor compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of section 2.4 rather than from the host's wall clock, so it equals the clock's current reading.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned in `data` is strictly less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a narrative states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with insertion order breaking ties between records that share a `created_at` — an ordering the whole document relies on wherever it speaks of the earliest or the latest record. The response envelope's `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` on every list response.

Several list endpoints also accept optional filters, which are ordinary query parameters combined conjunctively when more than one is supplied. Submissions may be filtered by `insured_id` and by `status`. Policies may be filtered by `submission_id`, `insured_id`, `quote_id` and `coverage_status`. Layers may be filtered by `policy_id`, `underlying_layer_id` and `status`. Claims may be filtered by `policy_id`, `circumstance_report_id` and `status`. Every filter is matched by exact equality against the stored field and its value is never validated against an enum or checked for existence, so an unrecognised value simply matches no record; `total_count` counts the filtered set, and `limit`/`offset` are applied to it.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Clients never supply an `id`, and the identifier carries no type prefix. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits stamped from the logical clock at insert.

Most resources — insureds, disclosure packages, submissions, quotes, exclusion terms, policies, coverage parts, layers, claims, circumstance reports, defense invoices, settlement proposals and run-off endorsements — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed from the logical clock on every successful write to that record, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the record is inserted and increases by exactly 1 on every successful write to it. `version` is the token used for optimistic concurrency control described in section 2.6, and it advances for derived-field recomputations just as it does for client-driven edits.

Litigation events are the single exception. A litigation event is write-once at creation: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, exposes no update endpoint and takes no concurrency token. Its one derived field, `weight_units`, is rewritten only by a scoring run, as described in section 3.2.

The logical clock is the system's only source of time. Before any operator sets it, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — a fixed startup default that never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ComplianceOfficer` moves it by submitting `to_iso8601` to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which returns the new `current_utc` and performs no entity transitions of its own. Every deadline, notice window, recency band, expiry and audit timestamp in this document is measured against the clock's current reading, and the clock also stamps `created_at` and `updated_at` on subsequent writes. Time-driven state changes are never applied by the clock endpoint; they are applied only when an operator advances the epoch, as described in section 2.11.

### 2.5. Monetary Precision and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are decimal quantities carried to 2 decimal places, and every rounding in this document uses the round-half-up rule. A monetary amount arriving in a request body is itself rounded half-up to 2 decimal places on receipt, before any rule reads it, whereas fractional inputs such as `participation_pct` and `premium_impact_pct` are carried exactly as supplied. Amounts are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, not strings.

Where a calculation has intermediate results, the point at which rounding happens is part of the specification and is stated in the narrative that defines the calculation. Two habits are enforced throughout. First, intermediate factors — exposure units, sector multipliers, recency factors, tail factors, retention credits, participation fractions and capacity utilisation ratios — are held unrounded, and rounding is applied to the money that comes out of the formula rather than to the factors that go into it. Second, when a total must be split into parts that have to add back up to it, each part is rounded to 2 decimal places first and a single residual adjustment is applied afterwards, rather than rounding the total once at the end.

Risk scores and their component subscores are also carried to 2 decimal places with round-half-up. Day counts derived from two instants are floored to whole days before any ratio is taken.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Four write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent record. In each case the field is a required, non-optional input, and a mismatch between the submitted value and the parent's stored `version` rejects the request with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). No endpoint other than these four accepts a version field, and those four never treat it as optional.

- `PATCH /dno/insureds/{id}` requires `expected_insured_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Insured`. An insured's version advances on every bind and on every release of Side C capacity, so a compliance officer working from an earlier read would otherwise overwrite a capacity cap that has already been re-derived. If the submitted value does not equal the insured's current `version`, the update is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /dno/submissions/{id}/quotes` requires `expected_submission_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Submission`. A new quote legitimately supersedes any prior `OFFERED` quote, so this operation cannot be blocked by a state precondition; the version check is what makes the supersede deliberate rather than accidental. If the submitted value does not equal the submission's current `version`, the issuance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /dno/claims/{id}/settlement-proposals` requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Claim`. A newer proposal legitimately supersedes the prior recommendation, so without the check a second recommendation would succeed on a claim whose defense spend and remaining retention have already moved. If the submitted value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the recommendation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /dno/defense-invoices/{id}/allocate` requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Claim` that owns the invoice. Allocation consumes the claim's remaining retention, which any earlier allocation has already reduced, so the caller must confirm that the retention baseline it observed is still current. If the submitted value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the allocation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

### 2.7. The Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

When more than one rule would reject the same request, the ordering below decides which error is returned: checks are evaluated in ascending order and the first one violated determines the response. The ordering is normative, not descriptive — a request that violates both a state rule and a fiscal rule always returns the state error. The scopes given are exhaustive; a check listed against a set of endpoints is applied on exactly those endpoints.

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.

3. Path resource existence, left to right — the not-found error of the first missing resource, on every endpoint carrying a path parameter (see section 2.8).
4. Ownership, for a `RiskManager` only — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on `POST /dno/quotes/{id}/accept`, `POST /dno/exclusion-terms/{id}/counter`, `POST /dno/exclusion-terms/{id}/resolve`, `POST /dno/policies/{id}/premium-payments`, `POST /dno/policies/{id}/circumstance-reports`, `POST /dno/policies/{id}/claims`, `POST /dno/claims/{id}/defense-invoices` and `POST /dno/settlement-proposals/{id}/respond` — those eight endpoints and no others.

On a layer subscription, band geometry is checked before money: overlap, then oversubscription, then the difference-in-conditions share, then primary-layer immutability, and only then whether the placed limit would breach the programme target.

### 2.8. Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Endpoints address resources through path parameters, and where a path names more than one resource the identifiers are resolved strictly from left to right. The first identifier that matches no stored record ends the request immediately with that resource's own not-found error and HTTP 404: `ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND` for an insured-scoped path, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` for a submission-scoped path, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` for a quote-scoped path, `ERR_EXCLUSION_TERM_NOT_FOUND` for an exclusion-term path, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` for a policy-scoped path, `ERR_LAYER_NOT_FOUND` for a layer path, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for a claim-scoped path, `ERR_CIRCUMSTANCE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` for a circumstance-report path, `ERR_DEFENSE_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` for a defense-invoice path, and `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND` for a settlement-proposal path.

Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved on the same principle. An `insured_id` in a submission body that matches no insured, and a `submission_id` that matches no submission, are reported as `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`; an `underlying_layer_id` that matches no layer of the same policy is reported as `ERR_LAYER_NOT_FOUND`. Existence of a body identifier is established once the request-shape check of ladder step 5 has passed, and always before any state, business or fiscal rule is evaluated.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity: Policies, Coverage Parts, Layers and Insured Capacity

The policy is the hub of a graph, and a single write frequently propagates through several records. Whenever it does, every record touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 — including records the client never named. Three cascades recur.

Erosion flows upward. A coverage part's `eroded_usd` rises when defense cost is advanced against it or when indemnity is split into it; its `remaining_limit_usd` is always its `limit_usd` less its `eroded_usd`, floored at 0. A policy's `total_eroded_usd` is the sum of `eroded_usd` across its coverage parts, and its `aggregate_remaining_usd` is `shared_aggregate_limit_usd` less `total_eroded_usd`. When a part's `eroded_usd` reaches or passes its `limit_usd` it moves from `ACTIVE` to `EXHAUSTED` and its `remaining_limit_usd` is set to 0; an exhausted part is skipped by every later waterfall, which is what pushes cost into `unfunded_excess_usd` rather than silently overspending the limit. The same erosion then reaches the tower: every layer of the policy that is still `ATTACHED` and whose `attachment_point_usd` is below the policy's `total_eroded_usd` moves to `EXHAUSTED`, and the policy's `tower_posture` re-derives to `ERODED`.

Release flows downward. When a policy's `coverage_status` becomes `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED`, every coverage part still `ACTIVE` moves to `RELEASED` and every layer still `ATTACHED` moves to `RELEASED`. A released Side C part leaves the insured's committed entity-securities exposure, which restores the insured's available capacity, re-derives its `capacity_posture` and lowers its `open_policy_count`; released layers leave the policy's `placed_limit_usd`, so `tower_posture` re-derives to `RELEASED`.

Reversal is possible in exactly one place. A denial of coverage recorded after defense money has already gone out claws that money back: advanced invoices are reversed against the coverage parts they eroded, restoring each part's `eroded_usd` and `remaining_limit_usd`, returning an `EXHAUSTED` part to `ACTIVE` when its `eroded_usd` falls back below its `limit_usd`, re-deriving the policy's `total_eroded_usd`, `aggregate_remaining_usd` and `tower_posture`, and returning layers whose `attachment_point_usd` is greater than or equal to the restored `total_eroded_usd` from `EXHAUSTED` back to `ATTACHED`. After such a clawback the identity that a policy's `shared_aggregate_limit_usd` equals its `aggregate_remaining_usd` plus its `total_eroded_usd` still holds, and `total_eroded_usd` equals its pre-advancement value exactly.

### 2.10. Parallel State Dimensions on a Policy

A policy carries two independent state dimensions that advance on their own axes. `coverage_status` is the coverage dimension and walks `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE`, then one of `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED` or, on a change of control, `RUNOFF` and from there `EXPIRED`. `premium_status` is the billing dimension and takes `INVOICED`, `PAID` or `DELINQUENT`. Neither dimension constrains the other's transition: recording a premium payment leaves `coverage_status` untouched, activating a policy leaves `premium_status` untouched, and a single epoch advance may move both dimensions of the same policy in one pass — for instance `coverage_status` from `INCEPTED` to `ACTIVE` while `premium_status` moves from `INVOICED` to `DELINQUENT`.

### 2.11. The Epoch Advance

No deadline in this system fires by itself. Setting the logical clock forward records a new time but changes no record. All time-driven transitions are applied when a `ComplianceOfficer` advances the epoch through `POST /dno/ops-runs`, which sweeps the portfolio in one pass and reports what it did. Its response body carries `epoch`, the new epoch counter; `current_utc`, the clock reading the sweep used; and eight counters — `activated_policy_count`, `expired_policy_count`, `delinquent_policy_count`, `lapsed_quote_count`, `expired_submission_count`, `reverted_submission_count`, `lapsed_circumstance_report_count` and `expired_runoff_count`. The sweep takes no request body.

The sweep applies the following, and only the following: policies whose `period_start` the clock has reached move from `INCEPTED` to `ACTIVE`; policies whose `period_end` the clock has reached move from `INCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`; policies in `RUNOFF` whose `runoff_end_date` the clock has reached move to `EXPIRED`, and their run-off endorsement moves from `ENDORSED` to `EXPIRED` in the same pass; policies whose `premium_status` is `INVOICED` and whose `premium_due_date` the clock has reached move to `DELINQUENT`; quotes in `OFFERED` whose `quote_valid_until` the clock has reached move to `LAPSED`; submissions in `DRAFT`, `SCORED` or `QUOTED` whose `expires_at` the clock has reached move to `EXPIRED`, lapsing any of their still-`OFFERED` quotes as they go; submissions in `QUOTED` that carry at least one quote and whose every quote is `LAPSED` or `SUPERSEDED` revert to `SCORED`; and circumstance reports in `REPORTED` whose `reporting_deadline` the clock has reached move to `LAPSED`. Every policy the sweep expires or releases also triggers the release and capacity cascades of section 2.9, so the affected insureds' capacity fields and the affected policies' `tower_posture` are recomputed within the same call. Each comparison against the clock is inclusive: a deadline is reached when the clock reading is greater than or equal to it. The counters count transitions rather than records, so a policy the sweep both activates and expires in one pass is counted in `activated_policy_count` and in `expired_policy_count` alike, and a policy it expires while also flagging the premium is counted in `expired_policy_count` and `delinquent_policy_count` alike. Audit accounting is per sweep, not per transition: every record the sweep writes to has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 for the pass taken as a whole, however many of the transitions and cascade steps above reached that same record — a policy that moves from `INCEPTED` to `ACTIVE` and from `INVOICED` to `DELINQUENT` in one sweep sees its `version` rise by 1, not by 2, and a policy the sweep expires sees its `version` rise by 1 even though the release cascade also passes over it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Risk into the House: Insureds, Disclosures and Litigation History

An `Underwriter` opens a file by registering the prospective insured organisation. Registration takes the organisation's `legal_name`, its `sector_code`, its `exchange_listing`, its reported `market_cap_usd`, its `fiscal_year_end` written as a month and day in `MM-DD` form (stored verbatim as the string supplied, since the server checks only that it is a string and never validates that shape), and the `entity_capacity_cap_usd` this insurer is prepared to commit in entity-securities limit to that one organisation. `sector_code` is one of `TECH`, `BIOTECH`, `FINANCIAL`, `ENERGY`, `INDUSTRIAL` or `CONSUMER`; `exchange_listing` is one of `LISTED`, `PRIVATE` or `NONE`. Market capitalisation must be at least 0 — zero is legitimate for a `PRIVATE` or `NONE` listing — and the capacity cap must be strictly greater than 0. Two further fields are optional at registration: `control_status`, which defaults to `INDEPENDENT` and is otherwise `CHANGE_OF_CONTROL_ANNOUNCED` or `ACQUIRED`, and `change_of_control_date`, which defaults to null. Whenever `control_status` is anything other than `INDEPENDENT`, `change_of_control_date` must be supplied; omitting it is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. A freshly registered insured starts with `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd` at 0, `available_side_c_capacity_usd` equal to `entity_capacity_cap_usd`, `capacity_posture` `OPEN` and `open_policy_count` at 0.

A `ComplianceOfficer` maintains the record afterwards, and may revise `control_status`, `change_of_control_date`, `market_cap_usd` and `entity_capacity_cap_usd` — each of them optional in the update body, with `expected_insured_version` mandatory as described in section 2.6. The same conditional rule applies on update, and it reads only the request body: when the body sets `control_status` to anything other than `INDEPENDENT` it must also carry a non-null `change_of_control_date` in that same body — a date already stored on the insured does not satisfy it — or the update is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. Only the fields actually present in the body are written, and a field supplied explicitly as `null` is written as a null. Because the capacity cap is one of the inputs to the insured's committed-capacity arithmetic, every accepted update re-derives `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd`, `available_side_c_capacity_usd`, `capacity_posture` and `open_policy_count` before responding, and increments the insured's `version` by 1.

Financial disclosure arrives as a package per fiscal year. Each package names its `insured_id`, the `fiscal_year` it covers (which must be 2000 or later), and the `filed_date` on which it was filed, and then records the twelve underwriting facts the risk assessment reads: `restatement_flag`, `material_weakness_count` (at least 0), `going_concern_flag`, `negative_operating_cash_flow_flag`, `debt_to_equity` (at least 0), `independent_director_ratio` (a fraction from 0 through 1 inclusive), `dual_class_shares_flag`, `staggered_board_flag`, `combined_ceo_chair_flag` and `auditor_change_flag`. A newly filed package's `financial_subscore` and `governance_subscore` both read 0 and stay at 0 until a scoring run consumes the package.

Litigation history is filed matter by matter. Each litigation event names its `insured_id`, its `case_type` — one of `SECURITIES_CLASS_ACTION`, `DERIVATIVE`, `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT`, `MERGER_OBJECTION`, `EMPLOYMENT_PRACTICES` or `FIDUCIARY` — and the `filed_date` on which the matter was commenced. Three fields are optional: `resolved_date`, which defaults to null and whose absence is how the system knows the matter is still open; `indemnity_paid_usd`, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0, recording indemnity already paid on the matter by any carrier or by the insured itself; and `outcome`, which defaults to null and is otherwise `DISMISSED`, `SETTLED`, `JUDGMENT` or `PENDING`. As with a disclosure package, a newly filed event's `weight_units` reads 0 until a scoring run consumes it.

### 3.2. Assessing the Risk: Submissions and the Scoring Run

A submission is the underwriting request. The `Underwriter` opens it against an `insured_id` and states what the broker is asking for: `requested_limit_usd` (strictly greater than 0) as the primary shared aggregate limit, `requested_retention_usd` (at least 0) as the per-claim retention that will apply to Side B and Side C, `requested_side_c_sublimit_usd` (at least 0) as the entity-securities sublimit, where 0 means no Side C part will be created at all, `requested_program_target_limit_usd` as the total tower limit the broker intends to place across primary and excess layers, `requested_period_start` and `requested_period_end` as the wanted policy period, and `retroactive_date` as the prior-acts date before which wrongful acts are excluded. One optional field, `erp_days`, sets the extended reporting period in days added to the end of the period for claim notice and circumstance reporting; it defaults to 90 and must be at least 0. Two shape rules are enforced at intake: `requested_period_end` must be strictly later than `requested_period_start`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID`, and `requested_program_target_limit_usd` must be greater than or equal to `requested_limit_usd`, or it is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_TARGET_BELOW_LIMIT`. An `insured_id` naming no insured is reported as `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` once the request-shape check has passed and before either of those two shape rules is evaluated. A new submission is created with `status` `DRAFT`, with `expires_at` set to its `created_at` plus 90 days, with `risk_score`, `risk_band`, `score_variant` and `scored_at` all null, `disclosure_packages_considered` 0 and `litigation_units` 0.00.

Scoring a submission moves it `DRAFT → SCORED`, and running it again on an already-scored submission is a legitimate self-loop that leaves it `SCORED → SCORED`. Either way the request carries one field, `variant`, whose members are `INDICATIVE` and `UNDERWRITTEN`. A submission in any other state — notably `QUOTED`, `BOUND` or `EXPIRED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. Before any arithmetic, two disclosure gates apply. The insured must have at least one disclosure package, or the run is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`. And the latest package selected for the run must have a `filed_date` strictly later than the logical clock less 540 days — a staleness window of eighteen months of thirty days — or the run is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STALE`. The freshness gate applies on the re-score self-loop exactly as it does on the first run.

The input set depends on the variant. Under `INDICATIVE` — the broker's pre-underwriting indication — the run reads exactly one disclosure package: the one with the greatest `fiscal_year` for that insured, and where two packages share the greatest `fiscal_year`, the one created most recently — the more recently inserted one where `created_at` also ties. It reads only those litigation events whose `case_type` is `SECURITIES_CLASS_ACTION` or `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT`. Under `UNDERWRITTEN` — bound-authority underwriting — the run reads the three packages with the greatest `fiscal_year` (or all of them when the insured has fewer than three), ordered by `fiscal_year` descending and then by `created_at` descending, and it reads every litigation event of the insured whatever its `case_type`. Both variants read every selected record with no status filter of any kind. The count of packages consumed is recorded on the submission as `disclosure_packages_considered`: exactly 1 under `INDICATIVE`, and the lesser of 3 and the insured's package count under `UNDERWRITTEN`.

Each consumed package yields two subscores, written back onto the package itself. Its `financial_subscore` is the lesser of 60 and the sum of: 18 when `restatement_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; 6 multiplied by the lesser of `material_weakness_count` and 3; 12 when `going_concern_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; 10 when `negative_operating_cash_flow_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; and 8 when `debt_to_equity` is greater than or equal to 2.0 and 0 otherwise. Its `governance_subscore` is the lesser of 50 and the sum of: 14 when `independent_director_ratio` is strictly less than 0.50 and 0 otherwise; 10 when `dual_class_shares_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; 8 when `combined_ceo_chair_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; 6 when `staggered_board_flag` is true and 0 otherwise; and 12 when `auditor_change_flag` is true and 0 otherwise.

How those subscores roll up is the sharpest difference between the two variants, and the two components are taken independently. Under `INDICATIVE` there is a single package, so the financial component is that package's `financial_subscore` and the governance component is that package's `governance_subscore`. Under `UNDERWRITTEN` the financial component is the greatest `financial_subscore` among the packages consumed and the governance component is the greatest `governance_subscore` among them, each maximum taken on its own — the two maxima may well come from different packages.

Litigation weight is graded by recency. For each selected event the run measures its age as the number of whole days from its `filed_date` to the logical clock, discarding any part-day. Under `UNDERWRITTEN` the recency factor is graded: 1.0 for an age of 1,095 days or fewer, 0.5 for an age greater than 1,095 days but no more than 2,190 days, and 0.0 for anything older — three years at full weight, three more at half weight, and nothing beyond six years. Under `INDICATIVE` the factor is binary: 1.0 for an age of 1,095 days or fewer and 0.0 otherwise. An event's `weight_units`, written back onto the event, is its case-type weight multiplied by that factor, where the case-type weights are 20 for `SECURITIES_CLASS_ACTION`, 15 for `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT`, 12 for `DERIVATIVE`, 8 for `FIDUCIARY`, 5 for `MERGER_OBJECTION` and 3 for `EMPLOYMENT_PRACTICES`. The litigation subscore is then the lesser of 70 and the sum of `weight_units` over the selected events plus an open-litigation surcharge of 10, where that surcharge is added only when the `UNDERWRITTEN` variant is running and at least one selected event has a null `resolved_date`; under `INDICATIVE` the surcharge is never applied. The capped result is stored on the submission as `litigation_units`.

The composite is the financial component plus the governance component plus the capped litigation subscore, and `risk_score` is that composite multiplied by the insured's sector exposure multiplier, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The single rounding happens after the multiplier, never before it. The multipliers are 1.30 for `BIOTECH`, 1.25 for `FINANCIAL`, 1.15 for `TECH`, 1.10 for `ENERGY`, 1.00 for `INDUSTRIAL` and 0.95 for `CONSUMER`. From the score the run derives `risk_band`: `LOW` when `risk_score` is strictly below 40, `MODERATE` when it is at least 40 and strictly below 70, `ELEVATED` when it is at least 70 and strictly below 100, and `SEVERE` at 100 or above.

Every accepted run overwrites `risk_score`, `risk_band`, `litigation_units` and `disclosure_packages_considered`, sets `score_variant` to the requested variant and `scored_at` to the logical-clock reading, rewrites `financial_subscore` and `governance_subscore` on every package the run consumed and `weight_units` on every event it consumed, and increments the submission's `version` by 1. A re-score never rewrites the banding already snapshotted onto a quote that has been issued.

### 3.3. Quoting the Risk and Negotiating the Wording

Issuing a quote moves the submission `SCORED → QUOTED`. The `Underwriter` states `offered_limit_usd` (strictly greater than 0), `offered_retention_usd` (at least 0), `offered_side_c_sublimit_usd` (at least 0) and `quote_valid_until`, the instant at or after which the offer can no longer be accepted, alongside the mandatory `expected_submission_version` described in section 2.6. The offered entity-securities sublimit may not exceed the offered primary limit; a sublimit above the limit is refused with `ERR_SIDE_C_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_AGGREGATE`. The new quote is created in `OFFERED`, records `issued_by_user_id` from the acting user, and snapshots the submission's current `risk_band` into `risk_band_at_quote` — one of `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `ELEVATED` or `SEVERE`. The rate is pinned to that snapshot for the life of the quote, so a later re-score of the submission never re-prices an offer already on the table. Any sibling quote on the same submission that is still `OFFERED` moves `OFFERED → SUPERSEDED` in the same call, and both that sibling's and the submission's `version` increment by 1. The call responds with the newly created quote; the term opened automatically alongside it is visible only through the quote's exclusion-term list.

Pricing runs in two stages, and the order within each is fixed. The technical premium — recorded as `net_premium_usd` — starts from an exposure measure of the offered limit. That measure sums, across four increased-limit bands, the amount of limit falling in the band expressed in millions of dollars multiplied by the band's factor: the first 5,000,000 of limit at a factor of 1.00, the next 5,000,000 at 0.65, the next 10,000,000 at 0.45, and everything above 20,000,000 at 0.30. Multiplying that exposure measure by the band rate for `risk_band_at_quote` — 9,000 for `LOW`, 14,000 for `MODERATE`, 22,000 for `ELEVATED` and 34,000 for `SEVERE` — gives the base premium. Two adjustments then apply. The entity-securities load is 0.00 whenever `offered_side_c_sublimit_usd` is 0; otherwise it is 0.20 when the insured's `exchange_listing` is `LISTED`, 0.08 when it is `PRIVATE`, and 0.00 when it is `NONE`. The retention credit is the lesser of 0.35 and 0.05 multiplied by the number of whole 250,000 blocks in `offered_retention_usd`, the block count being the quotient discarding any remainder. The evaluation order is pinned: take the base premium, multiply it by one plus the entity-securities load, then multiply that by one minus the retention credit, then clamp the result to a floor of 25,000 and a ceiling of 2,500,000, and only then round half-up to 2 decimal places. The exposure measure, the load and the credit are all kept unrounded.

Grossing up for commission looks circular and must not be solved as though it were: the broker commission is a percentage of the gross premium, while the gross premium is the net plus that commission. The pinned order resolves it. First, `gross_premium_usd` is `net_premium_usd` divided by one minus the commission rate of 0.125, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Second, `broker_commission_usd` is taken as the residual — `gross_premium_usd` less `net_premium_usd` — so that net and commission always add back to gross exactly. Third, `premium_tax_usd` is the surplus lines tax of 0.035 applied to the already-rounded gross premium, never to the net, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Fourth, `total_payable_usd` is `gross_premium_usd` plus `premium_tax_usd`. Multiplying the net by one plus the commission rate is not an acceptable substitute: it under-collects, and it leaves net plus commission unequal to gross.

Exclusion wording is negotiated on the quote, term by term. Issuing a quote automatically opens one term for the insurer: `exclusion_type` `CONDUCT`, `wording_variant` `BROAD`, `premium_impact_pct` 0.00, `proposed_by_role` and `last_actor_role` both `Underwriter`, `status` `PROPOSED`, `round_count` 1 and `agreed_variant` null. The `Underwriter` may open further terms on the same quote, each stating its `exclusion_type` — one of `CONDUCT`, `PRIOR_ACTS`, `INSURED_VS_INSURED`, `BODILY_INJURY` or `REGULATORY` — its `wording_variant` and its `premium_impact_pct`. Wording variants are `BROAD`, `FINAL_ADJUDICATION`, `NON_APPEALABLE_ADJUDICATION`, `DERIVATIVE_CARVEBACK`, `BANKRUPTCY_CARVEBACK`, `RETRO_DATE_FULL_PRIOR_ACTS` and `NONE`. The premium impact is a fraction from −0.25 through 0.25 inclusive, negative where the wording narrows cover and positive where it broadens it; a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each newly opened term starts in `PROPOSED` with `round_count` 1, `agreed_variant` null and both `proposed_by_role` and `last_actor_role` set to `Underwriter`. No term may be opened, countered or resolved unless the parent quote is still `OFFERED`; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_QUOTE_STATE`.

Countering is the negotiation cycle, and it alternates strictly. A counter supplies a fresh `wording_variant` and a fresh `premium_impact_pct`, which overwrite the outstanding values, sets `last_actor_role` to the acting role and increments `round_count` by 1. The insured's `RiskManager` countering an underwriter's outstanding proposal moves the term `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`; the `Underwriter` countering the insured's outstanding counter moves it `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`, and the cycle `PROPOSED → COUNTERED → PROPOSED` may continue from either side. Two guards bound it. A term may only be countered or resolved while its `status` is `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`; a term in `AGREED`, `REJECTED` or `IMPASSE` is terminal and any further action on it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERM_STATE`. And the acting role must differ from `last_actor_role` — a party may not counter or resolve its own outstanding wording — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_TERM_ACTOR_SEQUENCE`. Negotiation is capped at four rounds. While incrementing `round_count` would leave it strictly below 4 the term simply flips to the other side of the cycle; the counter that would take `round_count` to exactly 4 instead drives the term to `IMPASSE`, setting `round_count` to 4 and `last_actor_role` to the acting role. An `IMPASSE` term is terminal and can never become `AGREED`, which means it permanently blocks binding with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EXCLUSION_TERMS`.

Resolving a term ends the negotiation on it, and takes one field, `resolution`, whose members are `AGREE` and `REJECT`. Both resolutions are reachable from `PROPOSED` and from `COUNTERED` under identical preconditions — the term must be in one of those two states, the parent quote must be `OFFERED`, and the acting role must differ from `last_actor_role`. `AGREE` moves the term to `AGREED` and freezes `agreed_variant` to whatever `wording_variant` was outstanding at that moment; from then on the term's `premium_impact_pct` participates in the premium adjustment applied at binding. `REJECT` moves the term to `REJECTED`, leaves `agreed_variant` null, and makes the term contribute exactly 0.00 to that adjustment. Neither resolution rewrites `last_actor_role`: unlike a counter, resolving leaves that field naming the party whose wording was outstanding at the moment of resolution. Because the alternation check sits above the term-state check in the ladder of section 2.7, a further counter or resolve attempt on the now-terminal term while the parent quote is still `OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_TERM_ACTOR_SEQUENCE` when the acting role equals that retained `last_actor_role`, and with `ERR_INVALID_TERM_STATE` when it differs from it. A rejected `CONDUCT` term is a special trap: because the conduct exclusion must be `AGREED` for a bind to proceed, rejecting it still fails the bind, with `ERR_MANDATORY_EXCLUSION_MISSING`.

### 3.4. Binding: Policy Issuance, Coverage Parts and Entity-Securities Capacity

Acceptance is the insured's act, performed by the `RiskManager` that owns the insured, and it is the busiest transition in the system: the quote moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`, the submission moves `QUOTED → BOUND`, and a policy comes into existence. The request carries no body, and any body supplied is ignored. Six preconditions guard it, and the order in which they are reported is the ladder of section 2.7. The quote must still be `OFFERED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_QUOTE_STATE`. Every exclusion term on the quote must be in `AGREED` or `REJECTED`; a term still `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, or stuck in `IMPASSE`, is refused with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_EXCLUSION_TERMS`. A term with `exclusion_type` `CONDUCT` and `status` `AGREED` must exist on the quote, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_EXCLUSION_MISSING`. The logical clock must be strictly earlier than `quote_valid_until`; at or after that instant the offer is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The insured's `control_status` must be `INDEPENDENT` at the moment of binding, because a company already in play is not a company this insurer will newly incept cover on; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONTROL_CHANGE_BLOCKS_BIND`. And the entity-securities admission test described below must pass, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Binding cascades four levels deep. First, the accepted quote creates the policy with `coverage_status` `INCEPTED` and `premium_status` `INVOICED`, carrying a human-readable `policy_number` of the form `DO-NNNNNN`, where the six digits are a zero-padded monotonic counter that starts at `000001` and advances by one on each bind. The policy copies `submission_id`, `insured_id` and `quote_id` for addressability — the insured is copied onto the policy itself so that capacity aggregation reads a single field — and copies `period_start`, `period_end`, `retroactive_date` and `erp_days` from the submission, `shared_aggregate_limit_usd` from the quote's `offered_limit_usd`, `retention_usd` from the quote's `offered_retention_usd`, and `program_target_limit_usd` from the submission's `requested_program_target_limit_usd`. The coverage window is half-open: an instant equal to `period_start` is inside the period, and an instant equal to `period_end` is outside it. The policy's `gross_premium_usd` is the quoted gross premium adjusted for the wording that was actually agreed: sum the `premium_impact_pct` of every term on the accepted quote whose `status` is `AGREED`, multiply the quote's `gross_premium_usd` by one plus that sum, and round half-up to 2 decimal places — summing first, then multiplying, then rounding once. Terms in `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED`, `REJECTED` or `IMPASSE` contribute exactly 0.00. `premium_due_date` is set to the binding instant plus 30 days. The policy is created with `tower_sealed` false, `placed_limit_usd` 0.00, `total_eroded_usd` 0.00, `aggregate_remaining_usd` equal to `shared_aggregate_limit_usd`, `return_premium_usd` 0.00, `runoff_premium_usd` 0.00, `open_claim_count` 0, and `runoff_start_date`, `runoff_end_date` and `cancelled_reason` all null; the acceptance responds with that newly created policy rather than with the quote.

Second, creating the policy creates its coverage parts, in a fixed order that later rounding rules depend on: `SIDE_A` first, then `SIDE_B`, then `SIDE_C` — and `SIDE_C` only when the accepted `offered_side_c_sublimit_usd` is strictly greater than 0. The `part` field names which insuring agreement each represents: `SIDE_A` is individual non-indemnified loss, `SIDE_B` is company reimbursement, and `SIDE_C` is entity securities cover. `SIDE_A` and `SIDE_B` each take a `limit_usd` equal to the policy's `shared_aggregate_limit_usd`; `SIDE_C` takes the accepted sublimit. `SIDE_A` carries a `retention_usd` of 0, since non-indemnified individual loss is not subject to the retention; `SIDE_B` and `SIDE_C` each carry the policy's retention. Every part starts in `ACTIVE` with `eroded_usd` at 0 and `remaining_limit_usd` equal to its `limit_usd`.

Third and fourth, creating a `SIDE_C` part changes what the insurer has committed to this insured, and that recomputation re-derives the insured's posture. The insured's `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd` is the sum of `limit_usd` over every `SIDE_C` coverage part that is `ACTIVE` and whose policy belongs to this insured with a `coverage_status` of `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `RUNOFF`. `available_side_c_capacity_usd` is `entity_capacity_cap_usd` less `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd`, floored at 0. Utilisation is 0 when `entity_capacity_cap_usd` is 0 and otherwise `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd` divided by `entity_capacity_cap_usd`, compared unrounded, and it drives `capacity_posture`: `EXHAUSTED` when utilisation is greater than or equal to 1.00, `RESTRICTED` when it is greater than or equal to 0.80 but below 1.00, and `OPEN` otherwise. `open_policy_count` is the number of this insured's policies whose `coverage_status` is `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `RUNOFF`. The accepted quote's, the insured's and the submission's `version` each increment by 1 on a successful bind, and the policy's derived `aggregate_remaining_usd`, `placed_limit_usd` and `tower_posture` are computed at creation — a freshly bound policy carries no layers at all and therefore reads `INCOMPLETE`.

The admission test that guards acceptance is narrower than the aggregate above, because it looks only at periods that actually overlap. Sum the `limit_usd` of the qualifying `SIDE_C` parts whose policy period overlaps the candidate period running from `requested_period_start` to `requested_period_end`, add the quote's `offered_side_c_sublimit_usd`, and require the total to be no greater than the insured's `entity_capacity_cap_usd`. Overlap is judged on half-open intervals: two periods overlap when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends, which means a period beginning exactly at another's `period_end` does not overlap it. A total above the cap is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.5. Building the Tower: Layers, Participation and Sealing

A policy's primary limit is only part of the programme. The `Underwriter` subscribes the tower one band at a time, each layer naming its `policy_id`, the `carrier_name` writing it, its `attachment_point_usd` (at least 0) as the amount of underlying limit that must erode before it responds, its `limit_usd` (strictly greater than 0) as the band's limit of liability, its `participation_pct` (strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 1) as the fraction of the band that carrier subscribes, and its `form_type`. Two optional fields complete it: `underlying_layer_id`, the layer immediately beneath this one, which is null for the primary layer and must otherwise reference an existing layer of the same policy — a dangling reference is refused with `ERR_LAYER_NOT_FOUND` — and `dic_deviation_notes`, describing how the wording deviates. `form_type` is either `FOLLOWING_FORM`, which adopts the primary wording verbatim, or `DIFFERENCE_IN_CONDITIONS`, which deviates from it; for a `DIFFERENCE_IN_CONDITIONS` layer, `dic_deviation_notes` is mandatory and its absence is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. A new layer is created in `ATTACHED`, and its `is_primary` flag is true exactly when its `attachment_point_usd` is 0.

Layers may only be subscribed while the policy's `coverage_status` is `INCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`; any other coverage state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. They may not be subscribed at all once the tower has been sealed, which is refused with `ERR_TOWER_ALREADY_SEALED`. Beyond that, five geometry and money rules apply, in the order given. A band is the distinct pairing of an attachment point with a limit, and layers sharing a band are quota-share co-participants on it. The new layer's band must either match an existing band of the policy exactly — which is how co-participation is expressed — or not overlap any existing band at all, meaning the new band must start at or above the top of the existing band, or end at or below the existing band's attachment point; a partial overlap is refused with `ERR_LAYER_OVERLAP`. The sum of `participation_pct` across all `ATTACHED` layers on the new layer's band, including the new one, must be no greater than 1.00 when compared at 2 decimal places, or the subscription is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. The difference-in-conditions share — the sum of `limit_usd` multiplied by `participation_pct` over the layers whose `form_type` is `DIFFERENCE_IN_CONDITIONS` and whose `status` is `ATTACHED` or `EXHAUSTED`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, including the new layer — must be no greater than 40 percent of the policy's `program_target_limit_usd`, or the subscription is refused with `ERR_DIC_SHARE_EXCEEDED`. A layer whose `attachment_point_usd` is 0 may only be added when the policy has no layer attaching at 0 whose `status` is `ATTACHED` or `EXHAUSTED` yet, so a released primary does not block a replacement and co-participation on the primary band is never possible, and its `limit_usd` must equal the policy's `shared_aggregate_limit_usd`; a second primary, or a primary whose limit does not match, is refused with `ERR_PRIMARY_LAYER_IMMUTABLE`. Finally, the policy's placed limit including the new layer must not exceed `program_target_limit_usd`, or the subscription is refused with `ERR_TOWER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Placed limit and posture are derived, never supplied. The derivation reads every layer of the policy whose `status` is `ATTACHED` or `EXHAUSTED`; `RELEASED` layers are excluded. `placed_limit_usd` is the sum of `limit_usd` multiplied by `participation_pct` across that set, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The bands of that set, ordered by `attachment_point_usd` ascending, are contiguous when the set is non-empty, the lowest band attaches at 0, each successive band's attachment point equals the previous band's attachment point plus the previous band's limit, and every band's participations sum to exactly 1.00 compared at 2 decimal places. `tower_posture` is then decided by precedence, taking the first match reading down this list: `RELEASED` when the policy's `coverage_status` is `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED`, or when the policy has at least one layer and every layer of the policy is `RELEASED`; otherwise `ERODED` when the policy's `total_eroded_usd` is greater than 0; otherwise `SEALED` when `tower_sealed` is true; otherwise `CONTIGUOUS` when the bands are contiguous as just defined; otherwise `INCOMPLETE`, which is also what a policy carrying no layer at all reads.

Sealing the tower is the `Underwriter`'s declaration that the placement is complete. It takes no body, is permitted only while the policy's `coverage_status` is `INCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, and only while `tower_sealed` is still false, otherwise refused with `ERR_TOWER_ALREADY_SEALED`; it responds with the updated policy. Two structural conditions must hold. The bands must be contiguous from an attachment point of 0 with no gap, or the seal is refused with `ERR_TOWER_GAP`. And every band's `participation_pct` must sum to exactly 1.00 while `placed_limit_usd` equals the policy's `program_target_limit_usd`, or the seal is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_INCOMPLETE`. A successful seal sets `tower_sealed` to true, re-derives `tower_posture` — which reads `SEALED` unless the precedence above already puts `RELEASED` or `ERODED` first, so a policy whose `total_eroded_usd` is already above 0 reads `ERODED` even once sealed — makes the layers immutable, and increments the policy's `version` by 1. Sealing is the underwriter's declaration and nothing more: it is not a precondition for the claims side, and notice of a claim or of a circumstance may be given whether or not the tower has been sealed.

### 3.6. Money In, Money Back and the Run-Off Tail

A bound policy is invoiced, not paid. The insured's `RiskManager` records payment by submitting `amount_usd`, and the payment must be for the whole bound gross premium — an amount that differs from the policy's `gross_premium_usd` in either direction is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Payment is accepted while `premium_status` is `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT`, so a late payer can still cure, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE` once `coverage_status` has become `CANCELLED` — the only coverage state that blocks it, so `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE`, `RUNOFF` and `EXPIRED` all admit a payment. A premium already `PAID` is refused with that same `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. A successful payment sets `premium_status` to `PAID`, leaves `coverage_status` untouched, and increments the policy's `version` by 1. Left unpaid, the epoch advance moves `premium_status` from `INVOICED` to `DELINQUENT` once the clock reaches `premium_due_date`, and delinquency has teeth: while a policy is `DELINQUENT` no insurer money may leave it, so defense advancement on every claim of that policy is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_DELINQUENT` at HTTP 402.

Cancellation is a `ComplianceOfficer` act, taking an `effective_date` and a `cancelled_reason` of `INSURED_REQUEST`, `NON_PAYMENT` or `MATERIAL_MISREPRESENTATION`. It is permitted while `coverage_status` is `INCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE` otherwise. A policy with live claims cannot be cancelled: the policy's `open_claim_count` — the number of its claims whose `status` is `NOTICED`, `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED`, `DEFENSE_ADVANCING` or `SETTLEMENT_PENDING` — must be 0, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CLAIMS_BLOCK_CANCEL`. On cancellation the unearned premium is credited to `return_premium_usd`: count the whole days from the `effective_date` to the policy's `period_end`, discarding any part-day, clamp that count to no less than 0 and no more than 365 so that a cancellation before inception returns at most the whole premium, then multiply the policy's `gross_premium_usd` by that day count divided by 365 and round half-up to 2 decimal places. Cancellation then runs the release cascade of section 2.9 — active coverage parts to `RELEASED`, attached layers to `RELEASED` — and recomputes the insured's `aggregate_bound_side_c_usd`, `available_side_c_capacity_usd`, `capacity_posture` and `open_policy_count`, so the released Side C limit returns to the insured's available capacity. The supplied `cancelled_reason` is recorded in the policy's own `cancelled_reason` field, `premium_status` is left untouched, and both the policy's and the insured's `version` increment by 1.

A change of control calls for a tail, not a cancellation. A `ComplianceOfficer` endorses run-off cover by submitting `tail_years`, which must be 1, 2, 3 or 6; any other value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TAIL_YEARS`. The policy's `coverage_status` must be `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `RUNOFF` — a policy already in `RUNOFF` passes this state check precisely so that the duplicate guard reports the right thing — and any other coverage state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. The insured's `control_status` must be `CHANGE_OF_CONTROL_ANNOUNCED` or `ACQUIRED` with a non-null `change_of_control_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CHANGE_OF_CONTROL`, and the policy must not already carry an endorsement, or it is refused with `ERR_RUNOFF_ALREADY_ENDORSED`.

The endorsement is created in `ENDORSED` and copies the insured's `change_of_control_date` onto itself, because wrongful acts after that instant fall outside run-off cover. Its `runoff_start_date` is that same instant, and its `runoff_end_date` is `runoff_start_date` plus `tail_years` multiplied by 365 days. Pricing is a decay on the bound premium. `tail_factor_applied` is selected by `tail_years`: 0.75 for one year, 1.10 for two, 1.45 for three and 2.25 for six. `severity_load_applied` is 0.20 when the policy carries at least one claim whose `status` is anything other than `DENIED` — a single such claim is enough, and claims in `NOTICED`, `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED`, `DEFENSE_ADVANCING`, `SETTLEMENT_PENDING` and `SETTLED` all count while `DENIED` claims do not — and 0.00 otherwise. The load multiplies the tail factor rather than adding to it: take the policy's `gross_premium_usd`, multiply by `tail_factor_applied`, multiply by one plus `severity_load_applied`, and round half-up to 2 decimal places to get the gross tail cost. Against that stands `unexpired_premium_credit_usd`: count the whole days from `runoff_start_date` to the policy's `period_end`, discarding any part-day, clamp the count to no less than 0 and no more than 365, multiply the policy's `gross_premium_usd` by that count divided by 365, and round half-up to 2 decimal places. `runoff_premium_usd` is the gross tail cost less that credit, each of the two rounded before the subtraction, with the difference rounded to 2 decimal places and then floored at 0.

Issuing the endorsement moves the policy from `INCEPTED` or from `ACTIVE` to `RUNOFF`, copies `runoff_start_date`, `runoff_end_date` and `runoff_premium_usd` onto the policy, and increments the policy's `version` by 1. The endorsement itself also carries the `tail_years` requested and a `status` of `ENDORSED`, and it is the resource the call responds with. It also reaches sideways: every circumstance report of the policy still in `REPORTED` has its `reporting_deadline` re-derived, because the reporting window now runs from the end of the tail rather than the end of the original period. When the clock later reaches `runoff_end_date`, the epoch advance moves the endorsement `ENDORSED → EXPIRED` and the policy `RUNOFF → EXPIRED`, releasing the remaining active coverage parts and attached layers and recomputing the insured's capacity fields and the policy's `tower_posture` exactly as an ordinary expiry does.

### 3.7. Notice: Claims and Circumstance Reports

A management liability policy responds to claims first made and reported during its period, so notice timing is the whole game. The insured's `RiskManager` gives notice of a claim by naming its `policy_id`, the `claim_type`, the `claimant_name`, the `claimant_capacity` in which the claimant sues, the `wrongful_act_date` of the alleged act, and the `alleged_damages_usd` asserted (strictly greater than 0). `claim_type` is one of `SECURITIES_CLASS_ACTION`, `DERIVATIVE`, `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT`, `EMPLOYMENT_PRACTICES`, `INSURED_VS_INSURED` or `FIDUCIARY`. `claimant_capacity` is one of `THIRD_PARTY`, `SHAREHOLDER_DERIVATIVE`, `FORMER_OFFICER`, `BANKRUPTCY_TRUSTEE` or `COMPANY`.

Five gates stand in front of a notice. The policy's `coverage_status` must be `INCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `RUNOFF`, or the notice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. The clock reading must fall inside the claims-made window: at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end` for a policy not in run-off, or at or after `period_start` and strictly before `runoff_end_date` for a policy whose `coverage_status` is `RUNOFF`. The upper bound is exclusive, so a notice stamped exactly at `period_end` is outside the period and is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`. The `wrongful_act_date` must be at or after the policy's `retroactive_date`, unless the bound quote carries an `AGREED` `PRIOR_ACTS` term whose `agreed_variant` is `RETRO_DATE_FULL_PRIOR_ACTS`, in which case the retroactive date is waived entirely; an earlier act without that waiver is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_ACTS_EXCLUDED`. When the policy is in `RUNOFF`, the `wrongful_act_date` must additionally be strictly earlier than the policy's `runoff_start_date`, because the tail freezes cover as at the transaction; an act on or after that instant is refused with `ERR_POST_TRANSACTION_ACT`. And the insured-versus-insured screen must clear.

That screen is the one place where negotiated wording changes who may claim. Notice passes when the `claimant_capacity` is `THIRD_PARTY`. It also passes when the bound quote carries an `AGREED` `INSURED_VS_INSURED` term and one of three carve-backs applies: the `agreed_variant` is `NONE`, meaning no insured-versus-insured exclusion was written at all; or the `agreed_variant` is `DERIVATIVE_CARVEBACK` and the `claimant_capacity` is `SHAREHOLDER_DERIVATIVE`; or the `agreed_variant` is `BANKRUPTCY_CARVEBACK` and the `claimant_capacity` is `BANKRUPTCY_TRUSTEE`. Every other combination — a `FORMER_OFFICER` or `COMPANY` claimant, or a derivative claimant under bankruptcy-only wording — is refused with `ERR_INSURED_VS_INSURED_EXCLUDED`.

A claim that clears all five gates is created in `NOTICED` with `notice_date` set to the logical-clock reading and `retention_remaining_usd` initialised from the policy's `retention_usd`, with `retention_applied_usd`, `advanced_defense_usd`, `indemnity_paid_usd`, `insurer_share_usd`, `insured_retained_usd` and `unfunded_excess_usd` all at 0, `post_refusal_defense_usd` at 0, `hammer_applied` false, and `allocation_method`, `denial_reason`, `decided_by_user_id` and `circumstance_report_id` all null. The policy's `open_claim_count` and `version` each increase by 1.

Circumstance reporting is the insured's hedge against the clock. When the insured becomes aware of something that may give rise to a claim but has no claim yet, the `RiskManager` reports it against the policy with a `description` of the circumstance, the `discovered_date` on which the insured first became aware, the identified or anticipated `potential_claimants` as one free-text string rather than a list, and an `estimated_exposure_usd` (strictly greater than 0). The report is subject to the same policy-state gate and the same half-open claims-made window as a notice, refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE` and `ERR_NOTICE_OUTSIDE_PERIOD` respectively. It is created in `REPORTED` with `notice_date` set to the logical-clock reading, and that stored instant is the whole point of the mechanism. Its `reporting_deadline` is derived as the end of the reporting window plus the policy's `erp_days` days, where the window end is the policy's `runoff_end_date` when the policy's `coverage_status` is `RUNOFF` and its `period_end` otherwise. The resulting instant keeps the time of day of the window end, and it is exclusive.

Maturing a report into a claim is the `ClaimsAdjuster`'s act, and it takes the same five claim fields a fresh notice takes: `claim_type`, `claimant_name`, `claimant_capacity`, `wrongful_act_date` and `alleged_damages_usd`. The report must still be in `REPORTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CIRCUMSTANCE_STATE`; the clock must be strictly earlier than the report's `reporting_deadline`, so maturing exactly at the deadline is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The prior-acts test applies unchanged — `wrongful_act_date` at or after the policy's `retroactive_date` unless an `AGREED` `PRIOR_ACTS` term with `agreed_variant` `RETRO_DATE_FULL_PRIOR_ACTS` waives it, otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_ACTS_EXCLUDED` — and so do the insured-versus-insured screen, reported as `ERR_INSURED_VS_INSURED_EXCLUDED`, and, for a policy in run-off, the post-transaction test, reported as `ERR_POST_TRANSACTION_ACT`. What is deliberately *not* applied is the claims-made window: the report already fixed an in-period notice date, and the reporting-deadline test replaces the window test.

Maturity relates back. The report moves `REPORTED → MATURED`, and the new claim is created in `NOTICED` with `circumstance_report_id` pointing at the report and `notice_date` set to the report's own `notice_date` rather than the current clock reading — so a claim matured months after `period_end` still carries an in-period notice date. As with a direct notice, `retention_remaining_usd` is initialised from the policy's `retention_usd`, and the policy's `open_claim_count` and `version` each increase by 1. The call responds with that newly created claim rather than with the report. A report that is never matured is swept to `LAPSED` by the epoch advance once the clock reaches its `reporting_deadline`, after which it can no longer become a claim.

### 3.8. Coverage Decisions, Defense Allocation and Advancement

Every claim needs a coverage position before a single dollar moves. The `ClaimsAdjuster` records one by submitting `decision`, whose members are `ACCEPT_WITH_ROR` and `DENY`. Accepting coverage under a reservation of rights moves the claim `NOTICED → COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` and requires `allocation_method` in the same request — one of `RELATIVE_EXPOSURE` or `LARGER_SETTLEMENT` — whose absence is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`. That method is frozen on the claim and applied to every defense invoice on it thereafter, and the acting user is recorded as `decided_by_user_id`. Acceptance is only available while the claim is `NOTICED`; a claim already past that point is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Denial requires `denial_reason` — one of `CONDUCT_EXCLUSION`, `PRIOR_ACTS`, `INSURED_VS_INSURED`, `LATE_NOTICE` or `MISREPRESENTATION` — whose absence is likewise refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`, and it is available from `NOTICED`, from `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` and from `DEFENSE_ADVANCING`. Either decision records the acting `X-User-ID` as the claim's `decided_by_user_id` and increments the claim's `version` by 1; a denial also decrements the policy's `open_claim_count` and increments the policy's `version` by 1.

A denial recorded after defense money has already gone out is a compensating action, and it unwinds in four steps. Every defense invoice of the claim in `ADVANCED` moves `ADVANCED → CLAWED_BACK` and every invoice still in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → VOIDED`, and a voided invoice can never be allocated. Each clawed-back invoice reverses its `advanced_usd` from the coverage part it eroded, restoring that part's `eroded_usd` and `remaining_limit_usd` and returning an `EXHAUSTED` part to `ACTIVE` when its `eroded_usd` falls back below its `limit_usd`, and reverses its `retention_applied_usd` on the claim. The restored part erosion re-derives the policy's `total_eroded_usd`, `aggregate_remaining_usd` and `tower_posture`, and returns every layer whose `attachment_point_usd` is greater than or equal to the restored `total_eroded_usd` from `EXHAUSTED` back to `ATTACHED`. Finally the claim's own ledger resets: `advanced_defense_usd` to 0, `retention_applied_usd` to 0, and `retention_remaining_usd` back to the policy's `retention_usd`.

Defense bills arrive from counsel and are submitted by the insured's `RiskManager`. Each invoice names its `claim_id`, the `law_firm_name` submitting it, counsel's own `invoice_reference`, the `billed_amount_usd` (strictly greater than 0), and the three-way split of that total: `covered_matter_amount_usd` attributable solely to covered matters, `uncovered_matter_amount_usd` attributable solely to uncovered matters or uncovered defendants, and `mixed_matter_amount_usd` for work that advances both — each of the three at least 0. Two headcounts accompany the split: `insured_person_defendant_count`, the number of insured-person defendants the mixed work defends, and `entity_defendant_count`, the number of entity defendants it defends, each at least 0. Invoices may only be submitted while the claim is `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_ADVANCING`, otherwise refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The `invoice_reference` must be unique among the claim's defense invoices, and a reference already used by a non-`VOIDED` invoice of the same claim is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVOICE_REFERENCE`. And the split must reconcile: `covered_matter_amount_usd` plus `uncovered_matter_amount_usd` plus `mixed_matter_amount_usd` must equal `billed_amount_usd` exactly, and the two defendant counts must not both be zero, or the submission is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A new invoice is created in `SUBMITTED` with no allocation applied yet: `allocation_method_applied` is null and `covered_allocated_usd`, `retention_applied_usd`, `advanced_usd` and `unfunded_excess_usd` are each 0.00.

Allocating an invoice is the `ClaimsAdjuster`'s act, carrying only `expected_claim_version`, and it moves the invoice `SUBMITTED → ADVANCED` and the claim `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED → DEFENSE_ADVANCING`. A second allocation on the same claim keeps the claim in `DEFENSE_ADVANCING` and continues the retention draw-down where the previous one stopped. The invoice must still be `SUBMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`; the claim must be `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_ADVANCING`, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`; and the policy's `premium_status` must not be `DELINQUENT`, or it is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_DELINQUENT`. The invoice records `allocation_method_applied` as a copy of the claim's frozen `allocation_method`, so the bill carries a record of the method actually used, and the allocation responds with that updated invoice rather than with the claim.

Allocation splits the bill, and the frozen method decides how. The relative-exposure share is `insured_person_defendant_count` divided by the sum of `insured_person_defendant_count` and `entity_defendant_count`, and is 0 when that sum is 0. Under `RELATIVE_EXPOSURE`, `covered_allocated_usd` is `covered_matter_amount_usd` plus `mixed_matter_amount_usd` multiplied by that share, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places: mixed work is pro-rated by the defendant headcount ratio, so only the insured-person fraction of the mixed amount becomes covered. Under `LARGER_SETTLEMENT`, `covered_allocated_usd` is `covered_matter_amount_usd` plus the whole `mixed_matter_amount_usd` — a factor of 1.00 — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, on the theory that the work would have been incurred to defend the covered insured persons regardless; for any invoice with a positive `mixed_matter_amount_usd` and a positive `entity_defendant_count` this method yields strictly more covered cost than the other. Under both methods, `uncovered_matter_amount_usd` is never advanced.

The covered amount then falls through four ordered buckets, and which coverage parts are eligible depends on the claim's `claim_type`. A `SECURITIES_CLASS_ACTION` claim may reach `SIDE_A`, `SIDE_B` and `SIDE_C`. A `DERIVATIVE`, `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT`, `EMPLOYMENT_PRACTICES` or `FIDUCIARY` claim may reach `SIDE_A` and `SIDE_B`. An `INSURED_VS_INSURED` claim may reach `SIDE_A` only. The buckets are consumed in a fixed sequence that is never re-ordered by amount. First the claim's retention: the invoice's `retention_applied_usd` is the lesser of the claim's `retention_remaining_usd` and the covered amount, but only when `SIDE_B` is among the eligible parts for the claim type — Side A non-indemnified loss carries no retention, so for an `INSURED_VS_INSURED` claim the invoice's `retention_applied_usd` is 0.00. That amount is subtracted from what remains to be funded, deducted from the claim's `retention_remaining_usd` and added to the claim's `retention_applied_usd`. Second the policy's `SIDE_B` part, when `SIDE_B` is eligible and that part exists and is `ACTIVE`: it absorbs the lesser of its `remaining_limit_usd` and what remains, raising its `eroded_usd` and lowering its `remaining_limit_usd` accordingly. Third the policy's `SIDE_A` part, on the same terms. Parts that are `EXHAUSTED` or `RELEASED` are skipped, which pushes the remainder further down. `SIDE_C` never funds defense cost under any claim type. Fourth and last, whatever is still unfunded.

What comes out of the waterfall is recorded in three places. The invoice's `advanced_usd` is the total the insurer actually advanced across the coverage parts, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and its `unfunded_excess_usd` is whatever remained after the last eligible part, likewise rounded. The claim accumulates: `retention_applied_usd` and `retention_remaining_usd` as already described, `advanced_defense_usd` increased by the invoice's `advanced_usd`, and `unfunded_excess_usd` increased by the invoice's `unfunded_excess_usd`. The policy's `total_eroded_usd` is re-summed across its coverage parts and its `aggregate_remaining_usd` re-derived as `shared_aggregate_limit_usd` less `total_eroded_usd`, with `tower_posture` re-derived and the part-exhaustion and layer-exhaustion cascades of section 2.9 applied. The claim's, the invoice's, each touched coverage part's and the policy's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.9. Settlement Consent, the Hammer Clause and Funding the Resolution

Settling a D&O claim requires the insured's agreement, and the hammer clause is what prices disagreement. The `ClaimsAdjuster` recommends a settlement by submitting `recommended_amount_usd` (strictly greater than 0) together with the mandatory `expected_claim_version`, which moves the claim to `SETTLEMENT_PENDING` from either `COVERAGE_ACCEPTED` or `DEFENSE_ADVANCING`; any other claim state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The proposal is created in `RECOMMENDED` with `recommended_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` and with `insured_response`, `response_date`, `refusal_reason`, `hammer_pct_applied` and `insurer_indemnity_cap_usd` all null, and any earlier proposal on the same claim whose `status` is `RECOMMENDED`, `CONSENTED` or `REFUSED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` in the same call, each superseded proposal's `version` incrementing by 1. The claim's `version` increments by 1.

The insured's `RiskManager` — and only the `RiskManager` whose identity matches the policy's owning insured — answers by submitting `response`, whose members are `CONSENT` and `REFUSE`. The proposal must still be `RECOMMENDED`, or the answer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROPOSAL_STATE`. A refusal must state a non-empty `refusal_reason`, whose absence — or an empty string — is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_FIELD_REQUIRED`; a `refusal_reason` supplied alongside a `CONSENT` is ignored and the proposal's `refusal_reason` stays null. Either answer records `insured_response` and stamps `response_date` from the logical clock, and both branches set the two derived amounts on the proposal with equal specificity. Let the recommended amount be the settlement the insurer put on the table and let the retention baseline be the claim's `retention_remaining_usd` as observed at the moment of the answer; because no allocation can occur while the claim sits in `SETTLEMENT_PENDING`, that baseline is still the claim's `retention_remaining_usd` when the resolution is funded. On `CONSENT`, the proposal moves `RECOMMENDED → CONSENTED` and `hammer_pct_applied` is 1.00. On `REFUSE`, the proposal moves `RECOMMENDED → REFUSED` and `hammer_pct_applied` is 0.70 — the insurer will bear seventy percent of the excess over the retention and no more. In both branches `insurer_indemnity_cap_usd` is `hammer_pct_applied` multiplied by the recommended amount less the retention baseline, floored at 0 before the multiplication where the retention exceeds the recommended amount, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places once at this point; that stored value is reused verbatim at resolution and never recomputed. The proposal's `version` increments by 1.

Funding the resolution is the `ClaimsAdjuster`'s final act on the claim, moving it `SETTLEMENT_PENDING → SETTLED`. The request carries `final_resolution_amount_usd`, the amount the matter actually resolved for, on which no range constraint of any kind is enforced, and optionally `post_refusal_defense_usd` (at least 0, defaulting to 0), which records the defense cost the insured incurred after refusing consent to the recommended settlement; that amount is written onto the claim's own `post_refusal_defense_usd` for the hammer-clause narrative and is never advanced by the insurer. Four preconditions guard it, reported in the order of section 2.7. The claim must be `SETTLEMENT_PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The claim's newest settlement proposal, meaning the one with the greatest `created_at`, must be `CONSENTED` or `REFUSED`; a proposal still sitting in `RECOMMENDED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_PENDING`, and `SUPERSEDED` proposals are ignored entirely for this purpose. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that proposal's `recommended_by_user_id`, because the adjuster who recommended a settlement may not be the adjuster who funds it; the same user attempting both is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the policy's `aggregate_remaining_usd` must be strictly greater than 0, or the request is refused with `ERR_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`.

The insurer's share is a three-way minimum. First the retention is drawn: the claim's `retention_applied_usd` increases by the lesser of its `retention_remaining_usd` and the final resolution amount, and its `retention_remaining_usd` becomes its previous value less the final resolution amount, floored at 0. Then `insurer_share_usd` is the least of three quantities: the final resolution amount less the retention baseline floored at 0; the proposal's stored `insurer_indemnity_cap_usd`; and the policy's `aggregate_remaining_usd`. `indemnity_paid_usd` equals `insurer_share_usd`. `insured_retained_usd` is the final resolution amount less `insurer_share_usd`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — the portion the insured bears out of its own pocket. `hammer_applied` is true exactly when the governing proposal's `insured_response` was `REFUSE`.

The insurer's share is then split across the coverage parts that can actually respond, and the rounding order matters. The eligible set is every coverage part of the claim's policy whose `part` is among the eligible parts for the claim's `claim_type`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `remaining_limit_usd` is greater than 0, ordered by `created_at` ascending — which, given the fixed creation order at binding, means Side A, then Side B, then Side C. The basis is the sum of `remaining_limit_usd` across that set. When the basis is 0 no part is eroded at all and the whole `insurer_share_usd` is added to the claim's `unfunded_excess_usd`. Otherwise each part's share is `insurer_share_usd` multiplied by that part's `remaining_limit_usd` divided by the basis, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places first, part by part in creation order. The residual — `insurer_share_usd` less the sum of those rounded shares, rounded to 2 decimal places, and possibly negative — is then added to the share of the part with the greatest `remaining_limit_usd`, and where two parts tie on `remaining_limit_usd` the one with the earlier `created_at` takes it. Rounding each share once at the end from unrounded values is not an acceptable substitute: the parts must sum to `insurer_share_usd` exactly. Each part's `eroded_usd` then rises by its assigned share and its `remaining_limit_usd` becomes its `limit_usd` less its `eroded_usd`, and any part whose `eroded_usd` reaches or passes its `limit_usd` moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`.

From there the cascade of section 2.9 runs to its full depth: the policy's `total_eroded_usd` is re-summed and its `aggregate_remaining_usd` re-derived, every layer still `ATTACHED` whose `attachment_point_usd` is below the new `total_eroded_usd` moves `ATTACHED → EXHAUSTED`, and the policy's `tower_posture` re-derives to `ERODED`. The governing proposal reaches its own terminus in the same call: `CONSENTED → FUNDED` when the `insured_response` was `CONSENT`, and `REFUSED → HAMMER_APPLIED` when it was `REFUSE`. The policy's `open_claim_count` decreases by 1, the governing proposal's, the claim's, the policy's and every touched coverage part's `version` each increment by 1, and the call responds with the updated claim.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — registering an insured, filing a disclosure package or litigation event, opening a submission, issuing a quote, opening an exclusion term, binding a policy, subscribing a layer, reporting a circumstance, noticing or maturing a claim, submitting a defense invoice, recommending a settlement, or endorsing a run-off tail — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state transition, every allocation and both administrative sweeps, return `200 OK`. Reads return `200 OK`. Where the description below says a role is "Any role", all four of `Underwriter`, `RiskManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster` and `ComplianceOfficer` are authorized.
