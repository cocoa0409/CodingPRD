# Fixed-Wireless Rural ISP API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Fixed-wireless broadband in thinly populated country is a business of hard physical limits. A tower can only see so far before the curvature of the ground and the tree line get in the way; a sector antenna can only sell so much bandwidth before the customers already on it start noticing each other; a licensed channel can only be radiated at the power the regulator wrote on the licence, and only inside the dates the licence covers. Every commercial decision — whether a farmhouse eleven kilometres out can be served at all, whether the sales desk may promise a 100 Mbps tier this month, whether a truck roll should carry a wall bracket or a guyed roof mast — is downstream of one of those limits. Today those decisions live in spreadsheets, in the heads of two RF engineers, and in the judgement of whichever field technician happens to be on site, which is why the same premise gets qualified twice with different answers and why sectors quietly reach 900% oversubscription before anyone notices.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single system of record spanning the radio-frequency plant (towers, ground leases, backhaul transport circuits, spectrum licences, sectors, interference coordination with neighbouring operators, and outage incidents) and the commercial book (serviceability qualifications, capacity reservations, subscriber accounts, install work orders, customer-premises equipment custody, metered usage, and service-level credits). The two halves meet at the sector: a sector is simultaneously an RF object with an azimuth and a channel, and a capacity ledger whose posture decides whether the sales desk is allowed to sell one more connection.

The system is deliberately conservative about the order in which it says no. A request that violates several rules at once must be refused with a single, predictable error, because the field technician standing on a roof and the sales agent on a call both need the first actionable reason rather than a list. It is equally deliberate about arithmetic: mast heights are rounded up to the next half metre because half-metre mast sections are what the trucks carry, lease rents are rounded to whole cents at each anniversary because that is what the landowner's invoice says, and oversubscription ratios are carried to three decimal places because a sector sitting exactly on its ceiling must be treated as full. Implementers should read the rounding and ordering rules in this document as part of the contract, not as guidance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Roles and Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Header validation happens before anything else: an unauthenticated caller never learns whether the resource they addressed exists.

The seven roles are:

- **`NetworkEngineer`** — builds and operates the RF plant. Registers towers and drives their lifecycle, orders and accepts backhaul transport circuits, records spectrum licences, creates and activates sectors, opens and answers interference coordination requests with neighbouring operators, opens and closes outage records, and decides tall-mast mounting exceptions.
- **`CapacityPlanner`** — owns oversubscription policy. Publishes service plans, changes a sector's oversubscription ceiling, reserves sector bandwidth, and is the only role that may hand a reservation back.
- **`SalesAgent`** — qualifies premises against a candidate tower, reserves sector bandwidth, converts a qualified premise into an account, changes an account's plan, and schedules the first truck roll.
- **`FieldTech`** — runs the truck roll. Schedules visits, records the on-site survey, books a replacement visit when a resurvey is demanded, completes the install, and receives returned radios.
- **`BillingAdmin`** — runs the account book. Ingests metered usage, closes usage cycles, suspends, restores and cancels accounts, bills unreturned equipment, and prepares service-level credits.
- **`FinanceApprover`** — the second pair of eyes on money. Applies and reverses service-level credits, and never prepares them.
- **`Subscriber`** — the end customer, acting on their own account only.

The first six roles are **operator roles** and their identity is opaque: when `X-User-Role` is `NetworkEngineer`, `CapacityPlanner`, `SalesAgent`, `FieldTech`, `BillingAdmin`, or `FinanceApprover`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any record's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `surveyed_by_user_id` and `exception_approved_by_user_id` on an install work order, as `decided_by_user_id` on an interference coordination, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a service-level credit — and it does compare two such recorded identifiers against each other where segregation of duties applies (§2.11), but it never resolves an operator identifier to a row.

The `Subscriber` role is different: when `X-User-Role` is `Subscriber`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting subscriber account, and ownership checks compare the addressed account's `id` (or, for a listing, the account referenced by the resource) against `X-User-ID`. A caller in the `Subscriber` role may read their own account, their own metered usage days, their own install work orders, their own equipment assignments, and the service-level credit list; addressing any of those against another account's identifier is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The credit list, when requested by a `Subscriber`, must be scoped to that subscriber's own credits.

Authorization is per endpoint. §4 records, for every endpoint, the exact set of roles permitted to call it. A caller whose headers are well formed but whose role is not in that set is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Role membership is checked before the path identifiers are resolved, so an unauthorized caller likewise cannot probe for existence.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when the caller omits it, it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the first returned record; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the returned page.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, list endpoints accept exact-match filters drawn from the listed resource's own fields, and every filter is optional. The backhaul-circuit listing for a tower filters on `state`. The outage-event listing for a tower filters on `status` and on `cause`. The sector listing filters on `tower_id`, `spectrum_license_id`, `capacity_posture`, and `status`. The coordination listing for a sector filters on `status`, and the global coordination listing filters on `requesting_sector_id`, `neighbor_sector_id`, and `status`. The qualification listing filters on `candidate_tower_id`, `qualified_sector_id`, `requested_plan_id`, and `status`. The capacity-hold listing filters on `sector_id`, `serviceability_request_id`, `status`, and `origin`. The account listing filters on `sector_id`, `serviceability_request_id`, `service_plan_id`, `service_status`, and `usage_priority`. An account's install-work-order listing and its equipment-assignment listing each filter on `status`. The service-level credit listing filters on `subscriber_id`, `outage_event_id`, and `status`. An account's metered-usage listing takes no filter beyond `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system is evaluated against real wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock**, and every deadline, expiry, anniversary, cycle window, licence validity check and computed timestamp is resolved against that clock. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until it is moved it stays there: a hold created before any clock movement expires relative to that fixed instant, and a lease anniversary count taken before any clock movement is measured from that same instant.

A test-driving capability lets any of the six operator roles move the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `to_iso8601`; the response body reports the new setting as `current_utc`. The clock may be moved forward or backward, and doing so changes the outcome of every clock-derived value on the next read — a spectrum licence whose `status` was `ACTIVE` becomes `EXPIRED` the moment the clock passes its `effective_to`, without any write to the licence.

Two consequences follow, and both are load-bearing. First, clock-derived states are recomputed on read rather than stored: a `SpectrumLicense` reports `PENDING` while the clock is before `effective_from`, `ACTIVE` while the clock is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`, and `EXPIRED` once the clock reaches or passes `effective_to`. Second, deadline-driven state changes on capacity reservations are evaluated **lazily**: a reservation whose expiry instant the clock has reached is treated as lapsed at the start of any request that reads or writes that reservation, its sector, or the qualification it reserves for. There is no background sweeper, so the outcome depends only on where the logical clock sits when the request arrives.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Record Versioning

Every persisted resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id` on creation.

Almost every resource also carries three system-managed audit fields:

- **`created_at`** — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never changed.
- **`updated_at`** — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- **`version`** — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the record. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a capacity reservation raises a sector's `held_mbps`, the sector's `version` increments even though no client wrote to the sector directly.

There is one exception. A metered-usage day (`UsageRecord`) is immutable once ingested, and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Existence

Identifiers in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing decides the error. The 404 code is the one belonging to the segment that failed: a missing tower yields `ERR_TOWER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing transport circuit `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing spectrum licence `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing sector `ERR_SECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a missing coordination record `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing service plan `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, a missing qualification `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, a missing capacity reservation `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, a missing account `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing install work order `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing equipment assignment `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing outage record `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing service-level credit `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_FOUND`. So a request addressing a sub-resource under a tower that does not exist reports `ERR_TOWER_NOT_FOUND` and never looks for the sub-resource.

Identifiers carried in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and they are checked in the order the endpoint's input fields are listed. Creating a sector, for instance, names `tower_id` before `spectrum_license_id`, so a request naming a non-existent tower and a non-existent licence reports `ERR_TOWER_NOT_FOUND`. The same left-to-right discipline governs qualification creation (`candidate_tower_id` before `requested_plan_id`), coordination creation (`requesting_sector_id` before `neighbor_sector_id`), account creation (`serviceability_request_id` before `service_plan_id`), credit preparation (`outage_event_id` before `subscriber_id`), transport-circuit creation, capacity reservation, plan changes, work-order creation, and outage creation.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the server reports exactly one error, chosen by a fixed ladder of precedence. Implementations must evaluate the rungs in this order and stop at the first violated rung:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and `X-User-Role` recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's permitted set, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request-body shape** — required fields present, types correct, enum values drawn from the declared members, numeric values inside their declared ranges and decimal precision (including latitude and longitude ranges, non-negative traffic volumes, a reservation life between 1 and 30 days inclusive, and a measured throughput supplied on a transport-circuit acceptance), else `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
4. **Pagination bounds** — `limit` and `offset` inside the bounds of §2.3, else `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
5. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, else the matching 404 of §2.6.
6. **Body-reference existence** — every identifier in the body resolves, in field order, else the matching 404 of §2.6.
7. **Ownership** — a caller in the `Subscriber` role addresses only their own account's resources, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
8. **State** — the addressed record is in a state that permits the operation, judged against the transition tables of §3. The error is the state error of the addressed entity: `ERR_INVALID_TOWER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CIRCUIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SECTOR_STATE`, `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_QUALIFIED`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_HOLD_NOT_HELD`, `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_RETURNABLE`, `ERR_OUTAGE_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_CLOSED`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_PENDING`, or `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPLIED`, all HTTP 409. There is exactly one carve-out: an install completion attempted on a work order that is awaiting an engineering mounting decision is **not** reported at this rung — it falls through to rung 11 and is refused with `ERR_MOUNT_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`, because the actionable defect is the missing approval rather than the status label. Every other status that does not permit completion still reports `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` here.
9. **Stale-view protection** — the supplied expected version matches the parent record's current `version`, else the matching 412 of §2.8.
10. **Spectrum compliance** — the proposed radiated power fits inside the licence (`ERR_EIRP_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`), the proposed channel lies wholly inside the licence (`ERR_CHANNEL_OUTSIDE_LICENSE`), the licence is effective at the current logical clock (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`), and the guard-band-inflated proposal does not collide with the neighbour's occupied channel (`ERR_CHANNEL_OVERLAP_GUARD_BAND`). All HTTP 422.
11. **Structural mounting safety** — the erected mast fits the approved band (`ERR_MAST_EXCEEDS_STRUCTURAL_LIMIT`), the work order carries an approved mounting band before completion is accepted (`ERR_MOUNT_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`), and a band nominated on an exception decision is tall enough for the recommended mast (`ERR_MOUNTING_INCOMPATIBLE`). All HTTP 422.
12. **Two-person control and round limits** — segregation of duties is checked first (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403), and only then the exhaustion counters: a negotiation that has used up its rounds (`ERR_COORDINATION_ROUND_LIMIT`, 409) and a work order that has used up its resurveys (`ERR_RESURVEY_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409).
13. **Referential compatibility** — coordination is between sectors on different towers (`ERR_SAME_TOWER_COORDINATION`, 422), the plan permits the chosen equipment acquisition mode and matches the qualified plan (`ERR_PLAN_MISMATCH`, 422), the credited account is served from the tower that went down (`ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_AFFECTED`, 422), and the account has no install work order already open (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_ALREADY_OPEN`, 409).
14. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CIRCUIT_REF`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_REF`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_USAGE_RECORD`, and `ERR_CREDIT_ALREADY_ISSUED`, all HTTP 409.
15. **Temporal and plant-readiness rules** — `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`, `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_USAGE_DATE_OUT_OF_CYCLE`, `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_BACKHAUL_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and `ERR_TOWER_HAS_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIBERS`, all HTTP 409.
16. **Capacity and fiscal gates** — `ERR_SECTOR_CAPACITY_HOLD`, `ERR_SECTOR_FROZEN`, and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECTOR_CAPACITY`, all HTTP 409.

The practical reading of this ladder is that identity beats existence, existence beats state, state beats concurrency, concurrency beats physics, physics beats process, and process beats capacity. A stale-view rejection therefore surfaces even when the sector is also out of allowance, and a segregation-of-duties breach surfaces even when the work order has also exhausted its resurveys.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Sector and Account Writes

Two writes in this system supersede a value the caller has already read, and for those two a state precondition is not enough. Both therefore require the caller to declare which version of the parent record their decision was based on.

**Reserving sector bandwidth** requires `expected_sector_version` in the request body. The field is not optional: a request that omits it is refused by the body-shape rung with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Its value must equal the current `version` of the sector the reservation lands on. Consider a sales desk that read a sector at `version` 7, saw `capacity_posture` of `OPEN` and a comfortable remaining allowance, and then submitted a reservation after another writer had already consumed part of that allowance and advanced the sector to `version` 8. The desk is working from a stale view of the very allowance it is about to take, yet a second reservation on the same sector is a perfectly legitimate operation under the oversubscription policy, so this cannot be expressed as a forbidden state. The write is therefore rejected with `ERR_STALE_SECTOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) whenever `expected_sector_version` does not equal the sector's current `version`.

**Changing an account's plan** requires `expected_subscriber_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional and likewise refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` when absent. Its value must equal the current `version` of the account being changed. A plan change legitimately supersedes the previous plan and re-derives the sector allowance the account occupies, so a second plan change is a valid operation rather than a forbidden state; the only available protection is that the caller's view of the account must be current. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named above must not require one, and must not reject a request because one was supplied or omitted.

### 2.9. Numeric Discipline: Heights, Ratios, Percentages and Cents

All arithmetic in this system is decimal arithmetic. Implementations must not accumulate values through binary floating-point types where a rounding rule is specified, because several of the pinned orderings below differ from the naive result by one unit in the last place.

**Heights, in metres.** Line-of-sight margins are reported to one decimal place, rounded half up. Mast heights are never rounded in the ordinary sense: a required mast height is raised to the next exact multiple of half a metre, so 2.1 metres of raw requirement becomes 2.5 metres and 2.5 metres stays 2.5 metres. A mast height is never rounded down.

**Ratios.** A sector's oversubscription ratio and the transport-side ratio derived from it are both carried to three decimal places, rounded half up. Every threshold comparison on a ratio is inclusive: a ratio landing exactly on a threshold escalates to the stricter posture rather than staying below it.

**Allowances, in Mbps.** A sector's remaining allowance is an integer. It is derived by taking the product of the sector's usable capacity and its oversubscription ceiling, discarding any fractional part downward, and then subtracting the bandwidth already committed. The floor is deliberate: a sector must never sell a fractional Mbps it does not have. Comparisons against the remaining allowance are inclusive, so a plan needing exactly the remaining allowance fits.

**Percentages.** Measured uptime and the shortfall against a plan's committed uptime are both carried to three decimal places, rounded half up.

**Traffic volumes, in GB.** A metered day's total and an account's cycle total are carried to one decimal place, rounded half up. The cycle total is accumulated record by record in chronological order, rounding after each record is added — it is not summed at full precision and rounded once at the end.

**Money.** Every monetary field in this system is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere and no monetary field is ever represented as a string. Where a monetary value is derived by multiplication, the result is rounded half up to whole cents. Three monetary derivations use a floor rather than half-up rounding, and each says so where it is specified: the ceiling on a service-level credit as a fraction of the monthly charge, the ceiling on the rent credited against an unreturned radio, and the sector allowance above. A floor means a 6999-cent plan whose credit is capped at half of the monthly charge caps at 3499 cents, not 3500.

Where a calculation both rounds and then applies a further percentage, the order is pinned and stated at the point of specification. Implementations must not reorder rounding steps for convenience: rounding once at the end of a chain that this document rounds at each step produces a different, wrong number.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation Across the RF and Commercial Aggregates

A single write in this system routinely mutates records the caller never named. Cascades are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a response is returned, every downstream record has already reached its new state, and any derived roll-up has already been recomputed. Each record touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented in its own right, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Cascades are also **transitive, and each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request**. Three chains in particular run four records deep, and implementers should trace each hop rather than treating the endpoint as writing a flat set of fields.

The **transport chain** starts when a transport circuit degrades. Because the circuit leaves its accepted state, the tower's accepted transport rate falls to zero; because the tower now has no accepted transport, every sector on that tower recomputes its posture and lands on `FROZEN`; because those sectors are frozen, every reservation still held on them is released; and because those reservations are gone, the qualifications they were reserving for expire. Restoring the circuit later reverses the first two hops but **not** the last two: released reservations are never resurrected, and expired qualifications never return to a qualified state.

The **capacity-release chain** starts when an account is cancelled or a sector's oversubscription ceiling is loosened. The sector's sold bandwidth drops or its ceiling rises, so its posture is recomputed; because the recomputed posture is no longer withholding or frozen, the oldest deferred qualification on that tower is promoted and a fresh reservation is minted for it; and because that new reservation exists, the sector's held bandwidth rises and the posture is recomputed a second time. Exactly one deferred qualification is promoted per posture recomputation.

The **activation chain** starts when an install is completed. The work order reaches its terminal installed state; because of that, the account leaves its pending state and becomes live; because the account is live, its equipment assignment moves from assigned to deployed; and because the assignment is deployed, the account's derived monthly equipment charge is recalculated from the assignment's rental rate.

Ordering within a cascade matters wherever a later hop reads a value an earlier hop wrote. Where two quantities are conserved — held bandwidth converting into sold bandwidth, or a credit being reversed — the specification states the invariant that must hold after the cascade completes, and implementations must satisfy the invariant exactly, with no rounding drift.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two controls in this system require two different human beings, and both are enforced on `X-User-ID`.

The first governs tall-mast mounting. The `FieldTech` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `surveyed_by_user_id` when the on-site survey was filed may not be the `NetworkEngineer` who decides the resulting mounting exception. If the acting `X-User-ID` on the exception decision equals `surveyed_by_user_id`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The rule applies to all three exception outcomes — approval, a demand for a resurvey, and rejection — not only to approval.

The second governs money. The `BillingAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `prepared_by_user_id` when a service-level credit was prepared may not be the `FinanceApprover` who applies it. If the acting `X-User-ID` on the application equals `prepared_by_user_id`, the application is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Note that the two controls sit on different role pairs, and that neither is a role check: a `NetworkEngineer` is always permitted to decide exceptions and a `FinanceApprover` is always permitted to apply credits. What is forbidden is the same operator identifier appearing on both sides of one control. Interference coordination, by contrast, has **no** segregation-of-duties gate at all: the operator who opened a coordination request may legitimately be the operator who answers it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Tower Sites: Ground Leases, Transport and the Site Lifecycle

A `NetworkEngineer` registers a tower site before anything else can exist. Registration records the operator's own `site_code` as printed on the tower placard — between 4 and 32 characters inclusive, and unique across all towers with case treated as significant, so `RB-NORTH-01` and `rb-north-01` are two different sites. A second tower attempting to reuse an existing code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`. Position is recorded as `latitude` in decimal degrees between -90.0 and 90.0 inclusive to four decimal places, `longitude` between -180.0 and 180.0 inclusive to four decimal places, `ground_elevation_m` as the above-mean-sea-level elevation of the tower base between 0.0 and 4500.0 metres inclusive to one decimal place, and `antenna_agl_m` as the height above ground of the sector antenna mounting band, between 6.0 and 120.0 metres inclusive to one decimal place. Anything outside those windows, or carrying more precision than declared, is a body-shape violation and yields `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The ground lease is captured in the same registration. `lease_landowner_name` names the counterparty. `lease_monthly_base_rent_cents` is the year-one monthly rent in cents with no escalator applied and must not be negative. `lease_escalator_pct` is the annual escalator expressed as a decimal fraction to four decimal places between 0.0 and 0.15 inclusive, so a two-and-three-quarter-percent annual step is recorded as 0.0275 rather than as 2.75. `lease_start_date` is the commencement date in year-month-day form, and every lease anniversary is measured from it. `lease_term_months` is the total committed term, between 12 and 240 inclusive, and must be an exact multiple of twelve — a 30-month term is refused as a body-shape violation.

Upon registration the site opens in `PLANNED` `status`, with `active_sector_count` at zero and `backhaul_accepted_mbps` at zero, and its `current_monthly_rent_cents` is derived immediately.

**How the rent escalates.** The escalation is a compounding step function, and the rounding order is pinned. First count how many anniversaries of `lease_start_date` fall at or before the current logical clock; an anniversary landing exactly on the clock counts. Call that count the elapsed lease years. The rent for lease year zero is `lease_monthly_base_rent_cents` exactly. The rent for each subsequent lease year is the previous year's rent multiplied by one plus `lease_escalator_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Crucially, each year compounds on the **already-rounded** rent of the year before, not on a full-precision running value. `current_monthly_rent_cents` is the rent of the lease year the clock currently sits in. To make the difference concrete: a base rent of 45000 cents escalating at 0.0275 for three elapsed years yields 48817 cents under the pinned order, whereas compounding at full precision and rounding once at the end yields 48816 cents — the latter is wrong.

A computed lease-schedule read, available to `NetworkEngineer`, `BillingAdmin` and `FinanceApprover`, exposes that derivation against the current logical clock. It reports the `tower_id` it describes, `years_elapsed` as the count of anniversaries at or before the clock, `current_monthly_rent_cents`, `next_anniversary_date` as `lease_start_date` advanced by one more year than have elapsed, and `total_committed_cents`. The committed total sums, over every lease year from year zero through the final year of the term — that is, over `lease_term_months` divided by twelve years — that year's escalated monthly rent multiplied by twelve. Note that the total is a whole-lease figure and does not depend on where the clock sits, whereas `current_monthly_rent_cents` and `next_anniversary_date` do.

**Transport comes before service.** A tower carries no traffic without a backhaul circuit, and a `NetworkEngineer` orders one by naming the `tower_id` it hauls for, the `carrier_name` delivering it, the `circuit_ref` the carrier uses to identify it, the `committed_mbps` information rate (at least 1), and the recurring `monthly_cost_cents` (not negative). A `circuit_ref` already in use on another circuit is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CIRCUIT_REF`. A newly ordered circuit opens in `ORDERED` `state` with `measured_mbps`, `accepted_at` and `degraded_since` all absent.

Circuits move through a small cyclic machine, driven by a `NetworkEngineer` who names the target `to_state` and may attach a free-text `note`. `ORDERED → INSTALLED` records that the carrier has delivered. `INSTALLED → ACCEPTED` is the operator's acceptance test and **requires** `measured_mbps` in the body; omitting it is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Acceptance stores the supplied `measured_mbps`, stamps `accepted_at` with the current logical clock, clears `degraded_since`, recomputes the parent tower's `backhaul_accepted_mbps`, and recomputes the capacity posture of every sector on that tower. `ACCEPTED → DEGRADED` stamps `degraded_since` with the clock and, when `measured_mbps` is supplied, overwrites it with the fresh reading; it then drives the transport cascade described below. `DEGRADED → ACCEPTED` is the restore: it requires `measured_mbps` (again `ERR_VALIDATION` when absent), stores it, clears `degraded_since`, and leaves `accepted_at` at its original first-acceptance value rather than re-stamping it. Both `ORDERED` and `INSTALLED` may go straight to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal. Any target state that is not a declared move out of the circuit's current `state` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CIRCUIT_STATE`.

A tower's `backhaul_accepted_mbps` is the `committed_mbps` of the circuit on that tower whose `state` is `ACCEPTED`, and it is zero whenever no circuit on the tower is `ACCEPTED`. That single number is what makes degradation catastrophic. When a circuit falls from `ACCEPTED` to `DEGRADED`, the tower's `backhaul_accepted_mbps` becomes zero; because there is no accepted transport, every sector on the tower recomputes and lands on `FROZEN`; because those sectors are frozen, every capacity reservation still `HELD` on them moves to `RELEASED` with a `release_reason` of `BACKHAUL_DEGRADED` and the sectors' `held_mbps` fall accordingly; and because those reservations are gone, every qualification they were reserving for that sits in `QUALIFIED` or `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` moves to `EXPIRED`. Restoring the circuit to `ACCEPTED` recomputes `backhaul_accepted_mbps` and the sector postures, but reservations already `RELEASED` are **not** restored and expired qualifications do **not** come back.

**The site lifecycle.** A `NetworkEngineer` drives the tower through `PLANNED → ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE → DECOMMISSIONED` by naming a target `to_status` and, optionally, a `reason`. Bringing a `PLANNED` site to `ACTIVE` requires that at least one circuit on the tower be in `ACCEPTED` state; if none is, the request is refused with `ERR_BACKHAUL_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Activation recomputes `backhaul_accepted_mbps` and recomputes `capacity_posture` on every sector of the tower. `ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE` carries no additional precondition beyond the site currently being `ACTIVE`; maintenance does **not** freeze sectors and does not disturb capacity — it is an operational marker used by outage reporting. `MAINTENANCE → ACTIVE` again requires at least one `ACCEPTED` circuit, on pain of `ERR_BACKHAUL_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and again recomputes every sector's posture.

Decommissioning is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `MAINTENANCE`, and it is guarded commercially rather than technically: no account served by any sector of the tower may still be in `PENDING_INSTALL`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` `service_status`. If one is, the request is refused with `ERR_TOWER_HAS_ACTIVE_SUBSCRIBERS`. When the guard passes, the decommission cascade runs in full: every sector on the tower whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `DISABLED`; because each of those sectors has left `ACTIVE`, every reservation on them in `HELD` status transitions to `RELEASED` with a `release_reason` of `SECTOR_DISABLED`; and because each of those reservations has left `HELD`, every qualification they referenced in `QUALIFIED` or `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` transitions to `EXPIRED`. `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal, and any requested `to_status` that is not a declared move out of the tower's current `status` — including any attempt to leave `DECOMMISSIONED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TOWER_STATE`. A tower's `active_sector_count` is at all times the number of its sectors whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, so decommissioning drives it to zero.

### 3.2. Spectrum Licences and Sector Construction

Before any radio may transmit, a `NetworkEngineer` records the licence that authorises it. A licence carries the regulator's `license_ref`, unique across all licences and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_REF` when reused; a `band` drawn from `CBRS_3_5GHZ`, `EBS_2_5GHZ` and `LTE_700MHZ`; the block's `channel_center_mhz` between 600.0 and 6000.0 inclusive to one decimal place; its `channel_width_mhz` between 5.0 and 200.0 inclusive to one decimal place; the `max_eirp_dbm` ceiling between 20.0 and 65.0 inclusive to one decimal place; the regulator's `license_area_code`; and a validity window given as an inclusive `effective_from` and an exclusive `effective_to`, where `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`.

Two edges are derived from the block and reported on every read: `lower_edge_mhz` is `channel_center_mhz` less half of `channel_width_mhz`, and `upper_edge_mhz` is `channel_center_mhz` plus half of `channel_width_mhz`. The licence's `status` is not stored but recomputed against the logical clock on every read: `PENDING` while the clock is before `effective_from`, `ACTIVE` while the clock is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`, and `EXPIRED` once the clock reaches or passes `effective_to`.

A sector is the radio itself, and a `NetworkEngineer` builds one by binding it to a `tower_id` and a `spectrum_license_id` and describing its antenna and channel. `azimuth_deg` is the boresight bearing clockwise from true north, between 0.0 and 359.9 inclusive to one decimal place. `beamwidth_deg` is the horizontal three-decibel beamwidth, between 15.0 and 180.0 inclusive to one decimal place. `channel_center_mhz` and `channel_width_mhz` describe the occupied channel, the former between 600.0 and 6000.0 inclusive and the latter between 5.0 and 100.0 inclusive, both to one decimal place — note the sector's width ceiling of 100.0 MHz is tighter than the licence's 200.0 MHz. `tx_power_dbm` is conducted power at the radio port between 0.0 and 40.0 inclusive to one decimal place, and `antenna_gain_dbi` is the antenna gain between 0.0 and 30.0 inclusive to one decimal place. `capacity_mbps` is the usable aggregate downstream throughput of the sector in whole Mbps and must be at least 1. `max_oversubscription_ratio` is optional; when the caller omits it the sector takes 8.0, and when supplied it must lie between 1.0 and 20.0 inclusive to one decimal place.

Three values are derived at creation. `eirp_dbm` is `tx_power_dbm` plus `antenna_gain_dbi`, rounded to one decimal place. `lower_edge_mhz` and `upper_edge_mhz` follow the same half-width arithmetic as the licence. The sector opens in `PLANNED` `status` with `sold_mbps`, `held_mbps` and `committed_mbps` all zero, `capacity_policy_note` absent, and its `capacity_posture` derived immediately by the posture rules of §3.3.

Sector construction is where the spectrum envelope is first enforced. The derived `eirp_dbm` must not exceed the bound licence's `max_eirp_dbm`, else the creation is refused with `ERR_EIRP_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`. The sector's `lower_edge_mhz` must be at or above the licence's `lower_edge_mhz` and its `upper_edge_mhz` must be at or below the licence's `upper_edge_mhz`, else the creation is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_OUTSIDE_LICENSE`. A sector may be created against a licence that is not yet `ACTIVE`; effectiveness is enforced only at activation and at coordination time.

Bringing a sector on air moves it `PLANNED → ACTIVE`. Three preconditions apply, and each has its own code: the sector's `status` must be `PLANNED`, and activating a sector in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SECTOR_STATE`; the parent tower's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a tower in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TOWER_STATE`; and the bound licence's clock-derived `status` must be `ACTIVE` at the current logical clock, a `PENDING` or `EXPIRED` licence being refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. Activation recomputes the parent tower's `active_sector_count` and the sector's own `capacity_posture`.

Sectors are never disabled by direct request; `DISABLED` is reached only as a consequence of the parent tower being decommissioned, and it is terminal. The two ways in differ in exactly one respect. A sector leaving `ACTIVE` for `DISABLED` recomputes the tower's `active_sector_count`, sets its own `capacity_posture` to `FROZEN`, records `capacity_policy_note` as an explanation that the sector was disabled with its tower, and releases every reservation still `HELD` on it with a `release_reason` of `SECTOR_DISABLED`. A sector leaving `PLANNED` for `DISABLED` sets its `capacity_posture` to `FROZEN` and nothing further, because a planned sector carries no accounts and no reservations to unwind, and because it was never counted in `active_sector_count` in the first place. Once a sector is `DISABLED`, any capacity operation addressed to it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SECTOR_STATE`.

### 3.3. Sector Capacity Posture and the Oversubscription Ledger

Every sector is a small ledger of promised bandwidth, and the entire sales pipeline is gated on its verdict. Three quantities feed it.

`sold_mbps` is the sum, over every account whose `sector_id` is this sector and whose `service_status` is `PENDING_INSTALL`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, of the `downstream_mbps` of that account's **current** plan. A suspended account still occupies its bandwidth; only a `CANCELLED` account stops contributing.

`held_mbps` is the sum of `held_mbps` over every reservation on this sector whose `status` is `HELD`. Reservations in `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` status contribute nothing. Because reservation lapse is evaluated lazily against the logical clock (§2.4), a reservation whose `expires_at` the clock has reached is treated as lapsed **before** this sum is taken, so a stale reservation never inflates the ledger.

`committed_mbps` is simply `sold_mbps` plus `held_mbps`, and `oversubscription_ratio` is `committed_mbps` divided by `capacity_mbps`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

`remaining_allowance_mbps` is the whole number of downstream Mbps a further reservation or plan upgrade may still take on the sector. It is the product of `capacity_mbps` and `max_oversubscription_ratio` with any fractional part discarded downward, less `committed_mbps`, and it is never reported below zero.

**Posture is a four-way verdict with a pinned precedence.** Three fractions of the sector's own ceiling define the bands: the watch line sits at 0.50 of the ceiling, the withhold line at 0.75 of the ceiling, and the freeze line at 1.00 of the ceiling — that is, at `max_oversubscription_ratio` itself. All three comparisons are inclusive, so a ratio landing exactly on a line takes the stricter posture. Evaluate in this order:

1. If the sector's `status` is `DISABLED`, or the parent tower's `status` is `DECOMMISSIONED`, the `capacity_posture` is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if the parent tower's `backhaul_accepted_mbps` is zero — that is, no circuit on the tower is in `ACCEPTED` state — the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if `oversubscription_ratio` is at or above `max_oversubscription_ratio`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
4. Otherwise a provisional posture is read off the air ratio: `HOLD` when `oversubscription_ratio` is at or above three-quarters of `max_oversubscription_ratio`, `WATCH` when it is at or above half of `max_oversubscription_ratio`, and `OPEN` otherwise. A transport ratio is then formed by dividing `committed_mbps` by the parent tower's `backhaul_accepted_mbps` — using 1 as the divisor should that value be below 1 — and rounding half up to three decimal places. If that transport ratio is at or above 12.0 and the provisional posture is `OPEN` or `WATCH`, the posture is escalated to `HOLD`. A provisional posture of `HOLD` is already at that level and is left alone. The escalation is independent of the air ratio: a sector can be transport-limited into `HOLD` while sitting comfortably below its own watch line.

Posture is not a stored decision that ages; it is recomputed on every side effect that touches the ledger. That includes creating a reservation, releasing one, opening an account, changing an account's plan, changing an account's service status, activating a sector, changing a sector's oversubscription policy, changing a transport circuit's state, changing a tower's status, and the lazy lapse of a reservation.

**Changing the ceiling.** A `CapacityPlanner` may retune a sector's oversubscription policy by supplying a new `max_oversubscription_ratio` together with a mandatory `justification`. The new ratio is stored, the `justification` text is recorded on the sector as its `capacity_policy_note` — which stays absent until the first such override — and `oversubscription_ratio` and `capacity_posture` are recomputed immediately against the commitments already on the sector. Loosening a ceiling can therefore move a sector out of `HOLD` or `FROZEN` in one step, and when the recomputed posture is no longer `HOLD` or `FROZEN` the deferred-qualification promotion of §3.6 fires: the first `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` qualification on that tower, ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `intake_sequence` ascending, is promoted and a fresh reservation minted for it, which raises `held_mbps` and forces a second posture recomputation.

**What posture forbids.** A sector whose `capacity_posture` is `HOLD` accepts no new reservation and refuses one with `ERR_SECTOR_CAPACITY_HOLD`. A sector whose posture is `FROZEN` — at its ceiling, disabled, on a decommissioned tower, or on a tower with no accepted transport — likewise accepts none and refuses with `ERR_SECTOR_FROZEN`. Separately, and regardless of posture, a reservation or a plan upgrade that needs more downstream Mbps than `remaining_allowance_mbps` still offers is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECTOR_CAPACITY`; the comparison is inclusive, so a plan needing exactly the remaining allowance is admitted. The posture gates apply to reservation creation; the allowance gate applies both to reservation creation and to plan changes.

### 3.4. Coordinating Spectrum With Neighbouring Towers

Rural towers reuse the same bands, and a sector that wants to change channel or raise power must first ask the operator of an overlapping neighbour. A `NetworkEngineer` opens a coordination by naming the `requesting_sector_id` that wants the change, the `neighbor_sector_id` being asked to tolerate it, and the proposal itself: `proposed_channel_center_mhz`, `proposed_channel_width_mhz`, `proposed_eirp_dbm`, a `guard_band_mhz` between 0.0 and 20.0 inclusive to one decimal place expressing how much clear spectrum the requester will leave on each side of its block, and an `effective_from` instant at which the coordinated parameters would take effect once agreed.

Coordination is between towers, never within one. If the two named sectors share a `tower_id` the request is refused with `ERR_SAME_TOWER_COORDINATION` — intra-site channel planning is an internal matter and needs no negotiation.

Three spectrum checks run on the requesting sector's own licence, in this order. The `proposed_eirp_dbm` must not exceed that licence's `max_eirp_dbm`, else `ERR_EIRP_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`. The proposed block's edges — `proposed_channel_center_mhz` less half of `proposed_channel_width_mhz`, and `proposed_channel_center_mhz` plus half of `proposed_channel_width_mhz` — must both lie inside the licence's `lower_edge_mhz` and `upper_edge_mhz`, else `ERR_CHANNEL_OUTSIDE_LICENSE`. The requesting sector's licence must be clock-effective, that is its derived `status` must be `ACTIVE` at the current logical clock, else `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.

**The guard-band test.** Overlapping spectrum only matters when the two antennas actually illuminate each other, so the disjointness test is conditional on beam geometry. Take the two sectors' `azimuth_deg` values, form the absolute difference, and if that difference exceeds 180 degrees subtract it from 360 to get the short way round the compass. This circular separation is compared against a minimum of 60 degrees. When the circular separation is **60 degrees or more** the beams are considered to be pointing away from each other and no spectrum disjointness is required at all — the proposal passes this test regardless of channel. When the circular separation is **strictly less than** 60 degrees, the requester's proposed block inflated by `guard_band_mhz` on each side must be disjoint from the neighbour's occupied block. Concretely, either the inflated proposed upper edge must be strictly below the neighbour's `lower_edge_mhz`, or the inflated proposed lower edge must be strictly above the neighbour's `upper_edge_mhz`. Because both comparisons are strict, blocks whose edges touch exactly are accepted. Failure is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_OVERLAP_GUARD_BAND`.

A newly opened coordination sits in `REQUESTED` `status` with `round_number` at 1 and `decision_note`, `decided_by_user_id` and `last_response_at` all absent. Unlike the mounting-exception and credit controls of §2.11, coordination carries **no** segregation-of-duties gate: the same `NetworkEngineer` who opened a coordination is entitled to answer it.

**Answering a coordination.** The neighbour's operator responds with a `decision` and may attach a `note`, plus — when countering — a `revised_channel_center_mhz` and a `revised_eirp_dbm`. The coordination must be in `REQUESTED` status for any response to be entertained; a response addressed to a coordination in any other status is refused with `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_OPEN`. Every response, whatever its outcome, stamps `last_response_at` with the current logical clock and records `decided_by_user_id` as the responding operator's identifier.

Accepting moves the coordination `REQUESTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and **applies the proposal to the requesting sector**: its `channel_center_mhz` becomes `proposed_channel_center_mhz`, its `channel_width_mhz` becomes `proposed_channel_width_mhz`, and its `tx_power_dbm` becomes `proposed_eirp_dbm` less that sector's own `antenna_gain_dbi` — the negotiation is conducted in radiated terms but the radio is configured in conducted terms. The sector's `lower_edge_mhz`, `upper_edge_mhz` and `eirp_dbm` are then recomputed from the new channel and power, and the record `version` of both the coordination and the requesting sector is incremented. Note that the derived `eirp_dbm` after application therefore equals the agreed `proposed_eirp_dbm`.

Rejecting moves the coordination `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, and `REJECTED` is likewise terminal. It records the response text as the `decision_note`, and leaves the requesting sector's channel and power completely untouched — no edge, no power and no version on the sector changes.

Requesting a revision moves the coordination `REQUESTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, which is not terminal, and is the neighbour's counter-offer. It is permitted only while `round_number` is strictly below the coordination round limit of 3; at or beyond it the response is refused with `ERR_COORDINATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. When `revised_channel_center_mhz` or `revised_eirp_dbm` are supplied they are recorded as the neighbour's counter-proposal inside `decision_note`; they are advisory text and do **not** overwrite the coordination's proposal fields and do **not** touch either sector.

Withdrawing moves the coordination to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and is reachable from `REQUESTED` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED`. It stamps `last_response_at` and leaves both sectors untouched.

**Revising a proposal.** From `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the requester submits a fresh proposal consisting of `proposed_channel_center_mhz`, `proposed_channel_width_mhz`, `proposed_eirp_dbm` and `guard_band_mhz`, moving the coordination back to `REQUESTED` — this is the cycle that lets a negotiation iterate. The coordination must be in `REVISION_REQUESTED` status, else `ERR_COORDINATION_NOT_OPEN`, and `round_number` must be strictly below 3, else `ERR_COORDINATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. The same three licence checks re-run on the new numbers, with the same three codes — `ERR_EIRP_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`, `ERR_CHANNEL_OUTSIDE_LICENSE` and `ERR_CHANNEL_OVERLAP_GUARD_BAND` — so a revision can be rejected on spectrum grounds exactly as an original proposal can. On success the four proposal fields are overwritten with the new values and `round_number` is increased by one. Because the limit is checked before the increment, and because both the revision and the revision-request paths consume rounds, a negotiation can traverse at most rounds 1, 2 and 3 before further movement is refused.

### 3.5. Service Plans and Premise Qualification

**The plan catalogue.** A `CapacityPlanner` publishes the plans a premise may buy. A plan carries a `tier_code` unique across the catalogue and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE` when reused; `downstream_mbps` and `upstream_mbps`, both whole numbers of at least 1; `monthly_price_cents`, not negative; a `soft_cap_gb` monthly fair-use allowance, strictly greater than zero to one decimal place; a `deprioritization_factor` between 0.05 and 1.0 inclusive to two decimal places, being the fraction of downstream rate the account retains once it has passed its allowance; an `sla_uptime_pct` commitment between 90.0 and 100.0 inclusive to three decimal places; an `sla_credit_method` of either `SLA_SHORTFALL` or `PER_INCIDENT`, which decides how outage compensation is computed for accounts on the plan; and an `equipment_policy` of `RENTAL_ONLY`, `PURCHASE_ONLY` or `RENTAL_OR_PURCHASE`, which decides how the customer premises radio may be acquired.

**Intake.** A `SalesAgent` records an enquiry against a `candidate_tower_id` and a `requested_plan_id`, with a `contact_name` for the household, the premise position as `premise_latitude` and `premise_longitude` in decimal degrees to four decimal places, the premise's `premise_ground_elevation_m` above mean sea level between 0.0 and 4500.0 inclusive to one decimal place, the `roof_height_m` of the intended mounting surface above premise ground between 0.0 and 20.0 inclusive to one decimal place, a `declared_tree_line_height_m` for the vegetation along the path between 0.0 and 60.0 inclusive to one decimal place, an `obstruction_class` of `CLEAR`, `PARTIAL` or `BLOCKED`, and the `path_distance_km` from tower to premise, strictly greater than zero to one decimal place. Each enquiry is stamped with an `intake_sequence` drawn from a system counter that increases by one per enquiry — 1, 2, 3 and so on — which serves as the final tie-break when deferred enquiries compete for freed capacity.

An enquiry begins its life in `EVALUATING`, but that status is never observable: the same call that records the enquiry runs the desktop path study and the sector search to completion, so the enquiry is always returned already in `QUALIFIED`, `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` or `DENIED`.

**The desktop path study.** The geometry is a clearance calculation in metres above mean sea level, and it is used twice in the product — once here from declared values, and again later from field measurements (§3.7). Both variants share the arithmetic below and differ only in their inputs and outputs.

Three elevations are formed. The tower antenna's height above mean sea level is the tower's `ground_elevation_m` plus its `antenna_agl_m`. The obstruction top's height above mean sea level is the premise's `premise_ground_elevation_m` plus the obstruction height for the variant in play — `declared_tree_line_height_m` for the desktop study. The mounting surface's height above mean sea level is `premise_ground_elevation_m` plus the roof height for the variant in play — `roof_height_m` for the desktop study.

A Fresnel clearance requirement is then read off the link length. The band boundaries are half-open on the lower side and inclusive on the upper: a `path_distance_km` at or below 3.0 requires 1.5 metres of clearance; strictly above 3.0 and at or below 8.0 requires 3.0 metres; strictly above 8.0 and at or below 15.0 requires 4.5 metres. A partially obstructed path buys an extra allowance: when the enquiry's `obstruction_class` is `PARTIAL`, a further 1.0 metre is added. Both variants read `obstruction_class` from the enquiry — the field survey does not re-classify obstruction, so a premise recorded as `PARTIAL` at intake still carries the extra metre when the technician's measurements are used. A `CLEAR` path adds nothing. On top of the clearance and the partial-path allowance, a fixed safety margin of 0.5 metres is always added.

The required antenna height above mean sea level is therefore the obstruction top height plus the clearance requirement plus the partial-path allowance plus the 0.5 metre safety margin. The link margin is the tower antenna's height above mean sea level less that required height, rounded half up to one decimal place; a negative margin means the tower cannot see over the obstruction at all. The raw mast requirement is the required antenna height above mean sea level less the mounting surface's height above mean sea level, floored at 0.0 so that a premise whose roof already clears the obstruction needs no mast. That raw figure is then rounded **upward** to the next whole multiple of 0.5 metres, because masts are only sold in half-metre sections: a raw requirement of 2.6 metres becomes 3.0, and a raw requirement of exactly 3.0 stays 3.0.

The rounded mast requirement selects a mounting band, with every upper bound inclusive: at or below 1.0 metre a `WALL_BRACKET`; above that and at or below 3.0 metres a `NON_PEN_ROOF_TRIPOD`; above that and at or below 6.0 metres a `ROOF_MAST_GUYED`; above that and at or below 12.0 metres a `GROUND_POLE_MAST`. A requirement above 12.0 metres selects no band at all and leaves the mounting recommendation absent, because 12.0 metres is the structural limit of anything the field crews will erect at a residential premise.

**Denial takes precedence over everything, and the order is pinned.** In the desktop study only, five refusal conditions are tested in strict sequence and the **first** one that holds decides the outcome; later conditions are not evaluated and their fields are not filled in:

1. `path_distance_km` strictly greater than 15.0 — the enquiry is `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `LINK_DISTANCE_EXCEEDED`.
2. `obstruction_class` equal to `BLOCKED` — `DENIED` with `OBSTRUCTION_BLOCKED`.
3. the computed link margin strictly below zero — `DENIED` with `NO_LINE_OF_SIGHT`.
4. the rounded mast requirement strictly greater than 12.0 — `DENIED` with `MAST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
5. no sector on the candidate tower is in `ACTIVE` status — `DENIED` with `NO_ACTIVE_SECTOR`.

Because the geometry runs before the sector search, a premise beyond 15.0 kilometres of a tower with no sectors at all is denied for distance and not for absence of sectors. The desktop study writes its findings onto the enquiry as `desktop_los_margin_m`, `desktop_required_mast_height_m` and `desktop_mounting_type`; a denial reached at step 1 or step 2 records no geometry, and a denial for mast limit records the margin and the mast requirement while leaving `desktop_mounting_type` absent. A denied enquiry has no `qualified_sector_id` and no `qualification_expires_at`, and `DENIED` is terminal.

**Choosing a sector.** Only when none of the five refusals holds does the sector search run. A sector is eligible when its `tower_id` is the `candidate_tower_id`, its `status` is `ACTIVE`, the licence it is bound to is clock-effective with derived `status` of `ACTIVE`, its `capacity_posture` is `OPEN` or `WATCH`, and its `remaining_allowance_mbps` is at or above the requested plan's `downstream_mbps`. Eligible sectors are ranked by `oversubscription_ratio` ascending — the emptiest sector wins — then by `azimuth_deg` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending. The sector's `id` is **never** consulted as a tie-break, so two sectors identical on all three keys must still be separated by the declared ordering and not by identifier order.

When at least one sector is eligible, the top-ranked one becomes the enquiry's `qualified_sector_id`, the enquiry moves `EVALUATING → QUALIFIED`, and `qualification_expires_at` is set to the current logical clock advanced by the qualification validity of 30 days.

When no sector is eligible, the reason for the empty pool decides between deferral and denial:

- If at least one sector on the tower is `ACTIVE` with a clock-effective licence and was excluded **only** because its `capacity_posture` was `HOLD` or `FROZEN`, or **only** because its `remaining_allowance_mbps` fell short of the plan's `downstream_mbps`, the enquiry moves `EVALUATING → CAPACITY_DEFERRED`. This is not an error: the enquiry is returned successfully, `qualification_expires_at` is still set to the clock plus 30 days, and `qualified_sector_id` stays absent while the enquiry waits in the promotion queue.
- Otherwise, if the tower has no sector in `ACTIVE` status at all, the enquiry moves `EVALUATING → DENIED` with `denial_reason` `NO_ACTIVE_SECTOR`.
- Otherwise every `ACTIVE` sector was excluded because its licence was not clock-effective, and the enquiry is likewise `DENIED` with `NO_ACTIVE_SECTOR`. There is no separate licence-specific denial reason for an enquiry; from the customer's point of view an unlicensed sector is simply not on air.

**Waiting for capacity.** A deferred enquiry is promoted `CAPACITY_DEFERRED → QUALIFIED` by the system, never by a caller, whenever bandwidth frees up on the tower: an account cancels, a reservation is released or lapses, or a planner loosens a sector's oversubscription ceiling. The candidate set is every enquiry whose `candidate_tower_id` is the tower whose sector just recomputed, whose `status` is `CAPACITY_DEFERRED`, and whose `qualification_expires_at` the clock has not yet reached. That set is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `intake_sequence` ascending, and **exactly one** enquiry is promoted — the first in that order whose requested plan's `downstream_mbps` still fits inside the recomputed sector's `remaining_allowance_mbps`. Promotion sets `qualified_sector_id` to that sector and mints a capacity reservation for the enquiry with an `origin` of `AUTO_PROMOTION`, a `hold_days` of 14 and a `held_mbps` equal to the plan's `downstream_mbps`; the sector's ledger and `capacity_posture` are then recomputed a second time, after the promotion, so the posture a caller observes already accounts for the newly minted reservation.

**Two expiries that do not move a status.** There is deliberately no clock-driven transition out of `QUALIFIED`. An enquiry whose `qualification_expires_at` the clock has passed keeps reporting `QUALIFIED`, and the staleness is enforced at the point of use — attempting to reserve against it or to open an account from it is refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Likewise, a `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` enquiry past its expiry keeps reporting `CAPACITY_DEFERRED`; it is simply filtered out of the promotion candidate set and can never be promoted. `QUALIFIED` does move to `EXPIRED`, but only as a consequence of its reservation leaving `HELD` without being consumed, and that transition clears `qualified_sector_id` so the enquiry no longer points at a sector it has no claim on. `CONSUMED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal.

### 3.6. Reserving Bandwidth and Opening an Account

A qualification is a statement about geometry and capacity at one instant; it is not a claim on bandwidth. Between qualifying a premise and signing the household up, a `SalesAgent` or a `CapacityPlanner` takes out a capacity reservation so that the sector's allowance cannot be sold twice.

**Taking a reservation.** The caller names only the `serviceability_request_id` being reserved for, the `expected_sector_version` it believes the sector is at, and optionally `hold_days`. The `hold_days` window defaults to 14 when omitted and must otherwise be a whole number between 1 and 30 inclusive. Everything else is derived and cannot be chosen: `sector_id` is copied from the enquiry's `qualified_sector_id`, and `held_mbps` is copied from the `downstream_mbps` of the enquiry's requested plan. A caller who tries to reserve a different amount than the plan implies is simply ignored on that field — the reservation is always exactly plan-sized, because the reservation exists to model the account that will follow it.

Four business preconditions guard the reservation, and each carries its own code. The enquiry's `status` must be `QUALIFIED`, so a `CAPACITY_DEFERRED`, `DENIED`, `CONSUMED` or `EXPIRED` enquiry is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The current logical clock must be strictly before the enquiry's `qualification_expires_at`, else the request is refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED` — this is the point-of-use enforcement described at the end of §3.5, and a clock landing exactly on the expiry instant is already too late. The sector's `capacity_posture` must not be `HOLD`, else `ERR_SECTOR_CAPACITY_HOLD`, and must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_SECTOR_FROZEN`. And the sector's `remaining_allowance_mbps` must be at or above the plan's `downstream_mbps`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECTOR_CAPACITY`.

The stale-view guard of §2.8 applies here in full: `expected_sector_version` is mandatory and is compared against the `version` of the sector the enquiry is qualified on, with a mismatch refused as `ERR_STALE_SECTOR_VERSION`. Because the posture and allowance checks above are exactly the state a concurrent reservation would have moved, the version comparison is what makes those checks trustworthy rather than advisory.

A successful reservation opens in `HELD` `status` with `origin` `DIRECT`, no `release_reason`, and an `expires_at` set to the current logical clock advanced by `hold_days` days. Its side effects on the sector are immediate: `held_mbps` rises by the reserved amount, `committed_mbps`, `oversubscription_ratio`, `remaining_allowance_mbps` and `capacity_posture` are all recomputed, and the sector's `version` is incremented — which invalidates any other caller's `expected_sector_version` and forces them to re-read.

**Releasing a reservation.** A `CapacityPlanner`, and only a `CapacityPlanner`, may hand bandwidth back manually, optionally recording a `note`. The reservation must be in `HELD` status; one already `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_HELD`. A manual release moves the reservation `HELD → RELEASED` with a `release_reason` of `MANUAL`.

Three further paths reach `RELEASED`, and they are distinguished purely by `release_reason`. When the sector's posture recomputes to `FROZEN` because its tower has lost its accepted transport, the reservation is released with `BACKHAUL_DEGRADED`. When the sector itself transitions to `DISABLED` under a tower decommission, the reservation is released with `SECTOR_DISABLED`. And when the logical clock reaches or passes `expires_at`, the reservation moves `HELD → EXPIRED` — a different status, not `RELEASED` — with a `release_reason` of `LAPSED`. Lapse is evaluated lazily rather than by a timer: the reservation is settled at the moment something reads or recomputes the sector.

All four exits from `HELD` that are not consumption share the same unwinding. The sector's `held_mbps` falls by the reservation's `held_mbps`, `committed_mbps`, `oversubscription_ratio`, `remaining_allowance_mbps` and `capacity_posture` are recomputed, the sector's `version` is incremented, and the enquiry the reservation was protecting, if still `QUALIFIED`, moves to `EXPIRED` with its `qualified_sector_id` cleared. Because freeing bandwidth may open a sector back up, the deferred-promotion selector of §3.5 then runs on that tower.

**Opening the account.** A `SalesAgent` converts a reserved qualification into a subscription by naming the `serviceability_request_id`, the `service_plan_id` being bought, a `billing_email` for invoices, and an `equipment_mode` of either `RENTAL` or `PURCHASE`.

Four preconditions apply. The enquiry's `status` must be `QUALIFIED`, else `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The clock must be strictly before `qualification_expires_at`, else `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`. There must be a reservation for that enquiry still in `HELD` status, else `ERR_HOLD_NOT_HELD` — an account cannot be opened on unreserved bandwidth, and a lapsed reservation is exactly as fatal as no reservation. And the named `service_plan_id` must be the same plan the enquiry was qualified against, that is the enquiry's `requested_plan_id`, else the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_MISMATCH`; the geometry and the capacity search were both run against that plan's `downstream_mbps`, so substituting another plan at signing would invalidate both. `ERR_PLAN_MISMATCH` is also raised here when the chosen `equipment_mode` is not permitted by the plan's `equipment_policy`: `RENTAL` is refused against a `PURCHASE_ONLY` plan, `PURCHASE` is refused against a `RENTAL_ONLY` plan, and a `RENTAL_OR_PURCHASE` plan accepts either.

A new account is stamped with an `account_number` from a deterministic sequential series in the form `RB-000001`, `RB-000002` and so on, zero-padded to six digits. It opens with `service_status` `PENDING_INSTALL` and `usage_priority` `STANDARD`. Its `sector_id` is copied from the enquiry's `qualified_sector_id` and is **never** changed thereafter — an account stays on the sector it was sold on for the whole of its life, including across plan changes. `cycle_start_date` is absent because no billing cycle opens until the radio is actually installed; `cycle_usage_gb` is therefore zero, `consecutive_breach_cycles` is zero, `sticky_deprioritization_active` is false, and `deprioritized_at`, `service_start_date` and `cancelled_at` are all absent. `credit_balance_cents` and `settlement_balance_cents` both start at zero, and `effective_downstream_mbps` starts equal to the plan's `downstream_mbps`.

Opening an account has four consequences beyond the account itself. The enquiry moves `QUALIFIED → CONSUMED`, which is terminal. The reservation moves `HELD → CONSUMED`. On the sector, `held_mbps` falls by the reservation's amount and `sold_mbps` rises by exactly the same amount, so `committed_mbps` and hence `oversubscription_ratio`, `remaining_allowance_mbps` and `capacity_posture` are **unchanged** by the conversion — the reservation was always a stand-in for this account, and the ledger simply reclassifies the bandwidth from reserved to sold. The sector's `version` is nonetheless incremented, because two of its derived fields moved. Finally, exactly one equipment record is minted as a side effect: it opens in `ASSIGNED` `status`, its `acquisition_mode` copied from the account's `equipment_mode`, its `radio_serial` absent because no radio has been allocated yet, its `monthly_rental_cents` at 1000 for a `RENTAL` acquisition and 0 for a `PURCHASE`, its `purchase_price_cents` at 29900 for a `PURCHASE` and 0 for a `RENTAL`, its `replacement_cost_cents` at 34900 in both cases, its `settlement_cents` at 0 and its `settlement_basis`, `deployed_at`, `return_due_at`, `returned_at` and `return_condition` all absent. That record is **not** returned in the account-creation response and has no creation endpoint of its own; the only way to discover it is the per-account equipment listing described in §4. The account's `equipment_monthly_charge_cents` is derived from it and is zero while the radio is merely assigned.

**Changing plan.** A `SalesAgent` or a `BillingAdmin` may move an account to a different `service_plan_id`, supplying `expected_subscriber_version` for the stale-view guard of §2.8; a mismatch against the account's `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIBER_VERSION`. The new plan's `equipment_policy` must still permit the account's existing `equipment_mode`, else `ERR_PLAN_MISMATCH`. And because `sold_mbps` is computed from the account's **current** plan, an upgrade consumes sector allowance: when the new plan's `downstream_mbps` exceeds the old plan's by more than the sector's `remaining_allowance_mbps` can absorb, the change is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECTOR_CAPACITY`. A downgrade never fails that test and hands allowance back. On success, the sector's `sold_mbps` moves by the difference between the two plans' `downstream_mbps`, its ledger and `capacity_posture` are recomputed, and the account's `cycle_usage_gb` position is re-evaluated against the new plan's `soft_cap_gb` and `deprioritization_factor` so that `usage_priority` and `effective_downstream_mbps` reflect the plan the account is actually on. Both the account's and the sector's `version` are incremented. Note that a plan change does **not** move the account between sectors and does **not** create a new reservation, even when the upgrade would not have fitted at qualification time — the allowance check above is the only capacity gate.

### 3.7. The Truck Roll: Field Survey, Mounting Exceptions, Completion and Invoicing

A desktop path study is an estimate from LiDAR and declared heights; the technician on the driveway is the authority. The install work order is the record of that visit.

**Scheduling the visit.** A `FieldTech` or a `SalesAgent` books the appointment by naming the `subscriber_id` and the `scheduled_at` instant. The work order opens in `SCHEDULED` `status` with `resurvey_count` at zero and every survey and completion field absent. Booking is accepted for an account whose `service_status` is `PENDING_INSTALL`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — a visit to an existing account is a legitimate re-install or relocation — but an account already `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Only one visit may be in flight at a time: if the account already has a work order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `EXCEPTION_PENDING`, `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` or `MOUNT_APPROVED`, the booking is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_ALREADY_OPEN`. Work orders in `INSTALLED`, `SURVEY_FAILED` or `CANCELLED` status are finished and do not block a new booking. Note that `MOUNT_APPROVED` counts as in flight: a mount that has been approved but not yet built is still an open commitment.

**The field survey.** On site, the `FieldTech` records `measured_tree_line_height_m` between 0.0 and 60.0 inclusive to one decimal place, `measured_roof_height_m` between 0.0 and 20.0 inclusive to one decimal place, and a `structure_penetration_allowed` flag stating whether the householder and the roof structure permit a bolted-through mast. A free-text `note` may be attached. The survey is permitted only from `SCHEDULED`; a survey submitted against a work order in any other status is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`.

The survey re-runs the clearance arithmetic of §3.5 in its field variant. Every formula, every clearance band, the partial-path allowance, the 0.5 metre safety margin, the half-metre mast rounding and the four mounting bands are identical; the only differences are that the obstruction height comes from `measured_tree_line_height_m` rather than `declared_tree_line_height_m`, and the mounting surface height comes from `measured_roof_height_m` rather than `roof_height_m`. The `obstruction_class` is still read from the enquiry, so the extra 1.0 metre for a `PARTIAL` path applies to the field variant exactly as it does to the desktop variant. The field variant writes its findings onto the work order as `field_los_margin_m`, `recommended_mast_height_m` and `recommended_mounting_type`, and stamps `surveyed_by_user_id` with the surveying technician's identifier. It **never** mutates the enquiry: the `desktop_los_margin_m`, `desktop_required_mast_height_m` and `desktop_mounting_type` recorded at intake are preserved as the historical estimate, and the field variant cannot deny an enquiry or change its `status`.

A single survey then forks into one of four outcomes, decided entirely by the numbers it just produced:

- **Straight to an approved mount.** When `field_los_margin_m` is at or above zero, `recommended_mast_height_m` is at or below 12.0, and `recommended_mounting_type` is `WALL_BRACKET` or `NON_PEN_ROOF_TRIPOD`, the work order moves `SCHEDULED → MOUNT_APPROVED` and `approved_mounting_type` is set equal to `recommended_mounting_type`. These two bands are light structures that a technician may erect on their own authority, so no engineering exception is sought and `exception_approved_by_user_id` stays absent.
- **Held for an engineering exception.** When `field_los_margin_m` is at or above zero, `recommended_mast_height_m` is at or below 12.0, and `recommended_mounting_type` is `ROOF_MAST_GUYED` or `GROUND_POLE_MAST` — with the additional requirement that `structure_penetration_allowed` is true when the band is `ROOF_MAST_GUYED` — the work order moves `SCHEDULED → EXCEPTION_PENDING` and `approved_mounting_type` is deliberately left absent. These are guyed and ground-anchored structures that need an engineer's sign-off before anyone climbs.
- **Sent back for a resurvey.** When `field_los_margin_m` is at or above zero, `recommended_mast_height_m` is at or below 12.0, `recommended_mounting_type` is `ROOF_MAST_GUYED` and `structure_penetration_allowed` is false, the work order moves `SCHEDULED → RESURVEY_REQUIRED` and `resurvey_count` is increased by one. The geometry demands a mast that must be bolted through the roof and the premise forbids it, so the visit must be re-planned rather than escalated. A `GROUND_POLE_MAST` recommendation is unaffected by the penetration flag, since it is anchored in the ground and never touches the structure.
- **Failed on geometry.** When `field_los_margin_m` is strictly below zero, or `recommended_mast_height_m` is strictly above 12.0, the work order moves `SCHEDULED → SURVEY_FAILED`, which is terminal. When the failure is the mast limit, `recommended_mounting_type` is left absent because no band accommodates the requirement. The account is **not** automatically cancelled by a failed survey: it remains in `PENDING_INSTALL` and continues to occupy its share of the sector's `sold_mbps` until somebody cancels it explicitly.

All four outcomes set `field_los_margin_m`, `recommended_mast_height_m` and `surveyed_by_user_id`.

**Deciding an exception.** From `EXCEPTION_PENDING`, a `NetworkEngineer` records a `decision` together with a mandatory `note` and, when approving, an optional `approved_mounting_type`. The work order must be in `EXCEPTION_PENDING`; any other status is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The segregation-of-duties control of §2.11 applies to all three outcomes: the deciding engineer's identifier must differ from the work order's `surveyed_by_user_id`, else the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that check runs **before** the resurvey-exhaustion check below. The three outcomes are:

- **Approve** moves the work order `EXCEPTION_PENDING → MOUNT_APPROVED` and records `exception_approved_by_user_id`. The `approved_mounting_type` is taken from the body when supplied and defaults to `recommended_mounting_type` when omitted. Whichever band ends up approved, its ceiling — 1.0 metre for `WALL_BRACKET`, 3.0 for `NON_PEN_ROOF_TRIPOD`, 6.0 for `ROOF_MAST_GUYED`, 12.0 for `GROUND_POLE_MAST` — must be at or above `recommended_mast_height_m`, else the decision is refused with `ERR_MOUNTING_INCOMPATIBLE`. An engineer may therefore approve a **stronger** band than was recommended but never a weaker one.
- **Request a resurvey** moves the work order `EXCEPTION_PENDING → RESURVEY_REQUIRED` and records `exception_approved_by_user_id`. It is permitted only while `resurvey_count` is strictly below the resurvey limit of 2, else the decision is refused with `ERR_RESURVEY_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success `resurvey_count` is increased by one and the three survey outputs — `field_los_margin_m`, `recommended_mast_height_m` and `recommended_mounting_type` — are cleared, because the engineer has judged the measurements themselves unreliable and the next visit must produce fresh ones.
- **Reject** moves the work order `EXCEPTION_PENDING → SURVEY_FAILED`, which is terminal, and still records `exception_approved_by_user_id` — the field is an audit trail of who decided, not a record of approval, so it is stamped on a rejection exactly as it is on an approval. As with a survey-driven failure, the account is left in `PENDING_INSTALL` and is not cancelled.

**Re-booking after a resurvey.** From `RESURVEY_REQUIRED`, a `FieldTech` supplies a fresh `scheduled_at` and the work order returns `RESURVEY_REQUIRED → SCHEDULED`, ready for another survey — the loop that lets a difficult premise be visited more than once. The work order must be in `RESURVEY_REQUIRED`, else `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, and `resurvey_count` must be at or below the resurvey limit of 2, else `ERR_RESURVEY_LIMIT_REACHED`. Observe the deliberate asymmetry with the exception path: requesting a further resurvey demands `resurvey_count` **strictly below** 2, whereas re-booking a resurvey that has already been granted demands only that it be **at or below** 2. The count is incremented when the resurvey is ordered, not when the visit is booked, so a work order that has just been sent back for its second resurvey sits at exactly 2 and may still be re-booked once.

**Completing the install.** A `FieldTech` closes out the visit by reporting the `installed_mast_height_m` actually erected — between 0.0 and 12.0 inclusive to one decimal place — and the `radio_serial` of the customer premises unit left on the wall. Completion is permitted only from `MOUNT_APPROVED`, and the refusal is deliberately split: a work order still sitting in `EXCEPTION_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_MOUNT_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`, telling the technician that an engineer must sign the mount off first, while a work order in `SCHEDULED`, `RESURVEY_REQUIRED`, `INSTALLED`, `SURVEY_FAILED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with the generic `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The linked account's `service_status` must be `PENDING_INSTALL`, else `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; an account already `ACTIVE` cannot be activated a second time. And the `installed_mast_height_m` must not exceed the ceiling of the `approved_mounting_type` — the same four ceilings of 1.0, 3.0, 6.0 and 12.0 metres — else the completion is refused with `ERR_MAST_EXCEEDS_STRUCTURAL_LIMIT`. Note that this compares what was actually built against what was approved, which is a different test from the `ERR_MOUNTING_INCOMPATIBLE` check that compared the approved band against what was recommended.

On success the work order moves `MOUNT_APPROVED → INSTALLED`, which is terminal, storing `installed_mast_height_m`, `radio_serial` and an `installed_at` stamp taken from the logical clock, and computing `install_invoice_total_cents`.

**Pricing the install.** The invoice is a tiered calculation, and the order of evaluation is pinned because each step feeds the next. Begin with a labour charge selected by `approved_mounting_type`: 4900 cents for a `WALL_BRACKET`, 9900 for a `NON_PEN_ROOF_TRIPOD`, 19900 for a `ROOF_MAST_GUYED`, and 34900 for a `GROUND_POLE_MAST`. Add a mast kit charge of 1750 cents per half-metre section, where the number of sections is `installed_mast_height_m` divided by 0.5 and rounded **upward** to a whole number — a 2.6 metre mast bills six sections. Add the fixed trip charge of 9900 cents. The sum of trip charge, labour and mast kit is the subtotal.

A volume discount is then read off the **subtotal only**, with inclusive lower bounds: a subtotal at or above 30000 cents earns 5000 cents off, at or above 20000 earns 3000, at or above 10000 earns 1000, and anything below 10000 earns nothing. The net amount is the subtotal less that discount. A regulatory cost-recovery fee of 3.25 percent is then applied to the **net** amount and rounded half up to whole cents, and `install_invoice_total_cents` is the net amount plus that fee. The discount tier is selected once, from the pre-discount subtotal, and is **never** re-evaluated after the discount or the regulatory fee has moved the running total — an invoice whose subtotal was 20100 keeps its 3000-cent discount even though the net of 17100 would have qualified only for 1000.

**Activating service.** Completing an install is the event that turns a signed-up household into a live one, and it fires a chain of three. The work order becomes `INSTALLED`. The account moves `PENDING_INSTALL → ACTIVE`, with `service_start_date` set from the logical clock and `cycle_start_date` set to the calendar date of that same instant, which opens the account's first billing cycle. And the account's equipment record moves `ASSIGNED → DEPLOYED`, with `deployed_at` stamped from the clock and its `radio_serial` copied from the `radio_serial` reported on the completion, so that the unit on the wall and the record of it agree. The account's `equipment_monthly_charge_cents` is then recomputed and becomes the equipment record's `monthly_rental_cents` — 1000 cents for a rental, zero for a purchase — because the recurring charge only begins once the radio is deployed.

### 3.8. Account Life: Metered Cycles, Deprioritization, Suspension, Cancellation and Equipment Settlement

A live account carries **two independent state dimensions**. Its `service_status` records the commercial relationship — `PENDING_INSTALL`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `CANCELLED`. Its `usage_priority` records the traffic-shaping posture — `STANDARD` or `DEPRIORITIZED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's value: a `SUSPENDED` account may perfectly well be `DEPRIORITIZED`, and returning it to `ACTIVE` does not reset its shaping posture. `usage_priority` has no terminal value and can oscillate for the whole life of the account.

**Metering a day.** A `BillingAdmin` ingests one day of traffic at a time, supplying the `usage_date` in year-month-day form together with `downstream_gb` and `upstream_gb`, each not negative and to one decimal place. Each record is stamped with an `ingest_sequence` from a system counter that increases by one per record ingested, which fixes the processing order when two records share a `usage_date`, and its `total_gb` is derived as `downstream_gb` plus `upstream_gb` to one decimal place. A record also reports `in_current_cycle`, true when its `usage_date` falls inside the account's current cycle window. Metered days are the one record type in this product that carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, as noted in §2.5: they are immutable observations, never edited.

Two constraints guard ingestion. Only one record may exist per account per day, so a second record for the same `subscriber_id` and `usage_date` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_USAGE_RECORD`. And the `usage_date` must fall inside the account's current cycle window — at or after `cycle_start_date` and strictly before `cycle_start_date` advanced by the cycle length of 30 days — else the ingestion is refused with `ERR_USAGE_DATE_OUT_OF_CYCLE`. Back-dating into a cycle that has already been closed, or forward-dating into one that has not yet opened, is therefore impossible; history is only ever accumulated within the open cycle.

**Deriving the cycle position.** `cycle_usage_gb` is recomputed from scratch on every ingestion and on every cycle close. Take every metered day of the account whose `usage_date` lies in the half-open window running from `cycle_start_date` up to but not including `cycle_start_date` plus 30 days, and process them in order of `usage_date` ascending and then `ingest_sequence` ascending. Starting from zero, for each record in turn add that record's `downstream_gb` and `upstream_gb` to the running total and round the running total half up to one decimal place **before moving to the next record**. `cycle_usage_gb` is the final running total. The rounding happens once per record, not once at the end over a full-precision sum, and the two orders can differ by a tenth of a gigabyte on a long cycle.

The cycle is breached when `cycle_usage_gb` is **strictly greater** than the plan's `soft_cap_gb`; usage landing exactly on the cap is not a breach. `usage_priority` is then `DEPRIORITIZED` when either the current cycle is breached or `sticky_deprioritization_active` is true, and `STANDARD` otherwise. `effective_downstream_mbps` follows: while `DEPRIORITIZED` it is the plan's `downstream_mbps` multiplied by the plan's `deprioritization_factor`, floored at a minimum deliverable rate of 0.1 Mbps, and rounded half up to one decimal place — so a heavy user on a 25 Mbps plan with a factor of 0.05 is shaped to 1.3 Mbps rather than to 1.25, and a 1 Mbps plan with a factor of 0.05 is shaped to the 0.1 Mbps floor rather than to 0.1 by rounding coincidence. While `STANDARD`, `effective_downstream_mbps` is simply the plan's `downstream_mbps`.

Moving `STANDARD → DEPRIORITIZED` requires that the account's `service_status` be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; an account still in `PENDING_INSTALL` has no open cycle to breach. The transition stamps `deprioritized_at` with the current logical clock and recomputes `effective_downstream_mbps`.

**Closing a cycle.** A `BillingAdmin` closes the open cycle with no body at all; everything the close needs is already on the account. The close first judges the cycle that is ending against the plan's `soft_cap_gb`, then rolls the window forward, then re-derives the posture over the new window. There are four outcomes, one per combination of ending posture and cycle verdict:

- **A clean cycle on a `STANDARD` account** leaves `usage_priority` at `STANDARD`. `consecutive_breach_cycles` is set to zero and `cycle_start_date` advances by 30 days.
- **A breached cycle on a `STANDARD` account** moves the account `STANDARD → DEPRIORITIZED` by the ingestion rule above rather than by the close itself; the close then increases `consecutive_breach_cycles` by one and advances `cycle_start_date` by 30 days.
- **A clean cycle on a `DEPRIORITIZED` account** moves it `DEPRIORITIZED → STANDARD`, provided the closing cycle's `cycle_usage_gb` was at or below `soft_cap_gb` and `consecutive_breach_cycles` after the close is strictly below the sticky threshold of 2. `consecutive_breach_cycles` is set to zero, `sticky_deprioritization_active` is set to false, `deprioritized_at` is cleared, `cycle_start_date` advances by 30 days, and `cycle_usage_gb` is recomputed over the new window with `effective_downstream_mbps` restored to the plan's `downstream_mbps`.
- **A breached cycle on a `DEPRIORITIZED` account** does not itself fire the restore transition. `consecutive_breach_cycles` is increased by one, and once that counter reaches the sticky threshold of 2, `sticky_deprioritization_active` is set to true — which is what makes the shaping survive the next cycle: the account opens the following window already `DEPRIORITIZED` even at zero metered usage, and only a clean cycle can clear it. `cycle_start_date` advances by 30 days and `cycle_usage_gb` and `effective_downstream_mbps` are recomputed over the new window. The posture itself is then re-derived over that new window by the derivation rule above and by nothing else, so the account is still `DEPRIORITIZED` after the close only when `sticky_deprioritization_active` has become true or the new window is already breached in its own right: a single breach, which leaves the counter at one and the flag false, returns the account to `STANDARD` with `deprioritized_at` cleared and `effective_downstream_mbps` back at the plan's `downstream_mbps` until the new window breaches in its turn.

Because the counter is incremented before the sticky flag is tested, and because the flag is only cleared by a clean cycle that leaves the counter strictly below 2, an account that breaches twice in a row must serve a full clean cycle before its rate is restored.

**Suspension and reinstatement.** A `BillingAdmin` moves an account between commercial states by naming the target `to_status` and a mandatory `reason`. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` is a billing suspension; it explicitly does **not** release the account's share of the sector's `sold_mbps`, because a suspended household is expected back and its bandwidth is still allocated. `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` reinstates it. Both increment the account's `version`. Any target that is not a declared move out of the account's current `service_status` — including any attempt to leave `CANCELLED`, which is terminal — is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

**Cancellation, and what it frees.** `CANCELLED` is reachable from `PENDING_INSTALL`, from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`, and it stamps `cancelled_at` from the logical clock. Its effects run in a fixed chain. The sector's `sold_mbps` falls by the `downstream_mbps` of the account's current plan, and `committed_mbps`, `oversubscription_ratio`, `remaining_allowance_mbps` and `capacity_posture` are recomputed. Because that recomputation may have opened the sector back up, the deferred-promotion selector of §3.5 then runs on the tower: the first enquiry in `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` on that tower, ordered by `created_at` ascending and then `intake_sequence` ascending and still inside its `qualification_expires_at`, is promoted to `QUALIFIED` and given a fresh reservation with `origin` `AUTO_PROMOTION`, after which the sector's posture is recomputed a second time. One cancellation therefore promotes at most one waiting household, however many are queued.

Cancellation also settles the hardware, and the two entry states behave differently. Cancelling from `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` means the radio is on the customer's wall, so the equipment record moves `DEPLOYED → RETURN_PENDING`, its `return_due_at` is set to the logical clock advanced by the return window of 21 days, and the account's `equipment_monthly_charge_cents` drops to zero because the rental has stopped accruing. Cancelling from `PENDING_INSTALL` means no radio was ever deployed, so the equipment record moves `ASSIGNED → VOIDED` with `settlement_cents` at zero and `settlement_basis` recorded as `NONE`; there is nothing to return and nothing to bill. Cancelling from `PENDING_INSTALL` additionally cancels the visit: every work order for the account whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `EXCEPTION_PENDING` or `RESURVEY_REQUIRED` moves to `CANCELLED`.

**Closing out the hardware.** A radio in `RETURN_PENDING` leaves that status by one of exactly two routes, and both compute a settlement.

A `FieldTech` records a physical return, supplying the `return_condition` as either `GOOD` or `DAMAGED`. The record must be in `RETURN_PENDING`; one in `ASSIGNED`, `DEPLOYED`, `RETURNED`, `UNRETURNED_BILLED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_RETURNABLE`. On success it moves `RETURN_PENDING → RETURNED`, which is terminal, stamping `returned_at` from the logical clock and storing `return_condition`.

A `BillingAdmin` writes off a radio that never came back, optionally recording a `note`. The record must again be in `RETURN_PENDING`, else `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_RETURNABLE`, and the return window must have closed: the logical clock must be at or after `return_due_at`, else the write-off is refused with `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_OPEN`. The comparison is inclusive, so a clock landing exactly on `return_due_at` may already bill. On success the record moves `RETURN_PENDING → UNRETURNED_BILLED`, which is terminal.

**How the settlement is computed.** First establish the tenure: `months_in_service` is the number of whole days from `deployed_at` to the settlement instant divided by a settlement month of 30 days, with any fractional part discarded downward. A record that was never deployed has `months_in_service` of zero. The arithmetic then diverges completely by `acquisition_mode`.

For a `RENTAL` record, the operator owns the radio and is out of pocket if it does not come back. When the record is being written off as unreturned, the rent already collected is credited against the loss: the rental credit is `monthly_rental_cents` multiplied by `months_in_service`, capped at 60 percent of `replacement_cost_cents` with the cap's fractional cents discarded downward — on the standard 34900-cent radio the cap is 20940 cents, which a 1000-cent monthly rental reaches after 21 months and never exceeds however long the customer keeps it. `settlement_cents` is `replacement_cost_cents` less that credit, a positive amount owed by the customer, and `settlement_basis` is `RENTAL_CREDIT_OFFSET`. When the record is being returned with a `return_condition` of `DAMAGED`, `settlement_cents` is 50 percent of `replacement_cost_cents` rounded half up to whole cents, and `settlement_basis` is `RENTAL_DAMAGE`; the tenure is irrelevant to a damage charge. When the record is being returned with a `return_condition` of `GOOD`, `settlement_cents` is zero and `settlement_basis` is `NONE`.

For a `PURCHASE` record, the customer already owns the radio, which inverts every case. When the record is being written off as unreturned, `settlement_cents` is zero and `settlement_basis` is `NONE` — the operator cannot bill a customer for failing to return their own property. When the record is returned, in **either** condition, the operator buys it back at a depreciated value: the residual is `purchase_price_cents` multiplied by the depreciation months remaining, being 36 less `months_in_service` and floored at zero, divided by 36, rounded half up to whole cents; a restocking fee of 15 percent of that residual is then computed and rounded half up to whole cents; and `settlement_cents` is the **negative** of the residual less the restocking fee, signalling a refund the operator owes rather than a charge the customer owes. `settlement_basis` is `DEPRECIATED_REFUND`. Note the order: the residual is rounded to whole cents first and the restocking fee is computed from that rounded residual, not from a full-precision value.

The account's `settlement_balance_cents` is the sum of `settlement_cents` over its equipment records whose `status` is `RETURNED`, `UNRETURNED_BILLED` or `VOIDED`. Records still in `ASSIGNED`, `DEPLOYED` or `RETURN_PENDING` contribute nothing, so the balance only ever reflects concluded hardware. Because a purchase refund is negative, the balance can legitimately be below zero.

### 3.9. Outages, Service-Level Exclusions and Customer Credits

Rural towers go down, and the plans promise an uptime figure. Outage records are the operational log; credits are the money that follows from it.

**Recording an outage.** A `NetworkEngineer` opens an outage against a `tower_id`, naming its `cause` — one of `WEATHER_WIND`, `WEATHER_ICE`, `WEATHER_FLOOD`, `POWER`, `BACKHAUL`, `EQUIPMENT` or `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE` — and the `started_at` instant, which is inclusive. For planned work the `planned_notice_days` given to customers may be recorded at the same time. The outage opens in `OPEN` `status` with `ended_at`, `peak_wind_mph` and `duration_minutes` all absent, `sla_excluded` false and `total_credited_cents` zero. Note that a tower need not be in any particular `status` to have an outage recorded against it; a tower in `MAINTENANCE` is precisely where planned outages are expected.

**Closing an outage.** The engineer closes it by supplying the `ended_at` instant, which is exclusive, and — for wind events — the `peak_wind_mph` measured at the site. The outage must be in `OPEN` status, and closing one already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The interval must be well formed, with `ended_at` strictly after `started_at`, else `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. And the closed interval, taken as inclusive of `started_at` and exclusive of `ended_at`, must share no minute with any other already-`CLOSED` outage on the same tower, else `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; because the interval is half-open, two outages that merely abut — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — are accepted, while any genuine overlap of even one minute is refused. Closure stores `ended_at` and `peak_wind_mph`, computes `duration_minutes` as the whole minutes between the two instants with any fractional minute discarded, and fixes `sla_excluded`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

`sla_excluded` marks an outage the operator does not owe compensation for, and it is decided entirely by `cause`. A `WEATHER_WIND` outage is excluded when `peak_wind_mph` — treated as 0.0 when it was never supplied — is at or above 58.0 miles per hour. A `WEATHER_ICE` or `WEATHER_FLOOD` outage is always excluded as declared force majeure, regardless of any wind figure. A `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE` outage is excluded when `planned_notice_days` — treated as zero when it was never supplied — is at or above 7 days, so short-notice maintenance is **not** excluded and does attract credits. A `POWER`, `BACKHAUL` or `EQUIPMENT` outage is never excluded; those are the operator's own plant.

**Preparing a credit.** A `BillingAdmin` prepares one credit per affected household by naming the `outage_event_id` and the `subscriber_id`. Three preconditions apply. The outage must be `CLOSED`, since an open outage has no measurable duration; an `OPEN` outage is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_CLOSED`. The household must actually be served from the affected site: the tower of the account's `sector_id` must be the outage's `tower_id`, else the request is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_AFFECTED`. And no credit may already exist for that same outage and account in `PENDING` or `APPLIED` status, else `ERR_CREDIT_ALREADY_ISSUED`; a credit previously `DENIED` or `REVERSED` does not block a fresh attempt. The credit records `prepared_by_user_id` as the preparing administrator's identifier and copies its `method` from the plan's `sla_credit_method`, which selects between the two computations below.

**Common groundwork.** The measurement window is the account's own current cycle: from `cycle_start_date` inclusive to `cycle_start_date` plus 30 days exclusive. For any outage, its clipped minutes are the whole minutes of its half-open interval that fall inside that window, with any fractional minute discarded — an outage straddling a cycle boundary contributes only the part inside. Its creditable minutes are zero when the outage is `sla_excluded`, or when its `status` is not `CLOSED`, or when its clipped minutes fall short of the 60-minute minimum; otherwise its creditable minutes equal its clipped minutes. A monthly cap is also formed: 50 percent of the plan's `monthly_price_cents` with any fractional cent discarded downward, so a 6999-cent plan caps at 3499 rather than 3500.

**The `SLA_SHORTFALL` method** compensates against the uptime promise across the whole cycle. `cycle_outage_minutes` is the sum of creditable minutes over **every** `CLOSED` outage on the account's tower, not merely the one named in the request. `measured_uptime_pct` is 100 multiplied by the shortfall-free share of the month — that is, the standard monthly allowance of 43200 minutes less `cycle_outage_minutes`, divided by 43200 — rounded half up to three decimal places. `shortfall_pct` is the plan's `sla_uptime_pct` less `measured_uptime_pct`, floored at zero, rounded half up to three decimal places. The cycle's gross entitlement is the plan's `monthly_price_cents` multiplied by `shortfall_pct`, divided by 100, multiplied by the shortfall multiplier of 10, rounded half up to whole cents, and then limited to the monthly cap. Because the account may already have been credited for other outages in the same cycle, `prior_cycle_credited_cents` is the sum of `credit_cents` over every **other** credit for the same account whose `status` is `PENDING` or `APPLIED` and whose outage intersects the same cycle window; credits in `DENIED` or `REVERSED` status never count. `credit_cents` is the gross entitlement less that prior total, floored at zero. Note the precision discipline: all the cycle's minutes are aggregated first and the money is rounded **once** at the end, before the prior total is subtracted — rounding each outage's contribution separately would give a different answer.

**The `PER_INCIDENT` method** compensates strictly for the outage named in the request. `cycle_outage_minutes` is that single outage's creditable minutes. `measured_uptime_pct` and `shortfall_pct` are both recorded as 0.0 and `prior_cycle_credited_cents` as zero, because none of the three has any meaning under this method — they are reported for structural uniformity, not as measurements. `credit_cents` is the plan's `monthly_price_cents` multiplied by those creditable minutes, divided by the monthly allowance of 43200 minutes, rounded half up to whole cents, and then limited to the same monthly cap. A per-incident credit therefore refunds pro-rata for the minutes lost, and repeated outages in one cycle each earn their own credit up to the cap, without any prior-credit netting.

**Denial is decided in a pinned order** and, unlike everything else in this product, a denial is not an error: the credit record is created successfully with `credit_cents` at zero and a `denial_reason`. The first condition that holds wins:

1. the outage named in the request is `sla_excluded` — `denial_reason` is `SLA_EXCLUDED`;
2. that outage's clipped minutes fall short of the 60-minute minimum — `denial_reason` is `BELOW_MINIMUM_DURATION`;
3. the computed `credit_cents` is zero — `denial_reason` is `NO_SHORTFALL`.

A denied credit is created directly in `DENIED` status, which is terminal; `PENDING` is never observable for it. Only when none of the three holds is the credit created `PENDING` with `credit_cents` strictly above zero, `approved_by_user_id` and `reversal_reason` absent.

**Applying and reversing.** A `FinanceApprover` releases the money, optionally recording a `note`. The credit must be in `PENDING` status; one in `APPLIED`, `DENIED` or `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_PENDING`. The segregation-of-duties control of §2.11 applies: the approving identifier must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the credit moves `PENDING → APPLIED`, `approved_by_user_id` is stamped, the account's `credit_balance_cents` rises by `credit_cents`, and the outage's `total_credited_cents` rises by the same amount.

A `FinanceApprover` may reverse an applied credit, supplying a mandatory `reversal_reason`. The credit must be in `APPLIED` status, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPLIED`. On success it moves `APPLIED → REVERSED`, which is terminal, and both roll-ups are decreased by `credit_cents`. Because `credit_cents` is fixed at preparation and is **never** recomputed afterwards — not by a later cycle close, not by a further outage, not by a plan change — a reversal returns `credit_balance_cents` and `total_credited_cents` to exactly the values they held before the application, to the cent. `credit_balance_cents` is at all times the sum of `credit_cents` over the account's credits in `APPLIED` status, and an outage's `total_credited_cents` is the sum over its own `APPLIED` credits; both therefore exclude `PENDING`, `DENIED` and `REVERSED` rows.
