# Surplus Public Land Disposition API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Public agencies accumulate land they no longer need — a closed maintenance yard, a remnant strip left over from a road widening, a school site abandoned after a boundary change. Disposing of that land is not a real-estate transaction; it is a statutory procedure. Before a deed can be signed the agency must declare the parcel surplus by formal act of its governing body, serve notice on every other public agency and preference entity on the statutory registry, negotiate in good faith with any preference entity that asserts a right, obtain and defend an independent appraisal, complete environmental review, cure or reserve every defect in title, and then convey under exactly the authority the board granted — competitive sale, negotiated sale, or exchange. Each of those steps has its own clock, its own evidentiary record, and its own way of failing.

This document specifies the service that the real property office, the clerk of the governing board, compliance staff and treasury use to run that procedure end to end. Today the work is spread across spreadsheets, calendars and email threads, and the consequences are predictable: preference windows are closed early, negotiations are declared an impasse before the statutory duty has been discharged, minimum prices are computed from stale appraisals, easements that were supposed to be reserved never reach the deed, and sale proceeds land in the wrong designated fund. The service replaces all of that with one authoritative record in which every state change is guarded, every price is computed once and frozen, and every dollar posted to a fund can be traced back to the conveyance or the forfeited appraisal deposit that produced it.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, the system is a compliance engine before it is a workflow tool: an operation that would advance the disposition past an unsatisfied statutory requirement is refused with a specific, machine-readable reason rather than allowed with a warning. Second, time is explicit and deterministic. The service keeps a logical clock that operators move on demand, so notice deadlines, negotiation windows, appraisal validity, cooling-off periods and reverter cure periods can be exercised exactly as written, with no dependence on wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If `X-User-Role` is absent or does not name one of the eight declared roles, or if `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Presence of both headers is checked before anything else about the request — the role header first, then the user id header. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear on the list of roles the addressed endpoint declares; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `RealPropertyOfficer`, `BoardClerk`, `ComplianceMonitor`, `FinanceOfficer`, `AgencyRepresentative`, `Appraiser`, `Bidder` and `SystemAdministrator`.

A `RealPropertyOfficer` is real property office staff: they draft declarations of surplus, serve notices, run preference negotiations from the agency's side, order appraisals and accept reports, open and resolve title curative items, open and certify environmental reviews, create and publish offerings, close bidding, and drive conveyances through title clearance to recording. A `BoardClerk` records the acts of the governing body itself: adoption of the surplus resolution, the finding that fixes disposition authority, determinations on appraisal challenges, awards, award rescissions and withdrawals. A `ComplianceMonitor` judges good faith in preference negotiations, debars bidders for cause, and polices deed restrictions and reverter clauses after recording. A `FinanceOfficer` is treasury staff and owns money configuration only: creating and adjusting designated funds, and crediting verified funds to a bidder's account. A `SystemAdministrator` maintains the reference registries the workflow depends on — agencies, appraisers and bidders. An `AgencyRepresentative` is an officer of a noticed public agency, acting for that agency alone: responding to a notice, filing an appraisal challenge, and tendering negotiation rounds on its own preference offer. An `Appraiser` is a licensed appraiser who may deliver a report only on an engagement they personally hold. A `Bidder` is a prospective purchaser who submits and withdraws bids on competitive sales for its own bidder record only.

Five of the roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `RealPropertyOfficer`, `BoardClerk`, `ComplianceMonitor`, `FinanceOfficer` or `SystemAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `adopted_by_user_id` on a `SurplusDeclaration`, as `determined_by_user_id` on an `AppraisalChallenge`, or as `awarded_by_user_id` on a `DispositionOffering`, where the corresponding act writes it — and it may compare two such recorded identifiers against each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

Three of the roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agency`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Agency` compare the resource's agency reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Appraiser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Appraiser`; ownership checks compare an appraisal's appraiser reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Bidder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bidder`; ownership checks compare a bid's bidder reference to `X-User-ID`. For all three, an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no row of the backing entity is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RESOLVABLE` (HTTP 403). This resolution check runs after the role-permission check and before any ownership comparison.

Ownership is enforced in three places. Responding to a statutory notice requires `X-User-ID` to equal the notice's `recipient_agency_id`; tendering a negotiation round as the preference entity requires `X-User-ID` to equal the offer's `offeror_agency_id`; withdrawing a bid requires `X-User-ID` to equal the bid's `bidder_id`. A mismatch in any of the three is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Delivering an appraisal report has its own, distinct code: only the engaged appraiser may report, and an acting `Appraiser` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the appraisal's `appraiser_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_ENGAGED_APPRAISER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the start of the result set. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a list endpoint's own description states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first. Three collections have a domain ordering instead: designated funds are returned in ascending `priority_rank` order, the negotiation rounds of a preference offer are returned in ascending `round_number` order, and the proceeds allocation rows produced by a conveyance are returned in the ascending `priority_rank` order of the funds they credit.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the query, `total_count` reports how many records match the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the returned page. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Timestamps and Row Versions

Every resource the service creates is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id`, like any other client-supplied computed or system-owned field, is ignored rather than honoured.

Most resources also carry three common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant, with three fractional digits, at which the row was created. `updated_at` carries the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; it is the token the concurrency rules in §2.7 compare against. The `Agency`, `Parcel`, `SurplusDeclaration`, `StatutoryNotice`, `PreferenceOffer`, `Appraiser`, `Appraisal`, `AppraisalChallenge`, `EnvironmentalReview`, `TitleCurativeItem`, `DispositionOffering`, `Bidder`, `Bid`, `Conveyance` and `Fund` resources all carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Two resources are immutable event records and deliberately carry less. A `NegotiationRound` and a `ProceedsAllocation` each carry only `id` and `created_at`: neither exposes `updated_at` and neither exposes `version`. A negotiation round is never edited once tendered — it is the evidentiary log the good-faith score is computed from — and a proceeds allocation is never edited either; reversing one changes its own `status` and `reversed_at` fields only, which is the single exception to the no-mutation rule and does not introduce `updated_at` or `version` onto the row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Statutory Day Arithmetic

The service does not read wall-clock time. It keeps a logical clock which, before any operator moves it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Every timestamp the server writes — `created_at`, `adopted_at`, `issued_at`, `responded_at`, `reported_at`, `accepted_at`, `certified_at`, `resolved_at`, `submitted_at`, `awarded_at`, `recorded_at`, `violation_noticed_at`, `reverted_at`, `reversed_at`, `negotiation_opened_at` — is read from that clock, and every deadline comparison is made against it. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to the UTC instant supplied as `current_utc`, and is available to all eight roles; moving the clock mutates no entity and advances no state machine by itself, and it may be moved backwards as freely as forwards.

All window arithmetic is whole-day arithmetic on UTC instants: a day is exactly 86400 seconds, there is no timezone conversion and no calendar-month arithmetic. Boundary inclusivity differs deliberately from window to window and is stated with each rule in §3. Three families exist. Closed intervals, inclusive at both ends, govern appraiser engagement intervals and environmental circulation intervals, so two intervals that share a single endpoint day overlap. Half-open intervals, inclusive at the lower bound and exclusive at the upper, govern the bidding window of an offering, so two offerings whose windows merely touch do not conflict. Deadline instants are inclusive almost everywhere — a notice response, a negotiation round, a preference-window closure, a re-offer and a cure are all in time when the clock stands exactly on the deadline — with two exceptions: an accepted appraisal is usable only while the clock is strictly before its `expires_on`, and reverter enforcement requires the clock to be strictly after the cure deadline, so the cure deadline instant itself still belongs to the grantee.

### 2.6. Whole-Cent Money and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in the system is an integer number of cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. No monetary field ever holds a fractional value, and no monetary field may be negative in a request payload. Where a computation multiplies a monetary amount by a percentage, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude — and each formula in §3 states exactly how many times and at which step that rounding is applied. Percentages themselves (`allocation_pct`) are decimal fractions between 0 and 1 inclusive, carrying at most four decimal places.

Two invariants follow. Rounding is never applied twice to the same intermediate value, and the proceeds waterfall reconciles exactly: the allocation rows a conveyance produces always sum to that conveyance's frozen net proceeds, to the cent, because the signed rounding residual is folded into the last percentage row rather than discarded.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parcels and Declarations

Two write operations require the client to prove that its view of a parent record is not stale, because in both cases the domain would otherwise let a second writer quietly retire the first writer's work.

Ordering an appraisal must carry `expected_parcel_version`, a non-optional field on that request, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Parcel`. If it does not equal the parcel's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a newly accepted appraisal legitimately supersedes the previously accepted one for that parcel, so no state precondition would block a second engagement order — it would succeed and silently displace another writer's valuation. Note that the successful create itself advances `Parcel.version`, so the token a client just spent is stale for every other holder of the same token.

Creating a disposition offering must carry `expected_declaration_version`, again a non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `SurplusDeclaration`. A value that does not equal the declaration's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DECLARATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A declaration in `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` legitimately admits more than one offering over its life — re-offerings and adjacent bidding windows are both lawful — so a second create observes the same authorized state and would succeed on its own terms; the version token is what makes the stale view detectable.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests that do not name one of those two fields are never rejected for staleness, and supplying a version field to an endpoint that does not declare one has no effect.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties Across Board Acts

The governing body's own acts are kept apart by operator identity. The `BoardClerk` who determines an appraisal challenge must not be the same `X-User-ID` recorded in `adopted_by_user_id` on the declaration covering that appraisal's parcel — the same person may not both adopt the surplus resolution and rule on a challenge to the valuation that resolution depends on. Likewise, the `BoardClerk` awarding an offering must not be the `X-User-ID` recorded in `awarded_by_user_id` of an award that was rescinded on that same offering, so a rescission and the re-award that follows it are always two different hands. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), evaluated with the other authorization checks and therefore before any state or business rule on the same request.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Nested Resource Retrieval

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the response. A nested read therefore reports the missing parent, not the missing child. Each entity has its own 404 code: `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECLARATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREFERENCE_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENV_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONVEYANCE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

The same codes are used for foreign keys carried in a request body rather than a path. A `parcel_id`, `custodian_agency_id`, `declaration_id`, `appraiser_id`, `counterparty_bidder_id`, `counterparty_agency_id`, or any entry of `recipient_agency_ids` that names no existing row produces the corresponding not-found code. An acting `Bidder` whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no bidder record likewise produces `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND`, and an acting `AgencyRepresentative` whose id resolves to no agency produces `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND` where the operation reads its agency (as filing an appraisal challenge does).

Natural keys are unique across the whole store: a parcel's `apn`, an appraiser's `license_number`, and a fund's `fund_code` and `priority_rank` may each be taken by at most one row. An attempt to reuse one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When several rules would reject the same request, the service always reports the earliest in the following order, so that a caller sees the most fundamental problem first and a later check never masks an earlier one.

Authentication comes first: the `X-User-Role` header, then the `X-User-ID` header. Authorization follows: the endpoint's role list, then entity-backed identity resolution, then resource ownership, then the engaged-appraiser check, then segregation of duties. Request shape is next: required fields present, enum values drawn from their declared members, every `_cents` integer at or above zero, every date field a parseable ISO 8601 UTC instant, and every declared list-length and numeric range respected. After shape comes existence: path resources left to right, then body foreign keys. Then uniqueness and duplication: natural keys, then the one-active-declaration-per-parcel rule, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_DECLARATION` (HTTP 409). Then optimistic concurrency: `expected_parcel_version`, then `expected_declaration_version`. Then state preconditions on the addressed entity and its parents. Then temporal window checks. Then the statutory and regulatory business rules. Then constraint-satisfaction checks such as appraiser capacity, interval overlaps and counterparty shape. Fiscal checks — minimum prices, deposits, bidder funds, fund configuration and net proceeds — are evaluated last.

Within the state layer the addressed entity's own state is checked before rules that read a related entity's state, and where an operation touches two independent state dimensions on one row (as preference offers, bids and conveyances all do), the dimension the operation drives is checked first.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Derived Fields

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one entity. Cascades are specified as ordered links in §3, and every link in a cascade runs — a cascade is never partially applied. When a cascade mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed, and where the row carries a `version` it is incremented by 1 exactly as for a direct write.

Derived fields are recomputed rather than stored by hand, and they are recomputed on every write that could change them. A parcel's `open_title_item_count` counts the title curative items on it whose `status` is `OPEN`. A parcel's `valuation_status` and `disposition_ready` are derived as described in §3.2. A declaration's `preference_offer_count` counts the preference offers pointing at it, and its `unresolved_preference_count` counts those offers whose `negotiation_status` is `OPEN` or `IN_NEGOTIATION`, or whose `good_faith_status` is `UNREVIEWED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`. An appraiser's `active_engagement_count` counts that appraiser's appraisals in `ORDERED`, `REPORTED` or `CHALLENGED`. An offering's `qualified_bid_count` counts its bids whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`. A preference offer's `officer_round_count`, `entity_round_count` and `timely_response_count` count, respectively, its rounds tendered by the custodian agency, its rounds tendered by the preference entity, and its preference-entity rounds whose `is_timely` is true. A conveyance's `allocated_proceeds_cents` sums the `amount_cents` of its allocation rows whose `status` is `POSTED`. None of these derived values is ever accepted from a client.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Registries: Agencies, Parcels, Appraisers, Bidders and Funds

Nothing in the disposition workflow can begin until the reference data exists, and each registry is owned by exactly one role. A `SystemAdministrator` registers a public agency with its legal `name` (between 1 and 200 characters), its statutory class in `agency_type` — one of `STATE`, `COUNTY`, `MUNICIPAL`, `SCHOOL_DISTRICT`, `TRANSIT`, `HOUSING_AUTHORITY` or `FEDERAL` — and a `contact_email` to which statutory notices are delivered. Two optional flags describe the agency's statutory standing: `is_preference_entity` records whether the agency holds a preference right on surplus land and defaults to false when omitted, and `preference_category` names the class of preference asserted — `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING`, `PARK_OPEN_SPACE`, `SCHOOL_SITE`, `INFILL_INFRASTRUCTURE` or `NONE` — and defaults to `NONE`. Every newly registered agency starts with `notice_registry_status` set to `REGISTERED`.

An agency's registry standing is not static. The administrator may later amend `notice_registry_status` to `LAPSED` (or back to `REGISTERED`) and may amend `contact_email`; either amendment increments the agency's `version` by 1. Registry standing matters because an agency whose standing has lapsed cannot lawfully be served, and a notice round that names one is refused outright rather than partially served — see §3.4. Administrators and reading roles can narrow the agency list by `agency_type`, by `is_preference_entity`, by `preference_category` and by `notice_registry_status`.

Parcels are the property side of the ledger and belong to the `RealPropertyOfficer`. Recording one requires the assessor parcel number `apn` (1 to 40 characters, unique across every parcel in the inventory), a street `address` or legal location description, the parcel size in `acreage` which must be strictly greater than zero, the current `zoning_code`, the carrying value on the agency's books in `book_value_cents` which may not be negative, and `custodian_agency_id` naming the public agency that holds custody. A new parcel starts in `ownership_status` `AGENCY_OWNED`, `valuation_status` `UNVALUED`, `open_title_item_count` zero and `disposition_ready` false. Later, the officer may amend `book_value_cents`, `zoning_code` and `address`; each amendment increments the parcel's `version` by 1 and recomputes `disposition_ready`, and that version bump is precisely what invalidates an outstanding concurrency token as described in §2.7. The parcel list can be narrowed by `custodian_agency_id` — the reverse lookup from an agency to the parcels it holds — and by `ownership_status`, `valuation_status` and `disposition_ready`. Beyond `AGENCY_OWNED`, a parcel's `ownership_status` can read `CONVEYED` once a deed is recorded or `REVERTED_TO_AGENCY` once a reverter has been enforced.

Appraisers are registered by the administrator with a legal `name`, a state `license_number` unique across all appraisers, and `license_expires_on`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the licence lapses. The derived `license_status` reads `ACTIVE` while the logical clock is strictly before `license_expires_on` and `EXPIRED` at that instant or after it, and the appraiser list can be filtered on it. Each appraiser also exposes `active_engagement_count`, the count of their appraisals still in flight.

Bidders are likewise administrator-registered, with a `legal_name`, an `entity_type` of `INDIVIDUAL`, `CORPORATION`, `NONPROFIT` or `PARTNERSHIP`, and a `contact_email`. A new bidder is `CLEAR` of debarment and carries an empty cash ledger: `available_funds_cents`, `escrow_held_cents` and `lifetime_deposits_cents` all zero. Treasury, acting as `FinanceOfficer`, credits verified cash by supplying a positive `amount_cents`, which raises both `available_funds_cents` and `lifetime_deposits_cents` by that amount and increments the bidder's `version` by 1. Compliance, acting as `ComplianceMonitor`, may debar a bidder for cause by supplying a `debarment_reason`; `debarment_status` moves `CLEAR` to `DEBARRED`, the reason is stored, and `version` is incremented by 1. Debarment is not retroactive to bids already on file — those are graded out when bidding closes, as §3.8 describes — but it does bar new bids immediately. The bidder list is filterable by `debarment_status` and `entity_type`.

The three bidder counters obey a strict compensating discipline that no operation may break. Funding raises `available_funds_cents` and `lifetime_deposits_cents` together. Submitting a bid moves the deposit sideways: `available_funds_cents` falls by the bid's `deposit_amount_cents` and `escrow_held_cents` rises by the same amount, while `lifetime_deposits_cents` is untouched because it counts funding credits only. Releasing a deposit — on withdrawal, on disqualification, on award rescission or on a declaration withdrawal — reverses that movement exactly: `escrow_held_cents` falls by the bid's `deposit_amount_cents` and `available_funds_cents` rises by the same amount, so a release-and-resubmit round trip returns the ledger to the identical pair of values it held before the bid. Applying a deposit at recording is the one movement that is not compensating: `escrow_held_cents` falls by the deposit and `available_funds_cents` is not credited, because the cash has left the bidder for good and been applied to the purchase price. At all times `escrow_held_cents` equals the sum of `deposit_amount_cents` over that bidder's bids whose deposit is still held, and the sum of `available_funds_cents` and `escrow_held_cents` equals `lifetime_deposits_cents` less the total of every deposit that has been applied.

Designated funds are configured by the `FinanceOfficer` and are the destination of every dollar the system moves. A fund carries a `name`, an accounting `fund_code` unique across funds, a `priority_rank` of at least 1 and unique across funds which fixes its position in the waterfall (rank 1 first), an `allocation_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive giving its share of the post-reserve remainder, and an optional `fixed_reserve_cents` — a statutory reserve taken off the top before any percentage share is computed — which may not be negative and defaults to zero. A new fund starts with `balance_cents`, `lifetime_credits_cents` and `lifetime_debits_cents` all zero. Treasury may later adjust `allocation_pct` and `fixed_reserve_cents`, which increments the fund's `version` by 1; the shares must still sum to exactly 1 at the moment proceeds are posted, and §3.10 states what happens when they do not.

### 3.2. Curing Title and Establishing Disposition Readiness

A parcel almost never comes to market clean. As the real property office reviews the title report it opens a curative item for each defect it finds, supplying `item_type` — `LIEN`, `ENCROACHMENT`, `MISSING_ACCESS_EASEMENT`, `BOUNDARY_GAP` or `UNRECORDED_LEASE` — a `description` of the defect, and `cure_cost_estimate_cents`, the estimated cost of curing it, which may not be negative. The item is created `OPEN`, the parcel's `open_title_item_count` rises by 1, and `disposition_ready` recomputes to false. Curative items are listed per parcel, filterable by `status`, and each can be read on its own.

Resolving an open item is the officer's decision among three mutually exclusive outcomes, named by the `resolution` supplied with the request as `CURED`, `WAIVED_WITH_ENDORSEMENT` or `RESERVED_AS_EASEMENT`, and the three carry deliberately different consequences. Resolving it as `CURED` records `actual_cure_cost_cents` from the request when a value is supplied, and otherwise copies the original `cure_cost_estimate_cents` into it; `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `open_title_item_count` falls by 1, and `disposition_ready` is recomputed. Resolving it as `WAIVED_WITH_ENDORSEMENT` means a title insurer has endorsed over the defect, so no curative money was spent: `actual_cure_cost_cents` is set to zero regardless of any value in the request, `resolved_at` is stamped, `open_title_item_count` falls by 1, and `disposition_ready` is recomputed. Resolving it as `RESERVED_AS_EASEMENT` means the agency keeps the burden rather than clearing it, and both `easement_purpose` and `easement_area_sqft` must be supplied with an area strictly greater than zero — omitting either, or supplying an area of zero or less, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; on success both values are recorded, `actual_cure_cost_cents` is set to zero, `resolved_at` is stamped, `open_title_item_count` falls by 1, and `disposition_ready` is recomputed. A reserved easement also raises a floor that binds much later: the count of `RESERVED_AS_EASEMENT` items on a parcel becomes the minimum number of deed restriction clauses the eventual conveyance must carry, as §3.10 sets out.

Resolution is once and for all. An item that is no longer `OPEN` — already `CURED`, `WAIVED_WITH_ENDORSEMENT` or `RESERVED_AS_EASEMENT` — cannot be resolved again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_ITEM_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Which of the three outcomes was chosen matters to money as well as to title. Only items resolved `CURED` contribute their `actual_cure_cost_cents` to the curative deduction in minimum-price setting and to the curative recovery at conveyance; items that are still `OPEN`, that were waived with an endorsement, or whose burden was reserved as an easement contribute nothing to either calculation.

**Disposition readiness** is the single gate that both the board's authorization and the creation of an offering consult, and it is derived, never written. A parcel's `valuation_status` reads `CURRENT` when some appraisal on the parcel has reached `ACCEPTED` and the logical clock is strictly before that appraisal's `expires_on`; it reads `STALE` when an appraisal on the parcel reached `ACCEPTED` at some point but no accepted appraisal is currently usable, whether because a newer one superseded it, because it expired, or because a reverted conveyance invalidated it; and it reads `UNVALUED` when no appraisal on the parcel has ever reached `ACCEPTED`. The parcel's `disposition_ready` is true when, and only when, all three of the following hold: `ownership_status` is `AGENCY_OWNED`, `valuation_status` is `CURRENT`, and `open_title_item_count` is zero.

Readiness therefore propagates without anybody writing to the parcel. Resolving the last open curative item drops `open_title_item_count` to zero and flips `disposition_ready` to true on its own; accepting a newer appraisal supersedes the older one and leaves `valuation_status` at `CURRENT`; recording a conveyance sets `ownership_status` to `CONVEYED` and forces `disposition_ready` to false; enforcing a reverter sets `ownership_status` to `REVERTED_TO_AGENCY` and `valuation_status` to `STALE`, so readiness stays false until a fresh appraisal has been accepted. Any operation that requires readiness and does not find it is refused with `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_DISPOSITION_READY` (HTTP 422) — the parcel is either no longer agency-owned, or not currently valued, or still carries an open title item.

### 3.3. Valuing the Parcel: Engagement, Report and Acceptance

An appraisal moves through `ORDERED`, `REPORTED` and `ACCEPTED`, may detour through `CHALLENGED`, and ends in `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED`. The real property office starts it by engaging an appraiser: the order names the `parcel_id` being valued, the `appraiser_id` engaged, the valuation `method` — `SALES_COMPARISON`, `INCOME` or `COST` — and the engagement interval as `engagement_start_on` and `engagement_end_on`, two ISO 8601 UTC instants forming an interval that is closed at both ends. The interval must not run backwards: an `engagement_end_on` earlier than `engagement_start_on` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The order also carries `expected_parcel_version` as described in §2.7.

The appraiser's licence must cover the whole engagement, not merely the moment of ordering. The engagement is refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422) when the appraiser's `license_status` is `EXPIRED`, and equally when `license_expires_on` is not strictly later than `engagement_end_on` — a licence that lapses mid-engagement is no licence at all for this purpose.

Appraiser capacity is a hard limit of two concurrent engagements. Before the row is created the service counts that appraiser's other appraisals whose `status` is `ORDERED`, `REPORTED` or `CHALLENGED` and whose closed engagement interval intersects the requested closed interval; appraisals in `ACCEPTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED` have released the appraiser and are never counted. Because both intervals are closed, sharing a single endpoint day counts as an intersection: a requested start equal to an existing engagement's end overlaps, while a requested start one day after that end does not. If the count of intersecting in-flight engagements is already two or more, the order is refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); at most one such engagement may already exist for the order to succeed. On success the appraisal is created in `ORDERED`, the parcel's `version` rises by 1, and the appraiser's `active_engagement_count` is recomputed.

Delivering the report is the engaged appraiser's own act, and it requires the appraisal to be in `ORDERED` — any other status is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE` (HTTP 409). The report supplies `appraised_value_cents`, the opinion of value, which may not be negative; `effective_date`, the valuation date as an ISO 8601 UTC instant; and the report `narrative`. A malformed value or date is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the three values are recorded, `reported_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `expires_on` is computed as the `effective_date` plus 365 days, and the appraisal moves `ORDERED → REPORTED`. Note that `expires_on` is anchored to the valuation date supplied by the appraiser, not to the moment of submission, so a report with a back-dated effective date arrives with less of its validity left.

Acceptance is the real property office's act and turns one report into the governing valuation of the parcel. It requires the appraisal to be in `REPORTED`, and it additionally requires that no challenge against this appraisal is still `SUBMITTED`; either condition failing is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`. It also requires the report to still be inside its validity window — a clock at or beyond `expires_on` is `ERR_APPRAISAL_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Acceptance stamps `accepted_at`, moves the appraisal `REPORTED → ACCEPTED`, transitions every other appraisal on the same parcel that was `ACCEPTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, sets the parcel's `valuation_status` to `CURRENT`, recomputes `disposition_ready`, increments the parcel's `version` by 1, and recomputes the appraiser's `active_engagement_count` — because an accepted appraisal releases the engagement slot.

Two later events retire an accepted appraisal. Creating an offering evaluates the parcel's accepted appraisal against the clock: if the clock has reached or passed `expires_on`, that appraisal moves `ACCEPTED → EXPIRED`, the parcel's `valuation_status` becomes `STALE`, and the offering create that observed the expiry is itself refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_EXPIRED`. And a disposition that fails for want of a qualifying bid cascades back onto the valuation, as §3.9 describes, moving the accepted appraisal `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED` with `revaluation_required` set to true. Appraisals expose `challenge_count`, the number of challenges ever filed against them, and are listable with filters on `parcel_id`, `appraiser_id`, `status` and `method`.

### 3.4. Challenging the Reported Value

A preference entity that believes the report is wrong may challenge it, but only from a standing position and only with money at risk. Filing is an `AgencyRepresentative` act available only while the appraisal is `REPORTED`; any other status is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`. The challenger must have been served: an agency that received no statutory notice on the declaration covering the appraised parcel is turned away with `ERR_CHALLENGER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). The filing window runs 30 days from the report: a challenge arriving when the clock is still at or before `reported_at` plus 30 days is in time, and one arriving later is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).

The filing itself carries `asserted_value_cents`, the value the challenger says is correct, which may not be negative; the written `grounds`; and `deposit_paid_cents`, the deposit tendered, which may not be negative either. Two admissibility tests then apply. First, the dispute must be material: the absolute difference between `asserted_value_cents` and the appraisal's current `appraised_value_cents` must be at least 5 percent of that appraised value, where the threshold is the appraised value multiplied by five hundredths and rounded half up to a whole cent. A difference exactly equal to that threshold is admissible; anything smaller is `ERR_CHALLENGE_DELTA_TOO_SMALL` (HTTP 422).

Second, the deposit must cover the statutory requirement. The requirement is computed as the appraised value multiplied by one hundredth, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then clamped: if that raw figure is below 100,000 cents — one thousand dollars — the requirement is 100,000 cents, and if it is above 2,500,000 cents — twenty-five thousand dollars — the requirement is 2,500,000 cents. The resulting `required_deposit_cents` is frozen on the challenge at filing time and never recomputed afterwards, even if the appraised value later changes. A `deposit_paid_cents` at or above `required_deposit_cents` is sufficient; one cent short is `ERR_CHALLENGE_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

On a successful filing the challenge is created in `SUBMITTED`, `challenger_agency_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` both start at zero, `revised_value_cents` and `determined_by_user_id` start empty, and the parent appraisal moves `REPORTED → CHALLENGED` with its `challenge_count` incremented by 1. Challenges are readable individually and listable with filters on `appraisal_id`, `challenger_agency_id` and `status`.

Determination is a `BoardClerk` act on a challenge that is still `SUBMITTED`; a challenge already `SUSTAINED` or `DENIED` cannot be determined again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE` (HTTP 409). The segregation rule of §2.8 applies here. The request names the `determination` and, when sustaining, the `revised_value_cents`.

Sustaining the challenge records `determined_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and stores `revised_value_cents`, which must be supplied and may not be negative — omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The deposit comes back in full: `deposit_refunded_cents` is set to `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` stays zero. The parent appraisal then returns `CHALLENGED → REPORTED` with its `appraised_value_cents` overwritten by the revised value and `revaluation_required` set to true; `effective_date` is not re-dated, so `expires_on` is unchanged and the revision does not buy the report extra validity. Because the appraisal is `REPORTED` again, it can be challenged again, and the `REPORTED → CHALLENGED → REPORTED` loop may repeat.

Denying the challenge also records `determined_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and leaves `revised_value_cents` empty, and it moves money in the opposite direction through a three-link cascade. Link one: `deposit_forfeited_cents` is set to `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_refunded_cents` stays zero. Link two, caused by the forfeiture: an allocation row is created with no conveyance behind it — its `conveyance_id` is null — whose `fund_id` is the designated fund with the lowest `priority_rank`, whose `allocation_basis` is `CHALLENGE_FORFEITURE`, whose `amount_cents` equals the forfeited deposit, whose `residual_included_cents` is zero and whose `status` is `POSTED`. Link three, caused by that posting: the credited fund's `balance_cents` and `lifetime_credits_cents` both rise by that `amount_cents`. Because money must move, a denial requires at least one fund to exist; with none configured the determination is refused with `ERR_FUNDS_NOT_CONFIGURED` (HTTP 422). The parent appraisal returns `CHALLENGED → REPORTED` with its original `appraised_value_cents` intact.

### 3.5. Declaring Surplus and Serving the Statutory Notice Round

The disposition proper begins with a written finding. A `RealPropertyOfficer` drafts the declaration against a single `parcel_id`, supplying the `justification` — the finding that the parcel is no longer needed for agency use — and optionally an `intended_use_restriction`, the use restriction the agency intends to record on the deed, which is left empty when the conveyance is to be unrestricted. A declaration may only be drafted for a parcel the agency still holds: a parcel whose `ownership_status` is anything other than `AGENCY_OWNED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A parcel may also carry only one live declaration at a time — if another declaration on the same parcel has a `status` outside `CONVEYED` and `WITHDRAWN`, the draft is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_DECLARATION` (HTTP 409). The new declaration is created in `DRAFT` with `reoffer_count`, `preference_offer_count` and `unresolved_preference_count` all zero and every system field empty.

A declaration's `status` walks `DRAFT`, `ADOPTED`, `NOTICED`, `PREFERENCE_CLOSED`, then either `NEGOTIATION_AGREED` or straight to `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED`, and from `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` either onward to `CONVEYED` or back through `UNSUCCESSFUL` to `NOTICED` for another cycle. `WITHDRAWN` is reachable from every non-terminal status. `CONVEYED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal statuses. Declarations are listable with filters on `parcel_id` — the reverse lookup from a parcel to its declarations — on `status`, and on `authorized_disposition_type`. Any operation addressed at a declaration whose current `status` is not a from-state of the requested transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE` (HTTP 409); this single code guards adoption, notice issuance, preference-window closure, disposition authorization, re-offering, withdrawal, opening an environmental review, creating an offering, appending a negotiation round and concluding a negotiation.

Adoption is the governing body's act, recorded by a `BoardClerk` on a declaration still in `DRAFT`. The clerk supplies the `resolution_number` under which the board acted; the service stores it, writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `adopted_by_user_id`, stamps `adopted_at` from the logical clock, moves the declaration `DRAFT → ADOPTED`, and increments its `version` by 1. The identity captured in `adopted_by_user_id` is what the segregation rule of §2.8 later compares against.

Serving notice is the officer's act on an `ADOPTED` declaration. The call names `recipient_agency_ids`, a list of between 1 and 20 distinct agency ids, and may name `response_window_days`, the statutory response window applied to this round; when omitted the window is 60 days. A supplied window must fall within 60 through 90 days, both ends inclusive, and a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A list that is empty, longer than 20, or contains a duplicate is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an entry that names no agency is `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`. Every recipient must be in good standing on the registry at the moment of service: if any one of them has `notice_registry_status` `LAPSED`, the whole round is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_REGISTRY_LAPSED` (HTTP 422) and no notice is created.

On success the declaration moves `ADOPTED → NOTICED` and one statutory notice is created per recipient. Each notice records `declaration_id`, `recipient_agency_id`, `notice_round` of 1 for this first service, `status` `ISSUED`, `issued_at` from the logical clock, its own copy of `response_window_days`, and `response_deadline_at` computed as `issued_at` plus `response_window_days` days. In parallel the declaration records `notice_issued_at` from the clock, `notice_response_window_days` from the effective window, and `notice_response_deadline_at` computed as `notice_issued_at` plus `notice_response_window_days` days; its `version` rises by 1. Notices are readable individually and listable with filters on `declaration_id`, on `recipient_agency_id`, on `status` and on `notice_round`.

Responding is the recipient agency's own act, permitted only while the notice is `ISSUED` — a notice already answered or expired is `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and only while the clock stands at or before that notice's own `response_deadline_at`; a response one instant later is `ERR_NOTICE_RESPONSE_LATE` (HTTP 409), while a response exactly on the deadline instant is timely. The response names a `response_type` of either `EXPRESS_INTEREST` or `DECLINE`, and the two branches diverge sharply.

Declining is the simpler branch and imposes no standing requirement of its own: `responded_at` is stamped from the clock, `response_type` is recorded as `DECLINE`, the notice moves `ISSUED → DECLINED`, and no preference offer is created. Expressing interest is only open to an agency that actually holds a statutory preference right: if the recipient's `is_preference_entity` is false the response is refused with `ERR_NOT_PREFERENCE_ENTITY` (HTTP 422). It further requires `claimed_preference_category` to be supplied and to equal the recipient agency's own `preference_category`; a missing or mismatched claim is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The agency may also state its `intended_use` of the parcel. On success `responded_at` is stamped, `response_type`, `claimed_preference_category` and `intended_use` are recorded, and the notice moves `ISSUED → INTEREST_EXPRESSED`.

Expressing interest also opens a negotiation. A preference offer is created carrying the notice's `declaration_id`, the originating `notice_id`, an `offeror_agency_id` copied from the notice's `recipient_agency_id`, and a `preference_category` copied from the claimed category — which on an offer is always one of `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING`, `PARK_OPEN_SPACE`, `SCHOOL_SITE` or `INFILL_INFRASTRUCTURE`. It starts in `negotiation_status` `OPEN` and `good_faith_status` `UNREVIEWED`, with `negotiation_opened_at` stamped from the clock and `negotiation_deadline_at` computed as that instant plus 90 days. Its `ah_minimum_price_cents` — the minimum price quoted to this offeror — is computed at once by the minimum-price rules of §3.7, taking the affordable-housing variant when the offer's `preference_category` is `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING` and the market-floor variant in every other case. Finally the parent declaration's `preference_offer_count` rises by 1 and its `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed.

Closing the preference window ends the round. It is the officer's act on a declaration in `NOTICED`, and it is refused with `ERR_PREFERENCE_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN` (HTTP 409) unless at least one of two conditions holds: either every notice of the current round has a `status` other than `ISSUED`, meaning nobody is still owed a chance to reply, or the clock has reached `notice_response_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself qualifies. On success every notice of the current round still sitting in `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → EXPIRED`, forfeiting that recipient's preference right for the round and creating no preference offer; the declaration moves `NOTICED → PREFERENCE_CLOSED`, `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed over the surviving preference offers, and `version` rises by 1.

### 3.6. Preference Negotiations and the Round Log

A preference offer carries two status dimensions that advance independently on the same row. The negotiation dimension, `negotiation_status`, tracks the progress of the bargaining itself and walks `OPEN`, `IN_NEGOTIATION`, and then one of `AGREED`, `IMPASSE` or `LAPSED`. The compliance dimension, `good_faith_status`, grades the agency's negotiating conduct and walks `UNREVIEWED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, and then one of `SATISFIED` or `DEFICIENT`. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions, and an operation that drives one dimension leaves the other exactly as it was. Offers are readable individually — the read exposes both dimensions and the interim score — and listable with filters on `declaration_id`, `notice_id`, `offeror_agency_id`, `negotiation_status`, `good_faith_status` and `preference_category`.

Bargaining is recorded as an append-only log of rounds, and both sides write to the same log. Each round names the `party` that tendered it — `CUSTODIAN_AGENCY` for the disposing agency or `PREFERENCE_ENTITY` for the offeror — the `price_offered_cents` tendered, which may not be negative, a `terms_summary` of the terms, and optionally `responded_to_round_number`, the `round_number` of the opposite party's round this one answers. An opening round leaves `responded_to_round_number` empty. A `RealPropertyOfficer` may append rounds for the custodian agency; an `AgencyRepresentative` may append rounds only on its own offer and only with `party` set to `PREFERENCE_ENTITY`, and a representative acting on another agency's offer, or claiming the custodian side, is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Three gates guard an append. The parent declaration must be in `NOTICED` or `PREFERENCE_CLOSED`, so bargaining is confined to the statutory window and its immediate aftermath; any other declaration status is `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`. The offer's negotiation dimension must admit the append: the very first round is accepted while `negotiation_status` is `OPEN` and moves it `OPEN → IN_NEGOTIATION`, subsequent rounds from either party are accepted while it is `IN_NEGOTIATION` and leave it there, and an offer that has already reached `AGREED`, `IMPASSE` or `LAPSED` accepts nothing further and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE` (HTTP 409). And the negotiation window must still be open: the clock must stand at or before `negotiation_deadline_at`, which is 90 days after `negotiation_opened_at`, and an append at the deadline instant is in time while one after it is `ERR_NEGOTIATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). When `responded_to_round_number` is supplied it must name an existing round on this same offer that belongs to the opposite party and carries a strictly lower `round_number`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_ROUND_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422).

A round is an event record rather than an editable resource: it carries an `id`, a `created_at`, the `offer_id` of the negotiation it belongs to, and nothing else from the common audit set — no `updated_at` and no `version` — and no operation ever amends or deletes one. Each accepted round is numbered densely from 1 in `created_at` order, so its `round_number` is the count of rounds already on the offer plus one. Two derived values are then computed on it. `responded_within_days` is the number of whole days between the referenced round's `created_at` and this round's `created_at`, taking the elapsed time in whole 86400-second days and discarding any remainder; it is left empty when no round was referenced. `is_timely` is true when, and only when, `responded_within_days` has a value and that value is at most 15 — fifteen days exactly still counts as timely. Appending a round then recomputes the offer's `officer_round_count`, `entity_round_count` and `timely_response_count`, recomputes its `ah_minimum_price_cents` under the rules of §3.7, and recomputes `interim_good_faith_score` using the interim variant of the good-faith score described below. Rounds are listable per offer, filterable by `party`, and are returned in `round_number` order.

Concluding the negotiation is the officer's act, available only while `negotiation_status` is `IN_NEGOTIATION` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE` — and only while the parent declaration is in `PREFERENCE_CLOSED`, so no conclusion can be recorded before the statutory response window has been closed out; any other declaration status is `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`. The request names an `outcome`, and the two outcomes diverge in both their preconditions and their effects.

Concluding at `IMPASSE` requires nothing beyond those two state gates. The offer moves `IN_NEGOTIATION → IMPASSE`, `agreed_price_cents` stays empty, the declaration's `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed and its `version` rises by 1. Sibling offers are untouched, and the declaration itself does not change status — an impasse simply removes one offer from contention.

Concluding at `AGREED` carries two extra requirements. First, the price must clear the statutory floor: `agreed_price_cents` must be supplied and must be at least the offer's `ah_minimum_price_cents`, with a price exactly at the floor acceptable and anything below it refused as `ERR_AGREED_PRICE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Second, the agency's own negotiating duty must already have been discharged: at least three rounds tendered by `CUSTODIAN_AGENCY` must exist on the offer, and an agreement struck on fewer is refused with `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_ROUNDS_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422) — the agency cannot buy its way out of the statutory duty by conceding early. On success `agreed_price_cents` is stored, the offer moves `IN_NEGOTIATION → AGREED`, every sibling preference offer on the same declaration whose `negotiation_status` is `OPEN` or `IN_NEGOTIATION` is transitioned to `LAPSED`, the declaration moves `PREFERENCE_CLOSED → NEGOTIATION_AGREED`, its `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed and its `version` rises by 1.

### 3.7. Grading Good Faith, and the Minimum Price

Compliance grades conduct on a separate track. A `ComplianceMonitor` opens the review on an offer whose `good_faith_status` is `UNREVIEWED`, moving it `UNREVIEWED → UNDER_REVIEW`; an offer already under review or already determined is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GOOD_FAITH_STATE` (HTTP 409). Opening the review changes nothing on the negotiation dimension, and the declaration's `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed because an offer under review still counts as unresolved.

The determination is the same monitor's second act and requires `good_faith_status` to be `UNDER_REVIEW` — anything else is again `ERR_INVALID_GOOD_FAITH_STATE`. It additionally requires the negotiation dimension to have concluded: `negotiation_status` must be `AGREED`, `IMPASSE` or `LAPSED`, and a determination attempted while the offer is still `OPEN` or `IN_NEGOTIATION` is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STILL_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The monitor may supply a `remediation_note`, which is stored when present. The service then computes the final variant of the good-faith score, freezes it into `final_good_faith_score`, and branches: a score of at least 70 moves the offer `UNDER_REVIEW → SATISFIED`, and a score below 70 moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → DEFICIENT`. Either way `unresolved_preference_count` is recomputed on the declaration. A `DEFICIENT` offer is not a dead end for the offer itself, but it blocks the declaration from ever being authorized while it stands.

**The good-faith compliance score** is a pure projection of the round log, computed from three components less one penalty. Let the officer round count be the number of rounds on the offer whose `party` is `CUSTODIAN_AGENCY`, and the timely officer round count be the number of those same custodian-agency rounds whose `is_timely` is true. Engagement points are 45 multiplied by the smaller of the officer round count and 3, divided by 3 — so three or more custodian rounds earn the full 45, two earn 30, one earns 15 and none earns nothing. Timeliness points are 30 multiplied by the smaller of the timely officer round count and 3, divided by 3, on the same three-round scale. Note that the timeliness component counts custodian-agency rounds, whereas the stored `timely_response_count` field counts preference-entity rounds; the score never reads `timely_response_count`, and the two figures will differ on the same offer.

Concession points measure how close the agency's last price came to the floor it quoted. Take the `price_offered_cents` of the custodian-agency round with the highest `round_number` — rounds are numbered densely from 1, so this is unambiguous. The near band is the offer's `ah_minimum_price_cents` multiplied by 1.05, rounded half up to a whole cent; the wide band is the same `ah_minimum_price_cents` multiplied by 1.20, rounded half up to a whole cent. If that latest officer price is at or below the near band, concession points are 25. If it is above the near band and at or below the wide band, concession points are 12. Otherwise they are zero. Both band comparisons include the band value itself.

The premature-impasse penalty is 15, and it applies when the offer concluded at `IMPASSE` and the logical clock at the moment of conclusion was strictly before `negotiation_deadline_at` — walking away while statutory time remained. In every other case the penalty is zero.

The score is the sum of engagement points, timeliness points and concession points, less the premature-impasse penalty, rounded half up to a whole integer once over that sum, and then clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive.

Two variants of the score exist and they are not interchangeable. The interim variant is recomputed on every appended round and exposed as `interim_good_faith_score`; it omits concession points entirely, treating them as zero, omits the premature-impasse penalty, and then caps the result at 75 — price convergence cannot fairly be judged while the parties are still exchanging offers. For identical round data the interim score is therefore never higher than the final score, and it can sit below the passing mark of 70 while the final score clears it. The final variant runs once, at determination, and is stored in `final_good_faith_score`; it adds concession points, subtracts the premature-impasse penalty, applies no cap of 75, and is the only variant ever compared against the passing mark of 70.

**The minimum price** is computed by one rule with two variants, and it is frozen wherever it is stored. Start from the base value: the `appraised_value_cents` of the governing appraisal for the parcel, meaning the one whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. If that appraisal carries `revaluation_required` true, its `appraised_value_cents` has already been overwritten by the sustained challenge's `revised_value_cents`, so the rule always reads the appraisal's current `appraised_value_cents` and never reaches around it. Next compute the cure deduction: the sum of `actual_cure_cost_cents` over the title curative items on the parcel whose `status` is `CURED`, with items still `OPEN`, waived with an endorsement, or reserved as an easement contributing zero.

Two proportional reductions then apply. The write-down fraction is 0.35 for the `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING_PREFERENCE` variant and zero for the `MARKET_FLOOR` variant. The re-offer discount fraction is the smaller of two figures: the declaration's `reoffer_count` multiplied by 0.10, and the cap of 0.20 — so a first re-offer cycle discounts by a tenth, a second by a fifth, and no further cycle can discount more than a fifth. The gross figure is the base value multiplied by one minus the write-down fraction, multiplied by one minus the re-offer discount fraction, and that product is rounded half up to a whole cent exactly once, before anything is deducted. The raw minimum is then the gross figure less the cure deduction.

The variants part company at the floor. Under `MARKET_FLOOR` the answer is the larger of the raw minimum and the parcel's `book_value_cents`, so the agency can never offer public land below its carrying value on the open market. Under `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING_PREFERENCE` the answer is the larger of the raw minimum and zero: the 0.35 statutory affordable-housing write-down applies and the book-value floor is deliberately dropped, so from identical parcel, appraisal and curative data this variant yields a strictly lower minimum price than `MARKET_FLOOR` and may lawfully fall below book value.

Variant selection depends on the disposition. The `MARKET_FLOOR` variant applies when the offering is a `COMPETITIVE_SALE`, and also to a `NEGOTIATED_SALE` or `EXCHANGE` where no preference offer on the declaration has reached `AGREED` with a `preference_category` of `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING`. The `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING_PREFERENCE` variant applies to a `NEGOTIATED_SALE` or `EXCHANGE` where the `AGREED` preference offer on the declaration does carry `preference_category` `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING`, and it is also the variant stored in `ah_minimum_price_cents` on every affordable-housing preference offer while that offer is in negotiation; a preference offer in any other category stores the `MARKET_FLOOR` variant in the same field. Where no appraisal on the parcel has reached `ACCEPTED` the rule has no base value, which is why creating an offering in that condition is refused rather than priced.

### 3.8. Environmental Clearance, the Authority to Dispose, and Competitive Bidding

Environmental clearance runs on its own track alongside the preference process, and it is opened by the `RealPropertyOfficer` against a declaration. The review records a `determination_class` of `EXEMPT`, `NEGATIVE_DECLARATION`, `MITIGATED_NEG_DEC` or `FULL_EIR`, and a public circulation interval given by `circulation_start_on` and `circulation_end_on`. That interval is a **closed** interval: both endpoint days belong to it. A new review is created with `circulation_round` 1, `status` `CIRCULATING`, `certified_at` empty and `recirculation_reason` empty — except for an `EXEMPT` determination, which needs no public circulation at all and is created directly in `CERTIFIED` with `certified_at` stamped from the clock; for an `EXEMPT` review the supplied interval is ignored entirely, is not length-checked, and may be omitted.

For every class other than `EXEMPT` the interval must be long enough: the number of whole days from `circulation_start_on` to `circulation_end_on` must be at least 30, and a shorter interval is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATION_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Separately, no two reviews on the same declaration may hold overlapping closed circulation intervals, and because the intervals are closed, two intervals that share even a single endpoint day overlap; the collision is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The overlap test considers every review on the declaration in every round and in every status, including reviews already certified or sent back for recirculation. Reviews on a declaration are listable, filterable by `status`, and individually readable.

Certifying a review is the officer's act and carries two conditions rolled into one refusal. The review must be `CIRCULATING`, and the clock must have reached `circulation_end_on` — certification on the last circulation day itself is permitted, certification before it is not. A review that is not `CIRCULATING`, or one whose circulation has not yet run out, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENV_REVIEW_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the review moves `CIRCULATING → CERTIFIED` and `certified_at` is stamped from the clock.

Substantial new information sends the document back out. Recirculation requires the review to be `CIRCULATING` — anything else is again `ERR_INVALID_ENV_REVIEW_STATE` — and the request supplies a `recirculation_reason` together with the replacement `circulation_start_on` and `circulation_end_on`. The new window must respect the statutory buffer: its `circulation_start_on` must fall no earlier than 30 days after the prior round's `circulation_end_on`, and the boundary day itself is allowed, so a start exactly 30 days after the prior end is accepted while 29 days after is refused with `ERR_RECIRCULATION_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). The new window is also length-checked against the 30-day circulation minimum and tested for overlap against every other review on the declaration, yielding `ERR_CIRCULATION_TOO_SHORT` and `ERR_CIRCULATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` respectively. A recirculation is a single operation that passes through `RECIRCULATION_REQUIRED` and returns immediately to `CIRCULATING` on the new window, so the review a client reads afterwards is `CIRCULATING` again; `circulation_round` rises by 1, both circulation dates are replaced by the supplied values, and `recirculation_reason` is stored. `certified_at` remains empty, since nothing has been certified yet.

**Authorizing the disposition** is the board's finding that fixes which mechanism may be used, and it is the busiest gate in the system. The `BoardClerk` supplies a `disposition_type` of `COMPETITIVE_SALE`, `NEGOTIATED_SALE` or `EXCHANGE`, and optional `authority_findings`. The declaration must be in `PREFERENCE_CLOSED` or `NEGOTIATION_AGREED`; any other status is `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`. Beyond that the checks apply in this fixed order, so a request that offends several is reported against the earliest.

First, for a `COMPETITIVE_SALE` every preference offer on the declaration must have concluded its negotiation at `IMPASSE` or `LAPSED` — an offer still `OPEN` or `IN_NEGOTIATION`, and equally an offer that reached `AGREED`, means the preference process has not cleared the way for open competition, and the request is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STILL_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Second, the good faith of every preference offer the authorization depends on must already have been graded, meaning its `good_faith_status` is neither `UNREVIEWED` nor `UNDER_REVIEW`; otherwise `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_NOT_DETERMINED` (HTTP 422). For a competitive sale that scope is every preference offer on the declaration; for a negotiated sale or exchange it is the offer that reached `AGREED`. Third, none of those offers may carry `good_faith_status` `DEFICIENT`, or the authorization is refused with `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_DEFICIENT` (HTTP 422) — an agency that negotiated in bad faith cannot proceed to disposition at all. Fourth, a `NEGOTIATED_SALE` requires a preference offer on the declaration whose `negotiation_status` is `AGREED`, and without one the request fails with `ERR_PREFERENCE_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 422). Fifth, authority classes may not cross: a declaration sitting in `NEGOTIATION_AGREED` cannot be authorized as a `COMPETITIVE_SALE`, and the attempt is `ERR_OFFERING_TYPE_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Sixth, an `EXCHANGE` requires `authority_findings` to be supplied and non-empty, since an exchange rests on an express board finding; omitting it is `ERR_EXCHANGE_FINDINGS_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Seventh, an environmental review on the declaration must have reached `CERTIFIED`, or the authorization is refused with `ERR_ENV_REVIEW_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422). Eighth and last, the parcel must still be disposition-ready by the test of §3.2, and a parcel that is not is refused with `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_DISPOSITION_READY` (HTTP 422).

On success the declaration moves `PREFERENCE_CLOSED → OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` or `NEGOTIATION_AGREED → OFFERING_AUTHORIZED`, `authorized_disposition_type` is set to the supplied `disposition_type`, `authority_findings` is recorded when supplied, and `version` rises by 1. Which of the two from-states was used matters later: it is the reason a negotiated authority can never be re-badged as competitive.

**Creating the offering** is the officer's act, it carries the declaration's concurrency token in `expected_declaration_version`, and it is the moment every price is frozen. The request supplies `declaration_id`, `offering_type`, the bidding window as `bid_open_at` and `bid_close_at`, and at most one counterparty leg — `counterparty_bidder_id` or `counterparty_agency_id`. A `COMPETITIVE_SALE` must supply neither leg, and supplying either is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Unresolvable values of `declaration_id`, `counterparty_bidder_id` or `counterparty_agency_id` are 404s against the entity that failed to resolve. The concurrency token is then checked as described in §2.7, and after it the declaration must be in `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` — any other status is `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`.

The remaining checks run in this order. The supplied `offering_type` must equal the declaration's `authorized_disposition_type`, and a mismatch is `ERR_OFFERING_TYPE_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the board's authority governs, and the officer may not substitute another mechanism. Next an appraisal on the parcel must have reached `ACCEPTED`, or the offering cannot be priced and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_APPRAISAL` (HTTP 422). Next that accepted appraisal must still be inside its validity window, meaning the clock is strictly before its `expires_on`; a clock standing exactly on `expires_on` means the appraisal has already lapsed. Before the refusal is issued the service performs the expiry it just observed: the appraisal moves `ACCEPTED → EXPIRED`, the parcel's `valuation_status` becomes `STALE` and `disposition_ready` is recomputed to false, and those changes persist even though the offering create itself is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Next `bid_close_at` must be strictly after `bid_open_at`, and a window that is empty or inverted is `ERR_OFFERING_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Next the window must not collide with another live offering on the same parcel, as set out below, on pain of `ERR_OFFERING_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Next the parcel must still be disposition-ready, or `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_DISPOSITION_READY` (HTTP 422). And finally the counterparty legs are checked: a `NEGOTIATED_SALE` must supply exactly one resolvable counterparty leg, and where the declaration reached `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` from `NEGOTIATION_AGREED` the supplied `counterparty_agency_id` must equal the `offeror_agency_id` of the `AGREED` preference offer — no leg, both legs, or the wrong agency is `ERR_NEGOTIATED_BUYER_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); an `EXCHANGE` must supply `counterparty_agency_id` and leave `counterparty_bidder_id` empty, and anything else is `ERR_EXCHANGE_COUNTERPARTY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

The bidding window is a **half-open** interval: `bid_open_at` belongs to it and `bid_close_at` does not. Two offerings on the same parcel therefore conflict when, and only when, the new offering's `bid_open_at` falls strictly before the other's `bid_close_at` and the other's `bid_open_at` falls strictly before the new offering's `bid_close_at`. Adjacency is legal: a new window whose `bid_open_at` is exactly another window's `bid_close_at` does not conflict, and neither does the mirror case. The comparison set is every offering whose declaration points at the same `parcel_id` — not merely the same declaration — and only those in the four live statuses `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `EVALUATION` and `AWARDED`; an offering that has reached `FAILED_NO_QUALIFYING_BID`, `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED_CONVEYED` releases its window for reuse. This half-open treatment is deliberately unlike the closed intervals that govern appraiser engagements and environmental circulation, where sharing a single endpoint is an overlap.

On success the offering is created in `DRAFT` with three computed values frozen onto it and never recomputed thereafter: `minimum_price_cents` from the minimum-price rules of §3.7, `minimum_price_variant` recording which variant produced it, and `required_deposit_cents`. The required deposit is the offering's own frozen `minimum_price_cents` multiplied by 0.05, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then raised to the floor of 5,000,000 cents if the product falls below it — so every offering priced below 100,000,000 cents demands the same flat 5,000,000-cent deposit. The offering starts with `qualified_bid_count` zero, `rescind_count` zero, and `winning_bid_amount_cents`, `awarded_at`, `awarded_by_user_id` and `cancellation_reason` all empty. The declaration's `version` rises by 1. Offerings are readable individually and listable with filters on `declaration_id`, `counterparty_bidder_id`, `counterparty_agency_id`, `offering_type`, `status` and `minimum_price_variant`.

Publishing the offering opens it to bids. It requires the offering to be in `DRAFT` and requires the clock to be strictly before `bid_close_at` — an offering whose window has already run out can never be published, and either failure is `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the offering moves `DRAFT → OPEN`. Nothing else changes; in particular publishing does not re-derive the frozen prices.

**Submitting a bid** is the `Bidder`'s act on its own account: `bidder_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` and never from the request body. The request supplies `amount_cents`, the `deposit_amount_cents` tendered, and an optional `intended_use`. The checks run in this order. The offering must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. The offering's `offering_type` must be `COMPETITIVE_SALE`; a negotiated sale or an exchange never receives bids, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_COMPETITIVE_BID_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 409). The window must be live, meaning the clock stands at or after `bid_open_at` and strictly before `bid_close_at` — a bid at the opening instant is in time, a bid at the closing instant is too late — and a bid outside the live window is `ERR_BIDDING_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The bidder's `debarment_status` must be `CLEAR`, or `ERR_BIDDER_DEBARRED` (HTTP 422). The bidder must hold no other live bid on this same offering, where live means a `qualification_status` of `SUBMITTED`, `QUALIFIED`, `WINNING` or `BACKUP`; a second live bid is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID` (HTTP 409), while a bid whose predecessor was withdrawn or disqualified is permitted. Then `amount_cents` must be at least the offering's frozen `minimum_price_cents`, with a bid exactly at the minimum acceptable and anything below it `ERR_BID_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Then `deposit_amount_cents` must be at least the offering's frozen `required_deposit_cents`, with exact equality sufficient and one cent short refused as `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). And finally the bidder's `available_funds_cents` must be at least `deposit_amount_cents`, or the tender is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BIDDER_FUNDS` (HTTP 422).

On success the bid is created with `qualification_status` `SUBMITTED` and `deposit_status` `HELD`, with `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, `award_rank` and `disqualification_reason` empty. The deposit moves out of spendable cash and into escrow in one transfer: the bidder's `available_funds_cents` falls by `deposit_amount_cents` and `escrow_held_cents` rises by exactly the same amount, while `lifetime_deposits_cents` is untouched — that counter records funding credits from treasury, never internal movements. Bids are readable individually, showing both status dimensions and the award rank, and listable with filters on `offering_id`, `bidder_id`, `qualification_status` and `deposit_status`.

A bidder may withdraw its own bid, and only its own. Withdrawal requires the bid to be `SUBMITTED` with `deposit_status` `HELD` — a graded, winning or already-withdrawn bid cannot be pulled, and neither can one whose deposit has been released or applied, each of which is `ERR_INVALID_BID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and it requires the clock to be strictly before the offering's `bid_close_at`, with a later attempt refused as `ERR_BIDDING_CLOSED`. On success `qualification_status` moves `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` and `deposit_status` moves `HELD → RELEASED`, and the escrow is returned: `escrow_held_cents` falls by `deposit_amount_cents` and `available_funds_cents` rises by exactly that amount, restoring the ledger to the precise pair of values it held before the bid was submitted.

**Closing bidding** grades the field. It is the officer's act, it requires the offering to be `OPEN` and of type `COMPETITIVE_SALE` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE` — and it requires the window to have actually run out, meaning the clock stands at or after `bid_close_at`; closing while bidding is still live is refused with `ERR_BIDDING_STILL_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Every bid still in `SUBMITTED` is then graded on one criterion only: the bid moves `SUBMITTED → QUALIFIED` unless its bidder's `debarment_status` is now `DEBARRED`, in which case it moves `SUBMITTED → DISQUALIFIED` and its deposit moves `HELD → RELEASED`, with `escrow_held_cents` falling and `available_funds_cents` rising by `deposit_amount_cents`. Debarment is thus not retroactive at submission time but is fatal at grading time: a bid lawfully accepted from a clear bidder is graded out if the bidder was debarred in the interval. Grading does not test price or deposit sufficiency — those were enforced at submission and are tested again, independently, by the award ranking. The offering's `qualified_bid_count` is then recomputed, and the offering moves `OPEN → EVALUATION` when at least one bid qualifies. When none qualifies the offering moves `OPEN → FAILED_NO_QUALIFYING_BID` and a four-link cascade fires, which §3.9 sets out in full.

**Awarding** is the board's act and its two branches share almost nothing. Segregation of duties applies first, as described in §2.8: a clerk may not award an offering whose `awarded_by_user_id` records that same clerk from an award since rescinded, and the attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The state requirement then depends on the mechanism: a `COMPETITIVE_SALE` must be in `EVALUATION`, while a `NEGOTIATED_SALE` or `EXCHANGE` must be in `OPEN`, since neither ever passes through bidding or evaluation at all; the wrong status for the mechanism is `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`.

On the competitive branch the candidate set is assembled by filtering the bids on this offering: a bid is a candidate only if its `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`, its `amount_cents` is at least the offering's `minimum_price_cents`, its `deposit_amount_cents` is at least the offering's `required_deposit_cents`, and its bidder's `debarment_status` is `CLEAR` at this moment. Bids still `SUBMITTED`, and bids that are `DISQUALIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN`, are not candidates, and neither is a bid whose bidder was debarred after grading even though the bid itself still reads `QUALIFIED`. If the candidate set is empty the award is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_BID` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `amount_cents` from highest to lowest, so the greatest consideration to the public wins; ties on amount are broken by `submitted_at` from earliest to latest, so the earlier sealed bid prevails; any remaining tie is broken by `created_at` from earliest to latest. The row `id` is never consulted at any level of the ordering. Each candidate receives an `award_rank` from 1 upward in that order. The rank 1 bid moves to `qualification_status` `WINNING` and the offering's `winning_bid_amount_cents` is set from that bid's `amount_cents`; every candidate ranked 2 and below moves to `BACKUP`. The offering moves `EVALUATION → AWARDED`.

On the negotiated or exchange branch there are no bids to rank. The offering moves `OPEN → AWARDED` and `winning_bid_amount_cents` is set to the `agreed_price_cents` of the declaration's `AGREED` preference offer when such an offer exists, and otherwise to the offering's own frozen `minimum_price_cents`. Either branch stamps `awarded_at` from the logical clock and records `awarded_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`.

**Rescinding an award** is again the board's act, takes a `cancellation_reason`, and requires the offering to be `AWARDED` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. It also refuses to unwind a completed transfer: if a conveyance on the offering has already reached `RECORDED`, the rescission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE` (HTTP 409). The two mechanisms then diverge sharply. A `COMPETITIVE_SALE` returns to the ranking stage: the offering moves `AWARDED → EVALUATION`, the `WINNING` bid moves to `DISQUALIFIED` with its deposit moving `HELD → RELEASED` and the escrow returned to its bidder's `available_funds_cents`, every `BACKUP` bid returns to `QUALIFIED` so the field can be re-ranked, `winning_bid_amount_cents` is cleared, and `rescind_count` rises by 1. A `NEGOTIATED_SALE` or `EXCHANGE` has no field to fall back on and is abandoned: the offering moves `AWARDED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` stored, and `rescind_count` is not incremented. On either branch any conveyance on the offering whose `recording_status` is `PENDING_TITLE` or `READY_TO_RECORD` moves to `RESCINDED`, and its frozen `net_proceeds_cents` and `recoverable_curative_cost_cents` are both reset to zero.

### 3.9. When a Disposition Fails: the Unsuccessful Cascade, Re-Offering and Withdrawal

A competitive sale that closes with nothing to award does not merely stall the offering; it unwinds four things at once, and all four are part of the same close-bidding operation described in §3.8. The offering moves `OPEN → FAILED_NO_QUALIFYING_BID`, which also releases its bidding window so a later offering may occupy the same dates. The declaration moves `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED → UNSUCCESSFUL` and its `reoffer_eligible_at` is set to the logical clock plus 30 days, starting the statutory cooling-off period; its `version` rises by 1 on this failure cascade, and only on this branch — a close-bidding that grades at least one qualifying bid touches no declaration at all. The parcel's accepted appraisal moves `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED` with `revaluation_required` set to true, on the reasoning that a market that produced no qualifying bid has contradicted the appraised value. And because that appraisal is gone, the parcel's `valuation_status` becomes `STALE` and `disposition_ready` recomputes to false, so the parcel cannot be re-offered until a fresh appraisal has been ordered, reported and accepted. Note that the deposits of bids graded `DISQUALIFIED` during the same operation have already been released; a failed offering leaves no cash in escrow behind it.

Re-offering restarts the statutory process on an unsuccessful declaration and is the officer's act. It takes no request body. The declaration must be in `UNSUCCESSFUL`, and any other status is `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`. Then, in this order: every agency to be re-served must still hold `notice_registry_status` `REGISTERED`, and a lapsed recipient blocks the whole re-offer with `ERR_NOTICE_REGISTRY_LAPSED` (HTTP 422) rather than being silently dropped; the cooling-off period must have run, meaning the clock stands at or after `reoffer_eligible_at`, with the boundary instant itself sufficient and anything earlier refused as `ERR_REOFFER_COOLING_OFF` (HTTP 409); and the declaration's `reoffer_count` must be strictly below 2, so that a parcel may be re-offered at most twice before the disposition must be abandoned, with a third attempt refused as `ERR_REOFFER_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). The registry check therefore precedes the cooling-off check, and both precede the cycle-limit check.

On success the declaration moves `UNSUCCESSFUL → NOTICED`, `reoffer_count` rises by 1 and `reoffer_eligible_at` is cleared. A fresh notice round is then served: the service takes the highest `notice_round` that exists on this declaration, collects the distinct `recipient_agency_id` values of the notices in that round, and issues one new `StatutoryNotice` to each of them carrying `notice_round` equal to that highest round plus one, the same `response_window_days` the prior round used, `issued_at` stamped from the clock, and a `response_deadline_at` recomputed as that `issued_at` plus `response_window_days` days. The declaration's `notice_issued_at` and `notice_response_deadline_at` are recomputed from the new round, `notice_response_window_days` is unchanged because the window carries over, and `version` rises by 1. Notices from earlier rounds are left exactly as they were — their responses and expiries remain part of the record — and the preference offers created in earlier rounds keep whatever state they reached. The consequence for pricing is direct: the raised `reoffer_count` feeds the re-offer discount of the minimum-price rules in §3.7, so the next offering on this parcel is priced 10 percent below the un-discounted figure after one cycle and 20 percent below after two.

Withdrawal is the board's abandonment of the whole declaration and takes a `withdrawn_reason`. It is permitted only from `DRAFT`, before anything has been noticed, or from `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED`, where a live offering may exist; every other status, including `UNSUCCESSFUL`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECLARATION_STATE`. On success the declaration moves `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` or `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED → WITHDRAWN`, `withdrawn_reason` is stored, and `version` rises by 1. From `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED` the withdrawal also unwinds the market in three linked steps: every offering on this declaration whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `EVALUATION` moves to `CANCELLED`; every live bid on those offerings — meaning every bid whose `qualification_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `QUALIFIED`, `WINNING` or `BACKUP` — moves to `WITHDRAWN`; and every one of those bids whose `deposit_status` is `HELD` moves to `RELEASED`, with `escrow_held_cents` falling and `available_funds_cents` rising by that bid's `deposit_amount_cents` on the bidder concerned. A withdrawal thus never leaves a bidder's money in escrow. Each offering the cascade cancels records the declaration's `withdrawn_reason` as its own `cancellation_reason`, so the reason the offering died is legible on the offering itself. Offerings already in `AWARDED`, `FAILED_NO_QUALIFYING_BID`, `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED_CONVEYED` are not touched by the cascade at all.

### 3.10. Conveyance, Net Proceeds and the Waterfall

The conveyance file is opened by the `RealPropertyOfficer` on an awarded offering and captures the terms that will bind the grantee after closing: `closing_costs_cents` and `broker_fee_cents`, both of which must be zero or greater, the `deed_restrictions` array, which must hold between 1 and 10 clause strings inclusive, the `reverter_condition` whose breach will trigger reversion, and an optional `reverter_cure_period_days` which defaults to 180 and must fall within 30 through 730 inclusive. A count of restrictions outside 1 through 10, or a cure period outside 30 through 730, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Three further checks then apply in order. The offering must be in `AWARDED`, or `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. Then the deed must carry every burden the agency chose to keep: the number of entries in `deed_restrictions` must be at least the number of title curative items on the parcel whose `status` is `RESERVED_AS_EASEMENT`, and a deed with fewer clauses than reserved easements is refused with `ERR_EASEMENT_RESERVATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422). This is a floor on the count only — the system does not inspect the text of the clauses — and a parcel with no reserved easements is bound only by the general 1-to-10 requirement. Finally the offering must not already have a live conveyance: if another conveyance on this offering holds a `recording_status` of `PENDING_TITLE`, `READY_TO_RECORD` or `RECORDED`, the request is refused with `ERR_CONVEYANCE_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409), while a conveyance that reached `RESCINDED` frees the offering for a fresh file. Note the ordering: the easement floor is evaluated before the duplicate check.

On success the conveyance is created with `recording_status` `PENDING_TITLE` and `compliance_status` `NOT_MONITORED`, its `parcel_id` copied from the declaration behind the offering, and its `sale_price_cents` copied from the offering's `winning_bid_amount_cents` — the consideration is therefore the winning bid amount on a competitive sale, and the offering's minimum price (or the agreed preference price) on a negotiated sale or exchange, exactly as the award recorded it. The grantee is resolved from whichever leg the award used: for a competitive sale `grantee_kind` is `BIDDER` with `grantee_bidder_id` set to the `bidder_id` of the `WINNING` bid and `grantee_agency_id` left empty; for a negotiated sale or exchange the leg on the offering governs, so a `counterparty_bidder_id` yields `grantee_kind` `BIDDER` with `grantee_bidder_id` set from it, and a `counterparty_agency_id` yields `grantee_kind` `AGENCY` with `grantee_agency_id` set from it and `grantee_bidder_id` left empty. Exactly one grantee reference is populated. `recoverable_curative_cost_cents`, `net_proceeds_cents` and `allocated_proceeds_cents` all start at zero, and `recorded_instrument_number`, `recorded_at`, `violation_noticed_at`, `cure_deadline_at`, `violation_description`, `cure_note` and `reverted_at` all start empty. Conveyances are readable individually and listable with filters on `offering_id`, `parcel_id`, `grantee_bidder_id`, `grantee_agency_id`, `recording_status` and `compliance_status`.

**Clearing title** is the officer's certification that the file is ready for the recorder, and it is where the money is fixed. It requires `recording_status` to be `PENDING_TITLE`, and any other value is `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE` (HTTP 409). Then, in order: every title curative item on the parcel must have left `OPEN` — cured, waived with an endorsement or reserved as an easement all satisfy the test, and a single open item is `ERR_TITLE_ITEMS_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 422). An environmental review on the declaration must have reached `CERTIFIED`, or `ERR_ENV_REVIEW_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422); the check is repeated here because a review may have been recirculated after the authorization was granted. And the transaction must actually yield money to distribute: the provisional net, being `sale_price_cents` less `closing_costs_cents` less `broker_fee_cents`, must be strictly greater than zero, and a provisional net of exactly zero or less is refused with `ERR_NET_PROCEEDS_NON_POSITIVE` (HTTP 422).

Two figures are then computed once and frozen on the conveyance. The recovery cap is the provisional net multiplied by 0.15, rounded half up to a whole cent. The incurred curative cost is the sum of `actual_cure_cost_cents` over the title curative items on this conveyance's parcel whose `status` is `CURED`; items waived with an endorsement or reserved as an easement carry a zero cure cost and so contribute nothing. `recoverable_curative_cost_cents` is the smaller of the incurred curative cost and the recovery cap, so the agency recovers its curative spending off the top but never more than 15 percent of the provisional net. `net_proceeds_cents` is then the provisional net less `recoverable_curative_cost_cents`. Both values are stored on the conveyance and are not recomputed by any later operation; the conveyance moves `PENDING_TITLE → READY_TO_RECORD`. Note that `net_proceeds_cents` may legitimately be zero when the recoverable curative cost consumes the entire provisional net — the non-positive test applies to the provisional net, before the curative deduction, not to `net_proceeds_cents`.

**Recording the deed** is the single largest operation in the system. It requires `recording_status` to be `READY_TO_RECORD`, or `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE`, and it takes the `recorded_instrument_number` assigned by the county recorder. Before anything moves, two fiscal preconditions are tested in order: at least one fund must exist, since money cannot be posted to nowhere, and a store with no funds configured is refused with `ERR_FUNDS_NOT_CONFIGURED` (HTTP 422); and the sum of `allocation_pct` across every fund must equal exactly 1, with any other sum — short, over, or drifting after an adjustment — refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_PCT_SUM_INVALID` (HTTP 422) before a single allocation row is emitted.

On success five things happen together. The conveyance moves `READY_TO_RECORD → RECORDED` with `recorded_instrument_number` and `recorded_at` stored, and its compliance dimension moves `NOT_MONITORED → COMPLIANT`, arming the deed-restriction monitoring described in §3.11. The parcel's `ownership_status` moves `AGENCY_OWNED → CONVEYED`, its `disposition_ready` recomputes to false, and its `version` rises by 1. The offering moves `AWARDED → CLOSED_CONVEYED`, releasing its bidding window, and the declaration moves `OFFERING_AUTHORIZED → CONVEYED` — a terminal status — with its `version` raised by 1. The proceeds waterfall runs, posting one or more allocation rows and crediting the funds. And on a competitive sale the winning bid's deposit is consumed: the `WINNING` bid's `deposit_status` moves `HELD → APPLIED` and its bidder's `escrow_held_cents` falls by `deposit_amount_cents` while `available_funds_cents` is left alone, because that money has left the bidder for good. On a negotiated sale or exchange there is no bid and no deposit movement at all.

**The proceeds waterfall** distributes `net_proceeds_cents` across the funds in three passes, and every fund is ordered by `priority_rank` ascending. Because `priority_rank` is unique across funds that order is total and needs no tie-break; no row `id` ever participates in the ordering.

Pass one takes the statutory fixed reserves off the top. Starting with a remaining balance equal to `net_proceeds_cents`, the service walks the funds in `priority_rank` order and for each one takes the smaller of that fund's `fixed_reserve_cents` and the remaining balance. When that amount is greater than zero it posts an allocation row against the fund with `amount_cents` set to it, `allocation_basis` `FIXED_RESERVE` and `residual_included_cents` zero, and reduces the remaining balance by the same amount. When the amount is zero — because the fund has no reserve, or because the remaining balance has already been exhausted — no row is posted for that fund in this pass. The first bucket therefore fills first: if the net proceeds are smaller than the sum of the reserves, the earliest-ranked funds are paid in full and the later ones receive a partial row or nothing at all.

Pass two splits whatever survived pass one. The surviving remaining balance is captured as the percentage base, and every fund whose `allocation_pct` is strictly greater than zero receives a row, again in `priority_rank` order: `amount_cents` is that fund's `allocation_pct` of the percentage base, rounded half up to a whole cent, with `allocation_basis` `PERCENTAGE` and `residual_included_cents` zero, and the remaining balance falls by each share as it is posted. Every share is computed against the same captured base, never against a base that shrinks as rows are posted. Funds whose `allocation_pct` is zero receive no percentage row, even though they may already have received a fixed-reserve row in pass one — a single fund can therefore hold two allocation rows from the same recording.

Pass three disposes of the rounding residual. If the remaining balance is not zero after pass two, and at least one fund took part in the percentage split, the residual is folded into the last percentage row in `priority_rank` order: that row's `amount_cents` is increased by the remaining balance and its `residual_included_cents` is set to the remaining balance, after which the remaining balance is zero. The residual is signed, so a set of half-up shares that overshot the base produces a negative residual which reduces the last percentage row, and a set that undershot produces a positive one which increases it. The residual never lands on a fixed-reserve row and never lands on any percentage row but the last. The invariant this guarantees is exact: the sum of `amount_cents` over the rows posted by one recording equals that conveyance's `net_proceeds_cents` to the cent.

Every posted row is created with `status` `POSTED`, `reversed_at` empty, `conveyance_id` set to the recording conveyance and `fund_id` set to the credited fund. Each credited fund's `balance_cents` and `lifetime_credits_cents` both rise by that row's final `amount_cents`, the residual included. The conveyance's `allocated_proceeds_cents` is then set to the posted total, which by the invariant equals `net_proceeds_cents`.

Allocation rows are an event log, not an editable resource: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else from the common audit set, and no endpoint creates or amends one directly. They are listable three ways: per fund, where the listing includes the `CHALLENGE_FORFEITURE` rows described in §3.4 that belong to no conveyance and can be narrowed by `status` and `allocation_basis`; per conveyance, in `priority_rank` order of the credited fund and narrowable by `status`; and across the whole register, narrowable by `conveyance_id`, `fund_id`, `allocation_basis` and `status`.

### 3.11. Deed Restrictions, Cure, and Enforcing the Reverter

Once the deed is recorded, the conveyance's compliance dimension becomes the live half of the record while its recording dimension sits still at `RECORDED`. The `ComplianceMonitor` owns this phase, and the cycle it drives can repeat any number of times: a violation may be noticed, cured, noticed again and cured again without limit, until either the parties settle into compliance or the agency takes the land back.

Noticing a violation requires the conveyance to be in `recording_status` `RECORDED` and its `compliance_status` to be either `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`; a conveyance that is not yet recorded, one already `RESCINDED`, one with a violation already outstanding, or one already `REVERTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE` (HTTP 409). The monitor supplies a `violation_description`. On success `compliance_status` moves `COMPLIANT → VIOLATION_NOTICED` or `CURED → VIOLATION_NOTICED`, `violation_noticed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `violation_description` is stored, and `cure_deadline_at` is computed as `violation_noticed_at` plus `reverter_cure_period_days` days — the cure period frozen onto the conveyance when the file was opened, so the same conveyance grants the same cure period on every violation. Any `cure_note` recorded from an earlier cure is retained rather than cleared, so the audit trail of the previous cycle survives the new notice.

Accepting a cure requires `compliance_status` to be `VIOLATION_NOTICED` — any other value is `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE` — and requires the clock to stand at or before `cure_deadline_at`; a cure accepted exactly on the deadline instant is in time, and an attempt after it is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). The monitor supplies a `cure_note`. On success `compliance_status` moves `VIOLATION_NOTICED → CURED`, the `cure_note` is stored, and both `violation_noticed_at` and `cure_deadline_at` are cleared, because no violation is outstanding any more. `violation_description` is left in place as the record of what was cured. The reverter is thereby re-armed: a further violation notice is admissible from `CURED`, which is what closes the compliance cycle.

Enforcing the reverter is the mirror image. It requires `compliance_status` to be `VIOLATION_NOTICED` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_CONVEYANCE_STATE` — and it requires the clock to stand strictly after `cure_deadline_at`. On the deadline instant itself the grantee still holds the right to cure, so enforcement at exactly `cure_deadline_at` is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409); this is the one deadline in the system whose boundary instant favours the party being timed rather than the party doing the timing, and it is the exact complement of the cure test above, so at no instant are both operations available and at no instant are both refused.

Enforcement unwinds the transaction in four linked steps. The conveyance moves `compliance_status` `VIOLATION_NOTICED → REVERTED` and, on the other dimension, `recording_status` `RECORDED → RESCINDED`, with `reverted_at` stamped from the logical clock; this is the only operation that drives both dimensions of a conveyance at once. The parcel comes back to the agency: `ownership_status` moves `CONVEYED → REVERTED_TO_AGENCY`, `valuation_status` is set to `STALE` because a value determined before the failed conveyance can no longer be relied on, `disposition_ready` recomputes to false, and `version` rises by 1. Every allocation row on this conveyance whose `status` is `POSTED` moves to `REVERSED` with `reversed_at` stamped from the clock; rows already `REVERSED` are left alone, and rows belonging to other conveyances or to an appraisal-challenge forfeiture are untouched. For each row reversed, the credited fund's `balance_cents` falls by that row's `amount_cents` and its `lifetime_debits_cents` rises by the same amount, while `lifetime_credits_cents` is left exactly as it was — the credit happened, and the ledger records both sides rather than erasing the first. Finally the conveyance's `allocated_proceeds_cents` recomputes to zero, since no posted row remains. The frozen `sale_price_cents`, `recoverable_curative_cost_cents` and `net_proceeds_cents` are not disturbed: they remain the historical record of the reverted transaction. Neither the offering nor the declaration is moved by enforcement — the offering stays `CLOSED_CONVEYED` and the declaration stays `CONVEYED` — so a reverted parcel needs a fresh declaration before it can be disposed of again, and the duplicate-active-declaration check permits exactly that because the old declaration is in a terminal status.
