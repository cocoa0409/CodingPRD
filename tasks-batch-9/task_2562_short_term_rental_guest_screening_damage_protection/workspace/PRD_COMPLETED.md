# Short-Term Rental Guest Screening & Damage Protection API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Vacation-rental operators live between two unhappy extremes. Screen guests too loosely and a listing becomes the venue for an unauthorised party, a noise war with the neighbourhood, and a damage bill nobody wants to own. Screen them too tightly and legitimate travellers are turned away, hosts lose revenue, and the platform inherits a discrimination problem instead of a safety one. This document specifies the backend service that sits in the middle: a trust-and-safety system that scores guests from evidence rather than intuition, prices damage protection according to that score, enforces the local law each listing is bound by, and adjudicates damage claims through a documented, appealable process with a real money trail.

The service is deliberately opinionated about **who** may act. A host configures its listings, its monitoring devices and its damage claims, but never decides them. A trust-and-safety case worker decides claims and appeals, but may not decide both stages of the same case. A compliance reviewer publishes the local rule sets and the insurance stack, reviews identity documents and sensor disclosures, and administers platform-wide bans — but may not rule on a dispute of a ban it wrote itself. An operator registers accounts, records payment-processor signals, drives the logical clock and runs the nightly sweep, and holds no adjudication authority whatsoever. Every consequential write therefore leaves a named actor behind it, and several rules exist purely to keep two stages of the same decision in two different hands.

Money is the other axis the design turns on. Damage exposure is absorbed in a fixed order — the guest's own security deposit first, then a waterfall of insurance layers ordered by their declared position — and the totals must reconcile exactly: whatever the adjudicator approved is either recovered or absorbed by the host, never lost and never double-counted. When an appeal overturns an approval the reversal must be exact too, restoring each layer's consumed capacity and the guest's held deposit to the values they had before the approval was written. Every route in this specification is mounted beneath the `/rentals` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control surface described in §2.5.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every business request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting as. If either header is absent, empty, or holding nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs; surrounding whitespace is stripped from both values before the role is recognised and before any identity comparison, so a padded identifier still matches the resource it owns. If `X-User-Role` holds a string that is not one of the five recognised roles, or holds a recognised role that the target endpoint does not authorise, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are spelled exactly `Guest`, `Host`, `TrustAdjudicator`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin`.

- **`Guest`** — the travelling party. It books stays, uploads identity documents, appeals approvals levied against it, disputes bans, cancels its own bookings and uploads claim evidence. When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guest` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Guest` compare the resource's guest foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — directly through `guest_id` on a reservation, an identity document or a blocklist entry, and transitively through the reservation for a damage claim.
- **`Host`** — the listing owner. It creates and activates listings, registers monitoring devices and submits their disclosures, confirms, checks in and completes stays, files damage claims and submits them for review, appeals denials, uploads claim evidence, files incidents and may ban a guest at property or host scope. When `X-User-Role` is `Host`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Host` row; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the owning listing's `host_id`, or to `host_id` on the damage claim.
- **`TrustAdjudicator`** — the trust-and-safety case worker. It resolves reservations parked in manual screening, triages incidents, and adjudicates damage claims and their appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `TrustAdjudicator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `screening_decided_by_user_id` on a reservation, as `decided_by_user_id` and `appeal_decided_by_user_id` on a damage claim — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — the regulatory reviewer. It publishes jurisdiction rule sets and coverage layers, reviews identity documents and sensor disclosures, is the only role permitted to write a platform-scope ban, and rules on ban disputes and outright lifts. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an identity document or a monitoring device, and as `created_by_user_id` or `dispute_decided_by_user_id` on a blocklist entry, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the operator. It registers guest and host accounts, records payment-processor signals, publishes jurisdiction rules and coverage layers alongside compliance, files incidents, drives the logical clock and runs the adjudication sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. A `PlatformAdmin` can decide neither claims, appeals, screening decisions nor disputes, and can neither create nor lift blocklist entries — the operator sits deliberately outside the adjudication chain.

Where an endpoint ties an authorised `Guest` or `Host` to the addressed resource and the acting principal is not that owner, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Role membership is checked before ownership: an unrecognised or unauthorised role never reaches the ownership comparison. Which endpoints carry such a tie is settled action by action in §3; among the read endpoints only the single-listing read of §3.1 carries one, so every other read hands the addressed row, and every list hands every row matching the supplied filters, to any role the endpoint authorises without comparing identities at all.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`: the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from real time — so every response produced while the clock stands still carries the identical stamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- The same envelope also carries the framework-level rejections that no workflow rule produced, and it is used for the 500 case as well as for 4xx: a request whose path matches no route at all answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a request whose path exists but not for the method used answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, and an unexpected internal failure answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500. Those three identifiers sit outside the catalogue of §5, which enumerates only the errors the business rules raise.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no smaller than 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a `limit` or `offset` that is present but does not parse as an integer, while either parameter supplied as an empty string is read as omitted and falls back to its default.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still between two calls that set it, many rows routinely carry the identical `created_at`; rows sharing a stamp keep the order in which they were written, oldest write first, and ordering never looks at `id`. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` is true whenever rows remain beyond the current window.

Every list endpoint also accepts the domain filters named in §3; combining filters narrows the result set conjunctively, and an unsupplied filter — including one supplied as an empty string — is simply not applied. A filter over a string or enum field matches by exact equality and is never case-folded or partially matched; a boolean filter, meaning `payout_hold` on hosts and `voided` on settlement allocations, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case; an integer filter, meaning `evidence_round` on claim evidence, must parse as an integer. Any other value for a boolean or integer filter is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no domain-meaningful structure; a client must never parse one. Clients never supply `id` on creation.

Most resources also carry three common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant, with three fractional digits, at which the row was first written. `updated_at` is the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. Every other instant-valued field any representation exposes — the derived deadlines of §2.5 as well as `detected_at` on an incident, `captured_at` on an evidence artifact, `expires_on` on an identity document, and `submitted_for_review_at` and `decided_at` on a claim — is rendered in that same shape, always normalised to UTC and always carrying exactly three fractional digits, regardless of the precision or the zone offset the client wrote it with. An instant *supplied* by a client is accepted in ISO 8601 form with or without a seconds component and with one to nine fractional digits; a trailing `Z` or a `±HH:MM` offset is honoured and converted to UTC, and a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request. It advances at most once per request: however many times a single request writes to the same row, directly or through any number of cascade links, that row's `version` ends exactly one higher than it began, while `updated_at` tracks the last of those writes.

Three entities are exceptions and must be recognised as such. A jurisdiction rule set, a claim-evidence artifact and a settlement-allocation line each carry only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations, because all three are immutable once written. A jurisdiction rule set has no update endpoint at all; an evidence artifact is never edited after upload; an allocation line is only ever voided as part of an appeal reversal, and the void is recorded on the line's own `voided` flag rather than by re-versioning it.

Because a single request routinely mutates several rows, `version` counters move in more than one place. Reviewing an identity document, for instance, bumps the document's own `version` and separately bumps the guest's `version`, because the guest's derived counters and score changed. Every such secondary bump is called out explicitly in §3 where the cascade is described; when a rule says a cascade "recomputes" a field on another entity, that other entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1 wherever §3 declares that bump. A handful of aggregates are instead derived on read — a listing's `sensor_compliance_state`, `coverage_posture`, `open_reservation_count` and `open_claim_count`, a claim's `evidence_count`, a coverage layer's `consumed_cents` and a host's settlement counters — so their movement on its own never re-versions the row that exposes them; where such a row is re-versioned in the same request anyway, §3 declares that bump for its own reasons, and §3 also says so at each point where one of these aggregates moves with no bump at all.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every deadline, window, expiry and sweep comparison reads a server-side **logical clock** instead. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. An operator-only test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and returns that instant; it mutates no entity, and only the requests that follow observe the new reading. Being a harness facility rather than a business endpoint, it applies the header rules of §2.1 conditionally: a request that supplies `X-User-Role` with anything other than whitespace must name `PlatformAdmin` and must also carry a non-blank `X-User-ID`, drawing `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` or `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` respectively, while a request that omits `X-User-Role` altogether, or supplies nothing but whitespace in it, is accepted with no authentication check at all.

Nine derived deadline fields are computed from the clock or from data pinned against it, and each is described where it is produced: `check_in_at` and `check_out_at` on a reservation, `claim_window_ends_at` on a reservation, `response_due_at` on an incident, `adjudication_due_at` and `appeal_due_at` on a damage claim, `dispute_due_at` on a blocklist entry, plus the `expires_on` instant supplied with an identity document and the `submitted_for_review_at` and `decided_at` stamps recorded on a claim.

Deadline comparisons follow two different conventions, and the difference is intentional. Interactive rejections are inclusive of the boundary: an appeal is admissible while the clock is at or before `appeal_due_at`, and a dispute is admissible while the clock is at or before `dispute_due_at`. The sweep, by contrast, uses a strict comparison in every phase: a row is only acted on when its deadline instant is strictly earlier than the single clock reading the sweep captured, so an entity sitting exactly on its deadline survives the current sweep and lapses only on a later one.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and the Conserved-Loss Invariant

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. Percentage inputs — `coverage_pct` on an insurance layer and the derived `trust_fee_pct` on a reservation — are fractions between 0 and 1 rather than whole-number percentages.

Wherever a multiplication produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent: exactly one half of a cent rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. Rounding is applied at the points named in §3 and nowhere else. In particular it is applied once to a reservation's waiver fee, once to a reservation's trust-and-safety fee, and once per allocation line in the settlement waterfall — never to a running subtotal and never re-applied to a figure that has already been rounded.

The settlement ledger obeys a conservation rule that holds without exception: for any claim on which an approval has been written, the sum of `recovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents` equals `approved_amount_cents` exactly. Deductibles, co-insurance shortfalls and the uncovered tail are all absorbed by the host and all appear inside `host_absorbed_cents`; the uncovered tail also appears separately as `uncovered_cents` so that a shortfall caused by exhausted capacity can be distinguished from one caused by deductibles.

### 2.7. Pinning Terms Against Concurrent Change (Optimistic Concurrency)

Three creation operations pin facts they read from a parent resource, and each of the three therefore requires the client to declare the parent `version` those facts came from. In all three cases the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and a mismatch against the stored parent `version` is rejected with HTTP 412 before any state is written.

**Booking a stay** requires `expected_property_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the listing being booked. Creation copies or applies the entire commercial configuration of the listing — nightly rate, protection product, deposit amount, waiver fee, claim ceiling, event policy and turnover buffer — so a guest who priced against an older configuration must not be allowed to book on it. When `expected_property_version` does not equal the listing's current `version`, the booking is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). Note that a second booking of an unchanged listing is entirely legitimate product behaviour, so this rule cannot be reduced to a state precondition — only the version comparison expresses it.

**Filing a damage claim** requires `expected_reservation_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the completed stay. A claim is filed against a specific settled position of that stay: the deposit hold still standing on it and the claim window computed from its checkout. When `expected_reservation_version` does not equal the reservation's current `version`, the claim is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412), because the deposit may already have been released or applied by another claim in the interim. Filing a second claim against the same completed stay is permitted, so again no state precondition can substitute for the version check.

**Creating a blocklist entry** requires `expected_guest_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the guest being banned. Every ban is justified by the guest's evidence position at the moment it was written — the substantiated-incident count, the approved-claim count or the critical risk tier named in `reason_code`. When `expected_guest_version` does not equal the guest's current `version`, the entry is refused with `ERR_GUEST_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412), because a claim overturned on appeal or a document expiry may have moved the grounds out from under it. A second, broader ban on an unchanged guest is legitimate — that is precisely what supersession exists for — so this is a genuine version check rather than a disguised state precondition.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Confirmations, check-ins, completions, cancellations, reviews, decisions, appeals, disputes and lifts are all guarded by state preconditions and ownership checks alone.

### 2.8. Order of Precedence Among Rejections

When several rules would reject the same request, the response must carry the error of the **earliest** applicable rule in the following fixed order. Tests depend on this ordering, so it is stated once here and referenced by the workflow narratives that rely on it.

1. **Authentication.** Presence and non-emptiness of `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. **Role authorisation.** The role is recognised and listed for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request shape.** Every required body field present with the declared type, every enum field holding a declared value, every integer inside its declared bound, and `limit`/`offset` inside their permitted ranges — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An absent or blank body reads as an empty JSON object, so an action that declares no fields at all accepts either; a body that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not an object, is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the request's `Content-Type` is never consulted. A required field supplied as `null` counts as absent, while an optional field supplied as `null` falls back to whatever default it is documented with. Types are checked strictly: a field declared as an integer rejects a boolean and rejects a JSON number carrying a fractional part even when that part is zero, a field declared as a boolean rejects the strings `"true"` and `"false"`, a field declared as a number rejects a boolean, and a field declared as an enum rejects any non-string. A required string field must also be non-empty once surrounding whitespace is discounted, though the value is stored exactly as supplied.
4. **Path existence.** Every `{id}` in the URL resolves to a stored row, checked strictly left to right, each with its own not-found error.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign-key identifier in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the fields are declared on the entity, each with its own not-found error.
6. **Ownership.** An acting `Guest` matches the resource's guest, and an acting `Host` owns the listing or the claim — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Every action reads ownership at this position except the appeal filing of §3.8, which defers it behind that action's own terminality, claim-state and appellant-role gates.
7. **Concurrency.** The submitted `expected_property_version`, `expected_reservation_version` or `expected_guest_version` equals the stored parent version — the matching stale-version error from §2.7.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user who took the first-instance action — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Terminality.** The addressed resource is not already in a terminal state for the dimension the action would move — `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. This is checked *before* the declared-transition check, because no transition is ever declared out of a terminal state and the terminal case must report its own error rather than being masked as an invalid transition. Exactly one action departs from this position: the two dispute-window gates of §3.9 are pinned ahead of the `entry_state` reading of a blocklist entry, so a dispute filed against an entry whose window is already spent or lapsed answers `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` even where this step would otherwise have answered first. Every other action in §3 reads terminality here, ahead of its own preconditions.
10. **Declared transition.** The resource sits in a non-terminal state from which the requested transition exists — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
11. **State and window preconditions** specific to the action, in the order given in §3.
12. **Safety, privacy and compliance rules.**
13. **Constraint, temporal, referential and uniqueness rules.**
14. **Fiscal rules,** deliberately last: protection-product coherence on a listing, the claim ceiling on a claim, and the approved-amount bounds on a decision are the final checks a request meets, so a request that also violates an earlier rule reports the earlier rule instead.

### 2.9. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Body Reference Checks

URLs in this API address a single resource by `{id}` in the terminal path segment; deeper action segments qualify the verb rather than nesting a second resource. Where an identifier does appear in a path it is resolved before the body is examined, and each resource class has its own not-found error: `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JURISDICTION_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_LAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BLOCKLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. All thirteen carry HTTP 404.

The same catalogue governs foreign-key identifiers supplied in a request body, and the resolution order follows the order in which those fields are declared on the target entity rather than the order in which a client happened to serialise them. Creating a listing therefore resolves `host_id` before `jurisdiction_rule_id`; creating a blocklist entry resolves `guest_id`, then `property_id`, then `host_id`, then `supersedes_entry_id`, with an unresolvable supersession pointer reported as `ERR_BLOCKLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Body reference resolution applies to the creation of listings, monitoring devices, identity documents, reservations, incidents, damage claims, claim evidence, coverage layers and blocklist entries.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties in the Adjudication Chain

Two decisions in this system are explicitly two-person operations, and the rule is the same in both: the `X-User-ID` that took the first-instance action may never be the `X-User-ID` that reviews it. On the appeal decision of a damage claim, the acting adjudicator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's stored `decided_by_user_id`. On the dispute decision of a blocklist entry, the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the entry's stored `created_by_user_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

One consequence is worth stating up front, because it is easy to get wrong. When the adjudication sweep denies a claim for a lapsed service level, it stamps the literal string `"system"` into `decided_by_user_id`. That literal can never equal a real adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, so an appeal against a sweep denial is always admissible to any adjudicator — the segregation rule never deadlocks such a claim.

### 2.11. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

Several entities in this domain carry more than one independent state dimension, and a transition in one dimension does not imply a transition in another unless a rule says so explicitly.

A reservation carries three dimensions at once. `screening_state` records the trust-and-safety verdict and moves through `SCREENING`, `CLEARED`, `MANUAL_REVIEW` and `DENIED`, with `CLEARED` and `DENIED` terminal. `stay_state` records occupancy and moves through `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_STAY`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, with `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `deposit_state` records the security-deposit hold and moves through `NONE`, `HELD`, `APPLIED` and `RELEASED`, with `RELEASED` terminal. `SCREENING` is transient: the screening verdict is computed inside the request that creates the reservation, so no stored row is ever observed with `screening_state` reading `SCREENING`. For a listing sold with a damage waiver rather than a deposit, `deposit_state` reads `NONE` for the entire lifecycle of the booking.

A blocklist entry carries two dimensions. `entry_state` records enforcement and moves through `ACTIVE`, `DISPUTED` and `LIFTED`, with `LIFTED` terminal. `dispute_window_state` records the guest's procedural right and moves through `OPEN`, `DISPUTED`, `RESOLVED` and `EXPIRED`, with `RESOLVED` and `EXPIRED` terminal. The two advance on their own clocks: a dispute window can lapse to `EXPIRED` while the entry is still `ACTIVE`, and equally after the entry has already been `LIFTED`.

The remaining state machines are single-dimension. An identity document's `review_state` runs `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, with `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` terminal. A monitoring device's `disclosure_state` runs `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, with only `APPROVED` terminal — a rejected disclosure is expected to be remediated and resubmitted. A listing's `listing_state` runs `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` with no terminal state at all. An insurance layer's `layer_state` alternates between `AVAILABLE` and `EXHAUSTED`. An incident's `incident_state` runs `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ESCALATED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, `CLOSED`, `DISMISSED`, with `CLOSED` and `DISMISSED` terminal. A damage claim's `claim_state` runs `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `APPEALED`, `SETTLED`, `CLOSED_DENIED`, with `SETTLED` and `CLOSED_DENIED` terminal.

Cascades in this system are chained by **field change**, not by request. When accepting an identity document changes a guest's accepted-document counters, the counter change re-derives the guest's verification state; the new verification state re-runs the risk score; the new score re-bands the risk tier; and a re-band into the critical tier can itself create a platform ban that cancels future-dated bookings. Each link fires because the previous field moved, so an implementation that only handles the direct effect of a request will diverge. Every such chain is spelled out in §3 at the point where its first link is written.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Local Rule Sets, Listing Onboarding and Sensor Compliance

Everything a listing is allowed to do begins with the **jurisdiction rule set** it binds to. A `ComplianceOfficer` — or a `PlatformAdmin` acting alongside compliance — publishes one rule set per locality, identified by a `jurisdiction_code` such as `US-CA-SFO`. The code must not already exist, compared by exact equality and neither case-folded nor trimmed; a collision is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_CODE_TAKEN`. Publication also fixes `min_stay_nights`, which must be at least 1; `max_occupancy_cap`, the statutory head-count ceiling, also at least 1; the boolean flags `events_prohibited`, `registration_required`, `indoor_camera_allowed`, `indoor_audio_allowed` and `sensor_notice_required`; and `noise_threshold_db`, a decibel level supplied as a number that may carry a fractional part and that must be greater than zero. A rule set is immutable: there is no way to amend one, it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and rule sets can be looked up individually or listed with an optional `jurisdiction_code` filter.

A `Host` creates a listing by supplying `host_id` — which must equal its own `X-User-ID`, otherwise the creation is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — together with `jurisdiction_rule_id`, `address_line`, `city`, an optional `registration_number`, `max_occupancy` of at least 1, a `bedrooms` count of at least 0 kept purely for operator display, a `nightly_rate_cents` greater than zero, and a `claim_ceiling_cents` greater than zero that caps any single damage claim filed against the listing. The house rule on parties is declared as `event_policy`, either `EVENTS_ALLOWED` or `EVENTS_PROHIBITED`, and it is independent of whatever the jurisdiction says. An optional `turnover_buffer_nights`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, declares a mandatory cleaning gap between two consecutive bookings of the listing.

The damage-protection product is where listing configuration gets strict. `protection_product` is either `DEPOSIT` or `WAIVER`, and exactly one of the two accompanying money fields may be populated. On a `DEPOSIT` listing, `deposit_amount_cents` must be greater than zero and `waiver_nightly_fee_cents` must be exactly zero. On a `WAIVER` listing the reverse holds: `waiver_nightly_fee_cents` must be greater than zero and `deposit_amount_cents` must be exactly zero. Either field may otherwise be zero or above, but any combination that contradicts the declared product is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CONFIG_INVALID`. This is a fiscal check and is therefore evaluated after every structural, existence and ownership check on the same request.

A new listing lands in `DRAFT` with `suspension_reason` null. Two aggregates are derived immediately: `sensor_compliance_state`, which reads `COMPLIANT` for a listing with no devices installed, and `coverage_posture`, computed from the insurance layers visible to the listing. Two counters are maintained alongside them. `open_reservation_count` is the number of reservations of this listing whose `stay_state` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_STAY`. `open_claim_count` is the number of damage claims filed against stays at this listing whose `claim_state` is `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED`. Listings can be read individually, or listed with optional `host_id`, `jurisdiction_rule_id` and `listing_state` filters. The individual read by `id` is the one read in this system that carries an ownership tie: an acting `Host` may address only a listing whose `host_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and any other listing is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, while a `Guest` — which no field of a listing references — and the three operator roles may read any listing. No other read over listings restricts by owner: neither the listing list nor the coverage-summary read described below compares identities.

**Sensor posture** is an aggregate over the listing's monitoring devices, and its clause order is what makes it useful. If any device of the listing sits in `REJECTED`, the listing's `sensor_compliance_state` is `NON_COMPLIANT` — a single rejected disclosure poisons the listing even when every other device is approved. Otherwise, if any device sits in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_REVIEW`, the posture is `PENDING_REVIEW`. Otherwise the posture is `COMPLIANT`, and that includes the vacuous case of a listing with no devices at all: an unmonitored listing is deliberately bookable.

**Coverage posture** is the parallel aggregate over insurance. The layers *visible* to a listing are those with `scope` reading `PLATFORM_GLOBAL` plus those whose `property_id` names this listing. Of those, the *available* ones are the layers whose `layer_state` reads `AVAILABLE`. When the available count is zero the posture is `UNPROTECTED`; when the available count is below the visible count the posture is `REDUCED`; otherwise it is `FULL`. The zero clause is evaluated first on purpose, so a listing with no visible layer at all reads `UNPROTECTED` rather than falling through to `FULL`. A dedicated computed read exposes how that verdict was reached, returning `coverage_posture` alongside `visible_layer_count`, `available_layer_count` and `remaining_capacity_cents`, the last being the total unconsumed capacity across the available layers.

Activating a listing is a `Host` action, and the same capability serves two different transitions with deliberately different preconditions. The first activation moves `listing_state` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, and demands that the acting host owns the listing, that `listing_state` currently reads `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` — an activation aimed at a listing already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — that `sensor_compliance_state` reads `COMPLIANT`, a posture of `PENDING_REVIEW` or `NON_COMPLIANT` also drawing `ERR_INVALID_STATE` here rather than the `ERR_SENSOR_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED` that the confirmation gate of §3.4 raises, and that `registration_number` is present and non-empty whenever the bound jurisdiction has `registration_required` set true — a missing or blank registration number in a registration-required jurisdiction is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_REQUIRED`. Reinstatement moves `listing_state` from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` and demands all of the same things **plus** one more: `coverage_posture` must not read `UNPROTECTED`, and a reinstatement attempted with an empty insurance stack is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_UNPROTECTED`. These preconditions are evaluated in a fixed order — the state gate first, then the sensor posture, then the reinstatement-only coverage gate, then the registration duty — so a suspended listing that fails more than one of them reports the earliest. The asymmetry is intentional — a brand-new listing may go live before any insurance layer has been published for it, but a listing that has already been taken down may not come back without coverage. Either transition clears `suspension_reason` back to null and increases the listing's `version` by 1.

Suspension, by contrast, is never requested. A listing in `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` only as the tail of a cascade fired by the current request: either a monitoring device of the listing has just moved to `REJECTED`, in which case `suspension_reason` is set to `SENSOR_NON_COMPLIANT`, or an insurance layer visible to the listing has just moved from `AVAILABLE` to `EXHAUSTED` and left the listing's `coverage_posture` reading `UNPROTECTED`, in which case `suspension_reason` is set to `COVERAGE_EXHAUSTED`. A listing that never had an eligible layer in the first place is **not** suspended by this rule even though its `coverage_posture` also reads `UNPROTECTED` — the cascade needs an actual layer transition in the current request to fire. Suspension leaves reservations already sitting in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` untouched; it simply means no further confirmation on that listing can succeed. The listing's `version` increases by 1.

Monitoring devices are registered by the `Host` against a `property_id`, with a `device_type` of `NOISE_DECIBEL`, `OCCUPANCY_COUNTER`, `INDOOR_CAMERA` or `OUTDOOR_CAMERA`, a `placement_zone` of `EXTERIOR`, `COMMON_INTERIOR`, `BEDROOM` or `BATHROOM`, a `records_audio` boolean, and an optional `guest_disclosure_text` carrying the pre-booking notice shown to guests. Three privacy rules and one notice rule fire at registration, and they are evaluated in the order given here:

- **The absolute privacy floor.** A device whose `device_type` is `INDOOR_CAMERA` or `OUTDOOR_CAMERA` may never be placed in a `BEDROOM` or a `BATHROOM` zone. No jurisdiction flag unlocks this; the registration is refused with `ERR_SENSOR_PLACEMENT_PROHIBITED`.
- **Indoor cameras are jurisdictional.** An `INDOOR_CAMERA` in a `COMMON_INTERIOR` zone requires the bound jurisdiction's `indoor_camera_allowed` to be true, otherwise the registration is refused with `ERR_SENSOR_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITED`.
- **Audio capture is jurisdictional.** A device with `records_audio` true in any zone other than `EXTERIOR` requires the bound jurisdiction's `indoor_audio_allowed` to be true, otherwise the registration is refused with `ERR_SENSOR_AUDIO_PROHIBITED`.
- **Notice.** `guest_disclosure_text` must be present and non-empty whenever any one of three conditions holds: the jurisdiction has `sensor_notice_required` true, or the `device_type` is `INDOOR_CAMERA` or `OUTDOOR_CAMERA`, or `records_audio` is true. A jurisdiction that requires notice therefore demands disclosure copy even for a plain `NOISE_DECIBEL` sensor. A missing or empty disclosure in any of those cases is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_TEXT_REQUIRED`. The same rule is re-evaluated when the device is submitted for review.

A newly registered device starts in `DRAFT` with `reject_reason_code` and `reviewed_by_user_id` both null and its own `version` at 1. Registration immediately re-derives the listing's `sensor_compliance_state` — which reads `PENDING_REVIEW` unless a sibling device already sits in `REJECTED`, in which case the clause order above keeps the listing `NON_COMPLIANT` — and increases the listing's `version` by 1. Note carefully what this does and does not do: `PENDING_REVIEW` blocks new confirmations on the listing, but it never suspends an `ACTIVE` listing. Only a `REJECTED` device suspends.

Submitting a device for review is the `Host`'s move and serves two edges of a cyclic machine. From `DRAFT` it advances `disclosure_state` to `PENDING_REVIEW`; from `REJECTED` — after the host has remediated whatever the reviewer objected to — it returns `disclosure_state` to `PENDING_REVIEW` and clears `reject_reason_code` back to null. Either way the submission may carry a replacement `guest_disclosure_text`, and the notice rule above is enforced again at this point. A replacement supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string does replace the stored copy and is then tested by that rule like any other value; only omitting the field, or sending it as `null`, keeps the text already on the device. The listing's `sensor_compliance_state` is re-derived, reaching `PENDING_REVIEW` once no `REJECTED` device remains, and the device's `version` and the listing's `version` each increase by 1. A listing suspended for sensor non-compliance is **not** automatically reinstated by the resubmission: it stays `SUSPENDED` until the host explicitly reactivates it.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then rules on the disclosure, supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and, on rejection, a `reject_reason_code` drawn from `DISCLOSURE_UNCLEAR`, `PLACEMENT_DISPUTED` and `JURISDICTION_CHANGE`. A rejection submitted without a reason code is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either decision is admissible only from `PENDING_REVIEW`: whichever decision was submitted, a device still sitting in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and an already-`APPROVED` one with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. On the approving branch the three privacy rules are re-tested against the jurisdiction rule set as it stands at review time — a device that was compliant at registration but sits in a bedroom or bathroom zone, records audio outside the `EXTERIOR` zone without permission, or is an indoor camera where indoor cameras are not allowed, is refused at review with the same code it would have drawn at registration. A successful approval sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the officer's `X-User-ID`, moves `disclosure_state` to `APPROVED`, re-derives the listing's `sensor_compliance_state` — which becomes `COMPLIANT` once every sibling device of the listing is `APPROVED` — and increases the listing's `version` by 1.

Rejection is the interesting branch, because it is a three-link cascade. It sets `reject_reason_code` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, moves `disclosure_state` to `REJECTED`, and that new state re-derives the listing's `sensor_compliance_state` to `NON_COMPLIANT` because a rejected device now exists. The `NON_COMPLIANT` posture in turn drives the listing from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `SENSOR_NON_COMPLIANT`. Both the device's `version` and the listing's `version` increase by 1. Devices can be read individually or listed with optional `property_id` and `disclosure_state` filters.

### 3.2. Guest Onboarding, Identity Verification and the Risk Score

Accounts are opened by the operator, not by the parties themselves. Registering a guest takes a `full_name` — the legal name as printed on the identity document — an `email`, and a `country_code` giving the ISO-3166 alpha-2 country of residence as exactly two uppercase letters. Registering a host takes a `legal_name` and an `email`. In both cases the address must be unused: emails are compared case-insensitively, and with surrounding whitespace discounted on both sides of the comparison, across guest rows and, separately, across host rows, and a collision is refused with `ERR_EMAIL_ALREADY_REGISTERED`.

A freshly registered host begins with `payout_hold` false and with `settled_claim_count`, `denied_claim_count` and `total_recovered_cents` all at zero, and its `version` at 1. Hosts can be read individually or listed with an optional `payout_hold` filter, which is how an operator finds the hosts currently barred from filing.

A freshly registered guest begins with `payment_risk_signal` reading `CLEAN`, `chargeback_count` at zero, `verification_state` reading `UNVERIFIED`, every derived counter at zero, and `version` at 1. Its risk position is computed immediately from the baseline described below, which for a brand-new account yields a `risk_score` of 35 and a `risk_tier` of `ELEVATED`. Guests can be read individually, or listed with optional `risk_tier` and `verification_state` filters.

**Uploading identity documents.** A `Guest` submits verification artifacts one at a time, each carrying `guest_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` — a `doc_type` of `GOVERNMENT_ID`, `SELFIE` or `ADDRESS_PROOF`, an `issuing_country` given as exactly two uppercase letters, an opaque `document_ref` pointing at the stored artifact, and an `expires_on` instant. Nothing at upload compares `expires_on` to the logical clock: an artifact whose expiry is already behind the clock is accepted here and lands `PENDING` like any other, to be caught later by the review gate below or by the sweep's Phase 1. The row lands in `review_state` `PENDING` with `reject_reason_code` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null and its own `version` at 1; the guest's `verification_state` and `risk_score` are re-derived on the spot and the guest's `version` increases by 1. Documents can be read individually or listed with optional `guest_id` and `review_state` filters.

**Deriving the verification state.** A guest's `verification_state` is a function of that guest's documents alone, evaluated top down with the first matching clause winning. If `accepted_gov_id_count` is at least 1 *and* `accepted_doc_count` is at least 2, the state is `VERIFIED`. Otherwise, if the guest holds any document still in `PENDING`, the state is `PENDING_REVIEW`. Otherwise, if the guest holds any document in `EXPIRED`, the state is `VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Otherwise the state is `UNVERIFIED`. Here `accepted_doc_count` counts the guest's documents whose `review_state` is `ACCEPTED`, and `accepted_gov_id_count` counts the subset of those whose `doc_type` is also `GOVERNMENT_ID`. Documents in `REJECTED` contribute to neither count and satisfy no clause, so a guest holding nothing but rejected artifacts reads `UNVERIFIED`. Note the threshold shape: two accepted documents are needed in total and at least one of them must be a government ID, so two accepted selfies never reach `VERIFIED`.

**Reviewing a document** is a `ComplianceOfficer` action taking a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and, on rejection, a `reject_reason_code` drawn from `UNREADABLE`, `NAME_MISMATCH` and `SUSPECTED_FORGERY`; a rejection without a reason code is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either decision is admissible only from `PENDING`: whichever decision was submitted, an already-`ACCEPTED` document is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while a `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` one is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at the terminality step of §2.8. Acceptance additionally requires the document's `expires_on` to be strictly later than the logical clock — accepting an already-expired artifact is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`. Only the accepting branch consults `expires_on`, so rejecting a `PENDING` artifact whose expiry has passed succeeds normally.

Acceptance is the first link of the verification cascade, and each subsequent link fires because the previous field moved rather than because the request asked for it. It sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the officer's `X-User-ID` and moves `review_state` from `PENDING` to `ACCEPTED`. That raises the guest's `accepted_doc_count` by 1, and also `accepted_gov_id_count` by 1 when `doc_type` is `GOVERNMENT_ID`. The changed counters re-derive `verification_state`, which flips to `VERIFIED` once the two-document, one-government-ID threshold is met. The new `VERIFIED` value re-runs the risk score, which now earns the verification credit, so `risk_score` drops and `risk_tier` may re-band. Both the document's `version` and the guest's `version` increase by 1.

Rejection moves `review_state` from `PENDING` to `REJECTED`, records `reject_reason_code` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, and re-derives `verification_state` by the same clause order — which in the common case leaves the guest at `PENDING_REVIEW` while another `PENDING` document is still outstanding, and drops it to `UNVERIFIED` when none is. Both the document's `version` and the guest's `version` increase by 1, and the critical-tier ban cascade of §3.9 is tested on the recomputed live position exactly as on the accepting branch. Both `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal for a document: neither can be revisited, and a guest whose artifact was rejected must upload a fresh one.

**Payment signals** arrive from the processor and are recorded by a `PlatformAdmin`. The submission carries `payment_risk_signal`, one of `CLEAN`, `WATCH` or `ADVERSE`, and `chargeback_count`, an absolute lifetime count that must not be negative. Both values *overwrite* the stored ones rather than accumulating — a submission of three chargebacks after an earlier submission of five leaves the guest at three. Recording a signal re-runs the risk score, which may re-band `risk_tier` and, whenever the recomputed live tier reads `CRITICAL`, fire the automatic platform ban described in §3.9 — a resubmission of the identical signal and count still runs that test, because the ban cascade reads the resulting value rather than any movement. The guest's `version` increases by 1.

**The risk score itself** is a weighted sum over exactly seven guest aggregates: `verification_state`, `completed_stay_count`, `payment_risk_signal`, `chargeback_count`, `substantiated_incident_count`, `escalated_incident_count` and `approved_claim_count`. Nothing else moves it. It is assembled as follows, and the order of operations is binding:

1. Start from a baseline of 35 points.
2. Subtract 15 points when `verification_state` reads `VERIFIED`, and nothing otherwise.
3. Subtract 4 points for each completed stay in `completed_stay_count`, but never more than 20 points in total — the stay credit is floored at 20 points *before* it enters the sum, so a guest with eight completed stays earns the same 20-point credit as a guest with five.
4. Add the payment-signal penalty: nothing for `CLEAN`, 12 points for `WATCH`, 30 points for `ADVERSE`.
5. Add 5 points for each chargeback in `chargeback_count`.
6. Add an incident subtotal formed as 9 points for each substantiated incident plus 25 points for each escalated incident, then capped at 40 points. The cap applies to this subtotal only — it never touches the chargeback or claim terms.
7. Add 7 points for each approved claim in `approved_claim_count`.
8. Clamp the finished sum into the range 0 through 100 inclusive. The clamp is applied exactly once, to the total, never per term.

Every term is an integer, so no rounding occurs anywhere in this computation. An escalated incident is necessarily also a substantiated one, so it contributes both its 9-point substantiation weight and its 25-point escalation weight — 34 points together — before the 40-point cap is applied.

`risk_tier` is then read off `risk_score`, never off the pre-clamp sum, using bands inclusive at both ends: `LOW` from 0 through 24, `ELEVATED` from 25 through 49, `HIGH` from 50 through 74, and `CRITICAL` from 75 through 100. Worked through: a `VERIFIED` guest with no completed stays, a `CLEAN` signal and no adverse history scores 35 minus 15, or 20, landing in `LOW`. Let that guest's signal turn `ADVERSE` and the score becomes 35 minus 15 plus 30, or 50 — exactly the `HIGH` floor. Record five chargebacks on top and the score becomes 75, exactly the `CRITICAL` floor.

The score is read in two distinct modes, and the difference between them is observable. The **live** reading is what `risk_score` and `risk_tier` on the guest row always report; it reflects the aggregates as they stand at the moment of the read, is recomputed on every cascade that touches one of the seven inputs, and it is the only mode that fires the critical-tier ban cascade. The **booking snapshot** reading uses identical arithmetic but its result is frozen onto the reservation at creation as `risk_score_at_booking` and `risk_tier_at_booking`, is never recomputed for any reason, and fires no cascade. The divergence shows up on unchanged data: a stay booked while the guest read 20 in `LOW` still reports `risk_score_at_booking` of 20 and `risk_tier_at_booking` of `LOW` after an acknowledged incident has pushed the guest's live `risk_score` to 29 in `ELEVATED`, and any waiver fee on that booking stays priced at the `LOW` multiplier.

A dedicated computed read exposes the live position term by term, so a change can be attributed to a specific cascade. It returns `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `verification_state`, `completed_stay_count`, `substantiated_incident_count`, `escalated_incident_count`, `approved_claim_count`, `chargeback_count` and `active_blocklist_scope_count`. The guest row itself additionally exposes the two document tallies `accepted_doc_count` and `accepted_gov_id_count` defined above, which the risk-profile read deliberately omits because they feed `verification_state` rather than the score directly, plus `deposit_held_cents`, the sum of the deposit holds standing on that guest's reservations whose `deposit_state` reads `HELD`, and `active_blocklist_scope_count`, the number of blocklist entries against that guest whose `entry_state` is either `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` — the enforcing set, so filing a dispute does not reduce it.

### 3.3. Requesting a Stay: Calendar, Pricing and the Screening Decision

Booking is the single densest operation in this system. One request from a `Guest` pins a risk snapshot, prices the entire stay, runs a screening decision table, and — when that table returns a denial — cancels the booking before the response is ever assembled. The payload carries `guest_id`, which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`; `property_id`, naming the listing; `check_in` and `check_out` as calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, echoed back on the reservation as the client wrote them rather than re-rendered as instants; `guest_count`, the declared number of overnight guests, at least 1; `event_declared`, the guest's own declaration that a party or event will be held during the stay; and `expected_property_version`, the listing `version` the guest priced against, as required by §2.7.

Because a successful booking increases the listing's `version` by 1 — the listing's `open_reservation_count` has changed — a client booking twice in succession must re-read the listing between the two calls or the second `expected_property_version` will be stale.

**Gates, in order.** After the header, shape, existence, ownership and stale-version checks of §2.8 have passed, booking applies its own rules in this sequence:

1. **Standing bans.** If any blocklist entry whose `entry_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` covers this guest for this listing, the booking is refused with `ERR_GUEST_BLOCKLISTED`. Coverage is scope-dependent: a `PLATFORM` entry covers every listing, a `HOST` entry covers every listing of that host, and a `PROPERTY` entry covers the one listing it names.
2. **Date range.** `check_out` must be strictly later than `check_in`; equal or inverted dates are refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.
3. **Not in the past.** The derived `check_in_at` instant must be strictly later than the logical clock; a stay may not be booked into the past or onto the current instant, and a violation is refused with `ERR_CHECK_IN_IN_PAST`.
4. **Minimum stay.** The derived `nights` must be at least the bound jurisdiction's `min_stay_nights`; a shorter stay is refused with `ERR_MIN_STAY_NOT_MET`.
5. **Occupancy.** `guest_count` must not exceed the effective occupancy ceiling, which is the smaller of the listing's `max_occupancy` and the jurisdiction's `max_occupancy_cap`. Equality is accepted; only a strictly larger party is refused, with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
6. **Listing calendar.** No conflict with the listing's own live bookings, refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`.
7. **Guest calendar.** No conflict with the guest's own live bookings anywhere on the platform, refused with `ERR_GUEST_STAY_OVERLAP`.

Whether the listing is `ACTIVE` is deliberately *not* tested here. A request may be lodged against a `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` listing; the listing-state gate fires at confirmation instead.

**The two calendar clauses use different interval geometry, on purpose.** Both consider only reservations whose `stay_state` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_STAY`; rows already `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` never conflict with anything.

The listing clause is property-scoped and half-open. For each live booking of the same listing, the blocked span begins on that booking's `check_in` and runs up to but not including its `check_out` extended forward by the listing's `turnover_buffer_nights`. The requested span, likewise from `check_in` up to but not including `check_out`, must not intersect it. With `turnover_buffer_nights` at 0, a new booking beginning exactly on an existing booking's `check_out` date is therefore accepted; with `turnover_buffer_nights` at 1, that same booking conflicts.

The guest clause is guest-scoped, spans every listing on the platform, and is closed at both ends. For each live booking of the same guest anywhere, the requested span from `check_in` through `check_out` inclusive must not intersect that booking's span from its `check_in` through its `check_out` inclusive. The consequence is that a guest's own back-to-back stays at two different listings collide on the shared turnover date, even though the listing clause would have permitted each booking on its own.

**Pricing.** Once the calendar is clear, the charge is computed exactly once and never revisited; a later repricing of the listing has no effect on a booking already made. `nights` is the number of days from `check_in` up to but not including `check_out`. `accommodation_cents` is the listing's `nightly_rate_cents` multiplied by `nights`.

The protection product then splits the calculation. On a `WAIVER` listing, `deposit_hold_cents` is 0 and `waiver_fee_cents` is the listing's `waiver_nightly_fee_cents` multiplied by `nights` and again by a risk multiplier read from the pinned `risk_tier_at_booking` — 1.00 at `LOW`, 1.25 at `ELEVATED`, 1.60 at `HIGH` and 2.00 at `CRITICAL` — with the product rounded half up to a whole cent. The rounding acts on the full product, never per night. On a `DEPOSIT` listing the reverse holds: `waiver_fee_cents` is 0 and `deposit_hold_cents` is frozen at the listing's `deposit_amount_cents`.

`pre_fee_subtotal_cents` is `accommodation_cents` plus `waiver_fee_cents`, and it is the **only** input to the trust-and-safety fee tier selection. When `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` is below 100000 cents — that is, below 1,000.00 in major units — `trust_fee_pct` is 0.035; at 100000 cents or above it is 0.025. The larger stays get the cheaper rate. `trust_fee_cents` is then `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `trust_fee_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `total_charged_cents` is `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` plus `trust_fee_cents`. The refundable deposit hold is *not* part of `total_charged_cents`.

The tier selection is pinned to the pre-fee subtotal and is never re-selected from the total, even when adding the fee pushes the total across the break. A three-night stay at 30000 cents a night on a `WAIVER` listing with a 3000-cent nightly waiver fee, booked at tier `LOW`, produces `accommodation_cents` of 90000 and `waiver_fee_cents` of 9000, so `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` is 99000. Since 99000 is below the break, `trust_fee_pct` is 0.035, `trust_fee_cents` is 3465, and `total_charged_cents` is 102465 — above the break, but re-selecting 0.025 at that point would give 2562 and a total of 101562, which is wrong. The same listing booked at tier `ELEVATED` gives a waiver fee of 3000 multiplied by 3 and by 1.25, rounded to 11250, a `pre_fee_subtotal_cents` of 101250, a `trust_fee_pct` of 0.025, a `trust_fee_cents` of 2531, and a `total_charged_cents` of 103781.

Three derived instants are stamped at creation. `check_in_at` is `check_in` at 15:00 UTC, and it is the earliest instant the stay may be checked in. `check_out_at` is `check_out` at 11:00 UTC, and it is the earliest instant the stay may be completed. `claim_window_ends_at` is `check_out_at` plus 72 hours — three days — and it is the last instant a damage claim may be filed against the stay. A computed read exposes the whole pricing derivation independently of the stored row, returning `nights`, `accommodation_cents`, `waiver_fee_cents`, `pre_fee_subtotal_cents`, `trust_fee_pct`, `trust_fee_cents`, `total_charged_cents` and `deposit_hold_cents`.

**Screening.** With the risk tier and the charge both pinned, a decision table runs top down; the first matching clause wins and no later clause is consulted. Its verdict lands in `screening_state` and its justification in `screening_reason_code`.

1. `risk_tier_at_booking` is `CRITICAL` — the verdict is `DENIED` with reason `RISK_CRITICAL`.
2. `event_declared` is true and either the jurisdiction has `events_prohibited` true or the listing's `event_policy` is `EVENTS_PROHIBITED` — the verdict is `DENIED` with reason `EVENT_PROHIBITED`.
3. `event_declared` is true and `risk_tier_at_booking` is `HIGH` — the verdict is `DENIED` with reason `EVENT_RISK`.
4. `event_declared` is true, which by now means the tier is `LOW` or `ELEVATED` and events are permitted both locally and by house rule — the verdict is `MANUAL_REVIEW` with reason `EVENT_REVIEW`.
5. `risk_tier_at_booking` is `HIGH` with no event declared — the verdict is `MANUAL_REVIEW` with reason `HIGH_RISK_REVIEW`.
6. `risk_tier_at_booking` is `ELEVATED`, no event declared, and `guest_count` is greater than or equal to the listing's `max_occupancy` — the verdict is `MANUAL_REVIEW` with reason `ELEVATED_FULL_HOUSE`.
7. Anything else — the verdict is `CLEARED` with reason `LOW_RISK_AUTO_CLEAR`. In practice this is the `LOW` tier with no event declared, and the `ELEVATED` tier with no event declared and a party smaller than the listing's `max_occupancy`.

Two subtleties matter here. Clause 1 dominates clause 2, so a guest in the `CRITICAL` tier declaring an event in an event-banning jurisdiction is denied with `RISK_CRITICAL`, not with `EVENT_PROHIBITED`. And clause 6 compares `guest_count` against the listing's own `max_occupancy` using "greater than or equal", whereas the occupancy validation earlier in the request compares `guest_count` against the *effective* ceiling using strictly "greater than" — so a booking for exactly `max_occupancy` guests passes validation and is then routed to `MANUAL_REVIEW`.

A `CLEARED` verdict leaves `stay_state` at `REQUESTED`, awaiting the host's confirmation. A `MANUAL_REVIEW` verdict also leaves `stay_state` at `REQUESTED`, but it cannot advance until an adjudicator resolves the screening. A `DENIED` verdict fires a cross-dimension cascade inside the very same request: `stay_state` moves from `REQUESTED` to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason_code` set to `SCREENING_DENIED`, while `deposit_state` stays at `NONE` because no hold was ever placed. The reservation is returned to the client already cancelled. In every case the reservation's own `version` is 1, the listing's `open_reservation_count` is recomputed, and the listing's `version` increases by 1; the guest row is left alone, because pinning the snapshot only reads the guest and no rule re-versions it here.

**Resolving a manual review** is a `TrustAdjudicator` action carrying a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `DENY` and an optional free-text `note`. It is admissible only while `screening_state` is `MANUAL_REVIEW` and `stay_state` is still `REQUESTED`, in that order. The terminality gate of §2.8 reads the screening dimension alone here, so a booking still parked in `MANUAL_REVIEW` whose stay has meanwhile been cancelled is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Clearing sets `screening_reason_code` to `ADJUDICATOR_CLEARED`, records `screening_decided_by_user_id` as the adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, and moves `screening_state` from `MANUAL_REVIEW` to `CLEARED`, releasing the booking for host confirmation. Denying sets `screening_reason_code` to `ADJUDICATOR_DENIED`, records `screening_decided_by_user_id`, moves `screening_state` from `MANUAL_REVIEW` to `DENIED`, and cascades across dimensions exactly as an automatic denial does — `stay_state` moves from `REQUESTED` to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason_code` `SCREENING_DENIED`. Either outcome increases the reservation's `version` by 1, and neither touches any other row: unlike a voluntary cancellation, this denial leaves the listing un-re-versioned even though its `open_reservation_count` falls, and the guest is untouched because no deposit hold had been placed. Because both `CLEARED` and `DENIED` are terminal for the screening dimension, a second screening decision on the same reservation is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Reservations can be read individually — the read exposes all three state dimensions, the pinned charge and the pinned risk snapshot — or listed with optional `guest_id`, `property_id`, `stay_state`, `screening_state` and `deposit_state` filters.

### 3.4. Running the Stay: Confirmation Gates, Deposit Holds and Completion

Confirmation is where most of the trust-and-safety machinery actually bites, because it is the last moment before a guest is committed to a property. A `Host` confirming a booking must own the listing, and the request carries no body at all. Five preconditions are evaluated in this order:

1. `stay_state` must be `REQUESTED`.
2. `screening_state` must be `CLEARED`. A booking still parked in `MANUAL_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; so is one already `DENIED`.
3. No blocklist entry whose `entry_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` may cover this guest for this listing at property, host or platform scope — a ban that landed after the booking was requested is caught here, and the confirmation is refused with `ERR_GUEST_BLOCKLISTED`.
4. The listing's `sensor_compliance_state` must read `COMPLIANT`. A listing with any device sitting in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW` or `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_SENSOR_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED` — this is the rule that makes an unreviewed sensor disclosure commercially expensive without taking the listing down.
5. The listing's `listing_state` must be `ACTIVE`. A `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` listing is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_LISTED`.

A successful confirmation advances `stay_state` from `REQUESTED` to `CONFIRMED` and increases the reservation's `version` by 1. On a listing whose `protection_product` is `DEPOSIT`, it additionally places the security hold: `deposit_state` moves from `NONE` to `HELD`, the guest's `deposit_held_cents` rises by the reservation's `deposit_hold_cents`, and the guest's `version` increases by 1. On a `WAIVER` listing nothing happens to the deposit dimension at all — `deposit_state` remains `NONE` for the whole life of that booking, and the guest's `version` is untouched by the confirmation.

**Check-in** advances `stay_state` from `CONFIRMED` to `IN_STAY` and is a `Host` action with an empty body. It requires that the logical clock has reached or passed `check_in_at`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It also re-tests that the listing is still `ACTIVE`, refusing with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_LISTED` if the listing was suspended between confirmation and arrival. The reservation's `version` increases by 1.

**Completion** advances `stay_state` from `IN_STAY` to `COMPLETED`, again a `Host` action with an empty body, and requires that the logical clock has reached or passed `check_out_at`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Unlike confirmation and check-in it never re-reads the listing state, so a stay whose listing was suspended mid-stay still completes, and the listing is not re-versioned by the completion even though its `open_reservation_count` falls. Completion has consequences beyond the reservation. The guest's `completed_stay_count` rises by 1, which re-runs the risk score and earns another 4 points of stay credit — subject to the 20-point floor described in §3.2 — so `risk_score` drops and `risk_tier` may re-band downward. The guest's `version` and the reservation's `version` each increase by 1. Completion is also what opens the damage-claim window, which runs from this moment until `claim_window_ends_at`.

**Cancellation** is available to either the acting `Guest` of the booking or the `Host` that owns the listing, with an empty body; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It serves two edges, and the difference between them is entirely about money.

Cancelling from `REQUESTED` moves `stay_state` to `CANCELLED`, sets `cancel_reason_code` to `GUEST_WITHDREW` or `HOST_WITHDREW` according to the acting role, and leaves the deposit dimension untouched — `deposit_state` is still `NONE` because no hold had been placed. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` moves `stay_state` to `CANCELLED` and sets `cancel_reason_code` the same way, but it must also unwind the hold: when `deposit_state` reads `HELD`, it moves to `RELEASED`, `deposit_refunded_cents` is set to the full `deposit_hold_cents`, the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by that same `deposit_hold_cents`, and the guest's `version` increases by 1. This is a compensating action in the strict sense — the guest's `deposit_held_cents` returns to exactly the value it had before the confirmation. Either edge recomputes the listing's `open_reservation_count`, increases the listing's `version` by 1, and increases the reservation's `version` by 1. Because `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for the stay dimension, cancelling an already-completed or already-cancelled booking is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and cancelling a stay already `IN_STAY` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

One further cancellation exists that no client requests. When a newly created platform-scope ban covers the guest, every reservation of that guest whose `stay_state` is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` **and** whose `check_in_at` is still in the future is cancelled by the system with `cancel_reason_code` set to `BLOCKLISTED`; a standing hold is released exactly as on a voluntary cancellation, with `deposit_refunded_cents` set to the full `deposit_hold_cents` and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` reduced accordingly. Each affected reservation's `version` increases by 1, but the listing behind it is not re-versioned by this system cancellation the way a voluntary one is — `open_reservation_count` is a derived aggregate, and no cascade link is declared from the ban to the listing row. A stay already `IN_STAY` is left running — the platform does not evict a guest mid-stay — and neither does a stay whose `check_in_at` has already passed.

**The deposit dimension end to end.** For a `DEPOSIT` listing, `deposit_state` begins at `NONE` and moves to `HELD` at confirmation, provided `deposit_hold_cents` is greater than zero. From `HELD` there are two exits. It moves to `RELEASED` when the booking is cancelled, or when the sweep finds the claim window closed with no live claim standing against the stay; either way `deposit_refunded_cents` becomes the full `deposit_hold_cents` and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by that amount. Alternatively it moves to `APPLIED` when a damage claim on the stay is approved with a non-zero deposit slice: `deposit_applied_cents` on the reservation is set to that slice, `deposit_refunded_cents` stays at 0 until settlement, and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the whole `deposit_hold_cents` because the hold is no longer standing. From `APPLIED` there are likewise two exits. If the approval is overturned on appeal, `deposit_state` returns to `HELD`, `deposit_applied_cents` resets to 0, and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` rises again by the full `deposit_hold_cents`, restoring the conserved quantity to its pre-approval value; the `HELD` to `APPLIED` to `HELD` loop is a genuine cycle on this dimension. If instead the approval survives and the sweep settles the claim, `deposit_state` moves to `RELEASED` with `deposit_refunded_cents` set to `deposit_hold_cents` minus `deposit_applied_cents` — the guest gets back whatever the damage did not consume. `RELEASED` is terminal.

### 3.5. Incident Intake, Triage and Escalation

Trouble reaches the platform through three doors, and the `source` field records which one: `SENSOR` for a device reading, `NEIGHBOR_COMPLAINT` for a report from the neighbourhood, and `HOST_REPORT` for the host's own account. A `Host`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` may file an incident. The payload names the `reservation_id` the incident occurred during, an `incident_type` of `NOISE_VIOLATION`, `OVER_OCCUPANCY`, `UNAUTHORIZED_EVENT`, `PROPERTY_DAMAGE` or `SAFETY_THREAT`, a reporter-assessed `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, the `source`, a `detected_at` instant recording when the condition was observed, and a free-text `description` for the adjudicators. Three optional measurements ride along: `sensor_device_id`, naming the monitoring device that produced the reading and required whenever `source` is `SENSOR`; `decibel_reading`, the measured decibel level carried by noise reports; `observed_occupancy`, the head count carried by over-occupancy reports; and `complainant_ref`, the neighbourhood complaint tracking reference recorded for `NEIGHBOR_COMPLAINT` reports. A `SENSOR` report filed without a `sensor_device_id` is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a `sensor_device_id` that resolves to nothing is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` after the reservation has resolved. Neither measurement carries a bound of its own at intake — an implausible `decibel_reading` or `observed_occupancy` is stored as supplied and simply fails the acknowledgement gate below. An acting `Host` must own the listing behind the named reservation, otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `ComplianceOfficer` and a `PlatformAdmin` file against any stay with no ownership comparison at all.

A new incident lands in `incident_state` `OPEN` with `substantiated` false, `escalated` false, `law_enforcement_ref` null, `dismiss_reason_code` null, `response_sla_breached` false and `version` 1. One derived field is stamped at intake: `response_due_at` is `detected_at` plus 24 hours when `source` is `NEIGHBOR_COMPLAINT`, and null for every other source. Neighbours, in other words, get a one-day response commitment that sensors and hosts do not. If the incident is still `OPEN` when the sweep finds the logical clock past `response_due_at`, the sweep sets `response_sla_breached` to true. Intake writes the incident row and nothing else: it fires no cascade, so the reservation, its guest and the listing are all left untouched and un-re-versioned — the guest's `substantiated_incident_count` and `risk_score` only move once an adjudicator acts. Incidents can be read individually or listed with optional `reservation_id`, `sensor_device_id`, `incident_state` and `source` filters.

Triage forks. From `OPEN`, a `TrustAdjudicator` either **acknowledges** the incident — the substantiating branch — or **dismisses** it, the exonerating branch, and the two branches apply disjoint side effects.

**Acknowledging** takes an empty body but two measurement gates stand in front of it, each tied to a specific `incident_type`. A `NOISE_VIOLATION` may be acknowledged only when `decibel_reading` is present and is greater than or equal to the `noise_threshold_db` of the jurisdiction bound to the reservation's listing; a null reading or one below the threshold is refused with `ERR_NOISE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. An `OVER_OCCUPANCY` may be acknowledged only when `observed_occupancy` is present and is strictly greater than the effective occupancy ceiling — again the smaller of the listing's `max_occupancy` and the jurisdiction's `max_occupancy_cap`; a null count, or one merely equal to the ceiling, is refused with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_WITHIN_LIMIT`. Incidents of the other three types carry no measurement gate.

Acknowledgement moves `incident_state` from `OPEN` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and sets `substantiated` to true, and from there a four-link cascade unrolls. The guest's `substantiated_incident_count` — which counts that guest's incidents whose `incident_state` is `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ESCALATED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT` or `CLOSED` — rises by 1. The changed count re-runs the risk score, adding 9 points for the new substantiation, subject to the joint 40-point cap it shares with escalations. If the recomputed live `risk_tier` reads `CRITICAL` — the test is on the resulting value rather than on any movement, exactly as §3.9 sets out, and it is skipped only when an enforcing platform entry for that guest already stands — the platform ban described in §3.9 is created automatically. That ban in turn cancels every future-dated booking of the guest and releases the holds standing on them. Both the incident's `version` and the guest's `version` increase by 1.

**Dismissing** takes a `dismiss_reason_code` of `NO_EVIDENCE`, `DUPLICATE_REPORT` or `GUEST_EXONERATED`. It is admissible only from `OPEN`, and only while no damage claim referencing this incident sits in `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED`; an incident still underpinning a live claim is protected, and the dismissal is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_HAS_LIVE_CLAIM`. Dismissal records the supplied `dismiss_reason_code`, moves `incident_state` from `OPEN` to `DISMISSED`, and leaves `substantiated` at false. This is the deliberate divergence from the acknowledging branch: the guest's `substantiated_incident_count` does not move, so the guest's `risk_score` does not move either, and no cascade fires. `response_sla_breached` is frozen at whatever value it currently holds and the sweep stops considering the incident. Only the incident's `version` increases by 1.

**Escalating to law enforcement** is available from `ACKNOWLEDGED` only, and it carries a `law_enforcement_ref` holding the police report or case reference. It is warranted only when `severity` is `HIGH`, or when `incident_type` is `SAFETY_THREAT` or `UNAUTHORIZED_EVENT`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_WARRANTED`, as is an escalation whose `law_enforcement_ref` is missing, empty or nothing but whitespace — the warrant test is read first, so an unwarranted escalation that also omits the reference reports the same code either way. Escalation stores the reference, moves `incident_state` to `ESCALATED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, and sets `escalated` to true **permanently** — that flag is never unset. The guest's `escalated_incident_count`, which counts incidents on that guest's stays whose `escalated` flag is true, rises by 1, and the changed count re-runs the risk score adding 25 points for the escalation. The recomputed live position is then tested for the critical-tier platform ban of §3.9 exactly as on acknowledgement. Both the incident's `version` and the guest's `version` increase by 1.

**Closing** an incident files it away and is admissible from either `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `ESCALATED_LAW_ENFORCEMENT`. It takes an empty body and, like dismissal, is blocked while a damage claim referencing the incident sits in `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED`, with the same `ERR_INCIDENT_HAS_LIVE_CLAIM` rejection. Closing from either predecessor moves `incident_state` to `CLOSED` and increases the incident's `version` by 1, and it deliberately undoes nothing. Because `CLOSED` remains inside the set of states the substantiation count reads, the 9-point substantiation penalty survives closure; and because `escalated` is never unset, the 25-point escalation penalty survives it too. Both `CLOSED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal, so any further triage action on such an incident is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.6. Damage Claims: Intake, Evidence Rounds and First-Instance Adjudication

A damage claim is a `Host` asserting a monetary loss against a stay that has finished. The payload names the `reservation_id`, an optional `incident_id` linking the triaged incident that documents the damage, a `claimed_amount_cents` greater than zero, a `description` giving the host's narrative, and `expected_reservation_version` as required by §2.7. The acting `X-User-ID` must own the listing behind the reservation, otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Intake then applies five further gates, in this order:

1. **The stay must be finished.** A claim may only be filed against a reservation whose `stay_state` is `COMPLETED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED`.
2. **The host must not be under a payout hold.** When the host's `payout_hold` flag reads true — which happens while a guest's appeal against one of that host's approved claims is pending — no further claim may be filed, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_HOST_PAYOUT_HELD`.
3. **The window must still be open.** The logical clock must be at or before the reservation's `claim_window_ends_at`; a late filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
4. **A linked incident must be usable.** When `incident_id` is supplied, that incident must belong to the same reservation, and no other damage claim outside `CLOSED_DENIED` may already reference it. A cross-reservation link or a second live claim on the same incident is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_FOR_INCIDENT`.
5. **The ceiling must hold.** `claimed_amount_cents` must not exceed the listing's `claim_ceiling_cents`; a larger ask is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. Being a fiscal rule, this is evaluated last, so a claim that is both over the ceiling and outside its window reports the window error.

A new claim lands in `claim_state` `AWAITING_EVIDENCE` with `evidence_round` at 1 and `evidence_count` at 0. Its `host_id` is *not* supplied by the client — it is copied at creation from the `host_id` of the reservation's listing. Every temporal and monetary field starts empty: `submitted_for_review_at`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_reason_code`, `appealed_by_role`, `appeal_decided_by_user_id` and `appeal_decision` are all null, `sla_lapsed` is false, `appeal_round` is 0, and `approved_amount_cents`, `projected_recovery_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents` are all 0. Creation recomputes the listing's `open_claim_count`, increases the reservation's `version` by 1, and sets the claim's own `version` to 1; because that counter is derived on read, the listing itself is neither re-stamped nor re-versioned by the filing, and neither is the guest or the host. Claims can be read individually or listed with optional `reservation_id`, `incident_id`, `host_id` and `claim_state` filters.

**Evidence** is uploaded one artifact at a time, and both the `Host` and the `Guest` may contribute — the guest's ability to file counter-evidence is what makes the process defensible. Each upload names the `claim_id`, an `evidence_type` of `PHOTO`, `RECEIPT`, `REPAIR_ESTIMATE`, `POLICE_REPORT` or `SENSOR_LOG`, a `captured_at` instant, an opaque `file_ref`, and — for money-bearing artifacts only — an `amount_cents`. The money rule is strict in both directions: `amount_cents` must be present and greater than zero when `evidence_type` is `RECEIPT` or `REPAIR_ESTIMATE`, and must be null for every other type. Either violation is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_AMOUNT_REQUIRED`. The server stamps three fields the client cannot set: `uploaded_by_role`, taken from `X-User-Role` and therefore reading either `Host` or `Guest`; `uploaded_by_user_id`, taken from `X-User-ID`; and `evidence_round`, copied from the claim's `evidence_round` at the moment of upload. Each upload recomputes the claim's `evidence_count`, which is the running total of artifacts attached to the claim across every round — reopening a round never resets it; the count is derived on read, so an upload leaves the claim's own `updated_at` and `version` untouched and writes no other row. Neither uploader is tied to the claim and no state gate stands in front of the upload: the request compares no identities, so any authorised `Host` or `Guest` may attach an artifact to any claim, at any point in that claim's life including while it is `UNDER_REVIEW` or already terminal. Evidence artifacts are immutable — they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`, and there is no way to edit or delete one. They can be read individually, or listed with optional `claim_id`, `evidence_type` and `evidence_round` filters, which is the only discovery channel for them.

**Submitting for review** closes an evidence round and asks for adjudication. It is a `Host` action with an empty body, admissible only from `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, and only by the host whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `host_id`. Before the state moves, the evidence set is tested against a requirement schedule derived from the claim itself:

- At least 2 artifacts of type `PHOTO` are always required.
- When `claimed_amount_cents` is 50000 cents or more, at least 1 `RECEIPT` is also required. The comparison is inclusive, so a claim of exactly 50000 needs a receipt while a claim of 49999 does not.
- When `claimed_amount_cents` is 250000 cents or more, at least 1 `POLICE_REPORT` is also required, again inclusively.
- When `incident_id` is populated and that incident's `source` is `SENSOR`, at least 1 `SENSOR_LOG` is also required.
- Finally, when `evidence_round` is greater than 1, at least one artifact must carry an `evidence_round` equal to the claim's current `evidence_round`. A reopened round demands genuinely new material; resubmitting the identical file set that was already rejected does not satisfy it.

Every artifact of the claim counts toward the per-type minimums regardless of which round stamped it; only the freshness clause above looks at `evidence_round` at all. Any failure is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` and nothing about the claim changes.

A successful submission moves `claim_state` from `AWAITING_EVIDENCE` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, sets `submitted_for_review_at` to the current logical clock reading, and thereby recomputes `adjudication_due_at` as 120 hours — five days — after that instant. It also pins `projected_recovery_cents` by running the settlement waterfall of §3.7 in its **provisional** mode, whose gross loss is the smaller of `claimed_amount_cents` and the listing's `claim_ceiling_cents` — the host's full ask rather than any adjudicated figure. The provisional run writes no allocation rows, does not touch any layer's `consumed_cents`, and does not move `deposit_state`; it sets `projected_recovery_cents` and nothing else. Because it reads layer capacity at submission time, it can promise capacity that a competing claim consumes before the decision lands. The claim's `version` increases by 1.

**Asking for more evidence** sends the claim back and is a `TrustAdjudicator` action carrying an optional `note`. It is admissible only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and only while `evidence_round` is below 2; a second attempt to reopen is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. It moves `claim_state` from `UNDER_REVIEW` back to `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, increases `evidence_round` by 1 so that subsequent artifacts are stamped with the new round, and clears `submitted_for_review_at` back to null — which makes `adjudication_due_at` null again and takes the claim out of the service-level sweep entirely. The claim's `version` increases by 1. The `AWAITING_EVIDENCE` and `UNDER_REVIEW` pair therefore forms a loop that may be traversed at most twice in total, since `evidence_round` may reach 2 but not 3.

**Deciding at first instance** is a `TrustAdjudicator` action carrying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, a `decision_reason_code`, and — on approval — an `approved_amount_cents`. The reason code is drawn from `DAMAGE_CONFIRMED`, `PARTIAL_DEPRECIATION`, `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`, `NORMAL_WEAR`, `FRAUD_SUSPECTED` and `SLA_LAPSED`, the last of which is reserved for the sweep. Both branches are admissible only from `UNDER_REVIEW`.

On the approving branch, `approved_amount_cents` must be greater than zero and no greater than `claimed_amount_cents`; an absent, zero or excessive amount is refused with `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_INVALID`. The reservation's `stay_state` must still read `COMPLETED`. Approval sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock, `decided_by_user_id` to the adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, plus `decision_reason_code` and `approved_amount_cents`, moves `claim_state` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`, and recomputes `appeal_due_at` as 168 hours — seven days — after `decided_at`. It then runs the settlement waterfall of §3.7 in its **final** mode, with all of that mode's real side effects. The claim's `version` increases by 1.

On the denying branch, `approved_amount_cents` must be absent or zero; a non-zero amount accompanying a denial is refused with the same `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_INVALID`. Denial sets `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason_code` and recomputes `appeal_due_at` identically, moves `claim_state` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DENIED`, and writes no money at all: `approved_amount_cents`, `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents` all stay at 0, no allocation line is written, no layer capacity is consumed, and the reservation's `deposit_state` is untouched. The claim's `version` increases by 1.

A computed read reconciles the money on a claim from the outside, returning `approved_amount_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `allocation_count`, `allocated_total_cents`, `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents` — enough for a reader to verify the conservation rule of §2.6 without inspecting the individual allocation lines. `allocation_count` and `allocated_total_cents` see only the live lines: a line whose `voided` flag has been set by an overturned approval is excluded from the count and from the sum, so a fully reversed claim reports an `allocation_count` of 0 even though the voided lines remain individually readable.

### 3.7. The Insurance Stack and the Settlement Waterfall

Insurance in this product is layered rather than monolithic, and each rung is published separately by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin`. A rung declares its `source` — `DAMAGE_WAIVER` for the waiver pool funded by guest fees, `PLATFORM_PROTECTION` for the platform's own cover, `HOST_POLICY` for the host's insurance, and `OWNER_COVERAGE` for the building owner's — a `layer_order` of at least 1 giving its position in the waterfall, a `limit_cents` greater than zero capping the gross loss it may ever absorb across all claims, a `deductible_cents` of at least zero that it withholds before applying its percentage, a `coverage_pct` greater than 0 and no greater than 1 giving the fraction of the post-deductible gross it actually pays, and a `scope` of either `PLATFORM_GLOBAL` or `PROPERTY`.

Two coherence rules police publication. A `PROPERTY`-scoped layer must name a `property_id` and a `PLATFORM_GLOBAL`-scoped one must leave `property_id` null; furthermore a `DAMAGE_WAIVER` layer must be `PROPERTY`-scoped and attached to a listing whose `protection_product` is `WAIVER`, since there is no waiver pool on a deposit listing. Any violation is refused with `ERR_LAYER_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Separately, `layer_order` must be unique among the layers visible to the same target: unique across the `PLATFORM_GLOBAL` layers as a set, and unique within each individual listing's own layers. A duplicate position is refused with `ERR_LAYER_ORDER_CONFLICT`.

A published layer starts in `layer_state` `AVAILABLE` with `consumed_cents` at 0 and `version` 1, and its publication recomputes `coverage_posture` for every listing it becomes visible to. Because that posture is derived on read rather than stored, this recomputation — and every later re-derivation of it described below — leaves the affected listings' `updated_at` and `version` untouched on its own; only a listing actually driven into `SUSPENDED` is re-versioned, and the only field this recomputation moves is `coverage_posture`. `consumed_cents` is always the sum of `gross_cents` over the allocation lines charged against that layer whose `voided` flag is false. The layer sits in `AVAILABLE` while `consumed_cents` is below `limit_cents` and flips to `EXHAUSTED` the moment `consumed_cents` equals `limit_cents`; the flip re-derives `coverage_posture` for every listing the layer is visible to and increases the layer's `version` by 1. The reverse edge exists too: when allocation lines are voided by an overturned approval and `consumed_cents` falls back below `limit_cents`, the layer returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `AVAILABLE`, `coverage_posture` is re-derived again, and the layer's `version` increases by 1 — but a listing that was suspended for coverage exhaustion is **not** automatically reinstated by the layer's recovery; its host must reactivate it explicitly. Layers can be read individually or listed with optional `property_id`, `scope`, `source` and `layer_state` filters.

**The waterfall itself** runs in four steps whenever a claim's recovery is computed, and the order is not negotiable.

*Step one — the guest's deposit absorbs first.* When the reservation's `deposit_state` reads `HELD`, the deposit slice is the smaller of the gross loss still unabsorbed and the reservation's `deposit_hold_cents` reduced by its `deposit_applied_cents`. When `deposit_state` reads anything else, the deposit slice is 0. That slice is subtracted from the gross loss still unabsorbed.

*Step two — determine the eligible layers.* A layer is eligible when its `layer_state` reads `AVAILABLE`, when its `scope` is `PLATFORM_GLOBAL` or its `property_id` names the reservation's listing, and when it is not a `DAMAGE_WAIVER` layer facing a listing whose `protection_product` is `DEPOSIT` — a waiver pool is skipped entirely on a deposit listing. The eligible layers are ordered by `layer_order` ascending and, where two layers share a position across different visibility targets, by `created_at` ascending; because the logical clock routinely stands still between two publications, layers that tie on both keys fall back to the order in which they were written, oldest write first. They are never ordered by `id`.

*Step three — fill each layer before touching the next.* Walking the ordered list, with a zero-based `sequence_index` starting at 0: if no gross loss remains unabsorbed, stop. Compute the layer's remaining capacity as its `limit_cents` minus its `consumed_cents`; if that capacity is zero or below, skip the layer without consuming a `sequence_index`. Otherwise the gross charged to this layer is the smaller of the loss still unabsorbed and the layer's remaining capacity. From that charged gross, subtract the layer's `deductible_cents`, treating any negative result as zero, and multiply what remains by the layer's `coverage_pct`, rounding half up to a whole cent. That figure is the cash this line pays the host. One allocation line is written per charged layer, recording `coverage_layer_id`, `sequence_index`, `gross_cents` set to the charged gross, `deductible_cents` copied from the layer as it stands at settlement time, `allocated_cents` set to the rounded payment, and `voided` false. The loss still unabsorbed then falls by the charged gross and `sequence_index` advances by 1.

*Step four — close the books.* `recovered_cents` is the deposit slice plus the sum of `allocated_cents` across the lines written. `uncovered_cents` is whatever gross loss remains unabsorbed after the walk. `host_absorbed_cents` is the gross loss the run started from, minus `recovered_cents`. That last identity is what enforces the conservation rule of §2.6: `recovered_cents` plus `host_absorbed_cents` equals the starting gross exactly, always, with deductibles, co-insurance shortfalls and the uncovered tail all landing inside the host's share.

The rounding point deserves emphasis, because getting it wrong produces answers that are close but not equal. Rounding half up is applied **once per allocation line**, to that line's post-deductible gross multiplied by that line's `coverage_pct` — never to a net figure summed across layers. Consider an approval of 300000 cents with no deposit standing, against a first layer with `limit_cents` 130000, `deductible_cents` 24990 and `coverage_pct` 0.85, and a second layer with `limit_cents` 400000, `deductible_cents` 14990 and `coverage_pct` 0.65. The first line charges 130000 gross and pays 89259; the second charges the remaining 170000 and pays 100757; `recovered_cents` is 190016. Rounding the aggregate of 190015.5 in a single step would have produced 190015. The per-line figure is authoritative.

When no layer is eligible at all, the walk's body never executes: no allocation line is written, `recovered_cents` equals the deposit slice alone, and `uncovered_cents` equals the entire post-deposit remainder.

**The two modes of the waterfall differ in their gross and in their consequences.** The provisional mode, run at each submission for review, takes as its gross the smaller of `claimed_amount_cents` and the listing's `claim_ceiling_cents`, and its only output is `projected_recovery_cents`. The final mode, run at an approving decision, takes as its gross the `approved_amount_cents` and has real effects: it emits the allocation lines, raises each charged layer's `consumed_cents` — possibly to `EXHAUSTED` — moves the reservation's `deposit_state` from `HELD` to `APPLIED` when the deposit slice is greater than zero, and writes `deposit_applied_cents`, `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents` onto the claim. The claim's `deposit_applied_cents` mirrors the reservation's.

The divergence between the two modes is observable on identical claim data. A claim of 300000 against a single platform layer with `limit_cents` 250000, `deductible_cents` 20000 and `coverage_pct` 0.75, backed by a 50000-cent deposit, projects `projected_recovery_cents` of 222500 with no uncovered tail at submission. If a competing claim consumes 100000 of that layer's capacity before the decision lands, the same claim approved at 300000 recovers only 147500 and leaves `uncovered_cents` of 100000 — a 75000-cent swing on a claim whose own data never changed.

Approving a claim therefore fires a five-link cascade: the allocation lines are written, each charged layer's `consumed_cents` rises — a charged layer that does not reach its limit is not itself re-versioned, because `consumed_cents` is summed from the allocation lines rather than stored on the layer — a layer reaching its `limit_cents` flips to `EXHAUSTED`, that flip re-derives `coverage_posture` across every listing the exhausted layer was visible to, and each of those listings that is still `ACTIVE` and now reads `UNPROTECTED` is suspended with `suspension_reason` `COVERAGE_EXHAUSTED`. For a `PLATFORM_GLOBAL` layer that reach is every listing on the platform, not merely the one behind the claim. In parallel, when the deposit slice is non-zero the reservation's `deposit_state` moves from `HELD` to `APPLIED`, the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the reservation's `deposit_hold_cents`, and both the reservation's `version` and the guest's `version` increase by 1. Approval also raises the guest's `approved_claim_count` by 1, which re-runs the risk score adding 7 points for the approved claim and may re-band `risk_tier`.

Allocation lines are never created by a client request — only the final-mode waterfall emits them — so they are read-only artifacts. Each carries `claim_id`, `coverage_layer_id`, `sequence_index`, `gross_cents`, `deductible_cents`, `allocated_cents` and `voided`, plus `id` and `created_at`; like evidence artifacts they are immutable and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`. They can be read individually or listed with optional `claim_id`, `coverage_layer_id` and `voided` filters, which is the only discovery channel for them and the way the executed `sequence_index` ordering becomes observable.

### 3.8. Appeals and the Exact Reversal of Money

Exactly one appeal is admissible per claim, and only the party the decision went against may file it. The rule is symmetric: against an `APPROVED` claim the appellant must be the `Guest` of the reservation, since that guest is the charged party; against a `DENIED` claim the appellant must be the `Host` that filed it, since that host is the refused party. A guest attempting to appeal a denial, or a host attempting to appeal an approval, is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROLE_MISMATCH`. This action alone defers the ownership comparison of §2.8 step 6: the claim's terminality, its decided `claim_state` and the appellant-role test are all read first — so a wrong-party filing against a `SETTLED` claim reports `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and a host filing against another host's approval reports `ERR_APPEAL_ROLE_MISMATCH`, each ahead of `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — while the two gates below are read after ownership. The filing carries an optional `note` and is subject to two further gates: `appeal_round` must still be 0, otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED`, and the logical clock must be at or before `appeal_due_at`, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because `SETTLED` and `CLOSED_DENIED` are terminal, an appeal against an already-settled or already-closed claim is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Both filing branches move `claim_state` to `APPEALED`, set `appeal_round` to 1, record `appealed_by_role` as the appellant's role name — reading either `Guest` or `Host` — and increase the claim's `version` by 1 — but they differ in one respect. When the guest appeals an approval, the host's `payout_hold` is set to true and the host's `version` increases by 1, which bars that host from filing any further damage claim while the appeal is pending. When the host appeals a denial, `payout_hold` is **not** set and the host's `version` does not move, because nothing has been paid out that would need holding. That asymmetry is deliberate.

Ruling on the appeal is a `TrustAdjudicator` action carrying a `decision` of `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED`, an optional `decision_reason_code`, and — where the outcome creates an approval — an `approved_amount_cents`. It is bound by the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `decided_by_user_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every branch sets `appeal_decision` and records `appeal_decided_by_user_id` as the acting adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, and every branch increases the claim's `version` by 1. A `decision_reason_code` supplied here overwrites the code the first instance recorded; omitted or sent as `null`, it leaves the stored `decision_reason_code` exactly as it stands on every branch. Beyond that, the four combinations of appellant and outcome behave differently, and each is specified here in full.

**Guest appealed, and the appeal is `OVERTURNED`.** `claim_state` moves from `APPEALED` to `DENIED` and `appeal_decision` is set to `OVERTURNED`. Every allocation line of this claim has its `voided` flag set to true, so each charged layer's `consumed_cents` falls back to its pre-approval value and any layer that had flipped to `EXHAUSTED` returns to `AVAILABLE`. A layer returning to `AVAILABLE` restores `coverage_posture` on the listings it is visible to, but a listing that was suspended by that exhaustion cascade stays `SUSPENDED` until its host reactivates it. When the reservation's `deposit_state` reads `APPLIED` it returns to `HELD` and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` is restored exactly to its pre-approval value. On the claim, `approved_amount_cents`, `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents`, `host_absorbed_cents` and `deposit_applied_cents` are all reset to 0. The guest's `approved_claim_count` falls by 1 — floored at 0, so the counter can never be driven negative — which re-runs the risk score and removes the 7-point claim penalty; because the score is falling, no critical-tier ban cascade is tested on this branch. The host's `payout_hold` is cleared back to false, and the host's `version` increases by 1. The guest's `version` increases by 1 on this branch in every case, while the reservation's `version` increases by 1 only where its deposit actually returned from `APPLIED` to `HELD`.

**Guest appealed, and the appeal is `UPHELD`.** `claim_state` returns from `APPEALED` to `APPROVED` and `appeal_decision` is set to `UPHELD`. Every monetary field and every allocation line is left exactly as the first-instance approval wrote it — no line is voided, no layer capacity is restored, the deposit stays `APPLIED`, and the guest's `approved_claim_count` and `risk_score` are unchanged. The host's `payout_hold` is cleared back to false and the host's `version` increases by 1, because the appeal that justified the hold has been resolved; beyond the claim and the host no row moves on this branch, so neither the guest nor the reservation is re-versioned.

**Host appealed, and the appeal is `OVERTURNED`.** `claim_state` moves from `APPEALED` to `APPROVED` and `appeal_decision` is set to `OVERTURNED`. The submitted `approved_amount_cents` must be greater than zero and no greater than `claimed_amount_cents`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_INVALID`. This branch runs the final-mode settlement waterfall of §3.7 for the **first** time on this claim, so it writes the allocation lines, raises each charged layer's `consumed_cents`, may exhaust a layer and suspend the listing, may move the reservation's `deposit_state` from `HELD` to `APPLIED`, and writes `recovered_cents`, `uncovered_cents` and `host_absorbed_cents`. The guest's `approved_claim_count` rises by 1, the risk score is re-run with the 7-point claim penalty, and the critical-tier ban cascade of §3.9 is tested on the result exactly as at a first-instance approval. The guest's `version` increases by 1, the reservation's `version` increases by 1 only where the deposit actually moved from `HELD` to `APPLIED`, and the host row is untouched because no payout hold was ever placed on this appellant branch. If the original denial had come from the sweep, `sla_lapsed` is left true, recording that the approval arrived out of band.

**Host appealed, and the appeal is `UPHELD`.** `claim_state` returns from `APPEALED` to `DENIED` and `appeal_decision` is set to `UPHELD`. Every monetary field stays at 0, no allocation line is written, no layer capacity is consumed, and the reservation's deposit position is untouched. The host's `payout_hold` was never set on this appellant branch, so nothing is cleared and the host is not re-versioned; the claim is the only row this branch moves.

After any appeal decision the claim sits again in `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, waiting for the sweep to settle or close it once `appeal_due_at` has passed. No second appeal is admissible, because `appeal_round` is already 1.

One case is worth calling out because the segregation rule could otherwise strand it. When the sweep denies a claim for a lapsed adjudication service level, it stamps the literal string `"system"` into `decided_by_user_id`. That value can never equal a real adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, so the host's appeal against a sweep denial can be ruled on by any adjudicator — including one who had previously handled the claim in some other capacity.

### 3.9. Blocklist Enforcement, Dispute Rights and Supersession

A ban is recorded against a guest at one of three breadths named by `scope`: `PROPERTY` bans the guest from one listing, `HOST` bans the guest from every listing of one host, and `PLATFORM` bans the guest everywhere. The creation request carries `guest_id`, `scope`, an optional `property_id`, an optional `host_id`, an optional `supersedes_entry_id`, a `reason_code` and `expected_guest_version`. Both a `Host` and a `ComplianceOfficer` may create entries, but a `Host` is limited to `PROPERTY` and `HOST` scope; the `ComplianceOfficer` is the only role permitted to write `PLATFORM` scope, and a `Host` attempting it is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. That scope-sensitive refusal is read as part of the role step of §2.8 rather than with the four business checks below: it is answered after the request shape has been validated but before any body reference is resolved, so a `Host` sending `PLATFORM` together with a `guest_id` that names nothing still reads `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, while a body missing a required field or carrying an unknown `scope` reads its validation error first. The `PlatformAdmin` cannot create or lift bans at all — the operator role sits deliberately outside the enforcement chain.

Four business checks apply, in the precedence order of §2.8.

First, scope and target must agree. When `scope` is `PROPERTY` a `property_id` is required and `host_id` must be null; when `scope` is `HOST` a `host_id` is required and `property_id` must be null; when `scope` is `PLATFORM` both must be null. Any other combination — `PROPERTY` without a `property_id`, `HOST` without a `host_id`, or `PLATFORM` with either identifier set — is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_TARGET_MISMATCH`.

Second, the guest must not already be banned at the same breadth or wider. If any entry for this guest has an `entry_state` of `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` at the same scope, or at a broader one, the creation is refused with `ERR_BLOCKLIST_DUPLICATE_SCOPE`. A guest already under a `PLATFORM` ban therefore cannot receive a `HOST` or `PROPERTY` ban; a guest under a `HOST` ban cannot receive a second `HOST` ban, but can still receive a `PLATFORM` one.

Third, supersession must widen. When `supersedes_entry_id` is supplied, the referenced entry must belong to the same guest, must currently be `ACTIVE`, and its scope must be strictly narrower than the new entry's, where the ordering runs `PROPERTY` narrower than `HOST` narrower than `PLATFORM`. A pointer at an entry of another guest, at an entry that is `DISPUTED` or `LIFTED`, or at an entry of equal or wider scope is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_NOT_BROADER`.

Fourth, the grounds must be evidenced, and each `reason_code` carries its own requirement. `SUBSTANTIATED_INCIDENT` requires the guest's `substantiated_incident_count` to be at least 1. `APPROVED_CLAIM` requires `approved_claim_count` to be at least 1. `RISK_TIER_CRITICAL` requires the guest's `risk_tier` to read exactly `CRITICAL`. `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_ESCALATION` requires `escalated_incident_count` to be at least 1. A `reason_code` whose requirement is unmet is refused with `ERR_BLOCKLIST_GROUNDS_NOT_MET`.

The creation is also guarded by optimistic concurrency on the guest: the submitted `expected_guest_version` must equal the guest's stored `version`, and a stale value is refused with `ERR_GUEST_VERSION_STALE` as described in §2.7. This is what keeps a ban from being written on the strength of aggregate counts that another writer has already moved.

A successful creation starts the entry at `entry_state` `ACTIVE` and `dispute_window_state` `OPEN`, sets `dispute_due_at` to the entry's `created_at` plus 336 hours, records `created_by_role` and `created_by_user_id` from the request headers, leaves `auto_generated` false, and sets `version` to 1. When `supersedes_entry_id` was supplied the referenced entry is lifted in the same request, and that superseded entry's own `version` increases by 1 alongside its move to `LIFTED`. The guest's `active_blocklist_scope_count` is then recomputed over every remaining entry whose `entry_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED`, and the guest's `version` increases by 1. Because a supersession lifts exactly one narrower entry while adding exactly one broader entry, the recomputed count is unchanged in that case — the arithmetic is a recomputation over the enforcing set, not a blind increment.

A `PLATFORM`-scope entry does more than block future bookings. On becoming `ACTIVE` it cancels every reservation of that guest whose `stay_state` is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `check_in_at` is strictly later than the logical clock now, setting each one's `cancel_reason_code` to `BLOCKLISTED`. Where such a reservation has a `deposit_state` of `HELD`, that deposit moves to `RELEASED`, its `deposit_refunded_cents` is set equal to `deposit_hold_cents`, and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the same amount. Each cancelled reservation's `version` increases by 1. A stay already `IN_STAY` is left running: the cancellation reaches only future-dated stays. Narrower `PROPERTY` and `HOST` entries cancel nothing; they take effect at the next confirmation attempt.

The same machinery can fire without any request at all. Wherever §3 says a cascade re-runs a guest's risk score — a document upload or review, a payment signal, a completed stay, an acknowledged or escalated incident, an approved claim, and the document expiry of the sweep's Phase 1 — the resulting live position is tested once at that point, and the test is on the value rather than on the movement: when the guest's live `risk_tier` reads `CRITICAL`, whether or not the tier actually changed in this request, and no entry for that guest with `scope` `PLATFORM` and `entry_state` in `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` yet exists, the cascade of §3.2 creates one with `reason_code` `RISK_TIER_CRITICAL`, `auto_generated` true, and both `created_by_role` and `created_by_user_id` set to the literal string `"system"`. That entry then runs the future-dated cancellation described above. The guard against duplicates means a further recomputation while such an entry stands creates nothing — but a guest still reading `CRITICAL` after its automatic ban has been lifted earns a fresh one on the next cascade that touches its score.

Enforcement is read at confirmation time. A `Host` confirming a booking is refused with `ERR_GUEST_BLOCKLISTED` when any entry in the enforcing set `ACTIVE` or `DISPUTED` covers that guest for that listing — a `PLATFORM` entry covers every listing, a `HOST` entry covers every listing of that host, and a `PROPERTY` entry covers the single listing it names. `LIFTED` entries never block.

A blocklist entry carries two independent state dimensions, and one request can move both.

`entry_state` runs `ACTIVE`, `DISPUTED` and `LIFTED`, with `LIFTED` terminal. The banned `Guest` may dispute an `ACTIVE` entry by supplying a `dispute_statement_code` of `NOT_THE_GUEST`, `FACTS_DISPUTED` or `REMEDIED`. Two gates apply: `dispute_window_state` must read `OPEN`, and the logical clock must be at or before `dispute_due_at`; a window that is already `DISPUTED`, `RESOLVED` or `EXPIRED`, or a clock past the deadline, is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. These are the two gates this action answers first: both are evaluated ahead of any reading of `entry_state`, so a spent or lapsed window reports `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` even on an entry that is already `LIFTED`. Only an entry whose window is still `OPEN` and still inside its deadline reaches the enforcement dimension, where a `LIFTED` entry is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The ownership check of §2.8 step 6 still precedes both gates. A successful dispute moves `entry_state` from `ACTIVE` to `DISPUTED` and, in the same request, moves `dispute_window_state` from `OPEN` to `DISPUTED`, records the `dispute_statement_code`, and increases the entry's `version` by 1. The guest's `active_blocklist_scope_count` does not move, because `DISPUTED` remains inside the enforcing set — filing a dispute does not suspend the ban, and the entry keeps blocking confirmations while the dispute is pending.

Ruling on the dispute is a `ComplianceOfficer` action carrying a `dispute_reason_code`, and it is bound by segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the entry's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because an auto-generated entry records `"system"` as its creator, an auto-generated ban is always disputable and always decidable. Both outcomes set `dispute_decided_by_user_id` to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, move `dispute_window_state` from `DISPUTED` to `RESOLVED` so that no second dispute is admissible, and increase the entry's `version` by 1. Where they differ is the enforcement dimension. A `dispute_reason_code` of `GROUNDS_CONFIRMED` returns `entry_state` from `DISPUTED` to `ACTIVE`; the ban is enforceable again, its dispute right is spent, and the guest's `active_blocklist_scope_count` is unchanged because the entry never left the enforcing set. A `dispute_reason_code` of `GROUNDS_WITHDRAWN` or `IDENTITY_ERROR` moves `entry_state` from `DISPUTED` to `LIFTED`; the guest's `active_blocklist_scope_count` is recomputed and falls by 1, the guest's `version` increases by 1, and that guest becomes confirmable again at the lifted breadth. Reservations already cancelled by the ban are **not** reinstated by a lift — the cancellation is not undone, and the guest must book again.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may also lift an `ACTIVE` entry outright, without any dispute, and only an `ACTIVE` entry: an entry already `LIFTED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and one standing at `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since a pending dispute must be ruled on through the dispute decision rather than short-circuited by an outright lift. A successful lift moves `entry_state` to `LIFTED`, recomputes the guest's `active_blocklist_scope_count`, increases the guest's `version` by 1, and increases the entry's `version` by 1. The dispute dimension is deliberately left where it stands: an `OPEN` window on a `LIFTED` entry is not closed by the lift and can still lapse to `EXPIRED` in a later sweep. Finally, an `ACTIVE` entry moves to `LIFTED` as the side effect of another entry naming it through `supersedes_entry_id`.

`dispute_window_state` runs `OPEN`, `DISPUTED`, `RESOLVED` and `EXPIRED`, with `RESOLVED` and `EXPIRED` terminal. It moves `OPEN` to `DISPUTED` when the guest files, `DISPUTED` to `RESOLVED` when the officer rules, and `OPEN` to `EXPIRED` in the sweep when the clock has passed `dispute_due_at` with the window never exercised. After expiry a dispute attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Blocklist entries are readable one at a time across both dimensions, and listable with any combination of the filters `guest_id`, `property_id`, `host_id`, `supersedes_entry_id`, `scope`, `entry_state` and `dispute_window_state`, alongside `limit` and `offset`. A path or body identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_BLOCKLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.10. The Adjudication Sweep and the System Epoch

Deadlines in this system never lapse on their own. Every timer — document expiry, the neighbourhood-complaint response service level, the adjudication service level, the appeal window, the deposit claim window and the blocklist dispute window — is realised only when a `PlatformAdmin` advances the system epoch by running the adjudication sweep. The sweep takes no request body. It reads the logical clock exactly once, at the instant the run starts, and every phase compares its deadline field against that single reading; a clock advanced during the run would not be observed. Every deadline comparison is strict: an entity whose deadline is exactly equal to the captured reading survives this sweep and lapses only on a later one.

The sweep runs seven phases in a pinned, observable order. Rows written or moved by an earlier phase of the same run are visible to every later phase, so the order is part of the specification and not an implementation detail.

**Phase 1 — identity document expiry.** Every identity document whose `review_state` is `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED` and whose `expires_on` is strictly earlier than the captured reading moves to `review_state` `EXPIRED`. Each such move re-runs the affected guest's verification recomputation of §3.2, which may drop `accepted_doc_count` and `accepted_gov_id_count` below their thresholds and pull `verification_state` back out of `VERIFIED`, which in turn re-runs the risk score. A score that re-bands to `CRITICAL` fires the auto-generated `PLATFORM` blocklist cascade of §3.9, which cancels that guest's future-dated reservations and releases their deposit holds. A single expiring passport can therefore reach four entities in one phase. Each document moved here has its own `version` increased by 1, and each guest reached by the recomputation has its `version` increased by 1 — once for the whole run under the rule of §2.4, so a guest with three documents expiring in the same sweep, or a guest whose row is also moved by a later phase, still ends the run exactly one version ahead.

**Phase 2 — neighbourhood-complaint response service level.** Every incident whose `incident_state` is `OPEN` and whose `response_due_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading has `response_sla_breached` set to true. The incident is not moved out of `OPEN`; the flag records the breach and leaves triage to a human. The flagging run increases that incident's `version` by 1 and nothing else is touched — no reservation, guest or host row moves in this phase. An incident whose `response_sla_breached` already reads true is skipped outright by every later run: it is not counted again in `incidents_sla_breached` and its `version` does not move, so an `OPEN` complaint left unacknowledged across many sweeps is charged exactly one breach. An incident whose `response_due_at` is null — every source and type other than the `NEIGHBOR_COMPLAINT` of §3.5 — has no service level and is never considered here.

**Phase 3 — adjudication service level.** Every claim whose `claim_state` is `UNDER_REVIEW` and whose `adjudication_due_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading is denied: `claim_state` moves to `DENIED`, `sla_lapsed` is set to true, `decision_reason_code` is set to `SLA_LAPSED`, `decided_by_user_id` is set to the literal string `"system"`, and `decided_at` is set to the captured reading. Because this is a denial, `approved_amount_cents` stays 0, no allocation line is written, no coverage capacity is consumed and the reservation's deposit position is untouched. The denial stamps an `appeal_due_at` of the captured reading plus 168 hours. The claim's `version` increases by 1, and because nothing outside the claim moves, neither the guest, the host nor the reservation is re-versioned by this phase.

**Phase 4 — settlement of decisions whose appeal window has closed.** Every claim whose `claim_state` is `APPROVED` and whose `appeal_due_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading moves to `SETTLED`; the host's `settled_claim_count` rises by 1 and the host's `total_recovered_cents` rises by that claim's `recovered_cents`. Where the reservation's `deposit_state` reads `APPLIED` it moves to `RELEASED` and `deposit_refunded_cents` is set to `deposit_hold_cents` minus `deposit_applied_cents` — the unspent remainder of the hold goes back to the guest, and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` is not touched again because it was already reduced when the deposit was applied. Every claim whose `claim_state` is `DENIED` and whose `appeal_due_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading moves to `CLOSED_DENIED`; the host's `denied_claim_count` rises by 1 and `total_recovered_cents` is unchanged. Both `SETTLED` and `CLOSED_DENIED` are terminal, so no appeal is admissible afterwards. Every claim moved by this phase has its `version` increased by 1. The host's three counters are derived from the claim set on read, so the host row itself is not written and its `version` does not move; the reservation's `version` increases by 1 only where its deposit actually returned from `APPLIED` to `RELEASED`, and the guest row is not touched at all in this phase.

This is where the phase ordering earns its keep. A claim denied moments earlier in Phase 3 received an `appeal_due_at` of the captured reading plus 168 hours, which is strictly later than the captured reading, so Phase 4 in the **same** run cannot close it. The lapsed claim gets its full appeal window, and only a sweep run at least 168 hours later closes it.

**Phase 5 — deposit release.** Every reservation whose `deposit_state` is `HELD`, whose `claim_window_ends_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading, and against which no claim has a `claim_state` of `AWAITING_EVIDENCE`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPEALED` or `APPROVED`, moves its `deposit_state` to `RELEASED` with `deposit_refunded_cents` set equal to `deposit_hold_cents`, and the guest's `deposit_held_cents` falls by `deposit_hold_cents`. Each such reservation's `version` increases by 1 and the guest's `version` increases by 1, again at most once for the whole run. Claims sitting at `DENIED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED_DENIED` do not block the release, and a reservation carrying no claim at all is released on the strength of the closed window alone.

The ordering of Phase 3 before Phase 5 is likewise observable. `DENIED` is outside the live-claim set, so a claim that Phase 3 denied in this run no longer blocks its reservation, and Phase 5 releases that deposit in the same run. Running Phase 5 before Phase 3 would have found the claim still `UNDER_REVIEW`, held the deposit, and left the guest's money frozen for another epoch — that ordering is wrong.

**Phase 6 — blocklist dispute windows.** Every blocklist entry whose `dispute_window_state` is `OPEN` and whose `dispute_due_at` is strictly earlier than the captured reading moves to `dispute_window_state` `EXPIRED`. The entry's `entry_state` is untouched: an `ACTIVE` entry stays enforceable with its dispute right now spent, and a `LIFTED` entry that still carried an `OPEN` window has that window expire even though the ban itself is gone. The entry's `version` increases by 1, and the guest row is not touched, because no enforcing count moved.

**Phase 7 — posture recomputation.** Finally, every listing's `sensor_compliance_state` and `coverage_posture` are recomputed from scratch as described in §3.1, so that the effects of the preceding phases — most notably any coverage layer that reached `EXHAUSTED` — are reflected on every affected listing. Because both fields are derived at read time under §2.4, this recomputation writes nothing: it moves no `listing_state`, so a listing whose `coverage_posture` has just fallen to `UNPROTECTED` is **not** suspended by the sweep and keeps taking bookings until a host or officer acts, it never reactivates a `SUSPENDED` listing — reinstatement is always a host action — and no listing's `version` or `updated_at` moves in this phase. Its presence in the pinned order is a statement that the two postures every later read returns already reflect everything the six preceding phases did.

The sweep responds with a report of what each phase moved: `swept_at` carries the captured clock reading, `documents_expired` counts the Phase 1 moves, `incidents_sla_breached` counts the Phase 2 flags, `claims_denied_sla` counts the Phase 3 denials, `claims_settled` counts the Phase 4 moves into `SETTLED`, `claims_closed_denied` counts the Phase 4 moves into `CLOSED_DENIED`, `deposits_released` counts the Phase 5 releases, and `dispute_windows_expired` counts the Phase 6 expiries. A sweep that finds nothing to do returns every counter at 0 and still reports `swept_at`. Running the sweep twice at the same clock reading is safe: the second run finds every candidate already moved out of its qualifying state and reports zeroes.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every business endpoint is mounted under the `/rentals` prefix; the single clock-control endpoint is not, because it is a test-harness facility rather than part of the product surface. Endpoints that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201; endpoints that read, list or modify an existing resource respond with HTTP 200, including every state-transition action and the sweep. Each response carries the envelope of §2.2, with the created, updated or read resource in `data`, and list endpoints additionally populate the pagination fields in `meta`. All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3, in addition to the filters named in their description. A trailing slash is optional on every path, so `/rentals/guests` and `/rentals/guests/` address the same endpoint.
