# Payment Card Security Assessment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Qualified Security Assessor firm sells one thing: a defensible opinion. Every Report on Compliance the firm signs asserts that a named merchant or service provider met a fixed catalogue of payment card security requirements, on a specific inventory of systems, inside a specific network boundary, on the strength of evidence a certified individual personally gathered. When that opinion is later challenged — by an acquiring bank, by a card brand, by a forensic investigator after a breach — the firm must be able to show not merely the conclusion but the entire chain of reasoning that produced it: which network zones were claimed to be out of scope, which of those claims were actually tested, which components fell inside the sample and why, which testing procedures were satisfied by which artefact, and which gaps were closed before the report was finalised. Today that chain lives in spreadsheets, shared drives and the memory of the lead assessor, and it breaks in exactly the places that matter most.

This document specifies the API for the firm's assessment platform. The platform's central premise is that scope is not a document but a computed consequence: the moment a segmentation test fails, every zone beneath the failed boundary and every component inside those zones is dragged into the cardholder data environment, the sample that was drawn from the old inventory becomes invalid, and the engagement is no longer eligible to produce a report until the assessor either proves the boundary or accepts the wider scope. The same premise governs money, independence and time. The fee quoted to the client is derived from the sample size rather than typed in, and may never be allowed to drift above the ceiling the client approved. The individual who signs off on the quality of a report may never be the individual who led the engagement, and may never be someone who collected so much as a single evidence item on it. Certification lapses, vendor approvals expire and quarterly scan windows close, all measured against a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that a full assessment year can be exercised deterministically.

Three audiences read the resulting data. Assessors drive the engagement forward and need the platform to tell them, at any moment, what is blocking them. Quality-assurance reviewers need a report whose numbers they can audit without re-deriving them. Acquirers and card brands need an attestation that is traceable to a named, currently certified human being. The API therefore exposes both the raw records and a set of computed positions — a scope partition, a gap ledger, a finding ledger, a fee breakdown and two deliberately different compliance percentages — so that no consumer is ever forced to recompute the firm's own arithmetic.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-ID` cannot be resolved to a known assessor record, a known scanning-vendor record, or a recognised opaque operator, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers resolve but the named role is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before the request body is inspected at all.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `LeadAssessor`, `Assessor`, `QaReviewer`, `AsvVendor`, `ClientRepresentative` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `LeadAssessor` is the Qualified Security Assessor who owns one engagement end to end — scope, sampling, fieldwork, remediation coordination, report authoring and attestation. When `X-User-Role` is `LeadAssessor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Assessor` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Assessor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, every lead-only operation additionally requires that `X-User-ID` equal the engagement's `lead_assessor_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_ENGAGEMENT_LEAD` (HTTP 403) — being certified and holding the `LeadAssessor` role is never sufficient on an engagement somebody else leads.

An `Assessor` is a staff assessor executing testing procedures and collecting evidence. When `X-User-Role` is `Assessor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Assessor` record. This role is not tied to engagement ownership and holds no exclusive operations of its own; it shares the fieldwork surface with the lead. Its one lasting consequence is recorded rather than checked at the time: the identifier of the collecting caller is stamped onto every evidence item as `collector_assessor_id`, and having collected even one evidence item on an engagement permanently disqualifies that individual from quality-assurance review of that engagement's report.

A `QaReviewer` is the firm's internal quality-assurance reviewer. When `X-User-Role` is `QaReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Assessor` record; every quality-assurance operation additionally requires that `X-User-ID` equal the engagement's `qa_reviewer_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_ENGAGEMENT_QA_REVIEWER` (HTTP 403). The reviewer must be flagged `qa_qualified`, must not be the engagement's lead, and must not have collected evidence on the engagement.

An `AsvVendor` is the Approved Scanning Vendor that runs the quarterly external scans, publishes findings and rules on false-positive disputes. When `X-User-Role` is `AsvVendor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AsvVendor` record; ownership checks compare the target scan cycle's `asv_vendor_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a vendor acting on a cycle that is not its own is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

A `ClientRepresentative` is the assessed entity's own staff. When `X-User-Role` is `ClientRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Client representatives may raise false-positive disputes and record in-year changes, and may read anything, but may never record evidence, conclude a requirement or touch a report.

A `PlatformAdmin` is a firm-level operator that maintains the assessor and vendor registries and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Platform administration carries no engagement-level authority whatsoever: an administrator cannot conclude a requirement, decide a worksheet or attest a report. Its only overlap with assessment work is that it may open an engagement, a capability it shares with `LeadAssessor`.

Every read endpoint in the system admits all six roles. The write surface is partitioned: the registries and the clock belong to `PlatformAdmin`; scope definition, testing and evidence collection are shared between `LeadAssessor` and `Assessor`; sampling, scope lock, report authoring, attestation, submission, reassessment planning, segment re-parenting, segment withdrawal, segment revalidation, requirement reopening, worksheet drafting, worksheet submission, remediation assignment and scan-cycle scheduling are reserved to `LeadAssessor`; worksheet decisions and report quality-assurance reviews are reserved to `QaReviewer`; scan completion, finding publication and dispute decisions are reserved to `AsvVendor`; raising a false-positive dispute is shared between `LeadAssessor` and `ClientRepresentative`; and recording an in-year change is shared between `LeadAssessor`, `Assessor` and `ClientRepresentative`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise for a particular collection. Four pagination fields appear in the `meta` object of every list response: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is taken, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads and write responses carry `meta` without these four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource in the system is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded meaning — clients must never parse it or construct one. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two record types are deliberate exceptions because they are append-only historical facts and are never edited after the fact: evidence items and change-log entries carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those two record types both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely, and no operation in this specification ever bumps a version on them.

When an operation on one resource propagates into another — and the cascades described in §2.6 and §3 do this constantly — the `version` and `updated_at` of *every* row the operation actually changed are advanced, not merely those of the row named in the request path. A single request nevertheless advances any one row's `version` by at most 1, however many links of the same cascade rewrite that same row; later links only refresh its `updated_at`. Where a subsection below says a particular counter or cascade advances the engagement, that engagement's `version` increments by 1 for that single request no matter how many child rows the same cascade touched, and the same once-per-request rule holds for every other row a cascade rewrites more than once — a zone whose `segmentation_status` and whose `effective_class` are both written by one segmentation verdict ends that request one version higher, not two. A row created during the request starts at `version` 1 and stays there even if a later link of the same cascade rewrites it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical clock whose startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an administrative capability moves that clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant, answering with the new `current_utc` value. Before the clock is ever moved, every certification, vendor-approval, scan-window and due-date rule in this specification evaluates against the startup default.

Four computed positions read the clock and are re-derived on the next read after it moves. An assessor's `certification_status` is `ACTIVE` while the clock is strictly earlier than that assessor's `certification_expires_at`, and `LAPSED` from that instant onward. A scanning vendor's `effective_approval` is `REVOKED` whenever its `approval_status` is `REVOKED`; otherwise it is `EXPIRED` once the clock has reached or passed `approval_expires_at`; otherwise it is `APPROVED`. An engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag and its `reassessment_required` flag are likewise recomputed against the current clock instant. Timestamps the server stamps onto rows — `collected_at`, `recorded_at`, `last_validated_at`, `decided_at`, `closed_at`, `superseded_at`, `scan_completed_at` and `attested_at` — are all taken from the logical clock, never from wall-clock time.

### 2.6. Engagement Posture: The Gate Over Every Child Write

Each engagement carries a computed gate called `posture`, taking one of three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Posture is not stored by any caller and is never latched; it is recomputed from scratch at the end of every write that touches the engagement or any of its children, from three inputs plus the engagement's own `status`.

The three inputs are recomputed from the child rows on every write. `open_gap_count` is the number of remediation items of the engagement whose `status` is `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `RETEST_FAILED`. `unvalidated_segment_count` is the number of network segments of the engagement whose `segmentation_status` is `DECLARED`, `TEST_PLANNED` or `FAILED`. `active_plan_stale` is true exactly when the engagement's most recently created sampling plan has `status` `STALE`.

Posture is then decided in a strict order, and the order is observable because the two non-open postures raise different errors. First: if the engagement's `status` is `REPORTING`, `ATTESTED`, `SUBMITTED` or `REASSESSMENT_PLANNED`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Only if that first arm does not apply — that is, only when `status` is `SCOPING` or `FIELDWORK` — is the second arm considered: the posture is `RESTRICTED` when at least one of the three inputs is set, meaning `open_gap_count` is 1 or more, or `unvalidated_segment_count` is 1 or more, or `active_plan_stale` is true. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. A reporting engagement that still has an open gap is therefore `FROZEN`, not `RESTRICTED`.

A `FROZEN` posture refuses child writes with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Exactly eighteen operations are subject to that refusal: declaring a network segment, inventorying a system component, creating a sampling plan, adding a requirement assessment, amending the engagement's terms, re-parenting a segment, recording a segmentation test plan, recording a segmentation validation result, re-planning a segmentation test after failure, withdrawing a segment, starting requirement testing, recording a requirement conclusion, reopening a requirement, appending an evidence item, drafting a compensating control worksheet, submitting a worksheet, assigning a remediation item and recording a remediation retest. Operations outside that list are unaffected by a frozen posture — nominating a quality-assurance reviewer, scheduling a scan cycle, publishing scan findings, completing a scan, disputing a finding, deciding a dispute, recording a change-log entry, every report operation and reassessment planning all remain available on a frozen engagement.

A `RESTRICTED` posture refuses exactly two operations with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409): locking scope and creating the Report on Compliance. Child data entry stays open under a restricted posture — that is the whole point of the distinction. An assessor whose engagement is restricted can keep collecting evidence, keep concluding requirements and keep re-testing segments; what they cannot do is declare the scope final or start writing the report.

### 2.7. The Scope Partition and Its Cascades

Network zones form a forest within an engagement: each zone may name an enclosing zone through `parent_segment_id`, or leave it null to sit at a root. Every system component sits in exactly one zone. Out of that structure the engagement maintains a partition of its component inventory into three disjoint counts — `cde_component_count`, `connected_component_count` and `out_of_scope_component_count` — plus a convenience roll-up, `in_scope_component_count`, which is the sum of the first two. The partition is conserved: the three counts always add up to the total number of component rows on the engagement, so a component that leaves one count enters exactly one other.

Two class fields exist on every component and they deliberately disagree. `declared_scope_class` is a copy of the owning zone's own `segment_class` taken at component creation, and it never changes afterwards — it is what the client asserted before anybody tested anything. `scope_class` is the owning zone's `effective_class`, and it is recomputed on every segmentation verdict cascade. A zone's `effective_class` equals its own `segment_class` as long as segmentation has not been disproven; once a `FAILED` verdict lands on that zone or on any of its ancestors, the zone's `effective_class` becomes `CDE`. The reasoning is the auditor's default: an unproven boundary is treated as a cardholder data environment boundary.

Cascade propagation is depth-first through the parent chain and is described in full, link by link, in §3.4. Two invariants govern its correctness. First, a `FAILED` verdict and the compensating `PASS` verdict that later reverses it are exact mirrors: after the `PASS`, `cde_component_count` returns to the literal value it held immediately before the failure. Second, no cascade ever edits a zone's own declared `segment_class` or a component's `declared_scope_class`; the cascade moves `effective_class` and `scope_class` only, so the client's original assertion is always still readable next to the assessor's finding.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Revisions

Two write operations in this system re-derive data the client has already been shown, and both therefore require the client to prove it is working from the current state of the parent record. In each case the version field is a required input, never optional, and it is checked against the parent's live `version`.

Creating a sampling plan requires `expected_engagement_version` in the request payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target engagement. If that value does not equal the engagement's current `version`, the creation is refused with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new plan legitimately supersedes the engagement's prior active or stale plan and re-derives the `in_sample` flag of every component on the engagement; a writer holding a stale view of the engagement would silently discard a sampling decision it had never seen.

Drafting a compensating control worksheet requires `expected_requirement_assessment_version` in the request payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target requirement assessment. If that value does not equal the requirement's current `version`, the draft is refused with `ERR_STALE_REQUIREMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is symmetrical: a new worksheet supersedes the requirement's prior draft or rejected worksheet, so a writer working from a stale view of the requirement would supersede a worksheet whose rejection note it has not read.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may be made to fail on one. Both checks sit at the same rung of the validation ladder described in §2.10 — above every target-state check and below every safety, temporal, constraint and fiscal check.

### 2.9. Arithmetic, Precision and Rounding

Every rounding step in this specification rounds half up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 66.665 becomes 66.67 and 0.83335 becomes 0.8334. There is no banker's rounding anywhere in the system.

Money is carried to 2 decimal places: `approved_budget_amount` on input, and `assessment_fee_total` on output, along with every intermediate money value in the fee derivation. Compliance percentages — `as_found_compliance_percentage` and `as_attested_compliance_percentage` — are carried to 2 decimal places. Worksheet scores — `rigor_score`, `control_depth_points`, `monitoring_factor` and `risk_offset_points` — are all carried to 2 decimal places. The sampling `coverage_ratio` is the one value carried to 4 decimal places. A finding's `cvss_score` is carried to 1 decimal place. `estimated_hours` is a whole number, and every allocation, population and ledger count in the system is a whole number.

### 2.10. The Validation Ladder

When more than one rule could refuse a request, the refusal that surfaces is the one from the lowest rung of the following ladder. The ladder is global: it applies to every endpoint, and rules on a shared rung never fire on the same endpoint for the same request.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` must be present and must resolve to a known assessor, a known scanning vendor, or a recognised opaque operator — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role must be admitted by the target endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request shape.** Every required input must be present; enum inputs must fall inside their declared domain; numbers must be of the declared type and sign; `fieldwork_end_date` must not be earlier than `fieldwork_start_date`; `monitoring_interval_days` must be at least 1; `cvss_score` must lie between 0.0 and 10.0 inclusive; `control_refs` must be a non-empty array of strings; `limit` must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` must not be negative. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
4. **Path existence.** Each identifier in the URL path is resolved left to right, and the first unresolved one answers with that entity's own 404 code: `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASV_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CCW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_FINDING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHANGE_LOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Body-reference existence.** Identifiers supplied in the request body are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved. A `lead_assessor_id` or `qa_reviewer_id` that names no assessor answers `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`; an `asv_vendor_id` that names no vendor answers `ERR_ASV_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `parent_segment_id` or `segment_id` that names no zone answers `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `component_id` that names no component answers `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`. This rung applies to engagement creation, engagement amendment, quality-assurance reviewer nomination, segment declaration, component inventory, scan-cycle scheduling, evidence append, change-log recording and scan-finding publication.
6. **Ownership.** Lead-only operations require `X-User-ID` to equal the engagement's `lead_assessor_id` (`ERR_NOT_ENGAGEMENT_LEAD`); quality-assurance operations require it to equal `qa_reviewer_id` (`ERR_NOT_ENGAGEMENT_QA_REVIEWER`); vendor operations require it to equal the target cycle's `asv_vendor_id` (`ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`).
7. **Referential coherence.** Cross-engagement references, segment-graph cycles and unapproved scanning vendors are rejected here with `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE`, `ERR_SEGMENT_CYCLE` and `ERR_ASV_NOT_APPROVED` respectively.
8. **Posture.** `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN` and `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`, exactly as scoped in §2.6.
9. **Target state.** The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition exists, answering with the entity's own conflict code: `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REQUIREMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CCW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REMEDIATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ROC_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SCAN_CYCLE_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_DISPUTE_STATE`.
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_REQUIREMENT_VERSION`, exactly as scoped in §2.8.
11. **Segregation of duties and independence.** `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_QA_NOT_QUALIFIED` and `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_ACTIVE`.
12. **Temporal standing.** `ERR_ASSESSOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_ASSESSOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_SCAN_WINDOW_OVERLAP` and `ERR_NO_PASSING_SCAN`.
13. **Business constraints.** Uniqueness, completeness, sufficiency and eligibility rules, each described alongside the workflow it guards in §3.
14. **Fiscal ceiling.** `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, deliberately the last rung in the system.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright because they are easy to get backwards. On sampling-plan creation, a stale `expected_engagement_version` surfaces from rung 10 even when the plan the request would have produced would also have driven the fee above `approved_budget_amount` — the concurrency refusal wins over the fiscal refusal. On a report quality-assurance review, an independence breach surfaces from rung 11 even when the acting reviewer's certification has also lapsed — `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION` wins over `ERR_ASSESSOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. And because the fiscal rung is last, an engagement that is frozen and whose budget was subsequently cut below its current fee still answers `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FROZEN`.

### 2.11. Referential Coherence Within an Engagement

An engagement is a closed world. Every cross-entity reference carried in a request payload must resolve to a row belonging to the same engagement as the resource named in the path, and a reference that reaches across engagements is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Five references are policed this way: the `parent_segment_id` of a newly declared zone, the `parent_segment_id` supplied when re-parenting a zone, the `segment_id` of a newly inventoried component, the `component_id` of an evidence item, the `component_id` of a change-log entry and the `component_id` of a scan finding — the last of which must belong to the engagement that owns the scan cycle in the path.

Because zones reference other zones, the segment graph must additionally stay a forest. When a zone is re-parented, the server walks upward from the *proposed parent* through `parent_segment_id` links; if that walk ever reaches the zone being re-parented, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_CYCLE` (HTTP 422). The proposed parent itself counts as the first step of the walk, so a zone may not name itself as its own parent. The same forest check runs when a zone is first declared with a `parent_segment_id`.

Every foreign key in the system is reverse-reachable through a filtered list endpoint, which matters most for rows the caller never created directly: remediation items minted by a cascade are discoverable only by listing remediation items filtered by `engagement_id`, `requirement_assessment_id` or `source_scan_finding_id`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Firm's Registries and the Standing of Its People and Vendors

Before any assessment work can happen, the firm's administrative operator populates two registries. Registering a Qualified Security Assessor captures the individual's `full_name` — which must later match an attestation signature verbatim, so it is recorded exactly as the certificate spells it — an `email` address that must be unique across the whole registry, and a `certification_expires_at` instant at which that individual's certification lapses. An optional `qa_qualified` flag records whether the firm has authorised this individual to perform internal quality-assurance review; when the flag is omitted it defaults to false, which means a freshly registered assessor cannot be nominated as a reviewer until somebody amends the record. A new assessor's administrative `status` defaults to `ACTIVE`, the only other admissible value being `SUSPENDED`. Attempting to register a second assessor under an email already in the registry is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSESSOR_EMAIL`, and the same refusal guards an amendment that would collide with another assessor's address.

Two positions on an assessor record are derived rather than stored. `certification_status` reads `ACTIVE` while the logical clock is strictly earlier than `certification_expires_at` and `LAPSED` from that instant onward — the distinction from the administrative `status` field matters, because a currently certified individual may still be `SUSPENDED` and a never-suspended individual may still be `LAPSED`. `lead_engagement_count` reports how many engagements name this individual as their lead and have not yet reached `REASSESSMENT_PLANNED`; engagements that have already been carried forward into next year's plan drop out of the count. Amendment is the operator's lever for both simulated conditions the platform must handle: re-dating `certification_expires_at` to expire a certification mid-engagement, and flipping `status` to suspend an individual. An amendment may also revise `full_name`, `email` and `qa_qualified`, recomputes `certification_status` immediately, and advances the assessor's `version` by 1. Assessors can be listed with optional filters on `status`, on `certification_status` and on `qa_qualified`.

Approved Scanning Vendors are registered in parallel, with a trading `name` that must be unique across the vendor registry — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_NAME` on both registration and amendment — and an `approval_expires_at` instant at which the vendor's approval lapses. A new vendor's `approval_status` defaults to `APPROVED`; the only other value is `REVOKED`. What the rest of the platform actually consults is the derived `effective_approval`, which resolves in a fixed order: it is `REVOKED` whenever `approval_status` is `REVOKED`, regardless of dates; otherwise it is `EXPIRED` once the logical clock has reached or passed `approval_expires_at`; otherwise it is `APPROVED`. Amendment may revise `name`, `approval_expires_at` and `approval_status`, recomputes `effective_approval` and advances the vendor's `version` by 1. Vendors can be listed with filters on the stored `approval_status` and on the derived `effective_approval`.

Time itself is administrative. An administrator-only test-driving capability moves the server's logical clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc` value. Nothing about this capability is a business operation — it exists so that a full assessment year, with lapsing certifications, expiring vendor approvals, closing scan windows and maturing due dates, can be exercised deterministically from the startup default of `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

### 3.2. Commissioning an Engagement

Opening an engagement is the one assessment-level operation the firm's administrator shares with a lead assessor. The payload names the client through `client_legal_name`, the card-brand `merchant_level` — one of `L1`, `L2`, `L3` or `L4` — the `assessment_year` the assessment covers, the `acquirer_name` of the bank that will receive the attestation, the calendar dates `fieldwork_start_date` and `fieldwork_end_date` bounding onsite fieldwork, the `approved_budget_amount` the client has signed off as a fee ceiling, and the `lead_assessor_id` of the Qualified Security Assessor of record who will eventually sign the attestation. A `qa_reviewer_id` may optionally be named at this point; when it is omitted the engagement's `qa_reviewer_id` is null until somebody nominates one. Both fieldwork dates are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, `fieldwork_end_date` may not precede `fieldwork_start_date`, and `approved_budget_amount` must be greater than zero and is carried to 2 decimal places.

A new engagement starts in `status` `SCOPING` with `posture` `OPEN`, `scope_locked` false, `attested_at` null, `reassessment_due_date` null and `next_assessment_start_date` null. Its fee is computed immediately against a sample that does not yet exist, which by the arithmetic of §3.5 yields `estimated_hours` of 160 and a corresponding `assessment_fee_total`. The named lead's `lead_engagement_count` is recomputed as part of the same write.

Four checks on the people named in an engagement fire in the ladder order of §2.10, and their scopes differ endpoint by endpoint — a detail worth reading carefully. Nominating as quality-assurance reviewer the same individual who is the engagement's lead is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that refusal guards engagement creation, engagement amendment and the standalone nomination alike. Nominating a reviewer whose `qa_qualified` flag is false is refused with `ERR_QA_NOT_QUALIFIED`, which guards engagement creation and the standalone nomination but not amendment. Naming as lead or reviewer an assessor whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_ACTIVE`, guarding all three. Naming as lead or reviewer an assessor whose `certification_status` is `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, and that check guards engagement creation and the standalone nomination — and, later in the lifecycle, the quality-assurance review and the attestation — but not amendment.

A lead assessor cannot be in two places at once, and the platform enforces that against the closed interval running from `fieldwork_start_date` to `fieldwork_end_date` inclusive, widened by a three-day cooldown on both sides. For any *other* engagement led by the same individual whose `status` is not `REASSESSMENT_PLANNED`, the assignment is refused when the new engagement's start date falls on or before that other engagement's end date advanced by three days *and* that other engagement's start date falls on or before the new engagement's end date advanced by three days. Both conditions must hold for the conflict to exist; when either fails the two windows are far enough apart to be worked. A conflict answers `ERR_ASSESSOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the same calendar test re-runs whenever an amendment moves the fieldwork window.

Nominating the quality-assurance reviewer after the fact is a lead-only operation carrying just `qa_reviewer_id`; it runs the segregation-of-duties, qualification, active-status and certification checks in ladder order and advances the engagement's `version` by 1. Amending the engagement's commercial terms is likewise lead-only and may revise `approved_budget_amount`, `acquirer_name`, `fieldwork_start_date`, `fieldwork_end_date` and `qa_reviewer_id`. An amendment recomputes the fee and re-checks it against the possibly-new ceiling, re-checks the lead's calendar when the window moved, recomputes `posture` and advances `version` by 1. Because the fiscal rung is the last in the ladder, cutting `approved_budget_amount` below the fee the engagement has already accrued is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` only after every other rule on the amendment has passed.

Engagements can be read one at a time — the payload carries `posture`, the three partition counts of §2.7 together with `in_scope_component_count`, every gap and requirement count, the fee figures and both compliance percentages, while `total_component_count` is a field of the consolidated read described next rather than of the engagement read — or listed with filters on `status`, `posture`, `lead_assessor_id`, `qa_reviewer_id`, `merchant_level` and `assessment_year`. A dedicated computed read assembles the engagement's whole position into a single flat payload: `engagement_id`, `posture`, `cde_component_count`, `connected_component_count`, `out_of_scope_component_count`, `total_component_count`, `open_gap_count`, `unvalidated_segment_count`, `accepted_ccw_count`, `estimated_hours`, `assessment_fee_total`, `as_found_compliance_percentage`, `as_attested_compliance_percentage`, `quarterly_scan_compliant`, `scope_affecting_change_count` and `reassessment_required`. Every one of those numbers is derived on read from the engagement's children, never stored by a caller.

### 3.3. Drawing the Cardholder Data Environment Boundary

Scope definition begins with the client's own picture of its network. A lead or staff assessor declares each network zone with a `name` that must be unique within the engagement — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_NAME` — and a `segment_class` recording the class the client asserted before anything was tested, one of `CDE`, `CONNECTED` or `OUT_OF_SCOPE`. An optional `parent_segment_id` places the zone inside an enclosing zone; omitting it, or supplying null, makes the zone a root of the engagement's forest. Zones are created with `segmentation_status` `DECLARED`, with `effective_class` equal to their own declared `segment_class`, with `test_method` and `planned_date` both null, with `revalidation_round` at 0, with `last_validated_at` null and with `scope_expanded_by_failure` false. Because a `DECLARED` zone is by definition unvalidated, declaring one raises the engagement's `unvalidated_segment_count` and therefore drives `posture` to `RESTRICTED` — an engagement cannot lock scope or start a report while any zone's segmentation claim is still unproven.

Clients correct their own network diagrams, so a lead assessor may re-parent a zone. Only `parent_segment_id` may change through that surface, and supplying null detaches the zone back to a forest root. Three guards apply. The zone being re-parented must still be `DECLARED`; a zone whose testing has begun cannot be moved, and the attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`. The proposed parent must belong to the same engagement, or the request answers `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE`, and the upward walk from the proposed parent must not reach the zone being moved, or the request answers `ERR_SEGMENT_CYCLE`. Finally, the proposed parent's upward chain must not contain any zone whose `segmentation_status` is `FAILED`; that parent is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`. This last guard exists so that re-parenting can never move `effective_class`: because a zone cannot be grafted underneath a failed boundary, the component partition and any active sampling plan are left untouched by a re-parent, and the operation advances only the zone's own `version` by 1.

A zone that turns out not to exist is withdrawn by the lead with a free-text `reason`. Withdrawal is only available from `DECLARED`; any later state answers `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`. A zone may not be withdrawn while any system component still names it through `segment_id`, nor while any other zone still names it through `parent_segment_id`; either situation is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_HAS_COMPONENTS`. A successful withdrawal moves `segmentation_status` from `DECLARED` to `WITHDRAWN`, drops the engagement's `unvalidated_segment_count` by 1 and recomputes `posture` — which is how an engagement whose only blocker was a phantom zone flips from `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` — and advances both the zone's `version` and the engagement's `version` by 1. `VALIDATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal segmentation states: nothing moves out of either. Zones can be read individually or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `parent_segment_id`, `segmentation_status`, `segment_class` and `effective_class`, the first two of which make the zone's own two foreign keys reverse-reachable.

Inside those zones the assessor inventories system components. Each component is recorded with a `hostname` that must be unique within the engagement — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HOSTNAME` — a `component_type` naming the sampling stratum it belongs to (`SERVER`, `NETWORK_DEVICE`, `POS_TERMINAL`, `APPLICATION` or `DATABASE`), a `function_class` naming its functional role for requirement mapping (`PAYMENT_APPLICATION`, `DATA_STORE`, `SECURITY_SERVICE`, `CONNECTED_SUPPORT` or `SEGMENTATION_CONTROL`), a boolean `stores_chd` asserting whether the component stores cardholder data, a `facility_name` grouping it with everything else at the same physical location, a `facility_type` categorising that location (`DATA_CENTER`, `CALL_CENTER`, `RETAIL_STORE` or `OFFICE`) and a `segment_id` placing it in one of the engagement's declared zones. The zone must belong to the same engagement, or the request answers `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE`. Facilities must be internally consistent: within one engagement, a given `facility_name` may never appear under two different `facility_type` values, and the second, disagreeing component is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_CONFLICT`.

Component creation freezes one class field and derives the other. `declared_scope_class` is copied from the owning zone's own `segment_class` at that moment and never changes for the life of the row. `scope_class` is set from the owning zone's current `effective_class`, and will be rewritten by every later segmentation verdict cascade. `sampling_stratum` mirrors `component_type` for eligible components — those whose `scope_class`, that is the owning zone's current `effective_class`, is `CDE` or `CONNECTED` — and is null for components that fall outside that eligible population; a later segmentation cascade moves `scope_class` without rewriting `sampling_stratum`, which is next re-derived by the selection step of §3.5. `in_sample` starts false and stays false until a sampling plan actually selects the row. Each new component immediately re-derives the engagement's `cde_component_count`, `connected_component_count`, `out_of_scope_component_count` and `in_scope_component_count`. Facilities carry an ordinary review-priority rank — `DATA_CENTER` first, `CALL_CENTER` second, `RETAIL_STORE` third, `OFFICE` fourth — but that rank is descriptive only; the facility selector of §3.5 orders strictly by `facility_name` and never by category. Components can be read individually or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `segment_id`, `scope_class`, `declared_scope_class`, `component_type`, `in_sample` and `facility_name`.

### 3.4. Proving Segmentation — and the Cost of Failing To

A declared zone is a claim, and every claim gets tested. Recording the test plan moves the zone from `DECLARED` to `TEST_PLANNED`, writing the `test_method` the assessor will use — `PEN_TEST`, `CONFIG_REVIEW` or `TRAFFIC_ANALYSIS` — and the `planned_date` on which it will happen, and advancing the zone's `version` by 1. Planning is available to both the lead and a staff assessor, requires the engagement's `posture` not to be `FROZEN`, and requires the engagement's `status` to be `SCOPING` or `FIELDWORK`. Recording the plan does not change the engagement's `unvalidated_segment_count`, because `TEST_PLANNED` is still an unvalidated state.

The verdict is recorded with an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and an optional `note`, is likewise open to the lead and to a staff assessor, and requires a posture other than `FROZEN`. A verdict is only meaningful from `TEST_PLANNED`; a verdict against a zone that has no test plan answers `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE`.

A `FAIL` verdict is the single most consequential write in the platform, and it propagates in five links, each caused by the one before it rather than by the original call. **First**, the zone itself moves `TEST_PLANNED → FAILED`, its `scope_expanded_by_failure` flag is set to true, and its `effective_class` becomes `CDE`. **Second**, because that zone's `effective_class` became `CDE`, every zone in its descendant closure through `parent_segment_id` — children, grandchildren and onward, at any depth — also has `effective_class` set to `CDE`; their own declared `segment_class` values are left exactly as the client asserted them. **Third**, because those zones' effective classes became `CDE`, every system component whose `segment_id` lies anywhere in that closure and whose `scope_class` was `CONNECTED` or `OUT_OF_SCOPE` has its `scope_class` set to `CDE`; components already in `CDE` are unaffected. **Fourth**, because the component partition changed, the engagement's `cde_component_count`, `connected_component_count`, `out_of_scope_component_count` and `in_scope_component_count` are all recomputed, and the engagement's most recently created sampling plan — if that plan's `status` is `ACTIVE` — moves `ACTIVE → STALE`, which clears the engagement's `active_sampling_plan_id` to null and sets `active_plan_stale` to true. **Fifth**, because `active_plan_stale` became true, the engagement's `posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`, which is what blocks scope lock and report creation until a fresh plan is drawn. The engagement's `unvalidated_segment_count` does not move — `FAILED` is still an unvalidated state — and both the zone's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1.

A `PASS` verdict moves the zone `TEST_PLANNED → VALIDATED`, stamps `last_validated_at` from the logical clock and sets the zone's `effective_class` back to its own declared `segment_class`. Beyond that, a `PASS` behaves in one of two ways, and the branch is chosen by whether this zone's `scope_expanded_by_failure` was true at the moment of the verdict. **When it was true**, a compensating cascade runs that is the exact mirror of the failure cascade: `scope_expanded_by_failure` is cleared to false, every zone in this zone's descendant closure has `effective_class` restored to its own declared `segment_class`, and — because those effective classes changed — every component in the closure has `scope_class` set to its zone's restored `effective_class`. The partition returns to precisely the values it held before the failure, so `cde_component_count` recovers its pre-failure literal rather than merely a similar number. Still inside this branch, because the eligible population changed again, the engagement's most recently created sampling plan, if its `status` is `ACTIVE`, moves `ACTIVE → STALE`. **When `scope_expanded_by_failure` was false** — a zone that passed on the first attempt and never dragged anything into scope — nothing in the component partition moves and an `ACTIVE` sampling plan stays `ACTIVE`. Either way, the engagement's `unvalidated_segment_count` drops by 1, `posture` is recomputed, and both the zone's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1.

Failure is not final. The lead assessor may re-plan a failed zone's test, supplying a fresh `test_method` and `planned_date` that overwrite the previous pair. Re-planning is lead-only, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, is available only from `FAILED` — any other state answers `ERR_INVALID_SEGMENT_STATE` — and moves the zone `FAILED → TEST_PLANNED` while incrementing `revalidation_round` by 1 and advancing the zone's `version`. Crucially, re-planning does *not* undo the scope expansion: `scope_expanded_by_failure` stays true and the whole subtree keeps `effective_class` `CDE` until an actual `PASS` verdict lands. A zone may cycle between `FAILED` and `TEST_PLANNED` any number of times, and `revalidation_round` counts the round trips.

### 3.5. Sampling Methodology, Fee Derivation and Locking Scope

Payment card assessments are not exhaustive; they are sampled, and the sample is the assessor's most contestable judgement. The platform therefore derives the sample rather than accepting it. Drawing a plan is a lead-only operation that carries three inputs: a `plan_variant`, a `justification_text` recording the assessor's rationale — which the methodology requires and which may not be empty — and the `expected_engagement_version` demanded by §2.8. It requires the engagement's `posture` not to be `FROZEN`.

The `plan_variant` selects between two populations that deliberately disagree about the same component inventory. A `PRELIMINARY` plan draws from the components of the engagement whose `declared_scope_class` is `CDE` or `CONNECTED` — the classes implied by the zones' own declared classes at the time each component was inventoried, ignoring every later segmentation verdict. A `FINAL` plan draws from the components whose `scope_class` is `CDE` or `CONNECTED` — the effective classes after every segmentation cascade has been applied. On an engagement where a boundary failed, the `FINAL` population is strictly larger, and that divergence is the whole reason both variants exist. Whichever variant is chosen, the size of its population is recorded on the plan as `eligible_component_count`, and a variant whose eligible population is empty — no component qualifying as either `CDE` or `CONNECTED` — is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_SAMPLE_POPULATION`.

Component allocation is stratified, and the strata are iterated in one pinned order: `SERVER` first, then `NETWORK_DEVICE`, then `POS_TERMINAL`, then `APPLICATION`, then `DATABASE`. For each stratum in that order the platform counts the eligible components whose `component_type` matches. A stratum with no eligible components is allocated zero. A stratum with at least one eligible component is allocated the smaller of two quantities: its own population, and the larger of two floors — a minimum of 2 components per stratum, and one quarter of the stratum's population rounded *up* to a whole number. The per-stratum minimum is applied before the population cap, so a stratum whose population is exactly 1 contributes 1, not 2. Summing those allocations across the five strata in the pinned order gives `component_sample_size`, and the per-stratum figures are published as `stratum_allocations`, an ordered map whose keys appear in that same pinned stratum order including the strata allocated zero. `coverage_ratio` is `component_sample_size` divided by `eligible_component_count`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places.

The order of the ceiling and the sum is not interchangeable, and getting it backwards produces a materially smaller and materially wrong sample. The ceiling is applied per stratum and only then summed; taking one quarter of the pooled eligible population and rounding that up once is incorrect. For a six-component population split as three `SERVER`, two `APPLICATION` and one `DATABASE`, the pooled form would yield 2 while the correct per-stratum form yields 5 — two servers (a quarter of three rounds up to 1, raised to the floor of 2), two applications (a quarter of two rounds up to 1, raised to the floor of 2) and one database (its population caps the floor at 1).

Facility sampling runs on the same plan from the same variant population, over the distinct `facility_name` values held by the eligible components. `facility_population_count` is the number of such distinct facility groups holding at least one eligible component. When that population is zero, `facility_sample_size` is 0 and `sampled_facility_names` is an empty array. Otherwise `facility_sample_size` is the smaller of the population itself and the larger of two floors — a minimum of 1 facility, and thirty-four hundredths of the population rounded up to a whole number — with the minimum applied before the population cap. `sampled_facility_names` then holds the first `facility_sample_size` distinct facility names taken in ascending byte-wise order of `facility_name`, and that ordering is the only one ever used: never row identifier, never insertion order, never facility category. A population of one facility yields a sample of one; a population of three yields two; a population of five yields two.

Selecting the individual components follows in three steps. First, every component of the engagement has `in_sample` reset to false — including components that a previous plan had selected, so a redraw never leaves stale flags behind. Second, the strata are walked in the same pinned order, and within each stratum the eligible components of that `component_type` are ordered by `hostname` ascending, with the first however-many-were-allocated receiving `in_sample` true. Because `hostname` is unique within an engagement, ties are impossible and no identifier-based or random tie-break ever occurs: a `SERVER` stratum holding `app-01`, `app-02` and `app-03` with an allocation of 2 selects `app-01` and `app-02` and leaves `app-03` unselected. Third, `sampling_stratum` is set to the component's `component_type` for every eligible row and to null for every ineligible row.

Creating a plan supersedes whatever came before it. The engagement's previous plan — whether it was `ACTIVE` or had gone `STALE` — moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the logical clock, `SUPERSEDED` being terminal; the engagement's `active_sampling_plan_id` is repointed at the new row; and `active_plan_stale` is cleared to false once the new plan is `ACTIVE`, with `posture` recomputed accordingly. A `STALE` plan is not deleted and is still readable — it is simply no longer the engagement's active plan, and its `superseded_at` stays null until a newer plan replaces it. Plans can be read individually, including their `stratum_allocations`, or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `plan_variant` and `status`.

Every plan re-prices the engagement, because the fee is a function of the sample. The derivation runs in seven strictly ordered steps. Step one computes `estimated_hours` as a baseline of 160 hours, plus 12 hours for every component in the sample, plus 30 hours for every facility in the sample, plus 20 hours for every accepted compensating control worksheet on the engagement — the sample figures being read from the engagement's most recently created plan and the worksheet figure being `accepted_ccw_count`, the number of worksheets whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Step two multiplies those hours by the firm's rate of 250.00 per hour and rounds half up to 2 decimal places, giving the base amount. Step three computes an onsite surcharge of eight percent of that base amount, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, but only when the engagement's `merchant_level` is `L1`; for `L2`, `L3` and `L4` the surcharge is 0.00. Step four adds the base amount and the surcharge into a subtotal. Step five looks up a volume-discount percentage *against that subtotal* — ten percent at a subtotal of 120000 or more, six percent at 90000 or more, three percent at 60000 or more, and nothing below 60000. Step six computes the discount as that percentage of the subtotal, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Step seven subtracts the discount from the subtotal to give `assessment_fee_total`. Only two figures from that derivation are published, and they are published on the engagement — `estimated_hours` and `assessment_fee_total`, carried by the engagement read and by the consolidated read of §3.2; the base amount, the surcharge, the subtotal and the discount percentage and amount are intermediate values that appear in no payload, and the sampling plan's own payload carries no fee field at all.

Two plausible reorderings of those steps are wrong and produce different money. The tier lookup reads the post-surcharge subtotal, not the pre-surcharge base amount; and the surcharge is applied before the discount, never after it. A 226-hour `L1` engagement has a base of 56500.00, a surcharge of 4520.00 and a subtotal of 61020.00, which lands in the three-percent tier for a discount of 1830.60 and an `assessment_fee_total` of 59189.40. Keying the tier on the base amount instead would leave that engagement in the zero tier and report 61020.00. The recomputed total may never exceed the engagement's `approved_budget_amount`; when it would, the write that triggered the recomputation is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Three writes recompute the fee and re-check the ceiling: drawing a sampling plan, accepting a compensating control worksheet, and amending the engagement's terms.

With a `FINAL` sample in hand and the segmentation claims proven, the lead assessor closes scope definition. Locking scope is lead-only, requires `posture` `OPEN`, and requires at least one network segment of the engagement whose declared `segment_class` is `CDE` and whose `segmentation_status` is `VALIDATED` — an engagement with no validated cardholder-data zone is refused with `ERR_NO_CDE_SEGMENT`. It further requires the engagement's `active_sampling_plan_id` to be non-null and to point at a plan whose `plan_variant` is `FINAL` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; when the latest plan has gone `STALE` the engagement's posture is already `RESTRICTED` and the refusal is `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`. Scope lock is only available from `SCOPING`, and any later engagement status answers `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. A successful lock sets `scope_locked` to true, moves the engagement `SCOPING → FIELDWORK`, recomputes `posture` — which stays `OPEN`, since the preconditions guarantee no blocker — and advances the engagement's `version` by 1. Locking scope does not seal the inventory: zones and components may still be declared during `FIELDWORK`, but every such addition is expected to be recorded in the change log described in §3.9.

### 3.6. The Requirement Programme: Testing, Evidence and Conclusions

Fieldwork is organised requirement by requirement. Either the lead or a staff assessor adds a requirement to the programme with its dotted `requirement_ref` — something of the shape `6.4.1` — which must be unique within the engagement, a repeat being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT_REF`. The payload also carries the top-level `requirement_family`, one of `R1` through `R12`; a `testing_procedure_count` of at least 1, recording how many testing procedures the requirement carries and thereby setting the evidence floor for it; and an `applicability` determination of either `APPLICABLE` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Adding a requirement needs a posture other than `FROZEN`.

Each requirement carries two entirely independent state dimensions. The testing dimension, `test_status`, moves through `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_TESTING`, and then one of `IN_PLACE`, `IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW`, `NOT_IN_PLACE` or `NOT_TESTED` — plus the separate terminal value `NOT_APPLICABLE`. The gap dimension, `remediation_status`, moves through `NONE`, `GAP_OPEN`, and then `GAP_REMEDIATED` or the terminal `GAP_ACCEPTED_CCW`. The two dimensions advance on their own schedules, and the combinations they produce are exactly what the two compliance percentages of §3.8 discriminate: a requirement sitting at `IN_PLACE` with `NONE` and a requirement sitting at `IN_PLACE` with `GAP_REMEDIATED` are both in place, but only the first was in place all along.

Applicability is decided once, at creation, and it forks immediately. A requirement created `APPLICABLE` starts at `test_status` `NOT_STARTED` with `remediation_status` `NONE`. A requirement created `NOT_APPLICABLE` is written directly into `test_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, which is terminal and has no outbound transitions at all; creation is the only producer of that value. A `NOT_APPLICABLE` row never enters the compliance denominator, which is why `applicable_requirement_count` on the engagement counts only rows whose `test_status` is anything other than `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Creation also fixes the requirement's `risk_points` from its family, on a scale the firm calibrated by control criticality: `R3` carries 5 points; `R4`, `R6` and `R10` carry 4; `R1`, `R7`, `R8` and `R11` carry 3; `R2`, `R5` and `R9` carry 2; and `R12` carries 1. Those points drive both remediation severity and the worksheet risk offset of §3.7. Adding a requirement recomputes the engagement's `applicable_requirement_count` and both of its compliance percentages. Requirements can be read one at a time — the payload exposes both state dimensions and the evidence coverage described below — or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `requirement_family`, `test_status`, `remediation_status` and `applicability`.

Testing begins when a lead or staff assessor starts the requirement, which requires the row to be `APPLICABLE` and at `NOT_STARTED`, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, moves `test_status` `NOT_STARTED → IN_TESTING` and advances the requirement's `version` by 1. A start against a requirement that has already left `NOT_STARTED` answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUIREMENT_STATE`.

Evidence is then appended, one record per artefact, by the lead or a staff assessor, and only while the requirement is `IN_TESTING`; an append against a requirement in any other testing state answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUIREMENT_STATE`. Each record names its `evidence_type` — `DOCUMENT_REVIEW`, `INTERVIEW`, `OBSERVATION` or `SYSTEM_CONFIG` — the `procedure_ref` identifying which testing procedure it satisfies, and a `sufficiency` judgement of `SUFFICIENT` or `INSUFFICIENT` recording whether this artefact alone discharges the procedure. Three optional fields carry the rest: `component_id` names the sampled component that was observed or whose configuration was read, `interviewee_name` and `interviewee_title` name the client staff member interviewed and their job title, and `note` carries the assessor's free-text observation. Within one requirement, the combination of `procedure_ref`, `evidence_type` and `component_id` identifies an evidence record uniquely, so the same procedure may be evidenced once per technique and, for component-directed techniques, once per observed component.

Four constraints police what makes a usable evidence record. An `OBSERVATION` or `SYSTEM_CONFIG` record must name a `component_id`, and omitting it is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_TARGET_REQUIRED`. An `INTERVIEW` record must carry both `interviewee_name` and `interviewee_title`, and omitting either is refused with `ERR_INTERVIEWEE_REQUIRED`. A named component must belong to the same engagement as the requirement, or the append answers `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE`, and it must be a component the engagement's active plan actually selected — a component whose `in_sample` is false is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_IN_SAMPLE`, because evidence gathered outside the sample proves nothing about the sample. Appending a record stamps `collector_assessor_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` header and `collected_at` from the logical clock, and denormalises the owning engagement's identifier onto the record as `engagement_id` so that an engagement's collectors can be scanned in one query. Evidence records are historical facts: they carry `id` and `created_at` only, and are never edited, so neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them. They can be read individually or listed with filters on `requirement_assessment_id`, `engagement_id`, `component_id`, `collector_assessor_id`, `evidence_type` and `sufficiency`.

Every append re-derives three positions on the parent requirement. `evidence_item_count` is the number of evidence records attached to it, counting `INSUFFICIENT` records as well as `SUFFICIENT` ones. `evidence_type_coverage` lists the distinct `evidence_type` values for which at least one `SUFFICIENT` record exists, always presented in the order `DOCUMENT_REVIEW`, `INTERVIEW`, `OBSERVATION`, `SYSTEM_CONFIG`; an `INSUFFICIENT` record contributes nothing to it. `evidence_completeness` is true only when both halves hold: `evidence_item_count` has reached or passed `testing_procedure_count`, and `evidence_type_coverage` contains all three of `DOCUMENT_REVIEW`, `INTERVIEW` and `OBSERVATION`. A `SUFFICIENT` `SYSTEM_CONFIG` record is welcome and appears in the coverage list, but it is not one of the three the completeness gate demands. Because evidence is append-only, completeness once achieved is never lost.

Concluding a requirement is available to the lead or a staff assessor, only from `IN_TESTING`, and only with a posture other than `FROZEN`. The payload carries a `conclusion` of `IN_PLACE`, `NOT_IN_PLACE` or `NOT_TESTED`, together with a `conclusion_rationale` that is written onto the requirement and remains readable as the rationale of the most recent conclusion. Every conclusion, whichever arm it takes, requires the engagement's most recently created sampling plan to be `ACTIVE` with `plan_variant` `FINAL`; a conclusion attempted while that plan is `STALE`, is `PRELIMINARY`, or does not exist at all is refused with `ERR_SAMPLING_PLAN_STALE`. Two of the three arms additionally require `evidence_completeness` to be true, and a conclusion of `IN_PLACE` or `NOT_IN_PLACE` without it is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`. `NOT_TESTED` is the single conclusion that does not require completeness, precisely because it records that the procedure could not be executed.

An `IN_PLACE` conclusion moves `test_status` `IN_TESTING → IN_PLACE`, leaves `remediation_status` at `NONE`, recomputes the engagement's `unmet_requirement_count` and both compliance percentages, and advances the requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each by 1. A `NOT_TESTED` conclusion moves `test_status` `IN_TESTING → NOT_TESTED`, explicitly creates no remediation item and leaves `remediation_status` at `NONE`, recomputes `unmet_requirement_count` and both percentages, and advances the same two versions by 1 — a `NOT_TESTED` requirement counts against compliance without ever entering the gap ledger.

A `NOT_IN_PLACE` conclusion propagates in four links. **First**, `test_status` moves `IN_TESTING → NOT_IN_PLACE` and `remediation_status` moves `NONE → GAP_OPEN`. **Second**, because `remediation_status` became `GAP_OPEN`, a remediation item is minted with `source` `REQUIREMENT_CONCLUSION`, `status` `OPEN`, a `gap_description` the platform composes by quoting the requirement's `requirement_ref` and the trigger that produced the gap, and a `severity` derived from the requirement's `risk_points`. **Third**, because that remediation item now exists, the engagement's `open_gap_count` is recomputed upward and its `unmet_requirement_count` increases. **Fourth**, because `open_gap_count` has reached at least 1, the engagement's `posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`, which is what blocks report creation. Both compliance percentages are recomputed, and the requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1.

A conclusion is not irrevocable. The lead assessor may reopen a requirement whose conclusion no longer holds, supplying a `reason`, with a posture other than `FROZEN`. Reopening is available *only* from `test_status` `IN_PLACE`: a requirement still at `NOT_IN_PLACE` has nothing to reopen, and a requirement sitting at `IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW` — one whose gap an accepted worksheet already covers — may not be reopened at all. Either attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUIREMENT_STATE`. A successful reopen propagates in four links of its own. **First**, `test_status` moves `IN_PLACE → IN_TESTING`, `reopen_count` increments by 1, and `remediation_status` moves to `GAP_OPEN` — from `NONE` on a requirement that had never been gapped, or from `GAP_REMEDIATED` on one that had been remediated once already. **Second**, a remediation item with `source` `REQUIREMENT_REOPEN` and `status` `OPEN` is created; on a requirement that had already been remediated and is being reopened a second time, this is a second such item. **Third**, the engagement's `open_gap_count` rises by 1 and its `as_attested_compliance_percentage` falls. **Fourth**, `posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`. The requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1.

### 3.7. Compensating Controls, the Gap Ledger and Remediation Coordination

Some requirements cannot be met as stated. Where a documented business or technical constraint prevents the stated control, the standard permits the assessor to argue that other controls achieve the same objective, and that argument is recorded on a compensating control worksheet. Drafting one is lead-only, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, and is available only against a requirement whose `test_status` is `NOT_IN_PLACE` — the requirement must already have been tested and found wanting, and a draft against a requirement in any other testing state answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUIREMENT_STATE`. The draft carries the worksheet's six narrative sections in order: `constraint_text` for the constraint that prevents the stated requirement, `objective_text` for the objective of the original requirement, `identified_risk_text` for the risk arising from the absent control, `control_refs` as an array of at least one non-empty identifier naming the compensating controls themselves, `monitoring_interval_days` recording how often in days those controls are verified, `validation_text` narrating how they were validated, and `maintenance_text` describing how they will be maintained. It also carries the `expected_requirement_assessment_version` demanded by §2.8. A worksheet is created at `status` `DRAFT`, with `reviewer_note` and `decided_at` both null, and denormalises its engagement's identifier onto itself as `engagement_id`.

The worksheet's persuasiveness is scored, not argued. Four intermediate figures are computed and published on the worksheet, and they are recomputed both when the worksheet is drafted and again when it is submitted. `control_depth_points` awards 20 points for each *distinct* entry in `control_refs`, capped at 100 — duplicate entries collapse before counting, so listing the same control twice buys no depth. `monitoring_factor` is a step function of `monitoring_interval_days`: 100 when the interval is 1 day or less, 80 when it is 7 days or less, 55 when it is 30 days or less, and 25 when it exceeds 30 days. `risk_offset_points` starts at 100 and subtracts 15 for each of the parent requirement's `risk_points`, with a floor of zero — a worksheet for an `R3` requirement therefore starts from a far weaker position than one for an `R12` requirement. The three are then combined into a raw score weighting `control_depth_points` at forty hundredths, `monitoring_factor` at thirty-five hundredths and `risk_offset_points` at twenty-five hundredths.

One last adjustment applies after the weighted sum, never as a weight adjustment. `thin_control_capped` is true whenever fewer than 2 distinct `control_refs` were supplied; when it is true, `rigor_score` is the smaller of the raw score and a hard ceiling of 40.00, and when it is false `rigor_score` is simply the raw score. Either way the result is rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Four distinct controls verified weekly on an `R6` requirement give depth 80, monitoring 80 and a risk offset of 40, for a `rigor_score` of exactly 70.00 — the acceptance boundary. Holding everything else and moving the family to `R3` drops the risk offset to 25 and the score to 66.25. A single control verified daily on an `R12` requirement produces a raw score of 64.25, but because only one distinct control was named the published `rigor_score` is 40.00.

A requirement may go through several worksheets. Drafting a new one for the same requirement moves any earlier worksheet of that requirement that is still `DRAFT` or that was `REJECTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and a superseded worksheet is excluded from every rigor and acceptance query thereafter. `ACCEPTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal. Submitting a worksheet for decision is lead-only, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, is available only from `DRAFT` — any other state answers `ERR_INVALID_CCW_STATE` — and requires the parent requirement to still be `NOT_IN_PLACE`. Submission re-validates the paperwork: all five narrative sections `constraint_text`, `objective_text`, `identified_risk_text`, `validation_text` and `maintenance_text` must be non-empty and `control_refs` must hold at least one entry, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CCW_SECTION_INCOMPLETE`. A successful submission moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, refreshes the four rigor figures and advances the worksheet's `version` by 1. Worksheets can be read individually, including their rigor breakdown, or listed with filters on `requirement_assessment_id`, `engagement_id`, `status` and `thin_control_capped`.

The decision belongs to the engagement's quality-assurance reviewer alone, is available only from `SUBMITTED`, and carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` together with a `reviewer_note`. The reviewer must satisfy the independence rule of §3.8 here as well as on the report: a reviewer who collected evidence on this engagement, or who is the engagement's lead, is refused with `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION`. Acceptance additionally demands two things of the worksheet itself: `monitoring_interval_days` must not exceed 30, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_CCW_MONITORING_INTERVAL_TOO_LONG`, and `rigor_score` must have reached 70.00 — the threshold is inclusive, so a score of exactly 70.00 passes and 69.99 does not — or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_CCW_RIGOR_INSUFFICIENT`. The parent requirement must still be `NOT_IN_PLACE`.

Acceptance is a branching fork whose two arms carry radically different consequences. The `ACCEPT` arm propagates in four links. **First**, the worksheet moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` with `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock and `reviewer_note` written. **Second**, because the worksheet was accepted, the parent requirement moves `test_status` `NOT_IN_PLACE → IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW` and `remediation_status` `GAP_OPEN → GAP_ACCEPTED_CCW`. **Third**, because that requirement's gap is now covered, every remediation item of that requirement whose `status` is `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `RETEST_FAILED` moves to `CLOSED_BY_CCW` with `closed_at` stamped, and the engagement's `open_gap_count` and `unmet_requirement_count` are recomputed downward. **Fourth**, because the engagement's `accepted_ccw_count` rose and its `open_gap_count` fell, the fee is recomputed with the additional 20 hours the newly accepted worksheet attracts and re-checked against `approved_budget_amount`, both compliance percentages are recomputed, and `posture` is recomputed — flipping `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` once `open_gap_count` has reached 0. The worksheet's `version`, the requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1. The `REJECT` arm, by contrast, carries no cascade whatsoever: the worksheet moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with `decided_at` and `reviewer_note` written, the parent requirement is left entirely untouched with `test_status` still `NOT_IN_PLACE` and `remediation_status` still `GAP_OPEN`, `open_gap_count` does not move, and only the worksheet's `version` advances by 1.

Remediation items are never created by a caller. Each one is minted by a cascade and carries a `source` recording which cascade minted it — `REQUIREMENT_CONCLUSION` for a `NOT_IN_PLACE` conclusion, `REQUIREMENT_REOPEN` for a reopened requirement, or `SCAN_FINDING_REJECTED` for the scan cascade of §3.9 — together with `engagement_id` and `requirement_assessment_id` copied from the requirement that produced the gap and a `gap_description` minted by the cascade. `source_scan_finding_id` is populated only on items whose `source` is `SCAN_FINDING_REJECTED`, naming the finding that caused the revert, and is null on every other item. `severity` is derived from the parent requirement's `risk_points`: `CRITICAL` at 5 or more, `HIGH` at exactly 4, `MEDIUM` at exactly 3, and `LOW` at 2 or fewer. Items start at `status` `OPEN` with `owner_reference` and `due_date` both null and `retest_count` at 0. Because a cascade-created item has no creation call for the caller to observe, listing remediation items with filters on `engagement_id`, `requirement_assessment_id`, `source_scan_finding_id`, `status`, `severity` and `source` is the only way to discover them.

Coordinating the fix is the lead assessor's job. Assigning an item is lead-only, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, and writes an `owner_reference` naming the client-side owner and a `due_date` recording the client's commitment date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Assignment is available from `OPEN` and from `RETEST_FAILED`, and any other state answers `ERR_INVALID_REMEDIATION_STATE`; from `RETEST_FAILED` it overwrites the previous owner and date. Either way the item moves to `IN_PROGRESS`, the item's `version` advances by 1, and the item continues to count toward the engagement's `open_gap_count` — assigning a gap does not close it.

Recording the retest is open to the lead or a staff assessor, requires a posture other than `FROZEN`, is available only from `IN_PROGRESS`, and carries an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` plus an optional `note`. A `FAIL` moves the item to `RETEST_FAILED`, increments `retest_count` by 1, leaves the parent requirement completely untouched, and — because `RETEST_FAILED` is still an open-gap state — keeps the item counted so the engagement's posture stays `RESTRICTED`. Only the item's `version` advances. An item may cycle between `RETEST_FAILED` and `IN_PROGRESS` through reassignment as many times as the remediation takes.

A `PASS` propagates in four links and is the compensating half of the gap ledger. **First**, the item moves `IN_PROGRESS → RETEST_PASSED`, `retest_count` increments by 1 and `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. **Second**, because this was the requirement's last item not yet in a closed state — that is, because the requirement now has no item left whose `status` is `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `RETEST_FAILED` — the parent requirement moves `remediation_status` `GAP_OPEN → GAP_REMEDIATED` and `test_status` `IN_TESTING → IN_PLACE`. That second transition is subject to `evidence_completeness` still being true, which it always is, because evidence is append-only. **Third**, because the requirement is back in place, the engagement's `open_gap_count` decrements to its pre-reopen value; `unmet_requirement_count` does not move, because a reopened requirement was sitting at `IN_TESTING`, which that counter never counted. **Fourth**, `posture` recomputes to `OPEN`, and `as_attested_compliance_percentage` returns to the literal value it held before the reopen while `as_found_compliance_percentage` stays lower — permanently. The item's `version`, the requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1. `RETEST_PASSED` and `CLOSED_BY_CCW` are the two terminal remediation states.

### 3.8. The Report on Compliance: Projection, Independent Review, Attestation and Submission

Authoring the Report on Compliance is the lead assessor's act of closing fieldwork, and the platform will only permit it on an engagement that has nothing outstanding. The payload carries only the firm's own document version label, `roc_version_label`. Five preconditions guard it. The engagement's `posture` must be `OPEN`, which by §2.6 means `open_gap_count` is 0, `unvalidated_segment_count` is 0 and `active_plan_stale` is false; a single open gap, a single untested zone or a stale sampling plan answers `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_RESTRICTED`. The engagement must be at `status` `FIELDWORK`, and any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. Its `applicable_requirement_count` must be at least 1, so that the compliance denominator is non-zero, or the request answers `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_REQUIREMENTS`. No requirement of the engagement may still be at `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_TESTING` — every requirement must have reached a conclusion — or the request answers `ERR_REQUIREMENT_TESTING_INCOMPLETE`. And at most one report may ever exist per engagement, so a second attempt answers `ERR_ROC_ALREADY_EXISTS`; revisions reuse the existing row rather than creating another.

Creating the report writes a row at `status` `DRAFTING` with `revision_count` at 0, `qa_decision_note` null, `attestation_signature_name` null, `attesting_assessor_id` null, `attested_at` null, `submitted_to` null and `submission_reference` null. It runs the compliance projection for both variants, writing both percentages, the three requirement counts and `compliance_result` onto the report and mirroring both percentages onto the engagement. It then moves the engagement `FIELDWORK → REPORTING`, which by the first arm of the posture rule drives `posture` to `FROZEN` and shuts down the entire eighteen-operation child-write surface of §2.6, and advances the engagement's `version` by 1.

The compliance projection is deliberately computed twice over the same requirement population, and the two answers are meant to differ. The denominator for both is `applicable_requirement_count`, the number of requirement assessments on the engagement whose `applicability` is `APPLICABLE`; rows created `NOT_APPLICABLE` never enter it. The `AS_FOUND` numerator counts the applicable requirements whose `test_status` is `IN_PLACE` *and* whose `remediation_status` is `NONE` — a requirement that was ever gapped is excluded from it forever, even after a successful retest restored it. The `AS_ATTESTED` numerator counts the applicable requirements whose `test_status` is either `IN_PLACE` or `IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW`, so both remediated requirements and compensating-control-covered requirements count toward it. Each numerator is divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, surfacing as `as_found_compliance_percentage` and `as_attested_compliance_percentage` respectively. Alongside them the report publishes `in_place_count` (requirements at `test_status` `IN_PLACE`), `ccw_covered_count` (at `IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW`) and `not_in_place_count` (at `NOT_IN_PLACE` or `NOT_TESTED`), plus the `applicable_requirement_count` frozen at that recomputation. `compliance_result` is `COMPLIANT` when `as_attested_compliance_percentage` is exactly 100.00 and `NON_COMPLIANT` otherwise; it is keyed on the attested variant alone, because keying it on the as-found variant would report a fully remediated engagement as non-compliant.

Consider an engagement with four applicable requirements: two sitting at `IN_PLACE` with `remediation_status` `NONE`, one at `IN_PLACE` with `GAP_REMEDIATED`, and one at `IN_PLACE_WITH_CCW` with `GAP_ACCEPTED_CCW`. Its `as_found_compliance_percentage` is 50.00 while its `as_attested_compliance_percentage` is 100.00, with `in_place_count` 3, `ccw_covered_count` 1, `not_in_place_count` 0 and `compliance_result` `COMPLIANT`. Change the third and fourth requirements to `NOT_IN_PLACE` with `GAP_OPEN` and `NOT_TESTED` with `NONE`, and both percentages read 50.00, `not_in_place_count` reads 2 and `compliance_result` reads `NON_COMPLIANT`. Five writes recompute the projection: report creation, a requirement conclusion, a worksheet decision, a remediation retest and the report's quality-assurance review.

Handing the report to quality assurance is lead-only and available only from `DRAFTING`; any other report status answers `ERR_INVALID_ROC_STATE`. It requires the engagement's `qa_reviewer_id` to be set, and a report handed over on an engagement with no nominated reviewer is refused with `ERR_QA_REVIEWER_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The report moves `DRAFTING → IN_QA` and its `version` advances by 1; the engagement stays at `REPORTING` and its posture stays `FROZEN`.

The review itself belongs to the nominated reviewer, is available only from `IN_QA`, and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with a `qa_decision_note`. Independence is the point of the whole exercise and is checked in two parts: the acting reviewer must not be the engagement's `lead_assessor_id`, and no evidence item of this engagement may carry that individual as its `collector_assessor_id`. Either breach answers `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION`. The reviewer's own `certification_status` must also be `ACTIVE`, and a lapsed reviewer answers `ERR_ASSESSOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` — but as §2.10 records, the independence check sits one rung above the certification check, so a reviewer who is both non-independent and lapsed is told about the independence breach. The two arms of the decision are disjoint. `APPROVE` moves the report `IN_QA → QA_APPROVED`, writes `qa_decision_note` and refreshes both compliance percentages onto the report. `REJECT` moves the report `IN_QA → QA_REJECTED`, writes `qa_decision_note` and performs no recomputation at all. Either way the report's `version` advances by 1, and only the rejection arm can lead back to fieldwork.

Taking a rejected report back into fieldwork is lead-only, available only from `QA_REJECTED`, and carries a `revision_note`. It moves the report `QA_REJECTED → DRAFTING`, increments `revision_count` by 1, and — this is the engagement's own cycle edge — moves the engagement `REPORTING → FIELDWORK`. Because the engagement has left the frozen band of statuses, `posture` is recomputed away from `FROZEN`: to `OPEN` when nothing is outstanding, or to `RESTRICTED` when `open_gap_count` is at least 1, `unvalidated_segment_count` is at least 1, or `active_plan_stale` is true. Evidence collection, requirement reopening and every other child write become available again. Both the report's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1.

Attestation is the moment a human being puts their name to the firm's opinion. It is lead-only, available only from `QA_APPROVED`, and carries `attestation_signature_name` — the typed signature. Three conditions must hold. The lead assessor's own `certification_status` must be `ACTIVE` at the logical clock, or the attestation answers `ERR_ASSESSOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. The engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag must be true, meaning some scan cycle of the engagement is `PASSING` and its window contains the logical clock; otherwise the attestation answers `ERR_NO_PASSING_SCAN`. And the supplied `attestation_signature_name` must equal the lead assessor's `full_name` verbatim — no trimming, no case folding, no partial match — or the attestation answers `ERR_ATTESTATION_SIGNATURE_MISMATCH`. On success the report moves `QA_APPROVED → ATTESTED` with `attestation_signature_name`, `attesting_assessor_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and `attested_at` stamped from the logical clock; the engagement moves `REPORTING → ATTESTED` with its own `attested_at` stamped; `posture` stays `FROZEN`; and both the report's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1.

Submission delivers the attested report to its recipient. It is lead-only, available only from `ATTESTED`, and carries `submitted_to` — either `ACQUIRER` or `CARD_BRAND` — together with the recipient-side `submission_reference`. The report moves `ATTESTED → SUBMITTED`, which is its terminal state; the engagement moves `ATTESTED → SUBMITTED`; the engagement's `reassessment_due_date` is set to the logical-clock date advanced by 365 days; and both versions advance by 1. Reports can be read individually or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `attesting_assessor_id`, `status` and `compliance_result`.

### 3.9. Quarterly Scanning, False-Positive Disputes, In-Year Change and Reassessment

Compliance is not established once a year and then forgotten. The standard requires external vulnerability scanning every quarter by an Approved Scanning Vendor, and the platform tracks each quarter as a scan cycle. Scheduling one is lead-only and carries a `quarter_label` such as `2026-Q3` — client-facing, and unique within the engagement, a repeat being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUARTER_LABEL` — the `asv_vendor_id` of the vendor that will run the scan, and a `window_start` instant opening the scan window. The cycle's `window_end` is derived as `window_start` advanced by 90 days and is treated as exclusive, so a window opening at `2026-04-01T00:00:00Z` closes at `2026-06-30T00:00:00Z` and that closing instant belongs to the next window rather than this one. Two checks guard scheduling, and their order is fixed by the ladder: the named vendor's `effective_approval` must be `APPROVED` at the logical clock, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASV_NOT_APPROVED`, and only then is the calendar consulted. The proposed half-open window, running from `window_start` up to but not including its computed end, must not intersect the window of any existing scan cycle of the same engagement, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCAN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Adjacency is deliberately permitted: a cycle that starts exactly at another cycle's `window_end` is accepted, and two cycles may share that instant exactly. A new cycle is created at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `scan_completed_at` null, `attestation_of_scan_compliance` false and every ledger count at 0, and the engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag is recomputed.

Only the cycle's own vendor may publish findings against it, and only while the cycle is still `SCHEDULED`; a finding pushed at a completed cycle answers `ERR_INVALID_SCAN_CYCLE_STATE`. Each finding names its `plugin_reference` — the vendor's plugin identifier or a CVE — a `cvss_score` between 0.0 and 10.0 carried to 1 decimal place, and the `component_id` of the scanned host, which must belong to the engagement that owns the cycle or the request answers `ERR_CROSS_ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCE`. From the score the platform derives `severity_class`, which is `HIGH_OR_ABOVE` when `cvss_score` has reached 4.0 and `LOW` otherwise. The threshold is inclusive at 4.0, so a 4.0 finding is `HIGH_OR_ABOVE` while a 3.9 finding is not. New findings start with `dispute_status` `NONE`, `dispute_rationale` null, `decision_note` null and `decided_at` null.

Each cycle maintains a finding ledger of four counts. `raw_high_finding_count` is the number of child findings whose `severity_class` is `HIGH_OR_ABOVE`. `accepted_false_positive_count` is the number of those high findings whose `dispute_status` has reached `ACCEPTED_FALSE_POSITIVE`. `effective_high_finding_count` is the first minus the second. `open_dispute_count` is the number of child findings whose `dispute_status` is `DISPUTED`. The ledger is conserved at every point in the dispute lifecycle: `raw_high_finding_count` always equals `effective_high_finding_count` plus `accepted_false_positive_count`. A rejected dispute never decrements the effective count; only an accepted false-positive claim does.

Closing the scan belongs to the cycle's vendor, takes no payload, is available only from `SCHEDULED`, and branches on the ledger rather than on anything the caller supplies. When `effective_high_finding_count` is 0 the cycle moves `SCHEDULED → PASSING`; when it is 1 or more the cycle moves `SCHEDULED → FAILING`. Either way `scan_completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `attestation_of_scan_compliance` is set to true exactly when the resulting status is `PASSING`. The engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag is then recomputed: it is true when some scan cycle of the engagement is `PASSING` and the logical clock falls at or after that cycle's `window_start` and strictly before its `window_end`. A cycle that fails therefore makes the engagement non-compliant unless another passing cycle still covers the clock. Both the cycle's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1. `PASSING` is the cycle's terminal state. Cycles can be read individually, ledger included, or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `asv_vendor_id`, `status` and `quarter_label`; findings can be read individually or listed with filters on `scan_cycle_id`, `component_id`, `dispute_status` and `severity_class`.

Scanners produce false positives, and both the lead assessor and the client's own representative may say so. Raising a dispute carries a `dispute_rationale` arguing the claim. Three conditions apply: the finding's `severity_class` must be `HIGH_OR_ABOVE`, since a low finding does not block anything and there is nothing to gain by disputing it — a low finding answers `ERR_FINDING_NOT_HIGH_SEVERITY`; the finding's `dispute_status` must be `NONE`, and a second dispute on the same finding answers `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_DISPUTE_STATE`; and the parent cycle's `status` must be `FAILING` or `DISPUTE_OPEN`. The finding moves `NONE → DISPUTED`, `dispute_rationale` is written, the parent cycle's `open_dispute_count` rises by 1, and the cycle moves `FAILING → DISPUTE_OPEN`. Both the finding's `version` and the cycle's `version` advance by 1.

The vendor that raised the finding is not the party that decides the dispute in its own favour — the decision is the vendor's to make, but it must be the cycle's own vendor, and the assessor who raised the claim has no vote. Deciding carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and a `decision_note`, and is available only from `DISPUTED`. On `ACCEPT` the finding moves `DISPUTED → ACCEPTED_FALSE_POSITIVE` with `decided_at` and `decision_note` written; the cycle's `accepted_false_positive_count` rises by 1 and its `effective_high_finding_count` falls by 1, keeping the conserved identity intact; and when that decision leaves `open_dispute_count` at 0 with `effective_high_finding_count` also 0, the cycle moves `DISPUTE_OPEN → PASSING` and the engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag becomes true. The finding's, the cycle's and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1. `ACCEPTED_FALSE_POSITIVE` and `REJECTED` are both terminal dispute states.

A rejection propagates in five links and reaches all the way back into the requirement programme. **First**, the finding moves `DISPUTED → REJECTED` with `decided_at` and `decision_note` written and the cycle's `open_dispute_count` decremented; `effective_high_finding_count` does not change, because a rejected claim leaves the finding standing. **Second**, because the last open dispute has closed with `effective_high_finding_count` still at 1 or more, the parent cycle moves `DISPUTE_OPEN → FAILING`. **Third**, because the cycle is failing, the engagement's `quarterly_scan_compliant` flag is recomputed to false — a cycle cannot recover scan compliance while any effective high finding stands — and every requirement assessment of that engagement whose `requirement_ref` is exactly `11.3.1` and whose `test_status` is `IN_PLACE` reverts to `IN_TESTING` with `remediation_status` moving to `GAP_OPEN`. **Fourth**, because that requirement reverted, a remediation item is minted with `source` `SCAN_FINDING_REJECTED` and `source_scan_finding_id` naming this finding, and the engagement's `open_gap_count` increments. **Fifth**, because `open_gap_count` has reached at least 1, `posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`, which blocks report creation. The finding's `version`, the cycle's `version`, the requirement's `version` and the engagement's `version` each advance by 1.

Between assessments the environment keeps moving, and the client is expected to say so. Recording an in-year change is open to the lead, a staff assessor and the client's representative, and carries a `change_type` of `COMPONENT_ADDED_TO_CDE`, `SEGMENT_RECLASSIFIED`, `THIRD_PARTY_ADDED`, `APPLICATION_UPDATED` or `STAFF_CHANGE`; a non-empty `description` narrating what changed; the client's own `affects_scope` assertion of whether the change touches the cardholder data environment; and an optional `component_id` naming the component concerned, which must belong to the same engagement and is left null for purely organisational changes. Each entry is stamped with `recorded_at` from the logical clock. Change-log entries are historical facts and carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them. They can be read individually or listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `component_id`, `change_type`, `affects_scope` and `triggers_reassessment`.

Whether an entry actually matters is derived, not asserted. `triggers_reassessment` is true only when both halves hold: `affects_scope` is true *and* `change_type` is one of `COMPONENT_ADDED_TO_CDE`, `SEGMENT_RECLASSIFIED` or `THIRD_PARTY_ADDED`. An `APPLICATION_UPDATED` or `STAFF_CHANGE` entry never triggers, even when the client marked it as affecting scope, and a `COMPONENT_ADDED_TO_CDE` entry with `affects_scope` false does not trigger either. The engagement's `scope_affecting_change_count` counts the entries whose `triggers_reassessment` is true, and `reassessment_required` becomes true once that count has reached 3 — the threshold is inclusive, so the second triggering entry leaves the flag false and the third flips it true. At the moment `reassessment_required` first flips true, the engagement's `reassessment_due_date` is pulled forward to the logical-clock date advanced by 90 days, overwriting any 365-day date a submission had previously set; the accelerated date is never pushed back out again. For contrast, a submission on an engagement with no triggering changes sets `reassessment_due_date` to the clock date advanced by 365 days and leaves `reassessment_required` false.

The final act is planning next year. Recording the reassessment plan is lead-only and available only from engagement `status` `SUBMITTED`; attempting it earlier answers `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. The payload carries `target_year`, which must equal the engagement's `assessment_year` plus 1, and `planned_start_date`, which must fall on or after the logical-clock date; either breach is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REASSESSMENT_YEAR`. On success the engagement's `next_assessment_start_date` is written from `planned_start_date`, the engagement moves `SUBMITTED → REASSESSMENT_PLANNED`, its `posture` stays `FROZEN`, and its `version` advances by 1. `REASSESSMENT_PLANNED` is the engagement's terminal state, and reaching it has one further consequence worth noting: a `REASSESSMENT_PLANNED` engagement drops out of its lead's `lead_engagement_count` and out of the fieldwork-calendar conflict test of §3.2, freeing that assessor's diary for the following year's work.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The table below enumerates every endpoint the service exposes. Paths are given exactly as they must be routed; there is no version segment and no common prefix, so every route sits at the top level. Any request that brings a brand-new resource into existence answers `201 Created` and returns the created resource in `data` — this covers the creation of assessors, ASV vendors, engagements, network segments, system components, sampling plans, requirement assessments, evidence items, compensating control worksheets, reports on compliance, scan cycles, scan findings and change-log entries. Every other successful request, including all reads, all `PATCH` updates, all state transitions expressed as a `POST` to a named sub-resource, and the clock control, answers `200 OK` and returns the affected resource in `data`. The Authorized Role(s) column names the roles permitted to call the endpoint; "All roles" means all six of `LeadAssessor`, `Assessor`, `QaReviewer`, `AsvVendor`, `ClientRepresentative` and `PlatformAdmin`.
