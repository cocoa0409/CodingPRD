# Cosmetics Facility Registration & Product Listing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Cosmetics regulation has quietly become one of the most paperwork-dense corners of consumer-product oversight. An establishment that manufactures or processes cosmetics must keep a live registration on file, refresh it on a biennial cadence, and tell the agency promptly when the owner or operator changes hands. Every product that leaves that establishment must be listed with its ingredient inventory and its category code, must name a responsible person whose contact details appear on the label, and must be backed by a safety-substantiation dossier that a qualified assessor has signed. On top of that sits a continuous obligation stream: adverse events must be recorded and, when serious, reported inside a fixed deadline; good-manufacturing-practice readiness must survive inspection; and when a product turns out to be hazardous, the sponsor must produce a recall plan, execute a market withdrawal, and hand over distribution records that account for every unit shipped.

Today that work is spread across spreadsheets, shared mailboxes and PDF attachments, and the failure mode is always the same: a deadline that nobody recomputed after a reclassification, a lapsed registration that nobody noticed until an inspector arrived, an allergen that was disclosed on one label revision and not the next. This document specifies the backend service that replaces that patchwork. The service is a single system of record for establishment registration, product listing, safety substantiation, adverse-event recordkeeping, inspection engagement and recall execution, and it takes responsibility for every derived quantity in that chain — period bounds, reporting deadlines, readiness scores, adequacy scores, recovery rates and the enforcement posture that gates whether a product may be marketed at all.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, the service is **deterministic and auditable**: every deadline is measured against a server-held logical clock rather than wall time, every derived number has a pinned evaluation order and rounding boundary, and every compliance-relevant fact is appended to an immutable per-establishment ledger. Second, the service enforces **separation of decision-making duties**: the officer who accepts a recall plan may not be the officer who certifies that the withdrawal worked, and the reviewer who triaged a safety dossier may not be the reviewer who substantively clears it. All endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/cosmetics` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role it is acting under. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined; both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before anything is done with them, so a header holding only whitespace counts as blank and `X-User-ID` is compared in its trimmed form. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, or is a valid role that the addressed endpoint does not authorize, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `FacilityRegistrant`, `ResponsiblePersonAgent`, `RegulatoryReviewer`, `EnforcementOfficer` and `SystemOperator`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `FacilityRegistrant` is the establishment's registrant of record. It creates the facility, submits it for review, files renewals and ownership changes, and answers inspection observations.
- A `ResponsiblePersonAgent` is the label-of-record designee's agent. It creates responsible persons, files listings, ingredients and substantiation dossiers, records and reports adverse events, submits recall plans, records shipments and posts recovery movements.
- A `RegulatoryReviewer` is the agency reviewer. It decides registrations, ownership changes and listings, scores substantiation dossiers, schedules inspections and issues observations.
- An `EnforcementOfficer` is the agency enforcement officer. It opens recall actions, rules on recall plans and runs withdrawal effectiveness reviews.
- A `SystemOperator` is the platform operator. It drives the logical clock and runs the compliance sweep checkpoint.

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary label but the primary key of a persisted resource.

When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityRegistrant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Facility` compare the resource's facility reference to `X-User-ID`. The comparison follows the resource's ancestry: a listing is reached through its `facility_id`, an inspection engagement through its `facility_id`, an ownership-change filing through its `facility_id`, and every resource that descends from a listing — a substantiation dossier, an adverse event, a recall action, a market withdrawal, a distribution record — through the `facility_id` of the listing it hangs from. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Facility creation is the one exception — it is a create-root call with no ownership target, so the check is skipped there, and the `id` the service returns is precisely the value the registrant sends as `X-User-ID` on every subsequent facility call.

When `X-User-Role` is `ResponsiblePersonAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ResponsiblePerson`; ownership checks compare the target listing's `responsible_person_id` to `X-User-ID`. The listing is reached directly where the path names it, and otherwise through the chain: a substantiation dossier through its `listing_id`, an adverse event through its `listing_id`, a recall action through its `listing_id`, a distribution record through its `listing_id`, and a market withdrawal through its `recall_action_id` to that recall action's listing. Reading a responsible person directly is the one case with no listing in the chain: there `X-User-ID` is compared against that responsible person's own `id`, so an agent may read only itself. Creating a responsible person is the create-root exception with no ownership target; creating a listing is a special case in which `X-User-ID` is compared against the `responsible_person_id` supplied in the payload. Violations again yield `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

When `X-User-Role` is `RegulatoryReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — as `screening_reviewer_user_id` or `full_reviewer_user_id` on a `SafetySubstantiation` and as `decided_by_user_id` on an `OwnershipChangeUpdate` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `EnforcementOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `plan_decision_user_id` on a `RecallAction` and as `effectiveness_reviewer_user_id` on a `MarketWithdrawal`, purely so the segregation-of-duties comparisons of §2.12 can be made, and never compares it to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is never compared to any entity's `id` and is never persisted on a resource.

Ownership is checked only on endpoints whose authorized roles include `FacilityRegistrant` or `ResponsiblePersonAgent`. The three opaque roles are never ownership-checked; their authority comes from the role name alone. Two qualifications complete the rule. First, the two **system-wide collections** — the listing register at `GET /cosmetics/listings` and the shipment register at `GET /cosmetics/distribution-records`, the only two collections open to an entity-backed role whose path names no parent resource — authorize all five roles and apply no ownership check whatever, so a registrant or an agent that calls either one sees every record in the system and must narrow the result with that endpoint's own filters; every other endpoint open to an entity-backed role is ownership-checked. Second, where an endpoint is open to both entity-backed roles, the acting role is compared only against the reference its own chain above exposes, and a role whose chain does not reach the addressed resource is simply not checked there: reading an establishment as a `ResponsiblePersonAgent` passes through no listing, so any agent may read any establishment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn at random, per request, from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records: it defaults to 0 and must be at least 0, with no upper bound. A value outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a value that is not a base-ten integer; a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and takes its default, while surrounding whitespace and a leading `+` are tolerated. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error: it answers 200 with an empty `data` array and `has_more` false. The **filter** parameters that individual endpoints accept are read with a different discipline from `limit` and `offset`, and the difference is observable. A filter is applied only when the query string supplies it with a non-empty value, so a filter present but empty — `?status=` — is no filter at all rather than a demand to match the empty string. An applied filter selects by exact, case-sensitive equality against the named field, and a value outside that field's vocabulary is never a schema violation: it simply matches nothing and answers 200 with an empty `data` array. Query parameters an endpoint does not name are ignored. The one filter that is not a string comparison is §3.3's `is_fragrance_allergen`, read as that section describes.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states a different order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where §4 names a different ordering — registration periods in ascending `sequence_index`, ledger entries in ascending `sequence_no`, inspection engagements in ascending `window_start`, ownership-change filings and substantiation dossiers and withdrawal rounds newest first — that ordering governs instead. Because the logical clock advances only when an operator moves it, records routinely share a `created_at` to the millisecond, so the tie-break is part of the contract: records carrying equal sort keys are ordered by the sequence in which the service inserted them, earliest insertion first, and a newest-first list reverses both the key and that tie-break, so the most recently inserted of a tied group leads. The same insertion tie-break settles engagements that share a `window_start`; `sequence_index` and `sequence_no` are unique within their parent and never tie.

For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective skip count), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before pagination) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource the service persists carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Identifiers are identity only: no business rule anywhere in this document branches on the textual content of an `id`. Clients never supply an `id`; a payload that tries to is simply ignored on that key.

Thirteen of the fourteen resource types also carry the full set of audit columns. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, again with three fractional digits, and it is refreshed on **every** mutation — including mutations the resource suffers as a cascaded side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation, cascaded mutations included. The increment is counted once per request rather than once per write: a resource that several hops of a single request mutate rises by exactly 1 in total, even though every one of those hops refreshes its `updated_at`. One write path departs from the `version` half of that rule, and only that one: the per-item contribution write described in §3.4 refreshes an evidence item's `updated_at` without moving its `version`.

The `ComplianceLedgerEntry` resource is the exception. Ledger entries are append-only facts, never revised, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a ledger entry, and no endpoint exists to update or delete one.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and All Date Arithmetic

The service does not read wall-clock time. It holds a **logical clock**, and every deadline comparison, every window containment test, every period-bound derivation and every timestamp the service mints reads that clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That is a mandatory startup default; the server must never fall back to real time, not even on first boot. A `SystemOperator` may move the clock to any UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`; the call answers with the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it displaced, and performs no state transitions of its own.

Instants are rendered in two distinct shapes, and which shape a given field uses is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. The `created_at` and `updated_at` audit columns of §2.4 and the `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 always carry exactly three fractional digits. Every other instant the service renders — including the `current_utc` and `previous_utc` the clock call echoes, the sweep's `swept_at`, a ledger entry's `occurred_at`, and the domain instants `filed_at`, `filing_deadline_at`, `decided_at`, `received_at`, `deadline_basis_at`, `deadline_at`, `reclassified_at`, `submitted_at`, `response_due_at`, `response_submitted_at`, `plan_submitted_at` and `plan_decision_at` — omits the fractional part altogether when the instant carries no sub-second component, and otherwise carries exactly three fractional digits, any finer precision in a client-supplied value being truncated rather than rounded. So an instant taken from a logical clock standing on a whole second renders as `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, whereas a deadline derived by pinning a calendar date to the end of its day always renders as `2026-08-20T23:59:59.999Z`. On input the parse is permissive in a fixed way: a client-supplied instant may be written as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.ffffff`, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS`, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` or a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` that is read as midnight, with either `T` or a space separating the two halves, and with a trailing `Z`, `+00:00`, `-00:00` or no zone marker at all — every one of those is read as the same UTC instant. A calendar-date input field accepts exactly the same set of spellings and keeps only the date part, so a full instant supplied where a date is expected is accepted and truncated to its day. Any other spelling, a non-UTC offset included, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two families of comparison coexist and must not be confused. Registration periods, renewal windows, grace windows, inspection windows and distribution windows are **calendar-date** quantities in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and every comparison against them uses the UTC calendar date of the logical clock, never its time-of-day component. Reporting deadlines and response deadlines are **millisecond-resolution instants**, and comparisons against them use the full logical-clock instant. Where a deadline is derived from a calendar date, the derivation pins the instant to the very end of that day — 23:59:59.999Z — so that a filing made at any point during the deadline day is on time.

Interval inclusivity is deliberately non-uniform across the domain, and each rule states its own convention: registration periods and distribution windows are **closed-closed** (both endpoints inside the interval), while inspection windows are **half-open** with an exclusive upper bound. §3 restates the convention wherever it matters.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary-style and score-style quantity in this system is carried to **two decimal places** and rounded **half up**. That applies to `serious_event_points`, `event_points`, `adequacy_score`, `screening_contribution`, `full_contribution`, `readiness_score`, every value inside `category_subscores`, `reported_recovery_rate`, `provisional_verified_rate`, `verified_recovery_rate` and `effectiveness_rate_final`. Those two decimals are a property of the stored value and of the arithmetic rather than of the wire format: every one of these quantities is emitted as a JSON number and never as a string, so trailing zeros do not survive serialization — a stored 100.00 appears as `100.0` and a stored 9.50 as `9.5`, just as the four-decimal `concentration_pct` 0.0010 appears as `0.001`.

Where a rounding boundary sits inside a multi-step formula, the position of that boundary is part of the specification and not an implementation detail. Three rules pin it explicitly, and §3 gives each one in full:

- Adverse-event points are rounded **per event** and the already-rounded per-event contributions are then summed; summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different, incorrect answer.
- GMP readiness weights each category subscore and rounds **each weighted term** before the five terms are summed; the sum is then rounded again.
- Substantiation adequacy rounds **each evidence item's value**, rounds again after the read-across discount is applied, and rounds the final total.

Unit quantities — everything named `units_*`, `shipped_units_total`, `adjusted_denominator_units`, `observation_count`, `records_produced_count` and the various counters — are whole integers and are never rounded. The one integer truncation in the system is the unreachable-consignee credit of §3.8, which takes the **floor** to whole units. Ingredient concentrations are stored with **four** decimal places and compared at that stored precision.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Superseding Writes

Three write operations in this system supersede a predecessor record as part of their normal work. Because a caller that acts on a stale snapshot of the parent would silently discard a record it never observed, each of those three operations requires the caller to declare the parent's `version` it last read. The declared version is a **non-optional** input field, and if it does not equal the parent's current `version` the write is rejected before the operation's own state preconditions are evaluated.

**Filing an ownership change.** A `POST /cosmetics/facilities/{id}/ownership-changes` request must carry `expected_facility_version` matching the target facility's current `version`, obtained from a prior read of that facility. If the facility's version has advanced since the caller last read it, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check runs after authentication, role authorization, resource existence and the ownership check, and before every other business validation on the facility. On success the service supersedes each prior filing that is still open, inserts the new filing, and increments the facility's `version` by exactly 1 — which means replaying the identical request with the now-stale version fails, so ownership-change filing is deliberately not idempotent.

**Transferring a responsible-person designation.** A `POST /cosmetics/listings/{id}/transfer-responsible-person` request must carry `expected_listing_version` matching the target listing's current `version`, obtained from a prior read of that listing. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LISTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The version check runs immediately after the ownership check and **before** the designation-cap check, so a caller holding a stale version receives the stale-version rejection rather than `ERR_DESIGNATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. On success the outgoing designee's `active_designation_count` is decremented, the incoming designee's is incremented, and the listing's `version` is incremented by exactly 1.

**Opening a withdrawal round.** A `POST /cosmetics/recall-actions/{id}/withdrawals` request must carry `expected_recall_action_version` matching the target recall action's current `version`, obtained from a prior read of that recall action. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECALL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This check precedes the recall-status check, so a stale version wins over `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`. On success the predecessor withdrawal becomes `SUPERSEDED`, its distribution-record links are released, the new `OPEN` withdrawal is inserted, and the recall action's `version` is incremented by exactly 1.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three neither require nor honour an expected-version input.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence: the Order in Which Checks Run

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the fixed order below, and the first violated check produces the response. This ordering is normative — it is not a convenience and not "roughly" the order.

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Existence of the addressed resources, resolved left to right along the URL as described in §2.9: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_RESPONSIBLE_PERSON_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_RECALL_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
4. Ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
5. Request-schema conformance — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A request body that is absent, unparseable or not a JSON object is read as the empty object, so it violates this check only through the fields it then fails to supply, and a field present with an explicit `null` counts as absent — a required field so supplied is rejected, an optional one takes its documented default. A required string that is empty or whitespace-only is a violation, as is a value of the wrong JSON type, a value outside a stated range, and a number carrying more decimal places than the field allows. A JSON number whose fractional part is zero satisfies an integer field, a numeric string satisfies a decimal-valued field, and a boolean satisfies neither. Keys the operation does not name are ignored. Every free-text string field in this document is also bounded above, and a longer value is a violation of this same check: the ceiling is 200 characters for `facility_name`, `owner_operator_name`, `new_owner_operator_name`, `legal_name`, `product_name`, `assessor_name` and `consignee_name`; 300 for `us_address_line` and `label_contact_value`; 64 for `state_or_province`; 16 for `category_code`; 120 for `inci_name`; 32 for `cas_number`; 40 for `lot_code`; 500 for `citation`; 1000 for `event_description`, every `decision_note` and the delisting `reason`; 2000 for `report_narrative`, `response_summary` and `corrective_summary`; and 100 for the payload identifiers `facility_id`, `responsible_person_id` and `distribution_record_id`. `contact_phone` runs from 7 to 32 characters and `contact_email` from 3 to 254.
6. Category-code membership on listing creation — `ERR_UNKNOWN_CATEGORY_CODE`.
7. Window-bound ordering — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_BOUNDS`.
8. The three stale-version checks of §2.7, in the order `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_LISTING_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_RECALL_VERSION`.
9. Governing-facility registration admissibility — `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED`.
10. Governing-facility frozen posture — `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Ranks 9 and 10 are evaluated only on those operations whose own description in §3 names them; every other operation proceeds whatever the governing establishment's `registration_status` and `compliance_posture`, so — to take the three easiest to misread — a listing may be created, an ingredient added and a substantiation dossier opened under a `FROZEN` establishment even though submitting any of them is refused.
11. The per-entity state-admissibility checks, in this order: `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SUBSTANTIATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WITHDRAWAL_STATE`.
12. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
13. The listing-submission safety gates, in this order: `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_NOT_ADEQUATE`, `ERR_ALLERGEN_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_USE_LABEL_REQUIRED`, `ERR_LABEL_CONTACT_REQUIRED`; then the dossier gates `ERR_NO_REVIEW_ITEMS` and `ERR_FULL_REVIEW_REQUIRED_FOR_DATA_GAP`.
14. The temporal gates, in this order: `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_EXPIRED`, `ERR_RECEIVED_AT_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_REGISTRATION_PERIOD`, `ERR_INSPECTION_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
15. The collection-shaped constraints, in this order: `ERR_INSPECTION_TYPE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_LOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_INGREDIENT`, `ERR_ABBREVIATED_RENEWAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_OPEN_RECALL_BLOCKS_DELIST`, `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_SAME_PERSON`.
16. The referential checks, in this order: `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RECORD_LISTING_MISMATCH`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_IN_WITHDRAWAL`, `ERR_NO_DISTRIBUTION_RECORDS`.
17. The capacity and conservation checks, last of all, in this order: `ERR_DESIGNATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_LOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_RECORDED`.

Five consequences of that ordering are worth calling out, because each one is a place where the intuitive answer is the wrong one.

The segregation-of-duties check sits **after** the withdrawal-state check, not before it. A barred enforcement officer who runs an effectiveness review against a withdrawal that is still `OPEN` therefore receives `ERR_INVALID_WITHDRAWAL_STATE` (HTTP 409), not the 403 — the 403 only surfaces once the withdrawal has reached a reviewable state.

The listing-state check strictly outranks the allergen-completeness gate. A `RETURNED` listing with an undisclosed allergen that is resubmitted reports `ERR_ALLERGEN_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, because `RETURNED` is an admissible submit origin; a `DELISTED` listing with the same undisclosed allergen reports `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE` instead.

The inspection-period containment check strictly outranks the window-overlap check. A requested inspection window that both falls outside the facility's current registration period **and** overlaps an existing engagement reports `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_REGISTRATION_PERIOD`.

The same-type buffer check strictly outranks the raw overlap check. Two overlapping windows of the **same** `inspection_type` report `ERR_INSPECTION_TYPE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`; two overlapping windows of **different** types report `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

The record-linkage check strictly outranks the outstanding-units cap. A recovery movement naming a distribution record that is not linked to the addressed withdrawal reports `ERR_RECORD_NOT_IN_WITHDRAWAL` even when the movement's unit count would also have exceeded that record's outstanding balance.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

Paths in this API nest, and nested identifiers are resolved strictly **left to right**. The leftmost unresolved segment produces the error. A request naming an inspection engagement beneath a facility that does not exist reports `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the engagement lookup; a request naming an engagement that exists but belongs to a different facility than the one in the path reports `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, because the engagement must resolve *within* the facility named to its left. The addressed resource's own identifier is always the path variable `{id}`; a nested child keeps an explicit name, and the only such case in this API is `{inspection_id}`.

Identifiers supplied in payloads rather than paths are resolved with the same discipline and in payload order. When a records-production payload names several `distribution_record_ids`, they are validated in the order the payload lists them, so the first unresolvable identifier decides the error; a member that is not an identifier string at all halts that scan where it stands and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, unless an earlier member had already failed to resolve. A recovery entry names exactly one record, through the singular field `distribution_record_id`, and it too is resolved ahead of the schema check, so an identifier that matches no record is `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` even when the same payload is faulty in other ways.

### 2.10. Compliance Posture: the Enforcement Dial and Its Cascades

Each establishment carries a derived `compliance_posture` with three values in increasing severity: `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`. Posture is never set directly by a client. It is recomputed after every mutation that can move one of its inputs, and it is the single dial through which enforcement history reaches day-to-day product operations. A recomputation counts as a mutation of the establishment whether or not it changes the stored value — the establishment's `updated_at` and `version` move on every one of them — whereas a `POSTURE_CHANGE` ledger entry is appended only where the operation's own description in §3 says one is.

Posture is derived from two independent readings that are then combined. The **points reading** is `FROZEN` when the establishment's `serious_event_points` has reached 25.00 or more, `RESTRICTED` when it has reached 10.00 or more but not 25.00, and `OPEN` below 10.00. Both thresholds are inclusive at the boundary: exactly 10.00 is `RESTRICTED` and exactly 25.00 is `FROZEN`. The **override reading** is `FROZEN` when the establishment's `registration_status` is `LAPSED`, or when any recall action anywhere under the establishment's listings has reached `ESCALATED_MANDATORY`; it is `RESTRICTED` when any inspection engagement of the establishment has reached `ESCALATED_OAI`; and it is `OPEN` otherwise. The resulting `compliance_posture` is the more severe of the two readings, ranking `OPEN` below `RESTRICTED` below `FROZEN`. An establishment sitting at zero points with a single `ESCALATED_OAI` engagement is therefore `RESTRICTED`, and an establishment at zero points with a single `ESCALATED_MANDATORY` recall is `FROZEN`.

**The revalidation cascade.** Whenever a posture recomputation *raises* the posture out of `OPEN` — into either `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — the service must invalidate the establishment's cleared safety work. Every safety-substantiation dossier under any of the establishment's listings whose status is `ADEQUATE` moves to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, and each such listing's `safety_status` moves to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` with its `marketable` flag becoming false. Dossiers are visited in ascending order of the owning listing's `created_at`, and within a listing in ascending order of the dossier's own `created_at`. A posture recomputation that *lowers* the posture never reverses this cascade: once a dossier has been pushed to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, only a fresh submission and a fresh review decision can clear it again.

**Cascade discipline generally.** Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource. Where §3 describes a cascade in numbered hops, the hops execute in the stated order within the same request, every resource each hop touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1, and the ordering rule for multi-row hops (almost always ascending `created_at`) is part of the contract because the ledger entries the cascade appends must appear in that same order.

### 2.11. The Append-Only Compliance Ledger

Each establishment owns an append-only ledger of compliance facts. Entries are never updated and never deleted; the only way to correct the record is to append a further entry. Each entry names its `facility_id`, classifies itself with an `entry_type`, points at its subject through the pair `subject_entity` (naming the entity type: one of `Facility`, `ProductListing`, `AdverseEvent`, `InspectionEngagement`, `RecallAction`, `MarketWithdrawal`, `DistributionRecord` or `OwnershipChangeUpdate`) and `subject_id` (the identifier of that subject, resolved against whichever type `subject_entity` names — the pointer is polymorphic and therefore not a declared foreign key), carries a human-readable `detail`, records the logical-clock instant of the fact in `occurred_at`, and takes a `sequence_no` that is a 1-based monotonic counter assigned per establishment in append order. An entry is appended only where the operation's own description in §3 says one is; every other operation leaves the ledger untouched, so an operation §3 describes without naming a ledger entry — opening a dossier, attaching evidence, adding an ingredient, recording a shipment, moving a designation, scheduling an engagement, opening a recall or a withdrawal round among them — appends nothing at all.

The `entry_type` vocabulary is `POSTURE_CHANGE`, `UNIT_MOVEMENT`, `STATE_TRANSITION`, `DEADLINE_BREACH`, `ADVERSE_EVENT_REPORT`, `INSPECTION_OUTCOME`, `RECALL_DECISION`, `WITHDRAWAL_CLOSURE` and `OWNERSHIP_CHANGE`.

Two entry classes carry extra structured content. A `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry records `posture_before` and `posture_after`, each holding the `compliance_posture` value immediately before and immediately after the recorded fact; on every other entry class both fields are null. A `UNIT_MOVEMENT` entry records `signed_units_delta`, positive when units become verified-recovered and negative when a verified movement is reversed; on every other entry class `signed_units_delta` is 0.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties

Two decision pairs in this system must be made by different people, and both are enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against an identifier the service captured earlier. Both produce `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The first pair concerns recalls. The enforcement officer whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `plan_decision_user_id` when the recall plan was decided may not afterwards run the effectiveness review on **any** withdrawal round under that recall action, under either basis. A second, distinct enforcement officer must perform the review, and that officer's identifier is stored as `effectiveness_reviewer_user_id` on the withdrawal.

The second pair concerns safety dossiers. The reviewer whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `screening_reviewer_user_id` when a screening decision was issued may not afterwards issue a decision under the full review mode on the same dossier. Because a screening pass by one reviewer forces a different reviewer for the full pass, and because the full variant can turn an adequate screening result into a deficient one, this rule is the point at which a second pair of eyes actually changes the outcome. The rule bites only in that direction: a screening decision after a full decision is not restricted, and the full reviewer's identifier is captured separately as `full_reviewer_user_id`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishment Registration and the Biennial Renewal Cadence

Everything in this system hangs off a registered establishment, so the registration lifecycle is the natural place to start. A `FacilityRegistrant` opens the file by creating the establishment record, supplying the legal `facility_name`, the `owner_operator_name` of record, the regulated activity as `facility_type` (`MANUFACTURER`, `PROCESSOR` or `BOTH`), the two-letter uppercase `country_code`, and the sub-national `state_or_province`. Creation is a create-root call: it answers 201 with the new establishment, whose `id` the registrant then presents as `X-User-ID` on every later call touching that establishment or anything beneath it. A newly created establishment begins with `registration_status` at `DRAFT`, `compliance_posture` at `OPEN`, `serious_event_points` at 0.00, `registration_cycle_count` at 0, `abbreviated_renewal_eligible` true, `listed_product_count` and `open_serious_event_count` at 0, and `facility_fei`, `gmp_readiness_score`, `last_inspection_outcome`, `current_period_start`, `current_period_end`, `renewal_window_open_date` and `grace_end_date` all null. Its `version` is 1.

Submitting the registration for agency review is admissible only from `DRAFT`; from any other registration state the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE` (HTTP 409). The submission itself carries no payload, but it does re-assert that `facility_name`, `owner_operator_name`, `facility_type`, `country_code` and `state_or_province` are all present and non-blank. The transition `DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW` is the whole of the operation's effect, plus the customary bump of the establishment's `version` by exactly 1.

A `RegulatoryReviewer` then decides the pending file, supplying `decision` — either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` — and optionally a `decision_note`, which is checked for type and length and then discarded: the establishment has no field to hold it, so no response ever echoes it back. Both branches require `registration_status` to be `PENDING_REVIEW`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`. A rejection moves the establishment `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, which is terminal: no product listing may ever be created against a rejected establishment, and none of the renewal paths can revive it. A rejection appends one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry whose `subject_entity` is `Facility` and whose `subject_id` is the establishment, and increments `version` by 1.

Approval is the heavier branch and moves the establishment `PENDING_REVIEW → REGISTERED`. It mints the establishment's registration number into `facility_fei`, formatted as the literal prefix `FEI-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded ordinal; the ordinal is one greater than the number of establishments that already carry a `facility_fei`, so registration numbers are handed out strictly in the order approvals are granted rather than in the order the establishments were created — the first establishment ever approved carries `FEI-000001` and the next `FEI-000002` even if it was created first — and because an assigned number is never cleared or reassigned the sequence has neither gaps nor reuse. Approval also mints the establishment's first coverage window as a registration period with `period_kind` `INITIAL`, `sequence_index` 1 and `status` `ACTIVE`, whose `period_start` is the UTC calendar date of the logical clock at the moment of approval. `registration_cycle_count` rises by 1, the four derived date fields on the establishment are refreshed from the newly active period, `compliance_posture` is recomputed (which for a freshly approved establishment yields `OPEN`), one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry is appended recording that the registration was approved, and `version` increases by 1.

**How coverage windows are derived.** A registration term runs for **two years**, and the arithmetic that turns a `period_start` into a `period_end` is fixed. Take the start date's year and add two; keep the start date's month; take the day of month, but if that day does not exist in the target month, clamp it down to the last day that does exist in that month. That anchor date is the first day *after* coverage, so `period_end` is the anchor date minus one day — registration periods are closed-closed, meaning both `period_start` and `period_end` are days inside the coverage window. A period beginning 2026-08-01 therefore ends 2028-07-31. A period beginning 2026-02-29 anchors on 2028-02-29 (the day exists in that leap month, so no clamping) and ends 2028-02-28. A period beginning 2024-08-31 anchors on 2026-08-31 and ends 2026-08-30. From `period_end`, two further dates fall out: `renewal_window_open_date` is 90 days before `period_end`, and `grace_end_date` is 60 days after `period_end`. For the 2026-08-01 example those are 2028-05-02 and 2028-09-29 respectively. Each period also records the establishment it belongs to through its `facility_id`, and carries a `sequence_index` that is 1 for the initial period and one greater than its predecessor's for every successor.

The establishment mirrors the currently active period so that clients need not join: `current_period_start` and `current_period_end` copy the active period's bounds, and `renewal_window_open_date` and `grace_end_date` copy its two derived dates. All four are written at the moment a period is minted — at approval and at each renewal — and are never cleared again, so they read null only until the establishment's first period exists: when the sweep expires a period for want of a renewal the establishment goes on showing that now-`EXPIRED` period's four dates, which is precisely what the renewal and grace comparisons then read. A period's `status` is `ACTIVE` while it is the coverage window in force and becomes `EXPIRED` either when a successor period is minted or when the sweep observes that the establishment has run past `grace_end_date` without renewing. Registration periods are read back in ascending `sequence_index` order — the audit trail of the biennial cadence — and the listing may be narrowed with a `status` filter.

**Filing a renewal.** Renewal is the registrant's own operation and answers 201 with the newly minted period. The caller supplies `renewal_kind`, which is either `STANDARD` or `ABBREVIATED`. Four business conditions guard the call, and because more than one can be violated at once their relative order matters: the establishment's `registration_status` must be one of `REGISTERED`, `RENEWAL_DUE` or `LAPSED`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_FACILITY_STATE`; then the UTC calendar date of the logical clock must be on or after `renewal_window_open_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 422); then that same date must be on or before `grace_end_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422); and last, an `ABBREVIATED` renewal additionally requires `abbreviated_renewal_eligible` to be true, or the request fails with `ERR_ABBREVIATED_RENEWAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). A `STANDARD` renewal is always available while the window and grace conditions hold.

An accepted renewal expires the currently `ACTIVE` period and mints its successor. The predecessor it measures from is that `ACTIVE` period, or — when the establishment has already lapsed and the sweep expired it, so no period is `ACTIVE` at all — the establishment's period carrying the highest `sequence_index`; because minting a period always expires its predecessor first, those two rules never name different periods. The successor's `period_start` is always the predecessor's `period_end` plus one day — the cadence is gap-free and never drifts, even when the filing itself arrives late — and its `period_end`, `renewal_window_open_date` and `grace_end_date` follow the same two-year derivation described above. The successor's `period_kind` is `RENEWAL` when the logical clock's calendar date is on or before the predecessor's `period_end`, and `LATE_RENEWAL` when that date is after the predecessor's `period_end`. `sequence_index` is the predecessor's plus one, `registration_cycle_count` rises by 1, `registration_status` is set to `REGISTERED`, the establishment's four derived date fields and its `compliance_posture` are recomputed, and the establishment's `version` rises by 1. The ledger footprint depends on which status the renewal was filed from. A renewal filed from `REGISTERED` or `RENEWAL_DUE` appends exactly one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry recording that the renewal was accepted. A renewal filed from `LAPSED` appends **no** such entry: it takes instead the `POSTURE_CHANGE`-led footprint of the compensating cascade described below, so there is no ledger fact anywhere in the establishment's history that names a late renewal as a renewal acceptance. Consider the canonical sequence: a period running 2026-08-01 through 2028-07-31 is renewed on 2028-06-15, inside its window; the successor starts 2028-08-01, ends 2030-07-31, opens its own renewal window on 2030-05-02, has `grace_end_date` 2030-09-29, `period_kind` `RENEWAL` and `sequence_index` 2. Had the same establishment instead renewed on 2028-09-10 — after coverage ended but inside grace — the successor would still start 2028-08-01 and end 2030-07-31, but its `period_kind` would be `LATE_RENEWAL`.

Two of the renewal edges are worth naming explicitly because they are easy to miss. `RENEWAL_DUE → REGISTERED` is the ordinary in-window renewal. But a registrant may also renew while the establishment is still `REGISTERED` — the renewal window has opened and the sweep has not yet flipped the advisory status — and that produces the self-loop `REGISTERED → REGISTERED`, with exactly the same period-minting side effects and the same increment to `registration_cycle_count`. `RENEWAL_DUE` is advisory only: it never blocks any product operation, and every rule in this document that requires an admissible governing registration treats `REGISTERED` and `RENEWAL_DUE` identically.

**Lapse and its four-hop cascade.** When coverage runs out without a renewal, the sweep moves the establishment `REGISTERED → LAPSED` or `RENEWAL_DUE → LAPSED`, the condition being that the logical clock's calendar date is strictly after `current_period_end`. Lapse is the most consequential single transition in the system, and it executes as four ordered hops inside one request. Hop 1 sets `registration_status` to `LAPSED` and recomputes `compliance_posture`, which necessarily becomes `FROZEN` because a lapsed registration is one of the posture overrides. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, walks every product listing of the establishment whose `listing_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED`, in ascending listing `created_at` order, and moves each to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `FACILITY_LAPSED`. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, decrements the `active_designation_count` of each suspended listing's designee by 1. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, flips any designee whose `active_designation_count` has reached 0 to `status` `DORMANT`. Alongside those hops the establishment's `listed_product_count` is recomputed, every affected listing's `marketable` flag becomes false, and the `ACTIVE` registration period's `status` becomes `EXPIRED`. The ledger receives one `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry carrying `posture_before` and `posture_after` `FROZEN`, followed by one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per suspended listing in the same hop-2 order, and last — because the revalidation cascade named below fires in the same request — one further `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per revalidated listing in ascending listing `created_at` order, so a listing that was both suspended and revalidated takes two entries. The establishment's `version` and the `version` of every touched listing and designee each rise by exactly 1. Because the posture move is an escalation out of `OPEN`, the revalidation cascade of §2.10 also fires in the same request.

**Late renewal and its compensating cascade.** An establishment that has lapsed is not lost: a renewal filed while the logical clock's calendar date is still on or before `grace_end_date` moves it `LAPSED → REGISTERED`, mints the successor period with `period_kind` `LATE_RENEWAL` and a gap-free `period_start`, and recomputes `compliance_posture` — which lifts out of `FROZEN` unless accumulated points or an outstanding escalation still hold it there. The compensation then runs in three hops: hop 1 returns every listing of the establishment whose `listing_status` is `SUSPENDED` **and** whose `suspension_reason` is `FACILITY_LAPSED` to `LISTED` with `suspension_reason` cleared to null; hop 2 re-increments each restored listing's designee `active_designation_count`; hop 3 flips any designee that was `DORMANT` back to `status` `ACTIVE`. `listed_product_count` and each restored listing's `marketable` flag are recomputed, and the ledger receives a `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry followed by one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per restored listing in ascending listing `created_at` order. The exclusion is deliberate and absolute: a listing suspended with `suspension_reason` `RECALL_ESCALATION` is **not** restored by a late renewal, because the reason it was suspended has nothing to do with the registration lapse.

Establishments themselves are read individually by anyone, and the agency-side roles may browse the whole register, narrowing it with a `registration_status` filter, a `compliance_posture` filter, or both. The compliance ledger of an establishment reads back in ascending `sequence_no` order and accepts an `entry_type` filter and a `subject_entity` filter for reconciliation work.

### 3.2. Owner-Operator Changes and Abbreviated-Renewal Eligibility

Establishments change hands, and the agency expects to hear about it promptly rather than at the next renewal. A `FacilityRegistrant` files the change against its own establishment, supplying the incoming `new_owner_operator_name`, the calendar date `change_effective_date` on which the transfer actually took effect, and `expected_facility_version` carrying the `version` it last read from that establishment. The call answers 201 with the new filing. As §2.7 sets out, the version field is non-optional and a mismatch against the establishment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before any state condition on the establishment is examined; the reason is visible in the side effects below — a filing supersedes its predecessors, so a caller reasoning from a stale snapshot would silently discard a filing it never saw.

On acceptance of the filing itself, the service first moves **every** filing of that establishment still sitting in `FILED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one filing per establishment is ever open, and only then inserts the new row. The new filing starts in `status` `FILED`, records the establishment through its `facility_id`, stamps `filed_at` with the logical-clock instant at which the server accepted it, and derives `filing_deadline_at` as the end of the day 60 days after `change_effective_date` — that is, the instant 23:59:59.999Z on that day, so a filing made at any point during the deadline day is on time. The boolean `is_late_filing` is true exactly when `filed_at` is strictly after `filing_deadline_at`, and false otherwise, including the boundary case where the two are equal. Both `decision_note` and `decided_by_user_id` are null while the filing is `FILED` or `SUPERSEDED`. The establishment's own `version` rises by exactly 1, which is why replaying the identical filing request fails: ownership-change filing is deliberately not idempotent.

A `RegulatoryReviewer` rules on the open filing with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_note`. Either ruling requires the filing's `status` to be `FILED`; a filing that has already been decided or superseded yields `ERR_INVALID_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a filing identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Both rulings store the supplied `decision_note` and capture the deciding reviewer's `X-User-ID` in `decided_by_user_id`.

The two branches diverge in what they do to the establishment. `FILED → ACCEPTED` overwrites the establishment's `owner_operator_name` with `new_owner_operator_name`, which turns `abbreviated_renewal_eligible` false through the derivation stated below rather than by storing anything — the flag is computed on every read, so an accepted filing whose `change_effective_date` falls outside the establishment's current coverage window leaves it true, and a renewal that mints a fresh window restores it — appends an `OWNERSHIP_CHANGE` ledger entry whose `subject_entity` is `OwnershipChangeUpdate` and whose `subject_id` is the filing, and increments both the filing's `version` and the establishment's `version` by exactly 1. `FILED → REJECTED` touches neither `owner_operator_name` nor `abbreviated_renewal_eligible`; it records the note and the deciding reviewer and nothing else on the establishment.

Why the eligibility flag matters is a small piece of policy worth stating plainly. An establishment qualifies for the abbreviated renewal path only when two things hold at once: its `compliance_posture` is `OPEN`, and no accepted ownership change of that establishment has a `change_effective_date` falling within the current coverage window — that is, on or after `current_period_start` and on or before `current_period_end`, both endpoints inclusive. A change of ownership mid-period is therefore enough to force the establishment down the `STANDARD` path for the remainder of that period, and so is any posture escalation, since a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` establishment is never abbreviated-eligible.

Filings are read back newest first, optionally narrowed by a `status` filter, so a registrant can see at a glance which of its filings were accepted, rejected or superseded.

### 3.3. Designees, Product Listings and Ingredient Inventories

Before a product can be listed, somebody has to agree to be the name on the label. That party is created as a responsible person by a `ResponsiblePersonAgent`, supplying the `legal_name` that will be printed, a domestic `us_address_line`, a `contact_phone` reachable for adverse-event intake, a `contact_email` for the same purpose (which must carry exactly one `@`, with at least one character on each side of it), and optionally a `designation_cap` — the ceiling on how many listings this party may simultaneously hold in an active designation slot. When `designation_cap` is omitted it defaults to 25, and it may be set anywhere from 1 to 500. Creation answers 201 and is the second create-root call in the system: the returned `id` is the value the agent presents as `X-User-ID` thereafter. A new responsible person starts with `active_designation_count` at 0 and `status` `DORMANT`.

That `status` is never set directly. It is derived: a responsible person is `ACTIVE` whenever `active_designation_count` is 1 or more, and `DORMANT` when it is exactly 0. `active_designation_count` in turn counts the listings designated to that party whose `listing_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED` — no other listing state occupies a designation slot. Every operation in §3.6 that moves a listing into or out of those two states therefore moves a designation count and may flip a `status`, and the read of a single responsible person is the canonical way to observe the live count that the cap is checked against.

**Creating the register entry.** A listing is created in `DRAFT` by its designee's agent. The payload names the manufacturing or processing establishment through `facility_id`, names the designee through `responsible_person_id`, and carries the trade `product_name`, the declared `category_code`, the `product_form` (`LEAVE_ON` or `RINSE_OFF`), the `label_contact_type` (`DOMESTIC_ADDRESS`, `DOMESTIC_PHONE` or `ELECTRONIC_CONTACT`) with its literal `label_contact_value`, and three optional fields: `professional_use_only` and `professional_use_statement_present`, each defaulting to false, and `lot_authorized_units`, the per-lot unit ceiling, defaulting to 0. Because listing creation has no pre-existing ownership target, the ownership check compares `X-User-ID` against the payload's `responsible_person_id` rather than against a stored value; a `responsible_person_id` that resolves to nothing is `ERR_RESPONSIBLE_PERSON_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a `facility_id` that resolves to nothing is reported under the listing existence check as `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The governing establishment must have `registration_status` `REGISTERED` or `RENEWAL_DUE`; a `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `REJECTED` or `LAPSED` establishment cannot acquire new listings, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 409).

The declared `category_code` must be one of exactly eight registry codes, matched as a case-sensitive exact string; anything else is `ERR_UNKNOWN_CATEGORY_CODE` (HTTP 422). The registry is `01A` (baby shampoos), `02C` (bath oils and salts), `03B` (eyeliner), `05G` (hair straighteners), `07A` (blushers), `08D` (nail extension preparations), `11E` (skin peels) and `12A` (suntan gels and lotions). Four of those eight — `03B`, `05G`, `08D` and `11E` — are professional-use-permitted; the other four — `01A`, `02C`, `07A` and `12A` — are not, and §3.6 shows where that distinction bites.

A freshly created listing answers 201 and begins with `listing_status` `DRAFT`, `safety_status` `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, `listing_number` and `suspension_reason` both null, `allergen_disclosure_complete` true, `undisclosed_allergen_count` 0, `distributed_units_total` 0, `open_recall_count` 0, `open_serious_event_count` 0, `marketable` false and `version` 1. The `marketable` flag is the single boolean a client can read to answer "may this product be sold?", and it is true only when four conditions hold together: `listing_status` is `LISTED`, `safety_status` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, `allergen_disclosure_complete` is true, and the governing establishment's `compliance_posture` is anything other than `FROZEN`. Every operation in this document that can move one of those four inputs recomputes `marketable`. Listings are browsed with any combination of a `facility_id` filter, a `responsible_person_id` filter, a `listing_status` filter and a `safety_status` filter — the two state dimensions are filtered independently because they evolve independently.

**Ingredient inventories.** Ingredients are added to a listing one at a time, each answering 201, and each carrying the `inci_name` under the International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients, an optional `cas_number`, a `function_code` from `FRAGRANCE`, `PRESERVATIVE`, `COLORANT`, `SURFACTANT`, `ACTIVE` or `OTHER`, and a `concentration_pct` that must be greater than 0, at most 100, and expressed with at most four decimal places. Three booleans complete the row, each defaulting to false: `is_fragrance_allergen` marks the substance as being on the fragrance-allergen list, `label_disclosed` records whether the ingredient is separately named on the label, and `is_data_gap_flag` marks an unresolved toxicological data gap that §3.4 will make expensive. Ingredient names are unique within a listing compared case-insensitively, so a second row whose `inci_name` differs only in letter case is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INGREDIENT` (HTTP 409). Ingredients may be added to a listing in any state except `DELISTED`; a delisted listing rejects the addition with `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE` (HTTP 409). The listing's ingredients are read back in ascending `created_at` order, optionally narrowed by an `is_fragrance_allergen` filter, and each row carries its computed `disclosure_required`. That filter is the one boolean query parameter in the API and it is read permissively in one direction only: trimmed and lower-cased, a value of `true`, `1` or `yes` selects the ingredients whose flag is true, and **every** other non-empty value — `false`, `0`, `no` and equally any unrecognised word — selects the ingredients whose flag is false, an unrecognised spelling being no more a schema violation here than in any other filter.

**Fragrance-allergen disclosure.** Whether an ingredient must be separately named on the label depends on the product form, because a leave-on product sits on skin far longer than a rinse-off one. The disclosure threshold is a concentration of **0.001 percent** for a `LEAVE_ON` listing and **0.01 percent** for a `RINSE_OFF` listing. For each ingredient of the listing, `disclosure_required` is true when all three of the following hold: `is_fragrance_allergen` is true, `concentration_pct` is at or above the applicable threshold — the comparison is inclusive, and it is made against the stored four-decimal value — and the listing's `professional_use_only` is false. Professional-use products are exempt from fragrance-allergen label disclosure entirely, which is why the third clause is part of the per-ingredient derivation rather than a separate rule. From those flags, `undisclosed_allergen_count` is the number of ingredients whose `disclosure_required` is true while `label_disclosed` is false, and `allergen_disclosure_complete` is true exactly when that count is 0.

The vacuous case must resolve to complete, not incomplete: a listing with no ingredients at all, a listing whose fragrance allergens all sit below the threshold, and a professional-use-only listing all report `undisclosed_allergen_count` 0 and `allergen_disclosure_complete` true. Worked through concretely: a `LEAVE_ON` listing carrying an undisclosed allergen at 0.0010 percent has `disclosure_required` true on that row, `undisclosed_allergen_count` 1 and `allergen_disclosure_complete` false; the same row at 0.0009 percent falls below the leave-on threshold and the listing is complete; that same 0.0010 percent row inside a `RINSE_OFF` listing is also below its threshold and the listing is complete, while a `RINSE_OFF` listing with an undisclosed allergen at 0.0100 percent is incomplete; and an undisclosed allergen at 0.5000 percent on a professional-use-only listing is exempt, so the listing is complete. Because the professional-use flag participates in the derivation, the entire ingredient set is re-derived whenever that flag is evaluated — a listing that was complete while professional-use-only can become incomplete once it is treated as a consumer product.

Adding an ingredient therefore does more than insert a row. It computes that row's `disclosure_required`, recomputes the listing's `undisclosed_allergen_count`, `allergen_disclosure_complete` and `marketable`, and refreshes `data_gap_present` on every substantiation dossier of the listing that is not yet `SUPERSEDED` — so an ingredient carrying `is_data_gap_flag` can retroactively change how an already-open dossier must be reviewed. Those recomputed listing figures are derived on read rather than stored, so inserting the row leaves the listing's own `updated_at` and `version` alone, and the `data_gap_present` refresh likewise writes that single field without moving the dossier's `updated_at` or `version`.

### 3.4. Safety Substantiation: Evidence, Two Review Variants and Revalidation

Each product must be backed by a safety-substantiation dossier that a named assessor has signed. The agent opens a dossier against a listing, supplying the `assessor_name` and the methodological `assessment_basis` — `PRODUCT_SPECIFIC`, `INGREDIENT_READ_ACROSS` or `PANEL_MONOGRAPH`. The call answers 201 and may be made against a listing in any state except `DELISTED`, which rejects with `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`. The new dossier records the listing through its `listing_id` and starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `review_mode` null, `adequacy_score` null, `decided_at` null, and both `screening_reviewer_user_id` and `full_reviewer_user_id` null. Its `data_gap_present` is set from the listing's ingredients: it is true exactly when at least one ingredient of the listing carries `is_data_gap_flag`.

Opening a dossier is destructive to its predecessors, and deliberately so — the newest dossier is the only live one. Every dossier of that listing whose `status` is not already `SUPERSEDED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal: neither a further submission nor a review decision is ever accepted on a superseded dossier. In the same request the listing's `safety_status` resets to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `marketable` is recomputed to false — and this holds even when `listing_status` is `LISTED`, so filing a fresh dossier for a marketed product immediately takes that product out of marketable standing. In state-machine terms, the listing's substantiation dimension can enter `UNSUBSTANTIATED` from `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` or `SUBSTANTIATION_FAILED` by this route, and an `ADEQUATE` dossier reaching `SUPERSEDED` this way is the transition that makes it happen.

Evidence is attached to the dossier one item at a time, each answering 201, and each carrying the class of evidence in `evidence_type`, the credential of the attesting toxicologist in `reviewer_credential`, and a bibliographic `citation`. The five evidence classes are `CLINICAL_SAFETY_STUDY`, `IN_VITRO_TOXICOLOGY`, `READ_ACROSS_ANALOG`, `HISTORICAL_USE_DOSSIER` and `LITERATURE_REVIEW`; the four credentials are `BOARD_CERTIFIED_TOXICOLOGIST`, `ERT`, `QUALIFIED_SAFETY_ASSESSOR` and `OTHER`. Each item records the dossier it belongs to through `substantiation_id`, and is created with `screening_contribution` and `full_contribution` both 0.00 — those two numbers are filled in later, by the review decision. Items may be attached only while the dossier is still editable, meaning its `status` is `DRAFT`, `DEFICIENT` or `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`; otherwise the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_SUBSTANTIATION_STATE` (HTTP 409). A dossier identifier that resolves to nothing is `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Items are read back with both per-item contributions visible, optionally narrowed by an `evidence_type` filter.

Submitting the dossier for review carries no payload and is admissible from `DRAFT`, `DEFICIENT` or `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`; from `SUBMITTED`, `ADEQUATE` or `SUPERSEDED` it is `ERR_INVALID_SUBSTANTIATION_STATE`. Two further conditions apply, and the order between them and the state check follows §2.8: the governing establishment's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), and this posture check runs *before* the dossier-state check; the dossier must then hold at least one evidence item, or the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_REVIEW_ITEMS` (HTTP 422), which is checked *after* the state check. A successful submission moves the dossier to `SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_round` by exactly 1 — it counts every entry into `SUBMITTED`, including re-submissions after a deficiency or a revalidation demand, and ranges from 0 to 99 — sets the listing's `safety_status` to `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION`, refreshes `data_gap_present` from the listing's current ingredient set, recomputes `marketable`, and increments both the dossier's `version` and the listing's `version` by 1.

**The two review variants.** A `RegulatoryReviewer` scores the submitted dossier, supplying a single input: `review_mode`, either `SCREENING` or `FULL`. `SCREENING` is an initial triage pass; `FULL` is the substantive review, and it is the one that can flip an apparently adequate dossier to deficient. The decision requires the dossier's `status` to be `SUBMITTED`. When `data_gap_present` is true the mode is not free: only `FULL` is admissible, and a `SCREENING` decision on a data-gap dossier is refused with `ERR_FULL_REVIEW_REQUIRED_FOR_DATA_GAP` (HTTP 422) — a screening pass may not clear a product whose ingredient list carries an unresolved toxicological gap. The segregation rule of §2.12 also applies here: a `FULL` decision may not be issued by the reviewer already recorded in `screening_reviewer_user_id`, and such an attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Both variants begin from the same per-item raw value. Each evidence class carries a weight — 40 for `CLINICAL_SAFETY_STUDY`, 25 for `IN_VITRO_TOXICOLOGY`, 20 for `READ_ACROSS_ANALOG`, 10 for `HISTORICAL_USE_DOSSIER` and 5 for `LITERATURE_REVIEW` — and each credential carries a factor: 1.0 for both `BOARD_CERTIFIED_TOXICOLOGIST` and `ERT`, 0.6 for `QUALIFIED_SAFETY_ASSESSOR` and 0.3 for `OTHER`. An item's value is its evidence weight multiplied by its credential factor, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Under `SCREENING` the score is simply the sum of those per-item values, the total itself rounded half up to two decimal places. There is no per-class cap, no read-across discount, no data-gap penalty and no upper ceiling — a screening score may exceed 100.00.

Under `FULL` three further steps intervene. First, every item whose `evidence_type` is `READ_ACROSS_ANALOG` has its value multiplied by 0.5 and the result rounded half up to two decimal places again — read-across evidence is worth half of what it claims once somebody actually reads it. Second, the item values are grouped by `evidence_type`, summed within each group, and each group total is capped at 40.00, so no single class of evidence can carry a dossier on its own. Third, when `data_gap_present` is true, 15.00 is subtracted from the sum of the capped group totals; the result is floored at 0.00 so the score is never negative, and the final total is rounded half up to two decimal places.

In either mode a score of **60.00 or more** rules the dossier `ADEQUATE` and anything below 60.00 rules it `DEFICIENT`; the pass mark is inclusive at the boundary.

The two variants are not two views of one number, and the difference is observable. A dossier holding a `CLINICAL_SAFETY_STUDY` item attested by a `BOARD_CERTIFIED_TOXICOLOGIST` (40.00) plus an `IN_VITRO_TOXICOLOGY` item attested by an `ERT` (25.00), with no data gap, scores 65.00 under both modes and passes under both. But a dossier holding three `READ_ACROSS_ANALOG` items each attested by a `BOARD_CERTIFIED_TOXICOLOGIST` (20.00 each) plus one `IN_VITRO_TOXICOLOGY` item attested by a `QUALIFIED_SAFETY_ASSESSOR` (25 multiplied by 0.6, giving 15.00) scores 75.00 under `SCREENING` and is `ADEQUATE`, while under `FULL` the three read-across items become 10.00 each, group to 30.00, stay under the 40.00 class cap, and the total is 45.00 — `DEFICIENT`. The same evidence, a 30.00 spread, and opposite outcomes. Two `CLINICAL_SAFETY_STUDY` items attested by board-certified toxicologists plus one `IN_VITRO_TOXICOLOGY` item attested by an `ERT` score 105.00 under `SCREENING` but 65.00 under `FULL`, because the clinical group is capped from 80.00 down to 40.00 — both adequate, still divergent. Credential drag can sink a dossier in both modes: three `CLINICAL_SAFETY_STUDY` items each attested by `OTHER` are worth 12.00 apiece, giving 36.00 under `SCREENING` and, since 36.00 is under the class cap, 36.00 under `FULL` as well — deficient either way. And six `HISTORICAL_USE_DOSSIER` items attested by board-certified toxicologists sum to 60.00 raw, which would pass, but under `FULL` the single class is capped at 40.00 and the dossier is deficient.

Because `review_mode` is caller-supplied, a dossier can legitimately be scored twice under different modes on identical evidence and produce two different values in `adequacy_score`. Whichever decision came last is the one that governs the listing's `safety_status`.

A decision does a fixed set of things regardless of which way it goes. It stores the computed `adequacy_score`, the supplied `review_mode` and `decided_at` at the logical-clock instant of the decision; it records the deciding reviewer's `X-User-ID` in `screening_reviewer_user_id` when the mode was `SCREENING` and in `full_reviewer_user_id` when the mode was `FULL`; and it writes both `screening_contribution` and `full_contribution` onto every evidence item of the dossier, so a client can inspect what each item would be worth under either variant — `full_contribution` holds the item's value after any read-across discount but **before** the per-class cap is applied, since the cap is a property of the group rather than of the item. Those per-item writes refresh each item's `updated_at` to the decision instant but, uniquely among the write paths in this system, leave its `version` alone: an evidence item is mutated by nothing else, so its `version` reads 1 for the whole of its life however many times the dossier is scored. The dossier then moves `SUBMITTED → ADEQUATE` or `SUBMITTED → DEFICIENT`, the listing's `safety_status` becomes `SUBSTANTIATED` or `SUBSTANTIATION_FAILED` correspondingly, `marketable` is recomputed, one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry is appended against the listing as its `subject_entity`, and the `version` of both the dossier and the listing rises by 1.

A deficiency verdict does **not** touch `listing_status`. A product that was `LISTED` stays `LISTED` and merely becomes non-marketable — the two dimensions of a listing's state machine move independently, and no review decision has ever moved the submission dimension.

From `DEFICIENT` the sponsor may revise and resubmit, which is the `DEFICIENT → SUBMITTED` edge: it again requires at least one evidence item, again increments `revision_round`, and again drives the listing's `safety_status` back to `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION`. That closes the review cycle, and it may be traversed as often as the sponsor is willing to try.

**Revalidation.** The other way a dossier loses its cleared standing is the posture cascade of §2.10. When a recomputation raises the establishment's `compliance_posture` out of `OPEN`, every dossier of every listing of that establishment whose `status` is `ADEQUATE` moves `ADEQUATE → REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, each such listing's `safety_status` moves to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, `marketable` becomes false, and one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry recording that revalidation is required is appended per affected listing. Dossiers are visited in ascending order of the owning listing's `created_at`, and within one listing in ascending order of the dossier's own `created_at`. From `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` the sponsor resubmits — the `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED → SUBMITTED` edge — which likewise requires that the establishment's `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN`, increments `revision_round`, and returns the listing's `safety_status` to `UNDER_SUBSTANTIATION`. Nothing else clears a revalidation demand: a later de-escalation of posture, however far it falls, never restores a dossier to `ADEQUATE` on its own.

Dossiers are read individually — the read exposes `adequacy_score`, `review_mode`, `revision_round`, `decided_at` and both reviewer identifiers — and a listing's dossiers are listed newest first, superseded ones included, optionally narrowed by a `status` filter.

### 3.5. Bringing a Listing to Market: Submission Gates, Decisions, Transfers and Delisting

The submission dimension of a listing is where regulatory pressure concentrates, because submitting is the moment at which every other obligation is checked at once. Submission carries no payload. It is admissible from exactly two origins, `DRAFT` and `RETURNED`, and the two edges `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` share identical conditions and identical effects — the second is simply the resubmission that closes the submit-and-return cycle. From `LISTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `DELISTED` the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Seven business conditions guard a submission, and because a badly prepared listing routinely violates several at once, the order in which they are evaluated is normative. First the governing establishment must be admissibly registered — `registration_status` `REGISTERED` or `RENEWAL_DUE` — or the submission fails with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED`. Second the establishment's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or it fails with `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Third the listing's own `listing_status` must be an admissible origin, as just described. Only then do the four safety gates run, in this fixed order: the listing's `safety_status` must be `SUBSTANTIATED`, failing which the submission is refused with `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_NOT_ADEQUATE` (HTTP 422); `allergen_disclosure_complete` must be true, failing which it is refused with `ERR_ALLERGEN_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422); the professional-use labelling condition must hold, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PROFESSIONAL_USE_LABEL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); and the label-contact condition must hold, failing which it is refused with `ERR_LABEL_CONTACT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Last of all comes the capacity check on the designation slot, described below, which yields `ERR_DESIGNATION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Two of those gates need spelling out. The **professional-use labelling condition** is vacuously satisfied whenever `professional_use_only` is false. When it is true, two things must both hold: `professional_use_statement_present` must also be true, because the label has to actually carry the professional-use-only statement, and the listing's `category_code` must be one of the four professional-use-permitted codes of §3.3. A listing declared professional-use-only under `07A` is refused however impeccable its label, because blushers are not a professional-use category; a listing under `03B` with the statement present passes.

The **label-contact condition** is asymmetric between consumer and professional products, which is the point. A consumer listing — `professional_use_only` false — satisfies the requirement only with `label_contact_type` `DOMESTIC_ADDRESS` or `ELECTRONIC_CONTACT`; a phone number alone is not an acceptable adverse-event intake channel for a consumer product, so `DOMESTIC_PHONE` on a consumer listing is refused. A professional-use listing may use any of the three channels, `DOMESTIC_PHONE` included, since the trade is expected to be able to place a call. In both cases `label_contact_value` must be non-blank.

The **designation-slot capacity** check compares the designee that will hold the slot against its own ceiling: the current designee's `active_designation_count` must be strictly less than its `designation_cap`. A designee sitting at exactly its cap blocks the submission. On success the listing moves to `SUBMITTED`, the designee's `active_designation_count` rises by 1 and its `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry is appended against the listing, and the `version` of both the listing and the designee rises by exactly 1.

**The agency's listing decision.** A `RegulatoryReviewer` disposes of a submitted listing with a `decision` of `LIST` or `RETURN` and an optional `decision_note` — again checked and then discarded, the listing having no field to hold it. Either way the listing's `listing_status` must be `SUBMITTED`, and the decision call is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_POSTURE_FROZEN` when the governing establishment's `compliance_posture` is `FROZEN`. `SUBMITTED → LISTED` mints the register number into `listing_number`, formatted as the literal prefix `CPL-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded ordinal that is one greater than the number of listings already carrying a `listing_number`, so register numbers are handed out strictly in the order the agency first accepted each product rather than in the order the products were created, with neither gaps nor reuse — a number once minted is never cleared, so a product that is later delisted goes on occupying its ordinal; the number is minted only on the **first** listing decision, so a product that is later suspended and restored, or delisted and re-registered, keeps the number it was first given. Listing also increments the establishment's `listed_product_count` by 1, recomputes `marketable`, appends a `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry and increments the listing's `version` by 1. `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` sends the file back to the sponsor: it releases the designation slot, decrementing the designee's `active_designation_count` by 1 and flipping its `status` to `DORMANT` if that count reaches 0, appends its own `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry recording the return, and increments the `version` of both the listing and the designee by 1.

**Moving a designation.** A listing's label-of-record designee can be replaced without touching anything else about the product. The agent supplies the incoming `responsible_person_id` and `expected_listing_version`. The transfer is admissible against any listing except a `DELISTED` one, and an incoming designee identifier that resolves to nothing is `ERR_RESPONSIBLE_PERSON_NOT_FOUND` — resolved, note, **before** the addressed listing itself, because the existence step of §2.8 runs its own fixed order of resource types and responsible persons precede listings in it, which is the one place where a payload identifier outranks the identifier in the path: a transfer naming an unknown incoming designee against an unknown listing reports `ERR_RESPONSIBLE_PERSON_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the listing lookup. As §2.7 records, the stale-version rejection `ERR_STALE_LISTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412) is evaluated ahead of the listing-state check and well ahead of the capacity check. Two further conditions apply in this order: the incoming `responsible_person_id` must differ from the listing's current one, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_SAME_PERSON` (HTTP 422); and the **incoming** designee's `active_designation_count` must be strictly below its `designation_cap`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DESIGNATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Note which designee the cap is evaluated against: on a transfer it is the party receiving the designation, whereas on a submission it is the party already designated.

A successful transfer sets the listing's `responsible_person_id` to the incoming designee. If — and only if — the listing currently occupies a designation slot, meaning its `listing_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED`, the outgoing designee's `active_designation_count` is decremented (flipping it to `DORMANT` at 0) and the incoming designee's is incremented (flipping it to `ACTIVE`), so the total number of occupied slots across the system is conserved. A transfer of a `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` listing moves no counts at all. The listing's `version` rises by 1, as does the `version` of each designee the transfer touched. Nothing is appended to the ledger: a change of designee is an administrative substitution rather than a compliance fact, and it leaves both dimensions of the listing's state machine exactly where they stood.

**Suspension is never a client action.** No caller can put a listing into `SUSPENDED`; the state is reached only through the two cascades described elsewhere in this document — hop 2 of a registration lapse, which writes `suspension_reason` `FACILITY_LAPSED`, and hop 2 of a recall escalation, which writes `RECALL_ESCALATION`. Both cascades act only on listings whose `listing_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED`, both release the designation slot, both drop the establishment's `listed_product_count` by 1 when the prior status was `LISTED`, and both leave `marketable` false. The single edge out of `SUSPENDED` back to `LISTED` is the late-renewal compensation of §3.1, and it fires only for `FACILITY_LAPSED` suspensions.

**Delisting.** Withdrawing a product from the register is available to the designee's agent and to an `EnforcementOfficer`, and takes an optional free-text `reason` that is likewise checked and then discarded rather than stored on the listing. It is admissible from `LISTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETURNED`; from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or an already-`DELISTED` listing it is `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`. One business condition guards it: the listing's `open_recall_count` must be 0, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RECALL_BLOCKS_DELIST` (HTTP 409) — a product cannot be quietly removed from the register while an unfinished recall is still attached to it. Delisting sets `listing_status` to `DELISTED`, which is terminal, clears `suspension_reason` to null, decrements the designee's `active_designation_count` by 1 whenever the listing held a slot immediately beforehand (that is, when its prior `listing_status` was `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED`) and flips that designee to `DORMANT` at 0, decrements the establishment's `listed_product_count` by 1 when the prior status was `LISTED`, recomputes `marketable` to false, appends a `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry, and increments the listing's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Adverse-Event Recordkeeping, the Fifteen-Day Report Clock and the Points Engine

Adverse-event recording is deliberately the least gated operation in the system. A `ResponsiblePersonAgent` records an event against one of their listings whatever the listing's `listing_status` and whatever the establishment's `compliance_posture` — a frozen establishment, a lapsed registration or a delisted product must never become a reason not to write down that a consumer was harmed. The payload carries `event_description` (1 to 1000 characters), `received_at` (an ISO 8601 UTC instant), `seriousness` (`SERIOUS` or `NON_SERIOUS`) and an optional `outcome_code` drawn from `DEATH`, `HOSPITALIZATION`, `DISFIGUREMENT`, `SERIOUS_PERSISTENT`, `INFECTION` and `NONE`. One business condition applies: `received_at` must not be after the logical clock, since nobody can have received a report from the future; a later instant is refused with `ERR_RECEIVED_AT_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422).

Every event is born with `report_status` `RECORDED`, and that value is never observable, because the create request immediately applies whichever of the two automatic transitions the declared seriousness selects. On either branch the newly recorded event carries `submitted_at` and `reclassified_at` null, `lateness_days` 0 rather than null, and an omitted `outcome_code` left null. A `NON_SERIOUS` intake takes the `RECORDED → RETAINED` edge: `deadline_basis_at` and `deadline_at` stay null, `event_points` stays 0.00, no open-event counter moves, the establishment's `serious_event_points` and `compliance_posture` are untouched, and `retention_until` is set to the calendar date of `received_at` plus three years — or plus six years when the listing is `professional_use_only`, since professional-channel complaints are held for the longer term. Either term is a calendar-year addition that keeps the month and day of `received_at` and clamps only where the target month is shorter, so an event received on 29 February 2028 retains until 28 February 2031 on the three-year term. A `SERIOUS` intake takes the `RECORDED → REPORT_DUE` edge: `deadline_basis_at` becomes `received_at`, `deadline_at` is computed from it, `retention_until` becomes the calendar date of `received_at` plus six years, and both the listing's and the establishment's `open_serious_event_count` rise by 1.

**The fifteen-day clock.** The report deadline is the end of the fifteenth day after the day of the basis instant: take the calendar date of `deadline_basis_at`, add fifteen days, and pin the result to 23:59:59.999Z. The clock's basis is `reclassified_at` when that field is non-null and `received_at` otherwise. An event received 2026-08-05T10:00:00Z as `SERIOUS` is therefore due by 2026-08-20T23:59:59.999Z, and the time of day at which the report arrived plays no part in choosing the deadline — only in judging whether it was met. The deadline is inclusive: a filing whose `submitted_at` equals `deadline_at` to the millisecond is timely.

**Raising the seriousness of a retained event.** Either a `RegulatoryReviewer` or the listing's own `ResponsiblePersonAgent` may reclassify an event, supplying `seriousness` and optionally a revised `outcome_code`. Neither field steers the operation the way a reader might expect. `seriousness` is non-optional and must spell one of the two values, but its value is not consulted: the escalation always writes `SERIOUS`, so a request naming `NON_SERIOUS` escalates the event exactly as one naming `SERIOUS` does, and the only way to be refused is to address an event the operation does not admit. An `outcome_code` that is omitted or explicitly null leaves whatever code the event already carried in place — a reclassification never clears an outcome, it only overwrites one. The operation is admissible only against an event whose `report_status` is `RETAINED` and whose current `seriousness` is `NON_SERIOUS`; an attempt to reclassify an already-serious event, or an event in any other reporting state, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Seriousness is therefore raised at most once and never lowered. Reclassification takes the `RETAINED → REPORT_DUE` edge, sets `seriousness` to `SERIOUS` and `reclassified_at` to the logical clock, and **rebases the report clock on the reclassification instant**: `deadline_basis_at` becomes `reclassified_at`, not `received_at`, and `deadline_at` is recomputed from that later basis. An event received on 2026-08-05 and reclassified at 2026-08-25T09:30:00Z is due 2026-09-09T23:59:59.999Z, not 2026-08-20 — the sponsor is not retroactively in breach of a deadline that predates the decision to escalate. Because rebasing can only ever push a deadline later, reclassification never creates lateness by itself. Both open-event counters rise by 1, and `retention_until` is extended to the six-year term — but that term is measured from `received_at`, never from `reclassified_at`, so the retention horizon does not move with the reclassification.

**Filing the report.** The listing's agent files with an optional `report_narrative`, which is checked for type and length and then discarded: the event has no field to hold it, so no response ever echoes it back. The event's `report_status` must be `REPORT_DUE` or `REPORT_OVERDUE`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE`; a retained or already-reported event cannot be filed. Filing records `submitted_at` at the logical clock and then compares it with `deadline_at`. At or before the deadline the event takes `REPORT_DUE → REPORTED_TIMELY` with `lateness_days` 0. After it the event takes `REPORT_DUE → REPORTED_LATE` with `lateness_days` set to the number of whole days by which the filing overran, computed as the elapsed time from `deadline_at` to `submitted_at` divided by one day and rounded **up** to the next integer. Because deadlines always land at the last millisecond of a day, this is the same as the count of calendar days between the deadline's date and the filing's date: a filing at 2026-08-21T00:00:00.000Z against a 2026-08-20 deadline is one day late, and a filing at 2026-08-30T12:00:00Z against the same deadline is ten days late, not nine and a half. `REPORTED_TIMELY` and `REPORTED_LATE` are both terminal.

**How an event becomes points.** Each event carries its own `event_points`, and the establishment's `serious_event_points` is the sum of them. An event contributes only while its `report_status` is `REPORTED_TIMELY`, `REPORTED_LATE` or `REPORT_OVERDUE`; events that are `RECORDED`, `RETAINED` or still `REPORT_DUE` contribute exactly 0.00, which is the important asymmetry — a serious event inside its deadline costs the establishment nothing, and the cost appears the moment the deadline is missed or the report is filed. The per-event figure is a base severity multiplied by a lateness factor and a professional-use relief factor. The base is 2 for a timely report, 5 for a late one and 9 for a report that is overdue and still unfiled — the unfiled state is deliberately the most expensive of the three. The lateness factor is one plus a tenth per day of lateness, capped at 2.0, so lateness stops accruing at ten days. The relief factor is 0.5 when the event's listing is `professional_use_only` and 1.0 otherwise, halving the exposure for products sold only into the trade.

The rounding boundary inside this formula is normative and observable. **Each event's contribution is rounded to two decimals first, and the rounded contributions are then summed**; the sum is itself rounded to two decimals and finally clamped at the ceiling of 100.00. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different calculation and is wrong. Worked examples: a late report with nine days of lateness on a consumer listing scores 5 × 1.9 = 9.50; the same report ten days late scores 5 × 2.0 = 10.00; ten days late on a professional-use listing scores 5 × 2.0 × 0.5 = 5.00; one day late scores 5 × 1.1 = 5.50; a timely report scores 2 × 1.0 = 2.00; and an overdue unfiled event nine days past its deadline scores 9 × 1.9 = 17.10. Two professional-use events each three days late contribute 5 × 1.3 × 0.5 = 3.25 apiece for 6.50, and adding a third professional-use event one day late contributes 5 × 1.1 × 0.5 = 2.75 for a running total of 9.25.

Those two figures either side of ten matter because the points total drives the enforcement dial of §2.10. The points component of the posture is `FROZEN` at 25.00 or more, `RESTRICTED` at 10.00 or more, and `OPEN` below that, with both comparisons inclusive at the boundary and made against the two-decimal stored value: 9.50 leaves the establishment `OPEN`, 10.00 exactly makes it `RESTRICTED`, 24.99 is still `RESTRICTED` and 25.00 is `FROZEN`. The override component is `FROZEN` whenever `registration_status` is `LAPSED` or any recall anywhere under the establishment's listings is `ESCALATED_MANDATORY`, `RESTRICTED` whenever any inspection of the establishment is `ESCALATED_OAI`, and `OPEN` otherwise; the posture actually stored is whichever of the two components is the more severe, ranking `OPEN` below `RESTRICTED` below `FROZEN`. So an establishment with 0.00 points and one escalated inspection is `RESTRICTED`; with 0.00 points and one mandatory-escalated recall it is `FROZEN`; and with 30.00 points and a lapsed registration both components agree on `FROZEN`.

**What a late filing sets off.** A `REPORT_DUE → REPORTED_LATE` transition is a four-hop cascade. Hop 1 writes `submitted_at`, `lateness_days` and the event's own `event_points` on the late base. Hop 2 recomputes the establishment's `serious_event_points` and `compliance_posture`. Hop 3 fires only when hop 2 escalated the posture out of `OPEN`: every `ADEQUATE` substantiation dossier under any listing of the establishment moves to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, visited in ascending order of the listing's `created_at` and then the dossier's `created_at`. Hop 4 follows each of those: the listing's `safety_status` becomes `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and its `marketable` flag becomes false. Independently of the cascade, both `open_serious_event_count` values fall by 1, because the event is no longer awaiting a report. The ledger records this in a fixed order: one `ADVERSE_EVENT_REPORT` entry against the event, then — only when the posture actually changed — one `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry carrying the before and after values, then one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per revalidated listing. A timely filing does the same bookkeeping minus the escalation: `submitted_at`, zero lateness, points on the timely base, both counters decremented, the establishment's points and posture recomputed, and one `ADVERSE_EVENT_REPORT` ledger entry.

**Missing the deadline without filing.** The `REPORT_DUE → REPORT_OVERDUE` edge has no caller at all; it is applied by the compliance sweep of §3.9 when it finds an event past its `deadline_at`. The sweep sets `lateness_days` from the clock exactly as a filing would, computes `event_points` on the overdue base of 9, and then runs the identical hop-2-through-hop-4 chain of points, posture, substantiation and listing. Its ledger footprint has the same shape as a filing's, with one `DEADLINE_BREACH` entry against the event standing in the leading position that an `ADVERSE_EVENT_REPORT` entry would occupy — then, only where the posture actually changed, one `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry, and then one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per revalidated listing. What it pointedly does **not** do is move `open_serious_event_count`: the event is still unreported and must keep showing up as an open obligation.

**Catching up.** An overdue event can still be filed, taking the `REPORT_OVERDUE → REPORTED_LATE` edge. This recomputes `event_points` on the late base of 5 instead of the overdue base of 9, which for identical lateness strictly **lowers** the contribution — the 17.10 of the example above becomes 9.50 — and then recomputes `serious_event_points` and `compliance_posture`, at which point a de-escalation from `RESTRICTED` back to `OPEN` is entirely possible. Both open-event counters fall by 1. The de-escalation, however, does not undo history: no dossier that was moved to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` is restored by it, and no listing's `safety_status` is put back. Filing late repairs the establishment's standing going forward; it does not un-invalidate the safety files that the escalation invalidated.

An establishment's `open_serious_event_count` is at all times the number of its listings' events that are `SERIOUS` and whose `report_status` is `REPORT_DUE` or `REPORT_OVERDUE`, and the listing-level counter is the same tally restricted to that listing. All five roles may read a listing's events, newest first, and the list narrows on `seriousness` and on `report_status`; each row exposes `deadline_at`, `lateness_days`, `event_points` and `retention_until` alongside the narrative.

### 3.7. Good-Manufacturing-Practice Inspections: Windows, Readiness Scoring and the Fork

A `RegulatoryReviewer` schedules an inspection engagement against an establishment by naming an `inspection_type` — `GMP_ROUTINE`, `FOR_CAUSE` or `PREANNOUNCED_FOLLOWUP` — and a **half-open** on-site window whose `window_start` is the first day inspectors are on site and whose `window_end` is the first day they are not. Both are calendar dates. The engagement is created `SCHEDULED` with `observation_count` 0 and `readiness_score`, `outcome_class`, `category_subscores`, `response_due_at`, `response_submitted_at` and `response_summary` all null.

Four business conditions guard scheduling, and they are evaluated in this order. First the window must be well formed: `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422) — a zero-length window is not a window. Second the establishment must be admissibly registered (`REGISTERED` or `RENEWAL_DUE`), or `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED`. Third the window must lie **entirely inside** the establishment's current coverage period: since `window_end` is exclusive, the last on-site day is the day before it, and that day must fall on or before `current_period_end`, while `window_start` must fall on or after `current_period_start`. A window that pokes past the period end is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_OUTSIDE_REGISTRATION_PERIOD` (HTTP 422); the agency does not book on-site days it has no current registration to inspect against.

Fourth come the two collision rules, and their relative rank is what makes them worth stating carefully. The **same-type buffer** applies to every existing non-terminal engagement of the same establishment carrying the **same** `inspection_type`: the requested window must be separated from each of those windows by at least seven clear days on **both** sides. A violation is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_TYPE_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). The **overlap** rule applies to every existing non-terminal engagement of the establishment regardless of type: the requested half-open window must not overlap theirs, though adjacency is explicitly legal — an existing window whose `window_end` equals the requested `window_start` is fine, because the half-open convention means those two engagements share no on-site day. A violation is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The buffer rule outranks the overlap rule, so two overlapping `GMP_ROUTINE` windows report the buffer error while a `GMP_ROUTINE` window overlapping a `FOR_CAUSE` window reports the overlap error. "Non-terminal" here means an engagement whose `status` is not one of `CLOSED_NAI`, `CLOSED_VAI` or `ESCALATED_OAI`; closed history never blocks new scheduling. Note the asymmetry with the lot-window rule of §3.8: inspection windows are half-open and tolerate touching endpoints, distribution windows are closed-closed and do not.

**Scoring readiness.** The reviewer issues the engagement's findings as an `observations` payload: a list of items each naming a `category` from `PERSONNEL`, `PREMISES`, `EQUIPMENT`, `RAW_MATERIALS` and `RECORDKEEPING` and a `severity` from `MINOR`, `MAJOR` and `CRITICAL`. The engagement's `status` must be `SCHEDULED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE` (HTTP 409); observations are issued once, and `observation_count` is simply the number of items in that payload.

Readiness is scored in four steps and the rounding boundary between the third and the fourth is normative. Step one converts observations to deduction points per category: a `MINOR` finding costs 1, a `MAJOR` 3 and a `CRITICAL` 6, and the costs of every finding in a category are added together, giving 0 for a category with no findings. Step two turns each category's deductions into a subscore out of 100: cap the category's deduction points at 30, express the capped figure as a fraction of 30, and subtract that fraction of 100 from 100, rounding the result to two decimals. All five categories are scored, including those with no findings at all, which score 100.00; a category with twelve critical findings has 72 deduction points, is capped at 30, and scores 0.00 — the cap means a catastrophic category cannot drag the whole engagement below its own weight. Step three multiplies each subscore by that category's weight — `PERSONNEL` 0.15, `PREMISES` 0.20, `EQUIPMENT` 0.15, `RAW_MATERIALS` 0.20 and `RECORDKEEPING` 0.30, weights that sum to exactly 1.00, with recordkeeping deliberately the heaviest because documentation failures are the most predictive of the rest — **and rounds each of the five weighted terms to two decimals before any of them are added**. Step four adds the five rounded terms and rounds the total to two decimals, yielding `readiness_score`. The five step-two subscores are stored verbatim in `category_subscores`, keyed by category name.

Summing the unrounded weighted products and rounding once at the end is a different function and is wrong by a cent on real cases, which is exactly why the boundary is pinned. With no observations at all the subscores are 100.00 across the board, the terms are 15.00, 20.00, 15.00, 20.00 and 30.00, and the score is 100.00. With one deduction point each in personnel, premises, equipment and recordkeeping and none in raw materials, the subscores are 96.67, 96.67, 96.67, 100.00 and 96.67, the terms 14.50, 19.33, 14.50, 20.00 and 29.00, and the score 97.33 — the unpinned variant would report 97.34. With deductions of 7, 11, 13, 5 and 8 the subscores are 76.67, 63.33, 56.67, 83.33 and 73.33, the terms 11.50, 12.67, 8.50, 16.67 and 22.00, and the score 71.34, where the unpinned variant would report 71.33. With deductions of 13, 20, 16, 14 and 17 the subscores are 56.67, 33.33, 46.67, 53.33 and 43.33, the terms 8.50, 6.67, 7.00, 10.67 and 13.00, and the score 45.84 against an unpinned 45.83.

**The fork.** Issuing observations forks the engagement on a single inclusive comparison of `readiness_score` against 85.00. At 85.00 or above the engagement takes `SCHEDULED → CLOSED_NAI` and is over: `outcome_class` becomes `NAI`, `response_due_at` stays null because no response will be asked for, the establishment's `gmp_readiness_score` and `last_inspection_outcome` are both set — the latter to `NAI` — and one `INSPECTION_OUTCOME` ledger entry is appended. Below 85.00 the engagement takes `SCHEDULED → OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED`: `response_due_at` is set to the logical clock plus fifteen days, pinned to 23:59:59.999Z of that day, `outcome_class` is set to the non-terminal marker `PENDING_RESPONSE` rather than to any final classification, the establishment's `gmp_readiness_score` is updated, and — this is the part that is easy to get wrong — the establishment's `last_inspection_outcome` is **left alone**, because no outcome has been reached yet. No ledger entry is written on this branch either; the ledger records outcomes, and there is not yet an outcome.

**Answering the observations.** The `FacilityRegistrant` responds with a non-blank `response_summary`. The engagement's `status` must be `OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED`, or the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, and the logical clock must be at or before `response_due_at`, or it is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422) — the window is inclusive of its final millisecond. A successful response takes `OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, stores `response_submitted_at` at the logical clock and the narrative, and **decides nothing**. The engagement rejoins the queue of engagements awaiting closure, and its terminal classification is chosen by the compliance sweep of §3.9, never by the response itself. A responsive establishment therefore cannot know its outcome at the moment it answers.

**How an engagement ends.** Three edges close an engagement, and only the first is caller-driven. The `SCHEDULED → CLOSED_NAI` edge above is the clean one. The other two are the sweep's, and both branch on the score already stored. For an engagement in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, a `readiness_score` of 70.00 or more takes `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → CLOSED_VAI`: `outcome_class` becomes `VAI`, the establishment's `last_inspection_outcome` becomes `VAI`, the `compliance_posture` is explicitly **not** disturbed, and one `INSPECTION_OUTCOME` ledger entry is appended. A `readiness_score` below 70.00 takes `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED_OAI`, a four-hop cascade: hop 1 sets `outcome_class` and `last_inspection_outcome` to `OAI`; hop 2 recomputes the establishment's `compliance_posture`, which the escalated-inspection clause forces to at least `RESTRICTED`; hops 3 and 4 are the familiar revalidation chain, moving every `ADEQUATE` dossier of the establishment to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and each such listing's `safety_status` to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` with `marketable` false. The ledger takes an `INSPECTION_OUTCOME` entry, then a `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry, then one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per revalidated listing. Note that the two classifications are decided on thresholds fixed at issuance time: a score of 71.34 closes `VAI`, a score of 45.84 escalates.

The third closing edge punishes silence. An engagement still in `OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED` when the logical clock passes `response_due_at` takes `OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED → ESCALATED_OAI` on the sweep, with a cascade identical to the one above — **regardless of how high its `readiness_score` was**. An establishment that scored 84.99, one hundredth below the clean-close threshold, and then never answered is escalated exactly as harshly as one that scored 20.00. All three closing states are terminal; the establishment's `gmp_readiness_score` and `last_inspection_outcome` are each written where they arise and never afterwards recomputed from the collection: the score is stored at the moment observations are issued, whichever branch that issuance then takes, and the outcome whenever an engagement actually reaches one — `NAI` at issuance on the clean-close branch, `VAI` or `OAI` when the sweep closes or escalates the engagement — and in either case the most recent such write simply stands. So the score reads null until the establishment's first issuance and the outcome null until its first `NAI`, `VAI` or `OAI` — the two may well name different engagements, and issuing observations on an older engagement after a newer one has already been scored leaves the older score on show. Engagements are listed in ascending `window_start` order and the list narrows on `status` and on `inspection_type`.

### 3.8. Recall Response: the Plan-Revision Cycle, Escalation and Withdrawal Rounds

An `EnforcementOfficer` opens a recall action against a listing, declaring an `initiation_basis` of `MANDATORY_ORDER` or `VOLUNTARY_NOTICE` and a `hazard_class` of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`. The action is created with `status` `PLAN_REQUIRED` and `plan_round` 0, with `corrective_summary`, `distribution_depth`, `plan_submitted_at`, `plan_decision_user_id`, `plan_decision_at` and `effectiveness_rate_final` all null. Nothing beyond role authorization, the listing's existence and the shape of those two fields gates the call: a recall may be opened against a listing in any `listing_status`, a `DRAFT` or `DELISTED` one included, and neither the governing establishment's `registration_status` nor its `compliance_posture` is consulted, because enforcement must always be able to reach a product. Opening a recall raises the listing's `open_recall_count` by 1 and recomputes `marketable` — which is how a recall blocks a delisting even while the product remains on the register. Both of those listing figures are derived from the recall collection on every read rather than stored, and the count is the number of the listing's recalls sitting in one of the six non-terminal statuses — `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_SUBMITTED`, `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, `PLAN_REJECTED`, `EXECUTING` or `EXTENDED`. Both terminal statuses therefore drop out of it: a recall closed effective and one escalated to a mandatory order alike stop counting, and so stop standing in the way of a delisting. Recalls are readable individually and listed per listing with an optional `status` filter.

**The plan-revision cycle.** The listing's `ResponsiblePersonAgent` submits a recall plan carrying a non-blank `corrective_summary` and a `distribution_depth` of `WHOLESALE`, `RETAIL` or `CONSUMER` — how far down the chain the plan reaches. A submission is admissible from `PLAN_REQUIRED` or `PLAN_REJECTED`, and both edges into `PLAN_SUBMITTED` behave identically: `plan_round` rises by 1, `plan_submitted_at` is set to the logical clock, the stored narrative and depth are replaced by the newest ones, and the recall's `version` rises by 1. At most three submissions are ever accepted: the submission is refused with `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) once `plan_round` has reached 3, so the revision edge from `PLAN_REJECTED` back to `PLAN_SUBMITTED` can fire at most twice.

An `EnforcementOfficer` then disposes of the submitted plan with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_note` — checked and then discarded here too, so the recall retains the deciding identity and instant but never the narrative; the recall's `status` must be `PLAN_SUBMITTED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE` (HTTP 409). Every decision, whichever way it goes, records the deciding officer's `X-User-ID` in `plan_decision_user_id` and the instant in `plan_decision_at`, and bumps the recall's `version` by 1. That recorded identity has teeth: as §2.12 sets out, the officer who decided the plan is thereafter barred from running the effectiveness review on any withdrawal under this recall. Because only the **most recent** decision's officer is retained, a second officer deciding a later round replaces the first, and the earlier officer is no longer barred.

`PLAN_SUBMITTED → PLAN_ACCEPTED` appends one `RECALL_DECISION` ledger entry and opens the execution phase. `PLAN_SUBMITTED → PLAN_REJECTED` returns the file to the sponsor for revision and writes no ledger entry.

**The asymmetry at the third rejection.** A rejection at `plan_round` 3 branches on `initiation_basis`, and this is the single most consequential fork in the recall lifecycle. For a `MANDATORY_ORDER` recall the third rejection takes `PLAN_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED_MANDATORY`, which is terminal, and fires a four-hop cascade: hop 1 sets the status, after which no further plan submission is accepted at all; hop 2 suspends the parent listing with `suspension_reason` `RECALL_ESCALATION`, but only when its `listing_status` was `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED`; hop 3 follows from that suspension, decrementing the listing's designee's `active_designation_count` and flipping that designee to `DORMANT` at 0; and hop 4 follows from hop 1 independently, recomputing the establishment's `compliance_posture`, which the escalated-recall clause forces to `FROZEN`, which in turn moves every `ADEQUATE` substantiation dossier of the establishment to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and each such listing's `safety_status` to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`. The ledger takes a `RECALL_DECISION` entry, then a `POSTURE_CHANGE` entry, then — where hop 2 actually suspended the parent listing — one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry for that suspension, and last one `STATE_TRANSITION` entry per revalidated listing in ascending listing `created_at` order. The parent listing consequently takes two entries when it was both suspended here and revalidated by the same freeze.

A `VOLUNTARY_NOTICE` recall is never escalated to a mandatory recall by rejection alone. Its third rejection takes the ordinary `PLAN_SUBMITTED → PLAN_REJECTED` edge and leaves it sitting in `PLAN_REJECTED` with `plan_round` 3 — a live but exhausted recall, where the agency has rejected the sponsor's last permitted plan and the sponsor may not submit another. That is precisely the state in which a further plan submission draws `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`: an escalated mandatory recall would have failed earlier and differently, on the state check, because `ESCALATED_MANDATORY` does not admit a plan submission at all.

**Opening an execution round.** Either an `EnforcementOfficer` or the listing's agent opens a market-withdrawal round under the recall, supplying a `scope_depth` of `WHOLESALE`, `RETAIL` or `CONSUMER` and an `expected_recall_action_version`. The recall's `status` must be `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, `EXECUTING` or `EXTENDED`. The version check precedes that state check, so a caller working from a stale observation is told `ERR_STALE_RECALL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`. The reason this operation is guarded at all is destructive: opening a new round **supersedes** the recall's prior non-terminal withdrawal and releases the distribution records that round had produced, so a caller acting on a stale version would silently throw away another party's document production. Opening two rounds in succession under one executing recall is perfectly legal in either order — round two supersedes round one — but a caller who read the recall's `version` before round one existed must re-read it before opening round two.

A successful open creates a `MarketWithdrawal` with `status` `OPEN`, `records_produced_count` 0, `shipped_units_total` 0, `outstanding_units_total` 0 and both recovery rates 0.00 — while every figure that only an effectiveness review can produce is null rather than zero at this point, namely `provisional_verified_rate`, `unreachable_credit_pct`, `adjusted_denominator_units`, `effectiveness_verdict` and `effectiveness_reviewer_user_id` — moves the recall to `EXECUTING` — from `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, from `EXTENDED`, or as a self-loop from `EXECUTING` — and raises the recall's `version` by exactly 1. Any predecessor withdrawal whose `status` was `OPEN`, `RECORDS_PRODUCED`, `INTERIM_REVIEWED` or `EXTENDED_INEFFECTIVE` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal: each of its linked distribution records has its `withdrawal_id` cleared back to null so the successor round can produce them afresh, and the predecessor's `records_produced_count`, `shipped_units_total` and `outstanding_units_total` are all zeroed. The per-record verified unit tallies are deliberately **not** touched by that release, so the conservation invariant below still balances across a supersession. Withdrawal rounds under a recall are listed newest first with an optional `status` filter, and each round is readable on its own, exposing every pinned intermediate of the verified-basis calculation.

**Distribution records exist independently of any recall.** A `ResponsiblePersonAgent` records each shipment of a listed product as it happens, naming the `lot_code` (1 to 40 characters), the `consignee_name` (1 to 200 characters), the `consignee_class` (`WHOLESALE`, `RETAIL` or `CONSUMER`), the **closed-closed** distribution window `ship_date` to `delivery_date` — both days inclusive — and the `units_shipped`, which must be greater than zero. The governing establishment must be admissibly registered and its `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, so a frozen establishment cannot keep shipping; the listing's own `listing_status` is not consulted at all, so a shipment may be recorded against a listing in any state, a `DELISTED` one included. Two window rules apply, in order after the ordinary bounds check that `ship_date` be no later than `delivery_date` — a `ship_date` falling after the `delivery_date` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422), while a single-day window whose two dates are equal satisfies the check, these windows being closed-closed: the requested window must not overlap **or even touch** any existing window of the same listing bearing the same `lot_code` — an existing record whose `delivery_date` equals the requested `ship_date` is a collision, refused with `ERR_LOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) — and then the lot's aggregate capacity must hold: the sum of `units_shipped` over the listing's existing records with that same `lot_code`, plus the units now requested, must not exceed the listing's `lot_authorized_units`, or the shipment is refused with `ERR_LOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). That second rule is an aggregate: every individual record can be small enough to pass while the collection breaches the ceiling. A new record is created with `withdrawal_id` null, all five recovery buckets at 0 and `units_outstanding` equal to `units_shipped`, and the listing's `distributed_units_total` rises by the units shipped. That total counts every record of the listing, whatever its recovery state and whether or not it is linked to a withdrawal. Records are readable individually and listable across the system, narrowed by `listing_id`, by `withdrawal_id` and by `lot_code`.

**Producing the records for a withdrawal.** The document-production step is what binds existing shipment records to a withdrawal round. The agent names a `distribution_record_ids` list; the withdrawal's `status` must be `OPEN`, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WITHDRAWAL_STATE` (HTTP 409). Every identifier in the list must resolve to a record — validated in payload order, so the first unresolvable identifier is the one reported as `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — the list must name at least one record, or it is refused with `ERR_NO_DISTRIBUTION_RECORDS` (HTTP 422), and every named record must belong to the listing the recall was opened against, or it is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RECORD_LISTING_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Production sets each named record's `withdrawal_id` to this withdrawal, sets `records_produced_count` to the number of linked records and `shipped_units_total` to the sum of their `units_shipped`, sets `outstanding_units_total` equal to `shipped_units_total` because nothing has been verified yet, leaves both recovery rates at 0.00, and takes `OPEN → RECORDS_PRODUCED`. The shipped-unit figure fixed here is the denominator the whole effectiveness calculation rests on.

**Recovery movements and the conservation of units.** Each recovery entry posts one movement against one produced record, naming an `entry_kind`, the `distribution_record_id`, a `units` count that must be an integer of at least 1 and — for a reversal only — a `reversal_target_bucket`. A `reversal_target_bucket` supplied on any other kind of entry is ignored but still validated, so an unrecognised bucket spelling is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` whatever the `entry_kind` alongside it. The call is the one operation in this API that answers **201** without minting a resource of its own, and the resource in its `data` is the updated distribution record — every bucket and the recomputed `units_outstanding` — rather than the withdrawal the path addresses. The withdrawal's `status` must be `RECORDS_PRODUCED` or `INTERIM_REVIEWED`. The named record must currently be linked to the withdrawal in question, or the entry is refused with `ERR_RECORD_NOT_IN_WITHDRAWAL` (HTTP 422); that rule strictly outranks the outstanding-units guard below, so an unlinked record carrying an absurdly large unit count reports the linkage error rather than the quantity error.

Five of the six kinds add their `units` to exactly one bucket on the record: `REPORTED_RETURN` accumulates `units_reported_returned`, `REPORTED_DESTRUCTION` accumulates `units_reported_destroyed`, `IN_TRANSIT_RETURN` accumulates `units_in_transit_returns`, `VERIFIED_RETURN` accumulates `units_verified_returned` and `VERIFIED_DESTRUCTION` accumulates `units_verified_destroyed`. The sixth, `CORRECTION_REVERSAL`, names a target bucket and **subtracts** its units from it; that target may be spelled either as the movement kind whose bucket is meant (`REPORTED_RETURN`) or as the record's own field name (`units_reported_returned`), both being accepted for the same bucket, and anything outside those two spellings is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the units reversed must not exceed that bucket's current balance, or the entry is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_RECORDED` (HTTP 422), so no bucket is ever driven below zero. Only the two verified buckets reduce `units_outstanding`, which is at all times `units_shipped` minus the sum of `units_verified_returned` and `units_verified_destroyed`: consignee assertions and in-transit claims are recorded but do not discharge the obligation, because units are only accounted for once someone has actually seen them come back or be destroyed. A verified movement is therefore guarded: the `units` of a `VERIFIED_RETURN` or `VERIFIED_DESTRUCTION` entry must not exceed the record's `units_outstanding` **before** the entry, or it is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422). The reported and in-transit buckets carry no such cap and may legitimately exceed what was shipped, which is one of the reasons the reported basis is only ever an interim view.

Three invariants hold after every entry and every reversal. Per record, `units_verified_returned` plus `units_verified_destroyed` plus `units_outstanding` always equals `units_shipped` exactly — no entry may leave a record out of balance. Per withdrawal, the sum over its linked records of verified-plus-outstanding units always equals `shipped_units_total`, and `outstanding_units_total` always equals the sum of the records' `units_outstanding`. And in the ledger, every verified movement appends a `UNIT_MOVEMENT` entry whose `signed_units_delta` is the units added, while every `CORRECTION_REVERSAL` of a verified bucket appends one whose `signed_units_delta` is the units removed, negated — so summing `signed_units_delta` over a record's `UNIT_MOVEMENT` entries always reproduces the record's verified total. Reversals of a reported or in-transit bucket write no ledger entry, since no physical movement was ever asserted as verified. Every entry also refreshes the withdrawal's `reported_recovered_units`, `verified_recovered_units` and `outstanding_units_total`, and the listing's `distributed_units_total`. No rounding occurs anywhere in this rule; all of it is whole units.

**The interim review.** An `EnforcementOfficer` runs an effectiveness check against a withdrawal, supplying a `basis`. Whichever basis is chosen, the withdrawal's `status` must be `RECORDS_PRODUCED` or `INTERIM_REVIEWED` and its `records_produced_count` must be greater than 0, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WITHDRAWAL_STATE` and `ERR_NO_DISTRIBUTION_RECORDS` respectively. The segregation bar is evaluated **after** the state check, which produces a deliberate ordering effect: the officer who decided this recall's plan, aiming at a withdrawal that is still `OPEN`, is told `ERR_INVALID_WITHDRAWAL_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), because the state objection is the more fundamental one.

Under `REPORTED_BASIS` the review is a progress report and nothing more. It sums each linked record's `units_reported_returned`, `units_reported_destroyed` and `units_in_transit_returns` into `reported_recovered_units`, divides that by `shipped_units_total`, multiplies by 100 and rounds to two decimals into `reported_recovery_rate`. No unreachable-consignee credit is applied. The verdict is recorded as the interim marker `INTERIM`, `effectiveness_reviewer_user_id` is set to the reviewing officer, the withdrawal takes `RECORDS_PRODUCED → INTERIM_REVIEWED` or self-loops on `INTERIM_REVIEWED`, and `verified_recovery_rate`, the parent recall and the listing are all left strictly untouched. The reported basis is repeatable indefinitely, each run recomputing the rate against whatever recovery entries then exist.

**The closing review.** Under `VERIFIED_BASIS` the calculation runs in four pinned steps, in this order and once only. Step one sums each linked record's `units_verified_returned` and `units_verified_destroyed` into `verified_recovered_units` and computes `provisional_verified_rate` as 100 times that figure over `shipped_units_total`, rounded to two decimals. Step two selects `unreachable_credit_pct` from the **provisional** rate: 0 when it is 90.00 or more, 3 when it is 80.00 or more, and 6 otherwise — the acknowledgement that the harder a recall is going, the more of the distribution chain is genuinely unreachable. Step three computes `adjusted_denominator_units` as `shipped_units_total` minus the credit's whole-unit share of it, taking the credit percentage of the shipped total and discarding any fraction (a floor, never a rounding). Step four computes `verified_recovery_rate` as 100 times `verified_recovered_units` over `adjusted_denominator_units`, rounded to two decimals. Alongside them, `outstanding_units_total` is set to `shipped_units_total` minus `verified_recovered_units`, and `effectiveness_verdict` becomes `EFFECTIVE` when `verified_recovery_rate` is 90.00 or more and `NOT_EFFECTIVE` otherwise. All four intermediates are stored on the withdrawal, not merely the final rate.

The pinning of step two to the provisional rate is the crux, because step four's denominator shrink **raises** the rate above the provisional figure and can lift it across a tier boundary. With 1500 units shipped and 1345 verified, the provisional rate is 89.67, which sits below 90.00 and therefore selects the 3 percent tier; the credit is 45 units, the denominator is 1455, and the final rate is 92.44 — `EFFECTIVE`. Re-deriving the tier from that 92.44 would select 0 percent, restore the denominator to 1500 and report 89.67, which would read `NOT_EFFECTIVE`. The single pinned pass is authoritative and iterating the four steps to a fixed point is wrong. Three more cases: 1500 shipped with 830 units reported-or-in-transit and 700 verified gives a reported rate of 55.33 alongside a provisional 46.67, which selects 6 percent, a 90-unit credit, a 1410 denominator and a verified rate of 49.65 — `NOT_EFFECTIVE`, and a vivid illustration that the two bases disagree in both directions, the reported one inflated by unverified assertions and the verified one inflated by the denominator shrink. 1000 shipped with 950 verified gives a provisional 95.00, the 0 percent tier, an unchanged denominator and 95.00 final — `EFFECTIVE`. 1000 shipped with nothing verified gives a provisional 0.00, the 6 percent tier, a 60-unit credit, a 940 denominator and a final rate of 0.00 — `NOT_EFFECTIVE`, and note that the credit cannot manufacture recovery out of nothing. At the tier boundary, 1000 shipped with exactly 900 verified gives a provisional 90.00, which is inclusive of the high tier, so no credit at all and a final rate of 90.00 — `EFFECTIVE` on the effectiveness threshold's own inclusive boundary.

An effective verified review closes the round and the recall together: the withdrawal takes `RECORDS_PRODUCED → CLOSED_EFFECTIVE` or `INTERIM_REVIEWED → CLOSED_EFFECTIVE`, which is terminal; the parent recall moves `EXECUTING → CLOSED_EFFECTIVE`, also terminal, storing `effectiveness_rate_final` as the withdrawal's `verified_recovery_rate`; the listing's `open_recall_count` falls by 1 and its `marketable` flag is recomputed; and one `WITHDRAWAL_CLOSURE` ledger entry is appended against the withdrawal. Closing a recall does **not** restore a listing that was suspended with `suspension_reason` `RECALL_ESCALATION` — that suspension came from a different recall's escalation and is not lifted by this one's success. An ineffective verified review takes the withdrawal to `EXTENDED_INEFFECTIVE` and the recall `EXECUTING → EXTENDED`, leaves `open_recall_count` unchanged because the recall is still open business, and writes no closure entry; the parties then open a further withdrawal round, which supersedes the ineffective one and returns the recall to `EXECUTING`, closing the execution cycle.

### 3.9. The Compliance Sweep: Time Passing as an Operation

Nothing in this system happens because time has passed; things happen because the sweep is run and observes that time has passed. The sweep is a `SystemOperator` checkpoint that takes no payload and executes three steps against the logical clock, always in the same order, and its whole output is a set of counters plus the `swept_at` instant at which it ran — the logical-clock value it read.

**Step 1 walks the establishments** in ascending `created_at` order. An establishment whose `registration_status` is `REGISTERED` and whose `renewal_window_open_date` has arrived — and whose `current_period_end` has *not* yet passed, since one already beyond that date takes the lapse branch below in preference — is moved to `RENEWAL_DUE`, and each such move is counted in `renewal_due_facility_count` and appends one `STATE_TRANSITION` ledger entry against the establishment. The two branches are mutually exclusive: at most one of them fires per establishment per sweep. An establishment whose `registration_status` is `REGISTERED` or `RENEWAL_DUE` and whose `current_period_end` has passed is moved to `LAPSED` and counted in `lapsed_facility_count`, firing the four-hop lapse cascade of §3.1 in full: the `compliance_posture` is recomputed and forced to `FROZEN` by the lapsed clause, every listing of the establishment whose `listing_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LISTED` is suspended with `suspension_reason` `FACILITY_LAPSED` and counted in `suspended_listing_count`, each suspended listing's designee has its `active_designation_count` decremented and flips to `DORMANT` at zero, and the establishment's `ACTIVE` registration period is moved to `EXPIRED`. Because forcing the posture to `FROZEN` is an escalation out of `OPEN`, the same step also fires the revalidation cascade: every `ADEQUATE` substantiation dossier of the establishment moves to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` with its listing's `safety_status` following, and each dossier so moved is counted in `revalidation_required_count`.

**Step 2 walks the adverse events** in ascending `created_at` order. Every event whose `report_status` is `REPORT_DUE` and whose `deadline_at` has passed is moved to `REPORT_OVERDUE` and counted in `overdue_event_count`, with `lateness_days` and `event_points` computed on the overdue base, the establishment's `serious_event_points` and `compliance_posture` recomputed, and the points-driven revalidation cascade fired where the posture escalated out of `OPEN` — those dossiers also counting toward `revalidation_required_count`. One `DEADLINE_BREACH` ledger entry is appended per event swept. The open-event counters do not move, because the events remain unreported.

**Step 3 walks the inspection engagements** in ascending `created_at` order and closes those that are ripe. An engagement in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` closes `CLOSED_VAI` when its `readiness_score` is 70.00 or more, counted in `closed_vai_count`, or escalates to `ESCALATED_OAI` when it is below that, counted in `escalated_oai_count`. An engagement still in `OBSERVATIONS_ISSUED` whose `response_due_at` has passed escalates to `ESCALATED_OAI` whatever its score, also counted in `escalated_oai_count`. Each escalation forces the establishment's `compliance_posture` to at least `RESTRICTED` and, where that is an escalation out of `OPEN`, contributes further dossiers to `revalidation_required_count`.

**The step order is load-bearing.** Because establishments are swept before events, an establishment that lapses in step 1 also has its overdue events swept in step 2 within the same call, so a single run can freeze an establishment for a lapse and then add points on top of it. And because the posture is recomputed inside each step, an establishment that step 1 has already frozen is not further escalated by step 2 or step 3 — the dial only ever reads the most severe of its inputs — but the dossier revalidation attributable to each step is still counted where it occurred. Conversely, a dossier already sitting in `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` when the sweep starts is not counted again by any later step, because only dossiers actually moved out of `ADEQUATE` are counted.

**The sweep is idempotent for a fixed clock.** Running it twice without moving the logical clock in between yields a second response whose every counter is zero and whose `swept_at` equals the clock, because each of the three steps selects only records whose state still admits the transition, and the first run has already moved them all. That property is what makes the sweep safe to run as often as an operator likes: it is an observation of the clock, not an increment of anything. Nothing else in the system performs these transitions — no caller can mark an establishment `RENEWAL_DUE` or `LAPSED`, no caller can mark an event `REPORT_OVERDUE`, and no caller can decide an inspection's terminal `VAI` or `OAI` outcome — so a scenario that needs any of those states must advance the logical clock and run the sweep.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every route below is mounted under the `/cosmetics` prefix. Endpoints that mint a brand-new resource answer with HTTP 201 and carry precisely that newly minted resource in `data` — which for the nested create routes is the child just inserted rather than the parent named in the path, and for a renewal is the new registration period; every other successful call — reads, list retrievals, state transitions, decisions and ledger-producing operations — answers with HTTP 200 and carries in `data` the resource its path names **last**, so the two engagement actions nested under a facility answer with the engagement and not with the facility. The recovery-movement post of §3.8 is the single departure from both halves of that rule, minting no resource yet answering 201 with the distribution record it moved units on; and the two operator capabilities answer 200 with the payloads §2.5 and §3.9 describe rather than with any resource at all. Failures carry no resource payload and use the status paired with their error identifier in §5; where several checks could fire on one request, the precedence order of §2.8 decides which identifier is returned. Two router-level rejections sit ahead of everything in §2.8, because they are decided before the identity headers are even read: a request whose path matches no route in the table answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request whose method the matched path does not support answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405).
