# Faith-Based Institution Lending API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Lending to congregations, parochial schools, denominational agencies and nonprofit ministries does not fit the machinery built for commercial borrowers. There is no EBITDA to margin, no accounts-receivable ageing to discount, and the property offered as security is frequently a sanctuary whose only plausible buyer is another congregation. What a faith-based lender actually underwrites is a giving record: four fiscal periods of contributions net of everything a donor has restricted, tested against the annual principal-and-interest requirement, and cross-checked against a board resolution that the borrower's own bylaws must authorise. This API is the system of record for that discipline.

The platform carries a borrower from onboarding through to workout. An institution's fiscal history is captured period by period; a governance packet evidences the authority to borrow and, where a denomination requires it, the presbytery, synod or conference decision that ratifies it; capital-campaign pledges are valued twice — once on the lender's conservative attrition assumptions and once on the sponsor's own historical collection experience — so that the two numbers can never be confused for one another; special-purpose property is advanced against on a rate that reflects how little alternative use it has; and construction proceeds are released draw by draw through a retainage-and-waterfall administration that keeps the commitment ledger balanced at every step. After closing, covenants written on unrestricted giving are tested on a cadence, breaches escalate through a cure window into a hard breach that freezes construction funding, and distressed credits are restructured through workout options that explicitly price mission and community impact rather than ignoring it.

Two requirements shape almost every rule that follows. First, donor-restricted money is not the lender's collateral and is not the lender's coverage: restricted contributions, donor-restricted endowment draws and donor-restricted endowment assets are excluded from every credit calculation in this specification, and the endowment's market value is carried for disclosure alone. Second, the arithmetic is pinned. Where a figure could be computed in two defensible orders — round each period then sum, or sum then round; floor after each factor or floor once at the end — this document names the order the implementation must use, because a lender's covenant test that drifts by a dollar is a covenant test that cannot be defended.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and audit conventions, the logical clock, rounding discipline, stale-version protection, and the cross-cutting doctrines that individual workflows in §3 assume without restating.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal acts. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `InstitutionRepresentative`, `GovernanceOfficer`, `CreditAnalyst`, `CreditCommitteeMember` and `LoanServicer`. A request missing either header, presenting an `X-User-ID` that is empty or wholly whitespace, or presenting an `X-User-Role` value that is not one of those six strings — the comparison is exact and case-sensitive — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid request whose acting role is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint it targets is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) — for example, a `LoanServicer` attempting to waive a covenant breach, which is reserved to `CreditCommitteeMember`.

The roles divide into one borrower-side identity and five lender-side operator identities.

When `X-User-Role` is `InstitutionRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Institution`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Institution` compare the resource's institution foreign key to `X-User-ID`. That comparison is made directly where the resource carries `institution_id` itself — giving periods, governance packets, pledge campaigns, collateral assets and loan applications — and transitively otherwise: a pledge is owned through its campaign's `institution_id`, a loan through its application's `institution_id`, and a draw request, covenant or workout plan through its loan and that loan's application. An `InstitutionRepresentative` acting on a resource that belongs to any other institution is rejected with `ERR_INSTITUTION_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `InstitutionRepresentative` **and that addresses one identified resource** — a read by id, an action on a specific row, or a create whose body names the institution or a parent from which the institution is reachable. Collection list endpoints perform no ownership check and no ownership filtering: a representative listing a collection receives every institution's records, and it narrows the result to its own only by supplying the collection's own `institution_id` or parent filter.

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `GovernanceOfficer`, `CreditAnalyst`, `CreditCommitteeMember` or `LoanServicer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow demands an audit trail or a segregation-of-duties comparison: the verifying officer's identifier lands in `verified_by_user_id` on the governance packet, the underwriting analyst's in `prepared_by_user_id` on the application, the deciding committee member's in `decided_by_user_id`, and the approving committee member's in `approved_by_user_id` on a workout plan. Those recorded values are compared only against each other — never against an institution id.

Authority is deliberately partitioned. `PlatformAdmin` onboards institutions and controls the logical clock but holds no credit authority. `GovernanceOfficer` verifies the board vote against the bylaws and records the denominational decision, and cannot underwrite, decide credit or service loans. `CreditAnalyst` assembles the giving analysis, registers and assigns collateral, writes covenants and refers applications to committee. `CreditCommitteeMember` renders application decisions, waives covenant breaches and approves workout plans, and never prepares underwriting. `LoanServicer` originates approved loans, administers draws, runs covenant tests, files cures, manages pledge lapses and reinstatements, and proposes workout plans — but cannot approve its own proposals.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Two identifiers sit outside the catalogue of §5 because no endpoint's own rules produce them: a request to a path no endpoint declares is answered `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request whose method the addressed path does not declare is answered `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405). A trailing slash on any declared path is accepted as equivalent to the path without one.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts offset pagination through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. List responses report `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any record remains beyond the current window. Either parameter omitted, or supplied as an empty string, takes its default; a value that is not an integer, a `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a collection declares otherwise below, records are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Five collections invert that default and return the newest record first, sorted by `created_at` descending: institutions, governance authorizations, pledge campaigns, loan applications and loans. Giving periods use a two-key order that is neither of those: descending by `period_end`, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties between periods that end on the same instant. Pledges, draw requests, covenants and workout plans use the plain `created_at` ascending default.

Filters combine conjunctively with pagination, and every filter narrows `total_count` before the window is cut. Institutions may be filtered by `institution_type` and `denomination_code`. Giving periods accept `institution_id` and `source`. Governance authorizations accept `institution_id` and `status`. Pledge campaigns accept `institution_id` and `status`. Pledges accept `pledge_campaign_id`, `donor_tier` and `status`. Collateral assets accept `institution_id`, `assigned_loan_application_id` and `asset_type`. Loan applications accept `institution_id`, `governance_authorization_id`, `pledge_campaign_id`, `status` and `purpose`. Loans accept `loan_application_id`, `servicing_status` and `risk_posture`. Draw requests accept `loan_id`, `status` and `project_phase`. Covenants accept `loan_id`, `covenant_type` and `status`. Workout plans accept `loan_id`, `predecessor_workout_plan_id`, `option` and `status`. Every one of those filters, together with `limit` and `offset`, is optional. Each one matches by exact string equality against the stored field value; a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent, and a query parameter the collection does not declare is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Versioning

Each of the eleven resource collections in this system — institutions, giving periods, governance authorizations, pledge campaigns, pledges, collateral assets, loan applications, loans, draw requests, covenants and workout plans — is a root-level peer. There is no nesting in the URL hierarchy: a child records its parent as a foreign key in its own request body rather than living beneath the parent's path, and every foreign key is reachable in the reverse direction through the corresponding filtered list.

All eleven carry the same four server-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured segment; it is the bare UUID and nothing else.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recording when the resource was first persisted. The audit timestamps, like the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, are drawn from real server time and are never repositioned by the logical clock of §2.5, which governs only the date-sensitive rules enumerated there.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource. At creation it equals `created_at`.
- `version` (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation the server applies to that resource.

A "mutation" for versioning purposes includes mutations the client did not directly request. When a pledge installment shifts a campaign's bucket totals, the campaign's `version` increases and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though the client addressed the pledge. When a covenant test drives a loan's risk posture, the loan's `version` increases and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though the client addressed the covenant. Where a single call mutates two or three resources, each mutated resource independently advances its own `version` by 1; a cascade never advances one resource's counter by more than 1 per call.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every date-sensitive rule in this specification reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock's value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real time before the clock has been set. An operator holding `PlatformAdmin` may reposition it by submitting a `to_iso8601` instant to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object whose only member is `current_utc` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant the clock now reports, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Any instant is accepted, whether it precedes or follows the value the clock currently holds. Setting the clock mutates no stored entity and advances no `version`.

Four families of rule consult the clock, and all four are rebased the moment it moves: a collateral asset's `appraisal_age_days`, computed as whole days from `appraisal_date` to the current logical instant with any fractional day discarded; the giving-period selection window, which admits only periods whose `period_end` falls at or before the current instant; the expiry test on a governance packet's `expires_on`; and the lien-window comparisons that decide whether a proposed collateral occupation collides with a prior one. Timestamps supplied by clients — `resolution_date`, `expires_on`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `appraisal_date`, `lien_effective_from`, `lien_effective_to`, `as_of_date`, `payment_date`, `disbursement_date` — are ISO 8601 UTC instants. A supplied instant that carries no zone designator is read as UTC, one that carries an offset is normalised to UTC for every comparison, and a string that cannot be parsed as an ISO 8601 instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Where such a field is persisted, the string the client supplied is stored and echoed back on reads verbatim rather than reformatted to the server's own three-digit rendering.

### 2.6. Monetary Units, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

Money is carried in integer cents everywhere the field name ends in `_cents`, and those fields are exchanged as JSON integers, never as strings and never as decimals. Rates expressed in basis points — `interest_rate_bps` on an application and `sponsor_collection_rate_bps` on a campaign — are likewise integers, where 10000 basis points is the whole. Two quantities are carried in whole dollars rather than cents because the covenant contract is written in dollars: `net_available_for_debt_service_dollars` on an institution, `net_available_dollars` snapshotted onto an application, and the `last_test_numerator_dollars` and `last_test_denominator_dollars` recorded against a covenant test.

Three rounding modes appear, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Round half up to four decimal places** governs every ratio and fraction: `bylaws_quorum_pct`, `board_quorum_pct`, `threshold_ratio`, `last_test_ratio`, `membership_trend_pct`, `effective_attrition_pct` and `percent_complete`. A value exactly at the half-way point of the fourth decimal place rounds away from zero. The one fraction this mode does **not** govern is `completion_pct_at_disbursement`, which is *floored* to four decimal places by the integer arithmetic §3.7 pins for it rather than rounded half up.
- **Round half up to two decimal places** governs `mission_impact_score`, and only that quantity.
- **Truncation toward zero — flooring** governs money. Wherever this document says an amount is floored, any fractional cent is discarded rather than rounded, and where a formula floors after each of several multiplications, the flooring happens after each one and not once at the end.

Conversion from cents to whole dollars is round-half-up division by 100, and the *order* in which that conversion interleaves with summation is contractual: §3.5 pins it for the net-available figure, and no implementation may substitute the other order.

### 2.7. The Donor-Restricted Exclusion Doctrine

Money a donor has restricted is not available to service debt, and this system refuses to let it leak into a credit figure. Three exclusions implement that doctrine, and each is absolute.

Within a fiscal period, `restricted_contributions_cents` and `donor_restricted_endowment_draw_cents` are both subtracted out of `total_contributions_cents` before anything else happens, and the result is floored at zero so that a period can never contribute a negative amount of giving. That netted figure is stored on the period as `unrestricted_contributions_cents` and is the only contribution number any lending calculation reads.

At the institution level, `endowment_market_value_cents` is recorded for disclosure and is never an input to any calculation in this specification. It appears on institution reads, it is reported to committee, and it is arithmetically inert.

On the collateral side, an asset whose `asset_type` is `DONOR_RESTRICTED_ENDOWMENT` carries an advance-rate cap of zero, so its `lending_value_cents` is always 0, and it is barred from assignment outright: an attempt to pledge one to a loan application is rejected with `ERR_ENDOWMENT_COLLATERAL_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Campaign and Loan Writes

Two write endpoints require the client to declare the `version` it last observed on a parent resource, and reject the write when that declaration no longer matches. Both fields are mandatory inputs, not optional hints.

`POST /pledges` requires `expected_pledge_campaign_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PledgeCampaign`. If the campaign's current `version` differs from the submitted value — because another writer has recorded a pledge, an installment, a lapse or a reinstatement since the client read the campaign — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Pledges are purely additive, so such a create would otherwise satisfy every state precondition while being priced against a campaign whose bucket totals, projected values and status have all moved; the version check is what makes a stale-basis create deterministically refusable.

`POST /workout-plans` requires `expected_loan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Loan`. If the loan's current `version` differs, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A workout proposal is sized against the loan's outstanding principal, its term and its risk posture, and an earlier approved plan legitimately supersedes the lender's view of all three; the version mismatch is the only signal that the proposal was priced against a superseded loan state.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection perform no version comparison, and supplying an unrecognised version field to them has no effect on whether the request succeeds.

### 2.9. Hierarchical Reconciliation and the Two Loan State Dimensions

Many operations in this system mutate more than the resource the client addressed. Recording a giving period rewrites the five computed figures on its institution — the two period counts, the giving base, the net-available figure and the membership trend. Recording a pledge installment rewrites five figures on its campaign and may move the campaign's `status`, which in turn may push a loan application backwards in its lifecycle. Running a covenant test may move the loan's risk posture, which may suspend approved draws, which releases reserved commitment. The server performs each of these reconciliations immediately and synchronously within the originating request, and each reconciled resource independently refreshes its `updated_at` and advances its `version` as described in §2.4. There is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this specification.

A loan carries two state dimensions that move independently and never read each other. `servicing_status` tracks where the loan sits in its servicing lifecycle and moves only on cash movement or an explicit servicing action: `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW`, `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW`, `AMORTIZING`, and the terminal `PAID_OFF`. `risk_posture` tracks credit distress and moves only on covenant and workout events: `OPEN`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` and `WORKOUT_GATED`. A loan under a hard covenant breach still sits in `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW`; a loan that reaches `PAID_OFF` freezes its `risk_posture` at whatever value that dimension last held. No transition described anywhere in §3 changes both dimensions as a single act — each is driven by its own trigger.

### 2.10. Identifier Resolution and the Validation Ladder

Identifiers are resolved before rules are evaluated, and path identifiers are resolved left to right. Where a request names several resources — a path identifier plus one or more foreign keys in the body — the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the response, and the request stops there. Each resource kind has its own 404: `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GIVING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLEDGE_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRAW_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVENANT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_WORKOUT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. Ownership, however, can only be tested once the row carrying the institution key is in hand, so the interleaving of position 3 with resolution is fixed as follows: where the institution is named directly — the institution read, whose path identifier *is* the institution id, and any create whose body carries `institution_id` — the scope check runs first, and a representative naming an institution other than its own is answered `ERR_INSTITUTION_SCOPE_VIOLATION` even when that identifier would not have resolved; where ownership must instead be read off a resolved row or its ancestors, the row is resolved first, so an identifier that does not resolve answers its own 404 even to a representative of another institution. A foreign key that is absent, `null`, or of any type other than string is not a resolution failure at all: it produces no 404 and falls through to the request-shape rung at position 15.

Because many endpoints in this system can fail several checks at once, the order of evaluation is contractual rather than incidental. Validations are evaluated in the ascending order set out below; the **first** rule that a request violates determines the single error the response carries, and no later rule can mask an earlier one. Every position in the ladder is unique, so a request that violates two rules has exactly one correct answer.

1. Authentication headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, applied to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, applied to every endpoint.
3. An `InstitutionRepresentative` acts only within its own institution — `ERR_INSTITUTION_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, applied to every endpoint whose authorised roles include that role.
4. `institution_id`, wherever it appears in a path or body, resolves — `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`, on the institution read, and on creation of giving periods, governance authorizations, pledge campaigns, collateral assets and loan applications.
5. `giving_period_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_GIVING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, on the giving-period read.
6. `governance_authorization_id`, in a path or a body, resolves — `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, on every governance-authorization read and action and on loan-application creation.
7. `pledge_campaign_id`, in a path or a body, resolves — `ERR_PLEDGE_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, on every pledge-campaign read and action, on pledge creation, and on loan-application creation.
8. `pledge_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`, on the pledge read and on every pledge action.
9. `collateral_asset_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_COLLATERAL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, on the collateral read and on both collateral actions.
10. `loan_application_id`, in a path or a body, resolves — `ERR_LOAN_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, on every loan-application read and action, on loan origination, and on a collateral assignment.
11. `loan_id`, in a path or a body, resolves — `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, on the loan read, on both loan actions, and on creation of draw requests, covenants and workout plans.
12. `draw_request_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_DRAW_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, on the draw read and on every draw action.
13. `covenant_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_COVENANT_NOT_FOUND`, on the covenant read and on every covenant action.
14. `workout_plan_id` in the path, and `predecessor_workout_plan_id` in a workout-plan creation body, resolve — `ERR_WORKOUT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, on the workout read, on workout approval, and on workout creation.
15. The request body conforms to the endpoint's input schema — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), on every write endpoint. This covers a missing required field, an enum value outside its declared set, a negative cent amount (or a non-positive one where §3 requires strict positivity), a `percent_complete` outside the range above 0 and at or below 1, a `period_end` at or before `period_start`, a `lien_effective_to` at or before `lien_effective_from`, an installment whose `outcome` is `MISSED` with a non-zero `amount_cents` or whose `outcome` is `PAID` with an `amount_cents` of zero or less, a `decision` outside its allowed set, a workout option missing its option-specific field, and a construction completion whose `final_inspection_passed` is not true. Four conventions govern this rung everywhere it applies. A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON is treated as an empty object, so its required fields simply report as missing, whereas a body that parses to a JSON value that is not an object is itself rejected. A field present with the value `null` is treated exactly as though it had been omitted: it takes its default where the field is optional and reports as missing where the field is required. A required string field must contain at least one non-whitespace character, while an optional string field accepts any string, the empty one included. And an integer field rejects JSON booleans and rejects any number carrying a fractional part, while a ratio field accepts either an integer or a decimal number and is rounded half up to four places on ingest.
16. `expected_loan_version` matches the target loan's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), on workout-plan creation.
17. `expected_pledge_campaign_version` matches the target campaign's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), on pledge creation.
18. The governance packet occupies the state the requested transition departs from — `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on packet submission, board verification, the denominational decision and the return to draft.
19. The loan application occupies the state the requested transition departs from — `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on submit-underwriting, committee referral, the committee decision and loan origination. At origination this position answers every status other than `APPROVED`, with one carve-out: a `FUNDED` application that a loan already references is deferred to position 26 so that `ERR_LOAN_ALREADY_ORIGINATED` stays reachable.
20. The loan is still in its draw phase, meaning `servicing_status` is `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW` or `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW` — `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_DRAW_PHASE` (HTTP 409), on draw creation, approval, disbursement and voiding.
21. The draw occupies the state the requested transition departs from, and a construction completion additionally requires that no draw on the loan sits in `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` — `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on draw approval, disbursement, rejection, voiding and reinstatement, and on construction completion.
22. The covenant occupies the state the requested transition departs from, and an explicit cure additionally requires `last_test_result` to be `PASS` — `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on a cure and on a waiver.
23. The pledge is `ACTIVE` for an installment record or a lapse, and `LAPSED` for a reinstatement — `ERR_PLEDGE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on those three pledge actions.
24. The campaign is not `CLOSED` — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), on pledge creation, installment recording, pledge reinstatement, and a second close of an already-closed campaign.
25. The workout plan named in the path is `PROPOSED`, and the loan's `risk_posture` is `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `WORKOUT_GATED` — `ERR_WORKOUT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on workout creation and workout approval. A creation has no target plan yet, so it is judged by the posture clause alone; any predecessor it names is left to position 41.
26. No loan already references the application being funded — `ERR_LOAN_ALREADY_ORIGINATED` (HTTP 409), on loan origination. This is the answer to a repeat origination against the `FUNDED` application the first origination produced.
27. The loan's `servicing_status` is not `PAID_OFF` — `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), on covenant creation, a covenant test, a principal payment and a construction completion.
28. The board vote clears the bylaws quorum — `ERR_BOARD_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422), on board verification.
29. The amount under review does not exceed the institution's bylaws borrowing cap — `ERR_BYLAWS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on packet submission, where the amount tested is `authorized_amount_cents`, and on submit-underwriting, where it is `requested_amount_cents`.
30. The governance packet is `AUTHORIZED` — `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 409), on committee referral.
31. The packet's `expires_on` is at or after the current logical instant — `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409), on committee referral and on the committee decision.
32. A denominational decision is only recordable on a packet whose `denominational_body` is populated, and an institution that requires denominational approval cannot reach `AUTHORIZED` without one — `ERR_DENOMINATIONAL_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), on the denominational decision.
33. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the application's `prepared_by_user_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), on the committee decision.
34. The asset's `asset_type` is not `DONOR_RESTRICTED_ENDOWMENT` — `ERR_ENDOWMENT_COLLATERAL_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), on a collateral assignment.
35. The asset's `appraisal_age_days` is at most 730 — `ERR_COLLATERAL_APPRAISAL_STALE` (HTTP 422), on a collateral assignment.
36. The new giving period's closed interval shares no instant with any existing period of the same institution — `ERR_GIVING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on giving-period creation.
37. The proposed lien window clears every prior occupation of the same asset once each prior occupation's end is extended by the 30-day release buffer — `ERR_COLLATERAL_LIEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), on a collateral assignment.
38. At least one collateral asset is assigned to the application in first-lien position — `ERR_COLLATERAL_LINK_BROKEN` (HTTP 409), on committee referral.
39. Cross-referenced rows belong to the same institution — `ERR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409), on loan-application creation, where the governance packet's `institution_id` and the campaign's `institution_id` must both equal the application's `institution_id`, and on a collateral assignment, where the asset's `institution_id` must equal the target application's `institution_id`.
40. The asset is unassigned for an assignment and assigned for a release — `ERR_COLLATERAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 409), on both collateral actions.
41. A supplied `predecessor_workout_plan_id` references a plan on the same loan whose `status` is `APPROVED` — `ERR_PREDECESSOR_WORKOUT_INVALID` (HTTP 409), on workout creation.
42. The institution has at least four recorded giving periods — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_GIVING_HISTORY` (HTTP 422), on submit-underwriting.
43. Restricted contributions and the donor-restricted endowment draw together do not exceed total contributions — `ERR_RESTRICTED_EXCEEDS_TOTAL` (HTTP 422), on giving-period creation.
44. The plan's mission-impact score clears its option's floor — `ERR_MISSION_IMPACT_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 422), on workout approval.
45. Every covenant on the loan is `COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED` — `ERR_DRAW_BLOCKED_BY_COVENANT_BREACH` (HTTP 409), on draw disbursement.
46. A `PLEDGE_ADVANCE` application names a backing campaign — `ERR_PLEDGE_CAMPAIGN_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), on submit-underwriting.
47. The requested amount is within the maximum supportable principal — `ERR_LTV_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), on committee referral.
48. The aggregate draw on one board resolution stays inside the amount that resolution authorised — `ERR_AUTHORIZED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on an approving committee decision. Position 47 already caps any single request at the authorised amount, so only the aggregate across several applications can breach here.
49. The draw amount is within the free commitment — `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT` (HTTP 422), on draw creation, where `requested_amount_cents` is tested, and on draw approval and reinstatement, where `approved_amount_cents` is tested.
50. The net cash bucket of the disbursement waterfall is strictly positive — `ERR_DRAW_NET_NONPOSITIVE` (HTTP 422), on draw disbursement.
51. `forgiveness_amount_cents` does not exceed the loan's outstanding principal — `ERR_FORGIVENESS_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422), on workout approval.
52. `amount_cents` on a principal payment does not exceed the loan's outstanding principal — `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422), on a loan payment.

### 2.11. The Commitment Conservation Invariant

A construction loan's commitment is a closed system. At origination, the full principal sits in `undrawn_commitment_cents` and every other ledger bucket is zero. From then on, the sum of `disbursed_principal_cents`, `undrawn_commitment_cents` and `cancelled_commitment_cents` must always equal `principal_cents`. Approving a draw does not move money between those three buckets; it reserves capacity by increasing `reserved_draw_cents`, which is a claim against the undrawn balance rather than a fourth bucket of it. The quantity every sizing rule calls the **free commitment** is therefore `undrawn_commitment_cents` less `reserved_draw_cents`.

Retainage is conserved in the same spirit: cash withheld from a disbursement accumulates in `retainage_held_cents`, and the only path out of that bucket is into `retainage_released_cents` at construction completion. Principal outstanding is likewise an identity rather than an independent counter — `outstanding_principal_cents` is always `disbursed_principal_cents` reduced by `principal_repaid_cents` and reduced again by `forgiven_principal_cents`.

Two operations exist purely to restore these identities after the fact. Voiding a disbursed draw moves every bucket the disbursement moved back by the identical amount. Reinstating a suspended draw restores exactly the reservation that the suspension released. Neither invents a compensating amount; both replay the original figures.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below follow a credit through the institution's life with the lender: building the giving record, establishing authority to borrow, valuing campaigns and collateral, underwriting, committee, origination, construction administration, covenant surveillance and — where the credit deteriorates — workout.

### 3.1. Onboarding an Institution and Building the Giving Record

A `PlatformAdmin` opens the relationship by onboarding the borrower. The payload names the borrower's registered `legal_name`, classifies it through `institution_type` as one of `CONGREGATION`, `SCHOOL`, `DENOMINATIONAL_AGENCY` or `NONPROFIT_MINISTRY`, and records its `denomination_code` — the affiliation code such as PCA, ELCA, SBC or INDEPENDENT. Two bylaws figures are mandatory and shape everything downstream: `bylaws_borrowing_cap_cents`, the strictly positive maximum aggregate borrowing the bylaws permit, and `bylaws_quorum_pct`, the fraction of the total board vote a borrowing resolution must carry, which must be greater than zero, at most 1, and is held to four decimal places. Three inputs are optional: `requires_denominational_approval`, which defaults to false when omitted and, when true, forces the governance packet through a denominational decision before it can confer authority; `community_program_count`, the number of ongoing community programs such as a food pantry, tutoring or a shelter, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative; and `endowment_market_value_cents`, the donor-restricted endowment's market value, which defaults to 0, may not be negative, and — per §2.7 — is disclosure only.

Onboarding initialises five derived figures to 0: `audited_period_count`, `period_count`, `unrestricted_giving_base_cents`, `net_available_for_debt_service_dollars` and `cumulative_forgiveness_cents`; `membership_trend_pct` opens null, because no window exists yet to measure a trend over. The first two are simple counts — the number of giving periods on file whose `source` is `AUDITED`, and the number on file regardless of provenance. The last accumulates principal forgiven through approved forgiveness workouts, described in §3.9.

A `CreditAnalyst` or the institution's own `InstitutionRepresentative` then files fiscal history one period at a time. A period carries the `institution_id` it belongs to, a human `period_label` such as "2025Q3", and a **closed** interval delimited by `period_start` and `period_end` — both inclusive, both ISO 8601 UTC instants at midnight by convention, though the server enforces no midnight alignment and accepts an instant at any time of day. Its `source` is either `AUDITED` or `INTERNAL`. The financial payload is four non-negative cent amounts and one non-negative count: `total_contributions_cents`, `restricted_contributions_cents`, `donor_restricted_endowment_draw_cents`, `operating_expense_cents` (operating expenses excluding debt service) and `member_household_count`, the giving households of record as at the period's end. Because a period's interval is closed at both ends, two periods of the same institution collide if they share even a single touching endpoint: a new period is refused with `ERR_GIVING_PERIOD_OVERLAP` whenever its start falls at or before an existing period's end and that existing period's start falls at or before the new period's end. A period whose restricted contributions and donor-restricted endowment draw sum to more than its total contributions is refused with `ERR_RESTRICTED_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`.

Two figures are computed on the period itself and stored with it. `unrestricted_contributions_cents` is total contributions reduced by restricted contributions and reduced again by the donor-restricted endowment draw, with the result floored at zero if those two deductions together would exceed the total. `net_available_for_debt_service_cents` is that unrestricted figure reduced by the period's operating expense, again floored at zero, so a period that spent more than it took in unrestricted giving contributes nothing rather than a negative amount.

Filing a period also reconciles its institution. `period_count` rises by 1, and `audited_period_count` rises by 1 as well when the new period's `source` is `AUDITED`. The institution's `unrestricted_giving_base_cents`, `net_available_for_debt_service_dollars` and `membership_trend_pct` are all recomputed, and the institution's `version` advances by 1.

The **four-period window** is the unit of analysis for every giving figure in this system, and it is selected identically everywhere it is used. Candidates are the institution's giving periods whose `period_end` falls at or before the as-of instant — the current logical instant for institution reads and workout scoring, or an explicitly supplied date where an endpoint takes one. Candidates are ordered by `period_end` descending, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, and where two candidates share both a `period_end` and a `created_at` value the earlier-persisted row still sorts first, the server breaking that residual tie by its own insertion order. The window is then the first four candidates in that order. Which four depends on the **giving basis**: under `AUDITED_ONLY` the ordered candidate list is first filtered to periods whose `source` is `AUDITED` and the window is the first four of those; under `BLENDED` the window is simply the first four candidates of either provenance.

The two bases also treat the periods differently once selected. Under `AUDITED_ONLY`, each period contributes its full `unrestricted_contributions_cents` and no haircut applies. Under `BLENDED`, an `AUDITED` period still contributes its full unrestricted figure, but an `INTERNAL` period contributes only 85 percent of it — that is, its unrestricted figure multiplied by 1 less the 15 percent internal-statement haircut, floored to whole cents **per period** before anything is summed. The giving base is the sum of those per-period contributions across the window. When the window itself holds fewer than four periods the base is 0, and so is the net-available figure of §3.5 computed over the same window — whether that shortfall arises because the institution has fewer than four periods on file, because fewer than four of them end at or before the as-of instant, or because under `AUDITED_ONLY` fewer than four of the candidates are `AUDITED`.

An institution's `unrestricted_giving_base_cents` and `net_available_for_debt_service_dollars` are reported under the mandated basis rather than a caller-chosen one: `AUDITED_ONLY` when `audited_period_count` is 4 or more, and `BLENDED` otherwise. Both are 0 while `period_count` is below 4. All five figures — the two period counts, these two aggregates and `membership_trend_pct` — are recomputed from the periods on file, as at the current logical instant, on every institution read as well as on every period filing, so repositioning the clock changes what an institution read reports without any write having occurred and without advancing the institution's `version`.

`membership_trend_pct` measures the direction of the congregation over the same window. Take the window's newest period — the first element in the ordering above — and its oldest, the fourth. The trend is the newest period's `member_household_count` less the oldest period's, divided by the oldest period's, expressed as a fraction and rounded half up to four decimal places. A shrinking congregation therefore reports a negative trend. The figure is null rather than a number in exactly two cases: where the window under the basis in force holds fewer than four periods, and where the oldest period's `member_household_count` is 0, since the trend would then divide by zero.

### 3.2. Establishing Authority to Borrow

No congregation may borrow merely because its treasurer wishes to. An `InstitutionRepresentative` opens a **governance packet** that evidences the board's resolution: the `institution_id` whose board adopted it, the `resolution_date` on which it was adopted, the `bylaws_citation` — the article and section conferring authority to borrow — the vote tally as `board_votes_for` (which may be zero or more) and `board_votes_total` (which must be strictly positive), the strictly positive `authorized_amount_cents` the resolution authorises, and the `expires_on` instant after which the authorisation no longer supports a committee referral. `denominational_body` — the name of the presbytery, synod or conference that must approve — is optional and left null where the institution needs no denominational approval. A new packet opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, and with `denominational_decision_note`, `verified_by_user_id`, `board_quorum_pct`, `minutes_reference` and `rework_note` all null. Neither supplied instant is validated against the other or against the logical clock at this point: a `resolution_date` falling after `expires_on`, or an `expires_on` already in the past, is accepted on creation and bites only later, at the expiry test on referral.

Submission for governance review is the representative's own act, permitted only on its own institution's packet and only while the packet is in `DRAFT`; it takes no request body. The one substantive check is fiscal: a resolution authorising more than the bylaws permit — an `authorized_amount_cents` exceeding the institution's `bylaws_borrowing_cap_cents` — is refused with `ERR_BYLAWS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The board tally is not examined at submission; the quorum arithmetic belongs to verification alone, so a packet whose vote could never clear the quorum still submits successfully. On success the packet moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by 1.

Board verification is reserved to a `GovernanceOfficer`, who supplies a `minutes_reference` locating the resolution in the board minutes. The officer's job is arithmetic: the achieved quorum is `board_votes_for` divided by `board_votes_total`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and it must be greater than or equal to the institution's `bylaws_quorum_pct`. A tally that falls short is refused with `ERR_BOARD_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; a tally exactly equal to the required fraction passes. On success the computed quorum is stored as `board_quorum_pct`, `verified_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the supplied `minutes_reference` is retained on the packet in the field of that name, and `version` advances by 1.

Verification then forks, and the same act lands two states apart depending on the borrower. Where the institution's `requires_denominational_approval` is true, the packet moves `SUBMITTED → BOARD_VERIFIED` and still owes a denominational decision. Where it is false, the very same act moves the packet `SUBMITTED → AUTHORIZED` outright, and the packet is immediately usable for a referral. Both branches store the quorum, both stamp the verifier and both advance the version; only the destination state differs.

Recording the denominational decision is likewise a `GovernanceOfficer` act, available only on a `BOARD_VERIFIED` packet, and it carries a mandatory `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `note`. A packet whose `denominational_body` is null cannot take a decision at all and is refused with `ERR_DENOMINATIONAL_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` — which is also why an institution that requires denominational approval can never reach `AUTHORIZED` without naming the body that grants it. The submitted note is stored as `denominational_decision_note` in either branch — left null where the caller omits it — and `version` advances by 1 in either branch. `APPROVE` moves the packet `BOARD_VERIFIED → AUTHORIZED`; `REJECT` moves it `BOARD_VERIFIED → DENOM_REJECTED`.

A rejection is not the end of the packet. The `InstitutionRepresentative` may return a `DENOM_REJECTED` packet to `DRAFT` for rework — the transition `DENOM_REJECTED → DRAFT`, supplying a `rework_note`, provided the acting identifier owns the institution. That closes the governance cycle: `revision_round` rises by 1, `denominational_decision_note` and `verified_by_user_id` are both cleared to null, the supplied note is retained as `rework_note`, `board_quorum_pct` and `minutes_reference` are left exactly as verification set them rather than cleared, `version` advances by 1, and the packet may be reworked and resubmitted without opening a new one. Every one of these four governance acts is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INVALID_STATE` when the packet does not occupy the state the act departs from — board verification attempted on a `DRAFT` packet, for instance, or a return-to-draft attempted on a packet still in `SUBMITTED`.

### 3.3. Capital Campaigns, Pledge Attrition and the Two Valuations

A capital campaign is how a congregation finances a sanctuary or a school wing without waiting for the cash. An `InstitutionRepresentative` launches one against its own `institution_id` with a `campaign_name` as printed on the pledge cards, a strictly positive `goal_cents`, a strictly positive `pledge_period_months` fulfilment window, and `sponsor_collection_rate_bps` — the sponsor's own historical collection rate in basis points, between 0 and 10000 inclusive. A new campaign opens in `OPEN` with `total_pledged_cents`, `active_pledged_cents`, `lapsed_pledged_cents`, `fulfilled_pledged_cents`, `collected_to_date_cents`, `attrition_adjusted_value_cents` and `sponsor_projected_value_cents` all at 0, and `close_note` null.

Pledges may be recorded by an `InstitutionRepresentative` or a `CreditAnalyst`. Each names its `pledge_campaign_id`, a `donor_tier` of `LEAD`, `MAJOR` or `GENERAL`, a strictly positive `pledged_amount_cents` covering the whole campaign period, a strictly positive `installment_count` the donor has committed to, and — per §2.8 — the `expected_pledge_campaign_version`. A new pledge opens `ACTIVE` with `collected_cents`, `payments_made_count` and `missed_payments_count` all 0, `lapse_reason` and `reinstatement_note` both null, and `effective_attrition_pct` at its tier's base rate. The campaign is reconciled in the same call: `total_pledged_cents` and `active_pledged_cents` each rise by the pledged amount, both projected values are recomputed, and the campaign's `version` advances by 1.

**Attrition is the whole point of the exercise.** A pledge card is a promise, not cash, and the lender's assumption is that some fraction of every promise will never arrive. The base attrition rate is fixed by tier: 5 percent for a `LEAD` donor, 12 percent for a `MAJOR` donor and 22 percent for a `GENERAL` donor. A donor who has missed two or more installments is penalised a further 15 percentage points, and the resulting rate is capped at 60 percent no matter how the components add up. That figure, rounded half up to four decimal places, is reported on the pledge as `effective_attrition_pct`.

The **lender-conservative** valuation of a campaign starts from cash already in hand — `collected_to_date_cents`, summed over the campaign's pledges in every status — which is never discounted, because it has already arrived. Every pledge still in `ACTIVE` status then contributes its remaining balance, being its `pledged_amount_cents` less its `collected_cents`, multiplied by 1 less that pledge's own effective attrition rate, and floored to whole cents **per pledge**. The campaign's `attrition_adjusted_value_cents` is the collected cash plus the sum of those per-pledge contributions. Pledges in `LAPSED` or `FULFILLED` status contribute nothing beyond the cash already counted in the collected total.

The **sponsor-optimistic** valuation answers a different question and is never used for a credit decision. It aggregates first and discounts once: the remaining balances of every `ACTIVE` pledge are summed, that single total is multiplied by the campaign's `sponsor_collection_rate_bps` divided by 10000, the product is floored once to whole cents, and the collected cash is added. Because it ignores tier mix and missed payments entirely, it will normally exceed the conservative figure, and it is reported separately as `sponsor_projected_value_cents`. The conservative figure is the one that governs the underperformance gate, the campaign's own status and the sizing of a pledge-advance loan.

Installments are recorded against an `ACTIVE` pledge by a `LoanServicer` or an `InstitutionRepresentative`, naming the `installment_month` the payment relates to, an `outcome` of `PAID` or `MISSED`, and an `amount_cents`. `installment_month` is mandatory and is accepted either as an integer or as a string, with no further constraint on its value; an installment is not persisted as a resource of its own, so the month reference is not retained anywhere and only the installment's effects on the pledge and its campaign are recorded. The two outcomes are mutually exclusive in shape: `PAID` requires an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and `MISSED` requires an `amount_cents` of exactly zero. A `PAID` installment raises `collected_cents` by the amount and `payments_made_count` by 1; a `MISSED` installment raises `missed_payments_count` by 1. Either way `effective_attrition_pct` is recomputed, the campaign's buckets and both projected values are recomputed, and the campaign's `version` advances by 1.

An installment may also end the pledge's life. Once the applied installment brings `collected_cents` to or above `pledged_amount_cents`, the pledge moves `ACTIVE → FULFILLED`: its pledged amount leaves `active_pledged_cents` and enters `fulfilled_pledged_cents`, both projected values are recomputed, and the pledge's `version` advances by 1. `FULFILLED` is terminal. Separately, once a `MISSED` installment brings `missed_payments_count` to exactly 3, the pledge moves `ACTIVE → LAPSED` automatically: `lapse_reason` is set to the literal string `AUTO_THREE_MISSED_INSTALLMENTS`, its pledged amount leaves `active_pledged_cents` and enters `lapsed_pledged_cents`, both projected values are recomputed, `version` advances by 1, and the campaign may itself be driven `OPEN → UNDERPERFORMING`.

A `LoanServicer` may also lapse an `ACTIVE` pledge on judgement, supplying the mandatory non-empty `lapse_reason` to be stored verbatim. The bucket movement is identical to the automatic lapse: the pledged amount moves from `active_pledged_cents` to `lapsed_pledged_cents`, both projected values are recomputed, and `version` advances by 1 on the pledge and on the campaign. Lapsing is reversible. Reinstatement, also a `LoanServicer` act, moves the pledge `LAPSED → ACTIVE` provided the campaign is not `CLOSED`, and records a mandatory non-empty `reinstatement_note`, retained on the pledge in the field of that name. It clears `lapse_reason` back to null and preserves `collected_cents`, `payments_made_count` and `missed_payments_count` exactly as they stood — a reinstated pledge that had missed two installments still carries its penalty rate. The pledged amount moves out of `lapsed_pledged_cents` and back into `active_pledged_cents`, restoring the pledged-dollar conservation identity bucket for bucket, both projected values are recomputed, and the campaign may be driven `UNDERPERFORMING → OPEN`.

**The underperformance gate** is what turns a disappointing campaign into a credit event, and it has two conditions that must hold together. The first is a maturity condition: `total_pledged_cents` must have reached the floor of the goal multiplied by 90 percent, which keeps a brand-new campaign with no pledges at all comfortably in `OPEN`. The second is the underperformance condition proper: `attrition_adjusted_value_cents` must have fallen strictly below the floor of the goal multiplied by 80 percent. When both hold after any pledge create, installment record or lapse, the campaign moves `OPEN → UNDERPERFORMING` and its `version` advances by 1.

That transition cascades. Every loan application that names this campaign as its `pledge_campaign_id`, whose `purpose` is `PLEDGE_ADVANCE` and whose `status` is `UNDERWRITING`, is pushed `UNDERWRITING → REVISION_REQUESTED`: its `revision_reason` is set to the literal string `PLEDGE_CAMPAIGN_UNDERPERFORMING`, its `revision_count` rises by 1, and its `version` advances by 1. The reverse transition `UNDERPERFORMING → OPEN` fires whenever the conservative value recovers to or above the same 80-percent floor, following a reinstatement or an installment payment, and advances the campaign's `version` by 1 — but it does **not** roll those applications back. The pledged-dollar invariant is restored; the underwriting decision is not.

Closing a campaign is the representative's act, takes an optional `close_note` — stored on the campaign in the field of that name and left null where the caller omits it — and is available from either `OPEN` or `UNDERPERFORMING` — the transitions `OPEN → CLOSED` and `UNDERPERFORMING → CLOSED`. In both cases the campaign moves to `CLOSED` and its `version` advances by 1; closing recomputes nothing, leaving the buckets, both projected values and every dependent loan application exactly as they stood, and it cascades to no other resource. In both cases the campaign is frozen, and no further pledge creation, installment recording or reinstatement is accepted afterwards. `CLOSED` is terminal, and a second close attempt against it is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CLOSED`.

A computed read exposes one valuation at a time — the figure under the basis the caller selects, not both side by side — to any reader with visibility of the campaign. It accepts an optional `basis` selecting `LENDER_CONSERVATIVE` or `SPONSOR_OPTIMISTIC` and defaulting to `LENDER_CONSERVATIVE`; a `basis` omitted or supplied as an empty string takes that default, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). It answers with `basis` echoing the applied choice, `projected_value_cents` computed under that basis, and the raw material the figure was derived from: `collected_to_date_cents`, `active_pledged_cents`, `lapsed_pledged_cents`, `fulfilled_pledged_cents`, `total_pledged_cents`, `goal_cents`, `status`, plus the two gate thresholds as `underperform_threshold_cents` (the floor of the goal times 80 percent) and `maturity_threshold_cents` (the floor of the goal times 90 percent). Every bucket figure it reports is recomputed from the campaign's pledges at read time rather than read out of the campaign's stored bucket fields. The read mutates nothing.

### 3.4. Special-Purpose Collateral and Lien Occupation

A sanctuary is worth a great deal to the congregation that built it and comparatively little to anyone else, and the advance rate this system applies reflects exactly that. A `CreditAnalyst` or an `InstitutionRepresentative` registers a property against its `institution_id` with an `asset_type` of `SANCTUARY`, `EDUCATION_WING`, `PARSONAGE`, `LAND`, `FELLOWSHIP_HALL` or `DONOR_RESTRICTED_ENDOWMENT`, a short `description_label` for the parcel or structure, a strictly positive `appraised_value_cents` as of the appraisal, the `appraisal_date` of the report, and an `alternative_use_score` — an integer from 1 to 5 inclusive, where 1 is a single-purpose sanctuary with no alternative use and 5 is a property readily convertible to commercial use. Registration computes `appraisal_age_days` and `lending_value_cents` immediately, and leaves `assigned_loan_application_id`, `lien_position`, `lien_effective_from` and `lien_effective_to` all null, `release_note` null and `lien_history` an empty list. Only one of those two computed figures is stored: `appraisal_age_days` is derived against the current logical instant on every read of the asset, so repositioning the clock changes what a read reports without any write having occurred and without advancing the asset's `version`, while `lending_value_cents` is a stored figure rewritten only at registration, at assignment and at release — a stored value therefore does not pick up a seasoning discount that the clock has crossed since it was last written. An `appraisal_date` later than the current instant is accepted and yields a negative `appraisal_age_days`, which is neither stale nor seasoned.

The **lending value** is built in three multiplications, each floored to whole cents before the next begins, and the order is pinned.

First, the advance rate. The alternative-use score proposes a rate — 40 percent at a score of 1, 50 percent at 2, 60 percent at 3, 70 percent at 4 and 75 percent at 5 — and the asset type imposes a ceiling on it: 55 percent for a `SANCTUARY`, 65 percent for an `EDUCATION_WING`, 70 percent for a `PARSONAGE`, 50 percent for `LAND`, 60 percent for a `FELLOWSHIP_HALL` and 0 percent for a `DONOR_RESTRICTED_ENDOWMENT`. The effective advance rate is whichever of the two is lower, so a readily convertible sanctuary is still capped at 55 percent, and a donor-restricted endowment is valued at nothing whatever its score. The appraised value is multiplied by that effective rate and floored.

Second, the lien factor. A first lien carries a factor of 100 percent, a second lien 60 percent, and a third or worse lien 0 percent — any `lien_position` of 3 or higher collapses the value to zero. An asset with no current assignment is valued as though it would take a first lien. The result of the first step is multiplied by the lien factor and floored again.

Third, seasoning. When `appraisal_age_days` is strictly greater than 365 — that is, when the appraisal report is more than a year old on the logical clock — the running figure is multiplied by 90 percent; at 365 days or fewer no seasoning discount applies. The result is floored a third time, and that final integer is `lending_value_cents`.

Assigning collateral to a credit is a `CreditAnalyst` act. The payload names the `loan_application_id` taking the lien, the `lien_position` the lender will occupy, and the occupation window as `lien_effective_from` and `lien_effective_to` — a **half-open** interval, inclusive of its start and exclusive of its end, whose end must fall strictly after its start or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `lien_position` is an integer no smaller than 1 and carries no upper bound: a position of 3 or more is accepted and simply takes the third-or-worse lien factor of zero. The target application's own `status` is never examined, so an asset may be assigned to an application in any state. Four substantive checks guard it, in the order §2.10 fixes. A `DONOR_RESTRICTED_ENDOWMENT` asset is refused outright with `ERR_ENDOWMENT_COLLATERAL_INELIGIBLE`. An asset whose `appraisal_age_days` exceeds 730 — two years of staleness — is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_APPRAISAL_STALE`; at exactly 730 days it is still acceptable. An asset whose `institution_id` differs from the target application's `institution_id` is refused with `ERR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH`. An asset whose `assigned_loan_application_id` is already populated is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`.

The remaining check is the **lien release buffer**, which by the ladder of §2.10 sits at position 37 — evaluated *before* the institution-mismatch and already-assigned checks rather than after them — and it is where careless implementations go wrong. A released lien does not free the property instantly: the lender allows a 30-day tail after every prior occupation before a new one may begin. So each prior occupation of the same asset — the current assignment if any, and every entry already recorded in `lien_history` — is compared after extending its end by 30 days. The new occupation is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_LIEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` whenever the new window's start falls strictly before that extended end **and** the prior occupation's start falls strictly before the new window's end. A window that begins exactly 30 days after a prior occupation ended is therefore acceptable; one that begins 29 days after is not.

A successful assignment sets `assigned_loan_application_id`, `lien_position`, `lien_effective_from` and `lien_effective_to`, re-runs the lending-value computation with the assigned lien factor now in force — so pledging an asset in second position immediately cuts its recorded `lending_value_cents` by 40 percent — rewrites `lending_value_cents`, and advances the asset's `version` by 1.

Releasing a lien, also a `CreditAnalyst` act, takes a mandatory non-empty `release_note` — retained on the asset in the field of that name, overwriting whatever note an earlier release left there — and requires that the asset currently hold a lien; releasing an unassigned asset is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. The release appends the occupation just ended to `lien_history` as a record carrying `loan_application_id`, `lien_position`, `effective_from`, `effective_to` and `released_at`, with the list held in ascending `released_at` order and never rewritten once appended. `released_at` is the exception to §2.4's rule that audit timestamps come from real server time: it is the **current logical instant** of §2.5, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. The four current-occupation fields are cleared to null, the asset is re-valued at the first-lien factor, `lending_value_cents` is rewritten, and `version` advances by 1.

### 3.5. Underwriting: Snapshots, Coverage and the Sizing Caps

A credit request is opened by a `CreditAnalyst` or the borrower's `InstitutionRepresentative`. It names the borrowing `institution_id`, the `governance_authorization_id` of the packet evidencing authority to borrow, and optionally the `pledge_campaign_id` of a capital campaign backing the request. Its `purpose` is one of `SANCTUARY_CONSTRUCTION`, `SCHOOL_CONSTRUCTION`, `REFINANCE` or `PLEDGE_ADVANCE`; `requested_amount_cents` is the strictly positive principal sought; `term_months` is the requested amortisation term and must be exactly 120, 180 or 240; `interest_rate_bps` is the strictly positive quoted note rate in basis points. Every cross-referenced row must belong to the same institution — a governance packet or a campaign whose `institution_id` differs from the application's is refused with `ERR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH`. A new application opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0 and every snapshot and audit field null: `giving_basis`, `giving_base_cents`, `net_available_dollars`, `collateral_lending_value_cents`, `max_supportable_principal_cents`, `prepared_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `revision_reason`.

Submitting for underwriting is reserved to a `CreditAnalyst` and takes an optional `analyst_note`, which is validated as a string but persisted nowhere and echoed back on no read. It serves both the first submission and every re-submission: an application in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → UNDERWRITING`, and an application that came back from committee or from an underperforming campaign moves `REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDERWRITING` through the very same act. Three preconditions are checked in the ladder order of §2.10. The institution must have at least four giving periods on file, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_GIVING_HISTORY` — the giving base is undefined on a shorter history and would otherwise read as 0. The `requested_amount_cents` must not exceed the institution's `bylaws_borrowing_cap_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BYLAWS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And an application whose `purpose` is `PLEDGE_ADVANCE` must name a `pledge_campaign_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_CAMPAIGN_REQUIRED`.

On success the analyst's own figures are frozen onto the application. The mandated basis is resolved exactly as in §3.1 — `AUDITED_ONLY` when the institution's `audited_period_count` is 4 or more, `BLENDED` otherwise — and stored as `giving_basis`. The giving base computed under that basis is stored as `giving_base_cents`, and the net-available-for-debt-service figure under the same basis is stored as `net_available_dollars`. `prepared_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `revision_reason` is cleared to null, and `version` advances by 1. A re-submission overwrites all three snapshot figures with freshly computed values rather than preserving the earlier ones, which is the mechanism by which a revised application picks up newly filed giving periods. The two referral snapshots — `collateral_lending_value_cents` and `max_supportable_principal_cents` — are not touched by a submission at all: they stay null until the first referral and thereafter retain whatever that referral wrote, until a later referral overwrites them.

**The net-available figure, and the rounding order that defines it.** For each of the four periods in the window, take the period's unrestricted contribution under the basis in force — the full `unrestricted_contributions_cents` for an `AUDITED` period, or under `BLENDED` the floor of that figure multiplied by 85 percent for an `INTERNAL` period — subtract that period's `operating_expense_cents`, and floor the result at zero so a loss-making period contributes nothing rather than a negative amount. Convert that per-period cent figure to whole dollars by dividing by 100 and rounding half up. Only then sum the four rounded per-period dollar figures. That sum is the net-available-for-debt-service figure in whole dollars.

The order is not a matter of taste. Summing the four periods in cents and rounding the single total to dollars at the end produces a different answer — the two orders differ by a dollar on realistic figures — and an implementation must use the per-period-then-sum order everywhere this quantity appears: on the institution, in the application snapshot, in the covenant test of §3.8 and in the computed underwriting summary.

Referral to committee is the analyst's final underwriting act and takes an optional `referral_note`, which like the analyst's note is validated as a string but persisted nowhere. It is available only from `UNDERWRITING` and enforces four substantive gates in ladder order. The governance packet must be `AUTHORIZED`, or the referral is refused with `ERR_GOVERNANCE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. The packet's `expires_on` must fall at or after the current logical instant, or it is refused with `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`; a packet expiring exactly now is still good. At least one collateral asset must be assigned to this application in first-lien position, or the referral is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_LINK_BROKEN`. And the `requested_amount_cents` must not exceed the maximum supportable principal, or it is refused with `ERR_LTV_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`; a request exactly equal to that maximum is supportable.

On success two figures are snapshotted and the application moves `UNDERWRITING → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` with its `version` advanced by 1. `collateral_lending_value_cents` becomes the sum of `lending_value_cents` over the collateral assets assigned to this application whose `lien_position` is 1 — second-lien and worse assignments are excluded from the snapshot entirely. `max_supportable_principal_cents` becomes the maximum supportable principal as computed below.

**The maximum supportable principal** is the smallest of four caps, or five for a pledge advance, and each is computed independently.

The **debt-service cap** starts from the application's snapshotted `net_available_dollars`, divides it by the minimum debt-service coverage ratio of 1.2000, and floors the quotient to whole dollars — that is the annual debt service the giving can support. That dollar figure is then multiplied by the term factor for the application's `term_months`: 5.5 at 120 months, 7.0 at 180 months and 8.5 at 240 months. The product is floored to whole dollars and finally multiplied by 100 to express the cap in cents.

The **collateral cap** is the sum of `lending_value_cents` over the collateral assets assigned to this application whose `lien_position` is 1.

The **bylaws cap** is the institution's `bylaws_borrowing_cap_cents` as it stands.

The **authorised-amount cap** is the governance packet's `authorized_amount_cents`.

For an application whose `purpose` is `PLEDGE_ADVANCE`, a fifth cap applies: the floor of the backing campaign's `attrition_adjusted_value_cents` multiplied by 60 percent. The lender advances against the conservative campaign valuation and never against the sponsor-optimistic one. Where such an application names no `pledge_campaign_id` at all — reachable through the computed read below, since submit-underwriting refuses that combination — the fifth cap simply does not apply and the minimum is taken over the four caps that remain.

The maximum supportable principal is the lowest of the caps in force.

A computed read exposes the whole underwriting picture without mutating the application, and is available to `PlatformAdmin`, `CreditAnalyst`, `CreditCommitteeMember` and the borrower's `InstitutionRepresentative`. It accepts an optional `giving_basis` query selecting `AUDITED_ONLY` or `BLENDED`; when omitted or supplied as an empty string it defaults to the application's snapshotted `giving_basis`, or, where no snapshot exists yet, to the mandated basis, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). It answers with `giving_basis` echoing the applied choice, `period_count` and `audited_period_count` from the institution, `window_period_labels` — the four `period_label` values of the window in `period_end` descending order, or fewer than four where fewer periods qualify under the applied basis — `giving_base_cents` and `net_available_dollars` recomputed under the applied basis as at the current logical instant, `membership_trend_pct` to four decimal places or null on the terms of §3.1, `collateral_lending_value_cents` summed over first-lien assigned assets, `max_supportable_principal_cents`, `requested_amount_cents`, and `binding_cap`, which names which cap produced the minimum as one of `DEBT_SERVICE`, `COLLATERAL`, `BYLAWS_CAP`, `AUTHORIZED_AMOUNT` or `PLEDGE_ADVANCE_LTV`. Where two or more caps tie at the minimum, `binding_cap` reports the first to achieve it in exactly that listed order. The `max_supportable_principal_cents` this read reports is computed from the freshly recomputed `net_available_dollars` above rather than from the application's snapshot, so it can differ from the `max_supportable_principal_cents` stored on the application at referral once new periods are filed or the clock moves; the referral gate itself continues to use the snapshot, as §3.5 pins above.

### 3.6. The Committee Fork and Origination

Only a `CreditCommitteeMember` may render a decision, and the decision carries a mandatory `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DECLINE` or `REQUEST_REVISION` plus an optional `note`. Two gates precede all three arms, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.10 rather than in the order they are described here. Segregation of duties is absolute: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the application's `prepared_by_user_id`, and an analyst attempting to decide the application it underwrote is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The governance packet's `expires_on` is re-checked against the current logical instant, because a packet that was unexpired at referral may have lapsed while the application sat in committee; an expired packet is refused with `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`. Because the expiry test occupies position 31 and the segregation test position 33, a request that breaches both is answered `ERR_GOVERNANCE_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`. All three arms set `decided_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and advance the application's `version` by 1.

An **approving** decision carries one further fiscal gate that the referral could not have caught. A single board resolution may back several applications, and the resolution's `authorized_amount_cents` binds them in aggregate: the `requested_amount_cents` of the application being approved, added to the `requested_amount_cents` of every **other** application carrying the same `governance_authorization_id` whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `FUNDED`, must not exceed `authorized_amount_cents`. An aggregate that breaches it is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED`. Since the sizing caps of §3.5 already hold any single request at or below the authorised amount, only the aggregate can fail here. On success the application moves `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → APPROVED`; the submitted `note` is discarded on this arm alone, leaving `revision_reason` exactly as it stood rather than overwriting it.

A **declining** decision moves the application `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → DECLINED`, stores the submitted note as `revision_reason` — written as null where the caller omits it, on this arm and on the revision-requesting arm alike — and releases the collateral: every asset assigned to this application has its `assigned_loan_application_id`, `lien_position`, `lien_effective_from` and `lien_effective_to` cleared to null, with the occupation just ended appended to that asset's `lien_history` exactly as an explicit release would append it — every assigned asset whatever its lien position, and with the asset's `release_note` left untouched, since the committee supplies none. `DECLINED` is terminal.

A **revision-requesting** decision moves the application `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, stores the submitted note as `revision_reason`, raises `revision_count` by 1, and — in deliberate contrast to a decline — keeps every collateral assignment intact, since the analyst is expected to rework and re-submit against the same security. It is the only arm of the fork that re-enters the cycle.

Any of the three attempted while the application is not in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, as is a submit-underwriting attempt on an application that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or a referral attempt on an application that is not in `UNDERWRITING`.

Origination is a `LoanServicer` act available only against an `APPROVED` application. It names the `loan_application_id` being funded, the strictly positive `annual_debt_service_cents` drawn from the lender's amortisation schedule, and `interest_reserve_shortfall_cents` — the interest reserve still to be funded out of future draws, which may be zero but not negative. Attempting origination against an application in `DRAFT`, `UNDERWRITING`, `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `DECLINED` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`; attempting a second origination against the `FUNDED` application the first one produced is refused instead with `ERR_LOAN_ALREADY_ORIGINATED`.

A successful origination copies the application's `requested_amount_cents` into the loan's `principal_cents` — which is never mutated thereafter — and into `undrawn_commitment_cents`, copies `term_months` and `giving_basis` across, and opens the loan at `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW` on the servicing dimension and `OPEN` on the risk dimension. Every remaining ledger figure starts at 0: `disbursed_principal_cents`, `cancelled_commitment_cents`, `reserved_draw_cents`, `retainage_held_cents`, `retainage_released_cents`, `outstanding_principal_cents`, `principal_repaid_cents`, `forgiven_principal_cents` and `deferred_payments_count`, and the two construction close-out fields `final_inspection_passed` and `completion_note` open null rather than false and empty. The application itself moves `APPROVED → FUNDED` and its `version` advances by 1. The loan's `giving_basis` is now fixed and every covenant test on this loan will use it, regardless of how the institution's audited history subsequently grows.

### 3.7. Construction Draw Administration and the Disbursement Waterfall

Sanctuary and school projects are funded progressively, not in a lump. A draw request is raised by a `LoanServicer` or the borrower's `InstitutionRepresentative` against a `loan_id` whose loan is still in its draw phase, naming the `project_phase` the money funds — `SITE_WORK`, `FOUNDATION`, `SHELL`, `SANCTUARY_FITOUT`, `SCHOOL_FITOUT` or `FINAL` — a strictly positive gross `requested_amount_cents`, and `percent_complete`, the builder-certified completion fraction for that phase, greater than 0 and at most 1, carried to four decimal places. A request against a loan whose `servicing_status` is `AMORTIZING` or `PAID_OFF` is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_DRAW_PHASE`. A request whose gross amount exceeds the free commitment of §2.11 — `undrawn_commitment_cents` less `reserved_draw_cents` — is refused with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`. Both `project_phase` and `percent_complete` are recorded for the file and for filtering only: neither is an input to any gate or to any waterfall figure, the completion tier of the waterfall being computed from the commitment ledger instead, so a draw certified 95 percent complete on its phase may still sit in the lowest retainage tier. A new request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `approved_amount_cents`, `inspector_note`, `void_reason`, `disbursement_date`, `reinstatement_note` and every waterfall figure null. The loan's `risk_posture` and its covenants are not examined at creation: a request may be raised against a loan in `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `WORKOUT_GATED`, and the covenant gate bites only at disbursement.

Approval is a `LoanServicer` act on a `SUBMITTED` draw and carries the `approved_amount_cents` the servicer will actually fund together with a mandatory `inspector_note`. The approved amount may be zero but never negative, and may be less than or equal to the gross requested amount but never more — an `approved_amount_cents` above the draw's `requested_amount_cents` is a request-shape failure at position 15 and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, ahead of both the draw-phase test and the draw-state test. It must also fit inside the free commitment; an approval that breaches the free commitment is refused with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`. On success the draw moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `approved_amount_cents` and `inspector_note` are stored, the loan's `reserved_draw_cents` rises by the approved amount, and `version` advances by 1 on the draw and by 1 on the loan. Rejection is the sibling arm: on a `SUBMITTED` draw it stores the submitted `inspector_note`, moves the draw `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, advances the draw's `version` by 1, and leaves every ledger figure on the loan untouched. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Disbursement is where cash moves, and it is guarded twice over. The draw must be `APPROVED` and the loan must still be in `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW` or `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW`. Every covenant on the loan must be `COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED`; if any sits in `BREACH_PENDING_CURE` or `BREACHED` the disbursement is refused with `ERR_DRAW_BLOCKED_BY_COVENANT_BREACH`. An optional `disbursement_date` may be supplied for the record; it is stored on the draw in the field of that name exactly as the client rendered it, and written as null where the caller omits it.

**The waterfall.** Let the gross figure be the draw's `approved_amount_cents`. The computation runs in three steps, in this order.

Step one establishes the completion tier from the **post-draw** cumulative position — the tier this very draw creates, not the one it started from. Add the gross amount to the loan's `disbursed_principal_cents` as it stands immediately before this disbursement, multiply that post-draw total by 10000, divide by the loan's `principal_cents` discarding any remainder, then divide by 10000 again to express the result as a fraction floored to four decimal places. That fraction is stored on the draw as `completion_pct_at_disbursement`. Pinning the tier to the post-draw position is what makes the apparent circularity — retainage depending on the tier the draw itself produces — well defined.

Step two takes the retainage rate from that tier. Below a completion fraction of 0.5000 the rate is 10 percent; from 0.5000 up to but not including 0.9000 it is 5 percent; at 0.9000 and above it is 0 percent. `retainage_cents` is the gross amount multiplied by that rate, floored to whole cents.

Step three runs a three-bucket waterfall over what remains. The pool starts as the gross amount less the retainage. The first bucket sweeps the interest reserve: it takes whichever is smaller of the pool and the loan's `interest_reserve_shortfall_cents`, and that amount is stored as `interest_reserve_applied_cents` and removed from the pool. The second bucket takes the inspection fee: whichever is smaller of what remains in the pool and 45000 cents — a flat 450 dollars per disbursement — stored as `inspection_fee_cents` and removed from the pool. Whatever is left is the third bucket, `net_disbursed_cents`, the cash actually released to the institution. The third bucket must be strictly greater than zero; a draw whose retainage, interest-reserve sweep and inspection fee together consume the entire gross amount is refused with `ERR_DRAW_NET_NONPOSITIVE`.

A successful disbursement stores `completion_pct_at_disbursement`, `retainage_cents`, `interest_reserve_applied_cents`, `inspection_fee_cents` and `net_disbursed_cents` on the draw and moves it `APPROVED → DISBURSED`. On the loan, `reserved_draw_cents` falls by the approved amount because the reservation has been consumed; `disbursed_principal_cents` rises by the approved amount and `undrawn_commitment_cents` falls by the same amount; `outstanding_principal_cents` rises by the approved amount; `retainage_held_cents` rises by `retainage_cents`; and `interest_reserve_shortfall_cents` falls by `interest_reserve_applied_cents`. When this is the first disbursement on the loan — that is, when `disbursed_principal_cents` stood at 0 before it — the loan also moves `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW → CONSTRUCTION_DRAW` on its servicing dimension. `version` advances by 1 on the draw and by 1 on the loan. Note that the risk dimension is untouched: cash movement never changes `risk_posture`.

Voiding is the compensating action for a disbursement, available to a `LoanServicer` on a `DISBURSED` draw while the loan is still in `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW` or `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW`, and requires a `void_reason` which is stored on the draw. Every movement the disbursement made is replayed backwards using the figures the disbursement itself recorded: `disbursed_principal_cents` falls by the approved amount and `undrawn_commitment_cents` rises by the same amount, `retainage_held_cents` falls by `retainage_cents`, `interest_reserve_shortfall_cents` rises by `interest_reserve_applied_cents`, and `outstanding_principal_cents` falls by the approved amount. The draw moves `DISBURSED → VOIDED`, which is terminal, and `version` advances by 1 on the draw and by 1 on the loan. Where the void brings `disbursed_principal_cents` back to 0, the loan also moves `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW → PENDING_FIRST_DRAW`, returning to its pre-draw servicing state.

Suspension and reinstatement form the other reversible pair on a draw, and they are driven by credit rather than by cash. When a loan's `risk_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`, every draw on that loan sitting in `APPROVED` is moved `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and the loan's `reserved_draw_cents` falls by that draw's `approved_amount_cents`, releasing the reservation; `version` advances by 1 on the draw and by 1 on the loan. The sweep is driven by the transition into `RESTRICTED` rather than by the posture merely standing there, so a draw approved while the loan already sits in `RESTRICTED` is accepted and stays `APPROVED` — nothing re-sweeps it, and only the covenant gate at disbursement keeps its cash in. Suspended draws are never reinstated automatically. Once the posture recovers, a `LoanServicer` must reinstate each one explicitly with a `reinstatement_note`: the draw moves `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, the loan's `reserved_draw_cents` rises again by the approved amount, and `version` advances by 1 on the draw and on the loan. Reinstatement requires the loan's `risk_posture` to be `OPEN` or `WATCH` — a loan still `RESTRICTED` or `WORKOUT_GATED` cannot reinstate, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE`, the posture being read as part of the same position-21 state test that checks the draw's own status rather than as a separate rung — and the approved amount must still fit the free commitment, or the request is refused with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`.

Any draw act attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE` — a disbursement on a `SUBMITTED` draw, a reinstatement on an `APPROVED` draw, a void on a `REJECTED` draw, and so on.

Construction closes out through a `LoanServicer` act that requires `final_inspection_passed` to be present and true — a completion submitted with it false is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and accepts an optional `completion_note`, which is stored on the loan in the field of that name and written as null where the caller omits it; `final_inspection_passed` is likewise retained on the loan as true. No draw on the loan may still be open: if any sits in `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` the completion is refused with `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE`. The act is available from either draw-phase state and lands in the same place in both cases: `PENDING_FIRST_DRAW → AMORTIZING` for a loan that never drew, and `CONSTRUCTION_DRAW → AMORTIZING` for one that did. Both variants perform the same two ledger movements — the whole of `undrawn_commitment_cents` moves into `cancelled_commitment_cents` and `undrawn_commitment_cents` is set to 0, and the whole of `retainage_held_cents` moves into `retainage_released_cents` and `retainage_held_cents` is set to 0 — and both advance `version` by 1. A completion attempted on a `PAID_OFF` loan is refused with `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL`, and a completion attempted on an `AMORTIZING` loan — the state a first completion leaves the loan in, so this is how a second completion answers — is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_DRAW_PHASE`. That draw-phase test is the one place it does not sit at position 20: on this act it is evaluated after both the open-draw sweep of position 21 and the terminal test of position 27.

Principal repayment is likewise a `LoanServicer` act, carrying `amount_cents` — which may be zero but not negative — and a mandatory `payment_date`, which is validated as an ISO 8601 instant but persisted nowhere and echoed back on no read, an installment of principal not being a resource of its own. The amount must not exceed the loan's `outstanding_principal_cents`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`; a payment against a `PAID_OFF` loan is refused with `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL`. A successful payment raises `principal_repaid_cents` by the amount, lowers `outstanding_principal_cents` by the same amount, and advances `version` by 1. When the payment brings `outstanding_principal_cents` to exactly 0 **and** the loan is in `AMORTIZING`, the loan moves `AMORTIZING → PAID_OFF`. That state is terminal on the servicing dimension, and the loan's `risk_posture` freezes at whatever value the risk dimension last held.

### 3.8. Covenant Surveillance, the Breach Cycle and Risk Posture

Covenants on a faith-based credit are written on giving, not on earnings. A `CreditAnalyst` writes one against a live `loan_id`, naming the tested quantity through `covenant_type` — `DSCR_UNRESTRICTED_GIVING`, `MEMBERSHIP_TREND` or `PLEDGE_COLLECTION_RATE` — a strictly positive `threshold_ratio` held to four decimal places, and a contractual `test_frequency` of `QUARTERLY` or `ANNUAL`. A covenant may not be written on a `PAID_OFF` loan; the attempt is refused with `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL`. A new covenant opens `COMPLIANT` with `consecutive_fail_count`, `test_count` and `waiver_count` all 0, and `last_test_as_of`, `last_test_numerator_dollars`, `last_test_denominator_dollars`, `last_test_ratio`, `last_test_result` and `cure_evidence_note` all null. `waiver_note` is the one exception to that pattern: it is not a member of the covenant representation at all until a waiver writes it, so a covenant that has never been waived reports no such field rather than a null one. Writing it re-evaluates the loan's risk posture, which cannot change as a result — a brand-new covenant is compliant by construction.

Testing is a `LoanServicer` act carrying a single `as_of_date`, and it is refused with `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL` on a `PAID_OFF` loan. **The tested ratio is computed as follows, and it is the same ratio whatever the covenant's `covenant_type`.** The type is recorded, reported and filterable, but it does not select a different formula: a `MEMBERSHIP_TREND` or `PLEDGE_COLLECTION_RATE` covenant is tested on exactly the coverage arithmetic below rather than on a household trend or a collection rate of its own, and its `threshold_ratio` is therefore read as a coverage ratio too. The numerator is the net-available-for-debt-service figure of §3.5 in whole dollars, computed for the institution that borrowed — reached through the loan's application — under the basis frozen on the loan as `giving_basis`, with the four-period window selected as of the submitted `as_of_date` rather than the current clock. The denominator is the loan's current `annual_debt_service_cents` divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole dollars; "current" matters, because an approved term-extension workout may have lowered it. The ratio is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places — except where that conversion yields a denominator of 0 dollars, meaning an `annual_debt_service_cents` below 50, in which case the division is not attempted at all and `last_test_ratio` is recorded as exactly 0.0000, so the test fails against any threshold. The test result is `PASS` when the ratio is greater than or equal to the covenant's `threshold_ratio` and `FAIL` otherwise — the boundary is inclusive, so a ratio landing exactly on the threshold passes. The lender's own floor for this coverage is 1.2000, and it is the same figure the debt-service sizing cap of §3.5 divides by.

Every test, whatever its result and whatever the covenant's state, stores `last_test_as_of` from the submitted date, `last_test_numerator_dollars`, `last_test_denominator_dollars`, `last_test_ratio` and `last_test_result`, and raises `test_count` by 1. What differs is where the covenant lands and what happens to `consecutive_fail_count`.

From `COMPLIANT`, a failing test moves the covenant `COMPLIANT → BREACH_PENDING_CURE` and sets `consecutive_fail_count` to 1. This is the soft-breach window, and it is what drives the loan `OPEN → WATCH` on the risk dimension.

From `BREACH_PENDING_CURE`, the two possible results diverge sharply. A passing test moves the covenant `BREACH_PENDING_CURE → COMPLIANT` and resets `consecutive_fail_count` to 0 — the auto-cure, which applies to a first breach only — and may return the loan `WATCH → OPEN`. A second consecutive failing test instead moves the covenant `BREACH_PENDING_CURE → BREACHED` and sets `consecutive_fail_count` to 2, driving the loan `WATCH → RESTRICTED` and thereby suspending every `APPROVED` draw on it.

From `BREACHED`, a test does **not** move the covenant's status at all: the covenant transitions `BREACHED → BREACHED` and the loan's `risk_posture` stays `RESTRICTED`. `consecutive_fail_count` is reset to 0 on a passing result and raised by 1 on a failing one. This self-transition is not a no-op — it is the only way to record a `last_test_result` of `PASS` on a hard-breached covenant, and that recorded pass is precisely what an explicit cure requires as its evidence.

From `WAIVED`, testing resumes normally. A passing test moves the covenant `WAIVED → COMPLIANT` and resets `consecutive_fail_count` to 0. A failing test moves it `WAIVED → BREACH_PENDING_CURE` and sets `consecutive_fail_count` to 1, re-entering the soft-breach window rather than jumping straight back to `BREACHED`, and may move the loan's posture back to `WATCH`.

Filing cure evidence is a `LoanServicer` act carrying a `cure_evidence_note`, and it is available from `BREACH_PENDING_CURE` and from `BREACHED` alike — `BREACH_PENDING_CURE → COMPLIANT` and `BREACHED → COMPLIANT` respectively. In both cases the note is stored as `cure_evidence_note` and `consecutive_fail_count` is reset to 0. In both cases the covenant's `last_test_result` must already be `PASS`; a cure filed while the most recent test failed, or filed against a covenant that is already `COMPLIANT`, is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`. That requirement is the reason a hard breach has no test-driven exit: a passing test alone never lifts it, because the servicer must also file the evidence.

A waiver is the committee's instrument and is reserved to a `CreditCommitteeMember`, who supplies a mandatory non-empty `waiver_note`, retained on the covenant in the field of that name. It is available only from `BREACHED`, moving the covenant `BREACHED → WAIVED`, raising `waiver_count` by 1 and leaving `consecutive_fail_count` exactly as the breach left it — a waiver forgives the breach without rewriting its history, so the counter falls back to 0 only on the next passing test or an explicit cure; attempted on a covenant in any other state it is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Risk posture is derived, never set.** After every covenant creation, test, cure and waiver, and after every workout approval, the loan's `risk_posture` is recomputed from scratch — unless the loan's `servicing_status` is `PAID_OFF`, in which case the re-derivation is skipped entirely and the frozen posture of §2.9 stands, so a cure or a waiver filed after payoff still moves the covenant while leaving `risk_posture` exactly where payoff left it. The recomputation evaluates four clauses in exactly this order and takes the first that matches. If any workout plan on this loan has `status` `APPROVED`, the posture is `WORKOUT_GATED`. Otherwise, if any covenant on this loan is `BREACHED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if any covenant on this loan is `BREACH_PENDING_CURE`, the posture is `WATCH`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Because the clauses are ordered, `WORKOUT_GATED` outranks every covenant-derived posture and persists for as long as any plan on the loan remains `APPROVED`, and a loan with one covenant hard-breached and another in its cure window reads `RESTRICTED` rather than `WATCH`. The recomputation never reads and never writes `servicing_status`.

Where the recomputation changes the posture, the loan's `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed; the covenant that triggered it advances its own `version` by 1 independently. The `WATCH → RESTRICTED` step carries the cascade described in §3.7: every `APPROVED` draw on the loan is suspended and its reservation released from `reserved_draw_cents`. Recovery runs the other way and is partial by design — curing or waiving the breached covenant while another remains in its cure window yields `RESTRICTED → WATCH`, and bringing every covenant on the loan to `COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED` yields `RESTRICTED → OPEN` or `WATCH → OPEN` — but suspended draws stay suspended until a servicer reinstates them one by one.

### 3.9. Workout Options and the Mission Impact Score

A commercial lender facing a distressed borrower has a narrow menu. A faith-based lender has a wider one, because a congregation that feeds three hundred families a week is worth restructuring even when the arithmetic is marginal. This system makes that judgement explicit and scores it.

A workout plan is proposed by a `LoanServicer` against a `loan_id` whose loan is already showing distress: `risk_posture` must be `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `WORKOUT_GATED`, and a proposal against a loan in `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_WORKOUT_INVALID_STATE`. The proposal names its `option` — `PAYMENT_DEFERRAL`, `TERM_EXTENSION`, `MISSION_IMPACT_FORBEARANCE` or `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` — a `denominational_support_letter` boolean stating whether the denominational body filed a letter of support, a `narrative` setting out the servicer's case on mission and community impact, and, per §2.8, `expected_loan_version`. Each option requires its own quantitative field, and the two that do not belong to the option are ignored rather than forbidden — a non-applicable field is neither validated nor retained, and is recorded on the plan as null whatever the caller supplied, so a `PAYMENT_DEFERRAL` proposal carrying an out-of-range `extension_months` still succeeds: `deferral_months` is required for `PAYMENT_DEFERRAL` and must be between 1 and 24 inclusive; `extension_months` is required for `TERM_EXTENSION` and must be between 1 and 120 inclusive; `forgiveness_amount_cents` is required for `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` and must be strictly positive; `MISSION_IMPACT_FORBEARANCE` requires none of the three. A proposal missing its option-specific field is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A plan may supersede an earlier one. `predecessor_workout_plan_id` is optional and null for the first plan on a loan; when supplied it must reference a plan on the **same** loan whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and a reference to a plan on another loan or to a plan that is not approved is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_WORKOUT_INVALID`.

**The mission impact score** is computed once, at proposal time, and frozen on the plan; it is never recomputed at approval, so a plan proposed against a strong giving record keeps that score even if the record subsequently weakens. The window is the four most recent giving periods of the borrowing institution whose `period_end` falls at or before the current logical instant, ordered by `period_end` descending with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, regardless of `source`. The newest period is the first element of that window and the oldest is the last. Fewer than four qualifying periods does not block the score: the window is then simply all of them, and the newest and oldest are the first and last of that shorter list — a single qualifying period is both at once, and its two trends are therefore zero.

Two trends are measured across that window. The household trend is the newest period's `member_household_count` less the oldest period's, divided by the oldest period's. The giving trend is the newest period's `unrestricted_contributions_cents` less the oldest period's, divided by the oldest period's. Each trend is then scored on the same four-step scale: 100 points when the change is zero or positive, 70 points when it is negative but no worse than a 5 percent decline, 40 points when it is worse than that but no worse than a 15 percent decline, and 10 points for anything steeper. Both decline boundaries are inclusive and are evaluated by exact integer arithmetic on the change and the oldest period's figure, so no intermediate ratio is rounded before the comparison. Where the oldest period's figure is 0 the ratio is undefined and that trend takes the full 100 points, and where no giving period at all falls at or before the current logical instant both trends take 100 points. The household score is stored as `membership_trend_points` and the giving score as `giving_trend_points`.

Two further components are not trends at all. `community_points` is the institution's `community_program_count` multiplied by 20, capped at 100 — so five ongoing programs earn full marks and a sixth adds nothing. `denominational_points` is 100 when the plan's `denominational_support_letter` is true and 0 when it is false.

The four component scores are then weighted: 35 percent on `membership_trend_points`, 25 percent on `giving_trend_points`, 20 percent on `community_points` and 20 percent on `denominational_points`. The weighted sum, rounded half up to two decimal places, is stored as `mission_impact_score`. All four component figures are whole numbers and are stored on the plan alongside the score.

A successful proposal opens the plan in `PROPOSED`, with `proposed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the submitted `narrative` and `denominational_support_letter` retained in the fields of those names, `approved_by_user_id` and `approval_note` both null, and not one ledger figure on the loan moved. Proposing costs the borrower nothing until the committee acts.

Approval is reserved to a `CreditCommitteeMember` and takes an optional `approval_note`, stored on the plan in the field of that name where supplied and left null where omitted. The plan must be `PROPOSED` and the loan's `risk_posture` must still be `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `WORKOUT_GATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WORKOUT_INVALID_STATE`. A `PAID_OFF` loan is no bar to either act: `ERR_LOAN_TERMINAL` is not among the checks a proposal or an approval performs, so a plan may be proposed and approved against a paid-off loan whose posture froze in a distressed state, and the approval's re-derivation of posture is then the skipped no-op of §3.8, leaving that frozen posture in place. Two score floors then apply, and both are inclusive: a `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` plan requires a `mission_impact_score` of at least 92.00, and a `MISSION_IMPACT_FORBEARANCE` plan requires at least 50.00. A plan falling short of its floor is refused with `ERR_MISSION_IMPACT_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. `PAYMENT_DEFERRAL` and `TERM_EXTENSION` carry no score floor. Finally, a `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` plan whose `forgiveness_amount_cents` exceeds the loan's `outstanding_principal_cents` is refused with `ERR_FORGIVENESS_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`.

On approval the plan moves `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the four options diverge in exactly one respect each — what they do to the loan's ledger.

- `TERM_EXTENSION` sets the loan's `term_months` to its current value plus `extension_months`, then rescales the debt service: the new `annual_debt_service_cents` is the old annual debt service multiplied by the old term in months, divided by the new term in months, with the result floored to whole cents. A longer amortisation therefore lowers the annual requirement, which is what makes the next covenant test easier to pass.
- `PAYMENT_DEFERRAL` raises the loan's `deferred_payments_count` by `deferral_months`, accumulating across successive plans.
- `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` raises the loan's `forgiven_principal_cents` and the institution's `cumulative_forgiveness_cents` by `forgiveness_amount_cents`, and lowers the loan's `outstanding_principal_cents` by the same amount.
- `MISSION_IMPACT_FORBEARANCE` leaves every ledger figure on the loan unchanged. Its effect is entirely one of posture and record.

Where the plan named a predecessor, that predecessor moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal, and its own `version` advances by 1. Nothing the predecessor did is unwound: a successor's ledger effects apply on top of the predecessor's, so a second forgiveness plan forgives further principal rather than replacing the first forgiveness. Approval then re-derives the loan's risk posture, which by the ordering of §3.8 lands on `WORKOUT_GATED`, the transition being `WATCH → WORKOUT_GATED` or `RESTRICTED → WORKOUT_GATED` according to where the loan stood, and no movement at all where a predecessor plan had already gated it — and `version` advances by 1 on the plan, by 1 on the loan, by 1 on any superseded predecessor, and — where the option was `PARTIAL_FORGIVENESS` — by 1 on the institution whose `cumulative_forgiveness_cents` the approval rewrote.
