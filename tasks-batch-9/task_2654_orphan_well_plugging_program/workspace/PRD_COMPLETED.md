# Orphan Well Plugging Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Across every producing basin in the state there are wellbores nobody will pay to close. An operator stops filing production, lets its financial assurance lapse, sells its worst wells to a shell company, and eventually dissolves — leaving the agency with an unplugged hole that vents methane, threatens the shallowest protected groundwater zone beneath it, and sits inside a disturbed pad that no longer has an owner willing to reclaim it. The Orphan Well Plugging Program exists to move those wells, one at a time, from "somebody else's liability" to "closed, documented and revegetated," and this service is the system of record for that journey.

The API described here carries the whole program on a single spine. It maintains the well inventory together with the operator of record, its regulatory standing and its posted bond; it accumulates monthly production history and applies the statutory orphan classification criteria; it scores each candidate on methane emissions, groundwater exposure, proximity to residents and surface disturbance so that limited dollars are spent on the worst wells first; it negotiates or compels site access from surface owners; it procures plugging capacity through competitive unit-price solicitations; it records cement placement, casing removal and downhole conditions in the field; it tracks equipment removal, pit closure and revegetation afterward; and it administers the money — state appropriations, federal grants with match requirements, and bonds forfeited from insolvent operators — with the reporting that each stream demands.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **the program is an enforcement program, not just a work queue**: an operator that cannot cover its own plugging exposure is marked delinquent automatically, a well transfer into an insolvent operator is refused, and an already-approved transfer is unwound when the transferee is later declared insolvent. Second, **money and authority are separated from execution**: the officer who authors a determination cannot decide it, the officer who creates a solicitation cannot award it, and the officer who commits funding cannot disburse it. Both commitments show up throughout §2 and §3 as concrete, testable behaviour rather than as policy aspiration.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. Neither header is optional in practice: when `X-User-Role` is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), and the same error is returned when `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the eight declared roles, when `X-User-ID` is missing on an endpoint whose rules reference the acting user, or whenever the acting role is one of the two entity-backed roles (`Contractor`, `Operator`), which must carry their identity on *every* endpoint because that identity is what resolves ownership. Both headers are read with surrounding whitespace stripped, and a header that is present but empty or entirely whitespace counts as absent. Once authentication succeeds, the role is checked against the set of roles authorized for the requested endpoint (see the table in §4); a role outside that set is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `FieldInspector`, `DeterminationReviewer`, `ProcurementOfficer`, `AwardAuthority`, `FundingOfficer`, `Contractor` and `Operator`.

- A `ProgramAdmin` is the state program administrator. It registers operators and wells, resolves site access, decides transfers, takes enforcement action against operators, registers contractors and funding streams, orders plugging jobs, and reviews filed field reports. It has full read access across every collection. It cannot decide orphan determinations, cannot evaluate or award bids, and cannot run funding commitments or disburse allocations.
- A `FieldInspector` inventories wells, reports production, authors orphan determinations and submits them, opens site-access requests, mobilizes crews, files field reports and inspects reclamation tasks. It reads wells, production records, determinations, site accesses, transfers, contractors, contracts, jobs and reclamation tasks. It cannot decide the determinations it authors — that belongs to `DeterminationReviewer` — and it cannot touch procurement or funding.
- A `DeterminationReviewer` confirms, rejects or returns orphan determinations for revision, and reviews plugging field reports. It reads wells, production records, determinations, site accesses, transfers, jobs, reclamation tasks, funding sources and allocations.
- A `ProcurementOfficer` drafts and runs solicitations, registers contractors, records bid responsiveness, changes nothing about the award itself, and may order plugging jobs under awarded contracts. It reads operators, wells, contractors, solicitations, bids, contracts, jobs and funding sources. It cannot award, and it cannot commit or disburse funding.
- An `AwardAuthority` makes the final award decision on an evaluated solicitation and accepts or rejects federal grant reports. It reads operators, wells, contractors, solicitations, bids, contracts, jobs, funding sources, allocations and grant reports.
- A `FundingOfficer` registers funding streams, runs the commitment waterfall, disburses or deobligates allocations, and files federal grant reports. It reads operators, wells, transfers, contractors, contracts, jobs, reclamation tasks, funding sources, allocations and grant reports.
- A `Contractor` sees its own bids and jobs, submits bids and files field reports for its own work. It cannot read or write operators, wells, determinations, funding sources or allocations at all.
- An `Operator` sees its own operator record and its own wells, reports production for them, and proposes transfers of them. It cannot author or decide determinations, resolve site access, procure, or touch funding.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to data.** Six of the eight roles are agency staff whose identity is opaque: when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `FieldInspector`, `DeterminationReviewer`, `ProcurementOfficer`, `AwardAuthority` or `FundingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a rule or transition demands an audit trail — as `created_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a determination, as `decided_by_user_id` on a transfer, as `created_by_user_id` on a solicitation, as `evaluated_by_user_id` on a bid, as `awarded_by_user_id` on a unit-cost contract, as `inspected_by_user_id` on a reclamation task, as `committed_by_user_id` and `disbursed_by_user_id` on a funding allocation, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a grant report, and as `reported_by_user_id` on a production record — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contractor` compare the resource's contractor foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Operator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Operator` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Operator` compare the resource's operator foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In neither case does the server resolve that header value to a row for its own sake: an `X-User-ID` naming no persisted `Contractor` or `Operator` produces no not-found error, it merely fails every ownership comparison it is put to.

**Ownership violations.** Several operations enforce ownership beyond the role check, and every such rejection reports `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403): the well list, the single-well fetch, the filing of a production record against a well, the bid list, the single-bid fetch, the submission of a bid, and the filing of a plugging field report. That enumeration is exhaustive — no other endpoint applies an ownership test at all, so the single-operator fetch and a well's production-record list, for two examples inside the same subject area, are open to any authorized `Operator`-role caller regardless of whose data they hold. An `Operator`-role caller violates the rule when the target well's `operator_id` is not its own id; a `Contractor`-role caller violates it when the target bid's `contractor_id`, the `contractor_id` it supplies on a bid submission, or the `contractor_id` on the contract behind the target plugging job, is not its own id.

**Segregation of duties.** Three decisions are barred to the person who set them up, and all three report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): deciding an orphan determination whose `created_by_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; awarding a solicitation whose `created_by_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; and disbursing a funding allocation whose `committed_by_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`. The gate is a plain equality test on `X-User-ID`, which is exactly why the six staff roles record an opaque identifier rather than an entity id.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a valid integer, or an `offset` below 0. A query parameter supplied as an empty string counts as absent and falls back to its default, and query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Filter parameters are matched by exact, case-sensitive string equality against the stored value, so a filter naming a value outside a field's declared set is not a schema violation — it simply matches no rows; the one numeric filter, `min_screening_score`, is read as a decimal number and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it is not one.

Unless a specific list endpoint documents a different ordering in §4, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Rows that tie on the ordering key — whether that key is `created_at` or the alternative key a specific endpoint documents — are returned in insertion order, that is, in the sequence in which they were filed; the row `id` is never part of any ordering anywhere in this API. Four pagination fields accompany every list response inside `meta`: the effective `limit`, the effective `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Row Identity, Audit Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted row is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it and must not derive meaning from its shape. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the instant of creation. Timestamps the client supplies — `to_iso8601`, `expires_at`, `bid_due_at`, `new_bid_due_at` and `scheduled_start` — must be ISO 8601 strings; a trailing `Z` or an explicit UTC offset is honoured, a value carrying no zone at all is read as UTC, and every such value is normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared. A non-string or unparseable value there is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Most entities additionally carry `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation of that row) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every successful mutation of the row. The one exception is `ProductionRecord`: a production record is an append-only ledger line, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and both fields are absent from its representation entirely. `Operator`, `Well`, `OrphanDetermination`, `SiteAccess`, `WellTransfer`, `Contractor`, `Solicitation`, `Bid`, `UnitCostContract`, `PluggingJob`, `ReclamationTask`, `FundingSource`, `FundingAllocation` and `GrantReport` all carry the full set.

A single request often mutates several rows. Each mutated row independently refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version` — a cascade that touches a well, its operator and a transfer bumps three counters, not one.

### 2.5. The Logical Regulatory Clock

Waiting periods, notice windows, expiry dates and reporting periods all read from a server-side **logical clock**, never from real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability advances the clock to a supplied instant, given as `to_iso8601`; six of the eight roles may call it (`ProgramAdmin`, `FieldInspector`, `DeterminationReviewer`, `ProcurementOfficer`, `AwardAuthority`, `FundingOfficer`), and it returns no resource body. The supplied instant is assigned unconditionally: it may be earlier than the clock's current value, and moving the clock backwards does not undo anything an earlier advance already changed. Advancing the clock is not inert: every `FundingSource` whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock value flips from `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` to `EXPIRED`, and each such source's `version` increments.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed underneath a parent — production records, orphan determinations and site accesses under a well; bids under a solicitation; reclamation tasks under a plugging job; grant reports under a funding source. Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. The parent id is resolved first, and if no such parent row exists the request fails with that parent's not-found error before the child id is examined at all. Only once the parent resolves is the child looked up, and a child that exists but belongs to a different parent is treated as not found for that path.

The full set of not-found errors is one per entity: `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORPHAN_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELL_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_COST_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLUGGING_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECLAMATION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_GRANT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` — each with HTTP 404. The same catalogue also serves foreign keys supplied in a request body rather than in the path: the `operator_id` on well creation, the `successor_operator_id` on an enforcement action, the `to_operator_id` on a transfer proposal, the `contractor_id` on bid submission, the `well_id` and `contract_id` on plugging-job creation, and the `override_source_id` on a funding commitment all return the corresponding not-found error when they name no existing row.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Contended Rows

Exactly two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the state machine alone would happily accept a second write that the caller never intended.

A `POST /wells/{id}/orphan-determinations` request must carry `expected_well_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Well`. If it does not equal the well's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WELL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this matters is that a new determination legitimately supersedes any prior determination on the same well that is still `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, so state preconditions by themselves would let a second create succeed and silently discard the first inspector's work. The version check is what tells the caller that the well it classified is no longer the well it is writing against — its operator, lifecycle status, production history or screening score has moved on. On success the endpoint itself bumps `Well.version`.

A `POST /operators/{id}/enforcement` request must carry `expected_operator_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Operator`. A mismatch with the operator's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPERATOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Successive enforcement actions against one operator are each individually legal — a declaration of insolvency followed by a bond forfeiture, or a revocation after a bond deficiency — so no state precondition would reject the second action. The version check is what prevents an officer from acting on a bond deficiency, a well count or a standing that a prior action or one of its cascades has already changed. On success the endpoint bumps `Operator.version`.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. `expected_well_version` and `expected_operator_version` are meaningful only on the two endpoints named above, and both checks run after schema validation and before any state-machine check.

### 2.8. Cents, Scores and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in whole integer cents — `bond_amount_cents`, `required_bond_cents`, `authorized_cents`, `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `allocated_cents`, `ceiling_cents`, `obligated_cents`, `plugging_cost_estimate_cents`, `actual_cost_cents`, `last_round_cost_cents`, `funded_cents`, `compensation_cents`, `transferee_required_bond_cents`, `evaluated_unit_cost_cents`, `cement_per_sack_cents`, `rig_day_rate_cents`, `casing_per_ft_cents`, `mobilization_fee_cents`, `reported_cost_cents` and `invoice_cents` are all integers, and no fractional cent is ever stored. Because unit prices and quantities are integers, every cost arithmetic step is exact; the only place a fraction can appear is the state administrative fee, and §3.7 pins exactly where that fraction is resolved.

Where a value is not an integer cent amount, the rule is uniform: **two decimal places, rounded half up.** That applies to `screening_score`, `remediation_score`, `price_score`, `composite_score`, `cumulative_boe_60mo` and `federal_share_pct`. All of them — and the unrounded row-level `boe` alongside them — are carried in the JSON payload as plain numbers, never as strings. Two rounding disciplines deserve emphasis up front because they are easy to get wrong:

- **Round components before weighting, not after.** The four risk components that feed the two priority scores are each rounded to two decimal places before any weight touches them, and the weighted sum is then rounded to two decimal places again.
- **Round aggregates once, at the aggregate.** A production record's barrel-of-oil-equivalent value `boe` is stored unrounded at the row level; the two-decimal rounding happens exactly once, on the well's sixty-month aggregate.

Integer counters — `idle_months`, `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count`, `wells_plugged_count`, `revision_round`, `rework_round`, `reopen_round`, `inspection_round`, `waterfall_rank`, `casing_strings`, `spud_year` and the groundwater band value — are exact integers and never rounded.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

A great many writes in this system have consequences beyond the row named in the path, and those consequences are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a 2xx response is returned, every derived value and every dependent state has already settled. There is no deferred recomputation and no eventual consistency anywhere in this API.

Three cascade families recur throughout §3 and are worth naming here. **Assurance recomputation** fires whenever an operator's well inventory changes shape — a new well, an approved or voided transfer, a well reaching `PLUGGED`, a well reaching `RECLAIMED`, or a bond forfeiture — and it recomputes `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents`, then flips the operator between `ACTIVE` and `DELINQUENT` if the new requirement demands it. **Licensing propagation** carries a contractor's standing onto its contracts, suspending and restoring them. **Completion rollup** carries the third completed reclamation task up to the job and then to the well. In every case, each row touched by the cascade refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version`.

### 2.10. Order of Rule Evaluation

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first violation encountered is the response:

1. **Authentication.** A missing or empty `X-User-Role` fails first with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Then an undeclared role name, or a missing `X-User-ID` on an endpoint whose rules reference the acting user, also fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** A role not listed for the endpoint fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Resource ownership.** An `Operator`- or `Contractor`-scoped caller reaching outside its own data fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Because ownership is read off the target row, any path `{id}` the test needs is resolved before the test runs: a scoped caller naming a path id that exists nowhere receives that entity's not-found error rather than an ownership error. An ownership test on a foreign key carried in the *body* still runs ahead of step 6.
4. **Segregation of duties.** A self-decision, self-award or self-disbursement fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
5. **Path existence.** A path `{id}` naming no row fails with that entity's not-found error.
6. **Body reference existence.** A foreign key in the request body naming no row fails with that entity's not-found error.
7. **Schema validation.** Missing required inputs, wrong types, enum values outside their declared sets, numeric bound violations, malformed date strings and duplicate well numbers fail with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Types are checked strictly and nothing is ever coerced: a number supplied as a string is rejected, an integer field supplied as a fractional value is rejected, and a boolean is never accepted where a number is expected. An explicit `null` is indistinguishable from an omitted key — a required input is reported as missing, an optional one falls back to its documented default. Keys the endpoint does not declare are ignored, and string inputs are stored verbatim without trimming (so uniqueness and filter comparisons are byte-exact), though a value that is empty or entirely whitespace fails wherever a non-empty string is required. A request body that is absent, empty or not readable as JSON is treated as an empty object, so what is reported is its missing required inputs; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. **Stale version.** On the two OCC-guarded endpoints, a version mismatch fails with `ERR_STALE_WELL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_OPERATOR_VERSION`.
9. **Bond forfeiture standing.** A forfeiture attempt against an operator that is not `INSOLVENT` fails with `ERR_OPERATOR_STILL_SOLVENT`. This carve-out deliberately sits ahead of the generic state check in step 10, because bond forfeiture is only ever an edge out of `INSOLVENT`: without the carve-out every solvent-operator forfeiture would report the generic transition error and the specific error would be unreachable.
10. **State-machine edge.** If the requested action is not a declared edge out of the current state of the row the action *directly advances*, the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). For an action endpoint that row is the path `{id}` row; for a create endpoint it is the row being created, which advances from its initial state. The state of a merely *referenced* entity is never judged here — those gates live in step 9 and steps 11 onward. Ordering a plugging job, for example, advances the new job from its own initial state, so a well that is not `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED` is reported by the specific rule in step 11 rather than by this generic one. A lower-numbered specific rule always preempts this generic check.
11. **Specific referenced-state gates**, in this order: `ERR_WELL_NOT_ORPHAN_CONFIRMED`, then `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN`, then `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_EXPIRED`.
12. **Safety gates**, in this order: `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, then `ERR_STATUTORY_NOTICE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_REVEGETATION_WAITING_PERIOD`, then `ERR_RECLAMATION_INCOMPLETE`.
13. **Orphan criteria.** `ERR_ORPHAN_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`.
14. **Temporal conflicts**, in this order: `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
15. **Referential business gates**, in this order: `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, then `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_TRANSFER_TO_INSOLVENT_OPERATOR`, then `ERR_FORFEITED_BOND_OPERATOR_MISMATCH`.
16. **Uniqueness and limit constraints**, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCTION_MONTH`, `ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_TECHNICAL_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_CEILING`, `ERR_NO_RESPONSIVE_BIDS`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
17. **Fiscal gates**, in this order: `ERR_TRANSFEREE_ASSURANCE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_FEDERAL_MATCH_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL`, `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_ALLOCATION`.

### 2.11. Two Independent State Dimensions on a Well

One structural feature of the data model must be stated plainly, because it is unusual. A `Well` row carries **two independent state machines on the same row**. The `status` dimension tracks the well's regulatory lifecycle and moves through `PRODUCING`, `INACTIVE`, `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED`, `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`, `PLUGGED` and terminally `RECLAIMED`, starting at `PRODUCING`. The `access_status` dimension tracks the agency's legal right to set foot on the site and moves among `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED`, `ACCESS_PENDING`, `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`, `ENTRY_ORDERED` and `ACCESS_DENIED`, starting at `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED` and having no terminal state at all. The two dimensions never constrain one another: a well may be `ACCESS_PENDING` while still `PRODUCING`, and a well may be `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED` while its `access_status` is still `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED`. Only one operation reads across the two — crew mobilization, which demands an authorized `access_status` regardless of where `status` stands.


## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Operator Register, the Well Inventory and the Assurance Requirement

Everything in the program hangs off an operator of record. A `ProgramAdmin` registers one by supplying its legal `name`, the two-letter regulatory district code it is registered in as `region_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), the financial-assurance variant that governs it as `financial_assurance_tier` (either `BLANKET` or `INDIVIDUAL`), and the assurance it has actually posted as `bond_amount_cents` (which may be zero but never negative). A freshly registered operator has no wells, so its computed `required_bond_cents` is 0, its `active_well_count` and `orphan_candidate_count` are both 0, and its `status` begins at `ACTIVE`. Those two counters are derived, never supplied: `active_well_count` is the number of wells whose `operator_id` is this operator and whose `status` is anything other than `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED`, while `orphan_candidate_count` is the narrower number of wells whose `operator_id` is this operator and whose `status` is `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED` or `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`. Its `successor_operator_id`, `insolvency_declared_at` and `bond_forfeited_at` all begin null. Operators can be listed and filtered by regulatory standing, by district, or by the operator they are the named successor of — the filters are `status`, `region_code` and `successor_operator_id`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

Wells enter the inventory next, added by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `FieldInspector`. A well is identified by `api_well_number`, the state API well number, which must be non-empty and unique across the entire inventory; a duplicate is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Creation also takes the `operator_id` of the responsible operator, the `spud_year` in which the well was drilled (any integer — no calendar range is enforced on it), the total measured depth `total_depth_ft` (which must be strictly greater than zero), the number of casing strings in the wellbore as `casing_strings` (zero or more, later cross-checked against what the field crew actually pulls), and four environmental inputs that drive prioritization: the measured methane emission rate `methane_scfd` in standard cubic feet per day (zero or more), the depth to the shallowest protected groundwater zone `groundwater_depth_ft` in feet (strictly positive), the number of residents within one thousand feet of the wellhead as `population_within_1000ft` (zero or more), and the disturbed surface area `surface_disturbance_acres` covering pad, pit and access road (zero or more). Optionally the caller supplies `last_production_month`, the latest month with reported production in `YYYY-MM` form; when the well never produced at all, that field stays null. A new well begins with `status` `PRODUCING` and `access_status` `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED`, and `orphan_confirmed_at` null. Wells can be listed with filters `operator_id`, `status`, `access_status` and `min_screening_score` (returning only wells whose screening score is at least the supplied value), plus `limit` and `offset`. For an `Operator`-role caller the list is implicitly scoped: omitting `operator_id` returns only that operator's own wells rather than the whole inventory, and supplying an `operator_id` that is not its own is the `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` violation of §2.1.

Creating a well is not a passive insert. Both priority scores are computed immediately, writing `screening_score` and `remediation_score`; the idle-well and de-minimis figures are computed, writing `idle_months` and `cumulative_boe_60mo`; and the owning operator's assurance position is recomputed, refreshing `active_well_count` and `required_bond_cents` and possibly flipping the operator between `ACTIVE` and `DELINQUENT`.

**How the assurance requirement is computed.** Each well contributes a base amount determined by a depth tier whose upper edges are inclusive: a well no deeper than 3000 feet contributes 250000 cents ($2,500); a well deeper than 3000 feet but no deeper than 7000 feet contributes 750000 cents ($7,500); a well deeper than 7000 feet contributes 1500000 cents ($15,000). Only wells the operator is still responsible for count: a well is included when its `status` is `PRODUCING`, `INACTIVE`, `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED` or `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`, and it is excluded once it reaches `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED`, because a closed well no longer needs assurance behind it.

From there the two tiers diverge, and the difference is the order in which the cap is applied. Under the `BLANKET` variant the base amounts of all included wells are summed first, and a single blanket cap of 25000000 cents ($250,000) is then applied to that sum — so one very deep well still contributes its full 1500000 cents. Under the `INDIVIDUAL` variant each well's base amount is first capped at 1000000 cents ($10,000) and only then are the capped amounts summed, with no blanket cap at all — so a well deeper than 7000 feet contributes only 1000000 cents rather than 1500000, and an identical inventory yields a strictly lower requirement under `INDIVIDUAL` than under `BLANKET` whenever any well falls in the deepest tier. The result is written to `required_bond_cents`. Every amount involved is an integer number of cents, so no rounding occurs anywhere in this computation.

**Delinquency is automatic and reversible.** An operator is deficient when its `bond_amount_cents` is strictly less than its `required_bond_cents`. The boundary is strict in both directions: posting exactly the required amount is compliant. Whenever the requirement is recomputed — on a well being created under this operator, on a transfer into or out of this operator being approved or voided, on one of its wells reaching `PLUGGED`, on one of its wells reaching `RECLAIMED`, or on its own bond being forfeited — the system compares the two figures and reacts. An `ACTIVE` operator whose posted bond has fallen below the new requirement transitions `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`. A `DELINQUENT` operator whose posted bond now equals or exceeds the new requirement transitions `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE` — the cure is automatic, and it is the exact compensating direction of the deficiency rule. Both directions increment the operator's `version` by one.

**Production history.** Monthly volumes arrive one row at a time, filed by a `ProgramAdmin`, a `FieldInspector`, or by the `Operator` itself for its own wells. Each row names the `month` in `YYYY-MM` form — the value must match a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between 01 and 12 — plus the reported oil volume `oil_bbl` in barrels and the reported gas volume `gas_mcf` in thousand cubic feet, neither of which may be negative. The row's barrel-of-oil-equivalent figure `boe` is derived as the oil volume plus the gas volume divided by six, and it is deliberately stored **unrounded** at the row level; the two-decimal rounding happens exactly once, later, on the well's aggregate. The identity of the filer is captured in `reported_by_user_id` — the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the operator's own id when an `Operator` filed the row and an opaque staff identifier when a `ProgramAdmin` filed it. Production records are append-only ledger lines and are listed for a well ordered by `month` ascending, with `limit` and `offset` for paging. A month may be reported only once per well; a second attempt for the same well and the same month is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCTION_MONTH` (HTTP 409). When the reported `month` is later than the value already stored, the well's `last_production_month` advances to it, and in every case the well's `idle_months` and `cumulative_boe_60mo` are recomputed.

**Idleness and de-minimis production.** The well's `idle_months` is the whole count of calendar months from `last_production_month` to the logical clock's month — twelve times the difference in years plus the difference in month numbers. When `last_production_month` is null, meaning the well never produced, `idle_months` is the sentinel value 999. Twelve idle months (a full year of silence) is enough for the inventory record to be reclassified from `PRODUCING` to `INACTIVE`; twenty-four idle months (two full years) is the threshold the orphan criteria demand.

The de-minimis figure looks at a trailing window of sixty months (five years) ending at the logical clock's month, with **both endpoints inclusive**: the window starts fifty-nine months before the clock month and ends at the clock month itself, and production months are compared as `YYYY-MM` strings. Every production record of the well whose `month` falls inside that inclusive window contributes its unrounded barrel-of-oil-equivalent value — oil barrels plus gas thousand-cubic-feet divided by six — those contributions are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once to produce `cumulative_boe_60mo`. Records outside the window are excluded even though they remain stored. A well with no records at all inside the window has a sum of zero, which **satisfies** the de-minimis test rather than failing it.


### 3.2. Prioritization Scoring: Two Variants of One Formula

Limited dollars force the program to rank wells, and the agency ranks them twice with two different value judgements. Both rankings are built from the same four risk components, computed from operator-reported fields on the single well row with no cross-entity aggregation whatsoever, and each component is a number between 0 and 100 that is **rounded half up to two decimal places before any weight is applied to it**. Weighting unrounded components produces a different number and is wrong.

- The **methane component** takes the well's `methane_scfd` and divides it by 5, rounds that quotient half up to two decimal places, and caps the result at 100. Full scale is therefore reached at 500 standard cubic feet per day; a well venting more than that scores 100 and no more.
- The **groundwater component** is a band lookup on `groundwater_depth_ft`, and the band value is an exact integer with no rounding. Protected groundwater no deeper than 50 feet scores 100. Deeper than 50 feet but no deeper than 200 feet scores 75. Deeper than 200 feet but no deeper than 500 feet scores 40. Deeper than 500 feet scores 10.
- The **population component** multiplies `population_within_1000ft` by 4, rounds half up to two decimal places, and caps the result at 100 — so a well with 25 or more residents inside the thousand-foot radius reaches full scale.
- The **surface component** multiplies `surface_disturbance_acres` by 20, rounds half up to two decimal places, and caps the result at 100 — so five disturbed acres or more reaches full scale.

The **screening variant** is the inventory-triage ranking, and it is written to `screening_score`. It weights the methane component heaviest and the population component lightest: 0.35 times the methane component, plus 0.30 times the groundwater component, plus 0.20 times the population component, plus 0.15 times the surface component, with that weighted sum rounded half up to two decimal places. There is no population override in this variant. The screening score is the number the orphan classification criteria gate on: a well qualifies only when its `screening_score` is at least 40.00.

The **remediation variant** is the work-ordering ranking, and it is written to `remediation_score`. It differs from the screening variant in two ways, and both matter. First, before any weighting happens, a public-proximity override is applied: when the well has 5 or more residents within one thousand feet, the population component used by this variant is forced to 100 outright, regardless of what the multiply-and-cap rule produced; with fewer than 5 residents the ordinary population component is used unchanged. Second, the weight vector is different — 0.20 times the methane component, plus 0.25 times the groundwater component, plus 0.40 times the overridden population component, plus 0.15 times the surface component, with that weighted sum rounded half up to two decimal places. Because population carries the heaviest weight here and the override can lift it to full scale, identical well inputs routinely yield a different number from the screening variant, and that is the intent: triage cares most about the gas that is escaping, remediation cares most about the people standing next to it.

Both scores are computed together when the well is created, and both are recomputed together at three later points: when opening an orphan determination reclassifies the well `PRODUCING → INACTIVE`, when submitting a determination promotes the well to `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, and on the cascade that follows an operator's revocation or bond forfeiture; a confirmation additionally recomputes `remediation_score` alone. Filing a production month recomputes the two *criteria* figures of §3.1 but neither score, and the remaining `status` transitions — into `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`, `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED` — recompute neither score. None of that is observable, and the reason is worth stating: all four risk components read `methane_scfd`, `groundwater_depth_ft`, `population_within_1000ft` and `surface_disturbance_acres`, and no endpoint in this API ever edits any of those four after well creation. Every recomputation therefore necessarily reproduces the pair computed at creation, so a well's `screening_score` and `remediation_score` are fixed for its whole life. Neither score is ever computed lazily at read time — a well fetched from the API always exposes the two values that were current at its last write.

### 3.3. Orphan Determination: Authoring, Review and Confirmation

Classification is a two-person job by design: one person builds the case, a different person decides it. A `FieldInspector` (or a `ProgramAdmin`) opens a determination on a well by supplying `inspector_notes`, the narrative field observation supporting the classification, together with `expected_well_version` as described in §2.7. The new row starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `created_by_user_id`, and leaves `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_rationale`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at` and the three snapshot fields null.

Opening a determination has three further effects. Any determination already open on the same well — one whose status is `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` — transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, with its own `version` incremented; a determination that has already reached `CONFIRMED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is untouched. The well's idle and de-minimis figures are recomputed. And if the well has been silent long enough — twelve or more idle months — the inventory record itself is reclassified, transitioning the well `PRODUCING → INACTIVE`, recomputing `idle_months`, `cumulative_boe_60mo`, `screening_score` and `remediation_score`, and incrementing the well's `version` by one. The well's `version` is incremented by this endpoint in any case, and it is incremented exactly once: the reclassification does not stack a second increment on top of the endpoint's own, so the well's counter advances by one whether or not the reclassification fired. That single increment is exactly why the next caller's `expected_well_version` must be refreshed. The owning operator is not recomputed and not touched by this endpoint at all.

**Submission.** The authoring inspector submits the determination for review, optionally revising `inspector_notes` in the same call. Submission is legal only from `DRAFT` or from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and only for the author: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `created_by_user_id`. A submission attempted by anyone other than the author is treated as an illegal edge rather than as an ownership or segregation-of-duties failure — it reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — and it is tested after the status precondition and before the revision ceiling. On success `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock, and the three criteria snapshots are frozen from the well's current computed values — `snapshot_idle_months` from the well's `idle_months`, `snapshot_cumulative_boe_60mo` from its `cumulative_boe_60mo`, and `snapshot_screening_score` from its `screening_score`. Submission also promotes the well: a well sitting at `PRODUCING` or `INACTIVE` transitions to `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, both of its priority scores are recomputed, the owning operator's `orphan_candidate_count` is recomputed, and the well's `version` increments. When the submission is a re-submission out of `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `revision_round` increments by one first, and that increment is permitted only while `revision_round` is still below the ceiling of 2 completed revision cycles. The determination's own `version` increments on every submission.

**Decision.** Only a `DeterminationReviewer` decides, and only from `SUBMITTED`. The decision names an `action` — `CONFIRM`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_REVISION` — together with a `rationale`, which is stored as `decision_rationale`. In all three cases the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` is set to the logical clock, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`; a reviewer deciding its own determination is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The three snapshot fields are refreshed from the well at the instant of a `CONFIRM` or a `REJECT`, so the record shows the criteria as they stood when the decision was actually taken.

`REQUEST_REVISION` sends the case back: `status` becomes `REVISION_REQUESTED` and the well is deliberately **left** at `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, because the classification is still pending rather than resolved. A revision may be requested at most twice on one determination; a third request, made when `revision_round` already stands at 2, is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

`REJECT` closes the case negatively: `status` becomes `REJECTED`, and the well transitions `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE → INACTIVE`, which compensates precisely for the promotion the submission performed. The owning operator's `orphan_candidate_count` is recomputed and the well's `version` increments.

`CONFIRM` is the consequential branch, and it carries two extra preconditions. The well's owning operator must be in regulatory trouble — its `status` must be `DELINQUENT`, `INSOLVENT` or `REVOKED`; an operator in good standing keeps responsibility for its own wells. A confirmation whose owning operator stands outside those three is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — the operator is only a referenced row here, yet this particular precondition is nevertheless reported as a transition failure — and it is evaluated ahead of the numeric criteria. And the numeric criteria must all hold **against live data re-evaluated at decision time**, not against the snapshot taken at submission: the well's `idle_months` must be at least 24, its `cumulative_boe_60mo` must be no greater than 180.00, and its `screening_score` must be at least 40.00. If any of the three fails — fewer than 24 idle months, more than 180.00 barrels of oil equivalent across the trailing sixty months, or a screening score below 40.00 — the confirmation is rejected with `ERR_ORPHAN_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). The two re-evaluated figures are not merely inspected and discarded: once the criteria pass, the well's stored `idle_months` and `cumulative_boe_60mo` are overwritten in place with the freshly computed values, whereas `screening_score` is read exactly as stored and is never recomputed by this endpoint. Note carefully that the operator's regulatory standing is *not* part of the numeric criteria predicate; it is a separate precondition of this transition, and the revocation cascade described in §3.5 pushes wells to `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE` entirely independently of it.

On a successful confirmation `status` becomes `CONFIRMED`, the well transitions `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE → ORPHAN_CONFIRMED`, the well's `orphan_confirmed_at` is set to the logical clock, the well's `remediation_score` is recomputed because that is the number that will order the remediation queue, the operator's `orphan_candidate_count` is recomputed, and both rows' `version` counters increment. Every one of those well-side and operator-side effects, on a `REJECT` exactly as on a `CONFIRM`, is conditional on the well actually standing at `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE` when the decision lands: because a new determination supersedes only the `DRAFT` and `REVISION_REQUESTED` rows on a well, two determinations on one well can both reach `SUBMITTED`, and once the first of them has resolved the well's lifecycle dimension the second one moves alone — the well's `status`, `orphan_confirmed_at` and `remediation_score` are left as they are, the operator is not recomputed, and the well's `version` does not increment, even though a passing `CONFIRM` has still refreshed that well's `idle_months` and `cumulative_boe_60mo` in place. `CONFIRMED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal: no action moves a determination out of them, and an attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A decision aimed at a determination still in `DRAFT` reports the same error. Determinations are readable individually and as a per-well list ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `status`, `limit` and `offset` as filters.


### 3.4. Surface Access: Negotiated Agreements and Statutory Entry

The agency cannot plug a well it cannot legally reach, and reaching it takes either the surface owner's consent or the statute. A `ProgramAdmin` or `FieldInspector` opens an access request against a well by naming the `surface_owner_name` recorded in the agreement or in the statutory entry notice, and choosing a `mode`: `AGREEMENT` when access is being negotiated, or `STATUTORY_ENTRY` when it will be compelled. Two optional inputs round out the request. `waiting_period_days` is the statutory notice period, an integer from 0 through 180; when it is omitted it defaults to 30 for `STATUTORY_ENTRY` mode and to 0 for `AGREEMENT` mode. `compensation_cents` is the surface damage compensation offered to the owner; it may not be negative and defaults to 0 when omitted.

Creating the request stamps `notice_sent_at` from the logical clock and derives `window_end_at` as that instant plus `waiting_period_days` days. That pair delimits the **notice window**, and the window is **closed on both ends**: the instant at `window_end_at` is inside the window, not outside it. The new row's `status` begins at `REQUESTED`, with `legal_authority_citation` and `resolved_at` null. The well's access dimension mirrors the new row, moving `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED → ACCESS_PENDING` or, when a previous attempt was refused, `ACCESS_DENIED → ACCESS_PENDING`, and the well's `version` increments. A well may have only one live request at a time: the transition is available only while no site-access row on that well is still `REQUESTED`. When the well's access dimension is coming out of `ACCESS_DENIED`, one further condition applies — the closed notice window of every site-access row on that well whose status is `REQUESTED` or `DENIED` must have ended strictly before the logical clock, so a fresh attempt cannot be stacked on top of a notice period that is still running.

**Window conflicts.** Because notice windows are legal instruments rather than mere scheduling hints, they may not overlap on the same well. If any existing site-access row for the well whose status is `REQUESTED`, `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED` or `ENTRY_ORDERED` has a closed interval from its `notice_sent_at` through its `window_end_at` that overlaps the closed interval the new request would occupy, the create is rejected with `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because both intervals are closed, **windows that merely touch at an endpoint do conflict** — a new request whose `notice_sent_at` falls exactly on an existing window's `window_end_at` is refused. A row that was `DENIED` is not counted by this check, but note the asymmetry deliberately built into the model: a denied row still occupies its window for the purpose of the `ACCESS_DENIED → ACCESS_PENDING` waiting rule above, even though it does not itself trigger a window conflict.

On this create the well is only a mirrored, *referenced* row, so its access dimension is not judged by §2.10 step 10 at all; the three gates of the preceding paragraph — the `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED`/`ACCESS_DENIED` edge on the well, the one-live-request rule, and the `ACCESS_DENIED` waiting rule, each of which reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — are therefore evaluated **after** the window-conflict check of this paragraph, in that order. The precedence is what makes `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_CONFLICT` reachable at all: every status the conflict scan looks at also holds the well out of `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED` and `ACCESS_DENIED`, so with the well gate first no request could ever reach the conflict error. Both later gates remain reachable, because a prior window that has fully elapsed yields no conflict.

Access rows are readable individually and as a per-well list ordered by `notice_sent_at` ascending, with `status`, `limit` and `offset` as filters.

**Resolution.** Only a `ProgramAdmin` resolves a request, and only while it is `REQUESTED`. The call names an `outcome` and may carry `legal_authority_citation`. Executing the agreement — available only on a row whose `mode` is `AGREEMENT` — moves the row `REQUESTED → AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`, sets `resolved_at` to the logical clock, and mirrors the well to `ACCESS_PENDING → AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`. Ordering entry — available only on a row whose `mode` is `STATUTORY_ENTRY` — moves the row `REQUESTED → ENTRY_ORDERED`, stores the supplied `legal_authority_citation` (the statute the agency is relying on, which must be present and non-empty or the call fails schema validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), sets `resolved_at`, and mirrors the well to `ACCESS_PENDING → ENTRY_ORDERED`. Denying the request moves the row `REQUESTED → DENIED`, sets `resolved_at`, and mirrors the well to `ACCESS_PENDING → ACCESS_DENIED`. Only `ORDER_ENTRY` ever stores the citation: supplied alongside `EXECUTE_AGREEMENT` or `DENY` it is still type-checked as a non-empty string — a non-string value, or a string that is empty or entirely whitespace, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and is then discarded, leaving `legal_authority_citation` null on the resolved row. Every resolution increments the site-access row's `version` and the well's `version`. An outcome that does not match the row's `mode`, or any resolution attempted against a row that has already reached one of its three terminal states — `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`, `ENTRY_ORDERED` or `DENIED` — is not a declared edge for that row and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

**The notice period is not negotiable.** Entry may not be ordered while the surface owner's statutory response period is still running. If the logical clock is strictly before the row's `window_end_at` — that is, strictly before `notice_sent_at` plus `waiting_period_days` days — the order is rejected with `ERR_STATUTORY_NOTICE_PERIOD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). The clock standing exactly at `window_end_at` is sufficient, because the notice window is closed and has therefore fully elapsed at that instant.

### 3.5. Transfers and Enforcement Against Failing Operators

The classic orphan-well pathway is a transfer: a solvent operator sheds its worst wells onto a company that cannot possibly plug them, and the state inherits the hole. Two mechanisms in this API close that pathway — a forward-looking assurance test at transfer time, and a backward-looking unwind at insolvency time.

A transfer is proposed by a `ProgramAdmin` or by the releasing `Operator` itself, naming only the receiving operator as `to_operator_id`. The system copies the well's current `operator_id` into `from_operator_id`, sets `status` to `PENDING`, and immediately computes `transferee_required_bond_cents` — the receiving operator's assurance requirement under **its own** `financial_assurance_tier`, evaluated as though this well were already part of its inventory. Nothing about the well changes yet, and the new row's `decided_by_user_id`, `approved_at`, `voided_at` and `void_reason` all begin null. Nothing about the well is judged either: a proposal may be filed against a well in any lifecycle state — including one already committed to closure — and a well may carry several `PENDING` proposals at the same time, since no uniqueness rule and no one-live-proposal rule constrains a transfer at proposal time. Transfers are readable individually and as a list filtered by `well_id`, `from_operator_id`, `to_operator_id` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

A proposal into a failing operator is refused outright at proposal time and again at approval time: when the receiving operator's `status` is `INSOLVENT` or `REVOKED`, or when it is `DELINQUENT` and its `orphan_candidate_count` is greater than zero, the request is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_TO_INSOLVENT_OPERATOR` (HTTP 422). A delinquent operator with no orphan candidates may still receive wells; a delinquent operator that is already accumulating orphan candidates may not.

**Decision.** Only a `ProgramAdmin` decides, supplying an `action` and an optional `rationale` — the `rationale` is accepted (an empty string included) but the transfer row has no field for it, so it is never echoed back on any of the three actions. Approval is available only from `PENDING` and carries three further conditions, evaluated in this order: the well must not already be committed to closure, so its `status` must not be `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`, `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED`, and a well already in one of those three states reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) even though the well is only a referenced row; the receiving operator must not be a failing transferee under the full test of the preceding paragraph, so revocation, insolvency and delinquency-with-orphan-candidates alike are refused here too; and the receiving operator's posted `bond_amount_cents` must be at least its `transferee_required_bond_cents`. If the posted bond is strictly less than that forward-looking requirement, the approval is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFEREE_ASSURANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) — equality is sufficient and passes.

On approval the transfer moves `PENDING → APPROVED`, `approved_at` is set to the logical clock, and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `decided_by_user_id`. The well's `operator_id` becomes `to_operator_id` and its `version` increments. Both operators are then recomputed — `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents` on the releasing operator and on the receiving operator alike — and each of them may auto-transition `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` or `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE` as a result. The transfer's own `version` increments. A proposal naming the well's current operator as the receiving operator is not refused anywhere, and when `from_operator_id` and `to_operator_id` are the same operator that one row is recomputed twice by the decision — on a void exactly as on an approval — so its `version` advances by two.

Denial moves the transfer `PENDING → DENIED`, records `decided_by_user_id`, leaves the well's `operator_id` untouched, and increments the transfer's `version`. `DENIED` is terminal.

Voiding moves an already-approved transfer `APPROVED → VOIDED`. It is available only from `APPROVED`, and only while the well's `status` is not `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED`, `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED` — once the state has started spending money on a well, the chain of custody is frozen, and that refusal is again `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). A void sets `voided_at` to the logical clock and records `void_reason`, which takes exactly one of two literal values: `MANUAL_VOID` when an administrator voided the transfer directly through this endpoint — which also overwrites `decided_by_user_id` with the voiding administrator's `X-User-ID`, replacing the approver's — or `TRANSFEREE_INSOLVENT` when the void arrived as a cascade from an insolvency declaration, which leaves `decided_by_user_id` exactly as the approval left it. The well's `operator_id` is restored to `from_operator_id` and its `version` increments; both operators' `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents` are recomputed so that the pre-approval values are restored exactly, and each may again flip between `ACTIVE` and `DELINQUENT`. `VOIDED` is terminal.

**Enforcement.** A `ProgramAdmin` acts against an operator by naming an `action`, the mandatory `expected_operator_version` of §2.7, an optional `memo` for the file, and — for a declaration of insolvency — an optional `successor_operator_id` naming the operator that will take over. Three actions exist, and each one increments the operator's `version` on success.

**Declaring insolvency** is available from `ACTIVE` or from `DELINQUENT`, moving the operator to `INSOLVENT`. It sets `insolvency_declared_at` to the logical clock and stores `successor_operator_id` when one was supplied. It then unwinds recent transfers into this operator in four dependent hops. First, every transfer whose `to_operator_id` is this operator, whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and whose `approved_at` falls within the last 180 days of the logical clock — that is, at or after the clock minus 180 days — transitions `APPROVED → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `TRANSFEREE_INSOLVENT`. Second, caused by the first, each voided transfer resets its well's `operator_id` back to that transfer's `from_operator_id` and increments the well's `version` by one. Third, caused by the second, `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents` are recomputed for the reverting releasing operator and for this operator. Fourth, caused by the third, each recomputed operator whose `bond_amount_cents` is now strictly below its `required_bond_cents` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`, and symmetrically a recomputed operator standing at `DELINQUENT` whose posted bond now covers its recomputed requirement returns `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`; the operator being declared insolvent is exempt from both flips, since neither is an edge out of `INSOLVENT`. A transfer approved 181 days ago is left standing: the lookback is a hard 180-day boundary measured from `approved_at`.

**Revoking a permit** is available only from `DELINQUENT`, moving the operator to `REVOKED`. Every well of this operator whose `status` is `PRODUCING` or `INACTIVE` transitions to `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`; each such well's `screening_score` and `remediation_score` are recomputed, and the operator's `orphan_candidate_count` is recomputed afterward. Wells that have already progressed past `INACTIVE` are unaffected by this cascade.

**Forfeiting the bond** is available only from `INSOLVENT`, and it also lands on `REVOKED`. Beyond the state requirement it demands that the bond has not already been forfeited — `bond_forfeited_at` must still be null — and that there is something to forfeit, so `bond_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero; both of those refusals report `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). An attempt against an operator that has not been declared insolvent is rejected with `ERR_OPERATOR_STILL_SOLVENT` (HTTP 422); §2.10 explains why that specific error outranks the generic transition error. The action then runs four dependent hops. First, a new funding source is created with `kind` `FORFEITED_BOND`, `name` formed as the literal text `FORFEITED BOND ` followed by the operator's `name`, `authorized_cents` equal to the operator's pre-forfeiture `bond_amount_cents`, `bond_operator_id` set to this operator, `allowable_cost_categories` set to `PLUGGING` and `RECLAMATION`, `expires_at` set to the logical clock plus 3650 days (a ten-year obligation term), and `status` `ACTIVE`. Second, caused by the first, the operator's `bond_amount_cents` is set to 0 and `bond_forfeited_at` to the logical clock, because the posted assurance now lives inside the new funding source. Third, caused by the second, `required_bond_cents` is recomputed against the zeroed bond and the operator's `status` becomes `REVOKED` — an insolvent operator with no remaining assurance loses its permit. Fourth, caused by the third, every well of this operator whose `status` is `PRODUCING` or `INACTIVE` transitions to `ORPHAN_CANDIDATE`, each such well's two priority scores are recomputed, and `orphan_candidate_count` is recomputed.

`REVOKED` is the terminal standing for an operator: no further enforcement action is an edge out of it, and an attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. So does a revocation aimed at an operator that is still `ACTIVE`, since revocation is only ever an edge out of `DELINQUENT`.


### 3.6. Procuring Plugging Capacity

Plugging work is bought, not performed in house, so the program keeps a register of licensed contractors and buys capacity through competitive unit-price solicitations.

A contractor is registered with its legal `name`, its state plugging contractor `license_number` (non-empty and unique across the register; a duplicate is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked once every other input of the request has validated), the performance bond it has posted with the agency as `bond_amount_cents` (zero or more), and the agency's past-performance rating `performance_score`, a number from 0 through 100 inclusive. A new contractor's `license_status` is `GOOD_STANDING` and its `suspended_at` is null. Contractors are readable individually and as a list filtered by `license_status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

**Licensing standing gates everything downstream.** A licensing action names an `action` and an optional `reason`, and there are four legal moves. Suspending a licence is available only from `GOOD_STANDING`: it sets `license_status` to `SUSPENDED`, stamps `suspended_at` with the logical clock, and cascades to every unit-cost contract of this contractor whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, moving each one `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and incrementing its `version`. Reinstating is available only from `SUSPENDED`: it returns `license_status` to `GOOD_STANDING`, clears `suspended_at` back to null, and cascades in the opposite direction, moving every contract of this contractor whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` back `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented. Debarment is available from either `GOOD_STANDING` or `SUSPENDED`, and the two branches differ in their bookkeeping. Debarring a contractor that was in `GOOD_STANDING` sets `license_status` to `DEBARRED`, stamps `suspended_at` with the logical clock, and cascades every `ACTIVE` contract of this contractor to `SUSPENDED`. Debarring a contractor that was already `SUSPENDED` sets `license_status` to `DEBARRED` and nothing else — `suspended_at` keeps the value it was given at suspension, and there is no contract cascade, because that contractor's contracts were already moved to `SUSPENDED` when the suspension happened. `DEBARRED` is terminal in either case; the contractor's `version` increments on all four moves.

A solicitation is drafted by a `ProcurementOfficer` with the district it covers as `region_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), the narrative `scope_description` of the work being procured, the bid submission deadline `bid_due_at`, the cost ceiling `ceiling_cents` (strictly positive), and the `min_technical_score` a bid must reach to be eligible for responsiveness, a number from 0 through 100 inclusive. The new solicitation's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `reopen_round` is 0, and the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `created_by_user_id` — the value the award segregation-of-duties gate will later compare against. Solicitations are readable individually and as a list filtered by `region_code` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

**The procurement lifecycle** is driven by a `ProcurementOfficer` naming an `action`, and optionally `new_bid_due_at`. Opening is available only from `DRAFT` and only while the logical clock is strictly before `bid_due_at` — a solicitation whose deadline has already passed can never be opened, and that refusal is part of the `OPEN` edge itself, reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) rather than as the bid-deadline error — and it moves the row `DRAFT → OPEN`. Closing is available only from `OPEN` and moves the row `OPEN → EVALUATING`, after which no further bids are accepted. Reopening moves the row `EVALUATING → OPEN` and exists for the case where evaluation produced nothing usable; it is permitted only while the count of this solicitation's bids in `RESPONSIVE` status is exactly zero, only while `reopen_round` is still below the ceiling of 1 completed reopen cycle, and only when `new_bid_due_at` is supplied and is strictly after the logical clock. Those conditions are evaluated in exactly that written order, behind the `EVALUATING` edge check itself, and the ones that are not limit checks count as part of the `REOPEN` edge: a solicitation outside `EVALUATING`, or one still holding a `RESPONSIVE` bid, and equally a `new_bid_due_at` that lands at or before the logical clock, are all reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Because `new_bid_due_at` is required only by this one action, its absence is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` only after the `EVALUATING` check, the responsive-bid check and the reopen ceiling have each passed — so a `REOPEN` that omits it against a cancelled solicitation, or against one that has already used its reopen, reports the state or limit error instead. A successful reopen increments `reopen_round` by one and replaces `bid_due_at` with `new_bid_due_at`. A second reopen attempt, made when `reopen_round` already stands at 1, is rejected with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Cancelling is available from `DRAFT`, from `OPEN` and from `EVALUATING`, moves the row to `CANCELLED`, and sweeps the bid field clean: every bid on this solicitation whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `RESPONSIVE` or `NONRESPONSIVE` transitions to `NOT_AWARDED` without any score being computed, each with its own `version` incremented. `AWARDED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal; reopening a cancelled solicitation reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every lifecycle move increments the solicitation's `version`.

**Bidding.** A bid names its `contractor_id` and four unit prices, each strictly positive: `cement_per_sack_cents` per sack of cement placed, `rig_day_rate_cents` per rig day, `casing_per_ft_cents` per foot of casing removed, and the lump-sum `mobilization_fee_cents` charged once per job. It also carries `technical_score`, the technical evaluation score assigned at submission, a number from 0 through 100 inclusive. When the caller is acting in the `Contractor` role, `contractor_id` must equal its own `X-User-ID`. A bid may only be submitted into a solicitation whose `status` is `OPEN`; any other procurement state is rejected with `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The deadline is enforced independently: once the logical clock is at or after the solicitation's `bid_due_at`, submission is rejected with `ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422) — the boundary instant itself is already too late. One contractor may hold only one live bid per solicitation; a second attempt while a bid with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `RESPONSIVE`, `NONRESPONSIVE` or `AWARDED` already exists for that pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BID` (HTTP 409). A contractor whose `license_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED` may not bid at all, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

At submission the bid's `evaluated_unit_cost_cents` is computed against a fixed standard basket, so that four unrelated price schedules become one comparable number: 120 sacks of cement multiplied by `cement_per_sack_cents`, plus 4 rig days multiplied by `rig_day_rate_cents`, plus 800 feet of casing multiplied by `casing_per_ft_cents`, plus the whole `mobilization_fee_cents` once. The result is exact integer cents. If that basket cost is strictly greater than the solicitation's `ceiling_cents`, the bid is rejected on the spot with `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_CEILING` (HTTP 422); a basket cost exactly equal to the ceiling is accepted. An accepted bid begins at `status` `SUBMITTED`, with `price_score`, `composite_score`, `evaluated_by_user_id` and `nonresponsive_reason` all null. Bids are readable individually and as a list filtered by `solicitation_id`, `contractor_id` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`. For a `Contractor`-role caller that list is implicitly scoped exactly as the well list is for an `Operator`: omitting `contractor_id` returns only its own bids rather than the whole field, and supplying a `contractor_id` that is not its own id is the `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` violation of §2.1.

**Responsiveness may be revised in either direction.** A `ProcurementOfficer` records a determination by naming an `outcome` and, when marking a bid unresponsive, a `reason` — which stays optional even then: omitted, it leaves `nonresponsive_reason` null. Marking a bid responsive is available from `SUBMITTED` and from `NONRESPONSIVE`, requires the parent solicitation's `status` to be `OPEN` or `EVALUATING` — a solicitation in `DRAFT`, `AWARDED` or `CANCELLED` makes the move an illegal edge and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), not `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN` — requires the bid's `technical_score` to be at least the solicitation's `min_technical_score`, requires `evaluated_unit_cost_cents` to be no greater than the solicitation's `ceiling_cents`, and requires the bidding contractor's `license_status` to be `GOOD_STANDING`. It sets `status` to `RESPONSIVE`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `evaluated_by_user_id`, and clears `nonresponsive_reason` back to null. A technical score strictly below the minimum is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICAL_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422); equality passes. Those last three requirements are checked in the order written — technical score, then ceiling, then licensing standing — so on this endpoint `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` is reported only once the two numeric gates have passed, rather than in the position §2.10 gives it on bid submission. Marking a bid unresponsive is available from `SUBMITTED` and from `RESPONSIVE`, likewise requires the solicitation to be `OPEN` or `EVALUATING`, sets `status` to `NONRESPONSIVE`, stores the supplied `reason` as `nonresponsive_reason`, and records `evaluated_by_user_id`. The pair therefore cycles freely — `RESPONSIVE → NONRESPONSIVE → RESPONSIVE` is entirely legal right up until the solicitation is awarded. Both moves increment the bid's `version`. `AWARDED` and `NOT_AWARDED` are terminal for a bid.

**Award.** Only an `AwardAuthority` awards, only from `EVALUATING`, and never the officer who drafted the solicitation — an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the solicitation's `created_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The award takes no request body at all. The candidate pool is fixed **before any score is computed**: it is exactly the bids of this solicitation whose `status` is `RESPONSIVE` at the instant the award runs, and bids sitting at `SUBMITTED`, `NONRESPONSIVE`, `AWARDED` or `NOT_AWARDED` are excluded both from the price benchmark and from the winner pool. An empty pool is rejected with `ERR_NO_RESPONSIVE_BIDS` (HTTP 422), and a winning contractor whose `license_status` has since fallen out of `GOOD_STANDING` is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). That standing is tested on the top-ranked candidate only, and only after the ranking has been resolved, so an ineligible leader fails the whole award instead of handing the work to the runner-up; the rejection persists nothing at all — no bid keeps a `price_score` or a `composite_score`, no bid changes `status`, and the solicitation remains in `EVALUATING`.

Scoring proceeds in two steps over that fixed pool. The price benchmark is the lowest `evaluated_unit_cost_cents` among the candidates and nothing else — never the full bid population, and never recomputed after a winner is chosen. Each candidate's `price_score` is 100 multiplied by that lowest basket cost, divided by the candidate's own `evaluated_unit_cost_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places; the cheapest candidate therefore scores exactly 100.00. Each candidate's `composite_score` is 0.60 multiplied by its `price_score`, plus 0.40 multiplied by its `technical_score`, again rounded half up to two decimal places.

Winner selection is fully deterministic. Candidates are ranked by `composite_score` descending. A tie on composite score is broken by `evaluated_unit_cost_cents` ascending, so the cheaper basket wins. A remaining tie is broken by `created_at` ascending, so the earlier-filed bid wins — the row `id` is never used as a tiebreaker under any circumstances. The top-ranked candidate transitions `RESPONSIVE → AWARDED` carrying its computed `price_score` and `composite_score`; every other candidate transitions `RESPONSIVE → NOT_AWARDED`, also carrying its computed scores; and any bid on the solicitation that was never evaluated — still `SUBMITTED` — or that stands at `NONRESPONSIVE` transitions to `NOT_AWARDED` **without** any score being computed. The solicitation itself transitions `EVALUATING → AWARDED`.

The award creates the instrument the field work will run under: a unit-cost contract, returned with HTTP 201. It records `solicitation_id`, `contractor_id` and `winning_bid_id`, copies the winning bid's four unit prices verbatim into its own `cement_per_sack_cents`, `rig_day_rate_cents`, `casing_per_ft_cents` and `mobilization_fee_cents`, copies the solicitation's `ceiling_cents` as its total obligation ceiling, starts `obligated_cents` at 0, starts `status` at `ACTIVE`, and stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `awarded_by_user_id`. Contracts are readable individually and as a list filtered by `solicitation_id`, `contractor_id`, `winning_bid_id` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`. A contract has three states: `ACTIVE` while work may be ordered and performed, `SUSPENDED` while the contractor is not in `GOOD_STANDING`, and the terminal `CLOSED` once the contract is fully obligated. The route into `CLOSED` is described in §3.7.


### 3.7. Field Operations: Ordering the Job, Plugging the Well, Accruing the Cost

Work is ordered per well against an awarded contract. The order names the `well_id` to be plugged, the `contract_id` the work runs under, the `scheduled_start` at which the crew will occupy the site, and the planned quantities: `planned_cement_sacks` for plug placement (strictly positive), `planned_rig_days` (strictly positive), and `planned_casing_ft` of casing to be removed (zero or more). The two body foreign keys are resolved for existence left to right, `well_id` before `contract_id`, so a request in which both name nothing reports `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`. A job may only be ordered for a well whose `status` is `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED`; any other lifecycle state is rejected with `ERR_WELL_NOT_ORPHAN_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 409). A well may carry only one live job: the order is available only while no plugging job on that well exists in any status other than `RECLAIMED`. And the contract must be usable — a contract whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422).

**The estimate.** At creation the job's `plugging_cost_estimate_cents` is computed from the planned quantities priced at the contract's own rates: `planned_cement_sacks` multiplied by the contract's `cement_per_sack_cents`, plus `planned_rig_days` multiplied by its `rig_day_rate_cents`, plus `planned_casing_ft` multiplied by its `casing_per_ft_cents`, plus the contract's `mobilization_fee_cents` once. The result is exact integer cents. Note what the estimate deliberately omits: it carries **no** administrative fee. Only actuals do. That is precisely why the estimate and the actual diverge even when the field crew reports exactly the planned quantities.

**Contract exposure.** Ordering the job also raises the contract's `obligated_cents`, and the way that figure is built matters. For every plugging job on the contract — regardless of its status, including jobs that have already reached `RECLAIMED`, whose spend is permanently obligated — the contract takes the **greater** of that job's `plugging_cost_estimate_cents` and its `actual_cost_cents`, and sums those greater-of values. Taking the greater rather than the sum is what allows a job's estimate to be *replaced* by its own actual as field reports arrive, instead of being double-counted alongside it. If the contract's obligation computed that way, including the new job's estimate, would be strictly greater than the contract's `ceiling_cents`, the order is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). An obligation that lands exactly on `ceiling_cents` is accepted, and ordering a job never changes the contract's `status` — even a fully obligating order leaves an `ACTIVE` contract `ACTIVE`. The sole route into `CLOSED` is the field report described below.

**Crew scheduling.** The job's `crew_window_end_at` is derived as `scheduled_start` plus `planned_rig_days` plus 2 further days of demobilization buffer. The crew occupancy interval runs from `scheduled_start` up to but **not including** `crew_window_end_at` — it is half-open. A crew cannot be in two places at once, so if that half-open interval overlaps the same half-open interval of another plugging job whose contract names the same contractor and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `MOBILIZED`, `FIELD_REPORTED` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`, the order is rejected with `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the interval is half-open, back-to-back scheduling is legal: a job whose `scheduled_start` falls exactly on another job's `crew_window_end_at` does **not** conflict. Contrast this with the surface-access notice windows of §3.4, which are closed and therefore *do* conflict when they merely touch — the two rules use deliberately opposite boundary conventions.

A newly ordered job begins at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `rework_round` 0, `actual_cost_cents` 0, `funded_cents` 0, `last_round_cost_cents` null, `mobilized_at`, `plugging_accepted_at` and `reclaimed_at` null, and `cement_sacks_used`, `rig_days_used`, `casing_removed_ft`, `downhole_conditions` and `plug_intervals` all null because no field report has yet been filed. The well transitions `ORPHAN_CONFIRMED → PLUGGING_SCHEDULED` and its `version` increments. Jobs are readable individually and as a list filtered by `well_id`, `contract_id` and `status`, with `limit` and `offset`.

**Mobilization** takes no request body and is available only from `SCHEDULED`. It demands legal standing to be on the site: the well's `access_status` must be `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED` or `ENTRY_ORDERED`, reflecting the latest resolved site-access row, and anything else — including `NO_ACCESS_REQUESTED`, `ACCESS_PENDING` and `ACCESS_DENIED` — is rejected with `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 422). It also demands a contractor whose `license_status` is `GOOD_STANDING`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), and a contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the two are tested in exactly that order, as §2.10 requires. That order is load-bearing rather than academic: because suspending or debarring a contractor already carried its `ACTIVE` contracts to `SUSPENDED`, both gates are normally armed at once, and the licensing error is the one reported. On success the job transitions `SCHEDULED → MOBILIZED`, `mobilized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the job's `version` increments.

**The field report** is the documentary heart of the program, filed by the `FieldInspector` or by the `Contractor` performing the work. It carries `cement_sacks_used`, `rig_days_used` and `casing_removed_ft` as actually consumed in this round, a narrative `downhole_conditions` describing what the crew found in the wellbore, and `plug_intervals`, an array of objects each recording the `top_ft` and `bottom_ft` of a cement plug and the number of `sacks` placed in it. The report is available from `MOBILIZED` and from `REWORK_REQUIRED`, requires the contract's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, requires each of `cement_sacks_used`, `rig_days_used` and `casing_removed_ft` to be an integer of zero or more — unlike the planned quantities on the order, none of the three has to be strictly positive — and requires `plug_intervals` to be a non-empty array and `downhole_conditions` to be a non-empty string. Only the outer array is validated: the `top_ft`, `bottom_ft` and `sacks` inside each entry are stored verbatim exactly as supplied and their shape is never checked, so a non-empty array of any content at all is accepted. All five payload values are stored on the job, replacing whatever the previous round left there, so the job always exposes the most recent round's documentation.

**How a round's cost is computed.** The round number is the job's current `rework_round` plus one — 1 for the first report, 2 after one rework has been ordered, and so on. Four line items are priced at the contract's rates. The cement line is `cement_sacks_used` multiplied by the contract's `cement_per_sack_cents`. The rig line is `rig_days_used` multiplied by `rig_day_rate_cents`. The casing line is `casing_removed_ft` multiplied by `casing_per_ft_cents`. The mobilization line is the contract's full `mobilization_fee_cents` on round number 1, and on every later round it is half of that fee — 50 percent of `mobilization_fee_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — because a remobilization costs less than an original mobilization.

On top of those four lines sits the state administrative fee of 4.25 percent, and **the order of rounding here is pinned**: the fee is computed on each line item separately and rounded half up to whole cents *per line*, and only then are the four rounded fees added together to form the administrative fee for the round. Applying 4.25 percent to the combined line subtotal and rounding once produces a different number and is wrong. The round's `last_round_cost_cents` is the sum of the four line items plus that per-line-summed administrative fee, and it is then added to the job's `actual_cost_cents`, which is cumulative across every round ever filed. No earlier round is ever recomputed retroactively.

Filing the report then recomputes the contract's `obligated_cents` by the same greater-of-estimate-or-actual rule described above. If the recomputed obligation would strictly exceed the contract's `ceiling_cents`, the report is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED` and nothing is written. If it lands exactly on `ceiling_cents`, the contract transitions `ACTIVE → CLOSED` — fully obligated, with no new job orderable against it — and the contract's `version` increments. The job transitions `MOBILIZED → FIELD_REPORTED` or `REWORK_REQUIRED → FIELD_REPORTED` and increments its own `version`.

**Review.** A filed report is reviewed by naming an `action` and the reviewer's `findings` — required, and a non-empty string, but purely a validated input: no field of the job or of any other row retains it — and it is available only from `FIELD_REPORTED`. Sending the work back moves the job `FIELD_REPORTED → REWORK_REQUIRED` and increments `rework_round` by one; this is permitted only while `rework_round` is still below the ceiling of 2 completed rework cycles, and a third attempt made when `rework_round` already stands at 2 is rejected with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The operations cycle `FIELD_REPORTED → REWORK_REQUIRED → FIELD_REPORTED` is therefore bounded at two turns.

Accepting the plugging is the pivot of the whole workflow, and it fires four dependent hops. The job transitions `FIELD_REPORTED → PLUGGED` and `plugging_accepted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. First, exactly three reclamation tasks are created for the job, with `task_type` `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL`, `PIT_CLOSURE` and `REVEGETATION` respectively, each at `status` `PENDING`. Second, the well transitions `PLUGGING_SCHEDULED → PLUGGED` and its `version` increments. Third, caused by the second, the owning operator's `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents` are all recomputed — a plugged well drops out of the assurance base entirely. Fourth, caused by the third, an operator standing at `DELINQUENT` whose `bond_amount_cents` now equals or exceeds the freshly reduced `required_bond_cents` transitions `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`. The job's `version` increments as well.

### 3.8. Surface Reclamation and Job Closeout

Plugging the hole is only half the obligation; the surface has to come back too. The three tasks created at plugging acceptance are inspected independently by a `FieldInspector`, each starting at `PENDING` with `inspection_round` 0 and with `inspected_by_user_id`, `notes` and `completed_at` all null. They are readable individually, and as a per-job list ordered by `task_type` ascending — that is, `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL`, then `PIT_CLOSURE`, then `REVEGETATION` — filterable by `status` with `limit` and `offset`.

A task is advanced by naming an `action` and optionally `notes`; `notes`, when supplied, must be a non-empty string and is stored on all three actions alike, and when it is omitted the field keeps whatever an earlier move left there rather than being cleared. Starting work is available from `PENDING` and from `REWORK_NEEDED`, requires the parent job's `status` to be `PLUGGED`, and moves the task to `IN_PROGRESS`. Passing the inspection is available only from `IN_PROGRESS`: it moves the task to `COMPLETE`, stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `inspected_by_user_id`. Failing the inspection is available only from `IN_PROGRESS`: it moves the task to `REWORK_NEEDED`, increments `inspection_round` by one, and records `inspected_by_user_id`. The inspection cycle `IN_PROGRESS → REWORK_NEEDED → IN_PROGRESS` may repeat without a declared bound. `COMPLETE` is terminal; a pass attempted on a task still sitting at `PENDING` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every move increments the task's `version`.

**Revegetation is special in two ways, and both are enforced.** Grass does not take on freshly moved earth, so revegetation may not *start* until the same job's `PIT_CLOSURE` task has reached `COMPLETE` and at least 90 days have elapsed between that task's `completed_at` and the logical clock. If the pit closure task is not complete — it has no `completed_at` yet — or fewer than 90 days have passed since it completed, the start is rejected with `ERR_REVEGETATION_WAITING_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). Separately, revegetation sign-off is the job's final acceptance, so it may not be *passed* while any sibling task of the same job is outstanding: if either the `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL` or the `PIT_CLOSURE` task of that job is not `COMPLETE`, the pass is rejected with `ERR_RECLAMATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). That second rule is exactly what makes the automatic closeout below safe to treat as a pure side effect of the third completion. Neither rule applies to the `EQUIPMENT_REMOVAL` or `PIT_CLOSURE` tasks, which may be started and passed in any order relative to one another.

**Closeout.** When a pass makes all three of a job's reclamation tasks `COMPLETE`, the closeout rolls upward in one synchronous chain. The job transitions `PLUGGED → RECLAIMED` and `reclaimed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The well transitions `PLUGGED → RECLAIMED`, which is its terminal lifecycle state. The owning operator's `active_well_count`, `orphan_candidate_count` and `required_bond_cents` are recomputed, since a reclaimed well leaves the assurance base for good — the middle counter cannot actually move on this hop, because neither `PLUGGED` nor `RECLAIMED` was ever inside it — and the operator may again cure a delinquency in the process. Job, well and operator each increment their own `version`.


### 3.9. Funding Administration, the Commitment Waterfall and Federal Reporting

Three streams of money pay for this program and they do not have identical strings attached, which is why the program obligates them in a fixed order rather than proportionally.

A funding stream is registered with its `kind` — `STATE_FUND` for a legislative appropriation, `FEDERAL_GRANT` for a federal award, or `FORFEITED_BOND` for assurance seized from an operator — an identifying `name`, its total `authorized_cents` (strictly positive), the obligation expiry `expires_at`, and `allowable_cost_categories`, a **non-empty** array drawn from the values `PLUGGING`, `RECLAMATION` and `ADMIN` — an empty array is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two inputs are conditional. `federal_match_required_pct`, the non-federal match percentage the award demands as an integer from 0 through 100, is required when `kind` is `FEDERAL_GRANT` and must be omitted or null for any other kind. `bond_operator_id`, naming the operator whose forfeited bond created the source, is required when `kind` is `FORFEITED_BOND` and null for any other kind; it is a body foreign key like any other, so a `bond_operator_id` naming no operator is rejected with `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND` ahead of every schema check of this request, exactly as §2.10 step 6 requires. A new source begins with `status` `ACTIVE`, `committed_cents` 0 and `disbursed_cents` 0 — and it begins `ACTIVE` even when the `expires_at` it was handed already lies at or before the logical clock, because registration never inspects that date against the clock; such a source reaches `EXPIRED` only when the clock is next advanced, although the commitment eligibility test, the `override_source_id` rejection and the grant-report gate described below all read `expires_at` directly and therefore already treat it as lapsed, while the disbursement gate, which tests `status` alone, does not. Its computed `reporting_required` flag is true when and only when `kind` is `FEDERAL_GRANT` — that flag is what makes a source reportable. Sources are readable individually and as a list filtered by `kind`, `status` and `bond_operator_id`, with `limit` and `offset`.

A source has three availability states. It is `ACTIVE` while authority remains. It becomes `EXHAUSTED` at the moment a commitment pass leaves `committed_cents` exactly equal to `authorized_cents`, and it returns `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` the moment a deobligation leaves `committed_cents` strictly below `authorized_cents` again — the availability cycle `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` is entirely normal. It becomes `EXPIRED`, from either `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, when the logical clock is advanced to an instant at or after its `expires_at`, as described in §2.5; once `EXPIRED` no new commitment may name it and no allocation against it may be disbursed, and that state is terminal. Every one of these flips increments the source's `version`.

**The commitment waterfall.** A `FundingOfficer` commits money to a job in one all-or-nothing pass, and the need being covered is exactly the job's `plugging_cost_estimate_cents`. No precondition whatsoever is placed on the job's own `status`: a commitment may be run against a job standing anywhere in its lifecycle, from `SCHEDULED` through `RECLAIMED`. Nor is a job limited to one pass — nothing checks whether allocations already exist against it, so a second commitment on an already-funded job covers the full estimate over again and adds a fresh set of allocation rows alongside the first. Eligibility for the pool is explicit: a source qualifies when its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, `PLUGGING` is present among its `allowable_cost_categories`, its `authorized_cents` minus its `committed_cents` is strictly greater than zero, and — for a source whose `kind` is `FORFEITED_BOND` — its `bond_operator_id` equals the `operator_id` of the job's well. Sources that are `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` are excluded, as is a forfeited bond belonging to some other operator.

The eligible pool is then ordered: first by kind rank ascending, where `FORFEITED_BOND` ranks 1, `FEDERAL_GRANT` ranks 2 and `STATE_FUND` ranks 3 — the polluter's own money is spent before the taxpayer's; then, within a kind, by `expires_at` ascending, so authority that is about to lapse is used first; then by `created_at` ascending. The row `id` is never part of the ordering.

The pass then walks that ordered list once, tracking the cents still needed and the cents committed **on this job to non-federal sources so far in this pass**. For each source in turn, its available authority is `authorized_cents` minus `committed_cents`. For a non-federal source the contribution is the smaller of the cents still needed and that available authority. For a `FEDERAL_GRANT` a third limit applies, the match entitlement: when the grant's `federal_match_required_pct` is 0 the entitlement cap is simply the cents still needed, and otherwise the cap is the non-federal cents already committed on this job at the moment this source is evaluated, multiplied by 100 minus the required match percentage, divided by the required match percentage, with that quotient taken **as a floor** — rounded down to a whole cent, because a federal grant may never exceed its match entitlement by even one cent. The federal contribution is then the smallest of the cents still needed, the available authority, and that entitlement cap.

Two properties of that walk are load-bearing. It is a **single pass with a pinned order**: non-federal cents committed *later* in the same pass — a `STATE_FUND` reached after the grant — do not retroactively raise the grant's entitlement cap, and the waterfall never runs a second pass to try again. And a contribution that comes out at zero or below contributes nothing and creates no allocation row; the walk simply moves to the next source. A source that does contribute produces one allocation carrying its `source_id`, the contributed `allocated_cents`, and a `waterfall_rank` recording the 1-based position that source occupied in the ordered list. Each contribution reduces the cents still needed, and a contribution from a non-federal source also raises the running non-federal base that any later federal grant's cap is measured against.

If the walk ends with cents still needed, **nothing at all is persisted** — no allocations, no change to any source's `committed_cents`, no change to the job's `funded_cents` — and the failure is reported with one of two errors whose precedence is explicit. When at least one `FEDERAL_GRANT` contribution was throttled below the lesser of the cents then needed and its own available authority by the match cap, the answer is `ERR_FEDERAL_MATCH_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), because the honest diagnosis is a missing match rather than a missing appropriation. Otherwise the answer is `ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 422). The match error outranks the shortfall error.

On success one allocation per contributing source is persisted at `status` `COMMITTED` with `disbursed_cents` 0, `disbursed_by_user_id`, `disbursed_at` and `deobligated_at` null, and the acting `X-User-ID` stored as `committed_by_user_id` — the value the disbursement segregation-of-duties gate compares against. Each contributing source's `committed_cents` rises by its contribution, and any source whose `committed_cents` thereby reaches `authorized_cents` flips `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`. The job's `funded_cents` is recomputed from scratch as the sum of `allocated_cents` across its allocations in `COMMITTED` or `DISBURSED` status — which after one successful pass equals its `plugging_cost_estimate_cents`, and after a second such pass equals twice it. The response carries the created allocations, in waterfall order, with HTTP 201. That payload is an array (`response_type` `array`) but it is not a list endpoint, so its `meta` carries only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`: none of the four pagination fields of §2.3 — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more` — appears on it.

**Forcing a single source.** The optional `override_source_id` narrows the eligible pool to exactly that one source, which must still satisfy every eligibility test above — `ACTIVE` status, an `expires_at` strictly after the clock, `PLUGGING` among its allowable categories, remaining authority, and the bond-operator match — and which occupies `waterfall_rank` 1. A named source that is `EXPIRED`, or whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). A named `FORFEITED_BOND` source whose `bond_operator_id` differs from the `operator_id` of the job's well is rejected with `ERR_FORFEITED_BOND_OPERATOR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — one operator's seized assurance may not subsidize another operator's well. Those two are the only override failures with an error of their own. A named source that clears both and then fails one of the remaining eligibility tests — a `status` of `EXHAUSTED`, no `PLUGGING` among its categories, or no authority left — simply leaves the pool empty, so the pass covers nothing and the answer is `ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 422) rather than any bespoke eligibility error; the match error can never arise out of an empty pool.

Allocations are readable individually and as a list filtered by `job_id`, `source_id` and `status`, ordered by `waterfall_rank` ascending, with `limit` and `offset`.

**Disbursement and deobligation.** An allocation is advanced by a `FundingOfficer` naming an `action` and, when paying, the `invoice_cents` being paid, an integer of zero or more. Disbursement is available only from `COMMITTED` and carries four further conditions, of which the first is evaluated out of turn: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `committed_by_user_id`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and per §2.10 that test runs ahead of both schema validation and the `COMMITTED` precondition, so an officer naming `DISBURSE` against their own allocation is told about the conflict of interest even when `invoice_cents` is missing and even when the allocation has already been disbursed or deobligated; the job's `status` must be `FIELD_REPORTED`, `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED`, because work must be performed before it is paid for; the source's `status` must not be `EXPIRED`; and `invoice_cents` must be no greater than the allocation's `allocated_cents`, with a strictly greater invoice rejected as `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_ALLOCATION` (HTTP 422). On success the allocation's `disbursed_cents` is set to the invoiced amount, `status` becomes `DISBURSED`, `disbursed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `disbursed_by_user_id`, and the source's `disbursed_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `disbursed_cents` across its allocations in `DISBURSED` status.

Deobligation is available only from `COMMITTED` and only while the job's `status` is neither `PLUGGED` nor `RECLAIMED` — money cannot be pulled back off work the state has already accepted. It sets `status` to `DEOBLIGATED` and stamps `deobligated_at` from the logical clock. Because the allocation leaves the committed-or-disbursed set, the source's `committed_cents` is recomputed and its available authority is restored exactly to its pre-commitment value; the job's `funded_cents` is recomputed and therefore falls by the deobligated `allocated_cents`; and a source that had been sitting at `EXHAUSTED` transitions `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE`. Both `DISBURSED` and `DEOBLIGATED` are terminal: disbursing an already-deobligated allocation reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every transition increments the allocation's `version`.

**Federal reporting.** Only a federal grant is reportable. A `FundingOfficer` generates a reporting period against a source by supplying `period_start` and `period_end`, each an inclusive calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; a `period_end` earlier than `period_start` is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a report requested against a source whose `reporting_required` is false. A source that is `EXPIRED`, or whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_EXPIRED`. Reports are readable individually, and as a per-source list ordered by `period_start` ascending, filterable by `status` with `limit` and `offset`.

Three figures are computed at generation and recomputed on every submission, and the window comparison uses the **date part** of each timestamp against the inclusive span from `period_start` through `period_end`. The federal disbursements in scope are the allocations against this report's `source_id` whose `status` is `DISBURSED` and whose `disbursed_at` date falls inside the window; allocations still `COMMITTED` or already `DEOBLIGATED` are excluded, as are disbursements dated outside the window. Their `disbursed_cents` are summed into `reported_cost_cents`, an exact integer cent figure. The wells counted are the distinct plugging jobs behind those federal disbursements whose own `status` is `PLUGGED` or `RECLAIMED` — a job funded but not yet accepted is excluded — and that count is written to `wells_plugged_count`. Finally, the total project cost is the sum of `disbursed_cents` across **every** allocation, from any source whatsoever, whose `status` is `DISBURSED`, whose `job_id` is one of those counted jobs, and whose `disbursed_at` date falls inside the same window; `federal_share_pct` is then 100 multiplied by `reported_cost_cents`, divided by that total, rounded half up to two decimal places. When the total is zero, `federal_share_pct` is exactly 0.00 rather than an error.

A new report begins at `status` `DUE`, with `revision_round` 0, `submitted_by_user_id` null and `reviewer_note` null. Submission is available from `DUE` and from `REJECTED`, recomputes all three figures against the data as it stands at submission time, sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`; a resubmission out of `REJECTED` additionally increments `revision_round` by one, so that counter tracks completed reject-then-resubmit cycles. Review is available only from `SUBMITTED`: accepting moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and rejecting moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`; both write `reviewer_note` from the request, and a decision that supplies none writes it back to null, so an accepted report does not keep the note an earlier rejection left on it. A `reviewer_note` sent on a `SUBMIT` is validated as a non-empty string and then discarded — submission never alters the stored note. This endpoint does not split its two authorized roles across its three actions and applies no segregation-of-duties test of any kind: either a `FundingOfficer` or an `AwardAuthority` may submit, accept or reject, and the officer who submitted a report may be the very one who accepts it. The reporting cycle `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` therefore repeats until the report is accepted, and figures are recomputed afresh on each pass rather than being frozen at first submission. Every transition increments the report's `version`.
