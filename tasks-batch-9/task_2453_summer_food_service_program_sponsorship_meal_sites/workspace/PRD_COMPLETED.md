# SFSP Sponsorship & Meal Sites API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every summer, sponsoring organizations feed children in neighbourhoods where the school-year meal programs have gone quiet. The work is generous but the paperwork is unforgiving: a sponsor must prove that each proposed site sits in a low-income area, get a program-year budget approved before it spends a dollar, count meals child-by-child at the point of service, host unannounced monitoring visits in the first week of operation, and then file a monthly reimbursement claim that survives a state agency's edit checks. When any one of those obligations slips — an income dataset that expired before the season opened, a hot-holding cabinet that drifted below temperature, a monitoring visit conducted a day late — the meals served that day stop being reimbursable, and the sponsor discovers it weeks later when the claim comes back short.

This document specifies the API that carries that entire chain of obligations. It is mounted under `/sfsp` and it serves four constituencies at once: a state agency that approves and pays, sponsor administrators who plan and claim, on-site supervisors who count and log, and independent field monitors who inspect and open corrective actions. The system is deliberately built as a compliance ledger rather than a reporting tool: eligibility, monitoring coverage, food-safety status and corrective-action standing are all *preconditions* that gate the money, not annotations printed alongside it. A site whose critical deficiency is unresolved cannot have its meals claimed; a claim whose line item references a site with no timely first-week visit loses every meal on that line.

The second design commitment is exact reversibility of money. Advances paid to a sponsor before the season are recovered out of the sponsor's own reimbursement claims through an ordered waterfall, and a paid claim may be reopened for revision at any time. The advance ledger must therefore read identically before a payment and after that payment has been reversed — no drift, no rounding residue, no orphaned partial recovery. Everything in this specification that looks like an accounting detail is really that invariant being defended.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: who may call, what a response looks like, how records are identified and versioned, and in what order a malformed or non-compliant request is rejected.

### 2.1. Program Actors and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity it acts in. Four roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `StateAgencyReviewer`, `SponsorAdmin`, `SiteSupervisor`, and `FieldMonitor`. A request that omits either header, sends either header with an empty value, or whose `X-User-Role` is not one of those four strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); role matching is exact and case-sensitive, and `X-User-ID` is never validated beyond being non-empty. A well-authenticated request whose role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint it targets is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the four roles are backed by a persisted entity, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `SponsorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Sponsor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Sponsor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — directly on a sponsor, through `SponsorBudget.sponsor_id`, `MealClaim.sponsor_id` or `Site.sponsor_id`, and for a corrective action by following its `site_id` to that site's `sponsor_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Site`; ownership checks compare the resource's site reference to `X-User-ID` — directly on a site, or through `MealServiceDay.site_id`. A mismatch on any `SponsorAdmin` or `SiteSupervisor` write endpoint is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The other two roles are unscoped operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `StateAgencyReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the state agency acts across all sponsors, and the server does not compare its `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldMonitor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it in several places where a scaffold transition writes it: as `monitor_user_id` on the visits that monitor owns, as `opened_by_user_id` on a corrective action opened by that monitor's report, and as `closed_by_user_id` on an action that monitor closes. The server never compares a `FieldMonitor`'s `X-User-ID` to an entity `id`; it compares it to those recorded user-id fields. A `SiteSupervisor`'s identifier is similarly stamped onto `recorded_by_user_id` when it posts a meal count or a holding-log reading.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from the wall clock — so every response returned while the clock stands still carries the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints only, and are absent from the `meta` of every single-object response and every error response.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` skips that many records before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something that does not read as an integer at all, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An `offset` that runs past the end of the filtered collection is not an error: it yields an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Because the logical clock of §2.4 only advances when a caller sets it, many records legitimately share an identical `created_at`; records tied that way are returned in the order the server created them, which makes the ordering total and deterministic without ever falling back to `id`. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

Each collection read also accepts a set of equality filters, described in the workflow sections that own the corresponding resources. Budgets may be narrowed by `sponsor_id`, `program_year` and `status`; advances by `sponsor_id`, `status` and `request_month`; sites by `sponsor_id`, `area_eligibility_record_id`, `approval_status`, `operating_status` and `site_type`; service days by `site_id`, `service_date`, `status` and `food_safety_status`; meal count entries by `meal_service_day_id`, `reversal_of_entry_id`, `meal_type` and `entry_kind`; holding-log entries by `meal_service_day_id`, `meal_type` and `breach_flag`; monitoring visits by `site_id`, `monitor_user_id`, `status`, `visit_type` and `scheduled_date`; corrective actions by `site_id`, `monitoring_visit_id`, `follow_up_of_corrective_action_id`, `status`, `severity` and `origin`; claims by `sponsor_id`, `claim_month` and `status`; and claim line items by `meal_claim_id`, `site_id`, `edit_check_status` and `disallow_reason`. Filters combine conjunctively and match by exact equality. A filter whose value is malformed for the field it names — not a member of that field's declared enum set, or not a well-formed date, `YYYY-MM` month, integer or boolean — is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a boolean filter accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case. An identifier-valued filter is not existence-checked, so a well-formed identifier matching nothing simply yields an empty page rather than a 404, and query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Program Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Due dates, lateness flags, holding-time arithmetic, advance-demand timing and corrective-action escalation windows all resolve against a server-held **logical clock**, and an administrative capability reserved to the `StateAgencyReviewer` sets that clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant, echoing the new value back as `current_utc`, which is the sole key of that response's `data` object. `to_iso8601` is mandatory, and a missing, null or unparseable value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The clock may be set to any instant, earlier or later than the one it currently holds: there is no monotonicity requirement, and setting it backwards is accepted exactly as setting it forwards is. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must be initialised to exactly that value before any caller sets it explicitly.

Whenever this document says "the current date" or "the logical clock date", it means the UTC calendar date of that logical instant. Calendar arithmetic — a due date five days after an action was opened, the last calendar day of the month following an advance's `request_month` — is performed on UTC dates, never on local dates and never on elapsed-second counts.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Row Versioning

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and never used in a business decision or as a sort key. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits stamped from the logical clock at creation; `created_at` is the only sanctioned tie-break ordering key anywhere in this specification, and where two rows carry the same `created_at` the order in which the server created them settles the tie, as §2.3 sets out. Every other instant a resource or a response carries is rendered in that same ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits — including an instant the client supplied, such as a service day's `food_received_at` or a holding-log reading's `measured_at`, which is normalised into that canonical rendering rather than echoed back byte-for-byte as it was submitted. A client-supplied instant is accepted with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit numeric UTC offset, or with no zone designator at all; an offset is converted to UTC before the value is stored, and a value carrying no zone at all is read as already being UTC. Precision finer than a millisecond is truncated toward zero rather than rounded when the instant is rendered. Fields holding a calendar date rather than an instant remain plain `YYYY-MM-DD`, and fields holding a clock time remain the plain `HH:MM` string the caller supplied.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation and exactly equal to `created_at` on a row that has never been mutated, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative, such as a cascade that voids a service day or a recomputation that rewrites a derived counter.

Three record types are append-only and immutable once written, and they are the exception: an `AreaEligibilityRecord`, a `MealCountEntry` and a `HoldingLogEntry` each carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those three, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely — not null, not zero, absent. A correction to a meal count is expressed by writing a new reversing entry, never by editing the original. Apart from that one exception, every resource representation carries exactly the fields the workflow section owning it declares — no server-internal bookkeeping is ever exposed — and a field that has no value yet is present and null rather than omitted.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

When a path contains several identifiers, they are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces its own 404 before any later identifier is examined. A meal-count posting aimed at a service day that does not exist fails with `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND` regardless of what else the payload contains.

More generally, a request passes through a fixed gauntlet, and the *first* check that fails determines the response. The order is exactly this:

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the target endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request well-formedness: required fields present, enum members drawn from their declared sets, integers non-negative where declared, dates written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, clock times written as `HH:MM` on a 24-hour clock, `limit` between 1 and 100, `offset` at least 0, and every `decision`, `to_status` and `method` value inside its declared set — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A body that is absent or empty is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts no body at all; a body that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not a JSON object, is rejected here, and the request's `Content-Type` header is never inspected. A field supplied as an explicit `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted — required if the field is required, otherwise taking the field's documented default — and body fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Types are checked strictly: a string field rejects non-strings and rejects a value that is empty or nothing but whitespace, storing an accepted value untrimmed; an integer field rejects booleans and rejects a JSON number written with a fractional part even when that part is zero; a boolean field accepts only `true` and `false`; an array field rejects anything that is not a JSON array, and a map-valued field anything that is not a JSON object. A field carrying a decimal quantity may be supplied either as a JSON number or as a numeric string but never as a boolean, and it is rejected when it is not finite, when it falls outside the field's declared range, or when it carries more decimal places than that field declares.
4. Existence of the referenced records, in this sequence: sponsor (`ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND`), area eligibility record (`ERR_AREA_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`), sponsor budget (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`), advance payment (`ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_FOUND`), site (`ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`), service day (`ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND`), meal count entry (`ERR_COUNT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`), holding-log entry (`ERR_HOLDING_LOG_NOT_FOUND`), monitoring visit (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`), corrective action (`ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`), meal claim (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`), claim line item (`ERR_CLAIM_LINE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`).
5. Ownership for the entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
6. Segregation of duties on corrective-action review — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. Same-entity state legality, in this sequence: sponsor (`ERR_SPONSOR_STATE_INVALID`), budget (`ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID`), advance (`ERR_ADVANCE_STATE_INVALID`), site (`ERR_SITE_STATE_INVALID`), service day (`ERR_SERVICE_DAY_STATE_INVALID`), monitoring visit (`ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`), corrective action (`ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`), meal claim (`ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`).
8. Cross-entity readiness, in this sequence: sponsor approval (`ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED`), site cleared for review (`ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED`), site not suspended (`ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED`), site operating (`ERR_SITE_NOT_OPERATING`), budget approved (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`).
9. Stale-version protection on the sponsor — `ERR_STALE_SPONSOR_VERSION`.
10. Food-safety and eligibility gates, in this sequence: `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_BREACH`, `ERR_MEASURED_BEFORE_FOOD_RECEIVED`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
11. Referential business rules, in this sequence: `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_REVERSAL_TARGET_INVALID`, `ERR_SITE_SPONSOR_MISMATCH`, `ERR_AREA_RECORD_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ENROLLED_PCT_REQUIRED`.
12. Structural and uniqueness constraints, in this sequence: `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_COUNT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_RECORD`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_DAY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_MONTH`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_LINE_ITEM`, `ERR_NO_FINALIZED_DAYS`, `ERR_CLAIM_HAS_NO_LINE_ITEMS`, `ERR_CLAIM_HOLD_BLOCKING`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FORECAST_HISTORY`, `ERR_DAY_HAS_OPEN_SERVICE`, `ERR_NO_APPROVED_SITE`.
13. Temporal constraints, in this sequence: `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_OUTSIDE_SEASON`, `ERR_MEAL_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_MONITOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_MONITOR_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_VISIT_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_DEMAND_TOO_EARLY`, `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE`.
14. Fiscal ceilings, in this sequence: `ERR_ADMIN_RATIO_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_APPROVED_CEILING_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`, `ERR_APPROVED_EXCEEDS_REQUESTED`, `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_CAP`, `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_OPERATING_COST_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, `ERR_ADMIN_COST_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve to be called out, because they are easy to get backwards. First, a claim submission by a sponsor whose budget was never approved reports `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED` rather than either of the two cost-ceiling errors, because cross-entity readiness is checked before fiscal ceilings. Second, a site sitting in `SUSPENDED` reports `ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED` rather than `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED`: the "cleared for review" gate deliberately treats only `PENDING_REVIEW` and `DENIED` as failures, leaving suspension to the more specific check immediately after it.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Reversals

A great deal of this system's behaviour is second-order: one operation changes a row, and that change legitimately changes other rows. Those cascades are part of the specification, not an implementation nicety, and each hop independently mutates the row it touches — refreshing that row's `updated_at` and incrementing its `version`.

Three cascade families run through the workflows below. Terminating a sponsor denies its sites and voids their unserved days. Suspending or denying a site voids its unserved days, raises a claim hold on the site, and marks every open claim line item that references it. Rejecting a corrective-action response spawns a follow-up action one severity level higher, which — if that level is `CRITICAL` — suspends the site and fires the suspension cascade in turn.

Two of these have explicit **compensating** counterparts, and the compensation is deliberately asymmetric. Reinstating a suspended site clears the site's claim hold and releases the line-item holds that the suspension raised, but it never un-voids a service day: a day whose service was cancelled by a suspension stays cancelled, because the meals were genuinely not served. Reopening a paid claim, in contrast, is an *exact* reversal on the money side: every cent this claim recovered from every advance is handed back to that advance, each advance's status is recomputed from its restored balance, and the sponsor's ledger totals return to the values they held immediately before the payment.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on the Sponsor Ledger

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency against the `Sponsor` row, because both of them make sense only against a freshly-read view of that sponsor's advance position.

Requesting a new advance payment requires a non-optional `expected_sponsor_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Sponsor`. If that value does not match the sponsor's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SPONSOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is substantive rather than mechanical: a new advance request legitimately supersedes the sponsor's prior advance position in the recovery waterfall, so a request built on a stale view of `advance_outstanding_cents` would otherwise be accepted and quietly distort the ledger. The stale-version check forces the client to re-read the sponsor's advance ledger first.

Opening a monthly meal claim carries the same guard: the create request requires a non-optional `expected_sponsor_version` matching the target `Sponsor`'s current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SPONSOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because paying a claim recovers outstanding advances, and because a newly disbursed advance changes what the next claim will net out to, a claim created against a sponsor whose version has advanced since the client last read it is refused rather than silently accepted. Both of these operations also advance the sponsor's own `version` by 1 on success, so a client that creates two advances back to back must re-read the sponsor between them.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Every operation not named in this subsection is processed without any optimistic-concurrency check.

### 2.9. Money, Meal Counts and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is carried as an integer number of cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. Reimbursement rates are the sole exception: a rate is a fractional cent amount with exactly two decimal places, and the rounding rule that turns rates into money is pinned in §3.8 rather than left to the implementer. Where a cap is expressed as a percentage of another amount, the cap is computed with a floor — never a round — so that a ceiling can never exceed its stated percentage by even one cent.

Meal counts are integers throughout. Where an allowance is expressed as a percentage of a meal count, that allowance is also floored, which means small counts can produce an allowance of zero. Percentages used for eligibility are compared at two decimal places, temperatures at one decimal place, and the budget's administrative ratio is carried at four decimal places using round-half-up. Every one of those fractional quantities travels on the wire as a JSON number rather than a string, and the declared scale governs the comparison rather than the rendering: an `admin_cost_ratio` computed as 0.2000 is rendered as `0.2`, and an `eligibility_pct_used` of 50.00 is rendered as `50.0`. Elapsed holding time is expressed in whole minutes truncated toward zero.

The one place a genuine round-half-up appears in the money path is the conversion of a meal count multiplied by a fractional-cent rate into whole cents, and the *granularity* of that rounding is normative: it happens once per line item per meal type, before any summation. Summing meal counts across line items, or across meal types, before rounding produces a different and incorrect total.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties in Compliance Review

The role model deliberately splits authority so that no single operator can both create and clear an obligation. A `SponsorAdmin` submits a claim but can never validate or pay it; those decisions belong to the `StateAgencyReviewer`. A `SiteSupervisor` records point-of-service counts and closes a day's counting but cannot finalize the day for claiming; finalization belongs to the `SponsorAdmin`. And a `FieldMonitor` who filed the report that opened a corrective action may never review that same action: reviewing an action whose `opened_by_user_id` equals the reviewer's `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Actions opened by the system itself record the literal string `SYSTEM` as `opened_by_user_id`, so any monitor may review them.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Sponsor Into the Program

A sponsorship record is opened by the state agency, not by the sponsor: a `StateAgencyReviewer` registers the organization with its `legal_name`, its `sponsor_type` — one of `SCHOOL_FOOD_AUTHORITY`, `GOVERNMENT`, `NONPROFIT` or `CAMP_OPERATOR` — its `experience_status`, which is either `NEW` or `EXPERIENCED` depending on whether the organization ran a summer program in a prior year, and the `program_year` the record covers, which must be 2020 or later. The reviewer may also set `advance_eligible`, the state-agency flag that permits the sponsor to request advance payments at all; when the field is omitted it defaults to false. The new record begins in `application_status` `DRAFT` with `version` 1, and its four ledger counters — `advance_paid_cents`, `advance_recovered_cents`, `advance_outstanding_cents` and `reimbursement_paid_cents` — all begin at 0.

The sponsor then submits its own application, moving `application_status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`. Only a `SponsorAdmin` acting for that sponsor may do so, and only from `DRAFT`; a submission attempted from any other status is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_STATE_INVALID`. The submission takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and it bumps the sponsor's `version` by 1 and changes nothing else.

Review is a single decision endpoint that carries a `decision` value and an optional `reason`. From `SUBMITTED`, a `decision` of `APPROVED` moves the sponsor to `APPROVED` and stamps `approved_at` with the current logical instant; a `decision` of `DENIED` moves it to `DENIED` and stores the supplied text in `application_decision_reason`, and the `reason` is mandatory on that branch. From `APPROVED`, a `decision` of `TERMINATED` moves the sponsor to `TERMINATED` and likewise requires a `reason`, which is stored in `application_decision_reason`. `DENIED` and `TERMINATED` are terminal — no further application transition is possible from either. A `reason` supplied alongside an `APPROVED` decision is accepted and then ignored: `application_decision_reason` stays null on that branch. `approved_at`, once stamped, is never cleared, so a sponsor later moved to `TERMINATED` still carries the instant of its approval. Every one of these decisions increments the sponsor's `version` by 1.

Termination is the system's heaviest cascade, and it runs in two hops. First, every site belonging to that sponsor whose `approval_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `approval_status` `DENIED`, has `suspension_reason` set to the literal string `SPONSOR_TERMINATED`, and has `claim_hold` raised to true — a site-level hold only, which this cascade does not propagate down to claim line items the way the suspension cascade of §3.5 does. Second — *because* those sites became `DENIED` — every service day of each of those sites whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `OPEN` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal string `SITE_DENIED`, and each site's `voided_day_count` increases by one per day voided. The denial cascade is the one cascade that voids planned days irrespective of their `service_date`: a planned day already in the past is voided along with the rest, which is not true of the suspension and season-close cascades described in §3.5.

A sponsor's ledger fields are read-only projections maintained by the workflows that touch money. `advance_paid_cents` is the lifetime sum of `approved_amount_cents` over the sponsor's advances that reached `DISBURSED`; `advance_recovered_cents` is the lifetime sum of `recovered_amount_cents` over that sponsor's advances; `advance_outstanding_cents` is the conserved difference between the two; and `reimbursement_paid_cents` is the sum of `net_payable_cents` over the sponsor's claims currently sitting in `PAID`.

### 3.2. The Program-Year Budget: Approval, Revision and Amendment

Before a sponsor may claim a cent it must have an approved spending plan for the year. A `SponsorAdmin` opens one by supplying the `sponsor_id` it acts for, the `program_year` the plan covers — which must be 2020 or later and must equal the sponsor's own `program_year`; that equality is a cross-row comparison rather than a field-shape one, so it is made only after the sponsor has been resolved and ownership confirmed, and a mismatch is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — a strictly positive `operating_cost_budget_cents` covering food, labour and delivery, an `admin_cost_budget_cents` of at least zero, and a strictly positive `projected_total_meals` representing the reimbursable meals the sponsor expects to serve across the season. The budget opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0.

On every write, the server derives `admin_cost_ratio`: the administrative line divided by the sum of the operating and administrative lines, carried to four decimal places using round-half-up. That ratio is the gate on submission. A budget may be submitted only while the sponsor's `application_status` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED` — and only when `admin_cost_ratio` is at most 0.2000; a ratio above that threshold is rejected with `ERR_ADMIN_RATIO_EXCEEDED`. A ratio of exactly 0.2000 passes.

Submission is one operation serving three different starting points, and which transition it performs depends entirely on the budget's current `status`. From `DRAFT` it moves the budget to `SUBMITTED`. From `REVISION_REQUESTED` it moves the budget back to `SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_round` by 1, and clears `decision_reason` to null — this is the revision loop, and it can run any number of times. From `APPROVED` it moves the budget to `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, leaving the previously approved ceilings in force so that claims may continue to be submitted while the amendment is under review. All three paths require `admin_cost_ratio` at most 0.2000, all three stamp `submitted_at` with the submission instant on the logical clock, and all three increment the budget's `version` by 1. A submission attempted from `SUBMITTED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `WITHDRAWN` is rejected with `ERR_BUDGET_STATE_INVALID`.

The reviewer's decision endpoint accepts a `decision` of `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, plus `approved_operating_cost_cents`, `approved_admin_cost_cents` and `reason` as the branch requires. A field the chosen branch does not require is accepted and then ignored: a `reason` sent with an approval is never stored, and approved ceilings sent with a `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN` decision are never written. Approving a `SUBMITTED` budget writes both approved ceilings from the request onto `approved_operating_cost_cents` and `approved_admin_cost_cents`, records the decision instant in `decided_at`, and moves the budget to `APPROVED`. Both ceiling fields are mandatory on an approval, each must be at least zero — an approved ceiling of 0 is a legal approval, a negative one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and each must be no greater than the corresponding requested line — an approved operating ceiling above `operating_cost_budget_cents`, or an approved administrative ceiling above `admin_cost_budget_cents`, is rejected with `ERR_APPROVED_CEILING_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`. Ceilings equal to the requested amounts are accepted.

Approving an `AMENDMENT_PENDING` budget behaves as a sibling of the same shape and must be specified with equal precision: it overwrites both approved ceilings from the request under the identical "no greater than the requested line" rule, increments `revision_round` by 1, and returns the budget to `APPROVED`. A `decision` of `REVISION_REQUESTED` against a `SUBMITTED` budget requires a `reason`, stores it in `decision_reason`, and moves the budget to `REVISION_REQUESTED`. The same decision against an `AMENDMENT_PENDING` budget also requires a `reason`, stores it in `decision_reason`, moves the budget to `REVISION_REQUESTED` — and additionally clears both `approved_operating_cost_cents` and `approved_admin_cost_cents` back to null, which is the material difference between the two revision branches: a rejected amendment withdraws the ceilings, while a first-round revision request never had ceilings to withdraw. Finally, a `decision` of `WITHDRAWN` is available from `SUBMITTED` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, requires a `reason`, stores it in `decision_reason`, and moves the budget to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. Every decision, whichever branch it takes, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock and increments the budget's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Advance Payments and Their Recovery Standing

An experienced sponsor may draw part of its expected reimbursement before the season, and the system tracks each draw as a distinct advance with its own recovery balance. A `SponsorAdmin` requests one by supplying the `sponsor_id`, the `request_month` the advance covers, and a strictly positive `requested_amount_cents`, together with the `expected_sponsor_version` guard described in §2.8. The `request_month` is written as a four-digit year followed by a hyphen and a two-digit month, and only the three summer operating months are accepted — `06`, `07` and `08`; any other month is a malformed request. The sponsor must be in `application_status` `APPROVED` or the request is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED`. A new advance opens in `status` `REQUESTED` with `recovered_amount_cents` and `outstanding_cents` both at 0, and the successful create increments the sponsor's `version` by 1. No uniqueness applies to the pair of sponsor and `request_month`: a sponsor may hold any number of advances covering the same month, each with its own balance, and a second request for a month already covered is accepted rather than refused.

The state agency then approves or denies the request, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, an `approved_amount_cents` on the approval branch, and a `reason` on the denial branch. Approval is gated by four substantive checks in addition to the state check that requires `status` `REQUESTED`. The sponsor must be permitted advances at all — `advance_eligible` true *and* `experience_status` equal to `EXPERIENCED`, and a sponsor failing either half is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE`. The sponsor's budget for the program year must be in `APPROVED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`. Which budget that is, when a sponsor holds several, is pinned rather than left open: the candidates are that sponsor's budgets in `APPROVED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` ordered by `created_at` ascending, and the one consulted is the first candidate whose `program_year` equals the sponsor's own `program_year`, falling back to the earliest-created candidate when none matches. If there are no candidates at all the check fails with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`. The approved amount must not exceed `requested_amount_cents`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_APPROVED_EXCEEDS_REQUESTED`; it may however be 0, which is a legal approval, while a negative value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. And the approved amount must not exceed one quarter of the total approved budget: take the sum of `approved_operating_cost_cents` and `approved_admin_cost_cents`, multiply by 0.25, discard any fractional cent by flooring, and reject an `approved_amount_cents` above that figure with `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_CAP`. An amount exactly equal to the floored cap is accepted. When a single approval attempt trips more than one of these gates, the §2.6 gauntlet settles which is reported, and the order there is not the order this paragraph narrates them in: the budget gate (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`) is evaluated first, then `ERR_APPROVED_EXCEEDS_REQUESTED`, then `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_CAP`, and the advance-eligibility gate (`ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ADVANCE_ELIGIBLE`) last of the four. Approval writes `approved_amount_cents` and moves the advance to `APPROVED`; denial stores the text in `decision_reason` and moves the advance to `DENIED`, which is terminal.

Disbursement moves an `APPROVED` advance to `DISBURSED`. It takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated. It is refused unless the sponsor has at least one site whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, which is reported as `ERR_NO_APPROVED_SITE` — a sponsor cannot draw money before it has anywhere to serve meals. On success the advance's `disbursed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its `outstanding_cents` is set equal to `approved_amount_cents`, the sponsor's `advance_paid_cents` grows by that same amount, the sponsor's `advance_outstanding_cents` is recomputed, and both the advance's `version` and the sponsor's `version` increment by 1.

When an advance is still owed after the season has moved on, the agency issues a recovery demand. A demand may be issued from `DISBURSED` or from `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, requires `outstanding_cents` strictly greater than 0, and requires the logical clock date to be strictly after the last calendar day of the month *following* the advance's `request_month` — for an advance covering July, a demand becomes available only from the first of September onward, and an attempt on or before 31 August is rejected with `ERR_DEMAND_TOO_EARLY`. Both of those failures carry that same identifier: an advance whose `outstanding_cents` has already fallen to 0 or below is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_TOO_EARLY` exactly as a premature date is, so the two conditions are indistinguishable from the response. A demand requires a `reason`, stores it in `decision_reason`, stamps `demand_issued_at` from the logical clock, and moves the advance to `DEMAND_ISSUED`.

The remaining advance transitions are all driven by claim payment and claim reopening rather than by any direct call, and they are specified in §3.8 alongside the recovery waterfall. What matters here is the field-level meaning they maintain: `recovered_amount_cents` accumulates every cent recovered from this advance by any claim, and `outstanding_cents` is `approved_amount_cents` less `recovered_amount_cents` while the advance sits in `DISBURSED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DEMAND_ISSUED`, and 0 in every other status. `demand_issued_at`, once stamped, is never cleared — it survives a recovery reversal precisely so that an advance that was demanded before being retired can be restored to `DEMAND_ISSUED` rather than to `DISBURSED` when a payment is unwound.

### 3.4. Area Income Data and Site Eligibility

Area eligibility rests on income data the state agency curates centrally and never edits. A `StateAgencyReviewer` records one determination at a time, giving the `area_code` — a census tract or school attendance-area identifier — the `data_source` it came from, which is either `SCHOOL_DATA` or `CENSUS_ACS`, the `free_reduced_pct` itself, the `determination_date` on which the percentage was determined, and the `valid_through_date`, the last date on which this record may still support an eligibility determination. Both dates are written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and no ordering relationship between them is enforced: a `valid_through_date` earlier than the `determination_date` is filed exactly as given, since only `valid_through_date` participates in the staleness test the eligibility determination applies. The percentage runs from 0 to 100 inclusive with at most two decimal places, and its meaning depends on the dataset: for `SCHOOL_DATA` it is the share of children approved for free or reduced-price meals, while for `CENSUS_ACS` it is the share of children in households below 185 percent of the federal poverty line. The pair of `area_code` and `data_source` must be unique across all records; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_RECORD`. Both halves of that pair are compared by exact, case-sensitive string equality, so two `area_code` values differing only in letter case or in surrounding whitespace are distinct areas rather than a duplicate. These rows carry only `id` and `created_at`, per §2.5.

A `SponsorAdmin` registers each meal site under its own `sponsor_id`, giving the site's `name` as it appears on the application, its `site_type`, its `production_mode`, the list of `approved_meal_types` it may serve, a strictly positive `approved_max_attendance` that will act as the per-meal-type ceiling on reimbursable first meals, and the `operating_start_date` and `operating_end_date` bounding the season — both mandatory, and the end date must not precede the start date, a one-payload comparison rejected at gauntlet step 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the sponsor is resolved; the two dates may be equal, giving a one-day season. `site_type` is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED_OPEN`, `CLOSED_ENROLLED`, `CAMP` or `MIGRANT`. `production_mode` is either `SELF_PREP`, meaning meals are produced at the site, or `VENDED`, meaning they are bought from a food-service vendor and delivered. Two optional booleans shape later rules and both default to false: `is_open_air` marks a site serving outdoors with no climate-controlled holding, which is what arms holding-time enforcement in §3.6, and `is_rural` participates with `production_mode` in selecting the reimbursement rate class in §3.8. The sponsor must be in `application_status` `APPROVED`, or the registration is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED`. A new site opens with `approval_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `operating_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `eligibility_basis` `UNDETERMINED`, `claim_hold` false, and `compliance_flag_count` and `voided_day_count` both at 0.

Which extra field the registration must supply depends on the site type, and the two requirements are mirror images. A site whose `site_type` is `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED_OPEN` or `MIGRANT` must name an `area_eligibility_record_id`; omitting it is rejected with `ERR_AREA_RECORD_REQUIRED`, and naming a record that does not exist is rejected with `ERR_AREA_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. A site whose `site_type` is `CLOSED_ENROLLED` must supply `enrolled_free_reduced_pct`, the documented share of its enrolled children who are free or reduced eligible, running from 0 to 100 inclusive; omitting it is rejected with `ERR_ENROLLED_PCT_REQUIRED`. A `CAMP` site needs neither, since camps establish eligibility child by child. `area_eligibility_record_id` and `enrolled_free_reduced_pct` are both null when not supplied. Supplying the field a site type does not need is permitted rather than rejected: the value is stored on the site as given, and an `area_eligibility_record_id` is resolved — and so still yields `ERR_AREA_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` when it names nothing — whatever the `site_type` is. The eligibility determination described below then ignores whichever of the two fields its branch does not read, so an `enrolled_free_reduced_pct` carried by an `OPEN` site, or an area record cited by a `CAMP` site, never influences the eligibility outcome.

The `approved_meal_types` list must be a non-empty selection drawn from `BREAKFAST`, `AM_SNACK`, `LUNCH`, `PM_SNACK` and `SUPPER`, with no duplicates. Those faults are matters of well-formedness rather than of the size cap, so a value that is not an array, an empty array, a member outside the five, or a repeated member is rejected at gauntlet step 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — before the sponsor is even resolved. Its size is capped by site type: an ordinary site may list at most two meal types, while a site whose `site_type` is `CAMP` or `MIGRANT` may list at most three provided that at least one of the three is `AM_SNACK` or `PM_SNACK`. A list that breaks either bound — three types at a non-camp non-migrant site, or three types at a camp with no snack among them — is rejected with `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_COUNT_EXCEEDED`.

Eligibility itself is determined by the state agency in a separate step, and it is available only while the site's `approval_status` is still `PENDING_REVIEW`; attempting it later is rejected with `ERR_SITE_STATE_INVALID`. The determination writes two fields, `eligibility_basis` and `eligibility_pct_used`, and it evaluates a fixed sequence of branches where the first branch whose condition holds decides the outcome. If the site's `site_type` is `CAMP`, the basis is `CAMP_INDIVIDUAL` and `eligibility_pct_used` is left null. Otherwise, if the `site_type` is `CLOSED_ENROLLED`, `eligibility_pct_used` takes the value of `enrolled_free_reduced_pct`, and the basis is `ENROLLED_DOCUMENTATION` when that percentage is at least 50.00 and `NOT_ELIGIBLE` when it is below 50.00. Otherwise — that is, for `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED_OPEN` and `MIGRANT` sites — the server reads the area eligibility record the site names, sets `eligibility_pct_used` to that record's `free_reduced_pct`, and then applies three sub-branches in order. If the record's `valid_through_date` falls strictly before the site's `operating_start_date`, the income data is stale and the basis is `NOT_ELIGIBLE` no matter how high the percentage. Otherwise, if the record's `data_source` is `SCHOOL_DATA`, the basis is `AREA_ELIGIBLE` when the percentage is at least 50.00 and `NOT_ELIGIBLE` otherwise. Otherwise the record came from `CENSUS_ACS`, which carries a documentation surcharge, and the basis is `AREA_ELIGIBLE` only when the percentage is at least 55.00.

Both thresholds are inclusive and both are compared at two decimal places, which makes the boundary cases worth stating outright: 50.00 from school data is eligible and 49.99 is not; 55.00 from the census survey is eligible and 54.99 is not. A percentage of 52.00 is eligible under school data and not eligible under census data, which is the entire point of running two thresholds. Re-running the determination is permitted while the site remains in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and each run recomputes both fields from whatever the underlying data now says. The determination takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and every run counts as a mutation of the site: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by 1 even when it lands on exactly the `eligibility_basis` and `eligibility_pct_used` the site already carried.

### 3.5. A Site's Two Independent Status Axes

A site carries two status dimensions on the same row, and they evolve independently. `approval_status` is the regulatory axis, moving among `PENDING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `SUSPENDED`; `operating_status` is the operational axis, moving among `NOT_STARTED`, `OPERATING` and `CLOSED_FOR_SEASON`. Suspending a site on the approval axis never touches its operating axis, and closing the season never touches its approval axis. `DENIED` is terminal on the approval axis and `CLOSED_FOR_SEASON` is terminal on the operating axis. Serving reimbursable meals requires a specific pair of values across both axes, so both must be read before any conclusion is drawn about whether a site may operate.

The approval axis is driven by the state agency through a single decision that carries a `decision` value and an optional `reason`, and it never touches `operating_status`. The accepted decisions are `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REINSTATED`. From `PENDING_REVIEW`, a decision of `APPROVED` moves the site to `APPROVED`; this requires that the site's `eligibility_basis` is not `UNDETERMINED`, reported as `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED` when the determination step was skipped, that the basis is not `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, reported as `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and that the owning sponsor is itself in `application_status` `APPROVED`, reported as `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED`. Only the three affirmative bases — `AREA_ELIGIBLE`, `ENROLLED_DOCUMENTATION` and `CAMP_INDIVIDUAL` — permit approval. Those three gates are not evaluated in the order just named: per §2.6 the sponsor gate is cross-entity readiness and runs first, so an unapproved sponsor reports `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED` even when the site's eligibility was never determined, and only then do `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED` and `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` come into play, in that order. From `PENDING_REVIEW`, a decision of `DENIED` requires a `reason`, stores it in `suspension_reason`, and moves the site to `DENIED`; unlike the denial of a suspended site described below, this one fires no cascade at all: it voids no service days whatever their status or date, leaving any day already planned at that site in `PLANNED`, and it leaves `claim_hold` false.

Suspension is available from `APPROVED`, requires a `reason`, and fires a three-part cascade. The `reason` is stored in `suspension_reason` and `claim_hold` is raised to true. Then every service day of the site whose `status` is `PLANNED` and whose `service_date` is on or after the current logical clock date moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal string `SITE_SUSPENDED`, along with every service day currently in `OPEN` regardless of its date, and `voided_day_count` grows by one for each day voided. Finally, every claim line item that references this site and whose parent claim is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_OPENED` has its `hold_flag` raised to true, and each of those claims has its `blocking_hold_count` recomputed as the number of its line items with `hold_flag` true. That last hop only re-versions what it actually changes: a line item already carrying the target `hold_flag` value keeps its `updated_at` and `version` untouched, and a claim whose recomputed `blocking_hold_count` equals the value it already held is likewise left alone. Line items under a claim in any other status, and claims with no line item flag change at all, are never touched. A site may also be suspended without any operator action at all, as a consequence of a `CRITICAL` corrective action being created for it; that path is described in §3.7 and it runs the identical cascade.

Reinstatement moves a `SUSPENDED` site back to `APPROVED`. A decision of `REINSTATED` requests it, and a decision of `APPROVED` recorded against a suspended site is treated as the same reinstatement request. Reinstatement is permitted only when no corrective action for that site has `severity` `CRITICAL` together with a `status` of `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `ESCALATED` — while any such action is live, the suspension stands, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SITE_STATE_INVALID`. On reinstatement `suspension_reason` returns to null, `claim_hold` returns to false, every claim line item referencing this site under a `DRAFT` or `REVISION_OPENED` claim has its `hold_flag` lowered to false, and each affected claim's `blocking_hold_count` is recomputed under the same only-what-changes rule stated for the suspension. As §2.7 warns, reinstatement is a partial compensation by design: it releases the line-item holds the suspension raised, but the service days the suspension voided stay `VOIDED` forever. A `SUSPENDED` site may instead be pushed to `DENIED` with a `reason`, which stores the text in `suspension_reason` and voids every service day of the site still in `PLANNED` or `OPEN` with `void_reason` set to `SITE_DENIED` — irrespective of `service_date`, as §3.1 notes of every denial — incrementing `voided_day_count` per voided day. The `claim_hold` the suspension raised stays true through that denial, and the denial neither lowers it nor makes a second pass over the line-item holds the suspension already raised.

The operating axis is driven by the sponsor through a `to_status` value of either `OPERATING` or `CLOSED_FOR_SEASON`. Moving from `NOT_STARTED` to `OPERATING` requires that the acting `SponsorAdmin` owns the site and that the site's `approval_status` is `APPROVED`: a site still in `PENDING_REVIEW` or already `DENIED` is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED`, and a site in `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED`. Moving from `OPERATING` to `CLOSED_FOR_SEASON` requires that no service day of the site is currently in `OPEN` — an unclosed day blocks the season close and is reported as `ERR_DAY_HAS_OPEN_SERVICE` — and it voids every remaining `PLANNED` day whose `service_date` is on or after the current logical clock date, with `void_reason` set to the literal string `SEASON_CLOSED` and `voided_day_count` incremented per voided day. Closing the season leaves `approval_status` untouched: a `CLOSED_FOR_SEASON` site is still `APPROVED`, and the days it already finalized remain fully claimable. Any move that the current value of the addressed dimension does not permit — a second attempt to start operating, or a season close from `NOT_STARTED` — is rejected with `ERR_SITE_STATE_INVALID`, and per §2.6 that same-entity state check precedes both approval-axis gates: a site already `OPERATING` reports `ERR_SITE_STATE_INVALID` even if it has since been suspended or denied. Both axes increment the site's `version` by 1 on every successful move.

One derived field on the site is worth isolating because it anchors the monitoring deadlines. `first_operating_date` is not written when the operating axis moves; it is written when service actually begins, and it holds the earliest `service_date` among the site's service days that have ever reached `OPEN`. Before the first day opens it is null, and while it is null the site has no monitoring obligation clock running at all.

### 3.6. The Day of Service: Planning, Opening, Counting, Closing

Each calendar date a site intends to serve is planned in advance as its own record. A `SiteSupervisor` plans one by naming the `site_id` it acts for, the `service_date`, and a non-empty `meal_services` array in which each element describes one planned service with a `meal_type`, a `planned_meals` figure for that service — an integer of at least 0, stored on the day exactly as given and never read by any later calculation, so it is an operational note rather than a constraint on what may be counted — and the window it will run in, given as `service_start` and `service_end` in `HH:MM` on a 24-hour clock with the start strictly before the end. Each element must itself be a JSON object, and only those four members are retained: any other member of an element is discarded rather than stored. No `meal_type` may appear twice in the array, and every `meal_type` named must be a member of the site's `approved_meal_types` — a service for a meal the site is not approved to serve is rejected with `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_NOT_APPROVED`. The `service_date` must fall inside the site's season, on or after `operating_start_date` and on or before `operating_end_date`; a date outside that inclusive range is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_OUTSIDE_SEASON`. A site may hold only one live record per date: the pair of `site_id` and `service_date` must be unique across all service days whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`, so a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_DAY` while a date whose earlier record was voided may be planned afresh. Planning consults neither of the site's status axes — only that the acting supervisor owns the site — so a day may be planned at a site still in `PENDING_REVIEW`, at a suspended one, or at one that has closed for the season; it is opening the day, not planning it, that demands an approved and operating site.

Service windows within one day are separated by a mandatory rest interval. Each window is treated as a half-open interval that includes its `service_start` and excludes its `service_end`, no two windows may overlap, and when the windows are put in ascending order of `service_start`, the gap between the end of one window and the start of the next must be at least 60 minutes. A gap of exactly 60 minutes is accepted; two windows that merely abut, with no gap at all, are rejected, as is any genuine overlap. Every one of these failures reports `ERR_MEAL_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A newly planned day opens in `status` `PLANNED` with `food_safety_status` `OK`, `production_counts` and `food_received_at` both null, `breach_at` and `void_reason` both null, every counted and derived meal counter at 0, and its own `production_mode` copied from the site's `production_mode` at that moment and frozen thereafter — later changes at the site level never reach a day already planned.

Opening the day is the supervisor's morning act and it carries two pieces of information: `production_counts`, a map from meal type to the number of meals prepared at a self-prep site or delivered at a vended one, and `food_received_at`, the instant the food became ready or arrived, which becomes the baseline for all holding-time arithmetic on that day. `production_counts` must be a JSON object and each of its values must be a non-negative integer. Its keys must be exactly the meal types declared in the day's `meal_services` — no extras and none missing — and a mismatched key set is a malformed request reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; because that comparison needs the day itself, it is made after the service day has been resolved, so a `day_id` naming nothing still reports `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND` first, but it is made *before* ownership and state are examined, so a mismatched key set outranks both `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_STATE_INVALID`. The stored map is rewritten in the day's declared `meal_services` order whatever order the request listed its keys in. The day must be in `PLANNED`, or the attempt reports `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_STATE_INVALID`. The site must have cleared review, so a site in `PENDING_REVIEW` or `DENIED` produces `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED` and a site in `SUSPENDED` produces `ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED`; the site's `operating_status` must be `OPERATING`, or the attempt reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_OPERATING`. Opening moves the day to `OPEN`, stores both supplied values, and runs the classification of the next subsection once immediately — with no count entries yet posted this leaves all three counted totals and `reimbursable_meals` at 0 while `leftover_meals` becomes the whole of the production just recorded, so an opened day already reports its entire production as leftover until counting draws it down. Opening then reaches back into two other places. The site's `first_operating_date` becomes the earlier of its existing value and this day's `service_date`, and the site row is re-versioned only when that comparison actually moves the date. And because that date may have just moved, every monitoring visit of the site whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` — `FOLLOW_UP` visits included, whose recomputation simply lands back on their own `scheduled_date` — has its `due_date` recomputed against the new value; this sweep runs on every open, not only on the one that changes the date, and a visit whose recomputed `due_date` equals the value it already held keeps its `updated_at` and `version` untouched. Nothing is written for `is_late`, which §3.7 derives at read time.

Counts are posted one entry at a time while the day is `OPEN`, each entry naming a `meal_type` and three non-negative integers: `first_meals` for first meals served to children, `second_meals` for second meals served to children, and `adult_meals` for meals served to adults, which are never reimbursable under any circumstances. Every entry records its parent day in `meal_service_day_id`, the posting instant in `recorded_at`, and the posting supervisor's identifier in `recorded_by_user_id`. Posting is refused unless the day's `status` is `OPEN`, reported as `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_STATE_INVALID`; unless the day's `food_safety_status` is still `OK`, reported as `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_BREACH`; and unless the `meal_type` is among the site's `approved_meal_types`, reported as `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_NOT_APPROVED`.

A miscount is corrected by reversal, never by editing, because count entries are immutable. Supplying `reversal_of_entry_id` sets the new entry's `entry_kind` to `REVERSAL` and its three counts are applied to the day as negatives; leaving the field out sets `entry_kind` to `ORIGINAL`. A named target that does not exist reports `ERR_COUNT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. A target that exists but is unsuitable reports `ERR_REVERSAL_TARGET_INVALID`, and it is unsuitable when it sits on a different service day, when its own `entry_kind` is not `ORIGINAL`, when its `meal_type` differs from the reversing entry's, when another reversal already points at it, or when the reversing entry's `first_meals`, `second_meals` and `adult_meals` do not exactly equal the target's.

**How a meal becomes reimbursable.** The day's classification runs per meal type, taking the meal types in the order the `meal_services` array declares them and then, after those, any further meal type that count entries actually name — the gate on posting is the site's `approved_meal_types`, not the day's declared services, so a type the day never planned may still be counted — taken in ascending alphabetical order of the meal-type value. Such an undeclared type is classified by exactly the same four steps as any other, which on a `SELF_PREP` day means its available production reads as 0 and its reimbursable meals collapse to 0, while its counted meals still land in the day's counted totals. Classification begins by netting the counts: for each meal type, the net first meals are the sum of `first_meals` across that type's `ORIGINAL` entries less the sum across its `REVERSAL` entries, and the net second and net adult meals are computed the same way from `second_meals` and `adult_meals`. Those three nets, summed across meal types, are what the day publishes as `counted_first_meals`, `counted_second_meals` and `counted_adult_meals`.

Four steps then decide the reimbursable share for each meal type. First, the attendance cap: reimbursable first meals for that type are the lesser of the net first meals and the site's `approved_max_attendance`. Second, the second-meal rule: at a site whose `site_type` is `CAMP`, reimbursable second meals for that type are the lesser of the net second meals and 2 percent of the reimbursable first meals just computed, with that allowance floored to a whole meal; at every other site type, reimbursable second meals are zero, because second meals are never reimbursable off camp. Because the allowance is floored, a camp with 49 reimbursable first meals of a type earns an allowance of 0 second meals, and one with 50 earns exactly 1. Third, the production waterfall, which applies **only** on a day whose `production_mode` is `SELF_PREP`: the available production for that meal type is read from `production_counts`, reimbursable first meals are further reduced to no more than that availability, and reimbursable second meals are then reduced to no more than whatever availability survives after the first meals have taken their fill — first meals fill the bucket first, and if they exhaust it the second-meal allowance collapses to zero. A day whose `production_mode` is `VENDED` applies no production cap at this stage at all; the delivered-quantity reconciliation for vended sites happens later, during the state agency's edit check. Fourth, adult meals are set aside as never reimbursable.

The day's `reimbursable_meals` is the sum, across all meal types, of the reimbursable first meals plus the reimbursable second meals. Its `non_reimbursable_meals` is the conserved complement: the day's `counted_first_meals` plus `counted_second_meals` plus `counted_adult_meals`, less `reimbursable_meals`. And its `leftover_meals` is the total of all values in `production_counts`, less `counted_first_meals`, less `counted_second_meals`, less `counted_adult_meals`, less `discarded_meals`, with any negative result floored at 0.

Each individual entry also records its own contribution, and that contribution is a **marginal delta** rather than an independent computation: the day's totals are computed immediately before the entry is inserted and again immediately after, and the entry stores the differences as `reimbursable_first_meals` and `reimbursable_second_meals`. A reversal entry therefore carries zero or negative values in both. The entry's own `non_reimbursable_meals` is its `first_meals` plus `second_meals` plus `adult_meals`, less its `reimbursable_first_meals` and less its `reimbursable_second_meals`.

Closing the day is the supervisor's final act on it, moving `status` from `OPEN` to `CLOSED` and re-running the whole classification over every count entry the day accumulated, so that the three counted totals, `reimbursable_meals`, `non_reimbursable_meals` and `leftover_meals` are all rewritten from the complete set. Closing takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and it consults neither of the site's status axes nor the day's `food_safety_status`: a day that breached may still be closed, it simply cannot take further counts. Entries posted on a day that is later voided are retained as records but never contribute a meal to any claim.

**Food safety at open-air sites.** While the day is `OPEN`, the supervisor logs temperature observations, each naming the `meal_type` the reading applies to, a `temperature_class` of either `COLD` or `HOT` that selects the safe band, a `temperature_f` in degrees Fahrenheit between -20 and 250 inclusive with at most one decimal place, and the `measured_at` instant of the reading as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp. The reading's `meal_type` must be among the site's `approved_meal_types`, or the log is rejected with `ERR_MEAL_TYPE_NOT_APPROVED`, and `measured_at` must be at or after the day's `food_received_at`, with an earlier instant rejected as `ERR_MEASURED_BEFORE_FOOD_RECEIVED`. Each reading derives `holding_minutes` as the whole minutes elapsed from the day's `food_received_at` to `measured_at`, truncated toward zero. Like count entries, holding-log entries are immutable and record their parent day in `meal_service_day_id` and their author in `recorded_by_user_id`.

Whether a reading breaches is decided in a fixed order. If the site's `is_open_air` is false, the reading never breaches: `breach_flag` is false and `breach_reason` is null, because climate-controlled holding is exempt from this enforcement entirely. Otherwise the temperature band is tested first — a `COLD` item whose `temperature_f` is above 41.0, or a `HOT` item whose `temperature_f` is below 135.0, sets `breach_flag` true with `breach_reason` `TEMP_OUT_OF_BAND`. Otherwise the holding clock is tested — `holding_minutes` above 240 sets `breach_flag` true with `breach_reason` `HOLDING_TIME_EXCEEDED`. Otherwise `breach_flag` is false and `breach_reason` is null. All three comparisons are strict, so a cold reading of exactly 41.0 is compliant and 41.1 breaches, a hot reading of exactly 135.0 is compliant and 134.9 breaches, and exactly 240 holding minutes is compliant while 241 breaches. Note that the temperature test wins when both would fire: a reading that is both out of band and past four hours records `TEMP_OUT_OF_BAND`.

Only the **first** breaching reading of a day changes the day. When a breaching reading arrives while the day's `food_safety_status` is still `OK`, that status flips to `BREACH`, the day's `breach_at` is set to the reading's `measured_at`, the day's `discarded_meals` becomes the unserved production at that moment — the total of all values in `production_counts`, less `counted_first_meals`, less `counted_second_meals`, less `counted_adult_meals`, floored at 0 — and the day's `leftover_meals` is forced to 0, since food removed from service is discarded rather than left over. Once `food_safety_status` reads `BREACH` it never re-arms: subsequent breaching readings are stored with their own `breach_flag` and `breach_reason` but change nothing on the day, and, as noted above, no further meal counts may be posted at all.

**Finalizing and voiding.** A `CLOSED` day becomes claimable only when the sponsor signs it off, moving `status` to `FINALIZED`. Only a `SponsorAdmin` owning the site may do so, only from `CLOSED`, and only while the site's `claim_hold` is false — a day belonging to a site under hold cannot be finalized, and the attempt is reported as `ERR_SITE_SUSPENDED`. Finalizing takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and it changes nothing on the day beyond its `status`, leaving every counter exactly as the close computed it. A day may instead be voided by the sponsor from `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, supplying a `reason` that is stored as `void_reason`; voiding sets `status` to `VOIDED`, forces `reimbursable_meals` to 0, rewrites `non_reimbursable_meals` to the day's whole counted total — `counted_first_meals` plus `counted_second_meals` plus `counted_adult_meals`, since none of those meals can be reimbursed now — and increments the site's `voided_day_count` by 1, which re-versions the site row as well as the day's. The three counted totals themselves, `discarded_meals` and `leftover_meals` are all left standing at the values they held, so a voided day remains a faithful record of what was served. Voiding an `OPEN` day additionally removes that day's counts from every claim aggregation. `FINALIZED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal. Any operation attempted from a status that does not permit it — finalizing a `PLANNED` day, voiding a `FINALIZED` one, closing a day that was never opened — is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.7. Monitoring Visits, Findings and Corrective Actions

Oversight of an operating site is carried out by field monitors, each visit scheduled in advance by a `FieldMonitor` who supplies the `site_id` to be visited, the `monitor_user_id` who will carry it out, a `visit_type` of `FIRST_WEEK`, `FULL_REVIEW` or `FOLLOW_UP`, the `scheduled_date`, and the `HH:MM` bracket `window_start` and `window_end` the monitor will be on site. Scheduling resolves the site but consults neither of its status axes, so a visit may be booked at a site still in `PENDING_REVIEW`, at a suspended one, or at one that has closed for the season; and the acting monitor need not be the `monitor_user_id` named — that identifier is stored as supplied and is never checked against any registry — so one monitor may legitimately schedule work for another. A new visit begins in `status` `SCHEDULED` with both `findings` and `highest_severity` null, and with `is_late` false unless the site's `first_operating_date` already places the visit's `due_date` before the current logical clock date, in which case the read-time derivation described below reports `is_late` true from the moment of creation. Neither of those two fields is written before the report is filed, so a visit sitting in `SCHEDULED`, `CONDUCTED` or `CANCELLED` still carries null in both; `highest_severity` therefore takes a value from its enum only once a report has been filed.

Two scheduling constraints protect a monitor's day. A monitor's windows on one `scheduled_date` are treated as **closed** intervals that include both `window_start` and `window_end`, with no buffer between them: two windows collide when they share any instant at all, so a window beginning at exactly the moment another one ends is rejected, and the collision is reported as `ERR_MONITOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Note the deliberate contrast with meal service windows on a service day, which are half-open and additionally demand a 60-minute gap — a monitor's back-to-back windows fail for touching, whereas meal services fail unless they are a full hour apart. Separately, a monitor may hold at most 3 visits with a `status` other than `CANCELLED` on any single `scheduled_date`; a fourth is rejected with `ERR_MONITOR_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Cancelled visits are excluded from both tests, so cancelling a visit frees both its window and its slot against the daily cap.

Every visit carries a deadline derived from the site's `first_operating_date`, and that derivation is the reason opening a service day reaches back into the monitoring records. When the site's `first_operating_date` is null the visit's `due_date` is null and its `is_late` is false, because a site that has never served a meal owes no monitoring yet. Otherwise, a `FIRST_WEEK` visit is due 4 days after `first_operating_date`, a `FULL_REVIEW` visit is due 28 days after `first_operating_date`, and a `FOLLOW_UP` visit is simply due on its own `scheduled_date`. Lateness is then read one of two ways depending on where the visit stands: a visit whose `status` is `CONDUCTED`, `REPORT_FILED` or `CLOSED` is late when the UTC calendar date of its `conducted_at` falls after its `due_date`, while a visit still `SCHEDULED` is late when the logical clock's current date has already passed its `due_date`. Both comparisons are inclusive of the deadline itself, so a visit conducted on the `due_date` is on time and only the following day makes it late. `CANCELLED` visits are excluded from the due-date recomputation entirely, and a `CANCELLED` visit always reads `is_late` false whatever its `due_date` says. `is_late` is derived at read time from the visit's `status`, `conducted_at`, `due_date` and the logical clock rather than stored, so a `SCHEDULED` visit's flag turns true of its own accord once the clock passes its `due_date`, with no write to the visit.

Conducting the visit is the monitor's own act — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the visit's `monitor_user_id`, and any other monitor attempting it is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The visit must still be `SCHEDULED`, or the attempt reports `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`, and the logical clock's current date must have reached the `scheduled_date`; an attempt to conduct a visit before its scheduled day arrives is rejected with `ERR_VISIT_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, while conducting it on that exact date is permitted. Conducting takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and it moves `status` to `CONDUCTED`, stamps `conducted_at` with the logical clock's current instant, and recomputes `is_late`.

**The report fork.** Filing the report is where the visit's path splits, and the branch is chosen entirely by the `findings` array the monitor submits, each element of which must be a JSON object pairing a `finding_code` with a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`. Only those two members of an element are retained — anything else it carries is discarded rather than stored — the array is stored in exactly the order submitted, and two elements may repeat the same `finding_code` or the same `severity` without objection. On the **no-deficiency branch**, `findings` arrives empty: the visit stores that empty array, sets `highest_severity` to `NONE`, moves straight from `CONDUCTED` to `CLOSED`, and creates no corrective action whatsoever. On the **deficiency branch**, `findings` arrives non-empty: the visit stores the array, sets `highest_severity` to the greatest severity present under the ordering `MINOR` below `MAJOR` below `CRITICAL`, moves from `CONDUCTED` to `REPORT_FILED`, and opens **exactly one** corrective action — not one per finding. That single action takes `origin` `VISIT_FINDING`, `status` `OPEN`, `site_id` copied from the visit, `monitoring_visit_id` set to the visit, `severity` equal to the visit's `highest_severity`, `finding_code` equal to the `finding_code` of the highest-severity finding with ties broken by choosing the alphabetically first `finding_code`, and `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting monitor, who is necessarily the visit's `monitor_user_id`. Its `due_date` is set by the severity ladder described below. When that action's `severity` is `CRITICAL` **and** the site's `approval_status` is `APPROVED` at that instant, its creation immediately fires the site suspension cascade of §3.5 in full: the site moves from `APPROVED` to `SUSPENDED`, `suspension_reason` is written as the literal string `CRITICAL_FINDING:` immediately followed by the action's own `finding_code` with no space between them, `claim_hold` is raised, the site's planned and open service days are voided, and every draft line item of that site is flagged. A `CRITICAL` action created against a site whose `approval_status` is anything other than `APPROVED` — already `SUSPENDED`, still `PENDING_REVIEW`, or `DENIED` — is created exactly as described and changes nothing at all on the site: no cascade runs and no `claim_hold` is raised on that path. A visit still `SCHEDULED` may instead be cancelled by its own monitor with a `reason` stored as `cancel_reason`, which moves `status` to `CANCELLED`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

**The corrective action ladder.** An action's `due_date` is set from the logical clock's current date at the moment the action is created, and the interval depends on severity: 5 days for `CRITICAL`, 15 days for `MAJOR`, and 30 days for `MINOR`. Every action also carries a `rejection_count` that starts at 0, an `origin` recording whether it came from a visit finding or from the claim-side compliance trip described in §3.8, and an optional `follow_up_of_corrective_action_id` linking it back to the action whose rejection spawned it.

The sponsor responds by submitting a `response_note` and, optionally, an `evidence_ref` pointing at supporting documentation. A response is accepted from `OPEN` or from `REJECTED` — the second case is the rejection loop, and it simply overwrites the previously stored `response_note` and `evidence_ref` — and either way the action moves to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. Only the `SponsorAdmin` who owns the site behind the action's `site_id` may respond, so a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and an action in any other status reports `ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`.

A monitor then reviews the submitted response with a `decision` of either `CLOSED` or `REJECTED`, and the reviewer may not be the person who opened the action: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the action's `opened_by_user_id` the review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, which is the enforcement point for the principle stated in §2.10. Accepting the response moves the action to `CLOSED` and stamps `closed_by_user_id` with the reviewing monitor, and two things may follow from that closure. If the action just closed was the last one at `severity` `CRITICAL` for its site still sitting in an unresolved status — that is, in `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `ESCALATED` — then the site's `approval_status` returns from `SUSPENDED` to `APPROVED`, which clears `claim_hold` and lifts the `hold_flag` on that site's draft line items with each affected claim's `blocking_hold_count` recomputed. And if every action sharing the same `monitoring_visit_id` is now `CLOSED`, the originating visit bubbles up on its own from `REPORT_FILED` to `CLOSED`.

Rejecting the response requires a `reason` in the request — which is validated for presence and then never stored, on neither the rejected action nor the follow-up it spawns — and does two things at once, which is what makes the rejection cycle observable both as a loop and as a chain. The reviewed action moves to `REJECTED` and its `rejection_count` increases by 1; simultaneously, exactly one follow-up action is created with `follow_up_of_corrective_action_id` pointing at the rejected action, with `site_id`, `monitoring_visit_id` and `finding_code` all copied across, `origin` `VISIT_FINDING`, `status` `OPEN`, `opened_by_user_id` set to the reviewing monitor, a fresh `due_date` from the severity ladder, and a `severity` one level above the rejected action's — `MINOR` becomes `MAJOR`, `MAJOR` becomes `CRITICAL`, and `CRITICAL` saturates at `CRITICAL` rather than overflowing. Because the ladder can reach `CRITICAL`, a rejection is itself capable of suspending the site: if the follow-up action is created at `CRITICAL`, the suspension cascade fires from that creation exactly as it does for a critical visit finding. Note that the reviewer becomes the follow-up's `opened_by_user_id`, so the same monitor who rejected a response cannot review the follow-up they created.

Escalation is the state agency's remedy for an action that has gone nowhere. A `StateAgencyReviewer` escalates with a `reason`, and the timing gate depends on which status the action is in. From `OPEN`, the logical clock's current date must be strictly after the action's `due_date` — on the `due_date` itself escalation is premature. From `REJECTED`, either that same overdue condition holds **or** the `rejection_count` has reached 2, so a twice-rejected action may be escalated even while it is still within its deadline. Failing both tests is reported as `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE`, and escalating from any other status reports `ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`. The `reason` is required for presence and then never stored anywhere. Escalation moves the action to `ESCALATED` and then takes one of two branches on the site. When the site's `approval_status` is `APPROVED` it moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_ESCALATED`, firing the full suspension cascade, which is what raises `claim_hold` and voids days on that path. When the `approval_status` is anything else the site is not moved and no day is voided; instead `claim_hold` alone is raised, and raising it propagates exactly as the suspension cascade's last hop does — every claim line item referencing the site under a `DRAFT` or `REVISION_OPENED` claim has `hold_flag` set true and each affected claim's `blocking_hold_count` is recomputed, both under the only-what-changes re-versioning rule of §3.5. If the site's `claim_hold` was already true, that branch writes nothing whatsoever: neither the site nor any line item is re-versioned, and only the action itself changes. `ESCALATED` is terminal alongside `CLOSED`, and because an escalated action can never reach `CLOSED`, the automatic reinstatement above can never fire for it: a site suspended by escalation returns to `APPROVED` only through the state agency's explicit reinstatement decision described in §3.5.

### 3.8. The Monthly Claim: Line Items, Edit Checks, Reimbursement and Recovery

Reimbursement is claimed a month at a time. A `SponsorAdmin` opens one by naming the `sponsor_id`, the `claim_month` as a four-digit year and two-digit month, and the `expected_sponsor_version` guard of §2.8. Unlike an advance's `request_month`, a `claim_month` is **not** restricted to the three summer operating months: any month from `01` through `12` is accepted, and only the `YYYY-MM` shape itself is enforced. The sponsor's `application_status` must be `APPROVED`, or the create is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_APPROVED`, and a sponsor may hold only one claim per month, so a repeat of the same `sponsor_id` and `claim_month` pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_MONTH`. A new claim opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, and its `deadline_date` is fixed at creation as the last calendar day of the second month following `claim_month` — a claim for June 2026 is therefore due by 31 August 2026 — which is the 60-day filing window the program allows. It also opens with `meals_rate_value_cents`, `disallowed_meal_count` and `blocking_hold_count` all at 0, with `is_late` false, and with `claimed_operating_cost_cents`, `claimed_admin_cost_cents`, `preliminary_reimbursement_cents`, `final_reimbursement_cents`, `advance_offset_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `edit_check_failure_codes`, `submitted_at` and `paid_at` all null. The create advances the sponsor's `version` by 1. `MealClaim` has no terminal state at all, because even a paid claim can be reopened.

**Building the line items.** One line item is added per participating site, and the sponsor supplies only the `site_id`; everything else on the line is derived. The claim must be in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_OPENED`, or the addition reports `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`; the site must belong to the claim's own sponsor, so a `site_id` whose `sponsor_id` differs from the claim's is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SPONSOR_MISMATCH`; the pair of `meal_claim_id` and `site_id` must be unique, so adding the same site twice reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_LINE_ITEM`; and the site must have at least one service day in `status` `FINALIZED` whose `service_date` falls inside `claim_month`, with an empty month rejected as `ERR_NO_FINALIZED_DAYS`. The site's own `approval_status` is never consulted here: a suspended or even denied site may be added as a line item, because the hold machinery below — not the approval axis — is what stops such a claim from being submitted. The line's `rate_class` is `RURAL_OR_SELF_PREP` when the site's `is_rural` is true **or** its `production_mode` is `SELF_PREP`, and `OTHER` otherwise — either condition alone is enough to earn the higher rate. Its `finalized_day_count` counts the site's finalized days inside `claim_month`. Its `claimed_first_meals_by_type` maps each meal type to the sum, over those finalized days taken in ascending `service_date`, of the reimbursable first meals the classification of §3.6 produced for that meal type, and `claimed_second_meals_by_type` does the same for reimbursable second meals. The two maps always carry the identical key set: one key for every meal type any of those days classified, in ascending key order, **including** a type whose reimbursable total came out 0, which is therefore present with the value 0 rather than omitted. Days in `VOIDED`, `CLOSED`, `OPEN` or `PLANNED` never contribute a meal, which is why finalization is the gate that matters. The new line starts with `edit_check_status` `PENDING`, `disallow_reason` `NONE`, `disallowed_counts_by_type` null, and `hold_flag` copied from the site's current `claim_hold`; adding it recomputes the claim's `meals_rate_value_cents` and its `blocking_hold_count`.

**Meals times rates.** The money a line's meals are worth is computed per meal type, taking the meal types of `claimed_first_meals_by_type` in ascending key order. For each type, the net claimable meals are that type's claimed first meals plus its claimed second meals, less the disallowed count recorded for that type — which is zero before any edit check has run — and the per-type value in cents is that net multiplied by the applicable rate and rounded to a whole number of cents with half-up rounding. The rounding order is pinned: each pair of line item and meal type is rounded to whole cents **first**, and only the rounded per-type values are summed into the line's `meals_rate_value_cents`, which are themselves summed across the claim's lines into the claim's `meals_rate_value_cents`. Summing meal counts across line items or across meal types before rounding is incorrect and produces a different total.

The rates are fractional cents carried to exactly two decimal places. At the `RURAL_OR_SELF_PREP` rate class, a breakfast is worth 268.75 cents, a morning snack 126.25 cents, a lunch 470.50 cents, an afternoon snack 126.25 cents, and a supper 470.50 cents. At the `OTHER` rate class, a breakfast is worth 263.25 cents, a morning snack 123.00 cents, a lunch 462.75 cents, an afternoon snack 123.00 cents, and a supper 462.75 cents. Lunch and supper share a rate within each class, as do the two snacks.

**Submission.** The sponsor submits the claim with its asserted `claimed_operating_cost_cents` and `claimed_admin_cost_cents`. Submission is accepted from `DRAFT` and from `REVISION_OPENED` — the second being the post-payment revision loop — and from any other status reports `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The claim must carry at least one line item, or the attempt reports `ERR_CLAIM_HAS_NO_LINE_ITEMS`. Its `blocking_hold_count` must be 0, meaning no line item's site sits on claim hold; a claim with any held site is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_HOLD_BLOCKING`. Those two checks are evaluated in the order this paragraph narrates them and, uniquely on this endpoint, *ahead* of the budget-readiness check that follows — an inversion of the §2.6 group ordering that is deliberate and normative here, because a claim with nothing in it is not worth pricing: a sponsor with no line items and no approved budget reports `ERR_CLAIM_HAS_NO_LINE_ITEMS`, and one whose only line item is held reports `ERR_CLAIM_HOLD_BLOCKING`, in both cases rather than `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`. The sponsor's budget for the claim's program year must then be in `status` `APPROVED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, or the attempt reports `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`; the budget consulted is picked by the rule of §3.3, except that here a candidate whose `program_year` equals the four-digit year of `claim_month` is preferred first, a candidate matching the sponsor's own `program_year` second, and the earliest-created candidate last — note that a budget under amendment still supports claiming, so the sponsor is not locked out while an amendment is pending. Finally the asserted costs are tested against the approved ceilings: `claimed_operating_cost_cents` above `approved_operating_cost_cents` is rejected with `ERR_OPERATING_COST_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, and `claimed_admin_cost_cents` above `approved_admin_cost_cents` with `ERR_ADMIN_COST_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, each ceiling permitting equality.

Submitting moves `status` to `SUBMITTED`, writes both cost figures, stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock's current instant, and sets `is_late` true when the UTC date of `submitted_at` falls after `deadline_date` — submitting on the `deadline_date` itself is on time. Every line item is then re-aggregated from its site's finalized days in `claim_month` and the meals-times-rates computation is re-run, so a day finalized between two submissions is picked up. A line is re-versioned only when that re-aggregation actually moves something — its `finalized_day_count`, either claimed-meals map, or its `meals_rate_value_cents` — so a line whose figures come out identical keeps its `updated_at` and `version` untouched by the submission. Re-aggregation rewrites only those four fields: a line's `rate_class`, `hold_flag`, `edit_check_status`, `disallow_reason` and disallowance figures are left exactly as they stood. The claim's `preliminary_reimbursement_cents` is then written from the **preliminary** reimbursement variant, which is deliberately simple: it is the claim's `meals_rate_value_cents` plus its `claimed_admin_cost_cents`, with no disallowances subtracted and **no caps of any kind applied**. It represents the sponsor's asserted entitlement, computable without the state agency's cost review. Submitting from `REVISION_OPENED` behaves identically except that it also increases `revision_round` by 1.

**The edit-check fork.** A `StateAgencyReviewer` runs the edit checks against a `SUBMITTED` claim, and the run examines the line items in ascending `created_at`. Each line receives **at most one** `disallow_reason`, chosen by the first of three rules that fires, and the disallowed counts are the ones that rule computes.

The first rule is `MISSING_MONITORING`, and it disallows the whole line. It fires when the site's first-week monitoring obligation is not satisfied, meaning there exists no monitoring visit for that site with `visit_type` `FIRST_WEEK` whose `status` is `CONDUCTED`, `REPORT_FILED` or `CLOSED` and whose `conducted_at` UTC date falls on or before its own `due_date`; a late-conducted or `CANCELLED` first-week visit does not satisfy the obligation. When it fires, the disallowed count for every meal type is that type's claimed first meals plus its claimed second meals — the entire line's meals come out.

The second rule is `FOOD_SAFETY_BREACH`, tested only when the first did not fire. It applies when any of the site's finalized days inside `claim_month` carries `food_safety_status` `BREACH`, and the disallowed count for each meal type is the sum, across only those breaching days, of the reimbursable meals of that type. The map it produces is keyed in ascending order by every meal type those breaching days classified, so a type that those days served no reimbursable meal of is present carrying 0 rather than omitted. Days that never breached keep their meals.

The third rule is `EXCEEDS_PRODUCTION`, tested only when neither earlier rule fired, and it applies **only** to sites whose `production_mode` is `VENDED`. This is where the delivered-quantity reconciliation deferred from §3.6 finally happens: for each finalized day and each meal type declared in that day's `meal_services`, the excess is the reimbursable first plus second meals of that type on that day, less the day's `production_counts` value for that type, with negatives floored at 0; those excesses accumulate into the disallowed count per meal type and into a running total. Unlike the two rules above, this map is sparse: a meal type is given a key, in ascending key order, only when it contributed a strictly positive excess. The reason is applied only when the running total is strictly greater than 0 — a vended site whose claims never exceed a delivery is left at `NONE`. Self-prep sites never reach this rule, because their production cap was already applied at classification time.

When no rule fires, the line's `disallow_reason` stays `NONE`. Each line's `edit_check_status` becomes `FAILED` when its reason is anything other than `NONE` and `PASSED` otherwise, its `disallowed_counts_by_type` holds the map the firing rule produced or an empty map when the reason is `NONE`, and its `disallowed_meal_count` is the total of that map's values.

The claim then takes one of two mutually exclusive branches. On the **clean branch**, where no line failed, the claim moves from `SUBMITTED` to `VALIDATED`: every line item is set to `edit_check_status` `PASSED` with `disallow_reason` `NONE` and an empty `disallowed_counts_by_type`, the claim's `edit_check_failure_codes` is set to an empty array, and the final reimbursement variant runs to write `final_reimbursement_cents`. On the **disallowance branch**, where at least one line failed, the claim moves from `SUBMITTED` to `EDIT_CHECK_FAILED`: each failing line records its `edit_check_status`, `disallow_reason`, `disallowed_counts_by_type` and `disallowed_meal_count` while the passing lines are set to `PASSED`, the claim's `edit_check_failure_codes` is set to the distinct failing reasons in sorted order, the claim's `disallowed_meal_count` is recomputed, and the final reimbursement variant runs on the surviving meals to write `final_reimbursement_cents`. Both branches compute a final figure; what differs is the status, the failure codes, and the compliance consequences described next.

The disallowance branch fires a two-hop cascade on the sites behind the failed lines. First, for every line whose `disallow_reason` became `EXCEEDS_PRODUCTION` or `MISSING_MONITORING`, that line's site has `compliance_flag_count` increased by 1 — a `FOOD_SAFETY_BREACH` disallowance costs the claim its meals but does not add a compliance flag. Second, a site whose `compliance_flag_count` reaches the threshold of 2 has one corrective action opened against it with `origin` `COMPLIANCE_FLAG`, `severity` `MAJOR`, `status` `OPEN`, `monitoring_visit_id` null, `finding_code` `COMPLIANCE_FLAG_THRESHOLD`, `opened_by_user_id` the literal value `SYSTEM`, and a `due_date` 15 days out per the severity ladder; the same site then has `claim_hold` raised, which propagates exactly as it does on the escalation path of §3.7 — every line item of that site under a `DRAFT` or `REVISION_OPENED` claim has `hold_flag` set true and each affected claim's `blocking_hold_count` is recomputed, and nothing at all is written when the hold was already up. "Reaches the threshold" means exactly that: the trip fires only on the flag that brings `compliance_flag_count` to precisely 2, so a site already carrying 2 or more flags accumulates a third and a fourth without opening any further action and without a fresh hold. Because the opener is `SYSTEM` and no real monitor can share that identifier, any field monitor may review the response to such an action. Compliance flags accumulate permanently and are never decremented, so a site that trips one flag in June and another in July trips the threshold in July even though the June claim was later revised.

**The final reimbursement and its administrative cap.** The final variant applies the caps the preliminary variant skips, and it does so in a pinned order. Step one takes the claim's `meals_rate_value_cents`, recomputed with the disallowed counts subtracted. Step two produces the operating reimbursement as the lesser of that meals value and the claim's `claimed_operating_cost_cents`. Step three produces `admin_cap_cents` as 20 percent of **step two's output**, floored to whole cents. Step four produces the administrative reimbursement as the lesser of `claimed_admin_cost_cents` and that cap. Step five sums steps two and four into the gross figure that becomes `final_reimbursement_cents`.

The evaluation order in step three is the important part, because the administrative cap only looks circular. The cap is keyed to the operating reimbursement, not to the total reimbursement; the reading in which the cap is 20 percent of the combined total — which would require solving for the administrative amount that satisfies its own ceiling — is explicitly **not** the rule, and no fixed-point iteration is ever needed. Because the floor is used rather than rounding, the cap can never exceed 20 percent of the operating reimbursement by even one cent. For identical inputs the final figure is always less than or equal to the preliminary figure, and it is strictly smaller whenever `claimed_admin_cost_cents` exceeds the floored cap or any meal has been disallowed.

**Payment and the advance recovery waterfall.** A `VALIDATED` claim whose `final_reimbursement_cents` is not null may be paid by a `StateAgencyReviewer`, and payment is where the sponsor's outstanding advances are recovered. Payment takes no request body at all — a body sent with it is ignored outright rather than parsed or validated — and both of its preconditions, the `VALIDATED` status and the non-null final figure, report the same `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The candidate advances are that sponsor's advances whose `status` is `DISBURSED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DEMAND_ISSUED` and whose `outstanding_cents` is strictly positive; advances in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `RECOVERED` are excluded. Those candidates are ordered by `request_month` ascending and then, within a month, by `created_at` ascending — never by identifier — so the oldest debt is always recovered first. The remaining balance starts at the claim's `final_reimbursement_cents`, and each candidate in turn absorbs the lesser of the remaining balance and its own `outstanding_cents`: that amount is added to the advance's `recovered_amount_cents`, subtracted from the remaining balance, and accumulated into the claim's `advance_offset_cents`. Allocation stops as soon as the remaining balance reaches zero, leaving later advances untouched. The claim's `net_payable_cents` is its `final_reimbursement_cents` less `advance_offset_cents`. When there are no candidates at all, the offset is 0 and the net payable equals the final reimbursement exactly. Every figure is integer cents, so no rounding is possible anywhere in the waterfall.

Payment moves `status` to `PAID`, stamps `paid_at` with the logical clock's current instant, updates each touched advance's `recovered_amount_cents`, `outstanding_cents` and `status` as set out immediately below, adds `advance_offset_cents` to the sponsor's `advance_recovered_cents`, subtracts the same amount from its `advance_outstanding_cents`, adds `net_payable_cents` to its `reimbursement_paid_cents`, and advances the sponsor's `version` by 1. The ledger invariant to hold throughout is that the sponsor's `advance_outstanding_cents` equals the sum, over advances in `DISBURSED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DEMAND_ISSUED`, of each advance's `approved_amount_cents` less its `recovered_amount_cents`.

Each advance the waterfall touched then settles into a new status determined solely by how much of its balance the claim absorbed. An advance in `DISBURSED` from which a positive amount smaller than its `outstanding_cents` was recovered moves to `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, with `recovered_amount_cents` increased by the amount taken and `outstanding_cents` recomputed. An advance in `DISBURSED` from which the full `outstanding_cents` was recovered moves directly to `RECOVERED`, with `recovered_amount_cents` set equal to `approved_amount_cents` and `outstanding_cents` set to 0. An advance already in `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` whose remaining `outstanding_cents` was fully absorbed likewise moves to `RECOVERED` on the same terms, and an advance in `DEMAND_ISSUED` whose remaining `outstanding_cents` was fully absorbed also moves to `RECOVERED` on the same terms — a demand that is satisfied in full retires the advance rather than leaving it under demand. An advance in `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DEMAND_ISSUED` that is only partly absorbed keeps its existing status and simply carries a larger `recovered_amount_cents` and a smaller `outstanding_cents`. `RECOVERED` is terminal for as long as the payment that produced it stands.

**Reopening a claim.** A `StateAgencyReviewer` may reopen a claim with a `reason`, and the destination depends on where the claim stood. That `reason` is optional when the claim is reopened from `EDIT_CHECK_FAILED` and mandatory when it is reopened from `PAID`, where omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; since the requirement turns on the claim's status it is tested only after the status check, so an attempt from any other status reports `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` even when no `reason` was supplied. Supplied or not, the `reason` is never stored. Reopening from `EDIT_CHECK_FAILED` returns the claim to `DRAFT`: `revision_round` increases by 1, `submitted_at`, `preliminary_reimbursement_cents`, `final_reimbursement_cents` and `edit_check_failure_codes` are all set to null, and every line item returns to `edit_check_status` `PENDING` with `disallow_reason` `NONE`, `disallowed_counts_by_type` cleared and `disallowed_meal_count` back to 0 — every line is re-versioned by that reset whether or not its own figures moved, its `meals_rate_value_cents` is recomputed with no disallowances subtracted, and the claim's `meals_rate_value_cents` and `disallowed_meal_count` follow from those lines, the latter returning to 0. Reopening from `PAID` moves the claim to `REVISION_OPENED`: `revision_round` increases by 1 and every line item resets in exactly the same way, and in addition the payment itself is undone. Every advance this claim recovered from has `recovered_amount_cents` decreased by precisely the amount this claim took from it, applied in the reverse of the allocation order, and each such advance's `status` is recomputed — `DEMAND_ISSUED` when its `demand_issued_at` is not null and its `outstanding_cents` is positive, otherwise `RECOVERED` when `outstanding_cents` has reached 0, otherwise `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` when `recovered_amount_cents` is still positive, otherwise `DISBURSED`. The sponsor's `advance_recovered_cents` is reduced by `advance_offset_cents`, its `advance_outstanding_cents` restored by the same amount, its `reimbursement_paid_cents` reduced by `net_payable_cents`, and its `version` advanced by 1. Only then are the claim's own `advance_offset_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `paid_at` and `final_reimbursement_cents` set to null; `submitted_at` and `preliminary_reimbursement_cents` are deliberately left standing on this branch, unlike on the failed-claim branch. Because the paid branch writes the claim row twice — once for the status and `revision_round` change and once again when those four payment fields are cleared — reopening a paid claim advances the claim's `version` by 2, where reopening a failed one advances it by 1. This is a true compensating reversal: the advance-ledger invariant must read identically before the payment and after the revision, and the site-level `compliance_flag_count` values are pointedly **not** rolled back. Attempting to reopen a claim from any status other than `EDIT_CHECK_FAILED` or `PAID` reports `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, as does running an edit check on a claim that is not `SUBMITTED` or paying one that is not `VALIDATED`.

A computed read exposes every intermediate step of the reimbursement calculation for a claim as it currently stands, returning the `claim_id`, the claim's `status`, the `variant` that applies to that status, and then `meals_rate_value_cents`, `operating_reimbursement_cents`, `admin_cap_cents`, `admin_reimbursement_cents`, `preliminary_reimbursement_cents`, `final_reimbursement_cents`, `advance_offset_cents`, `net_payable_cents` and `disallowed_meal_count`. The read is available in every status with no state gate of its own. `variant` reads `PRELIMINARY` while the claim sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_OPENED`, and `FINAL` once it sits in `VALIDATED`, `EDIT_CHECK_FAILED` or `PAID`. The four intermediate figures — `meals_rate_value_cents`, `operating_reimbursement_cents`, `admin_cap_cents` and `admin_reimbursement_cents` — are always computed live by the five pinned steps of the final variant whatever `variant` says, reading a null `claimed_operating_cost_cents` or `claimed_admin_cost_cents` as 0. On a claim that has not been submitted this means `meals_rate_value_cents` already reports what its line items are worth while the other three read 0, because the operating reimbursement is capped by an asserted operating cost that is still null. The remaining five figures are the values stored on the claim, reported as null wherever the claim has not reached the step that writes them. Its purpose is auditability: a sponsor disputing a figure can see the cap that was applied rather than inferring it.

### 3.9. Outreach Planning and the Attendance Forecast

Deciding how many meals to prepare for a coming service date is the sponsor's hardest daily judgement: too few and children go without, too many and the surplus is waste the program pays for. The system offers a computed read that answers that question two different ways, and the caller chooses which philosophy to apply. The read takes the target `service_date` and a `method` of either `PEAK_PROJECTION` or `DECAY_WEIGHTED` — both are mandatory query parameters, and omitting either is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` raised at gauntlet step 3, before the `site_id` in the path is resolved — and it returns the `site_id`, the `service_date` asked about, the `method` used, the `forecast_meals` figure itself, `prior_days_used` recording how many historical days the answer rests on, `most_recent_production_meals`, and a `waste_risk_flag`.

The history window is built the same way for both methods. It contains the site's service days whose `status` is `FINALIZED` and whose `service_date` falls strictly before the requested `service_date`, ordered by `service_date` descending, and at most the 5 most recent of those are taken — fewer when the site has a shorter history, and `prior_days_used` reports how many were actually available. When the window is empty, the read is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FORECAST_HISTORY` rather than returning a guess. Days in `VOIDED` or `CLOSED` never enter the window, which has a visible consequence: voiding a day changes the forecast for every later date. The attendance figure read from each window day is its `counted_first_meals`, since first meals are the measure of children served, and the site's `approved_max_attendance` acts as the ceiling on any forecast.

`PEAK_PROJECTION` is the outreach-driven answer, used when the sponsor is advertising the site and wants to avoid running out. It takes the single highest attendance in the window, inflates it by 10 percent, rounds that product up to the next whole meal, and then caps the result at `approved_max_attendance`. Every day other than the peak is ignored entirely, and recency is irrelevant — a busy day four days ago drives the number just as hard as a busy day yesterday.

`DECAY_WEIGHTED` is the waste-minimisation answer, used when the sponsor is trying to cut leftovers. It takes a recency-weighted mean of the window: the most recent day carries a weight of 1, the day before it a weight of 0.6, the one before that 0.36, and so on, each step multiplying the weight by 0.6. The weighted sum of the attendance figures is divided by the sum of the weights, that quotient is rounded half-up to a whole meal, and the result is likewise capped at `approved_max_attendance`. Because older days still pull the number, a declining attendance series yields a materially smaller forecast under `DECAY_WEIGHTED` than under `PEAK_PROJECTION` for the identical window — the two methods are meant to disagree, and the disagreement is the information.

The waste signal is computed the same way whichever method produced the forecast. `most_recent_production_meals` is the total of all values in the `production_counts` of the most recent day in the window, reading as 0 when that day carries no `production_counts` at all, and `waste_risk_flag` is true when that production figure exceeds `forecast_meals` by strictly more than 10 percent of `forecast_meals`, with that tolerance floored to a whole meal. Reading the flag plainly: if the site prepared materially more food last time than the forecast now calls for, the sponsor is being told to prepare less.
