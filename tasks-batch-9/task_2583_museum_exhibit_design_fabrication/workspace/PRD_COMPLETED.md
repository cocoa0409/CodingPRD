# Museum Exhibit Design & Fabrication API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An exhibit design and fabrication firm lives in the gap between two organisations that measure success differently. The museum wants an interpretive storyline that teaches, objects that survive another century, and a gallery that keeps taking visitors' money while the shop is drilling into its walls. The firm wants a design frozen early enough to build, a materials list nobody will veto after the sheet goods are cut, and a settlement at the end of the job that matches what the shop actually spent. This service is the shared system of record for that negotiation. It carries a commission from interpretive planning through schematic and design-development review, artifact case design and conservation sign-off, material off-gassing screening, shop drawings and fabrication costing, installation inside an operating museum, commissioning with a punch list, and finally closeout with warranty registration and a long-term spare-parts commitment for the interactive elements.

Three forces make the domain harder than a linear project tracker. First, design approval is a **multi-party negotiation**: a seated panel drawn from curatorial, conservation, project-management, accessibility and fabrication roles votes on each design package, quorum is two thirds of the panel, and either of the two content-and-object guardians can end a round on their own with a veto. Rounds repeat, so a package can cycle between review and revision any number of times, and a revision issued late enough can push the whole project *backwards* through its phase ladder. Second, the installation calendar is **temporal**, and it uses two structurally different geometries at once: a per-zone exclusion rule that pads each work window with access and dust-settling buffers and treats touching edges as compatible, and a project-wide closure-capacity count that ignores buffers entirely and treats touching edges as concurrent. Third, conservation has an absolute brake. A rejected case, a rejected material, or a recorded conservation veto freezes every forward-moving mutation on the project, and a material rejection unwinds four links of consequences — voided shop drawings, released fabrication money, cases falling back to draft, gallery slots and reserved objects returning to the pool — with no drift in any conserved quantity.

Every rule below is written from the perspective of the operator who has to live with it, but the numeric contracts are exact: money is integer cents, quantities are rounded with a named method at a named point in the calculation, and every rejection has a single stable error identifier. The API is mounted at the service root, so the paths listed in §4 are absolute, and a trailing slash on any of them is accepted as equivalent to the path without one. All timestamps in the system are read from an explicit server-side logical clock, never from wall time, so a deadline test is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request identifies its caller with two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If `X-User-Role` is absent or does not name one of the six roles below, or if `X-User-ID` is absent or blank on a mutating request, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The single exception is the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.4, which any caller may invoke without the authentication check.

The six roles, spelled exactly as the system expects them, are `ProjectManager`, `Curator`, `Conservator`, `AccessibilitySpecialist`, `FabricationLead` and `MuseumOperations`. A `ProjectManager` owns the commission end to end: it opens the project, advances the phase, opens review rounds, rules on shop drawings and prototypes, sweeps lapsed review deadlines, waives non-blocking defects and settles closeout. A `Curator` owns interpretive content and the collection: it registers collection objects, designs cases alongside the project manager, and holds one of the two panel vetoes. A `Conservator` guards the objects: it rules on case environmental envelopes and material off-gassing, drives mount making, and holds the second veto. An `AccessibilitySpecialist` reviews visitor flow, egress and accessibility, may author design packages, and sits on the panel without a veto. A `FabricationLead` runs the shop: it specifies interactives and materials, issues shop drawings, submits prototypes, requests install windows and verifies punch items — but never rules on its own submissions. `MuseumOperations` speaks for the operating museum: it owns the gallery calendar and rules on every install window, noise limit and closure request, and it does not sit on the review panel.

Authorisation is checked against the endpoint's permitted role list **before any path segment is resolved**, so a caller whose role is not permitted receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) rather than a 404, and therefore cannot probe the API for the existence of an identifier it has no right to see.

None of the six roles is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any stored row; the same is true when `X-User-Role` is `Curator`, `Conservator`, `AccessibilitySpecialist`, `FabricationLead` or `MuseumOperations`. The server records the opaque identifier wherever a workflow needs to remember who acted — as `created_by_user_id` on the project, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a design package, an interactive and a shop drawing, as `requested_by_user_id` on an install window, as `raised_by_user_id` and `raised_by_role` on a punch item, as `verified_by_user_id` when a punch item is settled, and as `voter_user_id` alongside `voter_role` on a panel vote — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

Because the identifier is opaque, **segregation of duties** is enforced by comparing raw header strings: no trimming, no normalisation, no case folding. Five pairings are enforced, each rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the caller deciding a shop drawing may not be the caller stored in that drawing's `submitted_by_user_id`; the caller ruling on a prototype may not be the caller stored in that interactive's `submitted_by_user_id`; the caller verifying or waiving a punch item may not be the caller stored in that item's `raised_by_user_id`; the caller deciding an install window may not be the caller stored in that window's `requested_by_user_id`; and the caller casting a panel vote may not be the caller stored in the package's `submitted_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall time, so every response carries the same instant until the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. Every derived field of a resource — a project's six counters, its `posture` and its `uncommitted_cents`; a zone's reserved-versus-available slot split; a case's `placed_artifact_count`, its three envelope limits and its two feasibility flags; and an interactive's `maintenance_tier` — is computed at the moment the resource is serialized rather than stored, so no read can show a stale derivation.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request that matches no route at all, or that names a method the matched route does not accept, answers this same envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under the transport's own status — 404 for an unmatched path and 405 for an unaccepted method — and an unexpected server fault answers the same envelope with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under HTTP 500. The status column of §5 governs the application-level rejections named there, not these transport-level answers.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and so is a value that is not an integer at all; surrounding whitespace is ignored, and a parameter that is omitted, or supplied as an empty string, takes its default. `offset` has no upper bound, so an offset at or past the end of the collection is a legitimate request that returns an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. Because both parameters are read inside the handler rather than ahead of it, they are validated only after the identifiers in the path have been resolved: a malformed `limit` on a list nested under an unknown parent still answers that parent's 404. The four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` are returned inside `meta` on every list response, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Every filter a list endpoint accepts is matched by exact, case-sensitive equality against the value the resource itself reports for that field — the derived ones, such as a project's `posture` and an interactive's `maintenance_tier`, included — so a filter value outside that field's declared domain — or an identifier filter naming a row that exists nowhere — is not a malformed request, it simply matches nothing; an omitted or empty filter parameter is treated as absent. The one boolean-typed filter, `requires_gallery_closure`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1`, `0`, `yes` or `no` in any case and rejects anything else as malformed.

Unless a list is explicitly documented otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first. Two collections deviate, and the deviation is part of the contract: the top-level project list is ordered by `created_at` descending, newest commission first, and a package's review rounds are ordered by `round_number` ascending. Every other list takes the default ascending `created_at` order, including a project's gallery zones — `visitor_route_order` is a field the caller reads, not the order the list arrives in — and install windows, whether listed for a project or for a single zone, whose `starts_at` likewise does not reorder them. Because timestamps come from an explicit logical clock, many rows share a `created_at`; every ordering in this system therefore breaks ties on the order the rows were created, oldest first, and the descending project list reverses that tie-break along with the rest of the key, so the most recently created commission leads.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Resources that can be mutated after creation additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation that touches the row — including mutations the caller did not ask for directly, such as a counter refreshed by a cascade. Wherever a workflow below says a cascade "touches" an entity, it means precisely that: `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant and `version` advances by one. A stored instant keeps whatever sub-millisecond precision the clock carried when it was written, and every comparison uses that full precision, but it is rendered with its fractional part truncated — not rounded — to three digits.

One entity is deliberately immutable: a panel vote. A vote row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because a cast vote is never edited, never withdrawn and never re-tallied. Every other entity in the system — projects, design packages, review rounds, gallery zones, artifact cases, collection objects, interactives, material specifications, shop drawings, install windows and punch items — carries the full set of four common properties. Every field an entity declares is always present in its serialized form: a value no workflow has written yet is returned as JSON `null`, never omitted.

Time in this system is explicit. An administrative test-driving capability sets the server's logical clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and every deadline comparison, every sweep, every `decided_at`, `approved_at`, `verified_at` and `warranty_registered_at` reads that clock rather than wall time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, so an unset clock is a fixed earliest instant rather than a drifting value. Setting the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep; lapsing overdue review rounds is a separate, explicitly invoked operation. That endpoint takes the instant in a required `current_utc` field, is the one endpoint that demands no headers whatsoever — a call carrying neither header, or naming a role the system does not recognise, still succeeds — and it may move the clock backwards as freely as forwards. Its `data` carries a single field, `current_utc`, echoing the instant as the server normalised it. Every datetime a client supplies anywhere in this API is parsed the same way: a trailing `Z` in either case, or an explicit UTC offset, is honoured and normalised to UTC, and a value carrying no offset at all — including a bare calendar date — is read as already being UTC; anything that will not parse as an ISO 8601 instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.5. Nested Paths and Cross-Project Referential Integrity

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment produces its own 404 before the next is considered. An unknown project identifier yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown design package yields `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown review round yields `ERR_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown gallery zone yields `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown artifact case yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown collection object yields `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown interactive yields `ERR_INTERACTIVE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown material specification yields `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown shop drawing yields `ERR_DRAWING_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown install window yields `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown punch item yields `ERR_PUNCH_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`. All eleven are HTTP 404. The same rule applies to identifiers supplied in a request body rather than in the path: a body-supplied `project_id`, `zone_id`, `subject_case_id`, `subject_interactive_id`, `material_spec_id`, `artifact_id`, `interactive_id` or `case_id` that names nothing produces the corresponding 404.

A referenced row that *exists* but belongs to a different commission is a different failure. The system treats every cross-entity reference resolved inside one request as required to belong to the same project as the primary resource, and a mismatch is reported as `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409) — the row is not hidden from the caller, it is misfiled. This is enforced when a case names a zone, when a window names a zone, when a drawing names its material specification and its subject case or subject interactive, when a placement or a placement removal names a collection object, and when a punch item names an interactive or a case.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Two Write Paths

Exactly two write paths in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and each demands a version the client must have read first.

Creating a design package under a project requires `expected_project_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the owning project's currently stored `version`; if the project's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a package create supersedes the project's existing package of the same stage and closes that package's open review round — so a client working from a stale project view may be about to discard a package, and a review in flight, that it has never seen.

Reserving a collection object into an artifact case requires `expected_case_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional, and likewise compared against that case's currently stored `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A placement is perfectly legitimate on its own terms — a case holds many objects — but every placement rewrites the case's strictest-wins environmental envelope, so a client holding a stale case version is reasoning about an envelope that no longer exists.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Where a workflow below reads "the request carries the version the client last observed", it is one of these two and only these two.

### 2.7. The Five-Bucket Contract Ledger

All money is integer cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in the system. A commission's `contract_value_cents` is fixed at creation and is the conserved pool. It is partitioned at all times into exactly five buckets: `committed_fabrication_cents` (money promised to approved shop drawings but not yet settled), `retainage_held_cents` (money withheld at closeout), `warranty_reserve_cents` (the long-term interactive warranty and spare-parts reserve set at closeout), `released_cents` (cash released to the fabrication firm at closeout) and `uncommitted_cents` (everything not yet in one of the other four). `uncommitted_cents` is always the contract value less the sum of the other four buckets, and the five together always add up to `contract_value_cents` exactly.

At creation, the first four are zero and `uncommitted_cents` equals the whole contract value. Approving a shop drawing moves its computed cost out of `uncommitted_cents` and into `committed_fabrication_cents`. Voiding an approved drawing moves exactly the same amount back — a compensating action with no drift, which is why the void path never recomputes anything, it simply releases the amount the approval committed. Closeout is the only operation that populates the last three buckets, and it does so from a pinned seven-step waterfall whose final step is exact integer subtraction, so no cent can be lost or invented.

The gallery floor obeys the same conservation discipline in a different currency. Each gallery zone declares a `case_slot_capacity`, tracks `reserved_case_slots` and exposes `available_case_slots`. Neither derived number is stored: `reserved_case_slots` is the count of the zone's artifact cases standing at `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, recomputed every time the zone is read or a slot is tested, and `available_case_slots` is the declared capacity less that count — which is why the identity that capacity equals reserved plus available holds before and after every operation that touches a slot.

### 2.8. Rounding and Precision

Every rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every rounding happens at a named point, never "at the end" as a convenience.

Money is rounded to whole cents, and the fabrication cost calculation rounds **each of its three line items separately before they are summed**. Summing unrounded line items and rounding once is a different answer and is wrong; it can differ by up to one cent per line item. The closeout waterfall rounds to whole cents at its parts-premium step, its retainage step and its warranty-reserve step, and at no other step. A case's interior volume is rounded to four decimal places in cubic metres, and its silica gel charge is rounded to two decimal places using the already-rounded volume. A design package's flow capacity score and an interactive's durability index are each rounded to two decimal places. Buffer arithmetic on the install calendar is in whole minutes. Relative humidity, illuminance and off-gassing comparisons are exact comparisons on the stored numbers with no rounding at all, and every one of them is inclusive: a value exactly equal to a limit satisfies the limit. Every figure is returned as a JSON number and never as a string — money as an integer count of cents, every rounded quantity as a decimal number whose trailing zeros carry no meaning.

### 2.9. Project Posture: The Conservation Brake and the Restricted Gate

Each project exposes a derived `posture` that is never stored independently — it is a pure function of six counters, and those counters are themselves derived rather than held as independent tallies: `conservation_veto_count` is the one running total kept on the project row, while the other five are recomputed from the project's live design packages, artifact cases, material specifications and punch items every time the project is serialized or a gate consults its posture. A project read therefore always reports counters and a posture consistent with the rows as they stand at that instant, and no cascade can leave them stale.

The counters are: `conservation_veto_count`, the number of veto votes cast by a `Conservator` across every round of the project; `conservation_block_count`, which is the number of the project's artifact cases in `REJECTED` status plus the number of its material specifications in `REJECTED` status plus `conservation_veto_count`; `open_revision_count`, the number of the project's design packages sitting in `REVISION_REQUESTED`; `conditional_case_count`, the number of its artifact cases in `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`; `pending_material_count`, the number of its material specifications whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`; and `blocking_punch_count`, the number of its punch items whose severity is `BLOCKING` and whose status is still `OPEN`. Superseded design packages and closed punch items never contribute to any counter.

Posture is then evaluated in this exact order, first match winning. If `conservation_block_count` is greater than zero, posture is `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`. Otherwise, if `open_revision_count` is greater than zero, or `conditional_case_count` is greater than zero, or `pending_material_count` is greater than zero, posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise posture is `OPEN`.

`FROZEN_CONSERVATION` is the hard brake. While a project is frozen, fifteen forward-moving operations are refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN_CONSERVATION` (HTTP 409): advancing the phase, creating a design package, opening a review round, issuing a shop drawing, deciding a shop drawing, requesting an install window, deciding an install window, reserving an object into a case, submitting a case for conservation, advancing a case's mount making, submitting an interactive prototype, ruling on an interactive prototype, marking an interactive fabricated, submitting a material for conservation, and closeout — and the two conservation resubmissions in that list are the only two that discount the resubmitted row's own block, exactly as §3.5 and §3.6 describe. The freeze lifts only when every contributing block is cleared — each rejected case resubmitted, each rejected material resubmitted, and no conservation veto outstanding.

`RESTRICTED` is the softer gate, and only two operations demand a fully settled project: advancing the phase to `INSTALLATION`, and closeout. Both require posture to be exactly `OPEN`, and both report `ERR_PROJECT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) when posture is `RESTRICTED`. Note the asymmetry that follows from the ordering above: at those two gates a frozen project reports `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN_CONSERVATION`, while a merely restricted one reports `ERR_PROJECT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate rows the caller never named. The rules governing them are uniform. Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. A row that a single request touches more than once — directly and again through a cascade, or through two links of the same cascade — still advances its `version` by exactly one for that request, because the increment is recorded once per row per request. Every cascade that iterates over a set processes that set in a deterministic order, and the order is stated with the cascade. Every counter a cascade disturbs is recomputed from its definition in §2.9 rather than patched by guesswork, and the project's posture is recomputed afterwards. Cascades chain: a link fires because the preceding link fired, not because the original request said so, and the deepest chain in the system — material rejection through drawing void through case revert through object release and slot release — is four links long.

### 2.11. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is reported, and which one is not a matter of taste. Checks are evaluated in a fixed ladder, and the first failure wins:

1. Authentication of the two headers (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorisation for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), before any identifier is resolved.
3. Segregation of duties on the opaque caller identifier (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
4. Request-body shape: parseable JSON object, every required field present and non-null, every enum-typed field carrying a declared value, every number within its declared domain, every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). A field the endpoint treats as optional and the caller supplies as `null` is read exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its documented default. Types are strict and never coerced: a JSON string of digits is not a number, a JSON boolean is not a number, and a fractional value is not an integer. A body that is absent, empty or not a JSON object is itself malformed wherever the payload declares a required field; where a payload has no required field at all an absent or empty body is accepted, an endpoint that declares no payload ignores whatever body arrives, fields the payload does not declare are ignored, and the request's `Content-Type` is never inspected.
5. Existence of every referenced row, path-supplied and body-supplied alike, in the order the eleven 404s are listed in §2.5. Steps 3 to 5 interleave in one fixed way, and it is worth stating plainly: a row named by a **path** segment is resolved before the body is shape-checked, so an unknown path identifier answers its own 404 even when the body is malformed too, and the segregation test of step 3 — which can only be judged once the row it compares against is in hand — runs on that resolved row, still ahead of step 4. A row named in the **body** is necessarily resolved after step 4, since the body must parse before its identifiers can be read. Two operations reverse the path rule. The shop-drawing ruling of §3.7 looks the sheet up without raising, judges its segregation pairing if the sheet is on file, and reports the sheet's own 404 only after the body has passed step 4; and opening a review round of §3.3 shape-checks its body first as well, so a missing or malformed `panel_roles` or `decision_deadline_at` addressed to an unknown package answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Cross-project ownership of every resolved reference (`ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE`).
7. Optimistic-concurrency version match — the project version first, then the case version (`ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`).
8. Scoped uniqueness of human-authored codes (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), then single-instance constraints on votes and open rounds (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, `ERR_ROUND_ALREADY_OPEN`), then panel seating (`ERR_VOTER_NOT_ON_PANEL`).
9. Temporal shape of the request itself: a window's end after its start (`ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`), a review deadline in the future (`ERR_DEADLINE_IN_PAST`).
10. Project phase (`ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE`).
11. Project posture — the conservation freeze (`ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN_CONSERVATION`) and then the restricted gate (`ERR_PROJECT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`).
12. The state of the specific resource being acted on, in the order package, round, round deadline, mount dimension, case status, material status, drawing status, interactive status, window status, punch status.
13. Domain constraints and safety rules: panel composition, accessibility, the placement chain (already placed, not in case, unstable condition, security tier, then environmental envelope), empty-case submission, off-gassing, material clearance for a drawing, prototype clearance for a drawing, blocking punch items, warranty term, mount installation, objective coverage, noise, zone availability, per-zone window conflict, project-wide closure capacity, and zone slot exhaustion — in that order.
14. Fiscal capacity last of all (`ERR_FABRICATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NOTHING_TO_SETTLE`). Because money is checked last, a request that also violates a conservation or constraint rule never reports a budget error.

Two orderings inside step 13 matter enough to restate. On a placement, the security-tier check runs **above** the environmental-envelope check, so a request that breaks both reports `ERR_SECURITY_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` rather than `ERR_ENV_ENVELOPE_INFEASIBLE`. On an install-window approval, the per-zone buffered conflict check runs **above** the project-wide closure-capacity check, so a window that breaks both reports `ERR_ZONE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` rather than `ERR_CLOSURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Interpretive Planning and the Phase Ladder

A commission begins when a `ProjectManager` opens a project. The payload names the job as contracted — a `name` and a `museum_name`, each a non-blank string of 1 to 160 characters — and commits to the interpretive frame: a `storyline_title` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters agreed with the curators, and `objective_codes`, an array of between 2 and 8 unique learning-objective codes each matching the pattern of the literal prefix `OBJ-` followed by two to six characters drawn from the uppercase letters and digits. A `target_open_date` records the contracted public opening instant as an ISO 8601 UTC value, and `contract_value_cents` records the whole commercial envelope in cents; it must be at least 1,000,000 cents, which is to say ten thousand dollars. The optional `max_concurrent_closures` caps how many closure-requiring install windows the museum will tolerate running at once across the whole project; it must be at least 1 and at most 5, and when omitted it defaults to 2.

The new project opens in phase `INTERPRETIVE` at `version` 1, with `created_by_user_id` taken from the caller's header. Its ledger starts settled: `committed_fabrication_cents`, `retainage_held_cents`, `warranty_reserve_cents` and `released_cents` are each 0 and `uncommitted_cents` equals `contract_value_cents`, so the five-bucket identity of §2.7 holds from the first instant. All six posture counters are 0 and `posture` is therefore `OPEN`. The closeout-only fields are empty until closeout writes them: `parts_commitment_years` is 0, and `docent_kit_url`, `maintenance_manual_url` and `warranty_registered_at` are all null.

The project's `phase` walks a ladder of eight values — `INTERPRETIVE`, `SCHEMATIC`, `DESIGN_DEV`, `FABRICATION`, `INSTALLATION`, `COMMISSIONING`, `CLOSEOUT` and finally the terminal `WARRANTY_CLOSED`. A `ProjectManager` advances it one step at a time by naming the destination in `to_phase`; if the named destination is not the immediate successor of the phase currently stored, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE`. Each step has its own admission test, and a step whose admission test fails is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE` except where the step below names a different identifier; every step other than the two noted below additionally requires that the project is not in `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`. Whatever the step, a successful advance refreshes the project's `updated_at`, increments its `version` by one, and recomputes posture.

`INTERPRETIVE → SCHEMATIC` is interpretive-planning sign-off: content development counts as complete once `storyline_title` is non-blank and at least two learning objectives are on file. `SCHEMATIC → DESIGN_DEV` requires that the project already has a design package whose stage is `SCHEMATIC` and whose status is `APPROVED`. `DESIGN_DEV → FABRICATION` requires an `APPROVED` package at the `DESIGN_DEV` stage, and additionally requires that every code in `objective_codes` is carried by something buildable: each code must appear as the `objective_code` of at least one artifact case or at least one interactive belonging to the project — whatever status that case or interactive currently stands at, since the scan reads the `objective_code` field alone — and a code carried by nothing fails the advance with `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_ADDRESSED`. `FABRICATION → INSTALLATION` is one of the two gates that demands posture exactly `OPEN`; it further requires that the project has at least one artifact case and that every one of its cases is `APPROVED`, and that at least one shop drawing of the project stands at `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`. `INSTALLATION → COMMISSIONING` requires that no install window is still live — every window of the project must have reached `COMPLETED`, `DENIED` or `CANCELLED` — and that every collection object which still names a case has actually been physically installed, that is, its `placement_status` is `PLACED`. `COMMISSIONING → CLOSEOUT` requires `blocking_punch_count` to be 0, failing with `ERR_BLOCKING_PUNCH_ITEMS_OPEN` when a blocking defect is still open, and requires every interactive of the project to be `COMMISSIONED`. The final step to `WARRANTY_CLOSED` is not an advance at all but the settlement operation described in §3.9.

The ladder is not a one-way pipeline. When a `SCHEMATIC`-stage package of a project sitting in `DESIGN_DEV` falls into `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the project reverts `DESIGN_DEV → SCHEMATIC`; when a `DESIGN_DEV`-stage package of a project sitting in `FABRICATION` falls into `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the project reverts `FABRICATION → DESIGN_DEV`. Both reverts are system-driven, both deny outstanding install windows as described in §3.3, both recompute `open_revision_count` and posture, and both refresh the project's `updated_at` and increment its `version`.

Phase also decides which children may be created at all, and a mismatch is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE`. Gallery zones, collection objects and interactives may be registered only while the phase is `INTERPRETIVE`, `SCHEMATIC` or `DESIGN_DEV`. Artifact cases may be designed only in `SCHEMATIC` or `DESIGN_DEV`. Design packages may be issued in `SCHEMATIC`, `DESIGN_DEV` or `FABRICATION` — a design change during fabrication is exactly what makes the ladder cyclic. Material specifications may be submitted in `INTERPRETIVE`, `SCHEMATIC`, `DESIGN_DEV` or `FABRICATION`, because a material substitution mid-fabrication is normal and it is the conservator's ruling, not the phase, that gates it. Shop drawings may be issued only in `FABRICATION`, though taking an already-submitted sheet into review is permitted in either `DESIGN_DEV` or `FABRICATION`, and the ruling that follows carries no phase requirement of its own at all — the conservation freeze and the sheet's own status, not the phase, are what gate it. Install windows may be requested only in `FABRICATION` or `INSTALLATION` — windows are vetted while the shop is still building, which is precisely why a revert out of `FABRICATION` finds requested windows waiting to be denied — while the ruling on a requested window carries no phase requirement of its own at all, exactly like the shop-drawing ruling: the conservation freeze and the window's own status, not the phase, are what gate it. Opening an approved window for work requires `INSTALLATION`; completing an in-progress window, and cancelling a requested or approved one, are not phase-gated either. Punch items may be raised, and interactives commissioned, only in `COMMISSIONING`. Punch items may be verified or waived in `COMMISSIONING` or `CLOSEOUT`. Settlement requires `CLOSEOUT`.

A read of the project returns its live `posture`, all six counters and all five money buckets. The project list may be filtered by `phase` and by `posture`.

### 3.2. Design Packages: Visitor Flow, Accessibility and Supersession

A design package is the firm's formal submission of a design stage for panel review. Either a `ProjectManager` or an `AccessibilitySpecialist` may author one, naming the `stage` as `SCHEMATIC` or `DESIGN_DEV` and carrying the `expected_project_version` demanded by §2.6. The rest of the payload describes the visitor route in four numbers: `egress_clear_width_cm`, the narrowest clear egress width anywhere on the route; `wheelchair_turning_radius_cm`, the smallest turning radius provided; `peak_visitors_per_hour`, the designed peak throughput, which must be at least 1; and `circulation_area_sqm`, the non-case floor area on the route. The three dimensional figures must all be greater than zero. The package is stored against its owning project through the `project_id` it inherits from the path.

Before the package is stored, the system computes its flow and accessibility results. The route must provide 0.65 square metres of circulation area per visitor per hour of designed peak throughput, so the required area is `peak_visitors_per_hour` multiplied by 0.65. The circulation ratio is `circulation_area_sqm` divided by that required area, and `flow_capacity_score` is that ratio multiplied by 100, capped at 100.0, then rounded half up to two decimal places — a route with exactly the required area therefore scores 100.00, and a route with double the required area also scores 100.00 because of the cap. `accessibility_pass` is true only when all three minimums hold together: `egress_clear_width_cm` is at least 112, `wheelchair_turning_radius_cm` is at least 76, and `flow_capacity_score` is at least 60.0. All three comparisons are inclusive, so a value sitting exactly on its minimum passes. A package that fails any of the three is rejected outright with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET` (HTTP 422) and is not stored at all — which is why `accessibility_pass` is never persisted as false, and why a stored package always carries a `flow_capacity_score` of at least 60.00.

A package that passes is stored in status `DRAFT` with `review_round_count` and `revision_count` both 0, `submitted_by_user_id` taken from the caller's header, `approved_at` null and `superseded_by_package_id` null. Its `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and the terminal `SUPERSEDED`.

Issuing a package **supersedes** the project's earlier package of the same stage. Every existing package of this project whose `stage` matches the new one and whose status is not already `SUPERSEDED` — that is, any package in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and has its `superseded_by_package_id` set to the new package's identifier. Every review round still `OPEN` on a superseded package is closed as `CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` with its `decided_at` set to the current logical-clock instant. The project's `open_revision_count` and posture are then recomputed — a package that was sitting in `REVISION_REQUESTED` and is now `SUPERSEDED` no longer restricts the project — and the project's `version` is incremented. Each superseded package and each closed round has its own `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented. Because the self-referential pointer runs from the old package to the new one, the set of packages a given package displaced is readable in reverse, oldest first.

Packages of a project may be listed with `stage` and `status` filters, and a single package read back with its computed flow figures.

### 3.3. The Review Panel: Rounds, Quorum, Veto and Deadline Sweeps

Review is organised into rounds, and a `ProjectManager` opens one. The payload seats the panel in `panel_roles` and fixes the negotiation clock in `decision_deadline_at`. A panel must have between 3 and 5 entries with no duplicates, every entry drawn from `Curator`, `Conservator`, `ProjectManager`, `AccessibilitySpecialist` and `FabricationLead`, and it must include both `Curator` and `Conservator`, because those two hold the veto; any other composition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_COMPOSITION` (HTTP 422). The deadline must fall strictly after the current logical-clock instant — a deadline equal to the current instant is rejected with `ERR_DEADLINE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422), because such a round would be sweepable the moment it opened.

A round may only be opened on a package whose status is `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; a `SUPERSEDED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` package refuses with `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_STATE`, and a package whose `superseded_by_package_id` is set is never eligible. A package carries at most one live round: if any round of that package is still `OPEN`, the request fails with `ERR_ROUND_ALREADY_OPEN`. The owning project must not be in `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN_CONSERVATION`.

The new round records the package it reviews through its `package_id` and takes a `round_number` equal to the package's `review_round_count` plus one, so rounds are numbered from 1 within each package. Its `required_vote_count` is the quorum: two thirds of the seated panel, rounded **up** to a whole vote. A panel of three therefore needs 2 votes, a panel of four needs 3, and a panel of five needs 4. The round opens with `status` `OPEN`, with `cast_vote_count`, `approve_count`, `revise_count` and `veto_count` all 0, with `swept_by_deadline` false and `decided_at` null. The package's `review_round_count` is incremented and the package moves from `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` into `IN_REVIEW`; the project's `open_revision_count` and posture are recomputed, and the package's and project's `version` values each advance by one. Because a package in `REVISION_REQUESTED` may open a fresh round, and the round that follows may put it back into `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the review machine is genuinely cyclic — a package can re-enter review any number of times.

Each panelist casts exactly one vote, recording their `decision` as `APPROVE`, `REVISE` or `VETO` and optionally attaching a `comment` of up to 500 characters, which is stored as null when omitted. The vote row is immutable: it captures the `round_id` it belongs to, the caller's `voter_role` and the caller's `voter_user_id`, and it is never edited. Four admission tests guard a vote, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11: the caller may not be the author of the package under review, or the vote is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the round must not already hold a vote from this caller — neither the same `voter_role` nor the same `voter_user_id` may appear twice, since one role votes once per round and no role may revise its position — or the vote is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`; the caller's role must be seated on `panel_roles`, or the vote is refused with `ERR_VOTER_NOT_ON_PANEL`; and the round must still be `OPEN`, since a round that has reached `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `VETOED` or `CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` accepts nothing further and answers `ERR_INVALID_ROUND_STATE`. One further test is temporal: the logical clock must be strictly before `decision_deadline_at`, so a vote arriving exactly at the deadline instant is rejected with `ERR_ROUND_DEADLINE_PASSED` — the same boundary the sweep uses from the other side.

An accepted vote is appended and the four tallies are recomputed: `cast_vote_count` rises by one, and exactly one of `approve_count`, `revise_count` or `veto_count` rises with it. The response to a vote is the **updated round**, not the vote row, so the caller sees the new tally and the round's resolved status in the same reply; the individual votes are readable through the round's own vote list, oldest first.

Three outcomes can take a round out of `OPEN`, and their side effects diverge sharply. A `VETO` ends the round at once as `VETOED`, and **quorum is not required for a veto** — a single veto closes a round that has collected only one vote out of four. A round that reaches quorum with every vote an approval, that is with `cast_vote_count` equal to `required_vote_count` and `approve_count` equal to `required_vote_count`, becomes `APPROVED`. A round that reaches quorum with at least one `REVISE` and no veto becomes `REVISION_REQUESTED`. In all three cases `decided_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant and the round's `updated_at` and `version` are refreshed, and because each of the three drives its package, the package row and the owning project row are touched along with it, so both of those `version` values advance by one as well; a vote that leaves the round `OPEN` touches the round alone and moves neither.

An `APPROVED` round drives its package `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, sets the package's `approved_at` to the logical-clock instant, and recomputes the project's `open_revision_count` and posture. A `VETOED` round and a quorum-with-revisions round both drive the package `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`. That is the second link of the review cascade, and it does four things: the package's `revision_count` increases by one; the project's `open_revision_count` increases, which makes the project `RESTRICTED` for as long as any of its packages sits in `REVISION_REQUESTED`; the project's phase reverts if the stage and phase line up as described in §3.1, which is the third link; and that revert denies install windows, which is the fourth. When a veto's `voter_role` is `Conservator`, one further consequence fires: the project's `conservation_veto_count` increases by one, which raises `conservation_block_count` and drives the project straight to `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`. A `Curator` veto ends the round and sends the package back for revision exactly as a conservator's does, but it does not freeze the project.

The install-window denials deserve to be stated in full, because they are the least guessable part of the chain. Whenever a phase revert fires, **every** install window of that project whose status is still `REQUESTED` transitions to `DENIED` with its `denial_reason` set to the literal string `DESIGN_REVISION_REOPENED`, and each denied window has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Approved and in-progress windows are untouched; only requested ones are swept away, on the reasoning that a design under revision cannot justify booking gallery time it has not earned.

Deadlines are enforced deterministically rather than by a background job: a `ProjectManager` runs a review sweep over the project. Every round of that project that is still `OPEN` and whose `decision_deadline_at` is at or before the current logical-clock instant is lapsed, and the qualifying rounds are processed in ascending order of `decision_deadline_at` with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, so the sweep's effects are order-deterministic. Each lapsed round transitions `OPEN → REVISION_REQUESTED` with `swept_by_deadline` set to true and `decided_at` set to the clock instant — a lapse counts as a revision request, and no vote is required to produce one. Each lapsed round then drives its package `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` with the full cascade above, including the possible phase revert and the resulting window denials. The sweep recomputes the project's posture and increments the project's `version` — unconditionally, even when no round qualified — and answers with the updated project row rather than with the rounds it lapsed. A round's list channel accepts a `status` filter, and a single round can be read back with its live tallies and its quorum target.

### 3.4. Zones, Collection Objects and the Case Environmental Envelope

The gallery is described as a set of zones, registered by either `MuseumOperations` or a `ProjectManager`. Each zone carries a `zone_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique among the project's zones, and a `visitor_route_order` of at least 1 that places it on the interpretive walk and must likewise be unique among the project's zones — a collision on either is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409), with codes compared case-sensitively and exactly. `max_noise_dba` records the loudest work the museum will tolerate in that zone and must be greater than zero. `case_slot_capacity` declares how many artifact cases the floor plan can carry and must be between 1 and 20 inclusive; it is the conserved slot pool. Three flags are optional: `is_public_operating`, true by default, records whether the zone stays open to visitors during the install period; `closure_allowed`, false by default, records whether the museum will permit closing the zone to visitors for work at all; and `dust_settling_buffer_minutes`, 120 by default and constrained to between 0 and 480 inclusive, is the zone's own minimum settling period after dust-generating work.

A new zone starts with `reserved_case_slots` at 0 and `available_case_slots` equal to its capacity, so the slot identity of §2.7 holds from creation, and with `occupancy_state` at `OPERATING`. That state field takes three values — `OPERATING`, `PARTIAL_CLOSURE` and `CLOSED` — and it is driven only by install work, never set directly. A project's zones may be filtered on `occupancy_state`; a zone read returns its live reserved-versus-available split; and a zone exposes both the cases sited in it and its own install calendar, each filterable by `status`.

Collection objects are registered by a `Curator` alone. Each carries an `accession_number` of 1 to 40 characters unique among the project's objects, a `material_class` of `ORGANIC`, `METAL`, `PAPER`, `TEXTILE` or `COMPOSITE` naming the dominant material that drives its conservation limits, and the conservation envelope the object itself demands: `required_rh_min_pct` and `required_rh_max_pct`, both strictly between 0 and 100 with the maximum strictly greater than the minimum, and `max_lux`, the highest illuminance it tolerates, which may be 0 or more. A registrar-assigned `security_class` of `BASIC`, `ENHANCED` or `HIGH_VALUE` and a conservator's `condition_status` of `STABLE`, `FRAGILE` or `UNSTABLE` complete the row. Every object begins `UNPLACED` with `assigned_case_id` null, and its `placement_status` walks `UNPLACED`, `RESERVED`, `PLACED` and the terminal `DEINSTALLED`. Those four buckets always partition the project's objects. The object list may be filtered by `placement_status`, by `condition_status` and by `assigned_case_id`.

A case is designed by a `ProjectManager` or a `Curator` and sited in a zone by naming its `zone_id`. It carries a `case_code` of 1 to 40 characters unique among the project's cases, and optionally an `objective_code` naming the learning objective it interprets — null when the case is purely architectural, and, when non-null, required to be one of the owning project's `objective_codes`; a code that is not a member of that array is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The build tier is fixed by `security_tier`, one of `BASIC`, `ENHANCED` or `HIGH_VALUE`. The environmental design is fixed by four numbers: `target_rh_pct`, the relative-humidity setpoint inside the case, strictly between 0 and 100; `rh_tolerance_pct`, the one-sided excursion the design guarantees, between 0 and 20 inclusive; `target_lux`, the designed illuminance on the object plane, 0 or more; and `uv_microwatt_per_lumen`, the UV content of the case lighting, 0 or more. Geometry is `interior_l_cm`, `interior_w_cm` and `interior_h_cm`, each greater than zero, and `air_exchanges_per_day` records the measured air exchange rate, also greater than zero.

Two derived quantities are computed the moment the case is stored. `case_volume_m3` is the product of the three interior dimensions in centimetres divided by 1,000,000 — the number of cubic centimetres in a cubic metre — rounded half up to four decimal places. `silica_gel_kg` is the conditioned silica-gel charge needed to hold the setpoint: the already-rounded cubic-metre volume multiplied by `air_exchanges_per_day` multiplied by 3.5 kilograms per cubic metre per daily air exchange, rounded half up to two decimal places. The case is stored with `status` `DRAFT`, `mount_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `placed_artifact_count` 0, `conditions` null and `decided_at` null. Because the case is empty, `required_rh_min_pct`, `required_rh_max_pct` and `allowed_lux` are all null while `env_feasible` and `security_ok` are both true. Designing a case does **not** touch the zone's slot counters; a slot is only consumed when a conservator approves the case.

The case's real constraint appears when objects go into it, and it is a **strictest-wins** envelope recomputed over the set of objects currently reserved or placed in the case. Objects that are `UNPLACED` or `DEINSTALLED` are excluded from that set even if they once sat in this case. When the set is empty, the three derived limits are null and both feasibility flags are true. When it is not empty, `required_rh_min_pct` becomes the **largest** of the objects' individual humidity floors, `required_rh_max_pct` becomes the **smallest** of their humidity ceilings, and `allowed_lux` becomes the **smallest** of their `max_lux` values. `env_feasible` is true only when all five of the following hold together: the derived floor is no greater than the derived ceiling; the case's setpoint less its tolerance is at least the derived floor; the case's setpoint plus its tolerance is at most the derived ceiling; the case's `target_lux` is at most `allowed_lux`; and the case's `uv_microwatt_per_lumen` is at most 75.0 microwatts per lumen. Every one of those comparisons is inclusive, so a case whose `target_lux` exactly equals the strictest `allowed_lux` passes. `security_ok` is true when the case's build tier ranks at or above the highest security class among the objects in it, ranking `BASIC` as 1, `ENHANCED` as 2 and `HIGH_VALUE` as 3. Because the derivation uses only largest-of and smallest-of, it is order-independent and needs no tie-break.

Placing an object into a case is done by a `Curator` or a `Conservator`, and the request names the `artifact_id` alongside the `expected_case_version` of §2.6. The chain of admission tests is: the case's conservation status must be `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, since a case already under review or already ruled on refuses with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`; the object must be `UNPLACED`, because an object already `RESERVED`, `PLACED` or `DEINSTALLED` answers `ERR_ARTIFACT_ALREADY_PLACED` (HTTP 409); the object's `condition_status` must not be `UNSTABLE`, since an unstable object may not be committed to a case until conservation stabilises it, and the attempt answers `ERR_ARTIFACT_CONDITION_UNSTABLE` (HTTP 422); the case's security tier must rank at or above the object's security class, so a `HIGH_VALUE` object cannot go into an `ENHANCED` or `BASIC` case and the attempt answers `ERR_SECURITY_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422); and finally the envelope recomputed **with the incoming object included** must remain feasible, or the attempt answers `ERR_ENV_ENVELOPE_INFEASIBLE` (HTTP 422). The security test sits above the environmental test deliberately, so a request that breaks both reports the security failure.

A successful placement sets the object's `assigned_case_id` to the case, moves its `placement_status` `UNPLACED → RESERVED`, increments the case's `placed_artifact_count`, and rewrites `required_rh_min_pct`, `required_rh_max_pct`, `allowed_lux`, `env_feasible` and `security_ok` from the new set. Both rows have their `updated_at` refreshed and their `version` incremented by one. The response is the updated object.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to the same two roles, and it addresses the object by its identifier in the path. The object must currently be assigned to that case with `placement_status` `RESERVED` or `PLACED`, or the request answers `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_IN_CASE` (HTTP 409). A `RESERVED` object returns to `UNPLACED` and its `assigned_case_id` is cleared, so `placed_artifact_count` returns to exactly its pre-placement value with no drift. A `PLACED` object — one already physically installed — becomes `DEINSTALLED` instead and keeps its `assigned_case_id` as the historical record, so it never returns to the unplaced pool; it does, however, leave the envelope set, so the case's derived limits are recomputed from whatever remains and relax back to null bounds when the case empties. Either way both rows have their `updated_at` and `version` advanced and the response is the updated object, exactly as on a placement, and the case's list of assigned objects — which shows objects in any `placement_status` and accepts a `placement_status` filter — remains the reverse-lookup channel from a case to its contents.

### 3.5. Conservation Rulings on Cases, Zone Slots and Mount Making

A loaded case goes to the conservators for review, and either a `ProjectManager` or a `Curator` sends it. The case's `status` moves `DRAFT → CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED → CONSERVATOR_REVIEW` when a previously refused case is being reworked. An empty case has no envelope to review, so submission requires `placed_artifact_count` of at least 1 and otherwise fails with `ERR_CASE_HAS_NO_ARTIFACTS` (HTTP 422); the project must not be in `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`; and the envelope is recomputed immediately before the transition so the conservator rules on current numbers. Submitting from `REJECTED` additionally decrements the project's `conservation_block_count` by one and recomputes posture — which is why the rework path is only reachable once every *other* conservation block on the project has already been cleared, since a frozen project refuses the submission outright. Either way the case is the only row written — its `updated_at` and `version` advance, while the project's counters, being derived, need no write, so the project's own `version` does not move on a submission.

The ruling itself belongs to a `Conservator` and to no one else. The request carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `REJECT`, and optionally a `conditions` string. The case must be in `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`, or the ruling answers `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. In every branch the case's `decided_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, the project's `conditional_case_count`, `conservation_block_count` and posture are recomputed, and both the case row and the owning project row are touched, so both `version` values are incremented — the ruling, unlike the submission above, always writes the project.

An `APPROVE` demands three things of the case and its zone before it will land: `env_feasible` must be true, or the ruling is refused with `ERR_ENV_ENVELOPE_INFEASIBLE`; `security_ok` must be true, or it is refused with `ERR_SECURITY_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`; and the zone the case sits in must have at least one `available_case_slots`, or it is refused with `ERR_ZONE_CASE_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). On success the case becomes `APPROVED`, its `conditions` is set to null, and the zone reserves the slot: `reserved_case_slots` rises by one and `available_case_slots` falls by one, preserving the capacity identity, with the zone's own `updated_at` and `version` advancing.

An `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` carries exactly the same three preconditions and reserves a zone slot in exactly the same way, and it additionally requires a non-blank `conditions` string in the body — an approval with conditions but no conditions text is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The case becomes `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with the conservator's text stored in `conditions`, and the project's `conditional_case_count` rises by one, which by §2.9 drives the project to `RESTRICTED` and therefore blocks both the advance to `INSTALLATION` and closeout until the condition is discharged.

A `REJECT` has no envelope or slot preconditions at all — a conservator may always refuse — and it is the branch with teeth. The case becomes `REJECTED`. Every object in it whose `placement_status` is `RESERVED` returns to `UNPLACED` with its `assigned_case_id` cleared, taken in ascending `created_at` order, while an object already `PLACED` is deliberately left exactly where it is; `placed_artifact_count` therefore falls to the number of objects still `PLACED` in the case — 0 for a case that held nothing but reservations, and only in that case do `required_rh_min_pct`, `required_rh_max_pct` and `allowed_lux` null out. No zone slot is released, because a case under review never held one. The project's `conservation_block_count` rises by one, which drives posture to `FROZEN_CONSERVATION` and stops every forward-moving operation listed in §2.9 until the case is resubmitted.

Mount making runs on a **second, independent** state dimension of the same case. `mount_status` walks `NOT_STARTED`, `MOUNT_DESIGN`, `MOUNT_FABRICATED` and the terminal `MOUNT_INSTALLED`, and a `Conservator` advances it one step at a time by naming the destination in `to_mount_status`. If the named destination is not the immediate successor of the stored value, the step is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MOUNT_STATE` (HTTP 409). The two dimensions are otherwise independent — a case can legitimately sit in `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW` with its mount already at `MOUNT_DESIGN` — but three couplings exist. Entering `MOUNT_DESIGN` requires the case's conservation status to be `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` or `APPROVED`; a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` case refuses with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Entering `MOUNT_FABRICATED` requires the conservation status to be `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, and further requires that at least one shop drawing detailing this case stands at `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_MOUNT_STATE` — you cannot fabricate a mount from a sheet nobody has cleared. Entering `MOUNT_INSTALLED` requires the conservation status to be `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, requires the project phase to be `INSTALLATION`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE`, and requires that at least one install window **on this case's own zone** is currently `IN_PROGRESS`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE` — a window running on a sibling zone of the same project does not unlock the mount. Mount advance is one of the operations the conservation freeze blocks, and each successful step refreshes the case's `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

The mount dimension can also move backwards, but only by cascade: when a shop drawing detailing the case is voided and the case falls back to `DRAFT`, a mount standing at `MOUNT_FABRICATED` falls back to `MOUNT_DESIGN`. A mount already at `MOUNT_INSTALLED` is not disturbed.

A project's cases may be listed with `status`, `mount_status` and `zone_id` filters, and a single case read returns both state dimensions together with the full derived envelope.

### 3.6. Materials Screening and the Four-Link Conservation Cascade

Nothing goes into a case without passing the chamber. A `FabricationLead` or a `Conservator` submits a material for screening, naming the proprietary `material_name` — 1 to 120 characters, unique among the project's materials — and the assembly it is proposed for in `used_in`, one of `CASE_INTERIOR`, `CASE_EXTERIOR`, `MOUNT`, `GRAPHIC` or `INTERACTIVE_HOUSING`. The lab numbers follow: `oddy_test_result` reports the Oddy corrosion class as `PASS`, `TEMPORARY`, `FAIL` or `UNTESTED`; `voc_mg_per_m2_day` reports the total VOC emission rate from the chamber test and `formaldehyde_ppm` the steady-state formaldehyde concentration, each 0 or greater; and `cure_days` records the off-gassing cure period the fabricator commits to before installation, between 0 and 365 inclusive.

The **assembly decides the limits**, which is the point of the exercise: the same chamber numbers can be conservation-safe for a graphic panel and unsafe for a case interior. The VOC ceiling is 0.05 milligrams per square metre per day for `CASE_INTERIOR` and for `MOUNT`, 0.50 for `CASE_EXTERIOR`, 0.80 for `GRAPHIC` and 0.60 for `INTERACTIVE_HOUSING`. The formaldehyde ceiling is 0.02 parts per million for `CASE_INTERIOR` and `MOUNT`, and 0.10 for `CASE_EXTERIOR`, `GRAPHIC` and `INTERACTIVE_HOUSING`. The minimum cure period is 21 days for `CASE_INTERIOR` and `MOUNT`, 7 days for `CASE_EXTERIOR`, 3 days for `GRAPHIC` and 5 days for `INTERACTIVE_HOUSING`.

The derived `verdict` is evaluated in three ordered branches and the first match wins. The material is `UNSAFE` if its Oddy class is `FAIL` or `UNTESTED`, or its VOC rate is strictly greater than **twice** the applicable VOC ceiling, or its formaldehyde concentration is strictly greater than twice the applicable formaldehyde ceiling. Failing that, it is `CONSERVATION_SAFE` if its Oddy class is exactly `PASS` **and** its VOC rate is at most the ceiling **and** its formaldehyde concentration is at most the ceiling **and** its `cure_days` is at least the minimum. Anything else — a `TEMPORARY` Oddy class, a VOC rate above the ceiling but not above double it, an under-cured but otherwise clean material — is `CONDITIONAL_SAFE`. All comparisons are exact on the stored numbers and a value exactly equal to a ceiling satisfies it.

A freshly submitted material lands in `SUBMITTED` status with its verdict already computed, its `conditions` null and its `decided_at` null. That alone raises the project's `pending_material_count` and is enough to make an otherwise clean project `RESTRICTED`, and it increments the project's `version`. Materials may be listed with `status`, `verdict` and `used_in` filters, and each material exposes the shop drawings built from it — the blast radius of a rejection, visible before the rejection happens.

Moving a material into review is done by a `FabricationLead` or a `ProjectManager`, and the status may be `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`; anything else answers `ERR_INVALID_MATERIAL_STATE`. In every case the material must not be `UNTESTED` on the Oddy axis — an untested sample cannot be put in front of a conservator, and the attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_MATERIAL_STATE` as well — and the project must not be frozen, where that freeze test — exactly like the case rework path of §3.5, and unlike the other frozen-blocked operations of §2.9, which count every block — disregards this material's own `REJECTED` contribution to `conservation_block_count`, so a rejected sample is never barred from its own resubmission by the block it itself contributes, while any other outstanding block still refuses it. The verdict is re-derived from the current chamber numbers, so a reformulated sample is judged on current data, and the material moves to `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`. Resubmitting from `REJECTED` clears that rejection's contribution to `conservation_block_count`. Resubmitting an `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` material is a **re-review of a cleared material** — a new production lot has been chamber-tested — and it pushes the project back to `RESTRICTED` because the material is once more pending; resubmitting from `CONDITIONAL` also clears the stored `conditions`. Crucially, every shop drawing already issued from the material keeps its status through the resubmission: the drawings are voided only if the re-review actually ends in a rejection. This is what makes a spec that already carries approved, money-committing sheets capable of being rejected later. The material's and the project's counters are recomputed per §2.9 and both `version` values advance.

The ruling belongs to a `Conservator`. The material must be in `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`. An `APPROVE` is permitted only when the derived verdict is `CONSERVATION_SAFE`; an `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` is permitted when the verdict is `CONSERVATION_SAFE` or `CONDITIONAL_SAFE` and requires a non-blank `conditions` string, without which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either clearance attempted on an `UNSAFE` material is refused with `ERR_OFFGASSING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422): only `REJECT` is available on an unsafe material, so off-gassing cannot be waived by fiat. `APPROVE` lands the material in `APPROVED` with `conditions` nulled; `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` lands it in `CONDITIONAL` with the conservator's text stored. Both set `decided_at` to the logical-clock instant, drop the material out of `pending_material_count`, and recompute posture — so clearing the last pending material is what returns a project to `OPEN`. A clearance touches the material row and nothing else: the counters are derived, so the project row itself is not written and its `version` does not move, in contrast to the rejection branch below, which does touch the project.

`REJECT` is the deepest cascade in the system, and each of its four links fires because the previous link fired, not because the request asked for it.

**Link one.** Every shop drawing built from this material that is not already `VOID` or `REJECTED` — that is, every sheet in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED` — transitions to `VOID` with its `void_reason` set to the literal string `MATERIAL_SPEC_REJECTED`. The sheets are processed in ascending `created_at` order so the cascade is order-deterministic.

**Link two.** Each voided sheet that had actually committed money — one that stood at `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED` and therefore carries a non-null `actual_cost_cents` — releases exactly that amount from the project's `committed_fabrication_cents` back into `uncommitted_cents`. Sheets voided out of `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` move no money, because they never committed any. The five-bucket identity of §2.7 is preserved exactly; nothing is recomputed, the original commitment is simply reversed.

**Link three.** Each voided sheet that names a `subject_case_id` whose case stands at `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` sends that case back to `DRAFT`. The case gives up the zone slot it reserved at approval — `reserved_case_slots` falls by one and `available_case_slots` rises by one on its zone — its stored `conditions` is cleared, and a `mount_status` of `MOUNT_FABRICATED` falls back to `MOUNT_DESIGN`. A sheet detailing an interactive rather than a case reverts nothing.

**Link four.** Each reverted case releases its contents: every object in it whose `placement_status` is `RESERVED` returns to `UNPLACED` with `assigned_case_id` cleared, taken in ascending `created_at` order, while an object already `PLACED` stays exactly as it is; the case's `placed_artifact_count` therefore falls to the number of objects still `PLACED` in it, and only when that leaves the case empty do its derived envelope limits return to null bounds. The zone's `available_case_slots` is recomputed, the project's `conservation_block_count` rises by one for the rejection itself, and posture therefore becomes `FROZEN_CONSERVATION`.

Alongside all four links, the material's `decided_at` is set, its contribution to `pending_material_count` is dropped, and `conditional_case_count` is recomputed. Every row the cascade touched — the material, each voided drawing, each reverted case, each released object, each zone whose slot count moved, and the project — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly one.

### 3.7. Shop Drawings and the Fabrication Ledger

A shop drawing is the sheet the shop actually builds from, and only a `FabricationLead` issues one. The payload names the owning commission with `project_id`, the primary material with `material_spec_id`, and exactly one subject: either a `subject_case_id` naming the case the sheet details or a `subject_interactive_id` naming the interactive it details. Supplying both, or neither, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The sheet identifies itself with a `drawing_number` of 1 to 40 characters and an optional `revision` index of at least 1 that defaults to 1; the pair of number and revision must be unique among the project's drawings counting only sheets that are not `VOID`, so a voided sheet frees its number for re-issue at the same revision, and a collision answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Pricing inputs are `complexity_tier` — `STANDARD`, `PRECISION` or `CONSERVATION_GRADE` — the estimated `shop_hours`, which must be greater than zero, the `material_area_m2` of sheet goods consumed, also greater than zero, and the `finish_grade` of `MILL`, `SATIN` or `MUSEUM_GLOSS`.

Three referential gates guard the issue. The named material must already be cleared: its status must be `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and a material still `SUBMITTED` or in `CONSERVATOR_REVIEW`, or one that has been `REJECTED`, answers `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_CONSERVATION_APPROVED` (HTTP 422) — you may not build from a material nobody has cleared. A named subject case must stand at `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, or the issue answers `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A named subject interactive must have got past its prototype: its status must be `PROTOTYPE_APPROVED`, `FABRICATED` or `COMMISSIONED`, and anything earlier answers `ERR_PROTOTYPE_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). Issuing a sheet also requires the project to be in `FABRICATION` and not frozen.

The cost model has three line items, and **each is rounded to whole cents before the three are summed**. Labour is the hours multiplied by the shop rate for the sheet's complexity tier multiplied by a labour multiplier: the shop charges 95 dollars per hour at `STANDARD`, 145 dollars per hour at `PRECISION` and 185 dollars per hour at `CONSERVATION_GRADE`, recorded as 9,500, 14,500 and 18,500 cents per hour respectively. Material is the `material_area_m2` multiplied by the rate for the **material's** `used_in` assembly multiplied by a waste factor, where the rates per square metre are 42,500 cents for `CASE_INTERIOR`, 31,000 for `CASE_EXTERIOR`, 58,000 for `MOUNT`, 12,500 for `GRAPHIC` and 47,500 for `INTERACTIVE_HOUSING`. Finishing is a percentage of the **already-rounded** material line item — 0 percent for `MILL`, 7.5 percent for `SATIN` and 13.5 percent for `MUSEUM_GLOSS` — and it too is rounded to whole cents. The total is the plain sum of the three rounded line items. Summing the three unrounded figures and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer, and each line item participates in the rounding independently. Each computation reads exactly one drawing row plus the material row its `material_spec_id` names; sibling drawings never participate.

That model runs twice in a sheet's life with different inputs. At issue it runs as a **schematic estimate**: the hours are the submitted `shop_hours`, the labour multiplier is the schematic contingency of 1.15, and the waste factor is the schematic allowance of 1.20. The three results land in `estimate_labor_cents`, `estimate_material_cents` and `estimate_finishing_cents`, with `estimate_total_cents` as their sum, and **no money moves** — the estimate is advisory. The sheet is stored in `SUBMITTED` status with `submitted_by_user_id` from the caller's header, `void_reason` null, and every actual figure — `actual_shop_hours`, `actual_waste_factor`, `actual_labor_cents`, `actual_material_cents`, `actual_finishing_cents` and `actual_cost_cents` — still null.

A `ProjectManager` takes a submitted sheet into review, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; any other starting status answers `ERR_INVALID_DRAWING_STATE`. The ruling that follows is also the `ProjectManager`'s, and it is where the money moves. The `decision` is `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_AS_NOTED` or `REJECT`, and a free-text `note` may accompany it — the `note` is checked to be a string when it is present, with no length bound of any kind, and is then discarded rather than stored, so it appears on no sheet and in no response. The deciding caller may not be the sheet's submitter. Only sheets in `UNDER_REVIEW` may be decided.

Both approving branches require `actual_shop_hours` and `actual_waste_factor` in the body — the shop's real figures — and their absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. With them the cost model runs again as the **shop-drawing actual**: the hours are the supplied `actual_shop_hours`, the labour multiplier is exactly 1.00 because the schematic contingency is *dropped* rather than re-based, and the waste factor is the supplied `actual_waste_factor`. The three results land in `actual_labor_cents`, `actual_material_cents` and `actual_finishing_cents`, and their sum in `actual_cost_cents`. That total must fit inside the project's `uncommitted_cents`, or the ruling is refused with `ERR_FABRICATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402); because fiscal capacity is the last thing checked, a request that also breaks a conservation or state rule never reports a budget error. On success the amount moves out of `uncommitted_cents` and into `committed_fabrication_cents`, the project's `updated_at` and `version` advance, and the sheet's `version` advances.

The two approving branches differ only in what conservation status they will accept and what they record. `APPROVE` demands that the sheet's material be `APPROVED` outright and lands the sheet in `APPROVED`. `APPROVE_AS_NOTED` accepts a material that is either `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` and lands the sheet in `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`. A material cleared only with conditions can therefore never carry a sheet past `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`, and an `APPROVE` against such a material is refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_CONSERVATION_APPROVED`. The money effect of the two branches is identical; only the conservation status recorded on the sheet differs. A `REJECT` requires none of the actual figures, is unaffected by the material's status, lands the sheet in `REJECTED`, and moves no money at all — `committed_fabrication_cents` is untouched.

Voiding is the compensating half of approval, and either a `ProjectManager` or a `FabricationLead` may do it, supplying a `void_reason` that is required and must not be blank. A sheet in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED` becomes `VOID`; a sheet that is already `VOID` cannot be voided again and answers `ERR_INVALID_DRAWING_STATE`, and neither can a `REJECTED` one. When the sheet's `actual_cost_cents` is non-null, exactly that amount is released from `committed_fabrication_cents` back into `uncommitted_cents` — a reversal, not a recomputation, so no cent drifts. When the sheet names a subject case that stands at `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, that case reverts to `DRAFT`, gives back its zone case slot, clears its `conditions`, falls back from `MOUNT_FABRICATED` to `MOUNT_DESIGN` if its mount had got that far, and releases every `RESERVED` object in it to `UNPLACED`, in ascending `created_at` order, with `assigned_case_id` cleared, leaving any object already `PLACED` untouched so that `placed_artifact_count` falls only to the number still `PLACED`. Voiding a sheet still in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` **through this endpoint** moves no money and reverts no case — this suppression of the case revert belongs to the operation the caller asked for, and does not carry into the material-rejection cascade, whose link three of §3.6 applies its case revert to every sheet it voids irrespective of which of the four statuses that sheet was voided out of. The project's `conditional_case_count` and posture are recomputed, and every touched row — the sheet, the case, each released object, the zone and the project — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one.

Drawings can be read individually with both their estimated and, once decided, their actual cost breakdown. The project-scoped list accepts a `status` filter; the global list accepts any combination of `project_id`, `material_spec_id`, `subject_case_id`, `subject_interactive_id` and `status`, and is the reverse-lookup channel from a case or an interactive back to the sheets that detail it.

### 3.8. Interactives: Durability Index, Prototypes and Warranty

An interactive is a hands-on station — a touchscreen, a crank-and-gear mechanism, a projection, an audio post or a headset rig — and either a `FabricationLead` or a `Curator` may specify one while the project is in `INTERPRETIVE`, `SCHEMATIC` or `DESIGN_DEV`. The specification carries a `code` of 1 to 40 characters unique among the project's interactives, a `kind` of `TOUCHSCREEN`, `MECHANICAL`, `PROJECTION`, `AUDIO` or `AR_HEADSET`, and an `objective_code` that must be one of the strings in the project's `objective_codes` list, so no station exists outside the interpretive plan — a code outside that array is a malformed request answering `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, judged with the rest of the body's shape and therefore ahead of both the code-uniqueness and the phase gate. Duty data follows: `expected_daily_uses` of at least 1, `duty_hours_per_day` greater than zero and no more than 24, a manufacturer's `mtbf_cycles` of at least 1,000, `parts_availability_years` between 0 and 30 inclusive, and a proposed `warranty_months` between 0 and 120 inclusive. The new row is stored at status `SPECIFIED` with `prototype_iteration` zero, `submitted_by_user_id` from the caller, and `durability_index_verified`, `measured_daily_uses` and `measured_mtbf_cycles` all null.

The durability index is a 0-to-100 score, higher meaning more robust, and it is assembled from three penalties subtracted from a perfect 100. First the wear ratio: the daily uses multiplied by `duty_hours_per_day`, multiplied by the 320 operating days the museum counts in a year, divided by the mean cycles between failures. The wear penalty is 0.55 multiplied by the smaller of 60.0 and the wear ratio expressed as a percentage — that is, the ratio multiplied by 100. **The cap makes the penalty non-linear**: once the ratio passes 0.60 the wear penalty saturates at 33.0 and a station that is twice as hard-worn scores no worse than one merely at the cap. Second the kind penalty: 0.9 multiplied by the fragility of the kind, where the fragilities are 18 for `TOUCHSCREEN`, 26 for `MECHANICAL`, 14 for `PROJECTION`, 8 for `AUDIO` and 34 for `AR_HEADSET`. Third the parts penalty: five points for every year by which `parts_availability_years` falls short of ten, floored at zero, so eleven years of parts supply is penalised exactly as much as ten. The index is 100 less the three penalties plus a prototype credit, capped above at 100.0, and rounded half up to two decimal places. It is never floored, so a fragile, hard-worn, orphan-parts station can carry a negative index and that negative number is what is stored.

That formula runs in two variants. The **specification** variant uses `expected_daily_uses` and the catalogue `mtbf_cycles` with a prototype credit of zero, and writes `durability_index_spec`. The **verified** variant uses the bench-measured `measured_daily_uses` and `measured_mtbf_cycles` with a prototype credit of 8 points — the reward for having actually built and tested the thing — and writes `durability_index_verified`; because the credit can push a strong station past a perfect score, this variant is where the cap at 100.0 bites. The `maintenance_tier` is then derived from `durability_index_verified` when that field is non-null and from `durability_index_spec` otherwise, with inclusive lower boundaries: at least 80 is `LOW`, at least 60 is `MEDIUM`, at least 40 is `HIGH`, and anything below 40 is `CRITICAL`. A verified index of exactly 60.00 is therefore `MEDIUM`, not `HIGH`.

An interactive walks `SPECIFIED`, then `PROTOTYPE_SUBMITTED`, then either `PROTOTYPE_APPROVED` or `PROTOTYPE_REJECTED`, then `FABRICATED`, then `COMMISSIONED`. A `FabricationLead` submits a prototype from `SPECIFIED` or from `PROTOTYPE_REJECTED` — the rejected branch is the rework loop and may be walked any number of times — while the project is in `DESIGN_DEV` or `FABRICATION` and not frozen. The submission carries the bench figures `measured_daily_uses`, of at least 1, and `measured_mtbf_cycles`, of at least 1,000; it increments `prototype_iteration` by one and records the submitting user in `submitted_by_user_id`. Any other starting status answers `ERR_INVALID_INTERACTIVE_STATE`.

The prototype ruling belongs to a `ProjectManager` or a `Conservator`, carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `note` which, exactly like the shop-drawing ruling's, is validated as a string and then discarded rather than recorded anywhere, requires status `PROTOTYPE_SUBMITTED`, requires the project not be frozen, and forbids the submitter from ruling on their own bench work. `APPROVE` computes the verified index from the measured figures and re-derives `maintenance_tier` from it, then lands the station at `PROTOTYPE_APPROVED`; because the verified variant carries the prototype credit, an approval usually raises the index and can relax the tier by a whole band. `REJECT` lands the station at `PROTOTYPE_REJECTED` and deliberately leaves `measured_daily_uses`, `measured_mtbf_cycles` and `durability_index_verified` untouched, so the `maintenance_tier` keeps deriving from `durability_index_spec` — a rejected bench test never earns the credit.

Marking a station built is the `FabricationLead`'s, requires status exactly `PROTOTYPE_APPROVED` — anything else, including a station still `SPECIFIED`, answers `ERR_PROTOTYPE_NOT_APPROVED` — requires the project to be in `FABRICATION` or `INSTALLATION` and not frozen, and lands it at `FABRICATED`.

Commissioning is the `ProjectManager`'s and is the warranty gate. It requires status `FABRICATED`, answering `ERR_INVALID_INTERACTIVE_STATE` otherwise, and requires the project to be in `COMMISSIONING`, answering `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_PHASE` otherwise. The body may carry a `warranty_months`, an integer between 0 and 120 inclusive exactly as at specification, that **replaces the stored value before the check is applied**, which is how a supplier's improved term is recorded at the last moment; omitting it checks the stored value. That supplied term is written back to the row only once every precondition has passed, so a commissioning refused for any reason at all leaves `warranty_months`, `status`, `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were. No punch item of severity `BLOCKING` may still be `OPEN` against this station, or commissioning answers `ERR_BLOCKING_PUNCH_ITEMS_OPEN` — the blocking-defect scan is scoped to this interactive alone, not to the project. Finally the term must meet the floor its derived `maintenance_tier` demands: 12 months at `LOW`, 24 at `MEDIUM`, 36 at `HIGH` and 60 at `CRITICAL`, with a term exactly equal to the floor accepted and anything shorter refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_TERM_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). The tier that governs is the one derived at the moment of the check, so a prototype approval that lifted the index also lowered the warranty the museum must extract. On success the station lands at `COMMISSIONED`. The project-scoped interactive list accepts `status` and `maintenance_tier` filters, and an interactive may be read individually with both index figures and its derived tier.

### 3.9. The Install Calendar, Commissioning Punch List and Closeout Settlement

Installation work happens in scheduled windows on a named gallery zone, and either a `FabricationLead` or a `ProjectManager` may request one while the project is in `FABRICATION` or `INSTALLATION` and not frozen. The request carries `project_id`, the `zone_id` of the gallery it occupies — which must belong to the same project, or the request answers `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE` — a `work_class` of `QUIET`, `NOISY`, `DUST_GENERATING` or `CRANE_ACCESS`, the anticipated `noise_dba` between 0 and 130 inclusive, the nominal `starts_at` and `ends_at` instants, and an optional `requires_gallery_closure` flag that defaults to false. The `ends_at` must be strictly after the `starts_at`, and a zero-length or inverted range answers `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The window is stored at status `REQUESTED` with `requested_by_user_id` from the caller and `denial_reason` null. Requesting a window does not change the zone's `occupancy_state` — the gallery stays open until work actually starts — but a merely `REQUESTED` window already occupies the zone for conflict purposes, so the first request in a slot blocks the second even before anyone approves it.

Every window carries two buffers derived from its `work_class` at creation. The `pre_access_buffer_minutes` — the time the crew needs to protect objects and lay down floor covering before the nominal start — is 0 for `QUIET`, 15 for `NOISY`, 30 for `DUST_GENERATING` and 45 for `CRANE_ACCESS`. The `settling_buffer_minutes` — the time airborne dust and noise need to clear before visitors return — is 0 for `QUIET`, 30 for `NOISY`, 120 for `DUST_GENERATING` and 60 for `CRANE_ACCESS`, except that for `DUST_GENERATING` work the buffer is the **larger** of that 120-minute floor and the zone's own `dust_settling_buffer_minutes`, so a zone with a slow air exchange lengthens the window it hosts. The `effective_start_at` is the `starts_at` moved earlier by the pre-access buffer and the `effective_end_at` is the `ends_at` moved later by the settling buffer; these two effective instants, not the nominal ones, are what the calendar reasons about.

Two windows on the same zone conflict when the interval each defines with its effective instants overlaps the other's, and the comparison is **half-open**: they conflict when the new window's `effective_start_at` is strictly before the other's `effective_end_at` and the other's `effective_start_at` is strictly before the new window's `effective_end_at`. Touching edges therefore do not conflict, which is why two `QUIET` windows, whose buffers are both zero, may sit back to back with a zero-minute gap, while two `DUST_GENERATING` windows an hour apart do conflict because each has spread two hours past its nominal end. The occupying set is every other window on the same zone — excluding the window being judged — whose status is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; a `DENIED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` window never occupies its zone, so cancelling one immediately frees the slot for a rival that was blocked a moment earlier.

A gallery closure is scarcer than a zone slot, because closures are counted across the whole project rather than per zone. The closure-capacity check considers every window in the project whose `requires_gallery_closure` is true and whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, together with the window currently being approved, and it uses each window's **nominal** `starts_at` and `ends_at` with no buffers at all. It sweeps the endpoints in ascending timestamp order, adding one at each start and subtracting one at each end, and **at an equal timestamp every start is processed before every end**, with ties broken by the owning window's `created_at` ascending. That ordering makes the interval closed rather than half-open, so a closure beginning at exactly the instant another ends *does* count as concurrent — the precise opposite of the per-zone rule, and the two geometries must not be conflated. The peak is the highest running total the sweep reaches, and when it exceeds the project's `max_concurrent_closures`, which defaults to 2, the approval is refused.

Only `MuseumOperations` rules on a window, and the caller may not be the requester. The window must be `REQUESTED`, or the ruling answers `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`, and the project must not be frozen. The `decision` is `APPROVE` or `DENY`, with an optional `denial_reason` recorded on a denial. On an approval four checks run in a fixed order. First noise: the window's `noise_dba` must be no greater than the zone's `max_noise_dba`, or the ruling answers `ERR_NOISE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) — **unless** `requires_gallery_closure` is true, in which case the ceiling does not apply at all, because with the gallery closed there are no visitors to disturb. Second occupancy: the zone's `occupancy_state` must not be `CLOSED`, and if the window asks for a closure the zone's `closure_allowed` must be true, or the ruling answers `ERR_ZONE_CLOSED_FOR_WORK` (HTTP 422) — a zone on a required circulation route can never be closed no matter who asks. Third the per-zone conflict, answering `ERR_ZONE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Fourth, and only for a closure request, the project-wide capacity sweep, answering `ERR_CLOSURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Because capacity is checked last, a closure window that also collides with a neighbour on its own zone reports the conflict, not the capacity.

`MuseumOperations` starts an `APPROVED` window, which requires the project to be in `INSTALLATION` and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the window's `starts_at` — starting early answers `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`. The window becomes `IN_PROGRESS` and the zone's `occupancy_state` changes: to `CLOSED` when `requires_gallery_closure` is true, and to `PARTIAL_CLOSURE` otherwise. Completing an `IN_PROGRESS` window is also `MuseumOperations`', and it is the moment collection objects become truly installed. Every case in the window's zone that holds at least one `RESERVED` object must have reached `MOUNT_INSTALLED`, or completion is refused with `ERR_MOUNT_NOT_INSTALLED` (HTTP 409) — you may not hand the gallery back with objects sitting in cases whose mounts are not in. On success the window becomes `COMPLETED`, the zone returns to `OPERATING`, and every `RESERVED` object whose case sits in this zone becomes `PLACED`, processed in ascending order of the case's `created_at` and then the object's `created_at` so the resulting sequence is deterministic. A window may also be cancelled by `MuseumOperations` or the `FabricationLead` from `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, with an optional `denial_reason`; it becomes `CANCELLED` and stops occupying its zone. The project-scoped and zone-scoped window lists accept `status`, and the project-scoped list also accepts `zone_id` and `requires_gallery_closure`.

Commissioning collects defects as punch items. `MuseumOperations`, a `Curator`, a `ProjectManager` or an `AccessibilitySpecialist` may raise one while the project is in `COMMISSIONING`, giving a `severity` of `BLOCKING`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR` and a `description` of 1 to 500 characters that must not be blank. The item may point at one subject — either an `interactive_id` or a `case_id`, both of which must belong to the same project — but naming both at once is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and naming neither is a general project-level defect. The item is stored `OPEN` with `raised_by_role` set from the caller's role header and `raised_by_user_id` from the caller, and a `BLOCKING` item increments the project's `blocking_punch_count`.

A defect is cleared by verification or by waiver. A `FabricationLead` or a `ProjectManager` verifies an `OPEN` item while the project is in `COMMISSIONING` or `CLOSEOUT`, supplying a `verification_note` that is required and must not be blank; the caller may not be the person who raised it, the item becomes `VERIFIED`, `verified_by_user_id`, `verification_note` and `verified_at` are recorded, and a `BLOCKING` item decrements `blocking_punch_count`. A `ProjectManager` waives an `OPEN` item with a `waiver_reason`, likewise required and non-blank, again while the project is in `COMMISSIONING` or `CLOSEOUT`, but only when the severity is `MAJOR` or `MINOR`: **a `BLOCKING` defect can never be waived** and the attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_PUNCH_STATE`, which is also the answer when the item is not `OPEN`. A waived item becomes `WAIVED` with its `waiver_reason` and `verified_at` recorded. The project-scoped punch list accepts `status` and `severity`; the global list accepts `project_id`, `interactive_id`, `case_id`, `status` and `severity`.

Closeout is the last act, it belongs to the `ProjectManager`, and it settles the money. The project must be in `CLOSEOUT`; its `posture` must be exactly `OPEN`, so a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN_CONSERVATION` commission cannot be settled and answers `ERR_PROJECT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`; and `committed_fabrication_cents` must be greater than zero, or there is nothing to settle and the request answers `ERR_NOTHING_TO_SETTLE` (HTTP 409). The body carries the handover artefacts: a `docent_kit_url` and a `maintenance_manual_url`, each a non-blank string of 1 to 500 characters, and the `parts_commitment_years` the museum has secured, an integer of 0 or more carrying no upper bound.

The settlement is a **pinned seven-step waterfall**. Each step consumes the output of the step before it, no step is ever recomputed against a later figure, and the waterfall is never iterated. Step one totals fabrication: the sum of `actual_cost_cents` over every drawing in the project whose status is `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`. This is integer addition, so its result is order-independent, and `VOID` and `REJECTED` sheets contribute nothing. Step two prices the parts commitment as a percentage of that fabrication total, and the percentage is chosen from the project's `COMMISSIONED` interactives: if the project has none, the percentage is zero; otherwise take the **smallest** `parts_availability_years` among them — the weakest station governs the whole commission — and select the first band it satisfies, reading the bands in descending order of their threshold, where at least 15 years gives 0.5 percent, at least 10 years gives 1.2 percent, at least 5 years gives 2.8 percent and anything from 0 years up gives 4.5 percent. The parts premium is the fabrication total multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three adds steps one and two into a pre-retainage total.

Step four picks the retainage percentage from that pre-retainage total on the same first-band-satisfied basis: a total of at least 20,000,000 cents retains 5 percent, at least 5,000,000 cents retains 7.5 percent, and anything from zero up retains 10 percent, so a larger job is trusted with a thinner hold-back and a total sitting exactly on a boundary takes the lower rate. Step five is the retainage itself: the pre-retainage total multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Step six is the warranty reserve: two percent of the pre-retainage total **less the retainage already withheld**, rounded half up to whole cents — the reserve is deliberately computed on the net figure, not on the gross, so the retainage band choice also moves the reserve. Step seven is the release: the pre-retainage total less the retainage less the warranty reserve, by exact integer subtraction with no rounding of its own, which is what guarantees the three settlement figures reconstruct the pre-retainage total to the cent.

The results are written to `retainage_held_cents`, `warranty_reserve_cents` and `released_cents`. The `committed_fabrication_cents` bucket is zeroed, and `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed as the `contract_value_cents` less the sum of the three settlement figures, so the five-bucket ledger still reconciles exactly after settlement. The handover fields `docent_kit_url`, `maintenance_manual_url` and `parts_commitment_years` are stored, `warranty_registered_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and every punch item standing at `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED` is transitioned to `CLOSED` — items still `OPEN` are left alone, which is why a project carrying a blocking defect cannot have reached this phase in the first place. Finally the phase advances from `CLOSEOUT` to `WARRANTY_CLOSED` and the project's `version` is incremented by one. `WARRANTY_CLOSED` is terminal: nothing further may be written to the commission.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the API root and requires the `X-User-Id` and `X-User-Role` headers described in section 2.1, the logical-clock endpoint alone excepted. Creation endpoints — those that bring a new resource into being — answer HTTP 201 on success; the placement of §3.4 counts as one of them and answers 201 even though its `data` carries the updated collection object rather than a row of its own, and so does a panel vote, which answers 201 with the updated round in `data`. Each success body carries the created or updated resource in `data`, except the list endpoints, which carry an array together with the pagination `meta` block.
