# Adaptive & Inclusive Recreation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Municipal and non-profit recreation departments that serve disabled participants run several programmes at once that most scheduling software treats as unrelated: an intake and assessment practice that decides how much human support a person needs; an activity calendar whose staffing ratios are derived from that support need rather than from a fixed head-count; an adaptive-equipment cage whose chairs, harnesses and flotation devices carry weight ratings and inspection cycles; an accessible transport fleet whose wheelchair securement positions cannot be swapped for ordinary seats; a sliding-scale fee table backed by restricted grant money that must be encumbered before it is spent; and a caregiver-consent file that legally gates whether a person may get in the pool at all. When these are managed separately, the failure mode is always the same — a session that looks full on paper is actually unstaffed, a family is billed for a place that a lapsed consent quietly voided, or a grant is over-committed because two officers drew on it from different screens.

This document specifies the Adaptive & Inclusive Recreation API, a single back-end that holds all seven concerns in one consistent store and refuses any write that would break the relationship between them. Every resource is mounted under the `/recreation` prefix. The system is, at heart, an allocation engine wrapped in an audit trail: program slots, dedicated one-to-one support staffers, adaptive equipment units, vehicle positions and scholarship cents are five separate scarce quantities, each conserved by an explicit invariant, and each released back to its pool by a compensating action whenever a commitment is undone. Layered on top of that allocation core is a compliance posture — an operating period that can be restricted, frozen or closed, a segregation-of-duties rule between the officer who awards money and the officer who pays it out, and a strictly ordered validation ladder that decides which of seventy possible rejections a bad request earns.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows and should be read as governing whenever a narrower rule seems ambiguous. First, **order is part of the contract**: validations are numbered and evaluated in that number order, cascades are numbered by hop, rounding steps are applied in a pinned sequence, and list results are sorted deterministically. Second, **the two members of a pair are never interchangeable**: an accessible securement position is not an ambulatory seat, a dedicated one-to-one staffer is not a program slot, an encumbrance is not a disbursement, and a certification that is valid today is not necessarily valid on the session date. Implementations that collapse either pair will produce plausible-looking numbers that are wrong.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the parts of the contract that never vary by workflow: who may call, what a response looks like, how lists are paged, how time is told, how versions move, and in what order a request is judged.

### 2.1. Program Staff, Families and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. A request missing or carrying an empty `X-User-Role` is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER` (401); a request missing or carrying an empty `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID_HEADER` (401); the role header is checked before the id header. A role string that is not one of the six names below is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401), and a syntactically valid role that is not authorised for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `ActivityLead`, `FundingOfficer`, `TransportDispatcher` and `CaregiverUser`.

- A `ProgramAdmin` configures the programme year: operating periods, sliding-scale fee bands, activity programmes, sessions, staff members, certifications and the equipment catalogue, and is the only role that may drive the logical clock. A `ProgramAdmin` deliberately may not enrol a participant, sign a consent, or move a single cent.
- An `IntakeCoordinator` owns the human file — participants, caregivers, support assessments, consents, goals — and owns the enrolment roster. This role may not create money, staff a session, or drive the clock.
- An `ActivityLead` is a staff member who actually runs sessions: opening them, proposing and confirming assignments, locking staffing, delivering, recording attendance, issuing and receiving equipment, and rating goals.
- A `FundingOfficer` owns funding sources, scholarship awards, disbursements, releases and family payments.
- A `TransportDispatcher` owns transport runs and the two independent position pools on each vehicle.
- A `CaregiverUser` is a caregiver acting on behalf of exactly one participant.

Two of these roles are entity-backed and four are opaque, and the difference matters for every ownership check. When `X-User-Role` is `ActivityLead`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`; ownership and duty checks on any resource that references a staff member compare that resource's staff foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CaregiverUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Caregiver`; every resource that names a participant, an enrolment, a consent or a goal is compared against the `participant_id` recorded on that caregiver's own row. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `FundingOfficer` or `TransportDispatcher`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition asks for it — as `awarded_by_user_id` when a `FundingOfficer` creates a scholarship award and as `disbursed_by_user_id` when a (different) `FundingOfficer` pays one out — but it never compares such an identifier to any row's `id`.

Three further access rules follow from that identity mapping. An entity-backed role whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing row — no such `StaffMember` for an `ActivityLead`, no such `Caregiver` for a `CaregiverUser` — is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). A `CaregiverUser` that reaches for a resource belonging to any participant other than the one named on its own caregiver row is rejected with `ERR_CAREGIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). And an `ActivityLead` performing a delivery-time operation — delivering a session, recording attendance, checking equipment out, receiving equipment back, or recording a goal rating — must itself be staffed on that session: its `StaffMember` id must appear on a `StaffAssignment` for the session whose status is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `DELIVERED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_SESSION` (403).

Finally, the money desk is split. The `FundingOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` was stamped onto a scholarship award as `awarded_by_user_id` may never be the one who disburses it; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The same officer may, however, encumber and release the award — the prohibition covers payment out only.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, List Ordering and Filters

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the ordered result; when it is omitted it defaults to 0, and a supplied value must be 0 or greater. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before paging and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is still below `total_count`.

Unless a specific workflow says otherwise, every list endpoint returns rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows carry an identical `created_at`, the earlier-inserted row is returned first; the resource `id` is never used as a sort key or a tie-break anywhere in this system. Ordering is applied before paging, never after.

The filter parameters on a list endpoint are combined with logical AND, and any filter that is omitted is simply not applied. A filter parameter whose value is not a declared member of the enumeration it filters on is **not** an error on a read: the filter matches nothing, so the endpoint returns an empty array with `total_count` 0 and `has_more` false. The strict enumeration check described later in this document applies to write payloads, not to read filters.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Epoch Checkpoint

Nothing in this system reads the operating-system wall clock to decide whether something has expired. Instead the server keeps a **logical clock**, an ISO 8601 UTC instant that begins its life at exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` and that changes only when a `ProgramAdmin` sets it. There is no fallback to real time: if the clock has never been set, every age, expiry, cutoff and window comparison in this document is made against `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. Where a rule speaks of "the logical clock date" it means the calendar date of that instant in UTC.

Setting the clock takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the new instant, and the endpoint answers with `current_utc` plus five counters describing what the move swept up: `consents_expired`, `certifications_expired`, `assignments_lapsed`, `sessions_unlocked` and `items_inspection_due`. The value reported in `current_utc` is the string the caller supplied, unchanged; before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`.

Moving the clock is also the system's only **epoch checkpoint**, and the three sweeps it runs happen in a fixed order. First, consent expiry: every `ConsentArtifact` whose status is `SIGNED` and whose `expires_on` falls strictly before the new logical clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, and `consents_expired` counts those rows. Second, certification expiry: every `StaffCertification` whose status is `VALID` and whose `expires_on` falls strictly before the new clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, counted in `certifications_expired`, and each such expiry runs the certification-lapse cascade described in §3.5 — the assignments it knocks down are counted in `assignments_lapsed` and the sessions it forces back open are counted in `sessions_unlocked`. Third, equipment inspection: every `EquipmentItem` whose `item_kind` is `SERIALIZED`, whose `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE` and whose `inspection_due_date` falls strictly before the new clock date moves to `INSPECTION_DUE`, counted in `items_inspection_due`. Pooled items have no inspection cycle and are never touched by this third sweep.

The clock endpoint is the one mutating operation that is exempt from the global write gate, so an administrator can always advance time even in a frozen or closed period. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and performs the same three sweeps; because each sweep only advances rows whose expiry now lies in the past, a backwards move never revives an already-expired row.

### 2.5. Operating Periods, the Global Write Gate and the Financial-Write Gate

An `OperatingPeriod` is a named fiscal window with an inclusive first date `starts_on` and an inclusive last date `ends_on`, where `ends_on` must be the same as or later than `starts_on`. Its `name` is a human label such as a season or fiscal year. Its posture axis lives in `status`, which begins at `OPEN` and takes one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`, `CLOSING` or `CLOSED`.

**Resolving the current period.** Many rules ask what the current posture is. The current operating period is the row whose `starts_on` is on or before the logical clock date and whose `ends_on` is on or after it. When more than one row qualifies, rank them by `starts_on` descending and break ties by `created_at` ascending, taking the first. When no row qualifies at all — including the case where no period has ever been created — there is no current period and neither the global write gate nor the financial-write gate fires.

**The global write gate.** When the current period's `status` is `FROZEN` or `CLOSED`, every mutating endpoint is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` (409). Two mutating endpoints are exempt and continue to work: setting the logical clock, and moving a period along its own posture axis (`POST /recreation/operating-periods/{period_id}/posture`) — the second exemption exists so that a frozen period can always be thawed. All `GET` endpoints are exempt, since the gate covers writes only. `CLOSED` is a terminal posture and the gate treats it exactly as it treats `FROZEN`; to resume writing after closing the current period, either move the logical clock outside that period's date range or create a later period that covers the clock date.

**The financial-write gate.** When the current period's `status` is `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSING`, ordinary programme work continues but seven financial endpoints are rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_RESTRICTED` (409): creating a funding source, closing a funding source, creating a scholarship award, encumbering an award, disbursing an award, releasing an award, and recording a family payment against an enrolment charge. `CLOSING` behaves exactly like `RESTRICTED` for this purpose; the difference between them is only that `CLOSING` is the posture from which a period may be closed.

**Cascades are not re-gated.** Both gates are admission controls on an incoming request. Once a request has been admitted, every side effect it fires — including cascade hops that themselves transition rows on other entities — runs to completion without being re-tested against the gate. A cascade therefore never fails half-way because a posture check would have rejected it as a standalone call.

### 2.6. Identifiers, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. Two entities are the exception and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all: `FeeScaleBand`, which is immutable configuration once written, and `GoalRating`, which is an append-only observation. Clients must not expect either field on those two, and no rule in this document ever bumps a version on them.

`created_at` and `updated_at` are minted from the server's own UTC clock — **not** from the logical clock — and are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`. Instants that the caller supplies (a session's `start_at` and `end_at`, an assessment's `effective_from`, a pickup window's `pickup_start_at` and `pickup_end_at`, the clock's `to_iso8601`) are stored and echoed back **byte-for-byte as the caller wrote them**; the server does not normalise, re-render or re-zone them, so a caller who writes `2026-06-01T14:00:00Z` reads back `2026-06-01T14:00:00Z` and not `2026-06-01T14:00:00.000Z`. Comparisons between such instants are made on the instants they denote, not on their strings, so two spellings of the same moment compare equal. Date-typed fields (`starts_on`, `ends_on`, `date_of_birth`, `assessed_on`, `expires_on`, `issued_on`, `signed_on`, `inspection_due_date`, `review_due_on`, `new_expires_on`) are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates. Where the server stamps a field from the logical clock — an enrolment's `promoted_at`, a consent's `signed_on`, an item's recomputed `inspection_due_date` — it writes the logical clock's current instant or date exactly as that clock currently holds it. A `GoalRating`'s `rated_session_start_at` is a verbatim copy of the rated session's `start_at` string.

`version` is an integer that starts at 1 on every newly created row and rises by exactly 1 each time that row is mutated. The rule for multi-row requests is: **a request bumps each affected row once, by one, no matter how many cascade hops touch it.** A single enrolment withdrawal that walks eight cascade hops and rewrites the same session three times leaves that session's `version` exactly one higher than it found it. Every version bump is accompanied by a refresh of that row's `updated_at`. Recomputing a derived figure counts as a mutation for this purpose: when a session's staffing counters are re-derived, or an enrolment charge's money lines are recomputed, that row's `version` moves and its `updated_at` refreshes.

Several creates deliberately bump a *parent* row as well, and these bumps are what make the concurrency scheme in §2.9 usable. Creating a program session bumps its `ActivityProgram`. Creating an enrolment or a staff assignment bumps the `ProgramSession`. Creating a support assessment bumps the `Participant`. Creating an equipment reservation bumps the `EquipmentItem`. Creating a transport booking bumps the `TransportRun`. Creating a scholarship award bumps the `FundingSource`.

**Response projection.** Unless a specific endpoint declares a computed read shape, the object returned in `data` for a resource carries every field this document attributes to that entity — inputs, system-managed fields and computed figures alike — plus `id`, `created_at`, and (for all entities except `FeeScaleBand` and `GoalRating`) `updated_at` and `version`. Computed money lines, computed counters, computed tiers and computed flags are therefore all response-visible on their owning resource; a client never has to re-derive them. The serializer runs **after** the version bump and after every recomputation the request triggered, so a mutation response always shows the post-mutation `version` and the post-mutation values of every derived field on that row.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Existence Layer

Endpoints that address a nested or referenced resource validate identifiers left to right: identifiers in the URL path first, in the order they appear, and only then identifiers carried in the request body. The first identifier that does not resolve determines the error, and the request stops there. A request naming a session under a programme that does not exist therefore reports the missing programme, never the missing session.

The existence layer is itself ordered, and where one rung covers several resource families each family has its own error id. In rung order: a missing participant is `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`; a missing activity programme is `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; a missing program session is `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`; a missing enrolment is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` and a missing enrolment charge is `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND` at that same rung; a missing staff member is `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` for a missing certification and `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a missing assignment sharing that rung; a missing equipment item is `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` and a missing reservation `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`; a missing transport run is `ERR_TRANSPORT_RUN_NOT_FOUND` and a missing booking `ERR_TRANSPORT_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`; a missing funding source is `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, with `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND` for a missing award and `ERR_FEE_BAND_NOT_FOUND` for a missing or unmatched fee band; a missing consent is `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a missing support assessment and `ERR_CAREGIVER_NOT_FOUND` for a missing caregiver; a missing goal is `ERR_GOAL_NOT_FOUND` and a missing rating `ERR_RATING_NOT_FOUND`; and a missing operating period is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these carries HTTP 404.

### 2.8. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is held as whole integer cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in any response body. Wherever a computation produces a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded **half up** — a value exactly at the half rounds away from zero — and the rounded integer is what is stored.

Percentages and multipliers are expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points means a factor of exactly 1.0. A programme multiplier `fee_multiplier_bp` of 12000 therefore raises a band price by one fifth; a `sibling_discount_bp` of 1500 removes fifteen per cent; an award percentage `award_pct_bp` of 5000 awards half of net need. To apply a basis-point factor, multiply by the basis-point figure, divide by 10000, and round half up — in that order, once, at the point the specific formula says to round.

Rounding order is load-bearing in three places and each is pinned in the workflow that owns it: the series-price division in §3.3 floors before it distributes a remainder; the staffing formula in §3.5 rounds the risk-weighted point total up to whole points *before* dividing by staff capacity and rounding up again; and the family-responsibility formula in §3.8 either rounds each line separately or rounds the summed total, depending on the programme's declared policy. Implementations that reorder any of these three will differ by whole cents or whole staffers from the specification.

Distances are handled the same way. A transport run stores `route_miles_x10`, an integer number of *tenths* of a mile, so a route of eight and a half miles is stored as 85. The mileage fare formula multiplies in tenths and divides the tenths out before rounding.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Five creation endpoints allocate a scarce quantity out of a parent row whose balance the client read a moment earlier. Each of them therefore requires the client to echo back the parent's `version` as an ordinary, non-optional field in the request body. If the echoed number is not equal to the parent's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The point is not that two requests arrive together; it is that a client who has not observed the parent's latest state is not allowed to allocate from it.

- Creating an enrolment (`POST /recreation/enrollments`) must carry `expected_session_version` matching the current `version` of the `ProgramSession` named by `session_id`. That session's version advances on every enrolment create, confirm, withdrawal, promotion, demotion and staffing change, so a stale value means the client's picture of `slots_available`, `one_to_one_available` and `staffing_gap` is out of date.
- Creating a support assessment (`POST /recreation/support-assessments`) must carry `expected_participant_version` matching the current `version` of the `Participant` named by `participant_id`. Activating the new assessment will supersede whatever assessment is currently `ACTIVE`, and nothing in the state machine objects to that; only this check catches a writer who never saw the earlier supersession.
- Creating a scholarship award (`POST /recreation/scholarship-awards`) must carry `expected_funding_source_version` matching the current `version` of the `FundingSource` it draws on. The amount an award may claim is bounded by that source's `available_cents`, which moves on every encumbrance, disbursement and release.
- Creating an equipment reservation (`POST /recreation/equipment-reservations`) must carry `expected_item_version` matching the current `version` of the `EquipmentItem` named by `equipment_item_id`. That version advances on every hold, check-out, return, release, inspection and condition change.
- Creating a transport booking (`POST /recreation/transport-bookings`) must carry `expected_run_version` matching the current `version` of the `TransportRun` named by `transport_run_id`. Because the accessible and ambulatory counters move independently, a stale version leaves the client unable to know which of the two pools it is competing for.

No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind. In particular, none of the transition endpoints — confirm, withdraw, activate, encumber, release, lock, deliver — accepts or requires one.

When the award-creation client does not want to pick a source by hand, the funding-selection read described in §3.8 returns both the winning `funding_source_id` and the `funding_source_version` that must be echoed back as `expected_funding_source_version`.

### 2.10. The Ordered Validation Ladder

Every request is judged against one ladder of sixty numbered rungs, always evaluated in ascending rung order. The first rung that fails determines the response; no later rung is consulted. Rungs that do not apply to the endpoint being called are skipped without effect, which is why a single ladder can serve ninety endpoints. Six of the rungs are **sub-gate heads** and are marked below; the six sub-gates in ladder order are consent (A), assessment (B), capacity (C), equipment (D), transport (E) and funding (F), and both enrolment confirmation and system-driven waitlist promotion walk them in exactly that order.

1. `X-User-Role` is present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER` (401).
2. `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID_HEADER` (401).
3. `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
4. The named role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role set — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
5. For the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row (a `StaffMember` for `ActivityLead`, a `Caregiver` for `CaregiverUser`) — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). Skipped for the four opaque roles.
6. A `CaregiverUser` touches only resources belonging to the participant named on its own caregiver row, on every endpoint called as `CaregiverUser` that names a participant, an enrolment, a consent or a goal — otherwise `ERR_CAREGIVER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
7. For the five delivery-time operations (session deliver, enrolment attendance, equipment check-out, equipment return, goal rating), the acting `ActivityLead` holds a `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `DELIVERED` assignment on the session — otherwise `ERR_STAFF_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_SESSION` (403).
8. On award disbursement only, the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the award's `awarded_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. **The global write gate.** On every `POST`, `PATCH`, `PUT` and `DELETE` except setting the logical clock and moving a period's posture, the current operating period's `status` is neither `FROZEN` nor `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` (409). This rung sits above every existence check and every payload check, so a frozen period rejects a write that names a non-existent resource, and it sits below all eight authentication and authorisation rungs, so an unauthorised caller is still told it is unauthorised.
10. On the seven financial-write endpoints, the current period's `status` is neither `RESTRICTED` nor `CLOSING` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_RESTRICTED` (409).
11. Every referenced participant id resolves — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
12. Every referenced activity programme id resolves — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (404).
13. Every referenced program session id resolves — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` (404).
14. Every referenced enrolment id resolves, and at this same rung every referenced enrolment charge id resolves — `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
15. Every referenced staff member, certification or assignment id resolves — `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
16. Every referenced equipment item or reservation id resolves — `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` (404).
17. Every referenced transport run or booking id resolves — `ERR_TRANSPORT_RUN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSPORT_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` (404).
18. Every referenced funding source, scholarship award or fee-scale band id resolves, and the sliding-scale lookup performed during enrolment creation finds a covering band — `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FEE_BAND_NOT_FOUND` (404).
19. Every referenced consent, support assessment or caregiver id resolves — `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CAREGIVER_NOT_FOUND` (404).
20. Every referenced goal or goal-rating id resolves — `ERR_GOAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RATING_NOT_FOUND` (404).
21. Every referenced operating period id resolves — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` (404).
22. On every `POST`, `PATCH` and `PUT`, each field the endpoint declares required is present, carries the declared JSON type, and lies inside its declared numeric range — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). This rung is what enforces a rating `score` between 1 and 5, a `household_size` of at least 1, a session whose `end_at` is strictly later than its `start_at`, a period whose `ends_on` is on or after its `starts_on`, and every other range this document states.
23. Every enum-typed field in a write payload carries a declared member of that enumeration — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). The fields that route here are exactly the enum-typed inputs named in this document: `income_band`, `mobility_profile`, `guardianship_status`, `relationship`, `support_tier`, `consent_type`, `activity_type`, `risk_tier`, `pricing_mode`, `rounding_policy`, `waitlist_policy`, the members of `required_consent_types` and `required_certification_types`, `certification_type`, `assignment_role`, `item_kind`, `disposition`, `return_condition`, `position_type`, `fare_mode`, `source_kind`, `award_mode`, `mastery_mode`, `outcome`, `on_full` and `target_status`. No other field routes here; a malformed non-enum field is an `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) at rung 22 instead, and an out-of-enum *filter* on a read endpoint is not an error at all.
24. On every list endpoint, `limit` lies between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is 0 or greater — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
25. On the five concurrency-guarded creates, the supplied `expected_session_version`, `expected_participant_version`, `expected_funding_source_version`, `expected_item_version` or `expected_run_version` equals the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
26. On every state-transition endpoint, the target row is in a declared source state for the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
27. On every state-transition endpoint, the target row is not already in a terminal state of the axis being driven — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Because this rung sits below rung 26, a row in a non-terminal but wrong source state earns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while a row already parked in a terminal state earns `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.
28. On enrolment creation and enrolment confirmation, the enrolment's session has `status` `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_OPEN` (409).
29. On enrolment creation, the session's `start_at` is at least 24 hours after the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CUTOFF_PASSED` (422).
30. On transport-booking creation and goal-rating creation, cross-references cohere: a booking's enrolment and its run must serve the same session, and a rating's session must be one on which the goal's participant holds an enrolment at all — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_SESSION_MISMATCH` (422). Equipment fit is deliberately *not* tested here; it is sub-gate D at rungs 49 to 51, which keeps the fit, inspection and availability failures individually reachable.
31. On enrolment creation, the participant holds no other enrolment on the same session whose `enrollment_status` is `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409).
32. On goal-rating creation, the goal carries no existing rating for the same session — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_GOAL_RATING` (409).
33. On enrolment creation, on enrolment confirmation and on the waitlist-promotion walk, the participant's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
34. **Sub-gate A, head rung.** On enrolment confirmation and the promotion walk, every consent type in the programme's `required_consent_types` has a matching `SIGNED` consent artifact for the participant — otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_CONSENT_MISSING` (422).
35. **Sub-gate A, continued.** Each of those signed consents has an `expires_on` on or after the session's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` (422).
36. On consent signing, the signer satisfies the signer-authority rule for this participant and consent type — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_SIGNER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (422).
37. **Sub-gate B, head rung and only rung.** On enrolment confirmation and the promotion walk, the participant has a `SupportAssessment` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `assessed_on` plus 365 days falls on or after the session's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STALE` (422).
38. On staffing lock, when the programme's `requires_medication_admin` is true, the session carries at least one live assignment (status `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`) whose staff member holds a `MEDICATION_ADMIN` certification whose `expires_on` is on or after the session date — otherwise `ERR_MED_ADMIN_COVERAGE_MISSING` (422).
39. On assignment creation, assignment confirmation, assignment revalidation and staffing lock, the staff member holds a certification row for every type in the programme's `required_certification_types` — otherwise `ERR_STAFF_CERT_MISSING` (422).
40. On assignment creation only, every required certification has an `expires_on` on or after **today's** logical-clock date — otherwise `ERR_STAFF_CERT_EXPIRED_AT_ASSIGNMENT` (422).
41. On assignment confirmation, assignment revalidation and staffing lock, every required certification has an `expires_on` on or after the **session's** date — otherwise `ERR_STAFF_CERT_EXPIRES_BEFORE_SESSION` (422). This rung is deliberately not applied at assignment creation.
42. On assignment creation, the staffer has no colliding live assignment — otherwise `ERR_STAFF_TIME_CONFLICT` (409).
43. On enrolment creation, the participant has no colliding live enrolment — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_TIME_CONFLICT` (409).
44. On goal-rating creation, the goal's participant holds an enrolment on the rated session whose `enrollment_status` is `ATTENDED` — otherwise `ERR_RATING_SESSION_NOT_ATTENDED` (422).
45. **Sub-gate C, head rung.** On enrolment confirmation and the promotion walk, the session's `slots_available` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_FULL` (409), unless the confirm request asked for waitlist or deny routing, in which case this rung routes rather than raises.
46. **Sub-gate C, continued.** When the participant's effective tier at this session is `ONE_TO_ONE`, the session's `one_to_one_available` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_ONE_TO_ONE_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (409), with the same routing option as rung 45. The one-to-one pool is a separate counter from the programme slots and the two are never interchangeable.
47. On staffing lock, the session's recomputed `staffing_gap` is 0 — otherwise `ERR_STAFFING_GAP` (409).
48. On staffing unlock, the session's `unlock_count` is below 2 — otherwise `ERR_UNLOCK_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
49. **Sub-gate D, head rung.** On equipment-reservation creation, and on the confirmation/promotion walk when the programme declares a `required_equipment_model_code`, the item's `model_code` and `size_code` match the programme's requirement and its `weight_capacity_kg` is at least the participant's `weight_kg` — otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_FIT_MISMATCH` (422). Equal capacity fits.
50. **Sub-gate D, continued.** For a `SERIALIZED` item, its `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE` and its `inspection_due_date` is on or after the session's date — otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INSPECTION_OVERDUE` (409). Pooled items skip the inspection clause. This rung also guards equipment check-out.
51. **Sub-gate D, continued.** The item's `pooled_available` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE` (409). On the confirmation/promotion walk this rung instead requires the enrolment actually to hold a live reservation, as §3.6 explains.
52. **Sub-gate E, head rung.** On transport-booking creation, the requested `position_type` matches the participant's `mobility_profile` exactly — `WHEELCHAIR` requires `ACCESSIBLE` and `AMBULATORY` requires `AMBULATORY` — otherwise `ERR_POSITION_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422).
53. **Sub-gate E, continued.** On transport-booking creation, the run's pickup window is contained inside the arrival buffer — otherwise `ERR_TRANSPORT_WINDOW_MISMATCH` (422).
54. **Sub-gate E, continued.** For an accessible position, the run's `accessible_available` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_POSITIONS_FULL` (409). This rung is also walked during confirmation and promotion.
55. **Sub-gate E, continued.** For an ambulatory position, the run's `ambulatory_available` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_AMBULATORY_SEATS_FULL` (409), also walked during confirmation and promotion. A free accessible position never satisfies an ambulatory request, and the converse is equally true.
56. **Sub-gate F, head rung.** On award creation and award encumbrance, the named funding source's `status` is not `CLOSED` and its operating period's `status` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409). An `EXHAUSTED` but not closed source deliberately passes this rung and fails the next one, which is what keeps the insufficient-funds error reachable.
57. **Sub-gate F, continued.** On award creation and award encumbrance, the award amount is at least 1 cent and the source's `available_cents` is at least that amount; when no source was named, the funding selector returned a non-empty candidate set — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (409).
58. **Sub-gate F, continued.** On award creation, a supplied `requested_amount_cents` does not exceed the enrolment's `program_fee_cents` — otherwise `ERR_AWARD_EXCEEDS_NEED` (422).
59. On award disbursement, the supplied `amount_cents` is at least 1 and no greater than the award's `encumbered_cents` minus its `disbursed_cents` — otherwise `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_ENCUMBRANCE` (422).
60. **Sub-gate F, last rung of the ladder.** On enrolment creation, enrolment confirmation and the promotion walk, the participant carries no enrolment charge whose `status` is `OUTSTANDING` on a session that has already been delivered — otherwise `ERR_FAMILY_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` (402).

Two consequences of the ordering deserve to be stated on their own. Because the confirmation walk visits rungs 33, 34, 35, 37, 45, 46, 49, 50, 51, 54, 55 and 60 in that numeric sequence, a participant who is simultaneously inactive, unconsented, stale-assessed and blocked by an unpaid balance is always told they are not active first. And because rung 60 is the very last rung, an outstanding family balance is reported only after every consent, assessment, capacity, equipment and transport question has been answered in the affirmative.

**Error precedence where two rungs could both fire.** The rule is always the lower rung number wins, and it resolves every ambiguity in this document. A stale parent version (rung 25) is reported before a wrong source state (rung 26). A wrong source state (rung 26) is reported before a terminal state (rung 27). A frozen period (rung 9) is reported before any missing resource (rungs 11 to 21) and before any malformed payload (rung 22). A missing required field (rung 22) is reported before a bad enum member (rung 23). Within a single rung that covers several resource families, the resource named earliest in the URL path decides which sibling error id is used. And where a cascade would drive a transition whose preconditions are not met — a promotion candidate that fails sub-gate B, for example — no error is raised at all: the cascade simply skips that row, as §2.12 describes.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Five quantities in this system are conserved, meaning every action that commits a unit and every compensating action that gives it back must leave the stated equation true. These are not advisory; a workflow that appears to allow a drift is misread.

- **Programme slots.** For every session, `slot_capacity` equals `slots_committed` plus `slots_available`, and `slots_committed` equals the number of enrolments on that session whose `enrollment_status` is `CONFIRMED`. This must hold after enrolment confirmation, after withdrawal, after a waitlist promotion, after a funding-driven demotion back to the waitlist, after a session cancellation, and after attendance is recorded.
- **Dedicated one-to-one capacity.** For every session, `one_to_one_pool_size` equals `one_to_one_committed` plus `one_to_one_available`, and `one_to_one_committed` equals the number of `CONFIRMED` enrolments on that session whose effective tier at the session is `ONE_TO_ONE`. This must hold after confirmation, after withdrawal, after a support-assessment activation that changes somebody's tier, and after session cancellation.
- **Funding ledger.** For every funding source, `total_budget_cents` equals `available_cents` plus `encumbered_cents` plus `disbursed_cents`, and the source's `encumbered_cents` equals the sum, over its awards whose status is `ENCUMBERED` or `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED`, of each award's `encumbered_cents` minus its `disbursed_cents`. This must hold after an encumbrance, after a full or partial disbursement, after either variant of a release, after an enrolment withdrawal, after a source is closed, and after a period is closed.
- **Vehicle positions, as two pools.** For every run, `accessible_positions` equals `accessible_committed` plus `accessible_available`, and `ambulatory_seats` equals `ambulatory_committed` plus `ambulatory_available`. No action anywhere moves a unit between the two pools. This must hold after a booking is created, cancelled or marked a no-show, after an enrolment withdrawal, and after a session cancellation.
- **Physical equipment units.** For every item, `pooled_total` equals `pooled_available` plus `pooled_held` plus `pooled_checked_out`, where `pooled_held` is the number of reservations on the item whose status is `HELD` and `pooled_checked_out` the number whose status is `CHECKED_OUT`. This must hold after a reservation is created, checked out, returned in either condition, released, or voided by an inspection failure, and after an enrolment withdrawal.

### 2.12. How Cascades Behave

Six named cascades run through this system, and they are referred to throughout §3 by letter: **A** is the enrolment-withdrawal cascade, **B** the session-cancellation cascade, **C** the certification-expiry cascade, **D** the equipment-condition cascade, **E** the participant-exit cascade and **F** the period-close cascade. Cascade A is the deepest, at eight hops.

Three properties hold of all six. Hops run in their numbered order, each hop caused by the one before it, and a hop whose preconditions are unmet is skipped silently rather than raising an error — a cascade never fails a request that was already admitted. Every row a cascade touches has its `version` raised by one and its `updated_at` refreshed, once per request regardless of how many hops visited it. And a cascade's side effects are not re-tested against the write gates of §2.5.

Where a cascade could recurse — a release that frees a slot, a promotion that consumes it, a lapse that frees it again — the specification pins a deliberate stopping point, and those pins are stated in the workflows that own them. The three pins worth naming up front: waitlist promotion does not run for a slot freed by a session cancellation; waitlist promotion does not run for a slot freed by a funding-driven demotion; and at most one promotion runs per released slot.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Programme Year

Everything begins with an operating period. A `ProgramAdmin` creates one by supplying a `name`, a `starts_on` date and an `ends_on` date that is the same as or later than `starts_on`; the row is born with `status` `OPEN` and `version` 1. Periods are how the department expresses fiscal posture, and an administrator moves a period along its posture axis by nominating a `target_status`.

Eight posture moves are declared, and no others exist. From `OPEN` a period may go to `RESTRICTED`, to `FROZEN` or to `CLOSING`. From `RESTRICTED` it may return to `OPEN` or advance to `FROZEN`. From `FROZEN` the only way out is back to `OPEN`. From `CLOSING` a period may either be abandoned back to `OPEN` — in which case no funding source anywhere is touched — or completed to `CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal. Any nominated `target_status` that is not the far end of one of those eight edges is rejected at rung 26 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and any posture request at all against an already-`CLOSED` period is rejected at rung 27 with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Each successful move raises the period's `version` by one. Moving to `RESTRICTED` begins rejecting the seven financial writes; moving back to `OPEN` from `RESTRICTED` resumes accepting them; moving to `FROZEN` from either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` engages the global write gate; moving from `FROZEN` back to `OPEN` disengages it; and moving to `CLOSING` makes the period behave, for gate purposes, exactly as `RESTRICTED` does.

Completing a close is `CASCADE F`, four hops deep. Hop 1 sets the period's `status` to `CLOSED`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, closes every funding source whose `operating_period_id` names this period and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, lapses every scholarship award on each newly closed source whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, moving it to `LAPSED_NO_FUNDS`. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, moves each lapsed award's enrolment along its money axis from `QUOTED` to `UNFUNDED`, which in turn can demote a confirmed enrolment as described in §3.8. The period's own `version` rises by one, as does the version of every source, award and enrolment the later hops touched. Because `CLOSED` is treated exactly like `FROZEN` by the write gate, a close is effectively a one-way door for that date range.

**The sliding scale.** Pricing rests on a table of fee-scale bands, each one a `ProgramAdmin`-created row carrying an inclusive `household_size_min` of at least 1, an inclusive `household_size_max` no smaller than the minimum, an `income_band` of `BAND_A`, `BAND_B`, `BAND_C` or `BAND_D`, a `per_session_cents` price and a `series_cents` price, both zero or greater. Bands are immutable configuration: once written they are never edited, they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, and their prices are read only through the matching rule in §3.3.

**Activity programmes.** A programme is the template every one of its sessions inherits. Beyond its `name` it declares an `activity_type` of `AQUATICS`, `FITNESS`, `ARTS`, `SPORTS` or `COMMUNITY_OUTING`; a `risk_tier` of `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`, which selects the staffing multiplier; three policy switches that each pick a calculation variant — `pricing_mode` of `PER_SESSION` or `SERIES_FLAT`, `rounding_policy` of `ROUND_PER_LINE` or `ROUND_ON_TOTAL`, and `waitlist_policy` of `FIFO` or `EQUITY_WEIGHTED`; a `fee_multiplier_bp` of zero or more basis points; and a `scheduled_session_count` of at least 1, which is the number of sessions a flat series price is divided across. Five further inputs are optional. `required_consent_types` is a list drawn from `MEDIA_RELEASE`, `EMERGENCY_MEDICAL`, `BEHAVIOR_SUPPORT_PLAN` and `MEDICATION_ADMIN`, and defaults to the empty list when omitted or sent as null, meaning no consent is demanded. `required_certification_types` is a list drawn from `ADAPTIVE_AQUATICS`, `BEHAVIOR_SUPPORT`, `MEDICATION_ADMIN` and `CPR`, and likewise defaults to the empty list. `requires_medication_admin` is a boolean defaulting to false. `required_equipment_model_code` and `required_equipment_size_code` are strings that both default to null; a null model code means the programme needs no adaptive equipment, and a size code is meaningful only alongside a model code. Each programme also carries a computed `session_count_created`, which starts at 0 and counts the sessions built from it.

**Sessions.** Creating a session names its `program_id` and supplies `start_at`, an `end_at` strictly later than `start_at`, a `slot_capacity` of zero or more, and a `one_to_one_pool_size` of zero or more. The server stamps `sequence_number` as the programme's `session_count_created` plus one — so the first session of a series is sequence number 1 — then raises `session_count_created` by one and bumps the programme's `version`. The new row opens with `slots_committed` 0 and `slots_available` equal to `slot_capacity`, `one_to_one_committed` 0 and `one_to_one_available` equal to `one_to_one_pool_size`, `one_to_one_staffed` 0, `assigned_group_staff_count` 0, `required_group_staff_planned` 0, `required_group_staff_delivered` null, `staffing_gap` 0, `unlock_count` 0, and `status` `DRAFT`. The declared `scheduled_session_count` is a pricing divisor and a planning figure; the API does not refuse a session that would take a programme past it.

A session's lifecycle has five states and seven declared moves. Opening a `DRAFT` session for enrolment moves it to `OPEN`, and is allowed only while the session's `start_at` is still in the future relative to the logical clock. From `OPEN`, locking staffing moves it to `STAFFING_LOCKED` under the conditions in §3.5, and from `STAFFING_LOCKED` an unlock returns it to `OPEN`. A locked session that has finished — the logical clock is at or past its `end_at` — is delivered, moving it to `DELIVERED`, which is terminal. Cancellation is available from `DRAFT`, from `OPEN` and from `STAFFING_LOCKED`, and `CANCELLED` is likewise terminal. Cancelling a `DRAFT` session has no cascade at all beyond raising its `version`, because nothing can be attached to a draft.

Cancelling an `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED` session is `CASCADE B`. Hop 1 sets `status` to `CANCELLED`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, withdraws every enrolment on the session whose `enrollment_status` is `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CONFIRMED`; `slots_committed` and `one_to_one_committed` fall to 0 and `slots_available` and `one_to_one_available` are restored to their full pool sizes. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, cancels every transport booking of those enrolments whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, decrements on each affected run either `accessible_committed` or `ambulatory_committed` — never both — restores the matching available counter, and moves a run that was `BOARDING_FULL` back to `PLANNED` as soon as either available counter rises above 0. **Waitlist promotion deliberately does not run for slots released by cascade B**, because the session those slots belong to no longer exists as a place to be promoted into. When the cancelled session was `STAFFING_LOCKED`, one additional effect applies on top of hops 2 through 4: every staff assignment on the session whose status is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `LAPSED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. The session's `version` rises by one, as does the version of every enrolment, booking, run and assignment the cascade touched.

### 3.2. Intake: Participants, Caregivers, Assessments and Consent

An `IntakeCoordinator` opens a participant file with a `legal_name`, a `date_of_birth`, a `household_id` — an opaque grouping key that siblings share and that is not a foreign key to anything — a `household_size` of at least 1, an `income_band` from the same four-value enumeration the fee table uses, a `weight_kg` of at least 1 whole kilogram, a `mobility_profile` of `WHEELCHAIR` or `AMBULATORY`, and a `guardianship_status` of `SELF`, `GUARDIAN_REQUIRED` or `CONSERVATORSHIP`. The row is created in `status` `INTAKE` with `effective_support_tier` null and `version` 1.

Three of a participant's attributes are computed rather than stored from input. `age_years` is the number of whole years from `date_of_birth` to the logical clock's calendar date, discarding any part year — a person whose birthday falls tomorrow is still the younger age today. `effective_support_tier` mirrors the support tier of whichever support assessment of theirs is currently `ACTIVE`, and is null when no assessment is active. `enrollment_posture` is a derived summary recomputed on every read, described at the end of this subsection; when a participant is read on its own rather than through the eligibility view, that posture is computed with no session nominated, which means the consent branch is skipped and the check date is the logical clock date.

The intake axis is cyclic, not linear. A participant in `INTAKE` becomes `ACTIVE` only once at least one of their support assessments has reached `ACTIVE` status; that transition also refreshes `effective_support_tier` from the active assessment. An `ACTIVE` participant may be set `INACTIVE`, during which no enrolment may be created or confirmed for them; an `INACTIVE` participant may return to `ACTIVE`, again only when an active assessment exists — this re-entry is what makes the axis a loop. From either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` a participant may be moved to `EXITED`, which is terminal. Each move raises the participant's `version` by one.

Exiting is `CASCADE E`. Hop 1 sets the participant's `status` to `EXITED`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, discontinues every goal of theirs whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `MASTERED`, moving it to `DISCONTINUED`. Hop 3, also caused by hop 1, withdraws every enrolment of theirs whose `enrollment_status` is `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CONFIRMED` **and** whose session starts strictly later than the logical clock, running the full withdrawal cascade A for each one — so an exit that lands after a session has already begun leaves that session's roster alone. The participant's `version` rises by one, as does the version of every goal and enrolment touched.

A coordinator may amend the four attributes that drive banding and fit — `household_size`, `income_band`, `weight_kg` and `mobility_profile` — on an existing participant. Each is optional in the payload and an omitted field is left unchanged. The amendment raises `version` and refreshes `updated_at` whenever it is accepted, even when the supplied values match what is already stored. Crucially, an amendment **does not re-price any existing enrolment charge**: a charge's fee band is pinned at the moment the charge is created and is never re-matched.

**Caregivers.** A caregiver row attaches to exactly one participant by `participant_id` and records a `full_name`, a `relationship` of `PARENT`, `GUARDIAN`, `SIBLING` or `OTHER`, an `is_legal_guardian` boolean, and a `contact_phone`. Only a caregiver whose `is_legal_guardian` is true may sign on behalf of a participant who needs a guardian's signature. A caregiver's `id` is what that person presents as `X-User-ID` when acting as a `CaregiverUser`.

**Support assessments.** An assessment names its `participant_id`, the date it was performed as `assessed_on`, an `effective_from` instant from which it governs, and a `support_tier` of `INDEPENDENT`, `INTERMITTENT`, `SUBSTANTIAL` or `ONE_TO_ONE`. The create is concurrency-guarded on the participant, so it also carries `expected_participant_version`. On creation the server derives `support_points` from the tier — 25 for `INDEPENDENT`, 50 for `INTERMITTENT`, 100 for `SUBSTANTIAL`, and **0 for `ONE_TO_ONE`**, because a one-to-one participant consumes a dedicated staffer rather than contributing to the pooled load — and files the row in `status` `DRAFT`. The participant's own `version` rises by one on the create.

Activating a draft assessment is the pivot of the whole staffing model, and it does four things at once. The activating assessment moves `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`. Any other assessment of the same participant that was `ACTIVE` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal for that axis and reachable only this way — no client ever supersedes an assessment directly. The participant's `effective_support_tier` is set to the newly active tier. And every enrolment of that participant sitting on a session whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED` and whose `start_at` is at or after the new assessment's `effective_from` has its `effective_support_tier_at_session` recomputed, after which that session's `required_group_staff_planned`, `one_to_one_committed`, `one_to_one_available` and `staffing_gap` are all recomputed. **Enrolments sitting on sessions whose `status` is `DELIVERED` are not touched**, and neither are enrolments on sessions that start before the new assessment takes effect. Both the newly active assessment and the superseded one have their `version` raised by one, as does the participant.

**Consent.** A consent artifact names its `participant_id`, its `consent_type` — one of `MEDIA_RELEASE`, `EMERGENCY_MEDICAL`, `BEHAVIOR_SUPPORT_PLAN` or `MEDICATION_ADMIN` — and an `expires_on` date. It is filed in `status` `PENDING_SIGNATURE` with `signed_by_caregiver_id`, `signer_role` and `signed_on` all null.

Who may sign is decided by a three-branch rule that is evaluated in exactly this order. If the artifact's `consent_type` is `MEDICATION_ADMIN`, the accepted signer role is `LEGAL_GUARDIAN` — a medication-administration consent is never self-signed, at any age, whatever the participant's guardianship status. Otherwise, if the participant's `guardianship_status` is `SELF` **and** their `age_years` is 18 or more, the accepted signer role is `SELF`. In every other case the accepted signer role is `LEGAL_GUARDIAN`. When the accepted role is `SELF`, the signing request must omit `signed_by_caregiver_id` (or send it as null) and must be made by an `IntakeCoordinator`. When the accepted role is `LEGAL_GUARDIAN`, the request must supply a `signed_by_caregiver_id` naming a caregiver whose own `participant_id` is this participant and whose `is_legal_guardian` is true. Any other combination — a self-signature attempted by a caregiver, a guardian signature with no caregiver named, a caregiver named who belongs to a different participant or who is not a legal guardian, a self-signature on a medication consent — is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_SIGNER_NOT_AUTHORIZED` at rung 36. A signature is also refused when the artifact's `expires_on` already falls before the logical clock date, since an already-expired consent covers nothing.

A successful signature moves the artifact from `PENDING_SIGNATURE` to `SIGNED`, stamps `signed_on` with the logical clock's calendar date, stamps `signer_role` with `SELF` or `LEGAL_GUARDIAN` per the rule above, and stamps `signed_by_caregiver_id` with the acting caregiver's id — or leaves it null when the accepted role was `SELF`. The artifact's `version` rises by one. From `SIGNED` an artifact can go two further ways: the epoch checkpoint moves it to `EXPIRED` once its `expires_on` falls strictly before the logical clock date, and either an `IntakeCoordinator` or the participant's own `CaregiverUser` may revoke it to `REVOKED`. A `PENDING_SIGNATURE` artifact may also be revoked directly, in which case it never carries a `signer_role`. `REVOKED` is terminal; `EXPIRED` is not terminal but has no declared exit, so an expired consent is replaced by creating a new artifact rather than by renewing the old one.

**Eligibility as a single read.** Rather than making a coordinator guess, the system exposes a computed eligibility view for one participant, optionally aimed at a nominated session through a `session_id` query parameter. The date it evaluates against — call it the check date — is the session's calendar date when a session is nominated and the logical clock's calendar date when one is not; and when no session is nominated, no consent set is checked at all, because the required consent types belong to the programme, not to the participant. The view answers with `participant_id`, `enrollment_posture`, three booleans `consent_gate_ok`, `assessment_gate_ok` and `financial_gate_ok`, and the participant's current `effective_support_tier` and `age_years`. The posture itself is the first of five branches that holds, tested in this order: `NOT_ACTIVE` when the participant's `status` is anything other than `ACTIVE`; otherwise `CONSENT_BLOCKED` when some required consent type has no artifact of this participant that is both `SIGNED` and carries an `expires_on` on or after the check date; otherwise `ASSESSMENT_STALE` when there is no `ACTIVE` support assessment at all, or the active one's `assessed_on` plus 365 days falls before the check date; otherwise `FINANCIALLY_BLOCKED` when the participant holds any enrolment charge whose `status` is `OUTSTANDING` on a session that has already been delivered; and otherwise `CLEARED`. That branch order is not arbitrary — it is exactly the ladder order of rungs 33, 34, 37 and 60, so the posture predicts which rung a confirmation would fail on. The three booleans report the individual gates independently of the posture: `consent_gate_ok` is false exactly when the consent branch would fire, `assessment_gate_ok` false exactly when the assessment branch would fire, and `financial_gate_ok` false exactly when the financial branch would fire; when no session is nominated, `consent_gate_ok` is true because no consent set applies.

### 3.3. Requesting a Place, and What It Costs

An enrolment request is made either by an `IntakeCoordinator` or by the participant's own `CaregiverUser`. The payload names the `participant_id`, the `session_id`, the `expected_session_version` the concurrency rule demands, and optionally `transport_requested`, a boolean that defaults to false when omitted. That flag records only that the family has asked for a ride; it creates no booking by itself, and a booking may be made later regardless of what it says.

Five business questions are asked before the row is written, and they are asked in ladder order. The named session must currently be `OPEN`, or the request fails with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_OPEN` — a draft, locked, delivered or cancelled session accepts no new requests. The session's `start_at` must be at least 24 hours later than the logical clock; a session starting in exactly 24 hours is still enrollable, one starting in 23 hours 59 minutes is not, and the failure is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CUTOFF_PASSED`. The participant must hold no other enrolment on this same session whose `enrollment_status` is `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CONFIRMED`, or the request is a duplicate; a previously withdrawn, denied, attended or no-showed row does not block a fresh request. The participant's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. And the participant must not already be double-booked: over that participant's enrolments whose `enrollment_status` is `REQUESTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CONFIRMED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_TIME_CONFLICT` when the new session's `start_at` falls strictly before an existing session's `end_at` **and** that existing session's `start_at` falls strictly before the new session's `end_at`. These are strictly half-open intervals with no buffer whatsoever, so two sessions that touch exactly — the new one starting at the very instant the old one ends — do **not** collide for a participant. Finally, at the bottom of the ladder, the family balance rung applies here as well: a participant carrying an `OUTSTANDING` charge on an already-delivered session cannot request a new place, and is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` (402).

A successful request writes the enrolment in `enrollment_status` `REQUESTED` and `funding_status` `QUOTED`, with `waitlist_score` 0, `promoted_at` null, and `effective_support_tier_at_session` derived as §3.5 explains. The session's `version` rises by one even though no counter moved, which is exactly what makes the next client's stale-version check meaningful. No slot is consumed by a request; slots are consumed only at confirmation.

**Matching the sliding scale.** In the same operation, and before the charge row is written, the server matches the participant against the fee table. The candidate set is every fee-scale band whose `household_size_min` is less than or equal to the participant's `household_size` and whose `household_size_max` is greater than or equal to it, and whose `income_band` equals the participant's `income_band` — both bounds inclusive. Candidates are ranked by `household_size_min` **descending**, so the most specific band that still covers the household wins, and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending. When the candidate set is empty the whole enrolment request fails with `ERR_FEE_BAND_NOT_FOUND` (404) and nothing is written. The winning band's id is pinned onto the charge as `fee_scale_band_id` and is never re-matched, however the participant's household attributes change afterwards.

**Pricing one session.** The programme fee for the enrolment, stored as `program_fee_cents` on the charge, depends on the programme's `pricing_mode`, and the two branches produce genuinely different numbers for the same participant.

- Under `PER_SESSION`, the fee is the band's `per_session_cents` multiplied by the programme's `fee_multiplier_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The programme's `scheduled_session_count` and the session's `sequence_number` are ignored entirely. A band price of 4500 cents under a multiplier of 12500 basis points yields 5625 cents on every session of the series.
- Under `SERIES_FLAT`, the whole series is priced first: take the band's `series_cents`, multiply by `fee_multiplier_bp`, divide by 10000, and round half up to a whole cent — call that the series total. Divide the series total by the programme's `scheduled_session_count` and take the floor to get the per-session base. The remainder is the series total minus the base multiplied by the session count. The fee for this enrolment is the base, plus the entire remainder when and only when the session's `sequence_number` is 1. Every other session in the series carries the bare base. So a series total of 10000 cents across 3 sessions gives 3334 cents on session 1 and 3333 cents on sessions 2 and 3 — identical participants on the same programme legitimately pay different amounts depending which session they took.

**The charge row.** Exactly one enrolment charge exists per enrolment, and it is synthesised by the enrolment-creation cascade — no client ever creates one. It is born with the pinned `fee_scale_band_id`, the computed `program_fee_cents`, `scholarship_applied_cents` 0, `copay_cents` equal to `program_fee_cents`, `transport_fee_cents` 0, `sibling_discount_bp` 0, `amount_paid_cents` 0, a `family_responsibility_cents` computed by the rule in §3.8, a `balance_cents` equal to `family_responsibility_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents`, and `status` `QUOTED`. Because the charge is created by a cascade and not returned by the create call, the only way to discover it is through the enrolment-charge list read, filtered by `enrollment_id`.

**Settlement status.** A charge's `status` is not a state machine a client drives; it is re-derived from scratch every time the charge is recomputed and every time a payment is recorded, using four ordered branches. The charge is `VOIDED` when its enrolment's `enrollment_status` is `WITHDRAWN` or `DENIED`. Otherwise it is `QUOTED` when the enrolment is `REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED`. Otherwise it is `SETTLED` when `balance_cents` is 0. Otherwise it is `OUTSTANDING`. In the same recomputation, `scholarship_applied_cents` is refreshed as the sum of `encumbered_cents` over this enrolment's awards whose `status` is `ENCUMBERED`, `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` or `DISBURSED`; `copay_cents` becomes `program_fee_cents` minus `scholarship_applied_cents`, clamped so it can never fall below 0; and `balance_cents` becomes `family_responsibility_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents`, likewise clamped at 0 so an over-payment reads as a zero balance rather than a negative one. Note the ordering consequence: an enrolment that is confirmed but fully scholarshipped and carries no transport fee lands on `SETTLED` immediately, while a withdrawn enrolment reads `VOIDED` even if its family had already paid in full.

### 3.4. Confirmation, the Waitlist, and Coming Back Out Again

Confirmation is the operation that actually consumes capacity, and only an `IntakeCoordinator` may perform it. Its single optional input is `on_full`, which decides what happens when the capacity sub-gate — and only the capacity sub-gate — fails. `on_full` accepts `ERROR`, `WAITLIST` or `DENY`, and **defaults to `ERROR` when omitted or sent as null**. Under `ERROR` a full session raises `ERR_SESSION_FULL` or `ERR_ONE_TO_ONE_POOL_EXHAUSTED` and nothing changes. Under `WAITLIST` the enrolment moves from `REQUESTED` to `WAITLISTED`. Under `DENY` it moves from `REQUESTED` to `DENIED`. The routing applies only to rungs 45 and 46; a failure at any other rung raises its own error whatever `on_full` says.

The six sub-gates are walked in ladder order, and the walk begins only after the participant-active rung has passed. **Sub-gate A (consent)** demands, for every consent type in the session's programme `required_consent_types`, an artifact of this participant with that type whose `status` is `SIGNED` — missing one is `ERR_REQUIRED_CONSENT_MISSING` — and then demands that each such artifact's `expires_on` falls on or after the session's calendar date, failing which it is `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED`. A consent that has lapsed for one activity family blocks only enrolments on programmes that require that very consent type; it never blocks the participant's enrolments elsewhere. A programme with an empty `required_consent_types` passes sub-gate A vacuously. **Sub-gate B (assessment)** demands an `ACTIVE` support assessment whose `assessed_on` plus 365 days lands on or after the session date; equality passes, and both "no active assessment at all" and "an active but too-old assessment" report `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STALE`. **Sub-gate C (capacity)** demands `slots_available` of at least 1 and, only when the participant's effective tier at this session is `ONE_TO_ONE`, `one_to_one_available` of at least 1 as well; these are two separate pools and a free programme slot never substitutes for a missing one-to-one staffer. **Sub-gate D (equipment)** is skipped entirely when the session's programme declares a null `required_equipment_model_code`; otherwise it is evaluated over the enrolment's live reservations, as §3.6 sets out. **Sub-gate E (transport)** is skipped entirely when `transport_requested` is false, and is likewise satisfied without further checking when the enrolment already holds a live booking; otherwise it is evaluated over the runs serving the session, as §3.7 sets out. **Sub-gate F (funding)**, as walked by confirmation, consists of the last rung alone: the participant must carry no `OUTSTANDING` charge on an already-delivered session. The three award-side rungs of sub-gate F guard award creation and encumbrance rather than confirmation.

When all six pass, confirmation runs a fixed sequence of side effects. `slots_committed` rises by one and `slots_available` falls by one. When the enrolment's `effective_support_tier_at_session` is `ONE_TO_ONE`, `one_to_one_committed` rises by one and `one_to_one_available` falls by one; for every other tier those two counters are untouched. The session's `required_group_staff_planned` and `staffing_gap` are recomputed. Every scholarship award on this enrolment whose `status` is `PROPOSED` is auto-encumbered — moving cents out of its source's `available_cents` and into that source's `encumbered_cents` — and the enrolment's `funding_status` advances from `QUOTED` to `ENCUMBERED`. This enrolment's charge is recomputed, and then **every other charge whose enrolment is `CONFIRMED` on the same session and whose participant shares this participant's `household_id` is recomputed, in `created_at` ascending order** — one sweep, no second pass. Every transport booking on this enrolment whose status is `RESERVED` moves to `CONFIRMED`. The enrolment's `version` and the session's `version` each rise by one.

**Scoring the waitlist.** A row entering `WAITLISTED` has its `waitlist_score` computed then, and recomputed whenever it is reconsidered. The programme's `waitlist_policy` picks the variant. Under `FIFO` every candidate scores exactly 0, which collapses the ranking to arrival order. Under `EQUITY_WEIGHTED` the score is the support points of the enrolment's `effective_support_tier_at_session` — 25 for `INDEPENDENT`, 50 for `INTERMITTENT`, 100 for `SUBSTANTIAL`, 0 for `ONE_TO_ONE` — plus a flat bonus of 50 when that enrolment holds a scholarship award whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `ENCUMBERED`, and nothing otherwise. A later-arriving `SUBSTANTIAL` participant holding an award therefore scores 150 and outranks an earlier `INDEPENDENT` self-payer scoring 25, which is the opposite outcome from `FIFO` on identical rows. Promotion candidates are the session's `WAITLISTED` enrolments ranked by `waitlist_score` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; when the candidate set is empty, no promotion happens and no error is raised.

**Promotion.** When a slot is released by a withdrawal, the system attempts **at most one** promotion per released slot. It takes the top-ranked candidate and walks all six sub-gates against that row independently, in exactly the order a client-driven confirmation would. A candidate that fails any sub-gate is skipped silently — no error surfaces anywhere — and the next-ranked candidate is tried, until one succeeds or the candidate list is exhausted. The winner moves from `WAITLISTED` to `CONFIRMED`, has `promoted_at` stamped with the logical clock instant, and receives exactly the same counter, award, charge-recomputation and booking side effects as a client-driven confirmation, with its own `version` and the session's `version` each rising by one.

**Withdrawal is `CASCADE A`, eight hops deep.** A `CONFIRMED` enrolment may be withdrawn by an `IntakeCoordinator` or by the participant's `CaregiverUser` while its session's `status` is `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED`. Hop 1 sets `enrollment_status` to `WITHDRAWN`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, unwinds everything that row was holding: `slots_committed` falls by one and `slots_available` rises by one; when the tier was `ONE_TO_ONE`, `one_to_one_committed` falls by one and `one_to_one_available` rises by one; `required_group_staff_planned` and `staffing_gap` are recomputed; every equipment reservation of this enrolment whose status is `HELD` or `CHECKED_OUT` is released and the item's pooled counters restored; every transport booking of this enrolment whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` is cancelled and its run's position counter restored; every staff assignment on this session whose `assignment_role` is `ONE_TO_ONE` and whose `dedicated_participant_id` is this participant is withdrawn; every scholarship award on this enrolment whose status is `PROPOSED`, `ENCUMBERED` or `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` is released, returning its release amount to its source, and the enrolment's `funding_status` becomes `RELEASED`; and the charge's `status` becomes `VOIDED`. Hop 3, caused by the slot freed in hop 2, promotes the top-ranked waitlisted candidate that independently satisfies all six sub-gates. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, encumbers that promoted enrolment's `PROPOSED` award. Hop 5, caused by hop 4, moves the award amount out of that source's `available_cents` and into its `encumbered_cents`. Hop 6, caused by hop 5, moves the source from `OPEN` to `EXHAUSTED` when its `available_cents` has reached exactly 0. Hop 7, caused by hop 6, lapses every award on that now-exhausted source whose status is `PROPOSED` to `LAPSED_NO_FUNDS`. Hop 8, caused by hop 7, moves each lapsed award's enrolment `funding_status` to `UNFUNDED` and — when that enrolment is `CONFIRMED` and its participant's `income_band` is `BAND_A` or `BAND_B` — demotes it from `CONFIRMED` back to `WAITLISTED`. The withdrawn enrolment's `version` rises by one, as does the version of every row touched by hops 2 through 8.

That eighth hop is the system's most surprising behaviour and deserves restating plainly: **one family withdrawing can cost a different family its confirmed place**, because the promotion it triggers can drain the grant that a third enrolment was relying on. The demotion applies only to participants in the two subsidy-required income bands, `BAND_A` and `BAND_B`; a participant in `BAND_C` or `BAND_D` whose award lapses simply becomes unfunded and keeps their seat. A demotion frees `slots_committed` and, for a one-to-one participant, `one_to_one_committed`, restores the matching available counters, recomputes `required_group_staff_planned` and `staffing_gap`, recomputes that row's `waitlist_score`, and raises the enrolment's and the session's `version` by one each. **Waitlist promotion is deliberately not run for a slot freed by this demotion** — that pin is precisely what makes the cascade terminate rather than oscillate.

Withdrawal from `REQUESTED` or from `WAITLISTED` is much quieter, and the two behave identically: no session counter moves, because neither state ever held one; no promotion runs, because no slot was freed; any award on the enrolment whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ENCUMBERED` is released; the charge's `status` becomes `VOIDED`; and the enrolment's `version` rises by one.

**Attendance.** Once a session is `STAFFING_LOCKED` and the logical clock has reached or passed the session's `start_at`, the `ActivityLead` staffing that session records an `outcome` of `ATTENDED` or `NO_SHOW` against each confirmed enrolment. `ATTENDED` also moves the enrolment's `CONFIRMED` transport booking to `RIDDEN`, which leaves the run's counters alone because the seat was genuinely used. `NO_SHOW` moves that booking to `NO_SHOW` instead, which **does** decrement the run's committed counter for that position type and restores the matching available counter. Both outcomes raise the enrolment's `version` by one, and both are terminal on the roster axis.

### 3.5. Staffing a Session by Support Need

A staff member is a thin record — a `full_name` and a free-text `role_title` such as "Recreation Therapist" — but its `id` matters twice over: it is what an `ActivityLead` presents as `X-User-ID`, and it is the anchor for every certification and assignment.

**Certifications.** Each certification row names its `staff_member_id`, a `certification_type` of `ADAPTIVE_AQUATICS`, `BEHAVIOR_SUPPORT`, `MEDICATION_ADMIN` or `CPR`, an `issued_on` date and an `expires_on` date, and is filed with `status` `VALID`. Validity is inclusive: a certification whose `expires_on` equals the date being checked is still valid on that date. The `status` field is a reporting mirror maintained by the epoch checkpoint — it flips to `EXPIRED` when `expires_on` falls strictly before the new logical clock date — but **every qualification decision in this system compares `expires_on` against a date directly and never consults that mirror**, which is what lets a certification be "expired today" and still be judged valid for a session that already happened. Renewal supplies a `new_expires_on`. From `EXPIRED` a renewal is accepted when `new_expires_on` is on or after the logical clock date and returns the row to `VALID`; from `VALID` a renewal is accepted only when `new_expires_on` is strictly later than the current `expires_on`, leaving the row `VALID`. Either way `expires_on` is overwritten and `version` rises by one. **A renewal never revives a lapsed assignment on its own** — the assignment must be revalidated explicitly.

**Two qualification verdicts, deliberately disagreeing.** Whether a staffer is qualified is a single predicate — for every certification type in the session's programme `required_certification_types`, the staffer holds a certification row of that type whose `expires_on` is on or after the check date — evaluated against two different check dates. The `AT_ASSIGNMENT` verdict uses **today's** logical-clock date and is frozen onto the assignment as `qualified_at_assignment` at creation, never revisited. The `AT_SESSION_DATE` verdict uses the calendar date of the session's `start_at` and is stored as `qualified_at_session_date`; it is stamped at creation and recomputed at assignment confirmation, at revalidation, at staffing lock, and by the epoch checkpoint. On identical data the two verdicts disagree exactly when a certification lapses inside the interval between today and the session, which is the whole point: a staffer may legitimately be proposed today and refused at confirmation.

**Proposing an assignment.** An `ActivityLead` or a `ProgramAdmin` proposes an assignment by naming the `session_id`, the `staff_member_id`, an `assignment_role` of `LEAD`, `GROUP` or `ONE_TO_ONE`, and — required when and only when the role is `ONE_TO_ONE`, and null otherwise, defaulting to null when omitted — a `dedicated_participant_id`. Three checks run in ladder order before the row is written. The staffer must hold a certification row for every type the programme requires, or the proposal fails with `ERR_STAFF_CERT_MISSING`. Every one of those certifications must carry an `expires_on` on or after today's logical-clock date, or the proposal fails with `ERR_STAFF_CERT_EXPIRED_AT_ASSIGNMENT` — this is the only place the today-dated check is applied. And the staffer must be free: over that staffer's assignments whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` on sessions whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_TIME_CONFLICT` when the new session's `start_at` falls before the existing session's `end_at` extended by 30 minutes **and** the existing session's `start_at` falls before the new session's `end_at` extended by the same 30 minutes. That half-hour turnaround buffer is applied to both ends, which means **back-to-back sessions do collide for staff** even though, as §3.3 noted, they do not collide for participants — the two overlap rules are deliberately different. A successful proposal files the row in `status` `PROPOSED`, stamps both qualification flags, recomputes the session's counters and `staffing_gap`, and raises the session's `version` by one.

**How many staffers a session needs.** The requirement is derived from support need, not head-count, and the order of its two rounding steps is pinned. Start from a roster. Filter that roster down to the **pooled** participants, meaning every enrolment on it whose effective tier at this session is anything other than `ONE_TO_ONE`. If the pooled set is empty, the requirement is exactly 0 — the one-staffer floor does **not** apply to an empty set, so a session attended only by one-to-one participants, or by nobody, needs no group staff at all. Otherwise sum the support points of the pooled participants, counting 25 for each `INDEPENDENT`, 50 for each `INTERMITTENT` and 100 for each `SUBSTANTIAL`. Multiply that sum by the programme's risk multiplier — 10000 basis points for `LOW`, 12500 for `MODERATE`, 15000 for `HIGH` — divide by 10000, and **round the result up to a whole number of points**. Then divide those weighted points by the staff point capacity of 400 and **round up again**. The requirement is the larger of that figure and 1. Both ceilings must be taken in that order; dividing first and ceiling once gives different answers on many inputs.

The formula has two variants distinguished only by which roster it is fed. The `PLANNED` variant takes the enrolments whose `enrollment_status` is `CONFIRMED` — everyone expected — and is written to the session's `required_group_staff_planned` whenever the session is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED`. The `DELIVERED` variant takes the enrolments whose `enrollment_status` is `ATTENDED` — only those who actually turned up — and is written once, to `required_group_staff_delivered`, during the deliver transition. Because every no-show drops out of the delivered roster, the delivered requirement is normally strictly lower than the planned one for the same session. `required_group_staff_delivered` stays null until the session is delivered.

**Which tier a participant counts as.** The effective tier at a session, stored on the enrolment as `effective_support_tier_at_session`, also has two variants, and which one applies is decided by the session's own status. While the session is `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `STAFFING_LOCKED` or `CANCELLED`, the `CURRENT` variant applies: the tier is simply the `support_tier` of the participant's `ACTIVE` support assessment, or null when they have none. A mid-series re-assessment therefore changes the staffing arithmetic immediately for every session that has not yet been delivered. Once the session's status is `DELIVERED`, the `AS_OF_SESSION` variant applies instead: gather the participant's assessments whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` and whose `effective_from` is at or before the session's `start_at`, rank them by `effective_from` descending with ties broken by `created_at` descending, and take the first one's `support_tier`, or null when that set is empty. A delivered session consequently keeps the tier it was staffed under, even after the participant has been re-assessed — the two variants really do return different tiers on the same underlying data.

**The gap.** A session's `staffing_gap` is the sum of two independent shortfalls. Count the assignments on the session whose `assignment_role` is `LEAD` or `GROUP` and whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` — that is `assigned_group_staff_count`. Count the assignments whose `assignment_role` is `ONE_TO_ONE` and whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` — that is `one_to_one_staffed`. Assignments that are `LAPSED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `DELIVERED` count toward neither. The group shortfall is `required_group_staff_planned` minus `assigned_group_staff_count`, floored at 0; the one-to-one shortfall is `one_to_one_committed` minus `one_to_one_staffed`, also floored at 0; and `staffing_gap` is the two added together. Separately, `one_to_one_committed` is always the number of `CONFIRMED` enrolments on the session whose effective tier at the session is `ONE_TO_ONE`, and `one_to_one_available` is `one_to_one_pool_size` minus that count.

**Assignment lifecycle.** An `ActivityLead` confirms a `PROPOSED` assignment, which requires the recomputed `qualified_at_session_date` to be true — a false verdict is `ERR_STAFF_CERT_EXPIRES_BEFORE_SESSION` — and requires the session to be `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED`. Confirmation recomputes the session's `staffing_gap` and raises the assignment's `version`. A `CONFIRMED` assignment can be knocked down to `LAPSED` by the certification cascade, and a `LAPSED` assignment can be restored to `CONFIRMED` by revalidation, which re-runs the session-dated verdict and succeeds only when it now passes and the session is still `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED`; restoration puts the staffer back into `assigned_group_staff_count` or `one_to_one_staffed` and recomputes the gap. This lapse-and-restore loop means the assignment axis is not a pipeline. Withdrawal is available from `PROPOSED`, from `CONFIRMED` (provided the session is not already `DELIVERED`) and from `LAPSED`; withdrawing from `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` recomputes the session counters and the gap, while withdrawing from `LAPSED` changes no counter because a lapsed assignment was already excluded from both. `WITHDRAWN` and `DELIVERED` are terminal.

**Locking, unlocking and delivering.** Locking staffing takes an `OPEN` session to `STAFFING_LOCKED` and demands four things: the recomputed `staffing_gap` must be 0, or the lock fails with `ERR_STAFFING_GAP`; every assignment on the session whose status is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` must have a recomputed `qualified_at_session_date` of true, failing which the lock reports `ERR_STAFF_CERT_MISSING` when a required certification row is absent altogether and `ERR_STAFF_CERT_EXPIRES_BEFORE_SESSION` when a row exists but expires before the session date; and when the programme's `requires_medication_admin` is true, at least one live assignment on the session must be held by a staffer with a `MEDICATION_ADMIN` certification whose `expires_on` is on or after the session date, failing which the lock reports `ERR_MED_ADMIN_COVERAGE_MISSING`. On success every `PROPOSED` assignment on the session moves to `CONFIRMED`, no further enrolments may be created against the session, and the session's `version` rises by one.

Unlocking returns a `STAFFING_LOCKED` session to `OPEN`, raises its `unlock_count` by one and its `version` by one, and is capped: the third attempt, made when `unlock_count` already equals 2, is refused with `ERR_UNLOCK_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Delivering takes a `STAFFING_LOCKED` session to `DELIVERED` and is permitted only once the logical clock has reached or passed the session's `end_at`. Delivery does four things in order: every enrolment still sitting at `CONFIRMED` is moved to `NO_SHOW`, with the same transport-booking consequence a manually recorded no-show would have; `required_group_staff_delivered` is computed from the `DELIVERED` roster variant; every assignment on the session whose status is `CONFIRMED` moves to `DELIVERED`; and every charge on an `ATTENDED` enrolment is finalised by re-deriving its settlement `status`. The session's `version` rises by one.

**`CASCADE C`, the certification cliff.** When the epoch checkpoint expires a certification, four hops follow. Hop 1 sets that certification's `status` to `EXPIRED`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, takes every assignment held by that staffer whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, sitting on a session whose `status` is `OPEN` or `STAFFING_LOCKED` and whose programme's `required_certification_types` includes the expiring type, and moves it from `CONFIRMED` to `LAPSED`, setting that assignment's `qualified_at_session_date` to false. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, drops the affected sessions' `assigned_group_staff_count` or `one_to_one_staffed` accordingly and recomputes their `staffing_gap`. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, takes every affected session whose `status` is `STAFFING_LOCKED` and whose recomputed `staffing_gap` is now above 0 and moves it from `STAFFING_LOCKED` back to `OPEN`, raising its `unlock_count` by one — **unless that session's `unlock_count` already equals 2, in which case hop 4 is skipped for that session and it stays `STAFFING_LOCKED` carrying a non-zero gap.** That is a real, reachable state: a locked session that no longer meets its own staffing requirement and cannot be automatically reopened. The expired certification's `version` rises by one, as does the version of every assignment and session the later hops touched, and the checkpoint's `assignments_lapsed` and `sessions_unlocked` counters report how many of each moved.

### 3.6. The Adaptive Equipment Cage

The catalogue holds two structurally different kinds of row, distinguished by `item_kind`. A `SERIALIZED` row is one physical unit with an identity and an inspection cycle: it carries a `serial_number` and an `inspection_due_date`, both required for this kind, and its `pooled_total` is 1. A `POOLED` row is a bag of fungible units tracked only by count: its `serial_number` and `inspection_due_date` are both null, and its `pooled_total` is the number of units held, which must be at least 1. Both fields default to null and `pooled_total` defaults to 1 when the payload omits them. Every item also declares a `model_code`, a `size_code` and a `weight_capacity_kg` of zero or more whole kilograms, and is created with `condition_status` `SERVICEABLE`, `pooled_available` equal to `pooled_total`, `pooled_held` 0 and `pooled_checked_out` 0.

**Whether an item fits.** Four independent conditions must all hold for an item to be usable by a particular participant on a particular session. The **fit** condition: the item's `model_code` equals the programme's `required_equipment_model_code`, its `size_code` equals the programme's `required_equipment_size_code` exactly, and its `weight_capacity_kg` is greater than or equal to the participant's `weight_kg`. The weight tolerance is zero, so a participant of 90 kilograms fits a chair rated at exactly 90 and does not fit one rated at 89. The **condition** condition: `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE`. The **inspection** condition: either the item is `POOLED`, which has no inspection cycle at all, or its `inspection_due_date` falls on or after the session's calendar date. And the **availability** condition: `pooled_available` is at least 1. That last predicate carries two different occupancy semantics under one formula — for a serialized unit, whose `pooled_total` is 1, it means exclusive possession; for a pooled row it means aggregate capacity up to `pooled_total` concurrent holds.

**Placing a hold.** An `ActivityLead` or an `IntakeCoordinator` places a hold by naming the `enrollment_id`, the `equipment_item_id` and the `expected_item_version`. The three sub-gate D rungs are evaluated in order against the item: a fit failure is `ERR_EQUIPMENT_FIT_MISMATCH`, a condition or inspection failure is `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INSPECTION_OVERDUE`, and an availability failure is `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE`. On success the reservation is filed in `status` `HELD` with `return_condition` null, the item's `pooled_held` rises by one and `pooled_available` falls by one, and the item's `version` rises by one.

**What sub-gate D means during confirmation.** When the session's programme declares a non-null `required_equipment_model_code`, enrolment confirmation and waitlist promotion evaluate the same three rungs, but over the enrolment's **live reservations** — those whose status is `HELD` or `CHECKED_OUT`. Rung 49 requires every live reservation's item to satisfy the fit condition; rung 50 requires every live reservation's item to satisfy the condition-and-inspection condition; and rung 51 requires the enrolment to hold **at least one** live reservation, reporting `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE` when it holds none. An enrolment with no reservation at all therefore passes rungs 49 and 50 vacuously and fails at rung 51. When the programme's required model code is null, all three rungs are skipped and an enrolment may confirm with no equipment whatsoever.

**Issuing and receiving.** A held item is checked out to the participant only when the reservation is `HELD`, the item is `SERVICEABLE`, the enrolment's session is `STAFFING_LOCKED` and the enrolment itself is `CONFIRMED`. Check-out moves `pooled_held` down by one and `pooled_checked_out` up by one, leaving `pooled_available` **unchanged** — the unit was already out of circulation the moment it was held. Both the reservation's and the item's `version` rise by one.

A return carries a `return_condition` of `GOOD` or `DAMAGED`, and this is the fork where the two branches genuinely diverge. Under `GOOD` the reservation becomes `RETURNED_GOOD`, `pooled_checked_out` falls by one and `pooled_available` rises by one, and **the item's `condition_status` is not changed at all**. Under `DAMAGED` the reservation becomes `RETURNED_DAMAGED`, the same two counters move the same way, and then the item is taken out of service — from `SERVICEABLE` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, or from `INSPECTION_DUE` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — which runs the void cascade over that item's remaining held reservations. Same starting state, same input shape, two completely different blast radii. Either branch raises both the reservation's and the item's `version` by one.

A hold that is never issued can simply be dropped: releasing a `HELD` reservation moves it to `RELEASED`, lowering `pooled_held` by one and raising `pooled_available` by one. A `CHECKED_OUT` reservation is also moved to `RELEASED` — but only by the system, as part of the withdrawal cascade — in which case `pooled_checked_out` falls by one and `pooled_available` rises by one. All four of `RETURNED_GOOD`, `RETURNED_DAMAGED`, `RELEASED` and `VOIDED_INSPECTION_FAIL` are terminal.

**Inspections.** A `ProgramAdmin` records an inspection with a `disposition` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `RETIRE`. `PASS` is accepted from `INSPECTION_DUE` and from `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, returning the item to `SERVICEABLE` and pushing its `inspection_due_date` out to the logical clock date plus 180 days — an item can therefore go out of service and come back any number of times. `FAIL` is accepted from `SERVICEABLE` and from `INSPECTION_DUE` and lands on `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. `RETIRE` is accepted from `SERVICEABLE`, from `INSPECTION_DUE` and from `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and lands on `RETIRED`, which is terminal. Any disposition that is not the far end of one of those eight edges — a `PASS` on an already-`SERVICEABLE` item, a `FAIL` on an item already `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — is rejected at rung 26 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and any inspection at all against a `RETIRED` item is rejected at rung 27 with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

**`CASCADE D`.** Whenever an item leaves `SERVICEABLE` or `INSPECTION_DUE` for `OUT_OF_SERVICE` or `RETIRED` — by a failed inspection, by a retirement, or by a damaged return — three hops run. Hop 1 sets the item's `condition_status`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, moves every reservation on that item whose status is `HELD` to `VOIDED_INSPECTION_FAIL`. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, lowers `pooled_held` once per voided reservation and recomputes `pooled_available` accordingly. Reservations already `CHECKED_OUT` are **not** touched — the unit is physically in a participant's hands and comes back through the return path. A retirement straight from `OUT_OF_SERVICE` has no held reservations left to void, so hops 2 and 3 find nothing to do. The item's `version` rises by one, as does the version of each voided reservation. Separately, when the epoch checkpoint moves a serialized item from `SERVICEABLE` to `INSPECTION_DUE`, existing held reservations are left completely alone; the item merely stops satisfying the fit rule for new holds and for any confirmation walk that consults it.

### 3.7. Getting There: Runs, Positions and Fares

A `TransportDispatcher` plans a run against one session, naming the `session_id`, a `pickup_start_at`, a `pickup_end_at` strictly later than it, a `fare_mode` of `FLAT` or `ZONE_MILEAGE`, and the two capacity figures — `accessible_positions`, the wheelchair-securement positions on the vehicle, and `ambulatory_seats`, the ordinary seats — each zero or more. Two fare inputs are optional and both default to 0 when omitted: `flat_fare_cents`, which the flat mode uses and the mileage mode ignores entirely, and `route_miles_x10`, which the mileage mode uses and the flat mode ignores entirely. The run is created with `status` `PLANNED`, `accessible_committed` 0, `accessible_available` equal to `accessible_positions`, `ambulatory_committed` 0 and `ambulatory_available` equal to `ambulatory_seats`.

The two pools are the heart of this subsection: **a free securement position never satisfies a request for an ordinary seat, and a free ordinary seat never satisfies a request for a securement position.** No operation anywhere in the system moves a unit from one pool to the other.

**Booking a position.** A booking names the `transport_run_id`, the `enrollment_id`, a `position_type` of `ACCESSIBLE` or `AMBULATORY`, and the `expected_run_version`. Exactly one position is consumed. The checks run in ladder order. First, cross-reference coherence: the booking's enrolment and the named run must serve the same session, failing which the request is `ERR_PROGRAM_SESSION_MISMATCH`. Then sub-gate E's head rung: the `position_type` must match the participant's `mobility_profile` exactly — a `WHEELCHAIR` participant may take only an `ACCESSIBLE` position and an `AMBULATORY` participant only an `AMBULATORY` seat — failing which it is `ERR_POSITION_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Then the window rung: the run's `pickup_end_at` must fall at or before the session's `start_at` less a 15-minute arrival buffer, and its `pickup_start_at` must fall strictly before its `pickup_end_at`; equality exactly at the buffer boundary is accepted, and a violation is `ERR_TRANSPORT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`. Then the two capacity rungs, of which exactly one applies: an accessible booking needs `accessible_available` of at least 1 or fails with `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_POSITIONS_FULL`, and an ambulatory booking needs `ambulatory_available` of at least 1 or fails with `ERR_AMBULATORY_SEATS_FULL`.

**Pricing the ride.** A booking's `fare_cents` is computed once and pinned at creation; it never moves afterwards even if the run's inputs are later reinterpreted. A surcharge of 250 cents applies when and only when the `position_type` is `ACCESSIBLE`, and it is added **after** any rounding. Under the `FLAT` variant the fare is the run's `flat_fare_cents` plus that surcharge, and no rounding occurs at all because both terms are already whole cents; `route_miles_x10` plays no part. Under the `ZONE_MILEAGE` variant `flat_fare_cents` plays no part; instead take the base fare of 300 cents multiplied by ten, add 85 cents multiplied by the run's `route_miles_x10`, divide the whole sum by ten, round half up to a whole cent, and only then add the surcharge. A run of 85 tenths of a mile therefore prices at 300 times 10 plus 85 times 85, which is 3000 plus 7225, giving 10225 tenths of a cent; divided by ten that is 1022.5, which rounds half up to 1023 cents, and an accessible rider pays 1273. The same run priced flat at 1000 cents would cost that rider 1250 — identical runs priced the two ways differ in cents, which is exactly why `fare_mode` is a declared branch and not a convenience.

**What a booking does to the run.** On creation the matching committed counter rises by one and the matching available counter falls by one, the other pool being untouched; the enrolment's charge has its `transport_fee_cents` and `family_responsibility_cents` recomputed; and the run's `version` rises by one. Immediately afterwards, a run whose `status` is `PLANNED` and whose `accessible_available` **and** `ambulatory_available` have **both** reached 0 moves to `BOARDING_FULL` — a run with one pool empty and the other still open stays `PLANNED`.

**Booking lifecycle.** A `RESERVED` booking is confirmed — by a `TransportDispatcher`, or automatically as a side effect of its enrolment being confirmed — once its enrolment's `enrollment_status` is `CONFIRMED`; confirmation moves no counter, because the position was committed the moment the booking was reserved. From `CONFIRMED` the booking follows its enrolment: to `RIDDEN` when the enrolment is marked attended, which again moves no counter, and to `NO_SHOW` when the enrolment is marked a no-show, which lowers the run's committed counter for that position type and restores the matching available counter. Cancellation is available from `RESERVED` and from `CONFIRMED`, whether requested by a `TransportDispatcher` or an `IntakeCoordinator` or driven by the withdrawal or session-cancellation cascades; either way the run's committed counter for that position type falls by one, the matching available counter is restored, the other pool is untouched, and the enrolment charge's `transport_fee_cents` and `family_responsibility_cents` are recomputed. Both the booking's and the run's `version` rise by one. `RIDDEN`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

Whenever a counter frees on a run whose `status` is `BOARDING_FULL` — through a cancellation or a no-show — and either `accessible_available` or `ambulatory_available` is now above 0, the run moves back to `PLANNED`. The run therefore oscillates between the two states as bookings come and go. A run is moved to `CANCELLED`, from either `PLANNED` or `BOARDING_FULL`, only by the session-cancellation cascade; `CANCELLED` is terminal for a run.

**What sub-gate E means during confirmation.** Sub-gate E is skipped entirely when the enrolment's `transport_requested` is false. When it is true and the enrolment already holds a booking whose status is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RIDDEN` on a run serving this session, both rungs pass, because the position is already committed to that enrolment. When it is true and no such booking exists, the walk looks at the runs serving this session: a participant whose `mobility_profile` is `WHEELCHAIR` needs at least one such run with `accessible_available` of 1 or more, failing which the confirmation is `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_POSITIONS_FULL`, and a participant whose `mobility_profile` is `AMBULATORY` needs at least one such run with `ambulatory_available` of 1 or more, failing which it is `ERR_AMBULATORY_SEATS_FULL`. A session with no run at all and a participant who asked for transport therefore fails at whichever of the two rungs matches their mobility profile.

### 3.8. Scholarship Money, Sibling Discounts and What the Family Owes

A funding source lives inside an operating period, named by `operating_period_id`, and carries a `name`, a `source_kind` of `RESTRICTED_GRANT` or `GENERAL_FUND`, a `priority_rank` of at least 1 that fixes draw order, an `award_mode` of `NEED_PCT` or `FLAT_CAP` that selects how an award is sized, and a `total_budget_cents` of zero or more that is an immutable ceiling. Two mode-specific inputs are optional and both default to 0: `award_pct_bp`, a basis-point figure between 0 and 10000 inclusive used by the percentage mode, and `flat_award_cents`, a per-enrolment cap of zero or more used by the flat mode. The source is created `OPEN` with `available_cents` equal to `total_budget_cents`, `encumbered_cents` 0 and `disbursed_cents` 0. Restricted grants are conventionally given lower `priority_rank` values so that they are drawn before general funds, but the ordering is whatever the ranks say — the enumeration value itself grants no precedence.

A source moves along its availability axis without anyone asking. It goes from `OPEN` to `EXHAUSTED` the instant an encumbrance leaves its `available_cents` at exactly 0, and that move lapses every award on it whose `status` is `PROPOSED` to `LAPSED_NO_FUNDS`. It returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `OPEN` the instant a release lifts `available_cents` above 0 — and **already-lapsed awards are not revived by that return**; recovering them requires creating fresh awards. A `FundingOfficer` may close a source from `OPEN` or from `EXHAUSTED`, which lapses its `PROPOSED` awards in the same way, and the period-close cascade closes sources on the officer's behalf. `CLOSED` is terminal. Every one of these moves raises the source's `version` by one.

**Choosing a source automatically.** When an award is created without naming a source, the selector picks one. The candidate set is every source whose `status` is `OPEN`, whose `available_cents` is strictly above 0, and whose operating period's `status` is `OPEN`. Candidates are ranked by `priority_rank` ascending; ties are broken first by `available_cents` descending and then by `created_at` ascending. When the candidate set is empty, the award creation fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (409). The same selector is exposed as a read: given an `enrollment_id` it answers with that `enrollment_id`, the winning `funding_source_id`, the `funding_source_version` the caller must echo back as `expected_funding_source_version`, that source's `award_mode`, the `net_need_cents` for the enrolment, and the `projected_award_cents` the sizing rule would produce — and it raises the same `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` when nothing qualifies.

**Sizing an award.** Net need for an enrolment is its charge's `program_fee_cents` — the sliding-scale programme fee before any scholarship, and deliberately not the transport-inclusive family total. Sizing has three paths, tested in this order. When the request supplies `requested_amount_cents`, that number is the award amount, subject to the two fiscal rungs: it must not exceed net need, or the request fails with `ERR_AWARD_EXCEEDS_NEED` (422), and the source must hold at least that much in `available_cents`, or it fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. When `requested_amount_cents` is omitted and the source's `award_mode` is `NEED_PCT`, take net need multiplied by `award_pct_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up — then clip that raw figure down to the source's `available_cents` if it exceeds it. When `requested_amount_cents` is omitted and the mode is `FLAT_CAP`, the amount is the smallest of three numbers: net need, the source's `flat_award_cents`, and the source's `available_cents`; no percentage is ever taken. The two modes really do differ on identical inputs — a net need of 5000 cents against a source with 3000 cents available awards 2500 under a 5000-basis-point `NEED_PCT` source but 3000 under a `FLAT_CAP` source whose cap is 4000. Whichever path was taken, an amount that resolves to 0 cents is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`.

A successful creation files the award in `status` `PROPOSED` with `amount_cents` pinned, `encumbered_cents` 0, `disbursed_cents` 0, `awarded_by_user_id` stamped with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, and `disbursed_by_user_id` null. The source's `version` rises by one. When the target enrolment's `funding_status` was `UNFUNDED` or `RELEASED`, and its `enrollment_status` is none of `WITHDRAWN`, `DENIED`, `ATTENDED` or `NO_SHOW`, the new award returns that enrolment's money axis to `QUOTED` — this is the re-entry edge that makes the funding axis a loop rather than a pipeline, and it evolves entirely independently of the roster axis on the same row.

**Encumbering.** Moving an award from `PROPOSED` to `ENCUMBERED` — whether an officer asks for it or enrolment confirmation does it automatically — requires that the source is not `CLOSED`, that its operating period's `status` is `OPEN`, and that its `available_cents` is at least the award's `amount_cents`; the first two failures report `ERR_FUNDING_PERIOD_CLOSED` and the third `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. An `EXHAUSTED` but not closed source passes the first rung and fails the second, which is deliberate. On success the award's `encumbered_cents` is set to its `amount_cents`, the source's `available_cents` falls by that amount and its `encumbered_cents` rises by it, the source may in consequence move to `EXHAUSTED`, the enrolment's `funding_status` advances from `QUOTED` to `ENCUMBERED`, and the enrolment's charge is recomputed. The award, the source and the enrolment each gain one version.

**Disbursing.** Payment out takes an `amount_cents` that must be at least 1 and no more than the award's `encumbered_cents` minus its `disbursed_cents`, failing which it is `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_ENCUMBRANCE` (422) — and it must be performed by a different `FundingOfficer` from the one recorded in `awarded_by_user_id`. A payment strictly smaller than the outstanding encumbrance moves an `ENCUMBERED` award to `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED`; a payment exactly equal to the outstanding encumbrance moves an `ENCUMBERED` award straight to `DISBURSED`, and moves a `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` award to `DISBURSED`. In every case the award's `disbursed_cents` rises by the paid amount, the source's `encumbered_cents` falls by it and its `disbursed_cents` rises by it, and `disbursed_by_user_id` is stamped with the paying officer's `X-User-ID`. When the award reaches `DISBURSED`, the enrolment's `funding_status` advances from `ENCUMBERED` to `DISBURSED`, which is terminal on that axis. `DISBURSED` is terminal for the award too.

**Releasing.** Returning money to a source has two variants and they move different numbers of cents. From `ENCUMBERED`, the `FULL` variant releases the award's entire `encumbered_cents` and drops that field to 0. From `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED`, the `REMAINDER` variant releases only `encumbered_cents` minus `disbursed_cents` — the not-yet-paid part — and leaves `encumbered_cents` sitting at `disbursed_cents`. In both variants the source's `encumbered_cents` falls by the released amount and its `available_cents` rises by the same, and **`disbursed_cents` is never touched by a release**, on either the award or the source. A release may lift the source from `EXHAUSTED` back to `OPEN`. A `PROPOSED` award can also be released — by the withdrawal cascade — in which case zero cents move, because a proposed award never held any. `RELEASED` and `LAPSED_NO_FUNDS` are both terminal. From `ENCUMBERED` the enrolment's money axis follows the award to `RELEASED`, which drops the charge's `scholarship_applied_cents` to 0 and recomputes `copay_cents` and `family_responsibility_cents`.

**The sibling discount.** Before the family total is computed, the charge's `sibling_discount_bp` is derived. Count the distinct participants who hold a `CONFIRMED` enrolment on this same session and whose `household_id` equals this participant's `household_id` — counting this participant among them. When that count is 2 or more, `sibling_discount_bp` is 1500; otherwise it is 0. The value looks circular, because confirming one sibling changes the other sibling's discount, so the evaluation order is pinned: first set the enrolment's `enrollment_status` to `CONFIRMED` and update the session counters, then recompute this enrolment's charge so it counts itself, then recompute every other charge whose enrolment is `CONFIRMED` on the same session and whose participant shares the `household_id`, in `created_at` ascending order, and then stop — exactly one sweep, no further passes. Withdrawal is the mirror image: set the enrolment `WITHDRAWN`, void this charge, then re-run that same household sweep in `created_at` ascending order.

**What the family owes.** The application order is fixed and load-bearing: the sliding-scale programme fee first, then the scholarship, then the copay that remains, then the transport fee, and the sibling discount applied last to both money lines. Concretely, `copay_cents` is `program_fee_cents` minus `scholarship_applied_cents`, floored at 0; `transport_fee_cents` is the sum of `fare_cents` over this enrolment's bookings whose status is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `RIDDEN`; and then the programme's `rounding_policy` picks one of two ways to combine them.

- Under `ROUND_PER_LINE`, discount each line separately and round each separately: take `copay_cents` multiplied by 10000 minus the discount basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up; take `transport_fee_cents` through the identical treatment; and add the two rounded results. Two half-cent fragments can therefore both round up.
- Under `ROUND_ON_TOTAL`, add `copay_cents` and `transport_fee_cents` first, then discount and round the single sum: the total multiplied by 10000 minus the discount basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The two fragments now combine or cancel inside one rounding step.

On identical inputs the two policies routinely land one cent apart, which is why `rounding_policy` is declared per programme rather than assumed. With a 1500-basis-point sibling discount, a `copay_cents` of 3333 and a `transport_fee_cents` of 1023, the per-line policy gives 2833 plus 870, or 3703, while the on-total policy gives 4356 discounted to 3702.6, rounding to 3703 as well — but shift the copay by a single cent and the two answers separate.

The charge is recomputed, in full, on every one of these events: enrolment confirmation, enrolment withdrawal, award encumbrance, award release, transport-booking creation, transport-booking cancellation, and the recording of a family payment. Each recompute refreshes `scholarship_applied_cents`, `copay_cents`, `transport_fee_cents`, `sibling_discount_bp`, `family_responsibility_cents`, `balance_cents` and `status`, and raises that charge's `version` by one.

**Family payments.** A `FundingOfficer` records a payment of `amount_cents` against a charge. The paid amount is added to `amount_paid_cents`, then `balance_cents` and the settlement `status` are re-derived. Because `balance_cents` is clamped at 0, an overpayment leaves the balance at 0 and the charge `SETTLED` rather than negative. Payments are one of the seven financial writes, so they are refused with `ERR_PERIOD_RESTRICTED` while the current period is `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSING`, and with `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` while it is `FROZEN` or `CLOSED`. Each accepted payment raises the charge's `version` by one.

### 3.9. Progress, Mastery and the Session Debrief

Goal-setting belongs to the `IntakeCoordinator`. A goal names its `participant_id`, a `title` stating the goal, a `measure` naming what is scored, a `baseline_value` and a `target_value` each between 1 and 5 inclusive, a `mastery_mode` of `N_OF_M` or `CONSECUTIVE`, a `mastery_criterion_score` between 1 and 5 at or above which a rating counts as a hit, and a `review_due_on` date. Two mastery parameters are optional: `mastery_window_m`, the window length, defaults to 5 when omitted, and `mastery_hits_n`, the number of hits required, defaults to 4; both must be at least 1 and `mastery_hits_n` must not exceed `mastery_window_m`. The goal is filed `ACTIVE` with `rating_count` 0.

Ratings are recorded by the `ActivityLead` who ran the session, and a rating is one of only two entities in this system with no `updated_at` and no `version` — it is an append-only observation. The payload carries the `session_id`, a `score` between 1 and 5 inclusive, and an optional `note` that defaults to the empty string. The `rated_by_staff_id` is not supplied by the client; it is taken from the acting lead's `X-User-ID`. Three checks guard the write, in ladder order: the goal's participant must hold an enrolment on the rated session at all, or the cross-reference fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_SESSION_MISMATCH`; the goal must not already carry a rating for that same session, or it is `ERR_DUPLICATE_GOAL_RATING`; and the participant's enrolment on that session must have `enrollment_status` `ATTENDED`, or it is `ERR_RATING_SESSION_NOT_ATTENDED`. On success the server pins `rated_session_start_at` as a copy of the rated session's `start_at`, raises the goal's `rating_count` by one, re-derives mastery, and raises the goal's `version` by one.

**Deriving mastery.** Order the goal's ratings by `rated_session_start_at` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` descending — note that this is the order of the *sessions rated*, not the order the ratings were entered, so a rating recorded late about an early session sorts to the back. Under `N_OF_M`, take the first `mastery_window_m` entries of that ordering as the window; the goal is mastered when the window is full — that is, when at least `mastery_window_m` ratings exist — and the number of entries in it whose `score` is at or above `mastery_criterion_score` is at least `mastery_hits_n`. One weak session in the middle of a strong run is therefore forgiven. Under `CONSECUTIVE`, take the first `mastery_hits_n` entries of that same ordering; the goal is mastered when there are at least that many ratings and **every** one of them is at or above the criterion. The very same rating history that masters under `N_OF_M` fails under `CONSECUTIVE` whenever the newest rating is a miss.

Mastery is explicitly **not monotone**. A goal moves from `ACTIVE` to `MASTERED` when the recomputation says true and from `MASTERED` back to `ACTIVE` when it says false, and both directions are driven only by the system, immediately after a rating is persisted. A rating anchored to an older session can enter the window, evict a hit, and knock a goal back out of mastery; a later strong rating can pull it back in. Both moves raise the goal's `version` by one. From either `ACTIVE` or `MASTERED` a goal is discontinued when its participant exits, moving to `DISCONTINUED`, which is terminal and is reachable only that way.

**The staffing debrief.** A computed read exists for exactly the questions a coordinator asks after the fact: for one session it answers with that `session_id`, the `pooled_support_points` total actually summed for the current planned roster, `required_group_staff_planned`, `required_group_staff_delivered` (null until the session is delivered), `assigned_group_staff_count`, `one_to_one_committed`, `one_to_one_staffed` and `staffing_gap`. Reading it changes nothing and never advances a version.
