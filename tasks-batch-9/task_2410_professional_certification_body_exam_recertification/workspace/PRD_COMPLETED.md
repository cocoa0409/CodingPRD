# Professional Certification Body API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A professional certification body lives or dies by the defensibility of its credential. Every claim it makes in public — that a named person passed a psychometrically sound examination, that the examination measured what a job task analysis said the occupation requires, that the holder has kept current since — has to be reconstructable from an audit trail years after the fact. Today that trail is scattered across item-banking spreadsheets, a scheduling vendor's portal, a shared mailbox of education transcripts, and a recertification tracker that nobody trusts. When a candidate disputes a score, or a regulator asks how many operational items covered a blueprint domain on a given publication date, the answer takes weeks to assemble and frequently disagrees with itself.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that spans the whole credential lifecycle: job task analysis feeding a blueprint, item writing and blind peer review, response-data ingestion with psychometric monitoring and retirement of weak items, eligibility application review with deterministic audit sampling, test-centre and online-proctored scheduling with security incident handling, standard setting and cut-score adoption, score reporting and certificant registry publication, public verification, recertification by continuing education credit or by re-examination, and the disciplinary process that can suspend or revoke a credential outright.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties enforced on the acting user, not merely on the acting role**: an item's author may never review it, an application's auditor may never be one of its verifiers, a panellist may never adopt the cut score derived from the panel they sat on. The second is **determinism**: every temporal rule is evaluated against an explicit logical clock rather than wall time, every derived total is re-derived from its children rather than incrementally patched, and every sampling decision is driven by a monotonic counter rather than randomness. Both commitments exist so that any two replays of the same request sequence produce byte-identical records.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every collection and every operation. Where §3 describes what a business process means, this section fixes the shape of the wire contract, the order in which requests are rejected, and the numeric discipline applied to every computed field.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the public verification read carries two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which that user is acting. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, or supplies it empty, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent, unrecognised, or not among the roles the addressed operation admits is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The single exception is the public registry verification read, `GET /registry/verify`, which is reachable with no headers at all and never returns either of those two errors.

Ten roles exist, and their names are used verbatim throughout this document: `ProgramAdmin`, `ItemSpecialist`, `Psychometrician`, `Panelist`, `EligibilityReviewer`, `ExamOperations`, `Candidate`, `Certificant`, `EthicsOfficer`, and `Public`.

Eight of the ten are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `ItemSpecialist`, `Psychometrician`, `Panelist`, `EligibilityReviewer`, `ExamOperations`, `EthicsOfficer`, or `Public`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is nevertheless recorded wherever a workflow needs to remember who acted — as `author_user_id` on an item, as an entry in an item's `approver_user_ids`, as the `user_id` inside an application's `verifier_user_ids` entries, as `auditor_user_id` on an audited application, as `reviewer_user_id` or `audit_verifier_user_id` on a continuing-education activity, and as `opened_by_user_id` on a disciplinary case — and segregation-of-duties comparisons are made between those recorded values and the current `X-User-ID`.

Two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Candidate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Candidate`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Candidate` compare the resource's `candidate_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Candidate`-role caller may create, schedule, and cancel the schedule of only those exam registrations, and submit only those eligibility applications, whose `candidate_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; a violation is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Certificant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Candidate` behind the credential — that is, ownership is established by comparing the addressed `Certificant`'s `candidate_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Certificant`-role caller may therefore act only on recertification cycles, continuing-education activities, and disciplinary cases that hang off a `Certificant` whose `candidate_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; the same `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` guards claiming credit against another holder's cycle, closing it, electing re-examination on it, reinstating it, and responding to another holder's disciplinary case. For the disciplinary response in particular, the acting user must be the respondent: the case's `certificant_id` must resolve to a `Certificant` whose `candidate_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

Role membership is the coarse gate; segregation of duties is the fine one, and the two are independent. Two different `ItemSpecialist` users are required to approve one item, and the author — a third `ItemSpecialist` — may be neither of them. Role checks always precede ownership checks, and ownership checks always precede segregation-of-duties checks; the full ordering is given in §2.7.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Collection reads are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records skipped before the page begins and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the accepted range, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either parameter, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `offset` has no upper bound, and an offset beyond the end of the result set simply yields an empty page. Every list response echoes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page. Unless a specific operation says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock moves only when it is explicitly set, records sharing a `created_at` are the normal case: they are ordered among themselves by the sequence in which they were created.

Beyond pagination, each collection accepts a set of exact-match filters. Programs may be filtered by `credential_code`, `status`, and `blueprint_posture`. Items may be filtered by `program_id`, `domain_code`, `status`, and `flag_reason`, and the same four filters less `program_id` apply when reading the items of one program. Forms may be filtered by `program_id`, `status`, and `panel_status`, and the per-program form read accepts `status`. Candidates may be filtered by `email`, `status`, and `bar_reason`. Applications may be filtered by `candidate_id`, `program_id`, `status`, `education_status`, and `experience_status`. Exam sessions may be filtered by `modality`, `center_code`, `room_code`, `proctor_pool_code`, and `status`. Exam registrations may be filtered by `candidate_id`, `application_id`, `form_id`, `session_id`, `seat_status`, `score_status`, and `outcome`. Security incidents may be filtered by `status`, `severity`, and `incident_type`. Certificants may be filtered by `candidate_id`, `program_id`, `status`, `publication_status`, and `certificate_number`. Recertification cycles may be filtered by `certificant_id`, `reexam_registration_id`, `status`, and `path`, and the per-certificant cycle read accepts `status` and `path`. Continuing-education activities may be filtered by `status`, `category`, and `counted`. Disciplinary cases may be filtered by `certificant_id`, `status`, `outcome`, and `allegation_type`, and the per-certificant case read accepts `status` and `outcome`. Every one of these filters is matched verbatim against the stored field, with a boolean rendered as `true` or `false` and an integer as its decimal text; a filter value that matches nothing — including an unrecognised enum member — yields an empty page rather than an error, and the only filter value that is itself malformed is a `counted` value other than `true`, `1`, `false`, or `0`, which is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A query parameter that is not among the filters an operation accepts is ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource in this system carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, the former fixed at creation and the latter refreshed on every mutation that changes the record. Both of those two stamps are read from the **logical clock** described below rather than from wall time — `created_at` is the clock's reading at the instant of creation and `updated_at` is the clock's reading at the instant of the mutation — so a mutation applied while the clock has not been moved since the record was created refreshes `updated_at` to a value identical to `created_at`, and only an intervening `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` can make the two differ. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations reached indirectly through a cascade; within the servicing of one request a record advances by exactly one however many times that request's hops rewrite it, and a record created by the request stays at 1 even when a later hop of the same request writes to it. No entity in this system is exempt: certification programs, items, exam forms, candidates, eligibility applications, exam sessions, exam registrations, security incidents, certificants, recertification cycles, continuing-education activities, and disciplinary cases all expose `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

All temporal reasoning is done against a **logical clock** rather than wall time. The clock defaults to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and is moved by an administrative test-driving capability, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which takes a single `current_utc` instant and echoes it back verbatim as the `current_utc` of its `data` object, exactly as the client wrote it rather than renormalised. Every timestamp a client supplies anywhere in this API is parsed as an ISO 8601 instant: a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date is read as midnight UTC, an explicit offset is converted to UTC, and a value carrying no zone at all is taken to be UTC already; anything unparseable is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every expiry window, grace period, retake wait, check-in window, session-closure test, cycle boundary, and suspension release is evaluated against that clock. Setting the clock is deliberately inert: it never mutates a stored record. Time-driven state changes are materialised only by the maintenance run described in §3.11, which is why an application can sit past its expiry instant and still read as `APPROVED` until the run processes it.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three operations mutate a record by way of a parent whose state the client must have observed first, and each carries an explicit optimistic-concurrency guard naming that parent's `version`. In all three the guard is a required body field, not an optional one, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Claiming a seat — `POST /exam-registrations/{id}/schedule` — requires `expected_session_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ExamSession`. If the session's current `version` differs, the claim is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Seat claims from different registrations are all individually legitimate, so the remaining-capacity figure the client based its choice on is precisely the thing that can have moved underneath it; the stale version is the only deterministic signal that the client's view of the session is out of date.

Adopting a cut score — `POST /forms/{id}/adopt-cut-score` — requires `expected_form_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ExamForm`. If the form's current `version` differs, the adoption is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-adoption is legitimate while a form remains unpublished, and each adoption supersedes the previous `raw_cut_score`, so a writer whose view of the form predates a superseding adoption is reasoning from a stale panel picture and must re-read before adopting again.

Closing a recertification cycle — `POST /recertification-cycles/{id}/close` — requires `expected_certificant_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning `Certificant`. If the certificant's current `version` differs, the close is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Closing successive cycles is legitimate and each close advances the certificant's `valid_through` and bumps its `version`, which is why this guard cannot be collapsed into a state precondition on the cycle itself.

No other operation accepts a version field. An endpoint not named above must not require, and must not honour, an `expected_*_version` input.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and it is applied at the points named below and nowhere else. Intermediate quotients are never pre-rounded.

Item statistics are carried to four decimal places: an item's `p_value` and `discrimination_index` are each rounded half up to four decimals, and every subsequent threshold comparison is made against those already-rounded four-decimal figures. Standard-setting intermediates are carried to six decimal places: the trimmed panel mean and the standard error of that mean are each rounded half up to six decimals before the cut-score arithmetic continues. A form's `raw_cut_score` and a registration's `scaled_score` are integers, obtained by rounding half up once at the final step and then clamping. Continuing-education credit is carried to two decimal places, and the two-decimal rounding happens twice: once per activity when its award is computed, and once more when the cycle total is assembled. Monetary amounts are integer cents throughout; the administrative component of a reinstatement fee is rounded half up to whole cents. Counts, sequence numbers, and percentage tolerance comparisons are pure integer arithmetic with no division and no rounding at all.

### 2.7. Request Validation Order and Rejection Precedence

Validation is layered, and the layers are evaluated in a fixed ascending order. **The first violated rule wins and its error is returned; no later rule is evaluated.** This ordering is normative — when a request violates two rules at once, the returned error is determined by this list, not by which check is cheaper to run.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` present and non-empty on every endpoint except the public verification read → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorisation.** `X-User-Role` present and among the endpoint's declared roles, on every endpoint except the public verification read → otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves to a stored record → otherwise the matching 404: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Body-reference existence.** Every foreign identifier carried in a request body resolves to a stored record, with the 404 that matches the referenced collection. This layer guards item creation, form assembly, application submission, registration creation, seat claiming, and disciplinary case opening. A reference that is absent, null, or not a string is treated exactly as one that fails to resolve, so it too answers with that collection's 404 rather than with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and where an operation carries several references they are resolved in a fixed order, the first failure deciding: `program_id` for item creation; `program_id`, then the entries of `item_ids` left to right, then the entries of `pretest_item_ids` left to right, for form assembly; `candidate_id` then `program_id` for application submission; `application_id` then `form_id` for registration creation; `session_id` for a seat claim; and `certificant_id` for disciplinary case opening.
5. **Candidate ownership.** A `Candidate`-role caller acts only on its own registrations and applications → otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This layer guards registration creation, seat claiming, and seat cancellation.
6. **Certificant ownership.** A `Certificant`-role caller acts only on cycles, credit activities, and disciplinary cases belonging to a `Certificant` whose `candidate_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID` → otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This layer guards claiming credit against a cycle, closing a cycle, electing re-examination, reinstating a lapsed cycle, and responding to a disciplinary case.
7. **Schema.** All required body fields present, enum values within their declared sets, integers non-negative, array sizes within their declared bounds, timestamps ISO 8601 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write operation.
8. **Blueprint weights.** The `weight_pct` values across `domain_specs` sum to exactly 100 and every `domain_code` in the array is distinct → otherwise `ERR_BLUEPRINT_WEIGHTS_INVALID` (400). Checked both when a program is created and again when it is activated.
9. **Session window shape.** A session's `starts_at` is strictly before its `ends_at`, and `seat_capacity` is at least 1 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
10. **Credential code uniqueness.** A new program's `credential_code` is not already in use by a program whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` → otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_CODE_TAKEN` (409). A `credential_code` held only by `SUPERSEDED` programs is free to reuse.
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_session_version`, `expected_form_version`, or `expected_certificant_version` equals the parent's current `version`, as described in §2.5 → otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
12. **Transition legality.** The addressed record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action operation.
13. **Program active.** The owning certification program's `status` is `ACTIVE` → otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to application submission and form publication. Registration creation is the deliberate relaxation: because an application already approved under a program that has since been superseded stays usable (§3.1), a registration is admitted when the cited application's program is either `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and only a `DRAFT` program refuses it with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`.
14. **Form published.** The cited exam form's `status` is `PUBLISHED` → otherwise `ERR_FORM_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). Applies to registration creation, seat claiming, and check-in.
15. **Application approved.** The cited eligibility application's `status` is `APPROVED` → otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to registration creation.
16. **Certificant active.** The owning certificant's `status` is `ACTIVE`, not `SUSPENDED`, `EXPIRED`, or `REVOKED` → otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to claiming credit against a cycle, closing a cycle, and reviewing a credit claim.
17. **Revocation finality.** A `REVOKED` certificant admits no further disciplinary or recertification action → otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED` (409). Applies to opening a disciplinary case, requesting a response, deciding a case, and reinstating a lapsed cycle.
18. **Dimension freshness.** The eligibility dimension named in a verification request is still `PENDING` → otherwise `ERR_DIMENSION_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (409).
19. **Segregation of duties**, evaluated before any temporal or capacity check → otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Six distinct prohibitions live in this layer: an item's reviewer may not be its `author_user_id`; a second approver may not already appear in that item's `approver_user_ids`; an application's auditor may not appear as a `user_id` in its `verifier_user_ids`; a credit activity's audit verifier may not be its `reviewer_user_id`; a cut-score adopter may not appear in the form's `panelist_user_ids`; and the officer deciding a revocation may not be the case's `opened_by_user_id`. The layer guards item review, item submission for review, application audit decisions, credit audit verification, cut-score adoption, and disciplinary decisions.
20. **Candidate bar.** A `BARRED` candidate may neither register, claim a seat, nor check in → otherwise `ERR_CANDIDATE_BARRED` (409).
21. **Security hold.** A score may not be released while an `OPEN` security incident exists on the registration, and responses may not be submitted while `score_status` is `WITHHELD` → otherwise `ERR_SCORE_UNDER_SECURITY_HOLD` (409).
22. **Item provenance on a form.** Every item cited by a form belongs to the same program and its `domain_code` appears in that program's `domain_specs` → otherwise `ERR_ITEM_DOMAIN_MISMATCH` (422).
23. **Item form-eligibility.** Every scored item cited by a form is in `APPROVED`, `PRETEST`, or `OPERATIONAL` status — never `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, or `RETIRED` → otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FORM` (422).
24. **Pretest subset.** Every entry of `pretest_item_ids` also appears in `item_ids` → otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_FORM` (422).
25. **Panel membership.** A submitted rating's `panelist_user_id` appears in the form's `panelist_user_ids` → otherwise `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ON_PANEL` (422).
26. **Program agreement.** The cited form and the cited application belong to the same certification program → otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` (422).
27. **Room window.** Test-centre sessions sharing a `center_code` and `room_code` pair may not overlap → otherwise `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
28. **Candidate window.** A candidate may not hold two seats whose buffered windows intersect → otherwise `ERR_CANDIDATE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
29. **Session closure.** A session whose `starts_at` has been reached or passed admits neither new nor cancelled seat claims → otherwise `ERR_SESSION_CLOSED` (409).
30. **Check-in window.** Check-in falls inside the permitted window → otherwise `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
31. **Eligibility currency.** The cited application's `eligibility_expires_on` is not in the past at registration time → otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_EXPIRED` (409).
32. **Retake wait.** The mandatory wait since the latest consumed attempt has elapsed → otherwise `ERR_RETAKE_WAIT_ACTIVE` (409).
33. **Activity window.** A credit claim's `activity_date` falls inside its cycle's accrual window → otherwise `ERR_ACTIVITY_OUTSIDE_CYCLE` (422).
34. **Blueprint sufficiency.** A form may not be published while its program's `blueprint_posture` is `DEFICIENT` → otherwise `ERR_BLUEPRINT_DEFICIENT` (422).
35. **Form length.** The number of entries in `item_ids` equals the program's `form_length` exactly → otherwise `ERR_FORM_LENGTH_MISMATCH` (422).
36. **Panel quorum.** Enough ratings exist and at least one survives trimming → otherwise `ERR_PANEL_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (422).
37. **One rating per panellist.** A panellist submits at most one rating per form → otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANELIST_RATING` (409).
38. **Attempt ceiling.** Consumed attempts on the application are below the program's `max_attempts_per_cycle` → otherwise `ERR_MAX_ATTEMPTS_REACHED` (422).
39. **Credit sufficiency.** A cycle is closable only once its credits meet its requirement, or a passing re-examination is on file → otherwise `ERR_CREDITS_NOT_SATISFIED` (422).
40. **Due process.** A sanction or a revocation requires at least one completed response round → otherwise `ERR_DUE_PROCESS_INCOMPLETE` (422).
41. **Raw score range.** The submitted `raw_score` is at least 0 and at most the form's `scored_item_count` → otherwise `ERR_RAW_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).
42. **Statistics eligibility.** An item accepts response data only while its `status` is `APPROVED`, `PRETEST`, `OPERATIONAL`, or `FLAGGED` → otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_STATS` (422).
43. **Operational positions.** Every scored position of a form resolves to an `OPERATIONAL` item at publication time → otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (422).
44. **Exam fee.** The tendered `fee_paid_cents` is at least the program's `exam_fee_cents` → otherwise `ERR_EXAM_FEE_INSUFFICIENT` (402).
45. **Reinstatement fee.** The tendered `payment_cents` is at least the cycle's assessed `reinstatement_fee_cents` → otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_UNPAID` (402).
46. **Seat availability.** The session's `seats_reserved` is strictly below its `seat_capacity` → otherwise `ERR_SESSION_FULL` (409).
47. **Proctor pool ceiling.** The aggregate committed capacity of overlapping online-proctored sessions on one pool, including the new one, stays within the pool ceiling → otherwise `ERR_PROCTOR_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).

Five precedence outcomes are worth stating explicitly, because each is a case where the intuitive answer is the wrong one. Claiming a seat in a session that is both full and already started returns `ERR_SESSION_CLOSED`, not `ERR_SESSION_FULL`, because closure sits at layer 29 and capacity at layer 46. Registering with an insufficient fee against a form that is not published returns `ERR_FORM_NOT_PUBLISHED`, not `ERR_EXAM_FEE_INSUFFICIENT`, because form state sits at layer 14 and the fee at layer 44. A barred candidate claiming a seat that would also collide with another of their sittings returns `ERR_CANDIDATE_BARRED`, not `ERR_CANDIDATE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, because the bar sits at layer 20 and the window collision at layer 28. Assembling a form whose item list is both the wrong length and contains an item from another program returns `ERR_ITEM_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`, not `ERR_FORM_LENGTH_MISMATCH`, because provenance sits at layer 22 and length at layer 35. And a stale `expected_form_version` on a form that has already moved to `PUBLISHED` returns `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because the concurrency guard sits at layer 11 and transition legality at layer 12.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Collections in this API are flat: every entity is addressed as a top-level collection, and relationships between them are carried as explicit identifiers inside request bodies rather than as URL nesting. The nested paths that do exist fall into two families. The first is **reverse-reachability reads** — the items of a program, the forms of a program, the security incidents of a registration, the recertification cycles of a certificant, the disciplinary cases of a certificant, the credit activities of a cycle. The second is **action sub-paths** hanging off a single record, such as activating a program or adjudicating an incident.

Wherever a path contains more than one segment identifier, the segments are validated **left to right**, and the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides the error. Reading the credit activities of a cycle whose identifier does not resolve returns `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` regardless of the filters supplied; reading the incidents of a registration that does not exist returns `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`. A malformed pagination parameter, or a malformed boolean filter value, on a path whose identifiers all resolve returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter value that is merely unmatched returns an empty page as described in §2.3.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Invariants

Many operations in this system touch more than one record. Where §3 describes a cascade, the cascade is part of the operation's contract and not an eventual background reconciliation: by the time the response is emitted, every record the cascade names has already been updated, its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Consistency is immediate.

Derived fields are always **re-derived from scratch**, never incrementally patched. A program's `operational_item_count`, `deficient_domain_codes`, and `blueprint_posture` are recomputed from the current population of that program's items whenever an item crosses the `OPERATIONAL` boundary in either direction. A form's `retired_item_count` is recounted from the current status of every identifier in `item_ids`. A session's `seats_reserved` is recounted from the registrations pointing at it. A cycle's `credits_applied` is recomputed as the capped sum of its counted children plus any carried-in credit. Two counters are the deliberate exception, because their whole purpose is monotonicity: `verified_application_counter` and `certificants_issued_count` on a program only ever increase, never decrease, and are never recomputed from the current population. Re-derivation is also silent when it finds nothing to change: a program whose posture, a form whose `retired_item_count`, or a cycle whose credit total and satisfaction state come back identical is left entirely alone, with neither `updated_at` nor `version` moving, whereas a session whose seats are recounted by a cascade is always stamped even if the recount confirms the stored figure.

### 2.10. Status Codes and Write Semantics

Operations that bring a new resource into existence answer with **HTTP 201**. Operations that modify an existing resource — every action sub-path, the logical-clock capability, and the maintenance run — answer with **HTTP 200**. Both return the affected resource inside `data`, with the exception of the public verification read, the logical-clock capability, and the maintenance run, which each return their own purpose-built object described in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Definition, Blueprint Coverage and Activation

Everything in this system hangs off a certification program, and a program is the durable record of one job task analysis. A `ProgramAdmin` creates it with the credential's `name`, a short `credential_code` of between 2 and 12 characters, each of which must be an uppercase letter or a digit — any lowercase letter, space, hyphen or other punctuation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the `jta_version` label of the analysis that produced the blueprint (for example `JTA-2026.1`), a `form_length` of at least 1 giving the exact number of positions every form of this program must carry, a non-empty `accepted_education_credentials` list drawn from `DIPLOMA`, `ASSOCIATE`, `BACHELOR`, `MASTER`, and `DOCTORATE`, and the blueprint itself as `domain_specs`. That blueprint holds between 1 and 8 rows; each row names a `domain_code`, a `weight_pct`, and a `min_operational_items` count of at least zero. The `weight_pct` values are integers and must sum to exactly 100, and no `domain_code` may repeat inside the array — a violation of either constraint is `ERR_BLUEPRINT_WEIGHTS_INVALID`. A new program is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_CODE_TAKEN` when another program whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` already holds the same `credential_code`; a code held only by superseded programs is free to reuse.

The rest of the program record is a bank of tuning knobs, each optional at creation and each with a governing default. Psychometric behaviour is set by `psychometric_stat_basis`, which is either `POOLED` or `OPERATIONAL_ONLY` and defaults to `OPERATIONAL_ONLY`; by `min_discrimination`, defaulting to 0.15; by `min_p_value`, defaulting to 0.20; by `max_p_value`, defaulting to 0.95; and by `min_responses_for_stats`, defaulting to 30. Form durability is set by `form_retirement_tolerance_pct`, defaulting to 10. Standard setting is governed by `min_panelists`, defaulting to 5, by `panel_trim_count`, defaulting to 1, and by `cut_score_sem_multiplier`, defaulting to 0.5. Eligibility screening is governed by `education_recency_years`, defaulting to 10, by `min_experience_months`, defaulting to 24 — that is, two years of qualifying practice — by `audit_every_nth`, defaulting to 3 and never below 1, and by `eligibility_valid_days`, defaulting to 365. Examination commerce is governed by `exam_fee_cents`, defaulting to 45000 — a fee of four hundred and fifty dollars — by `max_attempts_per_cycle`, defaulting to 3, and by `retake_wait_days`, defaulting to 45. Recertification is governed by `cycle_length_months`, defaulting to 36 — a three-year cycle — by `required_ceu_credits`, defaulting to 60, by `ceu_credit_accrual_basis`, which is either `STRICT_CYCLE` or `CARRYOVER_ALLOWED` and defaults to `STRICT_CYCLE`, by `ceu_carryover_max_credits`, defaulting to 10, and by `recert_grace_days`, defaulting to 90 — roughly a quarter's forbearance after a cycle's nominal end.

A freshly created program has `status` `DRAFT`. Its `operational_item_count` starts at 0, its `verified_application_counter` starts at 0, and its `certificants_issued_count` starts at 0. Its `blueprint_posture` is computed immediately and will therefore read `DEFICIENT` unless every row of `domain_specs` asks for zero operational items, in which case it reads `COMPLETE`. Posture is derived, never supplied: `operational_item_count` is the number of the program's items whose `status` is `OPERATIONAL`; `deficient_domain_codes` is the list of those `domain_code` values from `domain_specs` for which the count of the program's `OPERATIONAL` items carrying that `domain_code` is strictly less than the row's `min_operational_items`, sorted in ascending order for determinism; and `blueprint_posture` is `COMPLETE` when `deficient_domain_codes` is empty and `DEFICIENT` otherwise. Items in `PRETEST`, `FLAGGED`, or `RETIRED` status never count toward coverage, and a domain whose `min_operational_items` is zero can never be deficient.

Activation is the `ProgramAdmin`'s gate, optionally annotated with an `activation_note`, and it moves the program `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. The program must currently be `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the blueprint weights are re-checked at this moment, so a program whose `domain_specs` no longer sum to exactly 100 is refused with `ERR_BLUEPRINT_WEIGHTS_INVALID` even though it was accepted at creation. Activation carries a supersession side effect: any *other* program holding the same `credential_code` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and both programs have their `version` incremented by 1. Activation also recomputes `blueprint_posture`, `operational_item_count`, and `deficient_domain_codes` on the newly activated program.

Supersession is a system-driven transition with no direct trigger of its own — it happens only because a sibling program with the same `credential_code` was activated. Its consequences are asymmetric and worth spelling out: new eligibility applications citing a `SUPERSEDED` program are refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`, but eligibility applications already `APPROVED` under that program remain usable for registration until their own `eligibility_expires_on` passes. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; a superseded program never returns to `ACTIVE`.

### 3.2. Item Authoring and the Peer-Review Loop

An `ItemSpecialist` writes an item against a program's blueprint, supplying the owning `program_id`, the `domain_code` the item measures — which must match one of the `domain_code` values in that program's `domain_specs`, or the creation is refused with `ERR_ITEM_DOMAIN_MISMATCH` — a `cognitive_level` of `RECALL`, `APPLICATION`, or `ANALYSIS`, and the `stem` text itself. Authorship is taken from the acting identity, not from the payload: `author_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` and any value a client supplies for it is ignored. The new item's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `approver_user_ids` list is empty, its `review_round` starts at 1, all twelve of its response accumulators start at 0, and its `stat_basis_applied` starts at `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` with both `p_value` and `discrimination_index` null.

Submitting an item for review — a transition into `IN_REVIEW` from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, optionally annotated with a `revision_note` — is reserved to the item's own author. Any other `ItemSpecialist` attempting it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and an item in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Two side effects attach. First, `approver_user_ids` is reset to the empty list, unconditionally: approvals never carry across a round. Second, `review_round` increases by 1 only when the submission arrives from `REVISION_REQUESTED`; a first submission out of `DRAFT` leaves the round number where it is.

Review is where the segregation-of-duties model earns its keep. A reviewing `ItemSpecialist` supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REVISE`, or `REJECT`, optionally with a `review_note`, and in every case the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the item's `author_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An `APPROVE` decision additionally requires that the acting user does not already appear in `approver_user_ids`, and the same error is returned when they do. Two distinct non-author approvals are required to clear an item. The first `APPROVE` of a round appends the acting user to `approver_user_ids` and leaves the item in `IN_REVIEW` — an `IN_REVIEW → IN_REVIEW` self-transition. The second `APPROVE`, which is by definition the one where `approver_user_ids` held exactly one entry before the call, appends the second user so the list length becomes 2 and moves the item `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`.

The two negative decisions are deliberately different in reach. `REVISE` moves the item `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and discards the round's partial approvals by resetting `approver_user_ids` to empty, which is what makes the `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` circuit a genuine revision loop rather than a resumption: an item that had already collected one approval before revision must collect two fresh ones afterwards. `REJECT` moves the item `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and a `REJECTED` item may never appear among the item identifiers of a newly assembled form — an attempt to cite one is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FORM`.

### 3.3. Response Data, Item Statistics and Psychometric Flagging

Once an item is `APPROVED` it starts collecting evidence. A `Psychometrician` ingests a batch of classical item-analysis counts: `is_pretest` says which pool the batch belongs to, `response_count` and `correct_count` give the overall totals, `upper_n` and `upper_correct` give the counts for the high-scoring criterion group, and `lower_n` and `lower_correct` give the counts for the low-scoring group. Ingestion is permitted only while the item's `status` is `APPROVED`, `PRETEST`, `OPERATIONAL`, or `FLAGGED`; a `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, or `RETIRED` item is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_STATS`. All seven fields are required, `is_pretest` being a boolean and each of the six counts a non-negative integer, and no relationship among the counts is validated: a batch whose `correct_count` exceeds its `response_count`, or whose group sizes exceed the overall total, is accepted and accumulated as given.

Every ingest is accumulated, never stored as a batch. When `is_pretest` is true the batch counts are added to `pretest_response_count`, `pretest_correct_count`, `pretest_upper_n`, `pretest_upper_correct`, `pretest_lower_n`, and `pretest_lower_correct` respectively; when it is false they are added to `operational_response_count`, `operational_correct_count`, `operational_upper_n`, `operational_upper_correct`, `operational_lower_n`, and `operational_lower_correct`. Statistics are then always recomputed from the twelve accumulators as they now stand, never from the incoming batch alone.

Which accumulators are read is a **program-level choice with two variants**, so the same twelve numbers can yield two different answers. Under the default `OPERATIONAL_ONLY` basis, the working totals are exactly the six operational accumulators: the response total is `operational_response_count`, the correct total is `operational_correct_count`, the upper-group size and correct count are `operational_upper_n` and `operational_upper_correct`, and the lower-group size and correct count are `operational_lower_n` and `operational_lower_correct`. Pretest evidence is ignored entirely under this basis, even in the common case where it holds the bulk of the responses. Under the `POOLED` basis, each working total is the sum of the corresponding pretest and operational accumulators — the response total is `pretest_response_count` plus `operational_response_count`, the correct total is `pretest_correct_count` plus `operational_correct_count`, the upper-group size is `pretest_upper_n` plus `operational_upper_n`, the upper-group correct count is `pretest_upper_correct` plus `operational_upper_correct`, the lower-group size is `pretest_lower_n` plus `operational_lower_n`, and the lower-group correct count is `pretest_lower_correct` plus `operational_lower_correct`. Merging pretest evidence in can both carry an item over the responses floor and move both statistics.

The arithmetic applied to whichever pool the basis selected is identical. If the working response total is strictly less than the program's `min_responses_for_stats`, or the upper-group size is zero, or the lower-group size is zero, then `stat_basis_applied` is set to `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` and both `p_value` and `discrimination_index` are set to null — the zero-size guards exist so that no division by zero is ever attempted, and they force the insufficient verdict even when the overall response total is enormous. Otherwise `stat_basis_applied` records the basis actually used, either `POOLED` or `OPERATIONAL_ONLY`; `p_value` becomes the working correct total divided by the working response total, rounded half up to four decimal places; and `discrimination_index` becomes the upper-group correct count divided by the upper-group size, minus the lower-group correct count divided by the lower-group size, rounded half up to four decimal places. Neither quotient is rounded before the subtraction. Because the floor comparison is strict, a working response total of exactly `min_responses_for_stats` — 30 responses under the default — does compute statistics rather than withholding them. Changing a program's basis flag never retroactively rewrites already-stored items; the new basis takes effect from the next ingest onward.

Flagging runs immediately after the statistics, on the same call, and it is **first-match-wins with a normative order**, because an item can breach two thresholds at once. If `stat_basis_applied` is `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, `flag_reason` is null — unknown is not the same as good, but it is not a flag either. Otherwise, if `discrimination_index` is strictly less than the program's `min_discrimination`, `flag_reason` becomes `LOW_DISCRIMINATION`. Otherwise, if `p_value` is strictly greater than `max_p_value`, `flag_reason` becomes `TOO_EASY`. Otherwise, if `p_value` is strictly less than `min_p_value`, `flag_reason` becomes `TOO_HARD`. Otherwise `flag_reason` is null. All three comparisons are strict against the already-rounded four-decimal statistics, so a `discrimination_index` of exactly 0.1500 does not flag, a `p_value` of exactly 0.9500 does not flag, and a `p_value` of exactly 0.2000 does not flag. An item that is simultaneously too easy and under-discriminating reports `LOW_DISCRIMINATION`, never `TOO_EASY`, purely because of the order of the tests.

The flag verdict then drives the item's own state, entirely as a system consequence of the ingest. An `APPROVED` item receiving its first batch with `is_pretest` true moves `APPROVED → PRETEST`, and an `APPROVED` item receiving a batch with `is_pretest` false stays `APPROVED` — its accumulators, statistics and `flag_reason` are all rewritten, but there is no direct `APPROVED → OPERATIONAL` path and no flag-driven transition out of `APPROVED`. From `PRETEST`, once `stat_basis_applied` is no longer `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, one of two mutually exclusive outcomes follows: a null `flag_reason` moves the item `PRETEST → OPERATIONAL`, while a non-null `flag_reason` moves it `PRETEST → RETIRED` with `retirement_reason` set to `PRETEST_FAILED` and `retired_at` stamped from the logical clock. The two moves chain inside one ingest: an `APPROVED` item whose first `is_pretest` batch already carries the working totals over the sufficiency floor becomes `PRETEST` and then immediately `OPERATIONAL` or `RETIRED` on that same call. The pretest failure path deliberately triggers **no form cascade at all**, because a pretest item never occupies a scored position on any form. Once operational, the item enters the monitoring cycle: a later ingest yielding a non-null `flag_reason` with sufficient data moves it `OPERATIONAL → FLAGGED`, and a further ingest yielding a null `flag_reason` with sufficient data moves it back `FLAGGED → OPERATIONAL`. This `OPERATIONAL → FLAGGED → OPERATIONAL` circuit may repeat indefinitely.

Each crossing of the operational boundary cascades into the owning program. A `PRETEST → OPERATIONAL` or `FLAGGED → OPERATIONAL` move increases the program's `operational_item_count` by 1 and then recomputes `deficient_domain_codes` and `blueprint_posture`, which can flip the program `DEFICIENT → COMPLETE`. An `OPERATIONAL → FLAGGED` move decreases `operational_item_count` by 1 and recomputes the same two fields, which can flip the program `COMPLETE → DEFICIENT` — and a deficient program can no longer have forms published, so a single item losing its discrimination is enough to close the publication gate with `ERR_BLUEPRINT_DEFICIENT`.

### 3.4. Item Retirement and the Form-Rebuild Cascade

Retirement is the `Psychometrician`'s deliberate act, distinct from the automatic pretest failure above. It is available only from `OPERATIONAL` or `FLAGGED` — any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and it requires an explicit `retirement_reason` of `PSYCHOMETRIC`, `CONTENT_OBSOLETE`, `EXPOSED`, or `PRETEST_FAILED`. The item moves to `RETIRED`, which is terminal, with `retired_at` stamped from the logical clock and `retirement_reason` taken from the request. The `OPERATIONAL → RETIRED` and `FLAGGED → RETIRED` paths are identical in every respect, including the full cascade below and the decrement of the program's `operational_item_count` followed by recomputation of `deficient_domain_codes` and `blueprint_posture`.

Retirement then propagates through four hops, and every hop is part of the same synchronous operation. **Hop one:** every exam form whose item list contains the retired item recomputes its `retired_item_count` — that count is the number of entries in `item_ids` whose item is now in `RETIRED` status, and it includes pretest positions, not only scored ones. **Hop two:** each such form whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` is tested against the program's tolerance, using deliberate integer arithmetic rather than a floating-point ratio — the form moves `PUBLISHED → NEEDS_REBUILD` when `retired_item_count` multiplied by 100 is strictly greater than `form_retirement_tolerance_pct` multiplied by the number of entries in `item_ids`. Forms in `ASSEMBLED` or `CUT_SCORE_SET` status merely refresh `retired_item_count` and never transition. To make the boundary concrete: on a twenty-position form under the default ten-percent tolerance, two retirements give 200 against 200, which is not strictly greater and leaves the form `PUBLISHED`; the third retirement gives 300 against 200 and rebuilds it. **Hop three:** because the form moved to `NEEDS_REBUILD`, every exam registration on that form whose `seat_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `UNSCHEDULED`, has `form_reassignment_required` set to true, and has its `session_id` cleared. **Hop four:** because those seats were released, every affected exam session recounts its `seats_reserved` and recomputes its `status`, which may flip a session `FULL → OPEN`.

A form in `NEEDS_REBUILD` is terminal: it never returns to `PUBLISHED`, and no new exam registration may cite it — an attempt is refused with `ERR_FORM_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Candidates whose seats were released carry `form_reassignment_required` as the marker that they need a different form before they can be scheduled again.

### 3.5. Form Assembly, Standard Setting and Publication

A `Psychometrician` assembles a form against an active program's blueprint, supplying the `program_id`, a `form_code` label for the form — recorded as given, with no uniqueness enforced against sibling forms — the ordered `item_ids` that constitute the form, the `panelist_user_ids` who will be eligible to rate it — a distinct list of at least one identifier — and a `panel_method` of `MODIFIED_ANGOFF` or `BOOKMARK`, which defaults to `MODIFIED_ANGOFF`. An optional `pretest_item_ids` list marks which of the form's positions are unscored pretest slots. Four content checks guard assembly, and by the precedence of §2.7 they are evaluated in this order: every cited item must belong to the same program and carry a `domain_code` present in the program's `domain_specs`, or `ERR_ITEM_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`; every scored item must be in `APPROVED`, `PRETEST`, or `OPERATIONAL` status, or `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FORM`; every entry of `pretest_item_ids` must also appear in `item_ids`, or `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_FORM`; and the number of entries in `item_ids` must equal the program's `form_length` exactly, or `ERR_FORM_LENGTH_MISMATCH`. A new form has `status` `ASSEMBLED`, `panel_status` `OPEN`, an empty `panel_ratings` list, a `scored_item_count` equal to the number of entries in `item_ids` less the number of entries in `pretest_item_ids`, and a `retired_item_count` computed from the current status of the cited items. Its `mean_expected_pass_rate` and `raw_cut_score` are both null until a cut score is adopted.

Standard setting begins with panel judgements. A `Panelist` — or a `Psychometrician` acting on a panellist's behalf — submits a rating naming the `panelist_user_id` it belongs to and that panellist's `mean_expected_pass_rate`, the proportion of candidates at the borderline of competence expected to answer correctly — required to be a non-negative number, with no upper bound enforced on it. Ratings are accepted only while the form is `ASSEMBLED` or `CUT_SCORE_SET`; a `PUBLISHED` or `NEEDS_REBUILD` form refuses them with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The named `panelist_user_id` must appear in the form's `panelist_user_ids`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ON_PANEL`, and a panellist who has already rated this form is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANELIST_RATING`. Each accepted rating is appended to `panel_ratings` as a record of the `panelist_user_id`, the `mean_expected_pass_rate`, and a `submitted_at` stamp from the logical clock. Once the number of entries in `panel_ratings` equals the number of entries in `panelist_user_ids`, `panel_status` moves to `RATINGS_COMPLETE` — including when a previous adoption had already moved it to `ADOPTED`, which such a completing rating overwrites.

Adopting the cut score is the `Psychometrician`'s act, optionally annotated with an `adoption_note`, and it requires `expected_form_version` as described in §2.5. The form must be in `ASSEMBLED` or `CUT_SCORE_SET` status — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the adopting user must not appear in the form's `panelist_user_ids`, or the adoption is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`: nobody ratifies their own judgement. Quorum is then tested twice: the number of entries in `panel_ratings` must be at least the program's `min_panelists`, and that number less twice the program's `panel_trim_count` must be at least 1; either failure is `ERR_PANEL_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Adoption moves the form `ASSEMBLED → CUT_SCORE_SET`, or leaves it in `CUT_SCORE_SET` when it is a re-adoption after further ratings, sets `panel_status` to `ADOPTED`, and increments the form's `version` by 1. Re-adoption is legitimate while the form is unpublished and recomputes over the full current rating set rather than incrementally, with the newly computed `raw_cut_score` superseding the prior value.

The cut-score computation itself proceeds in a fixed order. Take the `mean_expected_pass_rate` of every entry in `panel_ratings` as the working set of means and let its size be the panel count. Sort those means in ascending order, keeping insertion order among ties, then discard the lowest `panel_trim_count` means and the highest `panel_trim_count` means — exactly one from each end under the default, and the discard happens even when the extreme values are equal to their neighbours. Let the surviving count be the trimmed size. The trimmed mean is the sum of the surviving means divided by the trimmed size, rounded half up to six decimal places, and it is written to the form's `mean_expected_pass_rate`. The standard error of that trimmed mean is zero when exactly one mean survives trimming; otherwise take the sample variance of the surviving means — the sum over the survivors of the squared difference between each mean and the trimmed mean, divided by one less than the trimmed size — then take the square root of that variance, divide it by the square root of the trimmed size, and round the result half up to six decimal places. The adjusted pass rate is the trimmed mean minus the program's `cut_score_sem_multiplier` multiplied by that standard error. Multiply the adjusted pass rate by the form's `scored_item_count` and round half up to a whole number to get the unclamped raw cut. Finally clamp it: the lower bound is forty percent of `scored_item_count` rounded up to a whole number, the upper bound is ninety percent of `scored_item_count` rounded down to a whole number, and `raw_cut_score` is the unclamped raw cut raised to the lower bound if it falls below and then lowered to the upper bound if it rises above. The clamp is applied last, after the standard-error adjustment, precisely so that a hostile or badly calibrated panel cannot push the passing standard below forty percent of the scored positions.

Publication is the `ProgramAdmin`'s gate, optionally annotated with a `publication_note`, and it moves the form `CUT_SCORE_SET → PUBLISHED`. Four preconditions hold. The form must be in `CUT_SCORE_SET` status, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The owning program's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The program's `blueprint_posture` must be `COMPLETE`, or `ERR_BLUEPRINT_DEFICIENT`. And every identifier in `item_ids` that is not also in `pretest_item_ids` must resolve to an item whose `status` is `OPERATIONAL` at this moment, or `ERR_ITEM_NOT_OPERATIONAL` — an item that was merely `APPROVED` when the form was assembled is acceptable then but not now. Publication is what makes a form selectable by a candidate registering to sit it.

### 3.6. Eligibility Review, Dimension Verification and Audit Sampling

Before anyone can sit an examination they must exist as a candidate and clear the program's prerequisites. Either an `ExamOperations` or an `EligibilityReviewer` operator registers a candidate with an `email`, which must be unique across all candidates — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_EMAIL_TAKEN` — and a `full_name`, which is the legal name that will eventually appear on the credential. A new candidate's `status` is `ACTIVE`, with `barred_until` and `bar_reason` both null.

An eligibility application is submitted either by the candidate themselves or by an `EligibilityReviewer` on their behalf, and it cites the `candidate_id`, the `program_id` applied for, the applicant's highest `education_credential` from `DIPLOMA`, `ASSOCIATE`, `BACHELOR`, `MASTER`, and `DOCTORATE`, the four-digit `education_completion_year` in which that credential was earned, the self-reported `experience_months` of qualifying practice, which may not be negative, and `attestation_signed`, which must be true — an application submitted without a signed attestation to the code of conduct is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The cited program must be `ACTIVE`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. A new application has `status` `SUBMITTED`, both `education_status` and `experience_status` `PENDING`, an empty `verifier_user_ids` list, `attempts_consumed` at 0, and `eligibility_expires_on`, `audit_sequence_number`, `auditor_user_id`, and `denial_reason` all null.

Verification is a **fork**: the two prerequisite dimensions are decided independently and in either order. An `EligibilityReviewer` names the `dimension` being decided — `EDUCATION` or `EXPERIENCE` — and submits an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, optionally with a `review_note`. The application must be in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` status, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the named dimension must still be `PENDING`, or `ERR_DIMENSION_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. Each accepted call appends a record of the `dimension` together with the acting `user_id` to `verifier_user_ids`, in call order.

The reviewer's `outcome` is a veto, not a warrant. The education dimension clears the arithmetic when the application's `education_credential` appears in the program's `accepted_education_credentials` **and** its `education_completion_year` is at least the current year of the logical clock minus the program's `education_recency_years`. The experience dimension clears the arithmetic when `experience_months` is at least the program's `min_experience_months`. The dimension's own status field — `education_status` or `experience_status` — becomes `VERIFIED` only when the submitted `outcome` is `PASS` **and** the corresponding arithmetic clears; in every other case it becomes `FAILED`. A reviewer who submits `PASS` for an applicant who does not meet the thresholds still produces `FAILED`. Both comparisons are inclusive: `experience_months` exactly equal to `min_experience_months` verifies, and a credential completed in exactly the year that is `education_recency_years` before the current year verifies while one completed a year earlier fails.

A first call out of `SUBMITTED` that verifies its dimension moves the application `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. A call in which the decided dimension evaluates to `FAILED` sends the application straight to `DENIED` — from `SUBMITTED` if it is the first dimension decided, from `UNDER_REVIEW` if it is the second — with `denial_reason` set to `EDUCATION_FAILED` or `EXPERIENCE_FAILED` according to which dimension failed. Crucially, a denial **does not** touch the program's `verified_application_counter`: only fully verified pairs enter the audit sequence, so a denied application never shifts the sampling of applications that come after it.

The **join** happens on the call that leaves both `education_status` and `experience_status` at `VERIFIED`, and it has two divergent branches selected by a deterministic every-Nth rule. On that call the program's `verified_application_counter` increases by 1 and the application's `audit_sequence_number` is set to the counter's new value. If `audit_sequence_number` is an exact multiple of the program's `audit_every_nth` — that is, dividing the sequence number by the sampling interval leaves no remainder — the application moves `UNDER_REVIEW → AUDIT_SELECTED`; its `eligibility_expires_on` deliberately stays null, and any attempt to register against it is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED` until the audit is decided. Otherwise the application moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and `eligibility_expires_on` is stamped as the current logical-clock instant advanced by the program's `eligibility_valid_days` — a year of usable eligibility under the default. Two properties of the counter matter: it is per program, so applications under different programs sample independently, and with an `audit_every_nth` of 1 every application is sampled. Sampling therefore depends on the order in which applications *complete verification*, not the order in which they were created.

An audited application is resolved by an audit decision carrying an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and an optional `audit_note`. The application must be in `AUDIT_SELECTED` status, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the deciding user must not appear as a `user_id` among the application's `verifier_user_ids` — an auditor may never audit their own verification, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either way `auditor_user_id` records the acting identity. A `PASS` moves the application `AUDIT_SELECTED → APPROVED` and stamps `eligibility_expires_on` from the current logical-clock instant plus `eligibility_valid_days`, exactly as the unsampled branch would have. A `FAIL` moves it `AUDIT_SELECTED → DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `AUDIT_FAILED`. `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal.

### 3.7. Session Capacity, Registration and Seat Management

An `ExamOperations` operator publishes the delivery calendar as exam sessions. Each session declares a `modality` of `TEST_CENTER` or `ONLINE_PROCTORED`, a `starts_at` instant at which its window opens, an `ends_at` instant that must be strictly after `starts_at`, and a `seat_capacity` of at least 1. The location fields are modality-conditional and strictly so: a `TEST_CENTER` session requires both a `center_code` and a `room_code` and must leave `proctor_pool_code` null, while an `ONLINE_PROCTORED` session requires a `proctor_pool_code` and must leave both `center_code` and `room_code` null. A violation of either shape is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Physical rooms cannot be double-booked. Two `TEST_CENTER` sessions sharing the same `center_code` and `room_code` pair may not overlap on the **half-open** interval that runs from `starts_at` up to but not including `ends_at`: a conflict exists when the new session's `starts_at` is strictly before an existing session's `ends_at` and that existing session's `starts_at` is strictly before the new session's `ends_at`. Adjacency is therefore permitted — a session beginning at the exact instant another ends is accepted — while any genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Online proctoring is constrained collectively rather than individually, which is what makes it interesting: each existing session may be perfectly legal on its own while the aggregate breaches the pool. When an `ONLINE_PROCTORED` session is created, gather every existing `ONLINE_PROCTORED` session that shares the new session's `proctor_pool_code` and whose window overlaps the new one on the same half-open test — the new `starts_at` strictly before the existing `ends_at`, and the existing `starts_at` strictly before the new `ends_at`. Sum the `seat_capacity` of those overlapping sessions; if that committed total plus the new session's own `seat_capacity` is strictly greater than the pool ceiling of 50 concurrent seats, the creation is refused with `ERR_PROCTOR_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A combined total of exactly 50 is accepted and 51 is not. Note that declared capacity is what gets summed, not seats actually taken — an empty session still books its proctors — that adjacent windows never contribute, and that `TEST_CENTER` sessions are excluded from the sum even when they happen to carry a matching code.

A session's occupancy and availability are both derived. `seats_reserved` is the number of exam registrations pointing at the session whose `seat_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED`; the `UNSCHEDULED`, `NO_SHOW`, and `CANCELLED` states all release the seat. The session's `status` is `CLOSED` when the logical clock has reached or passed `starts_at`; otherwise it is `FULL` when `seats_reserved` is greater than or equal to `seat_capacity`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. Closure wins over fullness, so a session that is both past its start and at capacity reports `CLOSED`.

Registration is the candidate's commitment to sit. Either the candidate or an `ExamOperations` operator creates it, citing the `application_id` of the approved application that authorises the sitting, the `form_id` of the form the candidate will receive, and the `fee_paid_cents` tendered. The `candidate_id` is copied from the application rather than supplied, and `eligibility_expires_on_snapshot` pins the application's `eligibility_expires_on` at this moment so that a later expiry cannot retroactively invalidate a registration already created. The cited form and the cited application must belong to the same certification program, or `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. The tendered `fee_paid_cents` must be at least the program's `exam_fee_cents`, or `ERR_EXAM_FEE_INSUFFICIENT`. A new registration begins with `seat_status` `UNSCHEDULED`, `score_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `form_reassignment_required` false, `session_id` and `session_ends_at` null, and `raw_score`, `scaled_score`, `outcome`, and `score_status_before_hold` all null.

Attempt sequencing and the retake wait are both derived from the sibling registrations of the same application. Count the application's registrations whose `seat_status` is `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW` — that count is the application's `attempts_consumed`. If it is already greater than or equal to the program's `max_attempts_per_cycle`, the new registration is refused with `ERR_MAX_ATTEMPTS_REACHED`; under the default of 3 that means the fourth registration is turned away. Otherwise the new registration's `attempt_number` is that count plus 1. Then take the greatest `session_ends_at` among those same consumed registrations; when no attempt has been consumed there is no such instant and no wait applies. When there is one, the registration is refused with `ERR_RETAKE_WAIT_ACTIVE` if the logical clock is strictly earlier than that instant advanced by the program's `retake_wait_days` — so a candidate whose last sitting ended exactly `retake_wait_days` ago is allowed through, while one an hour short is not. A `CANCELLED` or still-`UNSCHEDULED` registration consumes nothing and starts no wait, and a score that is later voided does not un-consume its attempt, because `seat_status` stays `COMPLETED` regardless of what happens to the score.

Claiming a seat moves the registration `UNSCHEDULED → SCHEDULED` and requires the target `session_id` together with `expected_session_version`. Beyond the concurrency guard of §2.5, six conditions hold, and by §2.7 they resolve in this order: the registration must be `UNSCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the cited form's `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or `ERR_FORM_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the candidate's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CANDIDATE_BARRED`; the candidate must have no buffered window collision with another of their sittings, or `ERR_CANDIDATE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; the session must not be closed — the logical clock must be strictly before its `starts_at` — or `ERR_SESSION_CLOSED`; and the session's `seats_reserved` must be strictly below its `seat_capacity`, or `ERR_SESSION_FULL`. The buffered collision test pads **both** sides of **both** windows by 120 minutes: a conflict exists when the new session's `starts_at` less 120 minutes is at or before the existing session's `ends_at` plus 120 minutes, and the existing session's `starts_at` less 120 minutes is at or before the new session's `ends_at` plus 120 minutes. Because both comparisons are inclusive, back-to-back sittings are rejected outright, a 119-minute gap between two windows is rejected, and a 121-minute gap is allowed. On success the registration records `session_id`, writes `session_ends_at` as a snapshot of the chosen session's `ends_at`, the session's `seats_reserved` increases by 1 with its `status` recomputed — possibly flipping `OPEN → FULL` — and the session's `version` increments by 1.

Releasing a seat voluntarily moves the registration `SCHEDULED → UNSCHEDULED` and takes an optional `cancel_reason`. The registration must be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the logical clock must still be strictly before the held session's `starts_at`, or `ERR_SESSION_CLOSED` — a candidate cannot walk away from a session that has already begun. The compensating side effects mirror the claim exactly: the previously held session's `seats_reserved` decreases by 1 with its `status` recomputed, possibly flipping `FULL → OPEN`, and its `version` increments by 1. The registration's `session_id` is cleared, but `session_ends_at` is deliberately **retained**, because the retake-wait arithmetic reads it for sittings that were consumed rather than abandoned.

Two further seat releases are system-driven rather than candidate-driven. A form moving to `NEEDS_REBUILD` releases scheduled seats as described in §3.4, setting `form_reassignment_required`. A candidate being barred as the consequence of a substantiated critical security incident moves each of that candidate's *other* `SCHEDULED` registrations — every one except the registration the incident was raised against — to `CANCELLED`, clearing `session_id` and releasing the seat, with each affected session recounting `seats_reserved` and recomputing its `status`. Both `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for `seat_status`.

### 3.8. Sitting the Exam: Check-in, Scoring and Score Release

Check-in is performed by an `ExamOperations` operator, optionally annotated with a `checkin_note`, and it is the one call in this system that advances **both** of a registration's status dimensions at once: `seat_status` moves `SCHEDULED → CHECKED_IN` and, on the same call, `score_status` moves `NOT_APPLICABLE → PENDING`. Four preconditions apply, and by §2.7 they resolve in this order: the registration must be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the cited form's `status` must still be `PUBLISHED`, or `ERR_FORM_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the candidate's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CANDIDATE_BARRED`; and the logical clock must fall within the check-in window, which opens 30 minutes before the session's `starts_at` and closes at the session's `ends_at`, both ends inclusive, or `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Submitting responses closes the sitting. The operator supplies the `raw_score`, the number-correct count on the form's scored positions, which must be at least 0 and at most the form's `scored_item_count` — otherwise `ERR_RAW_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The registration's `seat_status` must be `CHECKED_IN`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and its `score_status` must be `PENDING`; a registration whose score has been put under a security hold has `score_status` `WITHHELD` and is refused with `ERR_SCORE_UNDER_SECURITY_HOLD`. On success `seat_status` moves `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED`, `score_status` moves `PENDING → SCORED`, the equated score and outcome are computed, and the owning application's `attempts_consumed` increases by 1.

Equating is a two-segment piecewise-linear transformation anchored at the cut, and **the pass decision is made on the scaled score, never on the raw score**. Let the cut be the form's `raw_cut_score` and the total be the form's `scored_item_count`. When `raw_score` is at least the cut, the unrounded scaled value is 800 if the total equals the cut; otherwise it is 500 plus the quantity `raw_score` minus the cut, multiplied by 300, divided by the quantity total minus the cut. When `raw_score` is below the cut, the unrounded scaled value is 500 if the cut is 0; otherwise it is 200 plus `raw_score` multiplied by 300 and divided by the cut. That value is then rounded half up to a whole number and clamped into the reporting band, raised to 200 if it falls below and lowered to 800 if it rises above, and the result is written to `scaled_score`. The registration's `outcome` becomes `PASS` when `scaled_score` is at least 500 and `FAIL` otherwise. The anchoring is exact by construction: a `raw_score` equal to the cut maps to precisely 500 and passes, one mark below the cut lands below 500 and fails, a `raw_score` of 0 maps to precisely 200, and a `raw_score` equal to the total maps to precisely 800.

Score release is the last operational step and is again an `ExamOperations` act, optionally annotated with a `report_note`. It moves `score_status` `SCORED → REPORTED`. The score must currently be `SCORED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and no security incident on this registration may still be `OPEN` — an unresolved incident blocks release with `ERR_SCORE_UNDER_SECURITY_HOLD`. `REPORTED` is terminal for `score_status`, as is `VOIDED`. What release triggers downstream depends entirely on the `outcome` and is described in §3.10.

### 3.9. Exam Security Incidents and Score Holds

Proctors raise security incidents against a specific sitting. An `ExamOperations` operator records the `registration_id` the incident concerns, an `incident_type` of `PROHIBITED_DEVICE`, `IMPERSONATION`, `CONTENT_DISCLOSURE`, or `PROCTOR_INTERRUPTION`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `CRITICAL`, and a free-text `description` holding the proctor's narrative. A new incident has `status` `OPEN`, `outcome_note` null, and `placed_hold` false. The mere existence of an `OPEN` incident on a registration is enough to block score release with `ERR_SCORE_UNDER_SECURITY_HOLD`, whatever its severity.

A `CRITICAL` incident goes further and freezes the score immediately. When the incident is opened and the registration's `score_status` is either `PENDING` or `SCORED`, that pre-call value is captured into `score_status_before_hold`, `score_status` moves to `WITHHELD`, and the incident's `placed_hold` is set to true. No hold is placed when the registration's `score_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `REPORTED`, or `VOIDED`; in those cases `placed_hold` stays false and `score_status_before_hold` stays null. Note that the hold touches only the score dimension — `seat_status` is untouched, so a candidate mid-sitting is not ejected by the paperwork.

Adjudication resolves an incident and is available only while its `status` is `OPEN`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The adjudicator submits an `outcome` of `SUBSTANTIATE` or `DISMISS`, optionally with an `outcome_note`.

Substantiation moves the incident `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED` and runs a cascade whose depth is governed by `severity`. **Hop one**, which always runs: the registration's `score_status` moves to `VOIDED` and both `scaled_score` and `outcome` are cleared to null. For a `MINOR` or `MAJOR` incident the cascade stops there — the candidate is not barred. For a `CRITICAL` incident three further hops follow. **Hop two:** because the score was voided, the candidate's `status` becomes `BARRED` with `bar_reason` `SECURITY_INCIDENT` and `barred_until` set to the current logical-clock instant advanced by 730 days — a two-year exclusion from testing. **Hop three:** because the candidate is barred, every *other* exam registration of that candidate whose `seat_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `session_id` cleared. **Hop four:** because those seats were released, each affected exam session recounts `seats_reserved` and recomputes its `status`, possibly flipping `FULL → OPEN`. While barred, the candidate can neither register, claim a seat, nor check in; each attempt is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_BARRED`.

Dismissal moves the incident `OPEN → UNSUBSTANTIATED` and is purely compensating. When the incident's `placed_hold` is true, the registration's `score_status` is restored to the value stored in `score_status_before_hold` — so a score frozen while `SCORED` returns to `SCORED` and a sitting frozen while `PENDING` returns to `PENDING` — and `score_status_before_hold` is cleared back to null. The candidate's `status` is untouched by a dismissal, and no seats are affected.

### 3.10. Credential Issuance, Registry Publication and Public Verification

Releasing a passing score is the moment a candidate becomes a certificant, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. When the released registration's `outcome` is `PASS` and the candidate does not already hold a credential awaiting re-examination, **hop one** mints a certificant for the candidate-and-program pair: its `candidate_id` and `program_id` are taken from the registration and its application rather than supplied by any client, its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, its `issued_on` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `certificate_number` is minted as described below. **Hop two:** because a certificant was minted, the program's `certificants_issued_count` increases by 1 and the certificant's `valid_through` is set to `issued_on` advanced by the program's `cycle_length_months`. **Hop three:** because the certificant now exists, its first recertification cycle is opened with `cycle_number` 1, `path` `CEU`, `starts_on` equal to the certificant's own `issued_on`, `carryover_credits_in` 0, `required_credits` pinned from the program's `required_ceu_credits`, and `accrual_basis_snapshot` pinned from the program's `ceu_credit_accrual_basis`; the certificant's `current_cycle_id` is repointed at it. When the released registration's `outcome` is `FAIL`, none of this happens — the registration simply becomes `REPORTED` and no credential side effects occur at all.

The certificate number is human-readable and is never derived from a UUID. It is composed as the program's `credential_code`, a hyphen, the four-digit year of the logical clock at minting, another hyphen, and the program's issue sequence zero-padded to five digits — where the sequence is the value of `certificants_issued_count` after that counter has been incremented for this minting. The counter never resets across calendar years, so numbering stays monotonic within a program for the life of the credential, and a certificant that is later revoked does not release its number back into the sequence.

A certificant's registry visibility is derived from its own lifecycle rather than set independently. `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` while `status` is `ACTIVE`, `WITHHELD` while `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, and `REMOVED` once `status` is `REVOKED`. Two more fields are projections of the certificant's recertification state: `current_cycle_id` names the certificant's cycle whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SATISFIED`, or `LAPSED` with the greatest `cycle_number`, and is null when every cycle of that certificant is `CLOSED` or `VOID`; `ceu_credits_applied` mirrors the `credits_applied` of the certificant's cycle whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SATISFIED`, and is 0 when no such cycle exists. `suspension_until` is null unless `status` is `SUSPENDED`.

Public verification is the outward face of the registry and the only capability in the system reachable with no headers whatsoever. A verifier supplies a `certificate_number` as a query parameter of that name and receives back that `certificate_number`, the issuing program's `credential_code`, the holder's `full_name`, a `credential_status`, the `publication_status`, the `issued_on` and `valid_through` instants, and a `verified_at` stamp recording the logical-clock instant at which the lookup was performed. A `certificate_number` that is absent altogether, or present but empty or nothing but whitespace, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; any other value is compared verbatim against the stored numbers, so one that merely carries stray surrounding whitespace matches nothing and yields `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_FOUND`. Suppression is deliberately indistinguishable from non-existence: when the matched certificant's `publication_status` is `WITHHELD` or `REMOVED`, the verification answers `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_FOUND` rather than revealing that a suppressed record exists. Where a credential is disclosed, `credential_status` carries the certificant's own `status` verbatim, so a lapsed-but-published credential is reported with `credential_status` `EXPIRED` rather than being hidden.

### 3.11. Recertification: Credit Accrual, Cycle Closure, Re-examination and Reinstatement

A recertification cycle is the unit in which a credential is maintained. Its `certificant_id` names the holder it belongs to, its `cycle_number` is 1 for the cycle minted with the credential and one greater for each successor, its `starts_on` is the instant the window opens, its `ends_on` is `starts_on` advanced by the program's `cycle_length_months`, and its `grace_ends_on` is `ends_on` advanced by the program's `recert_grace_days`. Two program settings are **snapshotted at cycle creation and never re-based afterwards**: `required_credits` pins the program's `required_ceu_credits`, and `accrual_basis_snapshot` pins the program's `ceu_credit_accrual_basis` as either `STRICT_CYCLE` or `CARRYOVER_ALLOWED`. Flipping the program's flag after a cycle has opened has no effect on that cycle. `carryover_credits_in` records credit carried in from the predecessor's close and starts at 0 for a first cycle, `credits_applied` is the derived credit total to two decimals, `carryover_credits_out` is the surplus computed at close, `path` is `CEU` or `RE_EXAM`, and `reinstatement_fee_cents` is null unless the cycle is `LAPSED`. `reexam_registration_id` is null on the credit path and names the passing sitting on the re-examination path.

A `Certificant` claims continuing-education credit against their own open cycle by submitting the `activity_date` on which the learning was completed, a `category` of `LIVE_INSTRUCTION`, `SELF_STUDY`, `PRESENTING`, `PUBLICATION`, or `VOLUNTEER`, the `provider` or publication name, and the documented `contact_hours`, which must be strictly greater than zero and carry at most two decimal places. The cycle's own `status` must be `OPEN` or `SATISFIED`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — an already-satisfied cycle still accepts further claims, but a `LAPSED`, `CLOSED`, or `VOID` one does not. The owning certificant's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE` — a suspended, expired, or revoked holder cannot accrue credit. The `activity_date` must fall inside the cycle's accrual window, or the claim is refused with `ERR_ACTIVITY_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`. A new claim has its `cycle_id` set to the cycle it was claimed against, `status` `SUBMITTED`, `counted` false, `credits_counted` 0.00, `reviewer_user_id` and `audit_verifier_user_id` null, and `review_note` null until a reviewer supplies one. Its `credits_awarded` is recorded immediately as the `contact_hours` multiplied by the category's credit multiplier, rounded half up to two decimals — a figure recorded but not yet counted toward anything.

The multipliers reflect the effort each activity type represents: an hour of `LIVE_INSTRUCTION` is worth 1.0 credits, an hour of `SELF_STUDY` is worth 0.5, an hour spent `PRESENTING` is worth 2.0, an hour spent on `PUBLICATION` is worth 5.0, and an hour of `VOLUNTEER` service is worth 0.25. Two categories are additionally capped per cycle, to stop a holder from meeting the whole requirement with the cheapest activity: `SELF_STUDY` contributes at most 20.0 credits per cycle and `VOLUNTEER` at most 5.0. `LIVE_INSTRUCTION`, `PRESENTING`, and `PUBLICATION` are uncapped.

Review is the `EligibilityReviewer`'s job. The reviewer submits an `outcome` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, or `SELECT_FOR_AUDIT`, optionally with a `review_note`, and the acting identity is recorded as `reviewer_user_id` in all three cases. The owning cycle's `status` must be `OPEN` or `SATISFIED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the owning certificant must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An `APPROVE` requires the activity to be `SUBMITTED`, moves it `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and sets `counted` to true. A `REJECT` also requires `SUBMITTED`, moves the activity `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and leaves `counted` false with `credits_counted` at 0. A `SELECT_FOR_AUDIT` is permitted from either `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`: arriving from `SUBMITTED` it moves the activity `SUBMITTED → AUDIT_SELECTED` with `counted` still false, since documentation must be verified before credit counts; arriving from `APPROVED` it moves the activity `APPROVED → AUDIT_SELECTED` and **compensates** by forcing `counted` back to false and `credits_counted` back to 0. Any other starting status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Audit verification closes the audit branch and is guarded by segregation of duties: the verifying user must differ from the activity's `reviewer_user_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The activity must be `AUDIT_SELECTED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An `outcome` of `VERIFY` moves the activity `AUDIT_SELECTED → VERIFIED` and sets `counted` to true; an `outcome` of `REVOKE` moves it `AUDIT_SELECTED → REVOKED`, which is terminal, and leaves `counted` false. Either way the acting identity is recorded as `audit_verifier_user_id`, and an optional `review_note` may accompany the decision. In summary, `counted` is true exactly when an activity's `status` is `APPROVED` or `VERIFIED` **and** its `activity_date` falls inside the cycle's accrual window; `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `AUDIT_SELECTED`, and `REVOKED` activities never count.

Every status change under a cycle re-runs the cycle's credit accrual from scratch, and the accrual has **two variants selected by the cycle's `accrual_basis_snapshot`**, so the same set of activities can yield two different totals. Under `STRICT_CYCLE`, the accrual window runs from the cycle's `starts_on` to its `ends_on` with both ends inclusive, and any carried-in credit is forfeited — the carry-in contribution is 0.00 regardless of what `carryover_credits_in` holds. Under `CARRYOVER_ALLOWED`, the window is extended backwards by a 90-day look-back, so it runs from 90 days before `starts_on` through `ends_on` inclusive, and the carry-in contribution is the lesser of the cycle's `carryover_credits_in` and the program's `ceu_carryover_max_credits`. An activity dated exactly on `starts_on` or exactly on `ends_on` is inside the window under both variants; an activity dated 90 days before `starts_on` counts under `CARRYOVER_ALLOWED` and is rejected at submission time with `ERR_ACTIVITY_OUTSIDE_CYCLE` under `STRICT_CYCLE`.

With the window and carry-in fixed, accrual proceeds in a pinned order. First, gather the eligible set: every activity under the cycle whose `counted` is true and whose `activity_date` falls inside the window. Second, compute each eligible activity's award as its `contact_hours` multiplied by its category multiplier, **rounded half up to two decimals at this point** — rounding precedes capping, and this is the same figure held in `credits_awarded`. Third, fill the capped categories in chronological order: process the eligible activities sorted by `created_at` ascending, breaking ties by `activity_date` ascending, then by `contact_hours` ascending, and then by the order in which the activities were created; for an activity in a capped category, its `credits_counted` is the lesser of its award and the category's remaining cap headroom, and the headroom is then reduced by that amount; for an activity in an uncapped category, its `credits_counted` is simply its award. Because the fill is chronological, the activity created first takes the headroom and later ones are truncated to whatever remains, which means a late `SELF_STUDY` claim can legitimately receive 0.00. Fourth, the cycle's `credits_applied` becomes the sum of `credits_counted` across the eligible set plus the carry-in contribution, rounded half up to two decimals. The two rounding points are distinct and both are required: rounding each award before capping and then rounding the sum is the specified behaviour, whereas summing unrounded awards and rounding once at the end is wrong.

The cycle's status is **re-derived from its children**, never patched, and the re-derivation reaches only a cycle that is currently `OPEN` or `SATISFIED` — `LAPSED`, `CLOSED`, and `VOID` are outcomes of explicit transitions and the credit invariant never moves a cycle out of them, however its credit total changes. A `CEU`-path cycle is `SATISFIED` exactly when its `credits_applied` is greater than or equal to its `required_credits`, with exact equality satisfying; a `RE_EXAM`-path cycle is `SATISFIED` exactly when its `reexam_registration_id` is set. So an approval that carries a cycle over the line bubbles it `OPEN → SATISFIED`, and an audit selection or an audit revocation that drops `credits_applied` back below `required_credits` compensates by reverting the cycle `SATISFIED → OPEN` — at which point closure is refused with `ERR_CREDITS_NOT_SATISFIED`. This `OPEN → SATISFIED → OPEN` circuit is the credit-reconciliation loop, and the invariant behind it holds at every observable moment: `credits_applied` always equals the rounded sum of the counted children's `credits_counted` plus the cycle's carry-in contribution.

Closing a satisfied cycle is available to the holder or to a `ProgramAdmin`, requires `expected_certificant_version` as described in §2.5, and demands both that the cycle's `status` be `SATISFIED` — otherwise `ERR_CREDITS_NOT_SATISFIED` — and that the owning certificant's `status` be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. It moves the cycle `SATISFIED → CLOSED`, which is terminal, through three hops. **Hop one:** `carryover_credits_out` is computed. Take the surplus as `credits_applied` minus `required_credits`; if the cycle's `accrual_basis_snapshot` is `STRICT_CYCLE`, or the surplus is zero or negative, then `carryover_credits_out` is 0.00; otherwise it is the lesser of the surplus and the program's `ceu_carryover_max_credits`, rounded half up to two decimals. Exactly meeting `required_credits` therefore carries nothing forward, and a twenty-five-credit surplus is truncated to the ten-credit ceiling under the default. **Hop two:** because the cycle closed, the certificant's `valid_through` advances by the program's `cycle_length_months` and the certificant's `version` increments by 1. **Hop three:** because `valid_through` advanced, a successor cycle is opened with `cycle_number` one greater than the closing cycle's, `starts_on` equal to the closing cycle's `ends_on`, `carryover_credits_in` equal to the closing cycle's `carryover_credits_out`, and `required_credits` and `accrual_basis_snapshot` freshly re-pinned from the program as it stands now; the certificant's `current_cycle_id` is repointed at the successor.

Not every holder wants to accumulate credit, and the re-examination path is the alternative. Electing re-examination, optionally annotated with an `election_note` — validated as a string when present but not retained anywhere on the cycle — is available to the holder while the cycle's `status` is `OPEN` or `LAPSED` and its `path` is still `CEU`; any other combination is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The election sets `path` to `RE_EXAM`, sets `required_credits` to 0, forces `counted` to false and `credits_counted` to 0 on every activity under the cycle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `AUDIT_SELECTED`, or `VERIFIED` — an activity already sitting at false and 0.00 is left entirely alone, with neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moving — and recomputes `credits_applied` to 0.00. The election leaves the cycle's `status` exactly as it found it, so an `OPEN` cycle stays `OPEN` and a `LAPSED` one stays `LAPSED` with its assessed `reinstatement_fee_cents` intact; from that point the cycle can be satisfied only by passing an examination. Satisfaction arrives as a system consequence of score release: when a registration whose candidate matches the certificant's `candidate_id` is reported with `outcome` `PASS`, and that candidate already holds a credential whose cycle is on the `RE_EXAM` path and still `OPEN` or `LAPSED`, the existing cycle records the reported registration in `reexam_registration_id` and moves `OPEN → SATISFIED` or `LAPSED → SATISFIED` — **instead of** a second certificant being minted. The search for that cycle is scoped by candidate alone and takes no account of which program the passing sitting was for, and where the candidate has more than one such cycle the one with the lowest `cycle_number` is the one satisfied. Satisfying it leaves the holder's own `status` untouched — an `EXPIRED` holder whose lapsed cycle is satisfied this way stays `EXPIRED`, and only their `publication_status` and `suspension_until` projection is refreshed — so returning such a holder to `ACTIVE` remains a matter of reinstatement.

A cycle that neither accumulates enough credit nor records a passing re-examination eventually lapses. When the logical clock is strictly past a cycle's `grace_ends_on` and the cycle is still `OPEN`, the maintenance run of §3.13 moves it `OPEN → LAPSED`, stamps its `reinstatement_fee_cents`, and moves the owning certificant `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, whose `publication_status` accordingly recomputes to `WITHHELD` and whose public verification then reports `credential_status` `EXPIRED`. The reinstatement fee is assessed in a deliberate two-step order, because the administrative tier is keyed on the **subtotal** rather than on the final total. First take the whole days elapsed between `grace_ends_on` and the current date — at least one by construction — and compute the late surcharge as 250 cents per day late, capped at 30000 cents, which makes the surcharge flat beyond the hundred-and-twentieth day late. Add that surcharge to the base reinstatement fee of 25000 cents to obtain the subtotal. Then select the administrative rate from the subtotal: 2 percent when the subtotal is at most 30000 cents, 5 percent when it is above 30000 and at most 45000 cents, and 8 percent when it is above 45000 cents. The administrative fee is that rate applied to the **whole** subtotal — an average rate, not a marginal one — rounded half up to whole cents, and `reinstatement_fee_cents` is the subtotal plus the administrative fee. Twenty days late therefore gives a 5000-cent surcharge, a 30000-cent subtotal, the 2 percent rate, a 600-cent administrative fee, and a 30600-cent total; keying the tier off the total instead would have selected 5 percent and produced 31500, which is precisely the error this ordering prevents.

Reinstatement is the holder's remedy. It carries `payment_cents`, requires the cycle's `status` to be `LAPSED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — requires the owning certificant not to be `REVOKED` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED` — and requires `payment_cents` to be at least the cycle's assessed `reinstatement_fee_cents`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_UNPAID`. On success the cycle moves `LAPSED → OPEN`, its `grace_ends_on` is extended by the program's `recert_grace_days`, and its `reinstatement_fee_cents` is cleared back to null; the owning certificant moves `EXPIRED → ACTIVE` with `publication_status` recomputed to `PUBLISHED` and its `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.12. Discipline, Sanction and Revocation

An `EthicsOfficer` opens a disciplinary case against a credential holder, naming the `certificant_id` of the respondent, an `allegation_type` of `CREDENTIAL_MISUSE`, `EXAM_MISCONDUCT`, `CEU_FALSIFICATION`, or `ETHICS_VIOLATION`, and a `description` carrying the allegation narrative. A case may not be opened against an already-revoked credential; the attempt is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. A new case has `status` `OPENED`, `response_round` 0, and `response_text`, `outcome`, `sanction_months`, and `decided_on` all null, with `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Due process is a negotiation loop, and it is the officer who initiates each round. Requesting a response, optionally annotated with a `request_note`, moves the case from `OPENED` or from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `RESPONSE_REQUESTED`; any other status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the respondent must not already be revoked, or `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. The respondent — acting in the `Certificant` role and only on their own case — then answers with `response_text`, which moves the case `RESPONSE_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, increases `response_round` by 1, and overwrites `response_text` with the latest submission. The `UNDER_REVIEW → RESPONSE_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` circuit may repeat as many times as the officer wishes, with `response_round` counting the rounds actually completed.

Deciding a case closes it. The officer submits an `outcome` of `DISMISSED`, `SANCTIONED`, or `REVOKED`, optionally a `decision_note`, and a `sanction_months` value of between 1 and 60 that must be present exactly when the `outcome` is `SANCTIONED` and must be null in every other case — a mismatch is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The case must be in `OPENED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` status, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the respondent must not already be revoked, or `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. A `REVOKED` decision additionally requires that the deciding officer not be the case's `opened_by_user_id`: the officer who brought the case may never be the one who ends the credential, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `SANCTIONED` or `REVOKED` decision requires at least one completed response round, so a case whose `response_round` is still 0 is refused with `ERR_DUE_PROCESS_INCOMPLETE`; by §2.7 that round test resolves before the sanction branch's own demand that the respondent be `ACTIVE`, so a `SANCTIONED` decision against a suspended holder on a case with no completed round reports `ERR_DUE_PROCESS_INCOMPLETE` rather than `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Every decision writes the `outcome` and stamps `decided_on` from the logical clock, and moves the case to `CLOSED`, which is terminal.

The three outcomes differ entirely in what they do to the credential. `DISMISSED` mutates no certificant at all. `SANCTIONED` requires the respondent's `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE` — and moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, setting `suspension_until` to the current logical-clock instant advanced by `sanction_months` months, recomputing `publication_status` to `WITHHELD`, and incrementing the certificant's `version` by 1; while suspended, credit claims against that holder's cycle are refused with `ERR_CERTIFICANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A suspension is not permanent — the maintenance run of §3.13 restores a suspended holder `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` once the clock reaches `suspension_until`, which makes `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` the sanction cycle.

`REVOKED` is the terminal sanction and runs a three-hop cascade. It is reachable from `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRED`, and only an already-`REVOKED` credential refuses it, with `ERR_CERTIFICANT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. **Hop one:** the certificant's `status` becomes `REVOKED`, its `publication_status` recomputes to `REMOVED` so the credential disappears from public verification, and its `current_cycle_id` is cleared. **Hop two:** because the certificant was revoked, every recertification cycle of that certificant whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SATISFIED`, or `LAPSED` moves to `VOID`, which is terminal. **Hop three:** because those cycles were voided, every continuing-education activity beneath them whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `AUDIT_SELECTED`, or `VERIFIED` has `counted` set to false and `credits_counted` set to 0, and each affected cycle's `credits_applied` is recomputed to 0.00. The certificant's `version` increments by 1. After revocation, no further disciplinary or recertification action is admitted on that holder.

### 3.13. The Scheduled Maintenance Run

Because setting the logical clock is inert, every time-driven transition in this system is materialised by one idempotent operations checkpoint, triggered by a `ProgramAdmin`. It accepts an optional `dry_run` flag and executes **four steps in a fixed order**, each step processing its candidate rows in `created_at` ascending order.

**Step one — registrations.** Every registration whose `seat_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose held session's `ends_at` the logical clock is strictly past moves to `NO_SHOW`. The seat is released, so the session's `seats_reserved` decreases by 1, and the attempt is nevertheless consumed: the owning application's `attempts_consumed` increases by 1 even though no score will ever exist, and because that derived total has changed the application is itself re-stamped, its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. A no-show therefore both frees a seat and burns an attempt.

**Step two — applications.** Every application whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `eligibility_expires_on` the logical clock is strictly past moves to `EXPIRED`. New registrations citing such an application are refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED`, but registrations already created are unaffected, because each carries its own `eligibility_expires_on_snapshot`.

**Step three — cycles.** Every cycle whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `grace_ends_on` the logical clock is strictly past moves to `LAPSED`, has its `reinstatement_fee_cents` assessed as described in §3.11, and drags its owning certificant `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with `publication_status` recomputed to `WITHHELD`.

**Step four — reactivations.** Every certificant whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` and whose `suspension_until` the logical clock has reached moves back to `ACTIVE`, with `suspension_until` cleared and `publication_status` recomputed to `PUBLISHED`. In the same step, every candidate whose `status` is `BARRED` and whose `barred_until` the logical clock has reached moves back to `ACTIVE`. Because step three runs before step four and each step considers only rows in the state the earlier steps left behind, a cycle that lapses in step three is never also reactivated in step four of the same run.

The run answers with a report object rather than an entity: `run_at` records the logical-clock instant of the run, and six identifier lists record what it touched — `no_show_registration_ids`, `expired_application_ids`, `lapsed_cycle_ids`, `expired_certificant_ids`, `reactivated_certificant_ids`, and `unbarred_candidate_ids`. When `dry_run` is true the run computes and reports exactly the same six lists while mutating nothing at all, which makes it safe to inspect before committing.
