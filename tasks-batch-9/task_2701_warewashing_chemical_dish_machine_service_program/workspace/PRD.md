# Warewashing Chemical & Dish Machine Service Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Commercial warewashing is sold as a program, not as a product. A chemical company places a dish machine in a customer's kitchen at its own expense, plumbs dispensers into that machine, commits to a minimum supply term, and then earns its margin back over the life of the agreement through detergent, rinse aid and sanitizer volume. Everything that makes the program profitable — that the dispensers meter the dose they are supposed to meter, that the final rinse actually reaches sanitising temperature, that the chemical delivered to the back door is the chemical that goes through the machine — is invisible unless somebody instruments it. This API is that instrument.

The system this document specifies carries the whole program on one surface. Commercial staff draft chemical supply agreements with a minimum term and a committed monthly spend, place machines against those agreements, and retrieve the equipment when the relationship ends. Device telemetry streams cycle counts, wash and rinse temperatures and rinse-line pressure off every installed machine; threshold breaches open alerts, alerts drive a machine's health axis, and a machine that goes down inside its kitchen's operating hours auto-raises a prioritised service ticket that the dispatch selector routes to a region-eligible, adequately certified technician. Field technicians titrate concentration, adjust doses and time sanitizer contact; an independent quality auditor decides whether that field evidence is trustworthy enough to become the record of truth. Where measured chemical consumption diverges from what the machines should have drawn, the system computes the variance, classifies it, and — under a strict two-auditor rule — converts a substantiated theft or dilution finding into a chargeback.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is that **evidence is append-only and adjudicated**: telemetry readings and calibration records are immutable rows, and a calibration only changes a dispenser's live dose once a second person has verified the visit that produced it. The second is that **every number is reproducible**: the service runs on an explicit logical clock rather than wall time, aggregation windows are half-open, rounding points are pinned to specific steps of specific formulas, and validation failures are reported in a fixed priority order so that the same request always produces the same rejection. All endpoints are mounted under `/warewashing`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts and cross-cutting principles that hold everywhere in the API. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, this section describes how every request is authenticated, shaped, ordered, timed and rounded.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller in two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity it is acting in. Either header missing or present but empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before any other check runs. The single exception is the logical-clock endpoint, which is a test-driving hook and requires no headers at all. Once the headers are read, the declared role must appear among the roles the addressed endpoint permits; a role that does not is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, and that check runs **before** the request body is parsed, so a forbidden role that also sends a malformed body still sees the role error rather than a shape error.

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

- **`AccountManager`** — the commercial owner of an account. Registers customers and kitchens, drafts, activates, renews and terminates supply agreements, places machines and raises retrieval orders, records and voids chemical deliveries, and may file a service ticket on a customer's behalf. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `reported_by_user_id` on a ticket the manager files) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- **`ServiceTechnician`** — the field engineer. Installs machines and confirms retrievals, fits dispensers, acknowledges alerts, schedules and works visits, and records concentration and efficacy verifications. When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's technician foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, scheduling a visit requires the submitted `technician_id` to equal `X-User-ID`, and submitting a visit requires the visit's `technician_id` to equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
- **`QualityAuditor`** — the independent compliance reviewer. Adjudicates submitted visits, drafts, publishes and closes usage-variance reviews, and opens, evidences and adjudicates investigation cases. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `created_by_user_id`, `published_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `opened_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` depending on the action, and compares those recorded values to one another to enforce separation of duties, but never compares them to any entity's `id`.
- **`CustomerContact`** — the kitchen-side contact for exactly one account. Reports machine problems and reads that account's own equipment, delivery, savings and inspection data. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's owning-customer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. The owning customer is resolved through `Site.customer_id`, through a placement's site to that site's customer, or through `ServiceAgreement.customer_id`. A `CustomerContact` addressing a resource that belongs to a different account is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even though the resource genuinely exists.
- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the operator of the platform itself. Maintains the chemical and machine catalogs and the technician roster, ingests device telemetry, runs the dispatch selector and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Ownership scoping is evaluated after the role check and before the request body is validated, so a `CustomerContact` reaching into another account's data learns nothing about whether its payload was well-formed.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it must be an integer no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100, and it defaults to 10 when omitted. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it must be an integer no smaller than 0, and it defaults to 0 when omitted. Values that are not integers, or that fall outside those ranges, are **rejected** with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` rather than silently clamped to the nearest legal value.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the immutable telemetry stream, which is ordered by `reading_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Time-Driven Sweep

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. A single server-held logical clock supplies every instant the platform stamps — a placement's `installed_at`, an alert's `opened_at`, a ticket's `response_deadline_at`, a visit's `arrival_at`, a placement's `retrieved_at`, the verification instant of a visit and a dispenser's `dose_effective_at` and `last_calibrated_at`. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time.

A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant. The clock never runs backwards: a `current_utc` earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Advancing the clock is the only way to age an agreement into its renewal notice window, to push an agreement past its term, or to carry a ticket past its response deadline.

Each advance runs a deterministic sweep, and the response reports what the sweep did through three counters — `agreements_moved_to_renewal_pending`, `agreements_expired` and `tickets_escalated` — alongside the new `current_utc`, so that a caller can assert the sweep ran rather than inferring it. The sweep has three parts, and every agreement it considers is visited in `created_at` ascending order:

1. **Renewal notice.** An agreement in `ACTIVE` moves to `RENEWAL_PENDING` when the number of whole days remaining until its term end is no greater than its `renewal_notice_days` and the new clock value is still strictly before the term end. "Whole days remaining" is measured as the difference between midnight UTC on `term_end_date` and the new clock value, divided by one day and rounded down toward zero.
2. **Expiry.** An agreement in either `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` moves to `EXPIRED` when the new clock value is at or after midnight UTC on its `term_end_date`. Expiry fires the full equipment-withdrawal cascade described in §2.10. A single large advance can satisfy both part 1 and part 2; when it does, the agreement goes straight to `EXPIRED` and the `RENEWAL_PENDING` hop is skipped entirely.
3. **SLA escalation.** A ticket whose `status` is `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE`, whose `response_deadline_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock value, and whose `sla_breached` is still false, has `sla_breached` set to true and `escalation_level` raised by one. Because the flag guards the increment, a second, later advance over the same already-breached ticket does not raise `escalation_level` again.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Mutability and Version Semantics

Every resource the API creates carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp — an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits.

Most resources are mutable and therefore additionally carry two fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every change to the row, including changes the row did not ask for — a counter recomputed by a cascade is a change. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row; it is the token the optimistic-concurrency mechanism of §2.7 compares against. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation: when a placement's `open_alert_count` is re-derived, or a customer's `equipment_on_placement_value_cents` is credited back, or an agreement's `placement_count` changes, that row's `updated_at` and `version` both move.

Two record types are deliberately immutable evidence and are the exception. A `TelemetryReading` and a `CalibrationRecord` each carry only `id` and `created_at`: **both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from these two resources**, neither has an update endpoint, and no field on either row is ever rewritten after insertion. A calibration record's `applied` flag is the one apparent exception, and it is not one — the flag is a projection of whether the owning visit was verified, and it is described in §3.6 as part of that verification.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Pinned Rounding Points

Monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent representations anywhere in the API. Percentages, doses, litres and kilowatt-hours are decimal numbers. Wherever a value must be reduced to a fixed number of digits, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of digits is fixed per field: monetary results to whole cents, `drift_pct` and `variance_pct` and `goal_attainment_pct` to two decimal places, doses and per-rack baselines to two decimal places, and the rinse-pressure factor of the sustainability report to four decimal places.

Where a formula rounds matters as much as how it rounds, and this specification pins each rounding point deliberately. Three of them are counterintuitive and are called out here because getting them wrong changes results by whole cents:

- **Delivery pricing rounds early.** The discounted price of one container is rounded to whole cents first, and the rounded per-container price is then multiplied by the container count. Multiplying the exact discounted price by the count and rounding the total once is wrong.
- **Early-termination equipment recovery rounds per machine.** Each placement's unamortized book value is rounded to whole cents individually, and the rounded per-machine amounts are then added together. Summing exact fractions and rounding the total once is wrong.
- **Expected chemical consumption rounds late.** Every per-dispenser and per-reading product is kept exact, and one single rounding to whole millilitres is applied to the site-level total. This is deliberately the opposite order from the early-termination fee, and the two must not be made consistent with each other.

Comparisons against thresholds always use the **rounded** value, not the exact one. A drift of `10.004` percent rounds to `10.00` and is therefore compared as `10.00`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations in this API create a child row whose meaning depends entirely on the state of a parent row that the client read earlier. In each case a second, legitimate write of the same shape is perfectly valid — placing a second machine on the same agreement, delivering the same product to the same kitchen again, re-titrating the same dispenser — so no state precondition can protect the client from having missed an intervening change. For those three writes, and only those three, the client must hand back the `version` it last observed on the parent, and a mismatch is refused.

- A `POST /warewashing/machine-placements` request must carry `expected_agreement_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ServiceAgreement`. If it does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the client's view of the agreement's term, its covered products or its placement ledger predates an accepted write.
- A `POST /warewashing/chemical-deliveries` request must carry `expected_site_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Site`. If it does not equal the site's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the delivery was priced against an out-of-date view of the site's inventory ledger.
- A `POST /warewashing/service-visits/{visit_id}/calibration-records` request must carry `expected_dispenser_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Dispenser`. If it does not equal the dispenser's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because the technician measured against a dose that has since been superseded.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. The three checks above run after path existence and body reference resolution but **before** every state, segregation, safety, referential, uniqueness and temporal rule, so a stale parent version masks every downstream violation the request would otherwise have reported.

### 2.8. The Rule Ladder: Which Rejection Wins

A single request can violate several rules at once. Which error the caller sees is not a matter of implementation convenience — it is part of the contract. Rules are evaluated in the following ladder, and the **first** rung that fails determines the response. Rules that share a rung are noted individually where their relative order matters.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role permission** — the declared role is one the endpoint accepts, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Evaluated before the body is parsed.
3. **Ownership scope** — a `CustomerContact` may only address its own account's resources and a `ServiceTechnician` may only act as itself, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. **Request shape** — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The body must be a JSON object; every required field must be present and non-null; every field must match its declared JSON type; enum fields must name a declared member; monetary and count fields must be integers no smaller than zero; percentage tolerances must be numeric and greater than zero; timestamps must be ISO 8601 UTC; unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. Interval fields must be strictly ordered — `period_start` earlier than `period_end`, and `scheduled_start_at` earlier than `scheduled_end_at`. `measured_ppm` must be greater than zero. `covered_product_ids` must be a non-empty array of distinct strings. An agreement create request carries no `term_end_date` at all, because the term end is derived.
5. **Pagination bounds** — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. **Path resource existence** — every identifier embedded in the URL must resolve to a live row of the expected entity type, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). An id that belongs to a different entity type is treated as not found.
7. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key supplied in a body must resolve to an existing row, otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_REFERENCE` (422). The keys in scope are `customer_id`, `site_id`, `product_id`, `machine_model_id`, `agreement_id`, `placement_id`, `dispenser_id`, `technician_id`, `ticket_id`, `visit_id`, `variance_review_id`, `follow_up_to_visit_id` and `override_technician_id`.
8. **Parent version currency** — the optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412).
9. **State legality** — the addressed row must be in a state from which the requested action is declared. The generic failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409); several situations carry their own more specific code at this same rung: `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_INSTALLED`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_DISPATCHABLE` and `ERR_OPEN_TICKET_BLOCKS_RETRIEVAL` (all 409).
10. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Deliberately placed *after* every state rule, so a case that is in the wrong stage reports its state error first even when the caller is also the wrong auditor.
11. **Safety** — `ERR_SANITIZER_LOCKOUT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_EFFICACY_FAILURE_UNRESOLVED` and `ERR_SITE_POSTURE_CRITICAL` (all 409).
12. **Referential business rules** — `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ON_AGREEMENT` (422), `ERR_DISPENSER_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH` (422) and `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH` (422). The region check in particular is evaluated before any schedule-overlap check.
13. **Uniqueness and value constraints** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPENSER_PRODUCT` (409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_TICKET` (409), `ERR_ASSET_TAG_TAKEN` (409), `ERR_NON_MONOTONIC_CYCLE_COUNT` (422), `ERR_ADJUSTED_DOSE_REQUIRED` (422), `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TELEMETRY` (422) and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` (409).
14. **Temporal rules** — `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409), `ERR_REVIEW_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409), `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_TERM` (422), `ERR_READING_BEFORE_INSTALL` (422) and `ERR_DELIVERY_IN_CLOSED_PERIOD` (409).
15. **Fiscal standing** — `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (402). This is the lowest rung in the whole ladder: a credit hold is reported only when the request is otherwise entirely valid.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. Publishing a variance review checks, in this order: that the review is still `DRAFT` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT`), then that the publishing auditor is not the drafting auditor (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), then that the site's posture is not `CRITICAL` (`ERR_SITE_POSTURE_CRITICAL`), then that the window carried usable telemetry (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TELEMETRY`), and only then that the window does not collide with another live review of the same kitchen and product (`ERR_REVIEW_PERIOD_OVERLAP`). And a machine placement against an agreement whose version the client has not caught up with reports the stale version rather than the agreement's status, the duplicate asset tag or the customer's credit hold, even when all four are true at once.

### 2.9. Addressing: Flat Collections, Nested Evidence Writes, Left-to-Right Resolution

The resource graph is deliberately flat. Customers, kitchens, chemical products, machine models, agreements, placements, dispensers, technicians, alerts, tickets, visits, deliveries, variance reviews and investigation cases are all addressed as top-level collections and related to one another through foreign keys carried in bodies and offered as list filters, not through deep URLs.

Three writes are the exception, because the rows they create are append-only evidence with no standalone identity in the product surface: a telemetry reading is ingested under its placement, and a calibration record is recorded under the visit that produced it. Those rows are nonetheless **read flat** — there are stand-alone read endpoints for a single telemetry reading and for a single calibration record, and flat list endpoints with foreign-key filters for both, so that a cascade-created or rejected-visit record is always discoverable. A calibration record from a rejected visit remains fully visible with its `applied` flag false.

Wherever a URL carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the request fails on the **first** one that does not resolve, with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. The identifiers that appear in paths are `customer_id`, `site_id`, `product_id`, `model_id`, `technician_id`, `agreement_id`, `placement_id`, `dispenser_id`, `record_id`, `reading_id`, `alert_id`, `ticket_id`, `visit_id`, `delivery_id`, `review_id` and `case_id`. For a nested evidence write this means the container is validated first: a calibration record posted under a visit that does not exist reports the missing visit, and never reaches the dispenser reference or the version token in the body.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers and Cascading Consistency

Two counters in this system are **conserved quantities**: they must return to their prior value when the event that raised them is undone, and any drift is a defect rather than an accounting nuance.

- **The equipment ledger.** `Customer.equipment_on_placement_value_cents` is at all times the sum, over that customer's placements whose `placement_status` is `PENDING_INSTALL`, `INSTALLED` or `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, of the `equipment_value_cents` of each placement's machine model. Placing a machine capitalises that model's book value onto the ledger; cancelling a never-installed placement or completing a retrieval credits exactly the same amount back, restoring the ledger to its pre-placement value.
- **The technician workload counter.** `Technician.open_ticket_count` is at all times the number of tickets assigned to that technician whose `status` is `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. Dispatch raises it by one; a closed ticket and a rejected visit each lower it by one. Because all three statuses count alike, moving a ticket from `DISPATCHED` to `ON_SITE` to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` leaves the counter untouched.

Alongside those, several fields are pure derivations, recomputed whenever anything they depend on changes rather than being written by clients: `Customer.active_agreement_count` (the count of that customer's agreements whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`), `ServiceAgreement.placement_count`, `ServiceAgreement.renewal_count`, `Site.open_ticket_count` (the count of tickets on that site's placements whose `status` is `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`), `Site.service_posture`, `MachinePlacement.open_alert_count`, `MachinePlacement.last_cycle_count`, `MachinePlacement.cycles_since_calibration`, `ServiceVisit.calibration_record_count`, `Technician.quality_flag_count`, `InvestigationCase.evidence_item_count` and `InvestigationCase.return_count`. None of these is writable through any endpoint; a request that supplies one is treated as sending an unknown field and it is ignored.

Three named cascades run through the system, and each is described in full where its trigger lives. Their shape is stated here so that a reader recognises the vocabulary:

- **Cascade alpha — equipment withdrawal.** An agreement reaching `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED` adjusts the customer's active-agreement count, schedules every `INSTALLED` placement of that agreement for retrieval, locks out every `ACTIVE` dispenser on those placements, suppresses every still-open alert on those placements, and cancels their `QUEUED` tickets. Full detail in §3.2.
- **Cascade beta — telemetry escalation.** A device reading breaches a model threshold, the breach opens an alert, the alert drives the placement's health axis, a placement driven to `DOWN` auto-raises a ticket, and the ticket re-derives the kitchen's posture and open-ticket count. Full detail in §3.4.
- **Cascade gamma — investigation closure.** An investigation case reaching a terminal outcome closes the variance review it was opened against, and a review closed with a dispenser-replacement remediation locks out the site's dispensers metering the reviewed product and degrades their machines. Full detail in §3.8.

Every row a cascade touches is genuinely mutated: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, exactly as though the change had been requested directly. Cascade steps that iterate over a set of rows always process that set in `created_at` ascending order, so the resulting sequence of version numbers is deterministic.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Field work and evidence adjudication are strictly separated capabilities, and commercial action can touch neither. A `ServiceTechnician` records measurements but never decides whether they stand; a `QualityAuditor` decides but never records; an `AccountManager` does neither.

Separation also holds *within* the auditor role, in two places. The auditor who drafts a usage-variance review may not be the auditor who publishes it: publication requires an `X-User-ID` different from the review's `created_by_user_id`. And the auditor who adjudicates an investigation case may be neither the auditor who opened it nor the auditor who published the review it was opened against: adjudication requires an `X-User-ID` equal to neither the case's `opened_by_user_id` nor the linked review's `published_by_user_id`. Converting an observed variance into a chargeback therefore takes at least three distinct auditor identities across the drafting, publication and adjudication steps. Every one of these violations is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.12. Windows: Half-Open Periods and Operating Hours

Every reporting and review period in this system is **half-open**: an instant belongs to a window when it is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. A delivery stamped exactly at a window's `period_end` falls outside that window, and two windows that meet exactly — one ending where the next begins — do not overlap. Review-window collision detection carries no buffer at all and relies on precisely this convention.

A kitchen's daily operating hours are expressed as two minute-of-day integers, `operating_window_start_minute_utc` and `operating_window_end_minute_utc`, each counted from midnight UTC and each in the range 0 through 1439. The start is inclusive and the end is exclusive. When the start value is strictly smaller than the end value the window is an ordinary same-day interval, and an instant is inside it when its minute of day is at or after the start and strictly before the end. When the end value is at or before the start value the window wraps past midnight, and an instant is inside it when its minute of day is at or after the start **or** strictly before the end. The minute of day of an instant is its hour multiplied by 60, plus its minute.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Accounts, Kitchens and the Two Catalogs

An account enters the system as a **Customer**: a foodservice operator identified by its legal `name`, between 1 and 120 characters. Registration also fixes `sustainability_goal_water_l`, the account's annual water-reduction goal in litres, which must be an integer of at least 1 and which later serves as the denominator of goal attainment in the savings report. Commercial standing lives in `billing_status`, which is either `CURRENT` or `CREDIT_HOLD` and defaults to `CURRENT` when the registration omits it — an account may be seeded straight onto hold. The two ledger counters, `equipment_on_placement_value_cents` and `active_agreement_count`, both start at 0 and are maintained by the system thereafter.

Under an account sit one or more **Sites** — individual kitchens. A site is created against its owning account by supplying `customer_id`, which is immutable once set, together with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `region_code` of 2 to 4 uppercase characters. The region code is the dispatch key: a technician is eligible to work a site only when the technician's own region code equals the site's. Contractual service level lives in `priority_tier`, either `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, defaulting to `STANDARD`; the premium tier buys a fixed uplift in dispatch priority. Site registration also records the daily operating window as `operating_window_start_minute_utc` and `operating_window_end_minute_utc` under the rules of §2.12, and four numbers that make sustainability reporting possible: `baseline_water_l_per_rack` and `baseline_kwh_per_rack`, both greater than zero and carried to two decimal places, describe the kitchen's pre-program consumption per rack, while `water_cost_cents_per_1000l` and `energy_cost_cents_per_kwh`, both integers of zero or more, are the tariffs that turn litres and kilowatt-hours into money. A site's own lifecycle is `status`, one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`.

Two derived fields on a site are worth understanding before anything else in §3 makes sense, because they gate publication of variance work. `open_ticket_count` is the number of service tickets on that site's placements whose status is `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. `service_posture` grades that backlog into one of three values, evaluated in this order: it is `CRITICAL` when at least one of those open tickets carries a `priority_score` of 700 or more; otherwise it is `ELEVATED` when at least one such open ticket exists at all; otherwise it is `NORMAL`. Both start at their empty values — `open_ticket_count` at 0 and `service_posture` at `NORMAL` — when the site is registered.

The **chemical catalog** is maintained by the platform operator. Each `ChemicalProduct` carries an `sku` that must be unique across the whole catalog and a `product_type` of `DETERGENT`, `RINSE_AID` or `SANITIZER`. Dosing and drift are governed by `concentration_target_ppm`, the label target in parts per million and strictly greater than zero, and `concentration_tolerance_pct`, the absolute drift percentage that still counts as a pass, greater than zero, carried to two decimal places and defaulting to `10.00` when omitted. `spec_dose_ml_per_rack` is the nameplate dose per rack, greater than zero to two decimal places. Container economics are `unit_size_ml`, the millilitres in one shipped container, and `unit_price_cents`, the undiscounted price of one container. Three further fields apply only to sanitizers: `sanitizer_min_ppm` and `sanitizer_max_ppm` bound the efficacy window, and `min_contact_seconds` is the minimum contact time for an efficacy pass, at least 1 and defaulting to `7`. All three are **required** when `product_type` is `SANITIZER` and must be **omitted** for a `DETERGENT` or a `RINSE_AID`; supplying them on a non-sanitizer, or leaving them off a sanitizer, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On a sanitizer the three concentration numbers must nest: `sanitizer_min_ppm` no greater than `concentration_target_ppm`, and `concentration_target_ppm` no greater than `sanitizer_max_ppm`, with `sanitizer_max_ppm` strictly greater than `sanitizer_min_ppm`.

The **machine catalog** is the other half of the platform's reference data. A `MachineModel` has a `model_code` unique across all models and a `machine_class` of `UNDERCOUNTER`, `DOOR_TYPE` or `CONVEYOR`. `racks_per_hour` is the rated throughput and is the yardstick the cycle-rate alert measures against. `rated_water_l_per_rack` and `rated_kwh_per_rack` are the nameplate consumption figures, each greater than zero to two decimal places. Four thresholds define what "healthy" means for a machine of this model: `min_final_rinse_temp_f`, the minimum sanitising final-rinse temperature in Fahrenheit; `min_wash_temp_f`, the minimum wash-tank temperature; and `min_rinse_pressure_psi` and `max_rinse_pressure_psi`, the acceptable band for final-rinse line pressure, where the maximum must be strictly greater than the minimum — a model definition that inverts them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `equipment_value_cents` is the machine's book value, strictly positive, and it is what the equipment ledger capitalises and what the unamortized-equipment view of an early-termination fee recovers. `max_cycles_between_calibrations` is the cycle budget after which the machine is treated as overdue for calibration.

Field capacity is modelled as a roster of **Technicians**. Each has a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `region_code` of 2 to 4 uppercase characters, and a `certification_level` of `L1`, `L2` or `SANITATION_CERTIFIED`. Dispatch availability is `status`, one of `AVAILABLE`, `OFF_DUTY` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`; the two derived counters `open_ticket_count` and `quality_flag_count` both start at 0. The roster is editable in exactly two respects — a technician can be taken off duty or brought back, and a certification change can be recorded — so a roster update accepts only `status` and `certification_level`. The counters are computed and cannot be written. Taking a technician off duty or suspending them removes them from the dispatch candidate set immediately but leaves every visit already booked in their name untouched and still binding on the schedule.

### 3.2. The Chemical Supply Agreement: Drafting, Activation, Renewal, Termination

Nothing can be placed in a kitchen without an agreement to supply it. An `AccountManager` drafts a `ServiceAgreement` against a `customer_id` with an `agreement_code` unique across all agreements, a `start_date` as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` date from which the minimum term counts, a `minimum_term_months` of at least 1 whole month, a `monthly_minimum_spend_cents` committed monthly chemical spend of zero or more, and `covered_product_ids` — the non-empty list of catalog product identifiers the agreement supplies. Every identifier in that list must resolve to a real product, or the draft is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_REFERENCE`. Two optional fields round out the draft: `renewal_notice_days`, how many days before the term end the renewal window opens, at least 1 and defaulting to 60, and `auto_renew`, a boolean defaulting to false that is surfaced on renewal notices as information only and never itself renews anything. A fresh draft has `status` `DRAFT`, `renewal_count` 0, `placement_count` 0, and both `early_termination_fee_cents` and `termination_effective_date` null. The request never carries `term_end_date`.

Activation is the moment the term begins to run. Activating a `DRAFT` agreement requires that it still be in `DRAFT` and that every identifier in `covered_product_ids` still resolve to an existing product; the agreement then moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. Activation is also where `term_end_date` is computed: the `start_date` advanced by `minimum_term_months` calendar months, keeping the same day of month, and clamping to the last day of the target month when the source day does not exist there — a term of one month starting on 31 January ends on 28 February in a common year. Activation raises the customer's `active_agreement_count` by one and increments the agreement's `version` by one.

Renewal extends the term. The renewal action accepts one optional field, `renewal_term_months`, and when it is omitted the extension is the agreement's own `minimum_term_months`. Renewal advances `term_end_date` by that many calendar months from its **current** value, using the same day-of-month-with-clamping rule, raises `renewal_count` by one, leaves the agreement in or returns it to `ACTIVE`, and increments `version` by one. There are two ways in. Renewing an agreement that is still `ACTIVE` is a self-loop — the status does not change, only the term extends — and it is permitted only inside the notice window: the clock must be at or after the term end minus `renewal_notice_days`, and strictly before the term end. Outside that band the request is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, whether it came too early or after the term had already lapsed. Renewing an agreement that the clock has already moved to `RENEWAL_PENDING` transitions it `RENEWAL_PENDING → ACTIVE` and needs no separate window test, because the clock placed it in `RENEWAL_PENDING` precisely by finding the window open and would have taken it to `EXPIRED` the moment the term lapsed. Between them, these two paths close the `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_PENDING → ACTIVE` cycle.

Termination is the sharpest operation in the commercial half of the system, and it behaves in two entirely different ways depending on where it starts. The request always carries `effective_date`.

From `DRAFT`, termination is a **void**: the agreement moves `DRAFT → VOID`, `early_termination_fee_cents` is set to 0 because an agreement that never activated carries no fee, `termination_effective_date` is set to the supplied `effective_date`, and `version` increments by one. No equipment cascade runs, because a `DRAFT` agreement cannot carry placements.

From `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`, termination is a real early exit: the agreement moves to `TERMINATED`. The supplied `effective_date` must be on or after the current logical clock's date; a back-dated termination is rejected. `termination_effective_date` is stamped from the request, `early_termination_fee_cents` is stamped from the fee calculation below, the customer's `active_agreement_count` drops by one, and the equipment-withdrawal cascade runs in full.

**The early-termination fee.** Two recovery theories are always both evaluated and the customer pays the larger. First, the count of remaining months: take the number of whole days from `termination_effective_date` to `term_end_date`, divide by 30 days, and round up to the next whole number; when that day count is zero or negative, the remaining-month count is zero. The **remaining-minimum** view then recovers the unspent chemical commitment as the remaining-month count multiplied by `monthly_minimum_spend_cents` multiplied by a shortfall rate of 60 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The **unamortized-equipment** view instead recovers book value: for each placement of this agreement whose `placement_status` is `PENDING_INSTALL`, `INSTALLED` or `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, multiply that machine model's `equipment_value_cents` by the remaining-month count, divide by the agreement's `minimum_term_months`, and **round that per-machine amount half up to whole cents before adding it to the running total**; placements already `RETRIEVED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. The fee stamped onto the agreement is whichever of the two totals is greater. When equipment value dominates the chemical commitment — a conveyor machine on a long term terminated early — the equipment view wins; when the commitment dominates, the first view wins.

**Cascade alpha: equipment withdrawal.** Whenever an agreement reaches `EXPIRED` (by clock advance) or `TERMINATED` (by an account manager), the following links fire in order, and each link is caused by the link above it:

1. The customer's `active_agreement_count` is recomputed downward by one.
2. Caused by the agreement leaving live status: every placement of this agreement whose `placement_status` is `INSTALLED` transitions `INSTALLED → RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, processed in placement `created_at` ascending order, with `retrieval_due_date` set to the current clock's date plus 30 days.
3. Caused by each placement reaching `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`: every dispenser of that placement whose `dispenser_status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → LOCKED_OUT` with `lockout_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_ENDED`.
4. Caused by each dispenser lockout: every alert of that placement whose `status` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` transitions to `SUPPRESSED`.

Alongside those four links, every ticket on those placements whose `status` is `QUEUED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, and the agreement's own `version` increments by one. A `RENEWAL_PENDING` agreement that expires runs exactly the same four links as an `ACTIVE` one that expires; a terminated agreement runs links 2 through 4 identically and adjusts the active-agreement count as described above. `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` and `VOID` are all terminal: no further transition is declared from any of them, and firing one anyway is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Placing, Installing and Withdrawing a Machine

A `MachinePlacement` is one physical dish machine in one kitchen under one agreement. It is the only entity in the system that carries **two independent status axes on the same row**, and keeping them separate is essential: `placement_status` tracks where the machine is in its commercial lifecycle, running `PENDING_INSTALL → INSTALLED → RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED → RETRIEVED` with `CANCELLED` as the alternative ending, while `operational_status` tracks the machine's health, moving among `UP`, `DEGRADED`, `DOWN` and `OFFLINE`. Neither axis constrains the other's value except where this document says so explicitly. Installing a machine does not touch its health axis; a machine going down does not change its placement status; both `RETRIEVED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal on the lifecycle axis and `OFFLINE` is terminal on the health axis.

An `AccountManager` creates a placement with `site_id`, `agreement_id`, `machine_model_id`, an `asset_tag` of 1 to 40 characters, and the agreement version token `expected_agreement_version` described in §2.7. `initial_cycle_count` is optional, must be zero or greater, and defaults to 0; it is the rack odometer reading captured at install and the baseline the very first telemetry delta is measured against. Four business rules guard the create, and they are reported in the ladder order of §2.8: the referenced agreement must be in `ACTIVE`, and a `DRAFT`, `RENEWAL_PENDING`, `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` or `VOID` agreement is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the site's owning customer must be the same customer the agreement belongs to, and a cross-account pairing is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_REFERENCE`; the `asset_tag` must not already be held by any placement whose `placement_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, and a collision is refused with `ERR_ASSET_TAG_TAKEN`; and finally the customer's `billing_status` must be `CURRENT`, with a `CREDIT_HOLD` account refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` only once everything else about the request has passed.

A new placement begins with `placement_status` `PENDING_INSTALL`, `operational_status` `UP`, `installed_at` null, `last_cycle_count` equal to `initial_cycle_count`, `cycles_since_calibration` 0, `open_alert_count` 0, and both `retrieval_due_date` and `retrieved_at` null. Creation immediately capitalises the machine: the model's `equipment_value_cents` is added to the customer's `equipment_on_placement_value_cents`, the agreement's `placement_count` rises by one, and the agreement's `version` increments by one — which is precisely why a client placing two machines in a row must re-read the agreement between them.

Installation is a technician's action. It requires the placement to be in `PENDING_INSTALL`, the financing agreement to still be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and the site to be `ACTIVE`. The placement moves `PENDING_INSTALL → INSTALLED` and `installed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, which from that moment is the earliest instant any telemetry sample may claim. Nothing else moves: `last_cycle_count` stays at whatever `initial_cycle_count` was supplied, `cycles_since_calibration` stays 0, and `operational_status` stays `UP` — the two axes really are independent.

Withdrawing equipment happens in two steps, and the first step behaves differently depending on how far the placement got. An `AccountManager` raises a retrieval order, optionally supplying `retrieval_due_date`. A placement still in `PENDING_INSTALL` never became an installation at all, so it is **cancelled outright**: `placement_status` moves `PENDING_INSTALL → CANCELLED`, `operational_status` is driven to `OFFLINE`, the customer's equipment ledger is credited back the model's `equipment_value_cents` at once, the agreement's `placement_count` falls by one and the agreement's `version` increments by one. A placement in `INSTALLED` is instead **scheduled**: it moves `INSTALLED → RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, `retrieval_due_date` takes the supplied value or, when the request omits it, the current clock's date plus 14 days. That scheduling then withdraws the machine's chemistry and its work queue in the same request — every dispenser of the placement whose `dispenser_status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `LOCKED_OUT` with `lockout_reason` `AGREEMENT_ENDED`; caused by each such lockout, every alert of the placement whose `status` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` becomes `SUPPRESSED`; every ticket of the placement whose `status` is `QUEUED` becomes `CANCELLED`, and each such ticket's visits still in `SCHEDULED` become `CANCELLED`, which releases the technician's booked window so that it no longer participates in schedule-overlap detection; and the site's `open_ticket_count` and `service_posture` are re-derived. Note that a retrieval order driven by cascade alpha rather than by an account manager uses a due date of the current clock's date plus 30 days, not 14 — the manual default and the cascade default differ deliberately.

The second step is the technician's confirmation that the machine has physically left the kitchen. It requires `placement_status` `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` and, critically, that the placement have **no** ticket in `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`; if one exists the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_TICKET_BLOCKS_RETRIEVAL`. Tickets in `QUEUED` never block, because the retrieval order already cancelled them. On success the placement moves `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED → RETRIEVED`, `retrieved_at` is stamped from the clock, `operational_status` is driven to `OFFLINE` from whichever of `UP`, `DEGRADED` or `DOWN` it held, every dispenser of the placement — whether it stood `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED_OUT` — becomes `REMOVED` with its `lockout_reason` cleared to null, the model's `equipment_value_cents` is subtracted from the customer's `equipment_on_placement_value_cents` restoring that ledger exactly to its pre-placement value, the agreement's `placement_count` falls by one with a `version` increment, and the site's `service_posture` is re-derived.

Once a placement is off `INSTALLED`, it accepts no telemetry: a reading submitted against a placement whose `placement_status` is `PENDING_INSTALL`, `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, `RETRIEVED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_INSTALLED`. The same requirement governs fitting a dispenser and filing a service ticket.

### 3.4. Dispensers, the Telemetry Stream and the Health Axis

A **Dispenser** is one chemical feed plumbed into one machine. A `ServiceTechnician` fits it by supplying `placement_id`, `product_id` and `calibrated_dose_ml_per_rack` — the dose per rack the dispenser is initially set to deliver, greater than zero to two decimal places. The placement must be `INSTALLED`. The product must appear in the `covered_product_ids` of the agreement covering the placement, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ON_AGREEMENT`: the program only meters chemistry it has contracted to supply. At most one dispenser whose `dispenser_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED_OUT` may meter a given product on a given placement, so fitting a second feed for the same product is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPENSER_PRODUCT`; a `REMOVED` dispenser frees the slot and a replacement may then be fitted. A new dispenser starts `ACTIVE` with `dose_effective_at` equal to its `created_at`, and with `prior_dose_ml_per_rack`, `last_calibrated_at`, `last_drift_pct` and `lockout_reason` all null. Its lifecycle runs `ACTIVE ↔ LOCKED_OUT` with `REMOVED` as the terminal state, and `lockout_reason` — one of `EFFICACY_FAILURE`, `AGREEMENT_ENDED` or `INVESTIGATION_HOLD` — records why a lockout happened.

Only one of the three lockout reasons is recoverable by re-testing. A dispenser locked out for `EFFICACY_FAILURE` returns `LOCKED_OUT → ACTIVE`, with `lockout_reason` cleared to null and its `version` incremented, when a later calibration on that dispenser achieves an efficacy pass inside a visit that is subsequently verified — that is the entire recovery path, and it closes the `ACTIVE → LOCKED_OUT → ACTIVE` cycle. Lockouts stamped `AGREEMENT_ENDED` or `INVESTIGATION_HOLD` are never cleared by re-calibration; a dispenser standing in either of those two states accepts no new calibration record at all and rejects the attempt with `ERR_SANITIZER_LOCKOUT_ACTIVE`. A dispenser locked out for `EFFICACY_FAILURE`, by contrast, *does* accept new records, precisely because that is how it gets out.

**Telemetry ingest.** The platform operator streams samples into an `INSTALLED` placement. Each `TelemetryReading` carries `reading_at`, the instant the machine sampled the cycle; `cycle_count_total`, the cumulative rack odometer; `wash_temp_f` and `final_rinse_temp_f`, integers in the range 0 through 250; `rinse_pressure_psi`, an integer in the range 0 through 80; and an optional `source` of either `DEVICE` or `MANUAL_AUDIT`, defaulting to `DEVICE`. The distinction between the two sources is load-bearing everywhere in the rest of this document: **only `DEVICE` readings** feed rack counts, consumption expectations, sustainability averages and alert evaluation; a `MANUAL_AUDIT` reading is recorded for the health-inspection dossier and opens no alert and contributes no racks. Two temporal rules guard ingest: `reading_at` must be at or after the placement's `installed_at` and at or before the current logical clock, so both back-dated and future-dated samples are refused with `ERR_READING_BEFORE_INSTALL`. And a `DEVICE` reading's `cycle_count_total` must be at least the `cycle_count_total` of the preceding `DEVICE` reading on that placement, or at least the placement's `initial_cycle_count` when there is no preceding one; a lower value is refused with `ERR_NON_MONOTONIC_CYCLE_COUNT`. Equality is legal and simply produces a zero delta.

Each reading's `cycle_delta` is computed once, at insert time, and never revisited. The preceding reading is the `DEVICE` reading on the same placement with the greatest `reading_at` strictly earlier than this reading's `reading_at`; when two candidates share that instant, the one with the smaller `created_at` wins. The delta is this reading's `cycle_count_total` minus that predecessor's `cycle_count_total`, or minus the placement's `initial_cycle_count` when no predecessor exists. Ingest also refreshes the placement's `last_cycle_count` to the newest `DEVICE` reading's `cycle_count_total` and updates `cycles_since_calibration`, which is `last_cycle_count` minus the `last_cycle_count` that stood when the placement's most recent calibration was applied.

**Alert evaluation (cascade beta, links 1 and 2).** A `DEVICE` reading is then judged against its machine model's thresholds. Six alert types are evaluated **in this fixed order**, every breach opens its own `TelemetryAlert`, and the reading's `triggered_alert_types` array lists the types it opened in exactly this order:

1. `LOW_FINAL_RINSE_TEMP` fires when `final_rinse_temp_f` is below the model's `min_final_rinse_temp_f`. Its `severity` is `CRITICAL` when the measured temperature is at or below 5 degrees Fahrenheit under that minimum, and `WARNING` otherwise.
2. `LOW_WASH_TEMP` fires when `wash_temp_f` is below the model's `min_wash_temp_f`. Its `severity` is `CRITICAL` when the measured temperature is at or below 10 degrees Fahrenheit under that minimum, and `WARNING` otherwise.
3. `LOW_RINSE_PRESSURE` fires when `rinse_pressure_psi` is below the model's `min_rinse_pressure_psi`. Its `severity` is `CRITICAL` when the measured pressure is at or below 3 psi under that minimum, and `WARNING` otherwise.
4. `HIGH_RINSE_PRESSURE` fires when `rinse_pressure_psi` is above the model's `max_rinse_pressure_psi`, always with `severity` `WARNING`.
5. `HIGH_CYCLE_RATE` fires only when a preceding `DEVICE` reading exists, and then only when the observed rack rate exceeds the model's rated throughput by more than ten percent: divide this reading's `cycle_delta` by the exact number of hours between the predecessor's `reading_at` and this reading's `reading_at`, and compare against `racks_per_hour` multiplied by 1.10. The comparison is made on the exact rational value with no rounding beforehand. Its `severity` is `WARNING`. With no preceding `DEVICE` reading this alert can never fire.
6. `CALIBRATION_OVERDUE` fires when the placement's `cycles_since_calibration`, evaluated **after** this reading has been applied, has reached or passed the model's `max_cycles_between_calibrations`, with `severity` `WARNING`, and only when this placement has no `CALIBRATION_OVERDUE` alert already standing in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

De-duplication is general: an alert of the same `alert_type` already standing in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` on this placement suppresses the opening of a second one, and the existing alert's `measured_value` is **not** refreshed with the newer breach. Each alert that does open records `placement_id`, `triggering_reading_id` pointing at the reading whose values breached the threshold, `opened_at` from the logical clock, `status` `OPEN`, `acknowledged_by_user_id` null, and `measured_value` — the breaching number itself, copied from the triggering reading: a temperature, a pressure, or a racks-per-hour rate as appropriate to the type. Each newly opened alert raises the placement's `open_alert_count`.

**Health-axis propagation (cascade beta, link 3).** The most severe alert opened by a single reading drives the machine. A `CRITICAL` alert sends the placement to `DOWN` from either `UP` or `DEGRADED`. A `WARNING` alert sends it `UP → DEGRADED` and leaves a placement already `DOWN` untouched — a warning never improves a machine's health. Both propagations require the placement to be `INSTALLED`, and neither touches `placement_status`. Two other events reach the same axis: a calibration record whose sanitizer efficacy fails locks its dispenser out and drives the placement to `DOWN`, and a dispenser locked out with `lockout_reason` `INVESTIGATION_HOLD` drives a placement that was `UP` to `DEGRADED`.

**Auto-ticketing (cascade beta, link 4) and posture refresh (link 5).** A placement that is driven to `DOWN` — whether from `UP` or from `DEGRADED` — auto-raises a service ticket with `origin` `TELEMETRY_AUTO`, `reported_symptom` `MACHINE_DOWN`, `alert_id` pointing at the triggering alert, `reported_by_user_id` null, and a priority score stamped from the state as it stands after the alert has been applied. The auto-ticket is created **only** when the placement has no ticket already standing in `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`; when one exists the auto-ticket is silently skipped rather than raising an error. Finally the site's `open_ticket_count` and `service_posture` are re-derived, and the placement's own `version` increments by one for the ingest.

Alerts have a short lifecycle of their own. A `ServiceTechnician` may acknowledge an alert standing in `OPEN`, which moves it `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and records the acting technician's `X-User-ID` into `acknowledged_by_user_id`. Acknowledgement is bookkeeping only: it does not clear the alert, does not change the placement's health axis, does not stop the alert from feeding the dispatch score, and leaves `open_alert_count` unchanged, because that counter treats `OPEN` and `ACKNOWLEDGED` alike. An alert leaves the open set in exactly two ways, both of them terminal: it becomes `RESOLVED` when a visit on the placement's ticket is verified with a confirmed repair, or `SUPPRESSED` when a dispenser on its placement is locked out with `lockout_reason` `AGREEMENT_ENDED`. Both transitions are available from `OPEN` and from `ACKNOWLEDGED` alike, and both re-derive `open_alert_count`.

### 3.5. Service Demand: Ticket Priority, SLA Bands and Technician Selection

Work reaches the field as a `ServiceTicket`, and a ticket arrives by one of exactly two routes recorded in `origin`. A `CUSTOMER_REPORT` is filed against an `INSTALLED` placement by a `CustomerContact` for its own account or by the `AccountManager` who owns the account, supplying `placement_id` and a `reported_symptom` — one of `MACHINE_DOWN`, `LOW_TEMP`, `NO_CHEMICAL`, `LEAK` or `RACK_QUALITY`, and mandatory on this route. The other route is the `TELEMETRY_AUTO` ticket that cascade beta raises when a machine is driven to `DOWN`, described in §3.4. A customer-filed ticket records `reported_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` — the `Customer` identity — and leaves `alert_id` null; an auto-raised ticket does the reverse, populating `alert_id` and leaving `reported_by_user_id` null. Either way the ticket starts with `status` `QUEUED`, `assigned_technician_id` null, `sla_breached` false and `escalation_level` 0. A placement may not carry two tickets with the same `reported_symptom` while both stand in `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION`; a customer's duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_TICKET`, and the auto-ticket of cascade beta obeys the same rule but is skipped in silence instead of erroring.

Whether the job needs a sanitation-certified engineer is decided at creation and recorded in `requires_sanitation_cert`. It is true when the `reported_symptom` is `LOW_TEMP` or `NO_CHEMICAL`, or when the alert the ticket was raised from has `alert_type` `LOW_FINAL_RINSE_TEMP`; otherwise it is false. When true, it narrows the eligible technician pool to those holding `certification_level` `SANITATION_CERTIFIED`.

**Priority scoring.** Every ticket is scored once, at creation, and the score is stamped into `priority_score` and never recomputed for the life of the ticket. **Every input to the score is read from the state as it stands immediately before the ticket row is inserted.** Two consequences follow directly and are the most common source of off-by-one-band errors: the ticket being created never counts towards the site-posture component, and a customer report of `MACHINE_DOWN` is scored against the placement's health as it was *before* the request, because that request's own drive of the machine to `DOWN` is applied only after the score has been stamped.

The score is the sum of five components, capped at a ceiling of 1000:

- **Symptom base.** `MACHINE_DOWN` contributes 400, `LOW_TEMP` 300, `NO_CHEMICAL` 250, `LEAK` 200 and `RACK_QUALITY` 100.
- **Machine health.** When the placement's `operational_status` is `DOWN`, this contributes 300 if the current logical clock falls inside the site's operating window and 90 if it does not — a machine down during service is the program's most expensive failure, and a machine down overnight is not. When the placement is `DEGRADED`, it contributes 60. When the placement is `UP` or `OFFLINE`, it contributes nothing. Operating-window membership is decided by the rule in §2.12, including the wrap-past-midnight case.
- **Contract tier.** A site whose `priority_tier` is `PREMIUM` contributes 120; a `STANDARD` site contributes nothing.
- **Sanitation exposure.** When the placement has at least one alert standing in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` whose `alert_type` is `LOW_FINAL_RINSE_TEMP`, this contributes 200; otherwise nothing.
- **Site backlog.** When the site's `service_posture` is `ELEVATED`, this contributes 80; a `NORMAL` or `CRITICAL` posture contributes nothing through this component.

The stamped score then selects a response commitment, and each band boundary is inclusive on its own side: a score of 700 or more gets 120 minutes, a score of 400 or more but under 700 gets 240 minutes, and anything below 400 gets 480 minutes. `response_deadline_at` is the ticket's creation instant advanced by exactly that many minutes, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant to whole seconds. Because the score is stamped once, a ticket that bounces back to `QUEUED` after a rejected visit keeps both its original `priority_score` and its original `response_deadline_at`; neither is ever recalculated. What does change over time is breach state, and only through the clock sweep of §2.4, which sets `sla_breached` true and raises `escalation_level` by one the first time an advance observes a still-open ticket past its deadline.

**Dispatch and technician selection.** A `PlatformAdmin` dispatches a ticket, which requires `status` `QUEUED`; a ticket already `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE`, `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`. A ticket returned to the queue by a rejected visit is dispatchable again, and may be routed to a different engineer.

With no override supplied, the selector runs in three stages. **Eligibility** admits a technician only when all four of these hold: their `status` is `AVAILABLE`, so `OFF_DUTY` and `SUSPENDED` engineers never enter the candidate set at all; their `region_code` equals the `region_code` of the site whose placement the ticket concerns; either their `certification_level` is `SANITATION_CERTIFIED` or the ticket's `requires_sanitation_cert` is false; and their `open_ticket_count` is strictly fewer than three, so an engineer already carrying three open jobs is full. **Ranking** then orders the survivors by `open_ticket_count` ascending — least loaded first — and, among equally loaded candidates, by certification rank descending, where `SANITATION_CERTIFIED` ranks 3, `L2` ranks 2 and `L1` ranks 1. **Tie-breaking** among candidates still level on both keys goes to the earlier `created_at`, so the longest-tenured engineer wins; the entity identifier is never used as a tie-break.

When the candidate set comes out empty the request fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` and **nothing at all is mutated** — the ticket remains `QUEUED`, `assigned_technician_id` remains null and `escalation_level` is untouched. When the request does supply `override_technician_id`, the ranking and tie-break stages are skipped, but the named engineer must still satisfy the whole eligibility filter: a region mismatch is reported as `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH`, and any other eligibility failure — unavailable status, missing sanitation certification, or a full workload — is reported as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN`.

A successful dispatch moves the ticket `QUEUED → DISPATCHED`, writes the selected engineer into `assigned_technician_id`, raises that technician's `open_ticket_count` by one, and increments the ticket's `version`. From there the ticket follows its visit: it moves `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE` when the assigned engineer arrives, `ON_SITE → PENDING_VERIFICATION` when the visit is submitted, and then either `PENDING_VERIFICATION → CLOSED` on a verified visit or `PENDING_VERIFICATION → QUEUED` on a rejected one, which closes the `QUEUED → DISPATCHED → ON_SITE → PENDING_VERIFICATION → QUEUED` cycle. The middle two hops leave `open_ticket_count` alone, because `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` and `PENDING_VERIFICATION` all count as open workload. Separately, a ticket standing in `QUEUED` becomes `CANCELLED` when its placement is scheduled for retrieval or cancelled outright, taking any of its `SCHEDULED` visits to `CANCELLED` with it and re-deriving the site's `open_ticket_count` and `service_posture`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.6. The Service Visit: Scheduling, Calibration Evidence and Adjudication

A `ServiceVisit` is one booked appearance on site against one dispatched ticket. The visiting engineer books it, supplying `ticket_id`, `technician_id`, and the window `scheduled_start_at` (inclusive) and `scheduled_end_at` (exclusive, and strictly later than the start). `follow_up_to_visit_id` is optional and, when present, must reference a visit of the **same ticket** whose `visit_status` is `REJECTED`; anything else — a visit of another ticket, or a visit that was not rejected — is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_REFERENCE`. This self-reference is how a re-do is traced back to the failed visit it replaces; a first visit leaves it null. Because a technician may only act as itself, the submitted `technician_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`.

Three checks then guard the booking, in this order. The ticket must be in `DISPATCHED` and the submitted `technician_id` must equal the ticket's `assigned_technician_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — an engineer cannot book themselves onto somebody else's job or onto a job nobody has dispatched. Next, the engineer's `region_code` must equal the `region_code` of the site whose placement is being serviced, or the request is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH`; this is evaluated **before** any schedule check, so an out-of-region booking reports region rather than conflict. Last, the proposed window must not collide with any other visit of the same engineer standing in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`, once a 30-minute travel buffer is applied to **both** sides of the comparison: the booking is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` when the new window's start is earlier than the existing window's end plus 30 minutes *and* the existing window's start is earlier than the new window's end plus 30 minutes. A visit beginning exactly 30 minutes after another ends is therefore accepted; one beginning 29 minutes after is rejected. Visits in `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` no longer occupy the calendar at all.

A booked visit begins with `visit_status` `SCHEDULED`, `arrival_at` null, `calibration_record_count` 0, `repair_confirmed` false, and `rejection_reason` and `verified_by_user_id` both null. Arriving on site moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `arrival_at` from the logical clock, and carries the owning ticket `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE`; arrival requires the ticket to be `DISPATCHED` with its `assigned_technician_id` equal to this visit's `technician_id`.

**Recording concentration evidence.** While a visit is `IN_PROGRESS` — and only then, with any other `visit_status` refused as `ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` — the engineer records one `CalibrationRecord` per dispenser measured. Each record carries `dispenser_id`, `measured_ppm` (a positive integer titration result), `measured_dose_ml_per_rack` (the dose the dispenser actually delivered per rack, established by draw-down, greater than zero to two decimal places), and the dispenser version token `expected_dispenser_version` of §2.7. `adjusted_dose_ml_per_rack` is optional in the payload and is the dose the engineer set the dispenser to. `contact_seconds` is optional in the payload and is the measured sanitizer contact time. The named dispenser must be fitted to the very placement this visit's ticket concerns; a dispenser on any other placement is refused with `ERR_DISPENSER_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`. Every record stores `visit_id`, `recorded_by_user_id` from the engineer's `X-User-ID`, and `applied` false.

**Drift and the concentration verdict.** `drift_pct` is the signed percentage by which the measurement missed the label: subtract the product's `concentration_target_ppm` from `measured_ppm`, divide by that same target, multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. The `calibration_verdict` is then decided by evaluating four branches strictly in order, with the first match winning:

1. When the record's own `efficacy_result` is `FAIL`, the verdict is `FAIL`.
2. Otherwise, when the absolute value of `drift_pct` is greater than 25.00, the verdict is `FAIL`.
3. Otherwise, when the absolute value of `drift_pct` is greater than the product's `concentration_tolerance_pct`, the verdict is `ADJUSTED`.
4. Otherwise the verdict is `PASS`.

Both boundaries are inclusive on the *passing* side: an absolute drift exactly equal to the product's tolerance is `PASS`, not `ADJUSTED`, and an absolute drift of exactly 25.00 is `ADJUSTED`, not `FAIL`. When the verdict comes out `ADJUSTED`, `adjusted_dose_ml_per_rack` must be present and greater than zero, or the record is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTED_DOSE_REQUIRED`. When the verdict is `PASS` an `adjusted_dose_ml_per_rack` may still be supplied, and it is honoured rather than discarded.

**Sanitizer efficacy.** `efficacy_result` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` for any dispenser metering a `DETERGENT` or a `RINSE_AID`, and for such a dispenser `contact_seconds` must be omitted. For a dispenser metering a `SANITIZER`, `contact_seconds` is required, and the result is `PASS` only when all three of these hold: `measured_ppm` is at or above the product's `sanitizer_min_ppm` and at or below its `sanitizer_max_ppm`; `contact_seconds` is at least the product's `min_contact_seconds`; and the placement's latest `DEVICE` telemetry reading — the one with the greatest `reading_at`, with the earlier `created_at` winning a tie — has a `final_rinse_temp_f` at or above the machine model's `min_final_rinse_temp_f`. When the placement has no `DEVICE` reading at all, that third clause is treated as satisfied. If any of the three fails, `efficacy_result` is `FAIL`.

An efficacy `FAIL` acts immediately, without waiting for the visit to be submitted or audited: the dispenser transitions `ACTIVE → LOCKED_OUT` with `lockout_reason` `EFFICACY_FAILURE`, and the placement's health axis is driven to `DOWN`. Recording any calibration record increments the visit's `calibration_record_count` by one and the dispenser's `version` by one — which is why an engineer taking two consecutive measurements on the same dispenser must re-read the dispenser between them to obtain a fresh `expected_dispenser_version`. The record itself is an immutable row with no `version` field and no update endpoint.

**Submission.** Submitting the visit accepts one optional field, `repair_confirmed`, defaulting to false, which is the engineer's assertion that the machine is operable again. Submission requires `visit_status` `IN_PROGRESS`, requires `calibration_record_count` to be at least 1 — a visit with no evidence cannot be submitted — and requires that, for every dispenser measured during the visit, the latest record on that dispenser within this visit does **not** stand at `efficacy_result` `FAIL`. An unresolved failure is refused with `ERR_EFFICACY_FAILURE_UNRESOLVED`; the remedy is to re-titrate the same dispenser inside the same visit until a later record achieves `PASS`. Submission moves the visit `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED` and the owning ticket `ON_SITE → PENDING_VERIFICATION`. Only the visit's own engineer may submit it.

**Adjudication.** A `QualityAuditor` — never the engineer — decides the fate of a `SUBMITTED` visit whose ticket stands in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. The request carries `decision`, which must be either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` (any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and `rejection_reason`, which is required when the decision is `REJECTED`. Either way, `verified_by_user_id` is stamped with the auditor's `X-User-ID`. The two branches share a source state and have deliberately **disjoint** effects.

*The verified branch.* Every calibration record of the visit whose `calibration_verdict` is `PASS` or `ADJUSTED` has `applied` set to true; records whose verdict is `FAIL` are never applied. For each dispenser whose records were applied, the values are taken from the **last** record of that dispenser within this visit, and the dispenser is updated as follows: `prior_dose_ml_per_rack` is set to the dose that was live until this moment, then `calibrated_dose_ml_per_rack` becomes `adjusted_dose_ml_per_rack` when the verdict was `ADJUSTED` and is left unchanged when the verdict was `PASS`; `dose_effective_at` and `last_calibrated_at` are both stamped from the logical clock; `last_drift_pct` takes the record's `drift_pct`; the placement's `cycles_since_calibration` resets to 0; and the dispenser's `version` increments by one. A dispenser standing `LOCKED_OUT` with `lockout_reason` `EFFICACY_FAILURE` whose latest record in this visit achieved `efficacy_result` `PASS` returns to `ACTIVE` with `lockout_reason` cleared to null.

When the verified visit also carried `repair_confirmed` true, the machine's health is reassessed. Every alert of the placement standing in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` becomes `RESOLVED` and `open_alert_count` returns to 0, and only **then** is `operational_status` re-derived: a placement that was `DOWN` or `DEGRADED` becomes `UP` when no alert remains in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` and no dispenser of the placement remains `LOCKED_OUT`, and becomes — or stays — `DEGRADED` when at least one alert remains open or at least one dispenser remains locked out. A machine can therefore go `DOWN → UP` on a clean visit and `DOWN → DEGRADED` on a visit that fixed the reported fault but left a warning or a lockout behind, and the pairing of `DEGRADED → DOWN` with `DOWN → DEGRADED` closes the health axis's own cycle. Finally the ticket moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → CLOSED`, the assigned technician's `open_ticket_count` drops by one, and the site's `open_ticket_count` and `service_posture` are re-derived.

*The rejected branch* is the compensating one, and it leaves both the dose ledger and the alert set exactly as it found them. No calibration record of the visit is applied — every record keeps `applied` false — and every dispenser the visit touched stands with its `calibrated_dose_ml_per_rack`, `prior_dose_ml_per_rack`, `dose_effective_at`, `last_calibrated_at` and `last_drift_pct` precisely as they were before the visit began, the live dose being restored from `prior_dose_ml_per_rack` wherever anything had been staged. No alert changes `status`, and the placement's `operational_status` is untouched: a machine driven `DOWN` by an efficacy failure during the visit stays `DOWN`, and a locked-out dispenser stays locked out. The `rejection_reason` from the request is recorded. The ticket returns `PENDING_VERIFICATION → QUEUED` with `assigned_technician_id` cleared, the technician's `open_ticket_count` drops by one — restoring the conserved workload counter of §2.10 — and that technician's `quality_flag_count` rises by one. The ticket keeps its original `priority_score` and `response_deadline_at`, and may be dispatched again, possibly to a different engineer, who may then book a follow-up visit pointing at this rejected one through `follow_up_to_visit_id`. `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are the visit's terminal states.

### 3.7. Chemical Deliveries and the Usage-Variance Review

**Recording a drop.** An account manager records a `ChemicalDelivery` by naming the `site_id` that received it, the `product_id` dropped, the `delivered_at` instant that will later place the drop inside or outside a review window, the `unit_count` of containers (an integer of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and the site version token `expected_site_version` of §2.7. The delivered product must appear in the `covered_product_ids` of the covering agreement — for a delivery, that is the agreement of the site's customer standing in `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` — and a product outside that list is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ON_AGREEMENT`. As with a placement, a customer on `SUSPENDED` billing cannot receive stock, and that credit hold is reported last of all, as `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD`, only once the request is otherwise entirely valid.

Volume is trivial: `quantity_ml` is `unit_count` multiplied by the product's `unit_size_ml`. Price is where the rounding order matters. The discount rate is 0.12 when `unit_count` is 25 or more, otherwise 0.05 when it is 10 or more, otherwise zero — the tier boundaries are inclusive, so 24 units earn 5 percent and 25 units earn 12 percent. The discounted **unit** price is then computed by multiplying the product's `unit_price_cents` by one minus that rate and rounding half up to whole cents, and `amount_cents` is that rounded unit price multiplied by `unit_count`. Multiplying the list price by the count and by the rate first, and rounding the total once at the end, produces a different figure and is wrong. The new delivery is stamped `status` `DELIVERED` and the site's `version` rises by one.

**Voiding a drop.** A mis-keyed delivery moves `DELIVERED → VOIDED`, and only from `DELIVERED`: a second void attempt on an already-voided delivery is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A void is refused outright, with `ERR_DELIVERY_IN_CLOSED_PERIOD`, when any usage-variance review of the **same site and same product** standing in `PUBLISHED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED` has a window containing the delivery's `delivered_at` — a published variance figure may not be rewritten underneath itself. Window containment is half-open, so a delivery stamped exactly at a published review's `period_end` is still voidable, while one stamped exactly at its `period_start` is not. A review still in `DRAFT` blocks nothing. Voiding increments the site's `version` by one and drops the delivery out of every aggregate that filters on `DELIVERED`: the `delivered_ml` behind a variance review's actual consumption, and the `chemical_spend_cents` of the sustainability report. A review created after the void therefore reports a different `actual_consumption_ml` over the very same window, while a review published before the void keeps its frozen figures untouched.

**Drafting a variance review.** A `QualityAuditor` opens a `UsageVarianceReview` over one `site_id` and one `product_id` for the half-open window `[period_start, period_end)`, where `period_end` must be strictly later than `period_start`. `basis` selects which expected-consumption variant to evaluate and must be either `NAMEPLATE` or `AS_CALIBRATED`, with anything else refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `opening_inventory_ml` and `ending_inventory_ml` are the counted stock on hand at each end of the window, each zero or greater. The window must lie inside the covering agreement's `[start_date, term_end_date]` — the agreement of the site's customer standing in `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`, or, when none is live, the most recently terminated or expired one — and a window straying outside it is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_AGREEMENT_TERM`. For a given site-and-product pair, the window must also not overlap any review already standing in `PUBLISHED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. That overlap test carries **no** buffer and treats windows as half-open, so a window beginning exactly at another's `period_end` is accepted. Drafts never block anything: two drafts may cover the identical window, which is precisely how the `NAMEPLATE` and `AS_CALIBRATED` bases are compared side by side. Publication is what claims a window, so the same overlap test runs again at publish time.

All five figures are computed once, at creation, and frozen from then on.

`rack_count_in_period` is the total number of racks the site washed inside the window. For each placement of the site whose `placement_status` is `INSTALLED`, `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` or `RETRIEVED`, take every `DEVICE` telemetry reading of that placement whose `reading_at` falls in the half-open window and sum their `cycle_delta` values; the site total is the sum over those placements. Placements still in `PENDING_INSTALL` and placements in `CANCELLED` contribute nothing, and `MANUAL_AUDIT` readings are excluded from every rack count. Because `cycle_delta` was fixed at insert time against the preceding `DEVICE` reading, a reading whose `reading_at` lies outside the window contributes nothing at all even when some of the cycles it counts were accrued inside the window: attribution is by `reading_at`, never by interpolation across a boundary.

`expected_consumption_ml` is how much product those racks should have drunk. The dispensers considered are those metering the review's `product_id` on placements of the review's site whose `dispenser_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED_OUT`; a `REMOVED` dispenser is excluded entirely. Each such dispenser is credited with the racks of its placement, as counted above. Under the `NAMEPLATE` basis, every rack is priced at the product's `spec_dose_ml_per_rack`, ignoring the dispenser's calibration history altogether. Under the `AS_CALIBRATED` basis, each individual `DEVICE` reading inside the window has its `cycle_delta` priced at the dose that was actually in effect at that reading's `reading_at`, which is the `adjusted_dose_ml_per_rack` of the applied calibration record on that dispenser with the greatest verification instant at or before that moment, and, when no applied record precedes the reading, the dispenser's creation dose — the `calibrated_dose_ml_per_rack` as it stood at `dose_effective_at`. An applied mid-period calibration therefore splits the window into differently priced segments, and the two bases can diverge substantially over the same racks. Per-dispenser and per-reading products are kept exact; a single round half up to whole millilitres is applied to the **site-level sum** at the very end, deliberately the opposite order from the early-termination equipment charge of §3.2.

`actual_consumption_ml` is what the kitchen really drew down: `opening_inventory_ml` plus the millilitres delivered inside the window minus `ending_inventory_ml`. The delivered figure sums `quantity_ml` over deliveries of the same site and product whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `delivered_at` falls in the half-open window; `VOIDED` deliveries are excluded.

`variance_pct` is the signed percentage by which actual overran expected: subtract `expected_consumption_ml` from `actual_consumption_ml`, divide by `expected_consumption_ml`, multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. When `expected_consumption_ml` is zero, `variance_pct` is null rather than any numeric value. The `classification` is then decided by evaluating six branches strictly in order, with the first match winning, and all comparisons made against the **rounded** `variance_pct`:

1. `expected_consumption_ml` is 0 → `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.
2. `variance_pct` is greater than 25.00 **and** the delivered millilitres inside the window are greater than 0 → `THEFT_SUSPECT`. Product cannot be stolen from a window in which none arrived, so a huge variance with no deliveries falls through to overuse instead.
3. `variance_pct` is less than −15.00 **and** there is dilution evidence → `DILUTION_SUSPECT`. Dilution evidence means there exists an applied calibration record on a dispenser of this site metering this review's product whose owning visit reached `VERIFIED` at an instant inside the half-open window and whose `drift_pct` is at or below −10.00; a big underrun with no such record is not a dilution finding.
4. `variance_pct` is greater than 8.00 → `OVERUSE`.
5. `variance_pct` is less than −8.00 → `UNDERUSE`.
6. Otherwise → `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`.

Every threshold is strict, so a `variance_pct` of exactly 25.00 is `OVERUSE` and never `THEFT_SUSPECT`, exactly 8.00 is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` and never `OVERUSE`, exactly −8.00 is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, and exactly −15.00 with dilution evidence present is `UNDERUSE` rather than `DILUTION_SUSPECT`.

**Publishing.** Publication makes a review the account's official variance record and is what unlocks escalation. It requires `status` `DRAFT`, refusing a review already `PUBLISHED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED` with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT`. The publisher's `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, per §2.11, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The site's `service_posture` must not be `CRITICAL` — the open-ticket backlog must be worked down before variance findings are made official — else `ERR_SITE_POSTURE_CRITICAL`. And the window must carry usable telemetry: a review whose `classification` came out `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, equivalently one whose `rack_count_in_period` or `expected_consumption_ml` is 0, is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TELEMETRY`. §2.8 pins the order of these four. Publication moves the review `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_by_user_id` from the caller, and increments the review's `version`. The frozen figures are **not** recomputed at publish time even if telemetry or deliveries have changed in the interim.

**Closing without an investigation.** A `PUBLISHED` review whose finding needs no further work moves `PUBLISHED → CLOSED` directly, incrementing its `version`. Two things block this. A review whose `classification` is `THEFT_SUSPECT` or `DILUTION_SUSPECT` cannot be closed this way at all — it must be escalated to an investigation case first, and attempting to close it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. And a review in any status other than `PUBLISHED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_PUBLISHED`; in particular an `ESCALATED` review is never closed by this route, only by its case's adjudication. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.8. The Investigation Case: Evidence, the Return Cycle and Remediation

A published variance finding that points at theft, dilution or a faulty dispenser is escalated into an `InvestigationCase`. The auditor opening it supplies `variance_review_id` and `opened_reason`, which must be one of `SUSPECTED_THEFT`, `SUSPECTED_DILUTION` or `DISPENSER_FAULT`. The named review must stand in `PUBLISHED`, else `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_PUBLISHED`: a `DRAFT` review must be published before it can be escalated, and an `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED` review is beyond escalation. At most one case may ever exist per review, so a second attempt against the same review is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Opening a case is a two-record write: the case is created with `case_status` `EVIDENCE_GATHERING`, `opened_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, `evidence_item_count` 0, `return_count` 0, `remediation` `NONE`, `chargeback_cents` 0, and `reviewed_by_user_id` and `finding_note` null; and in the same request the linked review transitions `PUBLISHED → ESCALATED`, incrementing the review's `version`.

**Gathering and submitting evidence.** Submitting a case for adjudication carries `evidence_types`, a non-empty array whose members must each be one of `DELIVERY_LOG`, `TELEMETRY_EXPORT`, `CALIBRATION_HISTORY`, `INVENTORY_COUNT`, `CCTV_REVIEW` or `STAFF_INTERVIEW`; an empty array or an unrecognised member is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Submission requires `case_status` `EVIDENCE_GATHERING` and moves the case `EVIDENCE_GATHERING → PENDING_REVIEW`, adding the **length** of the submitted array to `evidence_item_count`. That counter is cumulative across every submission the case ever makes: two submissions of two items each leave it at 4, and it never decreases.

**Adjudication.** Deciding a case requires `case_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, else `ERR_CASE_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, and requires an auditor who is neither the case's `opened_by_user_id` nor the `published_by_user_id` of the review the case was opened against, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — converting a variance into a chargeback therefore takes two independent auditors beyond the one who drafted the review. The segregation test is evaluated after the state test, so a wrongly staged case reports its state error first. The request carries `decision`, which must be `SUBSTANTIATE`, `DISMISS` or `RETURN` (anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), an optional `remediation`, and an optional `finding_note` that is recorded on the case whichever decision is taken.

`RETURN` is the non-terminal decision: it sends the case back `PENDING_REVIEW → EVIDENCE_GATHERING` and increments `return_count` by one, while the linked review stays `ESCALATED`. This closes the `EVIDENCE_GATHERING → PENDING_REVIEW → EVIDENCE_GATHERING` cycle, and a case may loop through it any number of times, accumulating evidence items and returns as it goes.

`SUBSTANTIATE` moves the case `PENDING_REVIEW → SUBSTANTIATED` and additionally requires `evidence_item_count` to be at least 2 — a single evidence item cannot substantiate a finding, and the attempt is refused as an undeclared transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` from the caller and takes `remediation` from the request, which must be one of `TRAINING`, `DISPENSER_REPLACEMENT` or `CHARGEBACK`. When and only when the ordered remediation is `CHARGEBACK`, `chargeback_cents` is computed from the review's **frozen** figures and never recomputed from live telemetry: take `actual_consumption_ml` minus `expected_consumption_ml`, keep it only if positive and otherwise treat the excess as zero, divide that excess by the product's `unit_size_ml` and round **up** to the next whole container, and multiply the resulting container count by the product's `unit_price_cents` — the list price, with no volume discount, because excess consumption is not a purchase. Any other remediation leaves `chargeback_cents` at 0.

`DISMISS` moves the case `PENDING_REVIEW → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, stamps `reviewed_by_user_id`, and forces `remediation` to `NONE` and `chargeback_cents` to 0 regardless of what the request asked for. `SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED` are both terminal.

**The escalation cascade.** Either terminal decision fires cascade gamma. Link 1 was the escalation itself when the case was opened. Link 2 is that the linked review transitions `ESCALATED → CLOSED`, incrementing its `version`; this is the only route by which an escalated review ever closes. Link 3 is conditional on the remediation actually ordered: only when `remediation` is `DISPENSER_REPLACEMENT` does every dispenser of the review's site metering the review's `product_id` whose `dispenser_status` is `ACTIVE` become `LOCKED_OUT` with `lockout_reason` `INVESTIGATION_HOLD`, processed in dispenser `created_at` ascending order. Link 4 follows each such lockout: that dispenser's placement moves `UP → DEGRADED` on the health axis, leaving a placement already `DEGRADED` or `DOWN` where it stands. A `TRAINING` or `CHARGEBACK` remediation, and every dismissal, fire links 2 alone: the review closes and every dispenser keeps `dispenser_status` `ACTIVE`, with no health-axis movement anywhere. An `INVESTIGATION_HOLD` lockout is one of the two irrecoverable ones of §3.4 — no calibration record clears it, and the dispenser must be removed and refitted.

### 3.9. Two Computed Reads: The Sustainability Report and the Inspection Dossier

Both of these are pure projections over a half-open window `[period_start, period_end)`. They persist nothing, mutate no state and increment no `version`; each request recomputes from whatever the stores hold at that instant. Both echo the `site_id` and the window bounds they were asked for alongside their figures.

**The sustainability report** answers what the programme saved against the kitchen's pre-programme baselines, and what it cost. Besides the window it takes `basis`, which must be either `RATED` or `TELEMETRY_MEASURED`, with anything else refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The placements considered are those of the site whose `placement_status` is `INSTALLED`, `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` or `RETRIEVED`, and each is credited with its racks over the window exactly as §3.7 counts them; `rack_count` reports the site total.

Under the `RATED` basis, each placement's effective water per rack is simply its machine model's `rated_water_l_per_rack`, so savings depend only on rack counts. Under `TELEMETRY_MEASURED`, that nameplate figure is inflated by an observed pressure factor: average the `rinse_pressure_psi` of the placement's `DEVICE` readings inside the window, divide by the model's `min_rinse_pressure_psi`, and round half up to four decimal places; when the placement has no `DEVICE` reading inside the window at all, the factor is exactly 1.0000. The effective water per rack is the nameplate litres multiplied by that factor. Because a well-pressurised machine rinses above its minimum, the same racks and the same tariffs yield a smaller water saving, and hence a smaller credit, under `TELEMETRY_MEASURED` than under `RATED`.

`water_saved_l` sums, over those placements, the racks multiplied by the site's `baseline_water_l_per_rack` minus the placement's effective water per rack. `energy_saved_kwh` sums the racks multiplied by the site's `baseline_kwh_per_rack` minus the model's `rated_kwh_per_rack`; energy takes no pressure factor under either basis. Both totals may come out **negative** when the installed machines are thirstier or hungrier than the baseline, and neither is floored at zero. Litres and kilowatt-hours are kept as exact decimals; each money output is rounded half up to whole cents exactly once. `water_savings_cents` is the litres divided by 1000, multiplied by the site's `water_cost_cents_per_1000l`, rounded half up to whole cents. `energy_savings_cents` is the kilowatt-hours multiplied by the site's `energy_cost_cents_per_kwh`, rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_savings_cents` is the sum of those two rounded figures.

The cost side is evaluated in a pinned order of six steps. The fee tier looks circular and is not:

1. `chemical_spend_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over deliveries of this site whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `delivered_at` falls inside the window.
2. `gross_program_cost_cents` is `chemical_spend_cents` plus a base service fee of 25000 cents.
3. `service_fee_rate` is 0.08 when `gross_program_cost_cents` is below 200000, otherwise 0.06 when it is below 500000, otherwise 0.04. The tier is chosen from that **pre-credit** figure.
4. `service_fee_cents` is `chemical_spend_cents` multiplied by `service_fee_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents. Note that the rate applies to the chemical spend alone, not to the gross programme cost that selected it.
5. `sustainability_credit_cents` is 0 whenever `gross_savings_cents` is zero or negative; otherwise it is `gross_savings_cents` multiplied by 0.05 and rounded half up to whole cents, then capped at 15000 cents by taking whichever of the two is smaller.
6. `net_program_cost_cents` is `chemical_spend_cents` plus the 25000-cent base fee plus `service_fee_cents` minus `sustainability_credit_cents`.

The credit must not feed back into step 2 or step 3: the tier is fixed before the credit is known, and `net_program_cost_cents` is the last value computed. Finally, `goal_attainment_pct` is `water_saved_l` divided by the **customer's** `sustainability_goal_water_l`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; a negative water saving yields a negative attainment.

**The inspection dossier** is the evidence pack a health inspector would be handed, and it takes only the window. Its cardinal rule is that **only evidence from `VERIFIED` visits counts**: calibration records attached to visits standing in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely, however good their readings were. The records in scope are those whose dispenser's placement belongs to this site, whose visit is `VERIFIED`, and whose visit's verification instant falls inside the half-open window — note that the window is tested against the moment of adjudication, not against when the measurement was taken.

`calibration_records_total` counts all records in scope. `sanitizer_tests_total` counts those whose `efficacy_result` is anything other than `NOT_APPLICABLE`, so detergent and rinse-aid calibrations swell the record total without counting as sanitizer tests. `sanitizer_tests_passed` counts those whose `efficacy_result` is `PASS`. `latest_efficacy_result` is the `efficacy_result` of the record with the greatest visit verification instant among records whose result is not `NOT_APPLICABLE`, resolving a tie by record `created_at` ascending with the last one winning; when no sanitizer test is in scope at all, it reports `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `final_rinse_temp_readings_total` counts the `DEVICE` telemetry readings of this site's placements inside the window, and `final_rinse_temp_exceptions` counts those among them whose `final_rinse_temp_f` is strictly below the placement's machine model's `min_final_rinse_temp_f`. `open_critical_alert_count` counts the alerts of this site's placements whose `status` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` and whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`, taken **as of now** rather than filtered by the window — an unresolved critical alert condemns the dossier whenever it was raised.

`dossier_status` is `READY` only when all four of these hold together: `sanitizer_tests_total` is at least 1, `latest_efficacy_result` is `PASS`, `final_rinse_temp_exceptions` is 0, and `open_critical_alert_count` is 0. In every other case it is `DEFICIENT`. A site with no sanitizer test at all in the window is therefore `DEFICIENT`, not vacuously ready.
