# Underground Gas Storage Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An underground natural gas storage field is simultaneously a reservoir engineering problem, a mechanical integrity problem and a commercial balancing problem, and the operator who runs one is answerable for all three at once. Gas that a shipper believes it has scheduled for tomorrow can become undeliverable tonight because a wellbore failed a pressure test, because a compressor burned working gas as fuel, or because a leak forced an evacuation order. The purpose of this API is to make that coupling explicit and machine-enforced: every commercial promise the operator makes is written against a reservoir posture that the server derives, and never against a number a client asserted.

This document specifies the complete HTTP contract for that system. It covers reservoir registration with a deliverability curve, wellbore lifecycle and mechanical integrity testing with remediation programs, compressor station horsepower allocation and fuel accounting, firm and interruptible storage service contracts, the daily nomination and allocation cycle with bounded revision rounds, metered gas movement tickets with commodity and fuel charges, imbalance balancing with cash-out settlement and shipper credit suspension, emergency response with evacuation coordination, and regulatory reporting on inventory verification and integrity program status.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **derived state is authoritative**: reservoir posture, deliverability rates, contract scheduled volumes, shipper credit standing and inventory balances are recomputed by the server after every mutation that could disturb them, and clients read them rather than set them. Second, **withdrawal of a promise is a first-class operation**: when the reservoir's capability falls, the system curtails already-confirmed schedules through a deterministic, fully specified sweep, so that two servers given the same history arrive at the same set of curtailed nominations in the same order.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every path in this specification is absolute — the API is mounted at the server root, so a storage field is addressed at `/storage-fields/{id}` with no additional prefix. This section defines the rules that hold for every endpoint regardless of which business process it serves.

### 2.1. Operating Personnel and Access Control

Each request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the five roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. The three failure modes are checked in that order — both headers present, then both non-empty, then the role recognised — and all three answer the same identifier, so a client cannot distinguish them from the status code. Header values are taken verbatim and are never trimmed, so only the empty string counts as empty: an `X-User-ID` of a single space is a well-formed opaque identifier, while a role name carrying stray whitespace or differing in case (`fieldoperator`, `Shipper `) fails the role comparison and is rejected as an unrecognised role rather than as an authorization failure. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognizes exactly five roles:

- `FieldOperator` — runs the field day to day. Registers reservoirs, wellbores and compressor units, turns the season, books and closes out compressor runs, and meters, confirms and reverses physical gas movements.
- `IntegrityEngineer` — owns wellbore integrity. Schedules mechanical integrity tests, records their outcomes, and closes remediation programs. Cannot move gas and cannot schedule commerce.
- `SchedulingCoordinator` — runs the commercial side. Onboards shippers, drafts and activates storage service contracts, runs the nomination allocation waterfall, and settles or reverses imbalance cash-outs.
- `ComplianceOfficer` — prepares and submits regulatory reports, records agency dispositions, and co-ordinates emergency response. Cannot meter gas, cannot schedule nominations, and cannot record integrity results.
- `Shipper` — the storage customer, who files and submits nominations and reads its own commercial records.

Four of these roles are operator staff and their identity is opaque to the data model. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldOperator`, `IntegrityEngineer`, `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ComplianceOfficer`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a mechanical integrity test, as `completed_by_user_id` on a remediation program, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` on a regulatory report — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

The fifth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Shipper`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Shipper` record, and ownership is enforced by comparison. A shipper may only touch a storage service contract whose `shipper_id` equals `X-User-ID`, whether that contract is named directly in the path, reached through the nomination named in the path, or reached through the nomination behind the gas movement ticket named in the path. A violation is reported as `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403). Single-resource reads are gated identically to writes. The list endpoints are the deliberate exception: when a `Shipper` lists storage contracts, nominations or gas movement tickets, the endpoint never raises `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_OWNED` and instead returns only the rows whose owning contract's `shipper_id` equals `X-User-ID`, with `total_count` counting only those rows. The acting `X-User-ID` is never itself looked up in the shipper collection: authentication does not require it to name an existing `Shipper` record, and `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_FOUND` is never raised on account of the identity headers. A `Shipper` header naming no persisted shipper is therefore authenticated normally and simply owns nothing — every ownership-gated read or write it attempts answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_OWNED`, and the narrowed list channels answer `200 OK` with an empty `data` array and a `total_count` of 0. Ownership is compared as an exact string equality against the contract's `shipper_id`, and a request whose contract cannot be reached at all — a nomination whose contract is missing, say — is treated as not owned rather than as not found. The ownership comparison reaches only records that hang off a storage contract, which is the complete list §2.11 gives at rung 6. In particular the single-shipper read channel is not ownership-gated: a `Shipper` authorized for `GET /shippers/{id}` may read any shipper record, its own or another customer's, and the channel answers `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown identifier rather than `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_OWNED`. The read channels for fields, wellbores, integrity tests, remediation programs, compressor units, compressor runs, emergency events and regulatory reports are likewise ungated and unnarrowed for every role the §4 table authorizes on them.

Two independent segregation-of-duties constraints operate on top of role authorization; both are described where the workflows that carry them are specified, and both surface as `ERR_SOD_REMEDIATION_SELF_VERIFICATION` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.4 and never from wall-clock time, so every response carries the same instant until a client advances the clock, and a response issued at the untouched epoch reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent from the `meta` of a single-resource response.
- Every field an entity's representation declares is always present as a key. A field that is unset, not yet stamped, not yet computed or not applicable is rendered as JSON `null` and is never omitted, so the key set of a given entity type is identical in every response that carries it. The single exception is the `ImbalanceLedgerEntry`, whose `updated_at` and `version` keys are absent altogether rather than null, as §2.4 requires.
- A request to a path this specification does not define, and a method this specification does not define on a path it does define, are both answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than a 404 or a 405, and are answered before authentication runs, so neither requires the identity headers. A trailing slash is not significant: `/wells` and `/wells/` address the same channel.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection — the top-level list channels as well as the sub-resource list channels such as the runs booked on a compressor unit, the balancing ledger of a contract, and the regulatory reports filed for a field — accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each must be supplied as a plain base-ten integer literal carrying no surrounding whitespace and no fractional part; `1.5`, `" 5"` and `abc` are all rejected with the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as an out-of-range value, and the two are checked for parseability before they are checked against their bounds. A parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had not been supplied at all and takes its default. `offset` has no upper bound — its only constraint is that it not be negative — so an offset at or beyond `total_count` is accepted and answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` array. Query parameters this specification does not define for a channel are ignored rather than rejected.

All list responses are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and `limit` and `offset` are applied only after that ordering has been established. The `id` value is never used as a sort key. Because every `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.4, rows created without an intervening clock advance genuinely share one `created_at` value, and rows that tie on `created_at` are ordered by the server's monotonic record-insertion sequence — the order in which the records came into existence, with cascade-created records following the record whose request created them. The ordering is therefore total and deterministic for any history, and paging through a collection never skips or repeats a row. The `total_count` reported in `meta` counts every row matching the endpoint's filters — including the ownership narrowing applied to a `Shipper` — as measured *before* `limit` and `offset` are applied, so a client can page to the end of a filtered set. Each list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used (the defaults when the client supplied none) and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned in `data` is strictly less than `total_count`, and false otherwise.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every business record in the system carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-05T13:00:00.000Z`). Clients never supply either value. Every instant-valued field in every representation is rendered in exactly that shape — four-digit year, zero-padded month and day, `T`, zero-padded hour, minute and second, a decimal point, exactly three fractional digits and a literal `Z` — regardless of how precise the value the client supplied was; there is no variant rendering that drops the fractional part or uses a numeric offset. Fields that carry a calendar date rather than an instant — `gas_day`, `term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `cash_out_through_date`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` — are rendered as bare `YYYY-MM-DD` strings and never as instants. The `id` is a lower-case hyphenated UUID; the monotonic insertion sequence that §2.3 uses to break `created_at` ties is internal server bookkeeping and appears in no representation.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every change to the record, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that record. At creation `updated_at` is stamped with the same instant as `created_at`, and because both are read from the logical clock a mutation performed without an intervening clock advance leaves `updated_at` unchanged while still moving `version` — `version` is therefore the only reliable evidence that a record was touched. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when the server reconciles a contract's scheduled volumes or a field's deliverability rates, that record's `updated_at` and `version` move even though no client wrote to it directly. The single exception is the balancing ledger. An `ImbalanceLedgerEntry` is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and those keys must be absent from its representation.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real system time. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and jumps the clock to it, returning the new value as `current_utc` in a `data` object that carries that one key and nothing else; setting the clock mutates no entity state and runs no curtailment sweep. The clock moves in whichever direction the supplied instant lies, so it may be wound backwards as freely as forwards. It retains microsecond resolution internally, while every timestamp the server renders — `current_utc` included — is truncated rather than rounded to three fractional digits, so sub-millisecond precision handed to the clock is never observable in a response. Every deadline in this specification — nomination cutoffs, mechanical integrity test windows, compressor run windows, remediation due dates, and every comparison of a gas day against "today" — is evaluated against this logical clock. Wherever a rule compares a gas day to the present, "today" means the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock.

### 2.5. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Gas volumes, money and pressures each have a fixed precision, and every rounding in this document is **round half up** unless stated otherwise. Where a rounding step is specified as part of a calculation, that step is mandatory and its position in the evaluation order is part of the contract: rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different published number and is incorrect.

- Reservoir and contract volumes that track physical gas — `working_gas_inventory_dth`, `retained_fuel_pool_dth`, `inventory_balance_dth`, `imbalance_dth`, `fuel_retention_dth`, `net_volume_dth`, `book_inventory_dth`, `variance_dth` and the ledger's `volume_dth` — are carried to 3 decimal places.
- Fuel burn quantities — `fuel_consumed_dth` on a run, `lifetime_fuel_dth` on a unit and `fuel_gas_used_dth` on a field — are carried to 2 decimal places.
- All money — `commodity_charge_usd`, `volume_discount_usd`, `fuel_charge_usd`, `total_charge_usd`, `cash_out_amount_usd`, `outstanding_penalty_usd`, `penalty_threshold_usd` and the ledger's `amount_usd` — is carried to 2 decimal places and is represented in JSON as a number, never as a string. Configured rate fields, namely `commodity_rate_usd_per_dth` and `index_price_usd_per_dth`, carry 4 decimal places as supplied.
- Nominated, confirmed and curtailed volumes (`nominated_volume_dth`, `confirmed_volume_dth`, `curtailed_volume_dth`) and metered volume (`actual_volume_dth`) are whole dekatherms — integers. The allocation waterfall never rounds a volume up; where it narrows a figure it truncates toward zero.
- Deliverability rates (`max_withdrawal_rate_dth_per_day`, `max_injection_rate_dth_per_day`) and pressures (`current_pressure_psig`, and every `*_psig` input) are integers, rounded half up where a computation produces a fraction.
- Percentages carry the precision named at the point of use: `inventory_pct` and `derate_factor` to 2 decimal places, `utilization_ratio` to 6 decimal places, `variance_pct` to 4 decimal places, and `duration_hours` to 4 decimal places.
- Every field named above is quantized to its stated precision both when the server writes it and again when it is serialized, so a client always reads back a value already rounded to that precision and never has to round the response itself. The JSON number is nonetheless rendered in its shortest form rather than padded to a fixed width, so a two-decimal money field with no cents may appear as `250.0` and a value the server holds as a whole number may appear as `250`; comparisons must therefore be numeric rather than textual. A field whose value is null is serialized as null rather than as a zero at that scale.
- The remaining quantities are envelope declarations that the server only stores and echoes, and they are returned at exactly the value supplied with no re-quantization to any fixed number of decimals: `total_capacity_dth`, `cushion_gas_dth` and `opening_working_gas_dth` on a field, the derived `max_working_gas_dth` computed from the first two, the contract rights `max_storage_quantity_dth`, `max_daily_injection_dth` and `max_daily_withdrawal_dth`, the report's `measured_inventory_dth`, and the `inventory_pct` of each declared `deliverability_curve` point. Within a curve point, `withdrawal_dth_per_day` and `injection_dth_per_day` are whole dekatherms per day. All of these are JSON numbers, never strings, exactly as money is.

### 2.6. Field Posture: the Master Gate over Reservoir Operations

Every storage field carries a derived gate called `field_posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Clients never set it. The server recomputes it immediately after any mutation that touches the field's wellbores, its emergency events, its working gas inventory or its pressure, and it is recomputed immediately after the deliverability recomputation described in §2.7 so that both numbers are consistent within one request.

The derivation is evaluated as an ordered chain of clauses, and the **first** clause that holds decides the answer:

1. If the field's `wells_in_service_count` is zero, the posture is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if any emergency event on this field has a `status` of `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if any wellbore on this field has an `integrity_state` of `MIT_FAILED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Every wellbore registered against the field counts for this clause regardless of its `well_type` and regardless of its `operational_state`, so an observation wellbore, a shut-in wellbore or a plugged wellbore left standing at `MIT_FAILED` holds the field at `RESTRICTED` until its integrity flag is cleared.
4. Otherwise, if the field's `current_pressure_psig` is strictly greater than the posture pressure ceiling — ninety-five percent (95 percent) of `max_pressure_psig`, that product rounded half up to a whole psig before the comparison is made — the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Because a field is registered before any wellbore is drilled into it, a newly created field always begins `FROZEN` by clause 1.

Posture is a gate, not merely a label. While a field is `FROZEN`, no nomination on it may be confirmed, no gas movement ticket on it may be confirmed, no contract written against it may be activated, and no compressor run may be booked on it; each of those attempts is rejected with `ERR_FIELD_FROZEN` (HTTP 422). While a field is `RESTRICTED`, firm service continues unaffected, but a nomination whose contract carries `service_class` of `INTERRUPTIBLE` may not be confirmed and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INTERRUPTIBLE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422). Note that the two conditions are reported separately: a frozen field always answers `ERR_FIELD_FROZEN`, and `ERR_INTERRUPTIBLE_RESTRICTED` is reserved for the restricted case.

### 2.7. Deliverability: the Curve, the Wellbores and the Two Evaluation Methods

A reservoir does not deliver gas at a constant rate. The higher the inventory, the higher the pressure, and the more gas can be withdrawn per day — while injection capability moves the other way. Each field therefore carries a `deliverability_curve`: an array of between 2 and 10 points, each an object with `inventory_pct`, `withdrawal_dth_per_day` and `injection_dth_per_day`. The array must be strictly ascending in `inventory_pct`, its first point must sit at an `inventory_pct` of 0, and its last point must sit at an `inventory_pct` of 100; a curve that violates any of those conditions is rejected as a malformed request.

Whenever the field is created, and after every mutation that could disturb `working_gas_inventory_dth`, the set of in-service wellbores or the posture inputs of §2.6 — a ticket confirmation or reversal, a compressor run completion, a wellbore registration, a wellbore service change, a mechanical integrity test result of any of the three outcomes, a remediation completion, or any advance of an emergency event on the field — the server recomputes the whole derived block in this order:

1. `max_working_gas_dth` is `total_capacity_dth` less `cushion_gas_dth`. This is the ceiling on working gas; the cushion gas below it is base gas that keeps the reservoir pressurised and is never delivered to a shipper.
2. `inventory_pct` is the working gas inventory divided by `max_working_gas_dth`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
3. `current_pressure_psig` is `base_pressure_psig` plus the *unrounded* fraction of working gas to `max_working_gas_dth` multiplied by `pressure_span_psig`, the sum rounded half up to a whole psig.
4. `deliverability_tier` is a band label read from `inventory_pct`: `TIER_1` below 25 percent, `TIER_2` from 25 up to but not including 50 percent, `TIER_3` from 50 up to but not including 75 percent, and `TIER_4` at 75 percent or above.
5. `wells_in_service_count` is the number of wellbores on this field whose `well_type` is `INJECTION_WITHDRAWAL` and whose `operational_state` is `IN_SERVICE`. Observation wellbores and wellbores that are shut in or plugged are not counted. The mechanical capacity of the field is the sum of `rated_rate_dth_per_day` over exactly that same set of wellbores.
6. The curve is evaluated at the current `inventory_pct` — the two-decimal figure step 2 has just published, not the unrounded fraction step 3 uses — under the field's `deliverability_method`, producing one withdrawal rate and one injection rate.
7. `max_withdrawal_rate_dth_per_day` is the lesser of the curve's withdrawal rate and the summed mechanical capacity; `max_injection_rate_dth_per_day` is the lesser of the curve's injection rate and that same summed mechanical capacity. With no in-service injection/withdrawal wellbore at all, the mechanical capacity is zero, so both rates are exactly 0 — and clause 1 of §2.6 has already forced the posture to `FROZEN`. The recomputation counts as a mutation of the field only when it actually moves a published number: the field's `updated_at` and `version` advance if and only if at least one of the seven values above or the `field_posture` of §2.6 now differs from the value the field already held, so a recomputation that lands on identical figures — registering an observation wellbore, registering a wellbore that is already shut in, plugging a wellbore that was already out of service, recording a passing integrity test — leaves the field's `version` untouched, exactly as the empty-cascade rule of §2.12 requires. The one exception is a request that also writes one of the field's own stored quantities: a gas movement ticket confirmation or reversal and a compressor run completion move `working_gas_inventory_dth`, `retained_fuel_pool_dth` or `fuel_gas_used_dth` in their own right, and each of those advances the field's `version` unconditionally even when all eight derived values happen to come back unchanged.

`deliverability_method` selects between two published evaluation methods and defaults to `CURVE_INTERPOLATED` when it is not supplied at registration.

Under `CURVE_INTERPOLATED`, the server brackets the current `inventory_pct` between two curve points: the low point is the last point whose `inventory_pct` is less than or equal to the field's `inventory_pct`, and the high point is the first point whose `inventory_pct` is greater than or equal to it. The interpolation fraction is the field's `inventory_pct` minus the low point's `inventory_pct`, divided by the high point's `inventory_pct` minus the low point's `inventory_pct`. Each rate is then the low point's rate plus the fraction multiplied by the difference between the high point's rate and the low point's rate, rounded half up to a whole dekatherm per day. When the low and high points are the same point — because the field's inventory sits exactly on a curve point — the rate is that point's rate with no interpolation.

Under `TIER_FLOOR`, there is no interpolation at all. The curve behaves as a step function with floor semantics: each rate is taken exactly from the highest curve point whose `inventory_pct` is less than or equal to the field's `inventory_pct`. For the same field state this method is strictly more conservative on withdrawal and strictly more generous on injection than interpolation, so the two methods legitimately publish different integers for byte-identical input.

Neither method ever extrapolates beyond the curve's declared span. An `inventory_pct` that falls below the first point's 0 — reachable only because the compressor fuel burn of §3.3 is not floored at zero — takes the first point's two rates unchanged under both methods, and an `inventory_pct` above the last point's 100 would likewise take the last point's rates unchanged; the evaluation clamps to the nearer endpoint rather than projecting the adjacent segment outward.

### 2.8. Curtailment Sweeps: Withdrawing Confirmed Schedules

When the reservoir's capability falls, schedules the operator has already confirmed must be withdrawn. The system does this through two named sweeps whose eligibility filter, ordering, tie-break and empty-result behaviour are fixed here so that they never vary by caller. In both sweeps, a nomination that has already been actualised is untouchable: any nomination against which a gas movement ticket exists in `CONFIRMED` status is excluded, and so is any nomination whose `gas_day` is earlier than today's UTC calendar date under the logical clock.

**Phase A — posture and credit sweeps.** Phase A fires when a field's `field_posture` moves to `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — because a wellbore was shut in, because a mechanical integrity test was recorded as a failure, or because an emergency event advanced to `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED` — and it fires in a second form when a shipper's credit is suspended by a cash-out. Where the trigger is a wellbore service change, "moves" is strict and the two sweeps are mutually exclusive within one request: phase A fires only when the recomputed posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` **and** differs from the posture the field held before the change, and when it does fire phase B does not also run, so a shut-in that both flips the posture and drops the deliverability rates runs phase A alone. Where the trigger is an integrity test recorded as a failure, or an emergency event reaching `RESPONDING`, phase A fires on the resulting posture whether or not that posture differs from the one already in force, because the new failure widens the set of nominations the posture condemns. The candidate set is every nomination in `CONFIRMED` status, on a contract written against the field being re-postured (in the credit-suspension form, on any contract of the suspended shipper), whose `gas_day` is today or later and which has no confirmed movement ticket. Where the posture became `RESTRICTED`, only nominations on `INTERRUPTIBLE` contracts are candidates; where it became `FROZEN`, both `FIRM` and `INTERRUPTIBLE` nominations are candidates. Candidates are processed in ascending order of `gas_day`, within a gas day in ascending order of `created_at`, and — because the logical clock of §2.4 lets several rows genuinely share one `created_at` — candidates that tie on `created_at` are ordered by the server's monotonic record-insertion sequence, the same third key §2.3 uses to make a list response total and deterministic. Each candidate is curtailed in full: `confirmed_volume_dth` becomes 0, `curtailed_volume_dth` becomes the whole `nominated_volume_dth`, `status` becomes `CURTAILED`, and one `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` ledger entry is appended carrying the nomination's `gas_day`, a `volume_dth` equal to the negative of the confirmed volume the nomination held immediately before the sweep, and an `amount_usd` of 0.00. The owning contract's `scheduled_injection_dth` and `scheduled_withdrawal_dth` are then recomputed and its `version` increments by one. If the candidate set is empty, nothing at all happens: no ledger entries, no version increments, and no error.

**Phase A in its seasonal form.** Turning a field's `season` runs the same mechanics against a different filter: every `CONFIRMED` nomination on an `INTERRUPTIBLE` contract of that field whose `direction` no longer matches the new `season`, whose `gas_day` is today or later, and which has no confirmed movement ticket, is curtailed in full with the same `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` entry and the same contract reconciliation. Firm nominations are never touched by a season turn.

**Phase B — deliverability sweeps.** Phase B fires when `max_withdrawal_rate_dth_per_day` or `max_injection_rate_dth_per_day` *falls* — a wellbore shut in, a failed integrity test, a compressor run's fuel burn, or a confirmed ticket that moved inventory into a lower curve band — while future gas days already carry confirmed volume. Only a direction whose own rate fell is swept: when `max_injection_rate_dth_per_day` fell while `max_withdrawal_rate_dth_per_day` did not, no withdrawal gas day is examined at all however oversubscribed it may be, and when both fell the injection direction is swept to completion before the withdrawal direction. Within a direction the affected gas days are taken in ascending `gas_day` order. For each combination of gas day (today or later) and direction where the sum of `confirmed_volume_dth` over that field's `CONFIRMED` nominations for that gas day and direction — firm and interruptible alike, and including any already actualised by a confirmed ticket — now exceeds the new field rate for that direction, the candidate set is the nominations on that gas day and direction that are `CONFIRMED`, whose contract's `service_class` is `INTERRUPTIBLE`, and which have no confirmed movement ticket. Candidates are ordered by `created_at` descending — the most recently created is curtailed first — and where two candidates cannot be separated that way, by their contract's `contract_code` ascending. The sweep curtails one nomination at a time, always in full and never partially, re-measuring the gas day's total and re-selecting from the surviving candidates after each curtailment, and stops as soon as the gas day's remaining confirmed total is less than or equal to the new rate — the boundary is inclusive, so a day whose total lands exactly on the new rate is left alone. Each curtailment writes the same `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` ledger entry and triggers the same contract reconciliation as phase A. When only firm volume remains and the gas day still exceeds the new rate, the sweep simply stops and the day is left oversubscribed: firm rights are never curtailed by phase B, and no error is raised.

Curtailment is not reversible by a later improvement in conditions. Restoring posture, restoring credit or restoring deliverability never un-curtails a nomination; the shipper must file or re-submit to reclaim the volume.

### 2.9. Position Reconciliation and Gas Volume Conservation

Derived commercial figures are never incrementally patched in isolation; they are reconciled from their sources. After every gas movement ticket confirmation or reversal, after every nomination confirmation, curtailment or supersession, and after every cash-out or cash-out reversal, the server recomputes the affected contract's position and the owning shipper's credit standing as follows.

`scheduled_injection_dth` is the sum of `confirmed_volume_dth` over that contract's nominations whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `CURTAILED` and whose `direction` is `INJECTION`; `scheduled_withdrawal_dth` is the same sum for `WITHDRAWAL`. Curtailed nominations are deliberately included, because a partially curtailed nomination still occupies the volume it was allocated — while a nomination swept to zero contributes nothing, which falls out of the same arithmetic. Nominations in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` status contribute nothing.

`imbalance_dth` is the sum of `volume_dth` over the contract's ledger entries whose `entry_type` is `DAILY_IMBALANCE` and whose `gas_day` falls strictly after `cash_out_through_date` — or over all such entries when `cash_out_through_date` has never been set — rounded half up to 3 decimal places. Curtailment credits and cash-out entries do not enter this figure.

`outstanding_penalty_usd` on the shipper is the sum of `amount_usd` over every ledger entry whose `entry_type` is `CASH_OUT` or `CASH_OUT_REVERSAL`, taken across **all** contracts of that shipper and not merely the one being settled, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The shipper's `credit_status` is then `SUSPENDED` when that outstanding figure is greater than or equal to its `penalty_threshold_usd` — the boundary suspends — and `GOOD` otherwise. The shipper record is mutated only when this recomputation actually moves its `outstanding_penalty_usd` or its `credit_status`, and the contract cascade of §3.7 hangs on the *change* rather than on the value: contracts are suspended only when the recomputation carries `credit_status` from `GOOD` to `SUSPENDED`, and returned to `ACTIVE` only when it carries the status back the other way, so a recomputation that leaves the status where it already stood suspends nothing, restores nothing and runs no credit sweep.

One invariant ties the physical and the commercial books together and must hold after every mutation: a field's `working_gas_inventory_dth` always equals its `opening_working_gas_dth`, plus the sum of `inventory_balance_dth` across all contracts written against that field, plus the field's `retained_fuel_pool_dth`, minus the field's `fuel_gas_used_dth`. Every operation specified in §3 is designed to preserve this equality, and a reversal must move each quantity back by the identical magnitude the confirmation moved it.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Records

Two writes in this system read a parent record, compute against the picture they read, and then commit something the parent cannot subsequently reject on state grounds alone. Both therefore require the client to declare which version of the parent it observed, and both are non-optional fields.

A `POST /nominations` request must carry `expected_contract_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `StorageContract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This protection exists because a nomination for the same contract, gas day and direction legitimately supersedes the prior one under the tariff, so no state precondition can catch the hazard: a writer holding a stale view of the contract would silently displace a schedule it never saw.

A `POST /gas-movement-tickets/{id}/confirm` request must carry `expected_storage_field_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `StorageField` behind the ticket's nomination. If that value does not equal the field's current `version`, the confirmation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FIELD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Confirmation moves working gas against a deliverability envelope and a cushion-gas floor read from the field; a confirmation computed from a stale inventory view would succeed on its own terms while breaching the envelope the field now holds.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named above must not require one, and must not reject a request for omitting one.

### 2.11. Resolution Order and the Validation Ladder

Checks are evaluated in a strict ascending order, and the **first** rule that a request violates determines the `error_id` returned. This ordering is part of the contract: when a request violates several rules at once, only the earliest-listed one is observable. Where a rung names a family of codes, the concrete code is chosen by the type of resource involved.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, non-empty, and naming one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears among the endpoint's authorized roles in §4 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Schema.** The body conforms to the endpoint's input contract: required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values drawn from the declared sets, and numeric ranges honoured — volumes and horsepower strictly positive, `derate_factor` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive, the deliverability curve ascending with endpoints at 0 and 100, `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, `window_end_at` strictly after `window_start_at`, `term_end_date` on or after `term_start_date`, `period_end_date` on or after `period_start_date`, gas days and term dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, instants parseable as ISO 8601 UTC, and pagination parameters within the §2.3 bounds. A nomination re-submission that supplies `nominated_volume_dth` must supply a value greater than zero and no greater than the volume already stored — a re-submission may lower the request but never raise it. The positivity half of that clause is judged here at rung 3, while the comparison against the stored volume can only be made once the nomination named in the path has been resolved, so it is judged immediately after rung 4: a re-submission that both names an unknown nomination and raises the volume answers `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, while one that names a real nomination and raises the volume answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` ahead of the ownership rung 6 and the state rung 7. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.

   The mechanics of this rung are fixed as follows. A body that is not valid JSON, and a body whose top-level JSON value is anything other than an object, are both `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an absent, empty or whitespace-only body is read as the empty object, so an action endpoint whose input contract declares no required field accepts a request carrying no body at all, while one that declares a required field answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` for the missing field. Keys the input contract does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. A field supplied explicitly as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it were absent — required, that is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; optional, it takes the documented default rather than being stored as null. Integer-valued fields accept any JSON number with no fractional part, written `5` or `5.0` alike, and reject `5.5`; JSON booleans are never accepted where a number is expected, and no numeric field accepts its value as a string. String fields reject non-string JSON values, and every identifier field additionally rejects the empty string. Enum fields are matched case-sensitively against the declared set. An instant may be written with a `Z` designator, with an explicit `±HH:MM` or `±HHMM` offset that the server converts to UTC, or with no zone designator at all, in which case it is read as UTC; the seconds component and the fractional part are both optional, and a fraction of one to nine digits is accepted and retained to microsecond resolution. A date must be exactly ten characters in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and must name a real calendar day, so `2026-02-30` is rejected.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the request path resolves to an existing record, validated strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides the answer. The code follows the resource type: a storage field gives `ERR_STORAGE_FIELD_NOT_FOUND`, a wellbore `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`, a mechanical integrity test `ERR_MIT_NOT_FOUND`, a remediation program `ERR_REMEDIATION_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, a compressor unit `ERR_COMPRESSOR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, a compressor run `ERR_COMPRESSOR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, a shipper `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_FOUND`, a storage contract `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, a nomination `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, a gas movement ticket `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, an emergency event `ERR_EMERGENCY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a regulatory report `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. All are HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing record, validated in the order the fields are listed in the endpoint's input contract, using the same per-type code mapping as rung 4 — so a `contract_id` that does not resolve gives `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, and a `well_id` that does not resolve gives `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`. Two references are additionally scoped, and a record outside its scope is reported exactly as if it did not exist: when opening a gas movement ticket, `well_id` must resolve to a wellbore whose `storage_field_id` equals the field behind the nomination's contract, and when opening an emergency event, a supplied `well_id` must resolve to a wellbore on the supplied `storage_field_id`. Both scope failures answer `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to the create endpoints for wellbores, mechanical integrity tests, compressor units, compressor runs, storage contracts, nominations, gas movement tickets, emergency events and regulatory reports.
6. **Shipper ownership.** When the acting role is `Shipper`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `shipper_id` of the contract the request touches — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_OWNED` (403). This rung guards nomination creation, nomination submission, the single-nomination read, the single-contract read, the contract's balancing-ledger channel and the single-ticket read. The shipper-visible list channels never raise it and filter instead, as described in §2.1.
7. **Transition legality.** The target record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Per-entity codes, all HTTP 409: a nomination gives `ERR_INVALID_NOMINATION_STATE`, a ticket `ERR_INVALID_TICKET_STATE`, a contract `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`, a mechanical integrity test `ERR_INVALID_MIT_STATE`, a remediation program `ERR_INVALID_REMEDIATION_STATE`, a compressor run `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, an emergency event `ERR_INVALID_EMERGENCY_STATE`, a regulatory report `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`, and a wellbore `ERR_INVALID_WELL_STATE`. Because a wellbore carries two state dimensions, its service change is judged on the pair together with the guards the transition declares: a move to `IN_SERVICE` is declared only from `SHUT_IN` with an `integrity_state` other than `MIT_FAILED` and no remediation program on the wellbore in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` status; a move to `SHUT_IN` only from `IN_SERVICE` with no gas movement ticket on that wellbore in `PENDING` status; a move to `PLUGGED` only from `SHUT_IN` with no mechanical integrity test on that wellbore in `SCHEDULED` status. Every other combination is `ERR_INVALID_WELL_STATE`. Likewise, completing a compressor run requires both that the run is `SCHEDULED` and that its unit is not `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and both failures answer `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Applies to every action endpoint.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied version field matches the parent's current `version`, as specified in §2.10 — `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` on nomination creation and `ERR_STALE_FIELD_VERSION` on ticket confirmation, both HTTP 412.
9. **Frozen field.** The field's posture is not `FROZEN` for any operation that schedules or moves gas on it — otherwise `ERR_FIELD_FROZEN` (422). Applies to nomination confirmation, ticket confirmation, contract activation and compressor run booking.
10. **Restricted field, interruptible service.** While the posture is `RESTRICTED`, a nomination on an `INTERRUPTIBLE` contract may not be confirmed — `ERR_INTERRUPTIBLE_RESTRICTED` (422). Firm contracts are unaffected. Applies to nomination confirmation.
11. **Wellbore integrity.** The ticket's wellbore has an `integrity_state` other than `MIT_FAILED` and no remediation program on that wellbore stands in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` status — otherwise `ERR_WELL_INTEGRITY_FAILED` (422). Applies to both opening and confirming a gas movement ticket. Integrity is deliberately checked *before* availability at rung 12, because a failed test always shuts its wellbore in as a side effect: the two conditions are co-violated on every failed wellbore, and only this order leaves both errors observable.
12. **Wellbore availability.** The ticket's wellbore has an `operational_state` of `IN_SERVICE` and a `well_type` of `INJECTION_WITHDRAWAL` — otherwise `ERR_WELL_NOT_IN_SERVICE` (422). Applies to both opening and confirming a gas movement ticket.
13. **Cushion gas floor.** On confirming a withdrawal ticket, `actual_volume_dth` is no greater than the field's `working_gas_inventory_dth` — otherwise `ERR_CUSHION_GAS_BREACH` (422). The boundary is inclusive: a metered volume exactly equal to the working gas inventory is permitted and leaves working gas at exactly 0.000.
14. **Working gas ceiling.** On confirming an injection ticket, the field's `working_gas_inventory_dth` plus the ticket's `net_volume_dth` is no greater than `max_working_gas_dth` — otherwise `ERR_WORKING_GAS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
15. **Pressure limit.** The `current_pressure_psig` that would result from confirming an injection ticket is no greater than the field's `max_pressure_psig`, and a recorded test's `result_pressure_psig` is likewise no greater than that limit — otherwise `ERR_FIELD_PRESSURE_LIMIT` (422). Applies to ticket confirmation and to recording an integrity test result.
16. **Evacuation order validity.** Advancing an emergency event to `EVACUATION_ORDERED` requires a `severity` of `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` and an `evacuation_radius_m` of at least 1 metre and at most 8000 metres — otherwise `ERR_EVACUATION_RADIUS_INVALID` (422). Applies to the emergency advance endpoint.
17. **Contract term.** A nomination's `gas_day` falls within the contract's `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, both boundaries inclusive — otherwise `ERR_GAS_DAY_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422). Applies to nomination creation.
18. **Nomination window.** The logical clock is strictly before the cutoff for the nomination's `gas_day`, which is 13:00:00 UTC on the day immediately before that gas day — otherwise `ERR_NOMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to nomination creation and nomination submission.
19. **Season direction.** A nomination on an `INTERRUPTIBLE` contract must have a `direction` equal to the field's current `season`; firm contracts may nominate against the season — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_DIRECTION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to nomination creation.
20. **Test window in the future.** A mechanical integrity test's `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the logical clock at creation — otherwise `ERR_MIT_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (422).
21. **Test window elapsed.** The logical clock has reached or passed a test's `scheduled_end_at` before a result may be recorded — otherwise `ERR_MIT_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` (409).
22. **Run window in the future.** A compressor run's `window_start_at` is at or after the logical clock at booking — otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (422).
23. **Run window elapsed.** The logical clock has reached or passed a run's `window_end_at` before the run may be completed — otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` (409).
24. **Test window conflict.** No other `SCHEDULED` mechanical integrity test on the same wellbore occupies an overlapping window once each existing window is padded by twelve hours on **both** sides — otherwise `ERR_MIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Concretely, the request is rejected when the new window's start falls before the existing window's end plus twelve hours **and** the existing window's start falls before the new window's end plus twelve hours. Windows are half-open, so two windows separated by exactly twelve hours are accepted.
25. **Horsepower capacity.** For every instant covered by the new compressor run's half-open window, the sum of `allocated_horsepower` over `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` runs on the same unit that cover that instant, plus the new allocation, is no greater than the unit's `rated_horsepower` — otherwise `ERR_HORSEPOWER_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (409). Adjacency is free: a run starting exactly at another run's `window_end_at` does not overlap it, and a sum exactly equal to `rated_horsepower` is accepted.
26. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user whose act created the obligation — otherwise `ERR_SOD_REMEDIATION_SELF_VERIFICATION` (403). Applies to completing a remediation program (compared against the triggering test's `recorded_by_user_id`) and to submitting a regulatory report (compared against the report's `prepared_by_user_id`).
27. **Uniqueness.** `field_code` is unique across all storage fields (`ERR_DUPLICATE_FIELD_CODE`); `duns_number` is unique across all shippers (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DUNS`); `contract_code` is unique across all storage contracts (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTRACT_CODE`); `unit_code` is unique within a `storage_field_id` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_CODE`); and at most one non-`REJECTED` regulatory report may exist for a given combination of `storage_field_id`, `report_type`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD`). All are HTTP 409.
28. **Ticket eligibility.** A gas movement ticket may only be opened against a nomination whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `confirmed_volume_dth` is greater than zero; any other nomination status, and a confirmed nomination whose confirmed volume is zero, answers `ERR_INVALID_NOMINATION_STATE` (409). At most one non-`REVERSED` ticket may exist per nomination; a second live ticket answers `ERR_TICKET_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409), and once the first ticket has been reversed a replacement may be opened. The nomination-status clause is evaluated before the duplicate-ticket clause.
29. **Metering overrun.** A ticket's `actual_volume_dth` is no greater than the nomination's `confirmed_volume_dth` increased by five percent (5 percent), that ceiling rounded half up to a whole dekatherm — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_OVERRUN_EXCEEDED` (422).
30. **Revision limit.** Re-submitting a curtailed nomination requires that its `revision_round` is still below two — otherwise `ERR_NOMINATION_REVISION_LIMIT` (422).
31. **Reporting period lock.** A gas movement ticket may not be reversed while a regulatory report on the same storage field, in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` status, covers the ticket's nomination `gas_day` — otherwise `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409).
32. **Remediation blocked by emergency.** An `OPEN` remediation program may not be completed while an emergency event on its wellbore's field stands in `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED` status — otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_ESCALATED` (409). A program that has already reached `ESCALATED` can never be completed at all, and that attempt is caught earlier, at rung 7, as `ERR_INVALID_REMEDIATION_STATE`.
33. **Contract rights.** A nomination's `nominated_volume_dth` is no greater than the contract's `max_daily_injection_dth` for an injection or `max_daily_withdrawal_dth` for a withdrawal, and for an injection it is additionally no greater than `max_storage_quantity_dth` less the contract's current `inventory_balance_dth` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_RIGHTS_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to nomination creation.
34. **Contract inventory.** On confirming a withdrawal ticket, `actual_volume_dth` plus `fuel_retention_dth` is no greater than the contract's `inventory_balance_dth` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
35. **Shipper credit.** The shipper behind the touched contract has a `credit_status` of `GOOD` — otherwise `ERR_SHIPPER_CREDIT_SUSPENDED` (402). Applies to contract activation, nomination creation, nomination submission and nomination confirmation.
36. **Settled period.** A cash-out's `period_start_date` is strictly after the contract's `cash_out_through_date` whenever that date is set — otherwise `ERR_CASH_OUT_PERIOD_SETTLED` (409).
37. **Something to settle.** A cash-out requires at least one `DAILY_IMBALANCE` ledger entry in the requested period, and a cash-out reversal requires an unreversed `CASH_OUT` entry to exist — otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_TO_CASH_OUT` (422).
38. **Report variance.** A report's `variance_pct` is no greater than 0.5000 percent, compared after rounding to 4 decimal places with the boundary inclusive — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to report submission.
39. **Open integrity findings.** A report whose `report_type` is `INTEGRITY_PROGRAM_STATUS` may only be submitted while its `open_finding_count` is zero — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_INTEGRITY_FINDINGS` (422).

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Read Channels

Records in this system are peers rather than nested children: a wellbore, a compressor unit, a contract, a nomination and a ticket are each addressed at the top level, and their relationships are carried by identifiers supplied in request bodies. Three read channels are the exception — the reports filed for a field, the runs booked on a unit, and the balancing ledger of a contract are reached through the owning record's path. For these, the owning identifier is resolved first and a failure to resolve it is answered with that record's own not-found code before any filter is *applied*, exactly as rung 4 of §2.11 requires — though rung 3 still precedes rung 4, so a request that carries both an unresolvable owning identifier and a malformed `limit`, `offset` or filter value answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than the not-found code.

Because relationships are by reference rather than containment, a single request routinely mutates several records. The rule is that **every** record a request touches is mutated in its own right: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one, whether the client addressed it or the server reached it through a cascade. A confirmed ticket moves the field's inventory and the contract's balance, so both records advance; a curtailment sweep touching four contracts advances four `version` values. Where a cascade is described in §3 as "recomputed", that recomputation is a mutation with these consequences. Conversely, when a sweep or reconciliation finds nothing to change, no `version` moves and no ledger entry is written — an empty cascade must be silent, not a no-op write.

The server is also permitted, and required, to move a record's state without a client request against that record. A wellbore is shut in by an integrity test result recorded against the test; a remediation program is escalated by an emergency event advancing on the field; compressor runs are cancelled by an evacuation order; contracts are suspended by a cash-out on a sibling contract of the same shipper; nominations are curtailed by all of the above. These server-driven transitions are as authoritative as client-driven ones.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning a Reservoir and Its Wellbores

A `FieldOperator` brings a storage field into the system by registering it with a human-readable `name` and an operator `field_code` of between 3 and 12 characters that must be unique across every field the system holds. The reservoir's physical envelope is declared at the same time: `total_capacity_dth` must be greater than zero, and `cushion_gas_dth` — the base gas that keeps the reservoir pressurised and is never delivered — must be greater than zero and strictly less than the total capacity. The pressure model needs `base_pressure_psig`, the wellhead pressure at zero working gas, and `pressure_span_psig`, the pressure rise from empty to full working gas, both strictly positive; `max_pressure_psig` is the regulatory maximum operating pressure and must be at least the base pressure plus the span. The operator supplies the `deliverability_curve` described in §2.7 and may optionally choose a `deliverability_method`, may optionally declare the reservoir's opening `season` (defaulting to `INJECTION`), and may optionally seed `opening_working_gas_dth`, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative or exceed the derived working gas ceiling.

Registration computes the whole derived block at once. `max_working_gas_dth` is set, `working_gas_inventory_dth` is initialised from `opening_working_gas_dth`, and `retained_fuel_pool_dth` and `fuel_gas_used_dth` both start at 0. The deliverability recomputation of §2.7 stamps `inventory_pct`, `current_pressure_psig`, `deliverability_tier` and both rate ceilings — both of which are 0, since no wellbore exists yet — and the posture derivation of §2.6 yields `FROZEN` for the same reason. Because the whole block is computed before the record is published, registration is not itself a mutation on top of the create: a freshly registered field is returned at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to `created_at`. A duplicate `field_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FIELD_CODE`.

Turning the reservoir between injection and withdrawal service is a distinct operator act that carries a new `season` value. The value supplied need not differ from the one the field already holds: a turn to the season already in force is accepted, still increments the field's `version`, and still runs the sweep below over whatever candidates the filter finds. The turn writes the `season` and nothing else — it does not recompute the derived block of §2.7 or the posture of §2.6, neither of which reads the season — and it is not gated by posture, so a `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED` field may still be turned. Beyond storing it, the turn runs the seasonal curtailment described in §2.8: interruptible nominations whose `direction` no longer matches the season lose their confirmed volume for every gas day from today forward, each producing a `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` ledger entry and a recomputation of the affected contract's scheduled volumes and `version`. Firm nominations survive a season turn untouched, which is precisely the commercial value of firm service.

Wellbores are registered against a field by supplying its `storage_field_id`, along with an API `well_name`, a `well_type` of either `INJECTION_WITHDRAWAL` for a wellbore that moves gas or `OBSERVATION` for one that only monitors, a `rated_rate_dth_per_day` that may be zero or greater, and `last_mit_passed_at`, the instant of the last passed mechanical integrity test. `casing_pressure_psig` records the last observed casing annulus pressure and defaults to 0 when not supplied, and `initial_operational_state` seeds the wellbore's `operational_state`, defaulting to `IN_SERVICE`. At registration, `service_change_count` starts at 0, `shut_in_reason` starts null whatever `initial_operational_state` was supplied — a wellbore seeded straight into `SHUT_IN` or `PLUGGED` carries no reason until a later transition writes one — and `mit_due_at` is computed as `last_mit_passed_at` plus 365 days. The wellbore's `integrity_state` is then seeded from that due date: it is `MIT_DUE` when `mit_due_at` is at or before the current logical clock, and `MIT_CURRENT` otherwise. Registering a wellbore recomputes the field's `wells_in_service_count`, its deliverability rates and its posture — which is how the first in-service injection/withdrawal wellbore takes a field from `FROZEN` to `OPEN`, provided no wellbore on it is `MIT_FAILED`, no emergency event on it stands at `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED`, and its `current_pressure_psig` is within the posture pressure ceiling.

Moving a wellbore between service states is a single operator capability that carries the desired `to_operational_state` and an optional free-text `reason`. The `reason` must be a string when it is supplied, but it is recorded nowhere on the wellbore: `shut_in_reason` is written from the transition itself and never from that text. Three moves are legal and each has its own guard. Taking a wellbore from `IN_SERVICE` to `SHUT_IN` requires that no gas movement ticket on that wellbore is still `PENDING`; the move sets `shut_in_reason` to `OPERATOR_REQUEST`, increments `service_change_count`, decrements the field's `wells_in_service_count`, and recomputes the field's deliverability, tier, pressure-derived figures and posture. Because the field's rates have just fallen, phase B of the curtailment sweep runs over every future gas day whose confirmed volume no longer fits, and if the shut-in took the last in-service wellbore out, the posture becomes `FROZEN` and phase A runs instead, taking firm and interruptible nominations alike. Returning a wellbore from `SHUT_IN` to `IN_SERVICE` requires that its `integrity_state` is not `MIT_FAILED` and that no remediation program on it stands in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` status; the move clears `shut_in_reason` to null, increments `service_change_count`, increments the field's in-service count, and recomputes rates and posture. A return to service runs through the same sweep decision as every other service change, so if it lifts the field out of `FROZEN` but only as far as `RESTRICTED` — because another wellbore on the field still stands `MIT_FAILED` — that counts as a move into `RESTRICTED` and phase A sweeps the interruptible nominations, even though the deliverability rates have just risen rather than fallen. Finally, `SHUT_IN` to `PLUGGED` requires that no mechanical integrity test on the wellbore is still `SCHEDULED`; it increments `service_change_count`, leaves `shut_in_reason` at whatever value the earlier shut-in wrote, and makes the wellbore terminal — a plugged wellbore can never re-enter service, and the field's posture is recomputed to reflect its permanent loss. Every other combination of current pair and requested target is `ERR_INVALID_WELL_STATE`. Wellbores can be read individually and listed with filters on `storage_field_id`, on `operational_state` and on `integrity_state`.

### 3.2. Wellbore Integrity: Testing, Failure and Remediation

Regulation requires that every wellbore's mechanical integrity be demonstrated on a 365-day cycle, and `mit_due_at` on each wellbore is the running deadline. An `IntegrityEngineer` books a test by naming the `well_id` under test, a `test_type` of `PRESSURE_TEST`, `TEMPERATURE_LOG` or `NOISE_LOG`, and the half-open occupation window `scheduled_start_at` to `scheduled_end_at`, where the end must be strictly after the start. A booked test starts with `status` `SCHEDULED` and with `outcome`, `result_pressure_psig`, `recorded_by_user_id` and `recorded_at` all null. The window must not begin in the past relative to the logical clock, and it must clear every other `SCHEDULED` test on the same wellbore by the twelve-hour buffer described at rung 24 of §2.11 — a wellbore cannot be occupied by two crews at once, and the buffer covers rig-up and rig-down on both sides of each window.

Recording a result is only possible once the booked window has fully elapsed under the logical clock, and the recorded `result_pressure_psig` must not exceed the field's `max_pressure_psig`. The submitted `outcome` decides everything that follows, and the three branches are deliberately asymmetric.

A `PASS` closes the test at `status` `PASSED` with `outcome` `PASS`, stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `recorded_at` from the logical clock, and takes the wellbore's integrity dimension to `MIT_CURRENT`. That assignment is unconditional in the prior value: a test is bookable on a wellbore in any state, so a wellbore standing at `MIT_FAILED` or `REMEDIATED` is carried to `MIT_CURRENT` by a passing test just as a `MIT_DUE` one is, and the remediation program the earlier failure opened is left `OPEN` — a passing test clears the integrity flag without closing the program. The wellbore's `last_mit_passed_at` is set to the test's `recorded_at`, its `mit_due_at` is recomputed as that instant plus 365 days, and its `casing_pressure_psig` is refreshed to the recorded `result_pressure_psig`.

A `FAIL` closes the test at `status` `FAILED` with `outcome` `FAIL`, stamping the same two audit fields, and then cascades hard. The wellbore's integrity dimension moves to `MIT_FAILED`, and its operational dimension is forced to `SHUT_IN` with `shut_in_reason` set to `MIT_FAILURE` — from whatever it held, `PLUGGED` included, since the failure is recorded against the test rather than against the wellbore. This forced shut-in is a safety action rather than an operator decision, so it does **not** increment `service_change_count`. A remediation program is opened automatically in `OPEN` status, carrying the `well_id` of the failed wellbore and the `triggering_mit_id` of the test that failed, with `opened_at` set to the logical clock and `due_at` computed as 45 days after that. The field's `wells_in_service_count` falls if that wellbore was in service, its deliverability is recomputed, and its posture becomes `RESTRICTED` — or `FROZEN`, if that wellbore was the last one in service. Phase A of the curtailment sweep then runs in the form matching the resulting posture, and as §2.8 records it runs whether or not that posture differs from the one the field already carried, so a second failure on an already-`RESTRICTED` field sweeps again.

An `INCONCLUSIVE` result is the deliberate non-cascade. The test still closes at `status` `FAILED`, but with `outcome` `INCONCLUSIVE`: no remediation program is created, the wellbore is not shut in, and neither of its state dimensions changes. An inconclusive log means the crew learned nothing, not that the wellbore is unfit.

Closing a remediation program is an act of independent verification, and the engineer who recorded the failing result may not perform it. The completing request carries `verification_notes`, and the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the `recorded_by_user_id` of the triggering test; a match is refused with `ERR_SOD_REMEDIATION_SELF_VERIFICATION`. Completion also requires that no emergency event on the wellbore's field stands in `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED` — remediation work cannot be signed off in the middle of an emergency response, and the attempt answers `ERR_REMEDIATION_ESCALATED`. When the program does complete, its `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_by_user_id` and `completed_at` are stamped from the acting user and the logical clock, and the supplied notes are stored. The wellbore's integrity dimension is set to `REMEDIATED` — unconditionally in whatever it then held, normally `MIT_FAILED` — and its `shut_in_reason` is cleared to null — but its `operational_state` deliberately stays `SHUT_IN` until an operator performs an explicit service change, because remediation proves the wellbore sound without deciding that it should be producing. The field's posture is recomputed and may leave `RESTRICTED` once the last failed wellbore has been cleared.

An emergency escalation can take the decision away entirely. When an evacuation is ordered on the field, every remediation program on that field's wellbores that is still `OPEN` becomes `ESCALATED`, which is terminal: the wellbore's integrity dimension stays `MIT_FAILED`, and any later attempt to complete the program is refused at the state rung with `ERR_INVALID_REMEDIATION_STATE`. Remediation programs can be read individually and listed with filters on `well_id`, on the `triggering_mit_id` that opened them, and on `status`; tests can be listed filtered by `well_id` and `status`.

### 3.3. Compressor Horsepower Allocation and Fuel Accounting

Injection needs compression, and compression burns gas out of the reservoir it is filling. A `FieldOperator` registers a compressor unit against a field's `storage_field_id` with a station-local `unit_code` that must be unique among that field's units, a nameplate `rated_horsepower` greater than zero, and an optional `derate_factor` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive that defaults to 0.00 and models the ambient and mechanical derate applied to whatever horsepower is allocated to it. A new unit starts with `status` `AVAILABLE`, `lifetime_fuel_dth` at 0 and `scheduled_run_count` at 0. A duplicate `unit_code` on the same field is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_CODE`.

Booking a run reserves horsepower on a unit for a half-open window. The request carries the `compressor_unit_id`, the window `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` (the end strictly after the start, and the start not in the past), the `allocated_horsepower` to reserve, and a `mode` of `INJECTION` or `WITHDRAWAL` that selects the fuel mode multiplier. The window must fit within the unit's nameplate at every instant it covers, per rung 25 of §2.11. Booking is also gated by posture: no run may be booked on a `FROZEN` field. The unit's own `status` gates nothing at booking — a run may be booked on a unit standing `AVAILABLE`, `COMMITTED` or even `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and the booking drives that unit to `COMMITTED` in every case, which is how a unit taken out of service by an evacuation becomes committed again.

The booking itself stamps four derived figures immediately, before any gas has moved: `duration_hours` is the window's length in hours rounded half up to 4 decimal places; `effective_horsepower` is the `allocated_horsepower` multiplied by one minus the unit's `derate_factor`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; `utilization_ratio` is that effective horsepower divided by the unit's `rated_horsepower`, rounded half up to 6 decimal places; and `utilization_band` is read from that six-decimal ratio — `LOW` below 0.50, `MID` from 0.50 up to but not including 0.85, and `HIGH` at 0.85 or above. The run starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `fuel_consumed_dth` null, and the unit's `scheduled_run_count` increments while its `status` becomes `COMMITTED`.

Closing out a run is only permitted once the logical clock has reached or passed its `window_end_at`, only while the run is still `SCHEDULED`, and only while its unit is not `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Completion computes the fuel actually burned. The band selected at booking picks a fuel rate — 0.0110 dekatherms per effective horsepower-hour for a `LOW` band run, 0.0092 for a `MID` band run, and 0.0105 for a `HIGH` band run. Note that this table is deliberately non-monotonic: the high-band rate sits *above* the mid-band rate, so a run that just crosses a utilization of 0.85 burns more fuel than one just below it even though its effective horsepower barely moved. The mode then applies a multiplier of 1.00 for `INJECTION` and 0.85 for `WITHDRAWAL`. The raw burn is the run's `effective_horsepower` multiplied by its `duration_hours`, multiplied by the band rate, multiplied by the mode multiplier. That raw figure is then clamped into the permitted range for a single run — never below 25.00 dekatherms and never above 5000.00 dekatherms, with the upper clamp applied before the lower one so that the floor always wins a conflict — and the clamped result is rounded half up to 2 decimal places to become `fuel_consumed_dth`.

The consequences of a completed run reach the whole field. The run's `status` becomes `COMPLETED`; the unit's `lifetime_fuel_dth` increases by the fuel burned, its `scheduled_run_count` decrements, and its `status` returns to `AVAILABLE` once that count reaches zero. On the field, `fuel_gas_used_dth` increases by the fuel burned and `working_gas_inventory_dth` falls by the same amount — compressor fuel is drawn out of working gas, which is why the conservation identity in §2.9 subtracts it. No cushion-gas floor and no capacity check guards that deduction: the whole burn is taken even when it drives `working_gas_inventory_dth` below zero, in which case `inventory_pct` goes negative, `current_pressure_psig` falls below `base_pressure_psig`, `deliverability_tier` reads `TIER_1`, and the curve evaluation clamps to its first point exactly as §2.7 describes. The field's `inventory_pct`, `current_pressure_psig`, `deliverability_tier`, both rate ceilings and its posture are recomputed and its `version` increments, and because the rates may have fallen into a lower curve band, phase B of the curtailment sweep runs over every future gas day that no longer fits.

A run can also end without ever burning fuel. When an evacuation is ordered on the run's field, every `SCHEDULED` run on that field moves from `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`: `fuel_consumed_dth` stays null, no fuel is drawn from the field, the unit's `scheduled_run_count` decrements, and the unit's `status` becomes `OUT_OF_SERVICE` rather than `AVAILABLE`. Compressor units can be read individually and listed with filters on `storage_field_id` and on `status`, and runs booked on a unit can be read through that unit's own run channel, filtered by `status`.

### 3.4. Onboarding Shippers and Writing Storage Service Contracts

A `SchedulingCoordinator` onboards a storage customer by registering a `Shipper` with its `legal_name`, which must be a non-empty string, its nine-digit `duns_number` — which must be exactly nine digits and unique across every shipper, a duplicate answering `ERR_DUPLICATE_DUNS` — and a `penalty_threshold_usd` greater than zero. That threshold is the credit limit against which cash-out exposure is measured. A newly registered shipper begins with `outstanding_penalty_usd` at 0.00 and `credit_status` `GOOD`.

Service is then written as a contract against one reservoir. The coordinator supplies the `shipper_id` of the customer, the `storage_field_id` of the reservoir, a globally unique `contract_code`, and a `service_class` of either `FIRM`, whose rights are unconditional, or `INTERRUPTIBLE`, whose rights are share-capped and curtailable. The commercial envelope consists of `max_storage_quantity_dth`, the maximum inventory the shipper may hold in the field; `max_daily_injection_dth` and `max_daily_withdrawal_dth`, the daily rights in each direction; and the term, bounded by `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, the latter on or after the former. All three volume rights must be greater than zero. Pricing consists of `commodity_rate_usd_per_dth`, the charge per dekatherm moved, and `index_price_usd_per_dth`, the index gas price used for fuel charges and for cash-out valuation, both greater than zero and both carried at 4 decimal places. Finally, `cash_out_method` fixes how the contract's imbalance will be settled and must be either `MONTHLY_AGGREGATE` or `DAILY_RATCHET`.

A newly written contract cannot yet carry a submitted nomination — a shipper may file a nomination against it, but no filing may be submitted until the contract is activated. It starts at `status` `DRAFT` with `activated_at` null, `inventory_balance_dth` 0.000, `imbalance_dth` 0.000, `cash_out_amount_usd` 0.00, `cash_out_through_date` null, and both `scheduled_injection_dth` and `scheduled_withdrawal_dth` at 0. A duplicate `contract_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTRACT_CODE`.

Activation moves the contract from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` and stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, at which point nominations against the contract may be submitted. Two conditions guard it beyond the state itself: the shipper's `credit_status` must be `GOOD`, and the reservoir the contract is written against must not be `FROZEN`. The coordinator can list contracts filtered by `shipper_id`, `storage_field_id`, `status` and `service_class`; a `Shipper` sees only its own rows on the same channel, as described in §2.1.

Contracts also participate in a genuine cycle later in their life. A cash-out that breaches the shipper's credit threshold takes every `ACTIVE` contract of that shipper to `SUSPENDED`, and reversing that cash-out brings them back from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`. That mechanism, and what it destroys on the way through, is described in §3.7.

### 3.5. The Nomination Cycle: Filing, Allocation and Revision

A nomination is a shipper's request to move a specific volume on a specific gas day, and it is the only place in the system where a customer initiates work. A `Shipper` files one by naming the `contract_id` it is nominating against, the `gas_day` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `direction` of `INJECTION` or `WITHDRAWAL`, a whole-dekatherm `nominated_volume_dth` greater than zero, and the `expected_contract_version` required by §2.10. The filing is checked against the contract's term, against the nomination cutoff — 13:00:00 UTC on the day before the gas day, and the clock must be strictly before it — and against the contract's daily rights, where an injection is additionally capped by the room left under `max_storage_quantity_dth` after the current `inventory_balance_dth`. Interruptible service must nominate with the reservoir's `season`: an interruptible nomination whose `direction` contradicts the season is refused with `ERR_SEASON_DIRECTION_MISMATCH`, while firm service may nominate against the season freely. Filing itself is not gated on the contract's `status`: a nomination may be filed against a `DRAFT`, an `ACTIVE` or a `SUSPENDED` contract alike, and it is submission rather than filing that demands an `ACTIVE` contract.

A filed nomination begins at `status` `DRAFT`, with `confirmed_volume_dth` 0, `curtailed_volume_dth` 0, `revision_round` 0, and both `submitted_at` and `confirmed_at` null. Filing also displaces any earlier work for the same slot: if a non-terminal nomination already exists on the same `contract_id`, `gas_day` and `direction`, it moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` status with its `superseded_by_nomination_id` set to the new nomination's `id`, and whatever confirmed volume it was holding is removed from the contract's scheduled totals. A `CONFIRMED` nomination may only be superseded this way while no gas movement ticket against it stands `CONFIRMED` — once gas has actually moved, the schedule that authorised it can no longer be displaced. That refusal is `ERR_INVALID_NOMINATION_STATE`, which this create endpoint therefore answers at rung 7 of §2.11 — ahead of the optimistic-concurrency check of rung 8, so a filing that is at once actualised and stale reports the nomination state rather than the stale contract version. Every filing increments the contract's `version` by one, which is exactly why the create is the endpoint that carries the optimistic-concurrency field.

Submission is the shipper's second act. It takes the nomination from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and may optionally carry a revised `nominated_volume_dth` — which may only *lower* the request, never raise it, and must remain greater than zero. Submission requires the owning contract to be `ACTIVE`, so a submission against a `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` contract is refused as an invalid contract state, and it requires the shipper's `credit_status` to be `GOOD`. The cutoff applies here too: a submission at or after 13:00Z on the day before the gas day is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Submission reconciles the owning contract's position but, unlike filing and confirmation, does not force a mutation on it: the contract's `version` moves only when that reconciliation actually changes a scheduled total or the imbalance, so a plain `DRAFT`-to-`SUBMITTED` submission leaves the contract's `version` untouched, exactly as the empty-cascade rule of §2.12 requires.

Allocation is the operator's act. A `SchedulingCoordinator` runs the confirmation on a `SUBMITTED` nomination, and the result is one of two states rather than a simple yes. The allocation waterfall computes how much of the request the field and the contract can actually carry, in this order:

1. The applicable **field ceiling** is the reservoir's `max_injection_rate_dth_per_day` for an injection or its `max_withdrawal_rate_dth_per_day` for a withdrawal.
2. The **contract bucket** narrows the request to the contract's own rights. The applicable daily right is `max_daily_injection_dth` for an injection or `max_daily_withdrawal_dth` for a withdrawal. The applicable inventory room is `max_storage_quantity_dth` less the contract's current `inventory_balance_dth` for an injection, and simply the current `inventory_balance_dth` for a withdrawal. The contract bucket is the smallest of the requested `nominated_volume_dth`, that daily right and that inventory room, truncated down to a whole dekatherm.
3. The **field bucket** measures what the gas day has left. Two sums are taken over all *other* nominations on the same field, the same `gas_day` and the same `direction` whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `CURTAILED` — a partially curtailed nomination still occupies the volume it holds, and a nomination swept to zero contributes nothing. The first sum covers those whose contract's `service_class` is `FIRM`; the second covers those whose contract's `service_class` is `INTERRUPTIBLE`. The nomination under confirmation is always excluded from both sums.
4. For a `FIRM` contract, the headroom is the field ceiling less the firm sum less the interruptible sum. For an `INTERRUPTIBLE` contract, the headroom is the lesser of that same figure and a second limit: thirty percent (30 percent) of the field ceiling, truncated down to a whole dekatherm, less the interruptible sum. Interruptible service is therefore capped at a thirty-percent share of the day's deliverability no matter how much room firm shippers have left unused.
5. `confirmed_volume_dth` is the smaller of the contract bucket and the headroom, floored at zero so that a negative headroom yields zero rather than a negative volume. `curtailed_volume_dth` is the requested `nominated_volume_dth` less that confirmed volume. No step in this waterfall ever rounds a volume upward.

When the waterfall confirms the whole request, the nomination moves from `SUBMITTED` to `CONFIRMED` with `curtailed_volume_dth` at 0. When it confirms less than the request — including nothing at all — the nomination moves from `SUBMITTED` to `CURTAILED` instead, and a `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` ledger entry is appended for the nomination's `gas_day` carrying a `volume_dth` equal to the negative of `curtailed_volume_dth` and an `amount_usd` of 0.00. Either way, `confirmed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the contract's `scheduled_injection_dth` or `scheduled_withdrawal_dth` is recomputed to include whatever volume was confirmed, and the contract's `version` increments by one. Confirmation is gated by posture — never on a `FROZEN` field, and never for an interruptible contract on a `RESTRICTED` one — and by the shipper's credit standing.

A curtailed nomination is not the end of the conversation. The shipper may re-submit it, which takes it from `CURTAILED` back to `SUBMITTED` for another allocation round: `revision_round` increments by one, `submitted_at` is refreshed, `confirmed_volume_dth` and `curtailed_volume_dth` are reset to 0, and the contract's scheduled total is reduced by the volume the nomination had previously been holding. Two rounds of revision are permitted; a third attempt — that is, a re-submission when `revision_round` has already reached 2 — is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_REVISION_LIMIT`. Re-submission also requires that the cutoff for the gas day has not yet passed and that no `CONFIRMED` gas movement ticket exists against the nomination.

Two further transitions are driven by the server rather than by a client. A `CONFIRMED` nomination becomes `CURTAILED` whenever a curtailment sweep selects it under §2.8, which requires that its `gas_day` is today or later and that no confirmed ticket exists against it; the sweep zeroes `confirmed_volume_dth`, moves the whole `nominated_volume_dth` into `curtailed_volume_dth`, reduces the contract's scheduled total, appends the `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` entry and increments the contract's `version`. Separately, a credit suspension is terminal for unstarted work: when a shipper's credit is suspended, every `DRAFT` and every `SUBMITTED` nomination on the affected contracts moves to `REJECTED`, from which there is no recovery — the shipper must file fresh nominations once credit is restored. Nominations can be listed with filters on `contract_id`, `gas_day`, `direction`, `status` and `superseded_by_nomination_id`, the last of which lets a shipper trace which later filing displaced an earlier one.

### 3.6. Metering the Physical Movement

A confirmed nomination is a promise; a gas movement ticket is the measurement that discharges it. A `FieldOperator` opens a ticket by naming the `nomination_id` the movement actualises, the `well_id` the gas physically travelled through, and the metered `actual_volume_dth` read at the wellhead. Only a nomination in `CONFIRMED` status with a `confirmed_volume_dth` greater than zero may carry a ticket — a fully curtailed nomination can never be actualised — and only one non-`REVERSED` ticket may exist per nomination at a time. The named wellbore must belong to the same reservoir as the nomination's contract, and a wellbore on a different field is reported as though it did not exist. The wellbore's readiness is checked at this point as well as at confirmation: its `integrity_state` must not be `MIT_FAILED`, no remediation program on it may stand `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, its `operational_state` must be `IN_SERVICE`, and its `well_type` must be `INJECTION_WITHDRAWAL`. Metering tolerance is five percent: `actual_volume_dth` may exceed the nomination's `confirmed_volume_dth` by at most five percent, that ceiling rounded half up to a whole dekatherm, and anything beyond it is refused with `ERR_TICKET_OVERRUN_EXCEEDED`. A newly opened ticket sits at `status` `PENDING` with every charge and derived volume field null and `confirmed_at` null.

Confirming the ticket is where money and gas both move, and it carries the `expected_storage_field_version` of §2.10. Before anything is written, the confirmation is checked against the reservoir's posture, the wellbore's two dimensions again, the cushion-gas floor for a withdrawal, the working-gas ceiling and the pressure limit for an injection, and the contract's own inventory for a withdrawal.

The charge breakdown then runs in a **pinned order of six steps**, and the order matters because step 4 selects a discount tier from an intermediate figure:

1. `fuel_retention_dth` is 1.20 percent of `actual_volume_dth` — that is, the metered volume multiplied by 1.20 and divided by 100 — rounded half up to 3 decimal places. This is the operator's in-kind fuel retention.
2. `net_volume_dth` is the volume the contract is actually credited or debited. For an `INJECTION` it is `actual_volume_dth` less `fuel_retention_dth`; for a `WITHDRAWAL` it is `actual_volume_dth` plus `fuel_retention_dth`. Either result is rounded half up to 3 decimal places. In both directions the shipper bears the retention.
3. `commodity_charge_usd` is `actual_volume_dth` multiplied by the contract's `commodity_rate_usd_per_dth`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
4. The volume discount tier is selected from `commodity_charge_usd` **alone** and from no other figure: no discount when that charge is below 5,000 US dollars, 2 percent when it is at least 5,000 but below 20,000, and 5 percent at 20,000 or above. `volume_discount_usd` is `commodity_charge_usd` multiplied by that percentage and divided by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Selecting the tier from a charge that already includes the fuel component is incorrect and produces a different published total for identical inputs; the tier decision must be made here, before step 5 exists.
5. `fuel_charge_usd` is `fuel_retention_dth` multiplied by the contract's `index_price_usd_per_dth`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
6. `total_charge_usd` is `commodity_charge_usd`, less `volume_discount_usd`, plus `fuel_charge_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

With the charges stamped, the ticket moves from `PENDING` to `CONFIRMED` and `confirmed_at` is set from the logical clock. On the reservoir, `working_gas_inventory_dth` moves by the full `actual_volume_dth`, signed by the nomination's `direction` — upward for an injection, downward for a withdrawal — and `retained_fuel_pool_dth` increases by `fuel_retention_dth`, which is how the retention stays inside the field while leaving the shipper's book. The field's `inventory_pct`, `current_pressure_psig`, `deliverability_tier`, both rate ceilings and its `field_posture` are recomputed and its `version` increments by one. On the commercial side, the contract's `inventory_balance_dth` moves by `net_volume_dth` — up for an injection, down for a withdrawal — and the signed daily delta between what was metered and what was scheduled, that is `actual_volume_dth` less the nomination's `confirmed_volume_dth`, is written to the balancing ledger as a `DAILY_IMBALANCE` entry stamped with the nomination's `gas_day`. Exactly one such entry is appended on every confirmation, including the confirmation whose metered volume matches the schedule to the dekatherm and whose delta is therefore 0.000. The contract's `imbalance_dth` is then reconciled from that ledger as §2.9 prescribes rather than patched in place, so it rises by the delta whenever the gas day is not already covered by the contract's `cash_out_through_date` and stays where it was when the gas day has already been settled. Finally, if a deliverability rate fell as a result of the inventory move — a large withdrawal can drop the field into a lower curve band — phase B of the curtailment sweep runs over the affected future gas days. Phase B is the only sweep a ticket confirmation ever runs: a confirmation whose recomputation also carries the field's `field_posture` into `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` still runs no phase A, because phase A belongs to the triggers §2.8 names and a ticket confirmation is not among them.

Reversal exists for mismeasurement. A `FieldOperator` reverses a `CONFIRMED` ticket with a stored `reversal_reason`, taking it to the terminal `REVERSED` status, but only while the movement is still open to correction: if a regulatory report on the same reservoir stands in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` status and covers the ticket's nomination `gas_day`, the reversal is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_LOCKED`. A permitted reversal moves every reservoir and contract quantity the confirmation moved back by the identical magnitude — inventory, retained fuel, contract balance and contract imbalance alike — so that the conservation identity of §2.9 holds exactly as it did before the ticket existed. A `DAILY_IMBALANCE` ledger entry carrying the negated `volume_dth` is appended rather than the original entry being edited, since the ledger is append-only. The field's rates, tier, pressure and posture are recomputed and its `version` increments by one, and the contract's position is reconciled in the same breath with its own `version` incrementing by one as well. What the reversal does *not* undo is the paperwork: the charge breakdown the confirmation stamped — `fuel_retention_dth`, `net_volume_dth` and all four money figures — together with `confirmed_at`, is left standing on the reversed ticket rather than being cleared back to null, so the record of what was originally billed survives. Nor does any curtailment sweep run on a reversal, of either phase, however the recomputed rates or posture move. Once a ticket has been reversed, a replacement ticket may be opened against the same nomination. Tickets can be listed with filters on `nomination_id`, `well_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Balancing, Imbalance Penalties and Cash-Out

Every contract carries an append-only balancing ledger, and its entries are the only durable record of what the shipper owes. An `ImbalanceLedgerEntry` records the `contract_id` it belongs to, an `entry_type` that discriminates its origin among `DAILY_IMBALANCE`, `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT`, `CASH_OUT` and `CASH_OUT_REVERSAL`, the `gas_day` it pertains to — which is null for the period-level `CASH_OUT` and `CASH_OUT_REVERSAL` entries — a signed `volume_dth` at 3 decimal places where a positive value means the shipper delivered more than it had scheduled, a signed `amount_usd` at 2 decimal places which is non-zero only on the two cash-out types, and an `origin_note` holding a free-text provenance label such as `ticket confirm 2026-08-05`. The `volume_dth` of a `CASH_OUT` or a `CASH_OUT_REVERSAL` entry is always 0.000, just as the `amount_usd` of a `DAILY_IMBALANCE` or a `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` entry is always 0.00. That note is descriptive only and is never a foreign key. Ledger entries are immutable: as noted in §2.4 they carry `id` and `created_at` and nothing else of the common properties. The channel that reads a contract's ledger accepts filters on `entry_type` and on `gas_day`.

Settling is the coordinator's periodic act, and it is not gated on the contract's `status`: a `DRAFT`, an `ACTIVE` and a `SUSPENDED` contract may each be settled, because the only substantive preconditions are the two below and neither reads the contract's state. A cash-out request names a `period_start_date` and a `period_end_date`, and it draws on the contract's `DAILY_IMBALANCE` entries whose `gas_day` falls inside that period — on or after `period_start_date` and on or before `period_end_date`, both boundaries inclusive — and strictly after whatever `cash_out_through_date` the contract already carries. Every one of these comparisons is a plain ordering comparison over the `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar-date strings of §2.4, which for that format is chronological. The requested period must begin strictly after that settled-through date, so a period cannot be settled twice, and there must be at least one daily imbalance entry inside it or there is nothing to settle. For each gas day in scope, the *scheduled* volume is the sum of `confirmed_volume_dth` over that gas day's non-`SUPERSEDED` nominations on the contract, and the day's *imbalance* is the signed volume its daily entries carry.

Two figures are shared by both settlement methods. A **tolerance** is granted at 2 percent of a scheduled volume: it is that scheduled volume multiplied by 2, divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and a deviation inside it is not billed. A **penalty multiplier** is then read from the billable deviation expressed as a percentage of the scheduled volume: 1.00 when that percentage is 5 or less, 1.25 when it is greater than 5 but no more than 10, and 1.50 when it exceeds 10. Both boundaries are inclusive at the top of their band. When the scheduled volume the percentage would be measured against is zero, no division is attempted: the percentage is 0 if the billable volume is zero as well, and otherwise it is treated as lying above the 10 percent band so that the 1.50 multiplier applies.

The contract's `cash_out_method` decides how those pieces are assembled, and the two methods produce genuinely different totals from identical history.

Under `MONTHLY_AGGREGATE`, the period is netted first and priced once. The scheduled total is the sum of the scheduled volumes across the period's gas days, and the net imbalance is the sum of the signed daily imbalances across those same days, so a day short and a day long cancel each other. The billable volume is the absolute value of that net imbalance less the tolerance computed on the scheduled total, floored at zero. The percentage is the billable volume divided by the scheduled total, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and it selects a single multiplier for the whole period. The assessed amount is the billable volume multiplied by the contract's `index_price_usd_per_dth`, multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Under `DAILY_RATCHET`, each gas day is priced independently and the results are summed, and the rounding order is pinned. For each gas day in the period, the billable volume is the absolute value of that day's imbalance less the tolerance computed on that day's scheduled volume, floored at zero; the day's percentage is that billable volume divided by that day's scheduled volume, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; and the day's amount is the day's billable volume multiplied by `index_price_usd_per_dth`, multiplied by the multiplier that day's own percentage selects, **rounded half up to 2 decimal places at that point**. The assessment is the sum of those already-rounded daily amounts, itself rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Summing unrounded daily products and rounding once at the end is forbidden and yields a total that differs in cents. Offsetting days do not cancel under this method — each day's absolute deviation is billed — and each day receives its own tolerance and its own multiplier band.

Whichever method applied, the settlement stamps `cash_out_amount_usd` with the assessed amount, sets `cash_out_through_date` to the `period_end_date`, appends exactly one `CASH_OUT` ledger entry with a null `gas_day` and an `amount_usd` equal to the assessment, and then reconciles `imbalance_dth` against that newly settled-through date exactly as §2.9 prescribes. That reconciliation leaves `imbalance_dth` at 0.000 when every `DAILY_IMBALANCE` entry the contract holds falls on or before the new `cash_out_through_date`, and leaves the entries for later gas days standing in the figure when it does not — settling one period never discards a daily imbalance that lies outside it. The shipper's `outstanding_penalty_usd` is then recomputed across all of its contracts and its `credit_status` re-evaluated as described in §2.9.

Credit suspension is the cascade that makes cash-out consequential. When the recomputed exposure reaches the shipper's `penalty_threshold_usd`, `credit_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and every `ACTIVE` contract of that shipper — not merely the one settled — moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`. On each suspended contract, every `DRAFT` and `SUBMITTED` nomination becomes `REJECTED`, and every `CONFIRMED` nomination whose `gas_day` is today or later and which has no confirmed movement ticket is curtailed to a `confirmed_volume_dth` of 0 with a `CURTAILMENT_CREDIT` entry appended for each, exactly as phase A of §2.8 prescribes in its credit-suspension form. Each affected contract's scheduled volumes are recomputed and its `version` increments by one. While a shipper's credit is suspended, contract activation and every nomination step for that shipper is refused with `ERR_SHIPPER_CREDIT_SUSPENDED`.

Reversal restores the money but not the schedule. A cash-out reversal carries a `reason` and requires an unreversed `CASH_OUT` entry to exist on the contract; where several unreversed cash-outs have accumulated it always targets the most recent of them, so repeated reversals unwind the settlement history newest first and each one restores the settled-through date that its own cash-out displaced. It appends one `CASH_OUT_REVERSAL` ledger entry whose `amount_usd` is the negation of that cash-out's amount and whose `origin_note` is the only place the supplied `reason` is ever retained — no field of the contract records it — resets `cash_out_amount_usd` to 0.00 rather than to the amount of any earlier cash-out that still stands, restores `cash_out_through_date` to the value it held before the cash-out being reversed, and recomputes `imbalance_dth` — which, because the reversal removes the settlement boundary, brings the previously settled daily entries back into the unsettled position. The shipper's `outstanding_penalty_usd` recomputes to exactly its pre-cash-out figure and its `credit_status` is re-evaluated. If credit returns to `GOOD`, every `SUSPENDED` contract of that shipper moves back from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` and becomes nominatable again. Nominations that were curtailed or rejected by the suspension are **not** restored: only the money quantity is conserved through a reversal, never the schedule.

### 3.8. Emergency Response and Evacuation Coordination

Leak detection and continuous monitoring feed the emergency channel, which either a `FieldOperator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may open. An event is recorded against the affected reservoir's `storage_field_id` with an `event_type` of `LEAK`, `OVERPRESSURE`, `WELLHEAD_FAILURE` or `THIRD_PARTY_DAMAGE`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL`, and the `detected_at` instant at which detection or monitoring flagged it. A `well_id` may optionally be supplied to implicate a specific wellbore and is left null for a field-wide event; when supplied it must name a wellbore on the same reservoir, and a wellbore elsewhere is reported as though it did not exist. A new event opens at `status` `DETECTED` with `evacuation_radius_m` at 0, `responder_notes` null, and both `contained_at` and `closed_at` null.

Response then advances along a fixed plan, each step carrying the requested `to_status` and optionally `responder_notes` that overwrite the latest responder narrative. Moving from `DETECTED` to `RESPONDING` is the point at which the reservoir stops being commercially available: `field_posture` is forced to `FROZEN` regardless of what its other inputs say, and phase A of the curtailment sweep runs in its frozen form, taking firm and interruptible nominations alike for every gas day from today forward.

Ordering an evacuation is the most destructive step in the system, and it is permitted only from `RESPONDING`, only on an event whose `severity` is `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL`, and only with an `evacuation_radius_m` supplied between 1 and 8000 metres inclusive; a `MINOR` event, a missing radius or a radius outside those bounds is refused with `ERR_EVACUATION_RADIUS_INVALID`. When it succeeds, the radius is stored and two cascades fire. Every remediation program on that reservoir's wellbores that still stands `OPEN` becomes `ESCALATED` and is thereby permanently unclosable, with the affected wellbores left at `integrity_state` `MIT_FAILED`. And every compressor unit on the reservoir becomes `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, with each of its `SCHEDULED` runs moving to `CANCELLED` without burning fuel and each cancellation lowering the unit's `scheduled_run_count` by one, as described in §3.3. This second cascade is unconditional over the reservoir's units rather than narrowed to the ones it actually changes: a unit that already stood `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and carried no scheduled run at all is still written, so its `version` increments by one along with the rest.

Containment may be reached from either `RESPONDING` or `EVACUATION_ORDERED`. In both cases `contained_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the reservoir's posture is recomputed from scratch — which is how a field leaves `FROZEN` once no event on it is responding or evacuating, and how it lands in `RESTRICTED` rather than `OPEN` if a wellbore on it is still `MIT_FAILED` or if its pressure still sits above the posture ceiling. Containment does not by itself restore anything that was curtailed.

Closing an event is the final step, permitted only from `CONTAINED`, and it is the coordination gate: no remediation program on that reservoir's wellbores may stand `ESCALATED`, because an escalated integrity finding means the field has unfinished business no matter how quiet the leak has become. That gate is declared on the closure edge itself rather than as a business rule of its own, so a blocked closure answers `ERR_INVALID_EMERGENCY_STATE` at rung 7 of §2.11 and never a dedicated identifier. Closure stamps `closed_at` and makes the event terminal. Emergency events can be read individually and listed with filters on `storage_field_id`, `well_id`, `status` and `severity`.

### 3.9. Regulatory Reporting on Inventory and Integrity

The agency requires two kinds of filing, and both are prepared by a `ComplianceOfficer` against one reservoir. A report names the `storage_field_id`, a `report_type` of either `INVENTORY_VERIFICATION` or `INTEGRITY_PROGRAM_STATUS`, the reporting period bounded by `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` (the latter on or after the former), and the `measured_inventory_dth`, an independently measured working gas volume greater than zero. At most one non-`REJECTED` report may exist for a given reservoir, report type and period pair; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD`.

Preparation takes a snapshot and computes the variance that the agency cares about. `book_inventory_dth` captures the reservoir's `working_gas_inventory_dth` at that moment, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `variance_dth` is the measured inventory less that book inventory, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and may be negative when the books claim more gas than the survey found. `variance_pct` is the absolute value of that variance divided by the book inventory, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; when the book inventory is exactly 0.000 no division is attempted, and `variance_pct` is 0.0000 if the variance is zero too and otherwise exactly 100.5000 — a figure deliberately outside the tolerance of rung 38, so a report prepared against an empty reservoir and a non-zero survey can never be submitted. `open_finding_count` records how many remediation programs on that reservoir's wellbores stand in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` status. The report opens at `status` `DRAFT`, with `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID` and `submitted_by_user_id`, `agency_outcome` and `agency_response_note` all null.

Submission requires a second officer. The acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the report's `prepared_by_user_id`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SOD_REMEDIATION_SELF_VERIFICATION` — the officer who prepared a filing may never be the one who files it. Two substantive gates follow. The reported variance must be within tolerance: `variance_pct` must be no greater than 0.5000 percent, compared on the 4-decimal rounded value with the boundary inclusive, and a wider variance is refused with `ERR_REPORT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`. That comparison is made against the `variance_pct` the preparation computed, which submission never recomputes, however far the reservoir's `working_gas_inventory_dth` has moved since the snapshot was taken. And a filing of type `INTEGRITY_PROGRAM_STATUS` may only go out when the reservoir has no open integrity work: `open_finding_count` is refreshed at submission to the current number of `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` remediation programs on the reservoir's wellbores — a refresh that happens on a report of either type, since it is the gate and not the refresh that is confined to `INTEGRITY_PROGRAM_STATUS`, so an `INVENTORY_VERIFICATION` filing also carries the freshened count on its record while remaining ungated by it — and must be zero, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_OPEN_INTEGRITY_FINDINGS`. A successful submission moves the report from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting user, and **locks the covered gas days**: while the report stands `SUBMITTED`, no gas movement ticket whose nomination `gas_day` falls in the reported period may be reversed on that reservoir.

The agency's disposition is recorded afterwards with an `agency_outcome` of either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` plus an optional `agency_response_note`; the note is stored on either outcome, and when it is omitted whatever narrative the record already carried is left in place rather than cleared. An `ACCEPTED` disposition moves the report from `SUBMITTED` to the terminal `ACCEPTED` status and the covered gas days stay locked permanently — an accepted filing can never be undermined by a later reversal. A `REJECTED` disposition moves the report from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, stores the agency's narrative, and unlocks the covered gas days so that the underlying measurements can be corrected. A rejected report is not terminal: it may be submitted again, moving from `REJECTED` back to `SUBMITTED`, and that re-submission is held to the same second-officer rule and the same variance tolerance as the original, re-locking the covered gas days on success. Re-submission clears neither `agency_outcome` nor `agency_response_note`, so a report can legitimately stand `SUBMITTED` while still carrying the `REJECTED` disposition and narrative of the round before; and the officer the second-officer rule bars is still the one named in `prepared_by_user_id`, never the one who submitted the round the agency rejected. Reports filed for a reservoir are read through that reservoir's own report channel, which accepts filters on `report_type` and `status`.
