# Apparel Rental Subscription Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fashion rental subscription business lives or dies on a single uncomfortable fact: the thing it sells is a finite pile of physical garments, and every member who wants a gown for Saturday is competing for the same hanger as every other member. This document specifies the backend service that runs that competition honestly. It covers the whole circuit a garment travels — bought into the catalog against a depth plan, matched to a member request, shipped, worn, returned, inspected, dry-cleaned, repaired, re-photographed, restocked, and eventually retired and liquidated on a resale channel — together with the money that moves alongside it in the form of subscription revenue attribution, late-return fees, damage assessments, waiver credits and dispute reversals.

Two problems dominate the design. The first is allocation: a physical unit is only rentable when both its logistics position and its physical condition say so, and those two dimensions are advanced by different people at different times, so the matching engine must read a *product* of two independent state machines rather than a single status column. The second is reconciliation: the same garment's economics must be readable two different ways — what it has actually earned and cost so far, and what the buying plan assumed it would earn and cost — and the two numbers must be derivable from the same stored rows without one contaminating the other. Layered on top are the operational safety rules a warehouse actually needs: a biohazard quarantine that freezes reconditioning, a bounded repair retry loop, a segregation-of-duties rule that stops the inspecting technician from signing off their own findings, and a bounded dispute cycle that lets a member argue a damage fee without letting them argue it forever.

Throughout, two quantities are treated as conserved and are audited after every mutation: the count of physical unit positions for a given style and size, and each member's outstanding fee balance. Every forward action in this specification that consumes one of those quantities has a compensating action that returns it exactly, and any implementation that lets either drift is incorrect regardless of how well individual endpoints behave. All resources are mounted flat at the root of the service.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including read-only ones, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the actor is claiming. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails at the very first rung of the validation ladder (rank 1) with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`. If `X-User-Role` is present but is not one of the five declared roles, the request fails at rank 2 with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. Both of these are 401 responses.

The five roles are `Member`, `WarehouseTech`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `InventoryPlanner` and `PlatformAdmin`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `Member` is a subscriber who reserves garments, swaps them, initiates returns, rates fit and disputes damage fees, and who sees only their own resources.
- A `WarehouseTech` is the reverse-logistics operator who receives inbound legs and then inspects, cleans, repairs, clears quarantine, photographs and restocks units.
- A `ClaimsAdjuster` is the damage and loss adjudicator who assesses, waives, charges and resolves disputes, and who never touches physical reconditioning.
- An `InventoryPlanner` is the merchandising and asset owner who acquires styles and units, plans depth, retires units and runs resale recovery.
- A `PlatformAdmin` provisions plans, technicians and memberships and drives the logical clock, but cannot reserve, inspect, adjudicate or merchandise.

Two of these roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's `member_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `WarehouseTech`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; the reverse-logistics rules that read certifications, employment status and prior involvement all resolve the acting technician by that header value. For both of these roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing `Member` row or `Technician` row respectively is rejected at rank 3 with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND` (401), before any permission check runs.

The remaining three roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `InventoryPlanner` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such a value for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and it is never subjected to the rank-3 actor-existence check.

Permission itself is checked at rank 4: if the acting role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called (see §4), the response is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Ownership is checked immediately afterwards at rank 5, and applies to every endpoint a `Member` may call: a `Member` may read or mutate only those members, reservations, shipments belonging to their own reservations, damage claims, fit-feedback records and fee-ledger entries whose owning member is themselves. A `Member` acting on another member's resource receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Because rank 4 precedes rank 5, a role that is simply not allowed on an endpoint learns that fact rather than leaking whether the target resource belongs to someone else.

Three further authorization rules are specific to warehouse work and are described where they belong, in §2.10 and §3.6: suspension of the acting technician, the certification requirements for repair, quarantine clearance and photography, and the segregation-of-duties rule between inspecting and repairing.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside the range from 1 to 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is rejected at rank 10 of the validation ladder with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest record appears first and page boundaries remain stable as new records are appended. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching record remains beyond the current page.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, listed per endpoint in §4. A filter whose value is an enum-typed field — for example a state filter, a `category`, a `direction`, a `damage_severity`, a `fit_rating`, a `kind`, a `channel`, a `status`, an `availability_state`, a `condition_state` or a `damage_risk_band` — must carry a declared member of that enum; anything else is rejected at rank 9 with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics: clients must treat them as opaque and must never derive a resource's kind from the shape of its identifier.

Most resources also carry three housekeeping properties. `created_at` is the instant the row was first persisted, `updated_at` is the instant of its most recent mutation, and both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations applied to it as a downstream effect of an operation aimed at some other resource. Whenever a rule in this document says that a state change "bumps the version" of an entity, it also means that the entity's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant.

Three record types in this system are immutable events rather than mutable resources, and they deliberately carry a reduced property set: an inspection record, a fit-feedback record and a fee-ledger entry each carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those three, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely — they are never emitted and never accepted. Everything else — membership plans, technicians, styles, rental units, members, reservations, shipments, reverse-logistics jobs, damage claims and resale listings — carries all four housekeeping properties.

All date and instant comparisons in this specification read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it: an admin-only test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and sets the clock to that value, echoing the new `current_utc` back. Every threshold in §2 and §3 that mentions "the current clock", "today", a grace period, a dispute window, a listing window or a rolling twelve-month window is evaluated against this logical clock at the moment the request is processed. Where a rule compares calendar dates rather than instants, the date is the UTC calendar date of the instant in question, and the arithmetic is whole-day integer arithmetic with no partial days.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and the Validation Ladder

Requests are validated against a single, globally ordered ladder of rules. The ladder is evaluated in ascending rank order; the **first** rule that fails determines the response, no later rule is consulted, and **no side effect of any kind is applied** — no state change, no cascade, no ledger entry, no version bump. This ordering is not cosmetic: several rules in this system would produce different error identifiers for the same request, and the rank decides which one the client sees.

The opening rungs of the ladder are cross-cutting and apply to essentially every request. Ranks 1 through 5 are the authentication and authorization rules of §2.1, in the order: missing headers, unknown role, actor not found, role not permitted, resource not owned.

Rank 6 resolves path identifiers. Nested paths are validated **strictly left to right**, so the outermost identifier is resolved before the one nested inside it and the client always learns about the outermost failure first. The identifiers are `plan_id` for a membership plan, `technician_id` for a technician, `style_id` for a style, `unit_id` for a rental unit, `member_id` for a member, `reservation_id` for a reservation, `shipment_id` for a shipment leg, `job_id` for a reverse-logistics job, `inspection_id` for an inspection record, `claim_id` for a damage claim, `listing_id` for a resale listing, `entry_id` for a fee-ledger entry and `feedback_id` for a fit-feedback record. An unresolvable identifier yields 404 with the error matching the missing resource kind: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RENTAL_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FEE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

Rank 7 resolves foreign keys supplied in a request body, with the same 404 vocabulary. The body-borne references this covers are `membership_plan_id`, `style_id`, `reservation_id`, `rental_unit_id`, `preferred_unit_id` and `supersedes_listing_id`; a `preferred_unit_id` that does not name an existing rental unit yields `ERR_RENTAL_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, and a `supersedes_listing_id` that does not name an existing listing yields `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`.

Rank 8 checks request shape on every write endpoint: all required body fields must be present and must carry the declared JSON type, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Rank 9 checks that every enum-typed body field, and every enum-typed list filter, carries a declared member value, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422). Rank 10 is the pagination check of §2.3. Rank 11 enforces the system's five uniqueness constraints — a plan's `code`, a technician's `employee_code`, a style's `style_code`, a rental unit's `unit_code` and a member's `email` must each be free at creation time — and rejects a collision with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409).

Ranks 12 and 13 are the optimistic-concurrency checks described in §2.6. Rank 14 is the membership-active check described in §3.2. Ranks 15 and 16 are the two generic state guards, and their order matters: rank 15 rejects a request whose trigger is not a declared transition out of the target resource's **current, non-terminal** state with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), while rank 16 rejects an action aimed at a resource that already sits in one of its declared terminal states with `ERR_RESOURCE_TERMINAL` (409). Rank 15 deliberately abstains when the current state is terminal, leaving that case to rank 16, so that a reservation already in `CLOSED` reports terminality rather than an undeclared transition. Rank 16 applies to action endpoints on reservations, shipments, reverse-logistics jobs, damage claims, resale listings and members.

Everything above rank 16 is domain-specific. Those rules are introduced in §3 alongside the workflow each one guards, and each is stated there with its explicit rank so that when several of them apply to the same request the evaluation order is unambiguous. As a general shape, the warehouse safety and reconditioning-integrity rules occupy ranks 20 through 28, the bounded-cycle and referential-integrity rules occupy ranks 29 through 46, and the fiscal rules — slot exhaustion, swap allowance, waiver availability, charge eligibility, settlement size, sale-price floor, outstanding balance and units-still-out — occupy ranks 50 through 57, so a fiscal objection is always reported after every structural, safety and temporal objection that also applies.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Two Repeatable Writes

Most write operations in this system are guarded by state preconditions: a reservation can only be released while it is `MATCHED`, a listing can only be sold while it is `LISTED`, and so a second attempt naturally fails on state. Two operations are different, because the product deliberately allows them to happen more than once against the same parent, so a state precondition cannot serialize them. Those two are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the **parent** resource's `version` instead.

The first is the swap. Swapping supersedes the member's current reservation and releases its unit before allocating a replacement, and a member on a generous plan may legitimately swap again and again. To keep the supersede chain well defined, a swap request must carry `expected_member_version` as a required, non-optional body field, holding the `version` of the `Member` the client last observed. When `expected_member_version` does not equal the member's current `version` — because some other writer has advanced that member since the client read it — the swap is rejected at rank 12 with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` and HTTP status 412, and nothing is superseded, released or allocated. A successful swap increments the member's `version` by 1, so a client that wants to swap twice must re-read the member in between.

The second is resale-listing creation. Creating a listing that supersedes an expired listing on the same retired unit is a legitimate operation the product permits repeatedly, so it is guarded by the unit's version rather than by listing state. A create request must carry `expected_rental_unit_version` as a required, non-optional body field, holding the `version` of the target `RentalUnit` the client last observed. A mismatch with the unit's current `version` is rejected at rank 13 with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412; a successful create increments the unit's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be required to supply one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. Money, Rounding and the Two Conserved Quantities

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents, and every field carrying money is named with a `_cents` suffix. Wherever a calculation multiplies money by a rate or divides it, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value exactly on the half boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Rounding happens only at the points each formula in §3 names explicitly; intermediate values that a formula does not mark as a rounding step are carried at full precision into the next step, and re-rounding an already-rounded value is not permitted. The fee ledger itself involves no rounding at all: it stores and sums exact integer cents.

Not every quantity uses half-up rounding. Depth planning rounds **up to the next whole unit** (a ceiling, never a half-up round), because half a garment cannot be bought. Scores are integers: the fit-confidence score is clamped into the range 0 to 100 at both ends, and the damage-risk score is rounded half up exactly once, on the final weighted sum, and lands in the range 0 to 100.

Two quantities in this system are **conserved**, and both are audited after every mutation that could disturb them.

The first is the **unit position count** for a given combination of style and size. For that pair, the total number of rental units counted across `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `OUT`, `INBOUND`, `PROCESSING`, `RETIRED` and `SOLD` must be identical before and after every allocation, every compensating release, every swap (which releases before it allocates), every delinquency cascade and every carrier-loss retirement — a unit never disappears, it only changes position. Additionally, when no shipment has moved, the combined count of units in `IN_STOCK` and `RESERVED` must also be unchanged: releasing a reservation moves a unit from `RESERVED` back to `IN_STOCK`, and allocating moves it the other way, but the pair's sum holds. A style's `floor_ready_unit_count` must equal the number of that style's units whose `availability_state` is `IN_STOCK` **and** whose `condition_state` is `FLOOR_READY`, and that equality must hold again once every hop of a cascade has completed, not merely at the end of the outermost hop. The carrier-loss path conserves positions too: when a leg's exceptions reach the cap, the unit leaves `OUT` or `INBOUND` and enters `RETIRED`, so the total is unchanged.

The second is the **member fee balance**. A member's `outstanding_fee_cents` must at all times equal the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every fee-ledger entry belonging to that member. Ledger entries are append-only: no entry is ever mutated and no entry is ever deleted, so a correction is always expressed as a new, offsetting entry. Each entry's `balance_after_cents` records the running signed sum over that member's entries ordered by `created_at` ascending, evaluated immediately after the entry was appended. The compensating-action invariant follows: for a claim that was charged and then overturned, the signed sum of that claim's own entries is exactly zero, so the member's `outstanding_fee_cents` returns to its exact pre-charge value and their `waiver_coverage_used_cents` returns to its exact pre-assessment value.

### 2.8. The Two-Dimensional Unit State Model

A `RentalUnit` does not have one status; it has two, and they advance independently. `availability_state` is the logistics dimension and takes one of `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `OUT`, `INBOUND`, `PROCESSING`, `RETIRED` or `SOLD`, starting at `IN_STOCK` when the unit is added to a style's depth, with `SOLD` as its only terminal value. `condition_state` is the physical dimension and takes one of `FLOOR_READY`, `NEEDS_CLEANING`, `NEEDS_REPAIR`, `QUARANTINED` or `CONDEMNED`, starting at `FLOOR_READY`, with `CONDEMNED` as its only terminal value.

Different actors drive different dimensions. A member's reservation and a warehouse dispatch move `availability_state`; a technician's inspection and reconditioning sign-offs move `condition_state`. The allocation engine reads the product of both: a unit is allocatable **only** when its `availability_state` is `IN_STOCK` **and** its `condition_state` is `FLOOR_READY`. Reconditioning moves `condition_state` while `availability_state` sits at `PROCESSING`, and retirement moves `availability_state` while `condition_state` may sit at `FLOOR_READY` (a planned refresh of a perfectly good garment) or at `CONDEMNED` (a garment destroyed in service). A unit that is physically idle but sits in any other combination is simply not a candidate, and no amount of business urgency changes that.

Because `condition_state` is separate from grade, a unit also carries a `condition_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`. The grade defaults to `A` at intake unless the planner supplies another value, and it is overwritten by every inspection that touches the unit. Grade drives money — residual value on a damage claim and resale price on a listing — while `condition_state` drives eligibility. The two must not be conflated.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Recomputation Duties

Consistency in this system is **immediate**. When an operation triggers downstream effects, all of them are applied within the same request, in the order stated, before the response is emitted; a client never observes a half-applied cascade, and there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation pass. Where a workflow in §3 numbers its cascade hops, that numbering is the required order of application, because later hops read values written by earlier ones — the delinquency check, for example, must read a fee balance that already includes the entry appended by the hop before it.

Every entity a cascade touches has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. A single request may therefore bump the versions of several resources: a warehouse receipt advances a shipment, a reservation, a rental unit and possibly a member, and each of those version counters moves. Derived values are never stale in a response: any computed field of any entity touched by a cascade is recomputed before the response is written, including a style's `floor_ready_unit_count`, `active_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units`, `replenishment_flag` and `demand_score`; a member's `outstanding_fee_cents`, `units_out_count`, `swaps_used_current_month`, `late_return_count`, `fit_confidence_score`, `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band`; a unit's `cumulative_servicing_cost_cents`, `realized_revenue_cents` and `occupied_until_date`; and a reservation's `waitlist_position`.

Cascades in this system reach as far as five hops. A damage charge, for instance, moves the claim, appends one or two ledger entries, may tip the member into delinquency, may cancel and release that member's reservations, and may thereby return units to stock and refresh their styles' depth fields — all in one request. Compensating actions are equally thorough: a release returns the unit, recomputes the style, and offers the freed unit to the waitlist in the same breath.

### 2.10. Warehouse Duty Segregation and Technician Fitness

Reverse logistics is staffed by technicians, each carrying an `employee_code`, a `full_name`, two independent certification flags and an employment `status`. `repair_certified` defaults to false and, when true, permits the technician to sign off repair completion and to clear a biohazard quarantine. `photography_certified` defaults to false and, when true, permits the technician to sign off re-photography. `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaults to `ACTIVE`, and either value may be set at creation time; only an `ACTIVE` technician may act on reverse-logistics work at all.

These three properties produce three rules that sit near each other on the validation ladder and must be evaluated in the stated order. At rank 25, on a repair sign-off, the acting technician's identifier must differ from the identifier of the technician who filed the job's inspection; if they are the same person, the response is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). At rank 26, the required certification must be present: repair completion and quarantine clearance require `repair_certified` to be true, and photography requires `photography_certified` to be true, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (403). At rank 27, on every endpoint a `WarehouseTech` may call, the acting technician's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED` (403). Because rank 25 precedes rank 26 and rank 26 precedes rank 27, a suspended technician who is also the inspector of the job and who lacks the certification learns about the duty conflict first.

A `PlatformAdmin` provisions technicians and may later revise `repair_certified`, `photography_certified` or `status` on an existing technician record. Revoking a certification has no retroactive effect on work already signed off; it only governs future sign-offs.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Catalog Acquisition, Depth Planning and Fleet Build-Out

Before anything can be rented, a `PlatformAdmin` defines the commercial tiers members subscribe to. A plan carries a `code` that is unique across all plans and is written in uppercase alphanumeric characters plus the hyphen, at least 3 and at most 24 characters long, alongside a human-facing `name`. Four numbers define what the tier entitles a member to: `item_slots`, the maximum number of garment units the member may hold or have committed at one time, which must be at least 1 and at most 8; `monthly_fee_cents`, the subscription fee charged per membership month, which may be zero but never negative; `monthly_swaps_allowed`, the number of swap operations permitted per UTC calendar month, which may be zero; and `shipping_cadence_days`, the minimum spacing in days between two outbound shipments for the same member, which must be at least 1. Two further numbers describe the optional damage waiver: `waiver_monthly_fee_cents`, the monthly price of the waiver, and `waiver_coverage_cents`, the lifetime damage-waiver coverage cap available to a member on this tier. Both may be zero and neither may be negative. A plan whose `code` is already taken is refused at rank 11 with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Merchandising is the `InventoryPlanner`'s domain. Acquiring a style into the rental catalog records a `style_code` unique across styles and between 3 and 32 characters long, the `brand` label, and a `category` drawn from `GOWN`, `BLAZER`, `DRESS`, `KNITWEAR` and `ACCESSORY`. Two prices are captured at acquisition and they serve different purposes: `retail_price_cents`, the manufacturer retail price, is the base for all residual-value and resale arithmetic, while `acquisition_cost_cents` on the style is a *reference* wholesale cost per unit used only by the forward-looking economics variant. Both must be strictly greater than zero. The `size_run` is a non-empty array of size strings drawn from `XS`, `S`, `M`, `L` and `XL`, and it is the gate every reservation's requested size must pass. Finally, `expected_rental_turns_per_unit` records how many completed rental turns each unit of the style is planned to deliver before retirement and must be at least 1 — it is a hard allocation ceiling, not an aspiration. A style whose `style_code` collides with an existing one is refused at rank 11 with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, and an unresolvable style identifier anywhere yields `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND`.

Four more inputs are pure planning assumptions and each defaults to 0 when the planner omits it: `planned_depth_units`, how many units the planner intends to own; `planned_eol_value_cents`, the planned end-of-life resale recovery per unit; `forecast_monthly_requests`, the expected number of reservation requests per thirty-day planning month; and `avg_rental_days`, the assumed number of days a unit is committed per turn. None may be negative. `planned_depth_units` is recorded for merchandising reference only and is never enforced against the actual fleet.

From the forecast the service derives the style's true depth requirement, and it does so on style creation, on every revision of the depth plan, and after every cascade hop that changes any unit's `availability_state`. The derivation runs as follows. First, the committed garment-days per planning month are the forecast request count multiplied by the sum of the assumed rental days and the two-day recondition buffer that every returning garment consumes before it can go out again. Next, `required_depth_units` is that committed-day figure divided by the thirty-day planning month and **rounded up to the next whole unit** — this step is always a ceiling and never a half-up round, so 60 committed garment-days give exactly 2 required units while 61 give 3. Then `active_unit_count` is the number of the style's rental units whose `availability_state` is anything other than `RETIRED` or `SOLD`; a unit's `condition_state` is irrelevant to this count, so a filthy unit awaiting cleaning still counts as active depth. `depth_gap_units` is the shortfall of active units against required units, floored at zero so a style that is over-bought reports a gap of zero rather than a negative number. `replenishment_flag` is true when `depth_gap_units` is at least one unit — that is, the moment the fleet is even a single garment short of the requirement, the style is flagged for buying.

A separate computed count, `floor_ready_unit_count`, answers the narrower question of how much of the fleet could ship today: it is the number of the style's units that are simultaneously `IN_STOCK` and `FLOOR_READY`. It is deliberately not the same number as `active_unit_count`, and the difference between them is precisely the fleet that is committed, in transit or under reconditioning.

Revising the plan later is a targeted operation: the planner may supply any combination of `forecast_monthly_requests`, `avg_rental_days` and `planned_depth_units`, and omitted fields keep their stored values. Whatever changes, the whole derivation above re-runs and `demand_score` is refreshed as well.

The **demand score** is the merchandising signal that tells a planner which styles members actually want, and it is refreshed whenever a member creates a reservation, swaps into a style, or files fit feedback. It sums three contributions: four points for every reservation ever raised against the style whose `state` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, three points for every fit-feedback record on the style whose `keep_intent` is true, and one point for each point of `occasion_match_score` across every fit-feedback record on the style. The total is capped at 100, so a runaway hit style saturates rather than growing without bound. Styles can be browsed with an optional `category` filter and an optional `replenishment_flag` filter, which together give the planner a buying worklist.

Physical depth is built one garment at a time. Adding a rental unit to a style records a `unit_code` that is unique across the entire fleet and between 3 and 40 characters long, and a `size` which must be one of the values in the parent style's `size_run`. The planner may supply `acquisition_cost_cents` for the unit, the actual landed cost paid for this specific garment, which must be strictly positive; when omitted it is taken from the parent style's reference `acquisition_cost_cents`. The unit's own landed cost, never the style's reference figure, is what the realized economics variant charges against it. The planner may also supply an opening `condition_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`; when omitted the unit opens at grade `A`. A duplicate `unit_code` is refused at rank 11 with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

A newly added unit opens `IN_STOCK` and `FLOOR_READY`, with `turns_completed` at 0 and `quarantine_hold` false, and it is therefore immediately allocatable. Its `last_returned_at`, `retired_at` and `retirement_reason` are null until the corresponding events occur, and its `occupied_until_date` is null until something is booked against it. Adding a unit refreshes the parent style's `active_unit_count` and `floor_ready_unit_count` and then re-derives `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`, so a planner buying against a replenishment flag watches it clear in the create response. The fleet can be browsed with optional `style_id`, `size`, `availability_state` and `condition_state` filters — the last two being exactly the two independent dimensions of §2.8, so an operator can ask for everything `IN_STOCK` but `NEEDS_REPAIR` and get the list of garments that look available and are not.

### 3.2. Membership Lifecycle and Plan Entitlements

Enrolment is an administrative act. A `PlatformAdmin` creates a member against a plan by supplying `membership_plan_id`, an `email` that must contain an at-sign and be unique across all members, and a `full_name`. An email already in use is refused at rank 11 with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, and a plan identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. Three optional inputs shape the membership from the start: `waiver_active`, which defaults to false and states whether the member pays for the damage waiver and may therefore consume the plan's coverage; `preferred_categories`, an array of merchandising categories the member wants surfaced, drawn from the same category vocabulary as a style's `category` and defaulting to the empty array; and `size_profile`, a free-form declared primary garment size such as `"M"`, which defaults to the empty string and may be at most 8 characters long. A newly enrolled member opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, a `fit_confidence_score` seeded at the neutral baseline of 50, a `damage_risk_score` of 0 and a `damage_risk_band` of `LOW`, and a `waiver_coverage_used_cents` of 0.

A member may revise their own styling preferences at any time, supplying either or both of `preferred_categories` and `size_profile`; omitted fields keep their stored values. It is worth being explicit about what `size_profile` does *not* do: it is echoed back on the personalization read and is never used as an allocation filter. The only size that governs matching is the size the member asks for on the individual reservation.

The membership `status` takes one of `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `DELINQUENT` or `CANCELLED`, and `CANCELLED` is terminal. Being `ACTIVE` is a precondition for a great deal, and rank 14 enforces it on exactly five operations: creating a reservation, swapping a reservation, initiating a return, creating a shipping leg, and dispatching an outbound leg. In all five the rejection is `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), whether the member is `PAUSED`, `DELINQUENT` or `CANCELLED`.

Pausing is a member-initiated (or admin-initiated) `ACTIVE → PAUSED` move, and it requires that the member be holding nothing: their `units_out_count` — the number of their reservations sitting in `MATCHED`, `SHIPPED`, `IN_POSSESSION` or `RETURN_INITIATED` — must be exactly zero. A member with anything committed or in the field is refused at rank 57 with `ERR_UNITS_STILL_OUT`. A pause bumps the member's `version` by 1. A paused member keeps any `WAITLISTED` reservations they already have, but cannot create new ones, cannot swap, and cannot have anything shipped. Resuming is the mirror `PAUSED → ACTIVE` move with no further precondition beyond the current status, and it also bumps `version`; notably a resume does **not** reset `swaps_used_current_month`, so a member cannot launder their swap allowance by pausing and resuming inside the same calendar month.

Delinquency is never requested; it is inflicted by the ledger. When a fee-ledger entry is appended — a damage fee posted by a charge, or a late-return fee posted by a warehouse receipt — the member's recomputed `outstanding_fee_cents` is compared against the delinquency threshold of 15,000 cents, that is 150 dollars. If the member's `status` is `ACTIVE` and the recomputed balance is at or above that threshold, the member moves `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` as the third hop of that ledger append, bumping `version`. The fourth hop then unwinds the member's forward commitments: every reservation of theirs in `WAITLISTED` moves `WAITLISTED → CANCELLED`, and every reservation in `MATCHED` moves `MATCHED → RELEASED`. The fifth hop returns each unit freed by the fourth from `RESERVED` back to `IN_STOCK`, after which the affected styles' depth fields are re-derived. A reservation already in `IN_POSSESSION` is deliberately left alone — the garment is on the member's body and the system does not pretend otherwise.

Recovery is symmetrical in money but not in inventory. When a settlement payment is recorded, or when a disputed claim is overturned, the member's balance is recomputed; if the member's `status` is `DELINQUENT` and the recomputed `outstanding_fee_cents` is now strictly below the 15,000-cent threshold, the member moves `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`, bumping `version`. Reservations cancelled or released by the delinquency cascade are **not** resurrected. The conserved quantity here is the fee balance, not the reservation set.

Cancellation ends the membership and can be requested from either `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, but only by a member who owes nothing: `outstanding_fee_cents` must be exactly zero, otherwise the request is refused at rank 56 with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_BALANCE`. The two branches differ in their cascade because the two states differ in what they can be holding. Cancelling from `ACTIVE` is the edge `ACTIVE → CANCELLED`: it sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, then as a second hop moves every `MATCHED` reservation of the member to `RELEASED` and every `WAITLISTED` reservation to `CANCELLED`; as a third hop returns every unit freed that way from `RESERVED` to `IN_STOCK`; and as a fourth hop re-derives `floor_ready_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag` on each style owning a touched unit. Cancelling from `PAUSED` is the edge `PAUSED → CANCELLED`: it sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and, as its second hop, moves every `WAITLISTED` reservation of the member to `CANCELLED`; there is no unit-release hop and no style-recomputation hop, because the pause precondition already guaranteed the member held no committed units. Either branch bumps the member's `version` by 1. Because `CANCELLED` is terminal, any subsequent action on the member is refused at rank 16 with `ERR_RESOURCE_TERMINAL`.

Beyond `outstanding_fee_cents` and `units_out_count`, a member record exposes several other derived figures that are always current when read. `swaps_used_current_month` counts the member's swap-created reservations inside the current UTC calendar month. `late_return_count` counts the member's reservations whose `late_days` is greater than zero. `waiver_coverage_used_cents` tracks how much of the plan's lifetime coverage the member has consumed and never exceeds `waiver_coverage_cents`; it is the sum of `waiver_applied_cents` across the member's claims whose `state` is `ASSESSED`, `DISPUTED`, `CHARGED` or `WAIVED`, which means coverage is consumed the instant a claim is assessed and is handed back when the claim leaves that set — an overturned dispute that lands the claim in `DISMISSED` restores it. `fit_confidence_score`, `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band` are described in §3.9. Members can be browsed by an administrator or adjuster with optional `membership_plan_id`, `status` and `damage_risk_band` filters.

### 3.3. The Availability Engine: Matching, Waitlisting and Swaps

A reservation is a member's request for a style over a rental window, and it is the point where subscription demand meets a finite hanger count. The member supplies the `style_id` they want, the `requested_size`, a `requested_start_date` and a `requested_end_date`, both UTC calendar days written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and optionally a `preferred_unit_id` naming a specific physical garment. The reservation's `member_id` is always taken from the `X-User-ID` header of the requesting member and is never read from the body, so a member cannot book on someone else's behalf. A reservation opens in `REQUESTED` and, within the very same request, is resolved either into `MATCHED` or into `WAITLISTED` — `REQUESTED` is never observable as a resting state. The full state vocabulary of a reservation is `REQUESTED`, `MATCHED`, `WAITLISTED`, `SHIPPED`, `IN_POSSESSION`, `RETURN_INITIATED`, `RETURNED`, `CLOSED`, `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED`, of which `CLOSED`, `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

The window itself is interpreted half-open: `requested_start_date` is the first day the member wants the garment and is inclusive, while `requested_end_date` is the return day and is the exclusive upper bound of the occupation interval. Two temporal rules police it. At rank 40, the end date must be strictly later than the start date and the window length — the number of whole days from the start date to the end date — must be at least 3 days and at most 14 days; otherwise the response is `ERR_INVALID_RENTAL_WINDOW` (422). At rank 41, the start date must not be earlier than the current logical clock date, so a member may book for today or any future day but never for yesterday; otherwise the response is `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (422). Before either of those, at rank 31, the `requested_size` must be a member of the parent style's `size_run`, otherwise `ERR_SIZE_NOT_IN_SIZE_RUN` (422). All three rules apply identically to a reservation create and to a swap.

**How a unit is chosen.** The engine builds a candidate set over the style's rental units and then ranks it. A unit enters the candidate set only when every one of the following holds: it belongs to the requested style; its `size` equals the `requested_size`; its `availability_state` is `IN_STOCK`; its `condition_state` is `FLOOR_READY`; its `quarantine_hold` is false; its `turns_completed` is strictly less than the style's `expected_rental_turns_per_unit`; and it has no interval collision with the requested window. Units in any other availability or condition combination are excluded even when they are sitting physically idle on a rack.

Interval collision is where the recondition buffer becomes visible. Each unit's occupied intervals are drawn from every reservation holding that unit whose `state` is anything other than `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED` — and that deliberately includes reservations already in `CLOSED`, because the two-day recondition buffer outlives the turn that earned it. For each such prior reservation, the occupied interval runs from its `requested_start_date` inclusive up to its `requested_end_date` extended on the right by 2 buffer days, exclusive. A collision exists when the new request's start date is earlier than that extended end **and** the new request's end date is later than the prior reservation's start date. Touching exactly at the buffer edge does not collide: a request whose `requested_start_date` equals the prior reservation's `requested_end_date` plus the two buffer days is accepted.

Among the candidates, the engine burns down the oldest remaining life first. The primary ranking is by `turns_completed` descending, so the unit that has already completed the most turns is preferred and the freshest garments are held back. Ties on `turns_completed` are broken by `created_at` ascending, so the unit that entered the fleet earliest wins. The unit's `id` is never consulted as a tie-breaker at any point, and an implementation that sorts by identifier will produce wrong allocations.

A `preferred_unit_id` short-circuits the ranking rather than the eligibility rules. If the named unit is in the candidate set, that unit is selected and the ranking expression is skipped entirely. If the named unit is not in the candidate set, the request is **rejected** rather than waitlisted, and the rejection reports the most specific failing reason: at rank 21, a unit whose `condition_state` is `CONDEMNED` yields `ERR_UNIT_CONDEMNED` (409); at rank 32, a unit that belongs to another style, carries a different size, is not simultaneously `IN_STOCK` and `FLOOR_READY`, or sits on quarantine hold yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE` (409); at rank 33, a unit whose `turns_completed` has already reached the style's `expected_rental_turns_per_unit` yields `ERR_UNIT_TURNS_EXHAUSTED` (409); and at rank 42, a unit whose existing commitments overlap the requested window under the buffer geometry above yields `ERR_UNIT_WINDOW_COLLISION` (409). Because 21 precedes 32 which precedes 33 which precedes 42, a condemned, over-turned, double-booked unit reports condemnation first.

When the candidate set is empty and the request named no `preferred_unit_id`, there is no error at all. The reservation resolves `REQUESTED → WAITLISTED`: its `allocated_unit_id` stays null, its `matched_at` stays null, and a `waitlist_position` is computed as the reservation's 1-based rank, ordered by `created_at` ascending, among the `WAITLISTED` reservations for the same combination of `style_id` and `requested_size`. The member's `units_out_count` is unchanged by a waitlisting, but the member's `version` is still bumped by 1. Outside the `WAITLISTED` state, `waitlist_position` is null.

When a unit is selected, the reservation resolves `REQUESTED → MATCHED`, stamping `allocated_unit_id` with the chosen unit and `matched_at` with the current logical clock instant. The second hop moves the chosen unit's `availability_state` from `IN_STOCK` to `RESERVED` and recomputes its `occupied_until_date`, which is the latest `requested_end_date` plus the two buffer days across all reservations holding that unit whose `state` is not `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and which is null when no such reservation exists. The third hop decrements the style's `floor_ready_unit_count` and re-derives `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`; the style's `demand_score` is refreshed as well, since a new request has been raised against it. The member's `units_out_count` is recomputed and their `version` is bumped by 1, and the unit's own `version` is bumped by 1.

Slots are the plan's throttle on all of this. At rank 50, before any unit is allocated, the member's `units_out_count` plus the count of their `WAITLISTED` reservations must be strictly less than the plan's `item_slots`; a member on a three-slot plan who already has two garments out and one on the waitlist is refused with `ERR_ITEM_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Note that a waitlisted request consumes a slot exactly as a held garment does, so a member cannot queue up unlimited future demand. Reservations can be listed with optional `member_id`, `style_id`, `allocated_unit_id`, `superseded_reservation_id` and `state` filters; a `Member` reading that list sees only their own rows.

**Giving a reservation up.** A member (or a `PlatformAdmin` acting for them) may abandon a reservation before it is dispatched, and the effect depends on which of the two pre-dispatch states it is in. A `MATCHED` reservation moves `MATCHED → RELEASED`; as a second hop its allocated unit moves `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` and the reservation's `allocated_unit_id` is set back to null; as a third hop the rank-1 `WAITLISTED` reservation for the freed unit's style and size is promoted `WAITLISTED → MATCHED` if one exists; and the member's `units_out_count` is recomputed while their `version` is bumped by 1. A `WAITLISTED` reservation instead moves `WAITLISTED → CANCELLED`, its `waitlist_position` is set to null, and every remaining `WAITLISTED` reservation for the same style and size has its `waitlist_position` recomputed so the queue compacts with no gaps. Both branches also re-derive the affected style's depth fields.

**Waitlist promotion** is the system's way of never letting a freed garment sit idle while someone is queued for it. It fires from three places: when a reconditioned unit is restocked, when a matched reservation is released, and when a unit retirement frees the style's queue. A promotion requires three things at once: the member owning the waitlisted reservation must be `ACTIVE`; the freed unit must satisfy the full eligibility filter above for that reservation, including the size match, the turns ceiling and the interval geometry; and the reservation must be rank 1 by `created_at` ascending among the `WAITLISTED` reservations for the same `style_id` and `requested_size`. When all three hold, the reservation moves `WAITLISTED → MATCHED`, stamping `allocated_unit_id` with the freed unit and `matched_at` with the current clock, clearing `waitlist_position` back to null; the freed unit moves `IN_STOCK → RESERVED`; and the member's `units_out_count` is recomputed with their `version` bumped by 1. If the rank-1 reservation is not eligible for this particular unit, no promotion happens — the queue is not scanned past its head for a better fit.

**Swapping** is the member-facing operation that trades one garment for another mid-cycle, and it is the only reservation operation guarded by optimistic concurrency. The request carries `expected_member_version` as described in §2.6, together with the replacement request's own `style_id`, `requested_size`, `requested_start_date`, `requested_end_date` and optional `preferred_unit_id` — a swap is a full re-specification, not a nudge, and the replacement may be for an entirely different style. The reservation being swapped must be `MATCHED` and the member must be `ACTIVE`.

The swap allowance is checked at rank 51: the member's `swaps_used_current_month` must be strictly less than the plan's `monthly_swaps_allowed`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SWAP_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` (409). Equality rejects — a member whose plan allows two swaps per month and who already has two swap-created reservations this month is refused, while a member sitting at one succeeds. The count itself is computed over a half-open window: it counts the member's reservations whose `is_swap` is true and whose `created_at` falls at or after the first instant of the UTC calendar month containing the current clock and strictly before the first instant of the following UTC calendar month. Crucially, that count is taken over reservations in **any** state, including `SUPERSEDED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` — a swap consumes the month's allowance even if the reservation it produced is later released, so the allowance cannot be recycled.

A successful swap proceeds in a pinned order. The original reservation moves `MATCHED → SUPERSEDED`. As the second hop, that reservation's allocated unit moves `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` — and this release happens *before* the replacement's allocation runs, deliberately, so that the just-freed garment is itself a candidate for the replacement request. As the third hop a new reservation is created with `is_swap` set to true and `superseded_reservation_id` pointing at the reservation it replaced, and that new reservation runs the same `REQUESTED → MATCHED` or `REQUESTED → WAITLISTED` resolution as any other request, with the same eligibility filter, the same ranking, the same buffer geometry and the same `preferred_unit_id` semantics. The member's `version` is bumped by 1. On a reservation that was not created by a swap, `is_swap` is false and `superseded_reservation_id` is null.

Because the swap allowance is consumed by the act of swapping rather than by the outcome, a swap whose replacement lands on the waitlist still counts. And because revenue attribution treats a swapped turn as half a turn (see §3.5), a member who swaps aggressively dilutes the revenue attributed to each garment they touch — which is exactly why the plan caps the behaviour.

### 3.4. Outbound Fulfilment, Delivery and Carrier Exceptions

Physical movement is modelled as shipping legs, and every leg belongs to one reservation. A warehouse technician (or a `PlatformAdmin`) creates a leg by supplying the `reservation_id` it serves, the `direction`, which is either `OUTBOUND` or `INBOUND`, a `planned_ship_date` as a UTC calendar day, and optionally an initial `carrier_reference`. The leg's `member_id` is copied from the linked reservation rather than supplied, and the leg opens in `state` `CREATED` with `exception_count` at 0 and `carrier_reference`, `dispatched_at`, `delivered_at`, `received_at` and `exception_reason` all null. The leg lifecycle runs across `CREATED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `EXCEPTION`, `DELIVERED` and `RECEIVED`, of which `DELIVERED` and `RECEIVED` are terminal — an action aimed at a leg already in either is refused at rank 16 with `ERR_RESOURCE_TERMINAL`, and an unresolvable leg identifier yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Two rules guard leg creation. At rank 39, duplicate legs are refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409): a reservation may never have a second `INBOUND` leg, and for an `OUTBOUND` creation it may not already have an `OUTBOUND` leg in any state other than `EXCEPTION`. The escape hatch for `EXCEPTION` is deliberate — a leg the carrier lost may be replaced by a fresh one. This same rule also guards return initiation, since that operation creates the inbound leg implicitly. At rank 43 comes the plan's shipping cadence, which exists so a member on a modest tier cannot receive a stream of parcels back to back. For an `OUTBOUND` leg, no other `OUTBOUND` leg of the same member whose state is anything other than `EXCEPTION` may have a `planned_ship_date` inside the **closed** window that starts at that other leg's `planned_ship_date` and extends forward by the plan's `shipping_cadence_days`; both endpoints of that window are inclusive. A collision is refused with `ERR_SHIPPING_CADENCE_COLLISION` (409). For a plan with a cadence of 7 days and an existing leg planned for the 10th of the month, a new leg planned for the 17th collides while one planned for the 18th does not.

Dispatch hands a leg to the carrier and is the operation that actually gets a garment moving. It is accepted from `CREATED` and, as the recovery path, from `EXCEPTION`. The leg must be in one of those two states, and for an `OUTBOUND` leg the member must be `ACTIVE` on either path. The precondition on the *linked reservation*, by contrast, guards only the first dispatch, the one out of `CREATED`, since that is the hop that actually drives the reservation forward: dispatching a `CREATED` `OUTBOUND` leg requires its reservation to be `MATCHED` and dispatching a `CREATED` `INBOUND` leg requires its reservation to be `RETURN_INITIATED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). On the `EXCEPTION` recovery path the reservation's state is deliberately **not** re-examined, because the dispatch that preceded the exception has already advanced it; re-imposing the check there would make the re-dispatch half of the `IN_TRANSIT → EXCEPTION → IN_TRANSIT` cycle described below unreachable on an `OUTBOUND` leg. Dispatch moves the leg `CREATED → IN_TRANSIT` or, on the recovery path, `EXCEPTION → IN_TRANSIT`, stamps `dispatched_at` with the current logical clock — always the most recent dispatch, so a re-dispatch overwrites it — and writes `carrier_reference` from the body when one is supplied. On an `OUTBOUND` leg only, dispatch cascades: the reservation moves `MATCHED → SHIPPED` with `shipped_at` stamped, and the allocated unit moves `RESERVED → OUT`. Each of those two hops fires only on its own declared edge — the reservation hop only from `MATCHED`, the unit hop only from `RESERVED` — so a re-dispatch out of `EXCEPTION`, whose reservation is normally already `SHIPPED` and whose unit is normally already `OUT`, leaves both of them untouched and does not re-stamp `shipped_at`. An `INBOUND` dispatch has no such cascade; the reservation is already `RETURN_INITIATED` and the unit is already `INBOUND`. Re-dispatching out of `EXCEPTION` additionally requires, at rank 38, that the leg's `exception_count` still be strictly below the cap of 2, otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_EXCEPTION_CAP_REACHED` (409).

Delivery applies to `OUTBOUND` legs only and moves the leg `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`, stamping the leg's `delivered_at`. It cascades to the reservation, which moves `SHIPPED → IN_POSSESSION` and stamps its own `delivered_at` with the same instant, and the member's `units_out_count` is recomputed. `DELIVERED` is terminal for the leg; the return journey is a separate leg.

Carrier trouble is recorded as an exception. From `IN_TRANSIT`, a technician may record an exception with a mandatory `exception_reason`; the leg moves `IN_TRANSIT → EXCEPTION`, its `exception_count` is incremented by 1, and `exception_reason` records the most recent reason, remaining null on a leg that has never been in exception. Rank 38 gates this too: the `exception_count` must be strictly below the cap of 2 before the increment, so the second exception is the last one a leg can absorb. A leg may therefore cycle `IN_TRANSIT → EXCEPTION → IN_TRANSIT` exactly once via re-dispatch.

When the increment brings `exception_count` to the cap of 2, the leg is treated as a **loss**, and three further hops fire. The second hop retires the unit: its `availability_state` becomes `RETIRED`, its `retirement_reason` becomes `LOSS`, its `retired_at` is stamped with the current clock, and its `condition_state` becomes `CONDEMNED`. The third hop depends on direction, and the two branches are genuinely different. On an `OUTBOUND` leg the reservation moves `SHIPPED → WAITLISTED`: its `allocated_unit_id` and `matched_at` are cleared to null and a fresh `waitlist_position` is computed, which closes the reservation cycle `MATCHED → SHIPPED → WAITLISTED → MATCHED` and gives the member another shot at the style without penalty. On an `INBOUND` leg the reservation instead moves `RETURN_INITIATED → CLOSED`: its `revenue_attributed_cents` is stamped, its `returned_at` is set back to null and its `rental_days` is null, because the garment never arrived to be measured, and its `late_fee_cents` is 0 — a carrier loss on the return leg never produces a late fee. Since the garment never reaches the warehouse, no reverse-logistics job is created on this path and the unit's `turns_completed` is **not** incremented. In neither branch is any fee charged to the member; the carrier's failure is not the member's debt. The fourth hop re-derives the style's `active_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`, since the fleet just lost a garment. Position conservation holds throughout: the unit left `OUT` or `INBOUND` and entered `RETIRED`, so the style-and-size position count is unchanged.

Legs can be listed with optional `member_id`, `reservation_id`, `direction` and `state` filters, which is how a warehouse builds its day's dispatch and receiving queues.

### 3.5. Return Initiation, Receipt and Late-Return Fees

The member starts the journey home. Initiating a return is available only on a reservation in `IN_POSSESSION`, only to the owning member, and only while that member is `ACTIVE`. The member may supply a `planned_ship_date` for the handover and a `carrier_reference`; when `planned_ship_date` is omitted it defaults to the current logical clock date. The reservation moves `IN_POSSESSION → RETURN_INITIATED`, an `INBOUND` shipping leg is created for it in `state` `CREATED` carrying that planned date, and the garment's unit moves `OUT → INBOUND` with its `version` bumped. Because rank 39 forbids a second inbound leg for the same reservation, a member who initiates a return twice receives `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The created leg is what the response returns.

Receipt at the warehouse is the single densest cascade in the system, and only a `WarehouseTech` may perform it. The leg must be `IN_TRANSIT` and `INBOUND`; it moves `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` with `received_at` stamped from the logical clock. The second hop advances the reservation `RETURN_INITIATED → RETURNED`, stamping its `returned_at` and computing its `rental_days` as the number of whole days from the `delivered_at` calendar date to the `returned_at` calendar date, counting the start day and not counting the return day; `rental_days` is null until the reservation reaches `RETURNED`.

The same hop prices lateness. The due date is the reservation's `requested_end_date` extended by a grace period of 2 calendar days. The receipt date is the UTC calendar date of the inbound leg's `received_at`; the outbound `delivered_at` is irrelevant to this calculation. `late_days` is the number of whole days by which the receipt date exceeds the due date, and it is floored at zero, so it is positive only when the garment arrives strictly after the grace period has elapsed — a receipt landing exactly on the due date yields `late_days` of 0 and no fee at all. `late_fee_cents` is 500 cents for each late day, capped at 5,000 cents, meaning the penalty stops growing after ten late days. Both figures default to 0 on a reservation that has never been late. When `late_fee_cents` is greater than zero, a fee-ledger entry of `kind` `LATE_FEE` is appended for the member with that amount as a debit, the member's `outstanding_fee_cents` is recomputed, and — as the third hop of that ledger append — the member may be tipped `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` with the full unwinding cascade of §3.2. When `late_fee_cents` is zero, no ledger entry is written at all.

The third hop of the receipt turns the garment over to reverse logistics: the unit moves `INBOUND → PROCESSING`, its `last_returned_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and a reverse-logistics job is created for that unit and reservation in `state` `RECEIVED`. Before the first return, a unit's `last_returned_at` is null. Note what does *not* happen here: `turns_completed` is **not** incremented on receipt. A turn is only counted when the garment is actually back on the floor, which is the restock step of §3.6.

The fourth hop re-derives the style's `floor_ready_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`.

**Attributing revenue to a turn.** A reservation's `revenue_attributed_cents` is the slice of subscription revenue the business books against that particular garment-turn, and it is stamped at the moment the reservation reaches `CLOSED` — never earlier. The plan snapshot is read at that moment from the member's current `membership_plan_id`. The base per-turn figure is the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` divided by the plan's `item_slots`, rounded half up to a whole cent. If the reservation's `is_swap` is false, that base figure is the attributed revenue. If `is_swap` is true, the attributed revenue is half the base figure, again rounded half up — so a base of 3,333 cents becomes 1,667 cents on a swap turn, rounding upward rather than truncating. The field defaults to 0 before it is stamped.

There are exactly two doors into `CLOSED`, and both stamp `revenue_attributed_cents` by the rule above. The ordinary door is `RETURNED → CLOSED`, which fires when the garment's reverse-logistics job reaches `RESTOCKED`, or when a unit whose job is stuck in `CONDEMNED_HOLD` is retired. The exceptional door is `RETURN_INITIATED → CLOSED`, which fires when an inbound leg is lost by the carrier as described in §3.4. Whenever a reservation reaches `CLOSED`, the unit's `realized_revenue_cents` — the sum of `revenue_attributed_cents` over the `CLOSED` reservations that held that unit — is recomputed.

### 3.6. Reverse Logistics: Inspection, Reconditioning and Restock

A reverse-logistics job is the work order that gets one garment from "just arrived" back to "rentable". It is created by the system, never by a client, and it records the `rental_unit_id` of the unit being reconditioned and the `reservation_id` of the rental turn that produced it. Its pipeline `state` runs across `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED`, `PROCESSING`, `READY_FOR_PHOTO`, `PHOTOGRAPHED`, `RESTOCKED` and `CONDEMNED_HOLD`, opening at `RECEIVED`, with `RESTOCKED` as its only terminal state. `CONDEMNED_HOLD` is a non-terminal sink: nothing inside the reconditioning pipeline can drain it, and only retirement of the underlying unit ever moves a job out of it. An unresolvable job identifier yields `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`. A new job opens with `cleaning_required` and `repair_required` both false, `repair_attempts` at 0, `servicing_cost_cents` at 0 and `condemned` false, and with `inspecting_technician_id`, `cleaning_completed_at`, `repair_completed_at` and `photographed_at` all null. Jobs can be listed with optional `rental_unit_id`, `reservation_id`, `inspecting_technician_id` and `state` filters.

The shape of the pipeline is a **fork-join**. Inspection forks into a cleaning leg, a repair leg, both, or neither, and `READY_FOR_PHOTO` is the join point where the legs come back together. Two boolean fields carry the fork: `cleaning_required` and `repair_required`, both defaulting to false, and two timestamps carry the join: `cleaning_completed_at` and `repair_completed_at`, both null while their leg is outstanding.

**Filing an inspection.** A `WarehouseTech` inspects a garment sitting on a job in `RECEIVED` or, as a re-inspection, in `PROCESSING`; the acting technician's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the unit's `availability_state` must be `PROCESSING`. The inspection record captures a `soil_level` of `NONE`, `LIGHT` or `HEAVY`; a `damage_severity` of `NONE`, `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` or `LOSS`; a `condition_grade_after` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, which overwrites the unit's `condition_grade` outright; an optional `biohazard_flag` defaulting to false; and optional free-text `notes`, which are null when omitted. The record's `technician_id` is taken from the `X-User-ID` header of the acting technician, and its `attempt_index` is its 1-based position among the inspections on the same job, assigned in `created_at` ascending order. An inspection record is an immutable event: it carries `id` and `created_at` and neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Inspections can be listed with optional `job_id`, `technician_id` and `damage_severity` filters, and an unresolvable inspection identifier yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`.

Filing an inspection moves the job `RECEIVED → INSPECTED` (or, for a re-inspection, `PROCESSING → INSPECTED`) and sets its `inspecting_technician_id` to the acting technician — the identity that the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10 will later measure a repair signer against. The two fork flags are then derived mechanically: `cleaning_required` becomes true exactly when `soil_level` is anything other than `NONE`, and `repair_required` becomes true exactly when `damage_severity` is one of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`. A `damage_severity` of `LOSS` does **not** set `repair_required`; a lost or destroyed garment is not repaired.

The unit's condition dimension then moves, and the branch taken is decided by the inspection's contents in a strict order of precedence. A `biohazard_flag` of true outranks everything else: the unit moves `FLOOR_READY → QUARANTINED`, its `quarantine_hold` is set to true, and its `condition_grade` is updated from `condition_grade_after`. Absent a biohazard, a `damage_severity` of `LOSS` moves the unit `FLOOR_READY → CONDEMNED` and sets the job's `condemned` flag to true with the job going to `CONDEMNED_HOLD`. Absent both, a `damage_severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE` moves the unit `FLOOR_READY → NEEDS_REPAIR`. Only when the garment is undamaged and non-hazardous does a `soil_level` other than `NONE` move it `FLOOR_READY → NEEDS_CLEANING`. The repair branch outranks the cleaning branch: a garment that is both soiled and damaged goes to `NEEDS_REPAIR`, and `cleaning_required` is still set to true, so the cleaning leg is remembered even though the condition dimension is showing the more urgent problem. In every one of these branches the unit's `condition_grade` is overwritten by `condition_grade_after` and the unit's `version` is bumped by 1.

Damage opens a claim. When the inspection's `damage_severity` is `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` or `LOSS`, a damage claim is created in `state` `OPEN` linking the reservation, the rental unit, the member who held the garment and the inspection whose finding opened it; a claim opened by a `LOSS` finding carries `severity` `LOSS`. Immediately afterwards, the member's `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band` are recomputed and persisted under the lifetime variant of §3.9. The job's `servicing_cost_cents` is rolled up as well.

Within the very same request that filed the inspection, the job leaves `INSPECTED` for one of three destinations, and which one is fully determined by the inspection. If the newest inspection's `damage_severity` is `LOSS`, the job goes `INSPECTED → CONDEMNED_HOLD` with `condemned` set to true. Otherwise, if either `cleaning_required` or `repair_required` is true, **or** the inspection's `biohazard_flag` is true, the job goes `INSPECTED → PROCESSING` to await sign-offs — a biohazard garment therefore waits in `PROCESSING` even when neither fork leg was set, because it still owes the quarantine clearance described below before it can rejoin the floor. Otherwise — neither leg required and `biohazard_flag` false — the job goes straight `INSPECTED → READY_FOR_PHOTO`, since a pristine garment still needs re-photographing before it goes back on the floor.

**Re-inspection** exists because a second look sometimes finds what the first missed, and it closes the job cycle `INSPECTED → PROCESSING → INSPECTED`. A re-inspection is filed from job state `PROCESSING` and requires that the job's `condemned` flag be false. It appends a second inspection record whose `attempt_index` is one greater than the highest existing index on that job. Both fork flags are recomputed from the newest inspection, and this is the subtle part: for every leg the newest inspection still demands, the corresponding completion timestamp is cleared back to null, so a `cleaning_completed_at` or `repair_completed_at` that had already been stamped is discarded and the leg must be signed off again. What is **not** cleared is `repair_attempts`. A job that has already consumed both permitted repair attempts re-opens its repair leg with no attempts left, and the next repair sign-off on it is refused at rank 28 with `ERR_REPAIR_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Finally, if a damage claim on this job already exists in `OPEN` or `ASSESSED`, its `severity` is re-copied from the newest inspection and it returns to `state` `OPEN`; a new claim is created only when none exists yet.

Two condition-dimension edges belong specifically to re-inspection. A unit sitting in `NEEDS_CLEANING` whose re-inspection reports a `damage_severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, with `biohazard_flag` false and the unit's `availability_state` still `PROCESSING`, moves `NEEDS_CLEANING → NEEDS_REPAIR`, takes the new `condition_grade_after` as its grade, has its claim severity re-copied as described above, and triggers a lifetime recompute of the member's `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band`. This is precisely the edge that re-opens the repair leg on a garment whose repair had already been accepted and which was therefore resting in `NEEDS_CLEANING`. A unit sitting in `NEEDS_REPAIR` whose re-inspection reports `damage_severity` of `LOSS` instead moves `NEEDS_REPAIR → CONDEMNED`, with the job's `condemned` flag set to true and the job driven to `CONDEMNED_HOLD`.

**Clearing a quarantine.** While `quarantine_hold` is true, every reconditioning step on the unit is frozen: cleaning sign-off, repair sign-off, photography and restock are all refused at rank 20 with `ERR_QUARANTINE_HOLD_ACTIVE` (409). The only way forward is a clearance by a technician whose `repair_certified` is true, which moves the unit `QUARANTINED → NEEDS_CLEANING` and sets `quarantine_hold` back to false. A quarantined garment therefore always passes through the cleaning leg on its way out, regardless of what its `soil_level` said: the clearance sets the job's `cleaning_required` to true when it was not already true, so the cleaning leg becomes the outstanding leg that the join of this section then waits on. The clearance does not move the job's own `state`, which stays wherever the inspection left it.

**The repair leg.** Signing a repair attempt carries a single body field, `outcome`, which is either `ACCEPTED` or `FAILED`. The rules that guard it stack in ladder order: at rank 20 the unit's `quarantine_hold` must be false; at rank 25 the acting technician must not be the job's `inspecting_technician_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403); at rank 26 the acting technician's `repair_certified` must be true, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (403); at rank 27 the technician's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED` (403); and at rank 28 the job's `repair_attempts` must be strictly below the cap of 2, otherwise `ERR_REPAIR_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). The leg must genuinely be outstanding: `repair_required` must be true and `repair_completed_at` must still be null. Every sign-off, accepted or failed, increments `repair_attempts` by 1 and re-rolls the job's `servicing_cost_cents`.

An `outcome` of `FAILED` behaves differently depending on whether it is the last permitted attempt. When the incremented attempt count is still strictly below the cap of 2 — that is, on the first failure — the job holds its place with a self-loop `PROCESSING → PROCESSING` and the garment stays in `NEEDS_REPAIR`, ready for another attempt; this is the bounded retry loop. When the incremented attempt count reaches the cap of 2, the job moves `PROCESSING → CONDEMNED_HOLD` with `condemned` set to true; as a second hop the unit moves `NEEDS_REPAIR → CONDEMNED`; and as a third hop, if the linked damage claim's current `severity` is `SEVERE` and its `state` is `OPEN` or `ASSESSED`, that severity is escalated to `LOSS`, because a garment that could not be saved is a total loss rather than a bad tear.

An `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` stamps `repair_completed_at` with the current logical clock, and its effect on the unit depends on whether the cleaning leg is also in play. When `cleaning_required` is true, the unit moves `NEEDS_REPAIR → NEEDS_CLEANING` and the job **stays** where it is on a second self-loop `PROCESSING → PROCESSING`, because signing off only one of two required legs is not the join. When `cleaning_required` is false, the unit moves `NEEDS_REPAIR → FLOOR_READY` and, the last outstanding leg now being closed, the job moves `PROCESSING → READY_FOR_PHOTO`.

**The cleaning leg** is ordered after the repair leg by policy: a cleaned garment is never re-opened for stitching. At rank 23, when both `cleaning_required` and `repair_required` are true, the job's `repair_completed_at` must already be non-null before cleaning may be signed off, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_PENDING` (409). Rank 20's quarantine check applies here too. Signing off cleaning stamps `cleaning_completed_at`, moves the unit `NEEDS_CLEANING → FLOOR_READY`, and re-rolls `servicing_cost_cents`.

**The join.** After any sign-off is applied, the job advances `PROCESSING → READY_FOR_PHOTO` exactly when both legs are settled: `cleaning_required` is false or `cleaning_completed_at` is non-null, **and** `repair_required` is false or `repair_completed_at` is non-null. The unit's `quarantine_hold` must be false for the job to make that move. If only one of two required legs has been signed off, the job remains in `PROCESSING` and waits.

**Re-photography** is the gate back onto the floor. It requires the job to be in `READY_FOR_PHOTO`, the acting technician's `photography_certified` to be true (rank 26), the unit's `condition_state` to be `FLOOR_READY`, and — at rank 21 — the unit not to be `CONDEMNED`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_CONDEMNED` (409); rank 20's quarantine check applies as well. It stamps `photographed_at` and moves the job `READY_FOR_PHOTO → PHOTOGRAPHED`.

**Restock** closes the loop. It requires the job to be in `PHOTOGRAPHED`, the unit to be `FLOOR_READY` with `quarantine_hold` false and not `CONDEMNED`, and — at rank 22 — that no damage claim on this unit and this reservation sits in `OPEN`, `ASSESSED` or `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_RESTOCK` (409). Money must be settled before a garment earns its next turn. The job moves `PHOTOGRAPHED → RESTOCKED`. Its second hop moves the unit `PROCESSING → IN_STOCK` and increments `turns_completed` by exactly 1 — this is the only place in the entire system where that counter moves. The third hop moves the linked reservation `RETURNED → CLOSED` and stamps its `revenue_attributed_cents`. The fourth hop re-derives the style's `floor_ready_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`, and the unit's `cumulative_servicing_cost_cents` is refreshed. The fifth hop offers the freshly available garment to the queue: the rank-1 `WAITLISTED` reservation for the unit's style and size is promoted `WAITLISTED → MATCHED` when one exists and the unit is eligible for its window.

**What reconditioning costs.** A job's `servicing_cost_cents` is re-rolled on every inspection, every repair sign-off, every cleaning sign-off, every photography sign-off and every restock, and it always reads from the **newest** inspection on the job — the one with the greatest `attempt_index`. It is the sum of three components. The cleaning component is 0 cents when `cleaning_required` is false; otherwise it is keyed on the newest inspection's `soil_level`, contributing 0 cents for `NONE`, 1,200 cents for `LIGHT` and 2,600 cents for `HEAVY`. The repair component is a per-attempt rate keyed on the newest inspection's `damage_severity` — 0 cents for `NONE`, 1,500 cents for `MINOR`, 4,200 cents for `MODERATE`, 9,800 cents for `SEVERE` and 0 cents for `LOSS` — multiplied by the job's `repair_attempts`, floored at zero attempts, so two failed moderate attempts cost 8,400 cents in labour even though nothing was fixed. The photography component is 800 cents once `photographed_at` is non-null and 0 cents before that. No rounding is involved anywhere: these are exact integer cents.

A unit's `cumulative_servicing_cost_cents` is the sum of `servicing_cost_cents` across the jobs on that unit whose `state` is `RESTOCKED`. Jobs sitting in `CONDEMNED_HOLD` contribute nothing to it, which means the labour spent on a garment that was ultimately condemned never lands in that unit's servicing rollup.

### 3.7. Damage, Loss, Disputes and the Fee Ledger

A `DamageClaim` is the money side of an inspection finding. It is a child of the `Reservation` on whose turn the damage happened, and it carries `reservation_id`, `rental_unit_id`, `member_id` and `inspection_id` — the four coordinates of "this member, this garment, this turn, this finding" — none of which a client ever supplies, because the claim is always created by the inspection cascade described in §3.6. Its `severity` starts as a copy of the opening inspection's `damage_severity`, is re-copied when a later inspection on the same job supersedes it, and is persistently downgraded one step by each `PARTIAL` dispute verdict. Its `state` runs over `OPEN`, `ASSESSED`, `DISPUTED`, `CHARGED`, `WAIVED` and `DISMISSED`, beginning at `OPEN`, with `WAIVED` and `DISMISSED` terminal. Four computed money fields — `residual_value_cents`, `gross_fee_cents`, `waiver_applied_cents` and `net_fee_cents` — are written by the assessment and rewritten by a `PARTIAL` verdict; `dispute_round` counts disputes filed and starts at 0; `dispute_reason` holds the text from the most recent dispute and is null before any; `resolution` holds the most recent adjuster verdict from `UPHELD`, `OVERTURNED` or `PARTIAL` and is null before any; `assessed_at` and `charged_at` stamp the most recent assessment and the arrival at `CHARGED`.

**Pricing a claim.** A claims adjuster prices an `OPEN` claim, which requires that the linked job carry at least one inspection and moves the claim `OPEN → ASSESSED` with `assessed_at` stamped at the current logical clock. The price is built in a fixed order. First the residual value: take the style's `retail_price_cents`, multiply by the residual rate for the unit's current `condition_grade` — which the inspection has already overwritten, so the assessment always prices the garment as it is now, not as it was — and round half up. The residual rates are 0.55 for grade `A`, 0.40 for `B`, 0.25 for `C` and 0.10 for `D`; the result is `residual_value_cents`. Second, a severity floor: when the claim's `severity` is `LOSS` the floor is the whole residual value, and otherwise it is a flat 2,500 cents for `MINOR`, 9,000 cents for `MODERATE` and 22,000 cents for `SEVERE`. Third, a percentage component: multiply the residual value by 0.10 for `MINOR`, 0.35 for `MODERATE`, 0.75 for `SEVERE` or 1.00 for `LOSS`, rounding half up. Then `gross_fee_cents` is the larger of the severity floor and the percentage component, but never more than the residual value — the floor pushes small claims up, the percentage pushes expensive garments up, and the residual value is a hard ceiling that no combination may pierce. Each multiplication is rounded half up to whole cents before that comparison; there is no rounding afterwards. Note the consequence for cheap garments: a `MODERATE` finding on a grade-`D` piece whose residual value is below 9,000 cents is billed at exactly the residual value, because the 9,000-cent floor is clipped by the ceiling.

Waiver credit is then applied. The member's remaining coverage is the plan's `waiver_coverage_cents` minus the member's `waiver_coverage_used_cents`, floored at zero. If the member's `waiver_active` is true, `waiver_applied_cents` is the smaller of `gross_fee_cents` and that remaining coverage; if `waiver_active` is false it is 0. `net_fee_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` minus `waiver_applied_cents`. The assessment then recomputes the member's `waiver_coverage_used_cents` from that member's claims in `ASSESSED`, `DISPUTED`, `CHARGED` or `WAIVED`, so coverage is consumed the moment a claim is assessed even though no ledger entry has been written yet — a member with a single 30,000-cent coverage allowance cannot have two assessed claims each quietly assuming the full amount.

**Absorbing a claim under the waiver.** An adjuster may absorb an `ASSESSED` claim entirely, moving it `ASSESSED → WAIVED`. At rank 52 this requires the member's `waiver_active` to be true and the plan's `waiver_coverage_cents`, minus the member's `waiver_coverage_used_cents`, plus the coverage this same claim has already reserved through `waiver_applied_cents`, to be at least the claim's `gross_fee_cents`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Adding this claim's own reservation back before comparing is what lets a claim that already holds part of its coverage be absorbed in full without double-counting itself. Absorption raises `waiver_applied_cents` to the full `gross_fee_cents`, sets `net_fee_cents` to 0, and adjusts the member's `waiver_coverage_used_cents` so the total consumed by this claim equals `gross_fee_cents`. A `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry of amount 0 is written for audit; the member's balance does not move, because nothing was ever charged.

**Charging a claim.** An adjuster charges an `ASSESSED` claim, which requires the member's `status` not to be `CANCELLED` and, at rank 53, `net_fee_cents` to be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_TO_CHARGE` (409) — a fully waived or fully reversed claim has nothing left to bill. The claim moves `ASSESSED → CHARGED` with `charged_at` stamped. The cascade then runs. Hop two appends a `FeeLedgerEntry` of `kind` `DAMAGE_FEE` whose `amount_cents` is the full `gross_fee_cents` and, when `waiver_applied_cents` is greater than zero, a second entry of `kind` `WAIVER_CREDIT` whose `amount_cents` is the negation of `waiver_applied_cents`; the member's `outstanding_fee_cents` is then recomputed, so the balance rises by exactly `net_fee_cents` while the ledger still shows the gross charge and the credit separately. Hop three checks delinquency: when the recomputed `outstanding_fee_cents` is greater than or equal to 15,000 cents and the member's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the member moves `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` and `version` increments. Hop four is the delinquency cascade of §3.2 — every `WAITLISTED` reservation of that member moves `WAITLISTED → CANCELLED` and every `MATCHED` reservation moves `MATCHED → RELEASED`, while an `IN_POSSESSION` reservation is left alone, because garments already in the member's hands are recovered through the normal return path. Hop five returns each unit released by hop four `RESERVED → IN_STOCK`, recomputes the depth fields of each affected style, and re-evaluates waitlist promotion for those styles.

**Disputing a claim.** The member who owns the claim — and only that member, matched by `member_id` — may dispute it from either `ASSESSED` or `CHARGED`, on the edges `ASSESSED → DISPUTED` and `CHARGED → DISPUTED`, incrementing `dispute_round` by 1 and storing the supplied `dispute_reason`. Two guards apply in ladder order. At rank 29 `dispute_round` must be strictly below the cap of 2, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409): a member gets two bites, not an unbounded appeal. At rank 30, when the claim's `state` is `CHARGED`, the current logical clock's calendar date must be no later than the calendar date of `charged_at` plus 7 days, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409); the boundary is inclusive, so a dispute filed exactly 7 days after the charge date is accepted and one filed on the eighth day is not, and the window is not applied at all to an `ASSESSED` claim, which has no charge date to measure from. Disputing a charged claim does not touch the ledger — the balance stands until the verdict.

**Resolving a dispute.** An adjuster resolves a `DISPUTED` claim by supplying a `resolution`, and the three verdicts diverge sharply.

A `resolution` of `PARTIAL` moves the claim `DISPUTED → ASSESSED`, records `resolution` as `PARTIAL`, and **persistently** downgrades `severity` one step: `LOSS` becomes `SEVERE`, `SEVERE` becomes `MODERATE`, `MODERATE` becomes `MINOR`, and `MINOR` stays `MINOR`. Because the downgrade is written to the claim, a second `PARTIAL` verdict downgrades from the already-downgraded value rather than from the original finding, so two partial verdicts on a `LOSS` claim land on `MODERATE`. All four money fields are then recomputed at the new severity by the assessment arithmetic above. If the claim had already been charged, the ledger is restated rather than patched: an entry of `kind` `DISPUTE_REVERSAL` is appended whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the signed sum of this claim's prior entries, and the freshly recomputed pair is then posted afresh — a `DAMAGE_FEE` entry of `gross_fee_cents` plus, when `waiver_applied_cents` is greater than zero, a `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry of the negation of `waiver_applied_cents`.

A `resolution` of `UPHELD` moves the claim `DISPUTED → CHARGED` and records `resolution` as `UPHELD`. Rank 53's `ERR_NOTHING_TO_CHARGE` applies here exactly as it does to a direct charge: `net_fee_cents` must be strictly greater than zero. `charged_at` is stamped only when it was still null, so upholding a dispute on a claim that was already charged preserves the original charge instant and, with it, the dispute window's origin. The `DAMAGE_FEE` and, where applicable, `WAIVER_CREDIT` pair is appended only when this claim has no unreversed `DAMAGE_FEE` entry yet — upholding a charge that was never reversed must not bill the member twice.

A `resolution` of `OVERTURNED` moves the claim `DISPUTED → DISMISSED`, which is terminal, records `resolution` as `OVERTURNED`, and sets `net_fee_cents` to 0. This is the compensating action for the charge. Hop two, when a `DAMAGE_FEE` entry for this claim exists, appends a `DISPUTE_REVERSAL` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the signed sum of this claim's prior entries and recomputes the member's `outstanding_fee_cents`, which therefore returns to exactly its pre-charge value. Hop three decrements the member's `waiver_coverage_used_cents` by this claim's `waiver_applied_cents`, releasing the coverage a dismissed claim no longer needs. Hop four checks the reverse delinquency edge: when the recomputed `outstanding_fee_cents` is below 15,000 cents and the member's `status` is `DELINQUENT`, the member returns `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE` and `version` increments. The reservations that the delinquency cascade cancelled and released are **not** resurrected — the conserved quantity is the fee balance, not the reservation set, so a wrongly charged member is made whole in money and must re-request the garments by hand.

**The ledger.** A `FeeLedgerEntry` is an append-only event, never mutated and never deleted, and it carries no `updated_at` or `version`. It names its `member_id`; its `damage_claim_id` is set on `DAMAGE_FEE`, `WAIVER_CREDIT` and `DISPUTE_REVERSAL` entries and null otherwise, while its `reservation_id` is set on `LATE_FEE` entries and null otherwise. Its `kind` is one of `DAMAGE_FEE`, `LATE_FEE`, `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT` and `DISPUTE_REVERSAL`. Its `amount_cents` is signed: positive for `DAMAGE_FEE` and `LATE_FEE`, negative for `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT` and `DISPUTE_REVERSAL`, with the one exception of the audit-only zero-amount `WAIVER_CREDIT` written by a full absorption. Its `direction` is derived, not supplied: `DEBIT` when `amount_cents` is greater than zero and `CREDIT` when `amount_cents` is less than zero. Its `balance_after_cents` records the member's `outstanding_fee_cents` immediately after this entry was appended, so replaying a member's entries in ascending `created_at` order reproduces the running balance entry by entry. Its `memo` is free text and null when omitted. Entries are listed in append order and may be filtered by `member_id`, `damage_claim_id`, `reservation_id` or `kind`.

**Settling a balance.** A member, or a platform administrator on their behalf, settles part or all of an outstanding balance by supplying an `amount_cents` and an optional `memo`. At rank 54 the amount must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than the member's current `outstanding_fee_cents`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422); the API accepts no overpayment and no zero or negative payment, and note that this one is a request-content rejection at 422 rather than a state conflict. The settlement appends a `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT` entry in the `CREDIT` direction whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the amount supplied, recomputes `outstanding_fee_cents`, and, when the balance falls below 15,000 cents and the member's `status` is `DELINQUENT`, returns the member `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`. The created ledger entry is the resource returned, not the member.

Every append to the ledger re-audits the balance invariant of §2.7: the member's `outstanding_fee_cents` always equals the signed sum of that member's entries across all five kinds, and `balance_after_cents` on each entry equals the running sum up to and including it. No entry is ever rewritten to make that arithmetic come out; corrections are made by appending compensating entries.

### 3.8. Retirement and Resale Recovery

A garment leaves the rentable fleet through retirement and leaves the balance sheet through resale. An inventory planner retires a unit by supplying a `retirement_reason`, one of `TURNS_EXHAUSTED`, `CONDEMNED_DAMAGE`, `LOSS` or `PLANNED_REFRESH`. Retirement sets `availability_state` to `RETIRED`, stamps `retired_at` with the current logical clock, records the supplied reason and increments the unit's `version`. It is permitted from exactly two origins on the availability dimension, and the two branches are deliberately symmetric in their guard and differ only in their cascade.

The first branch, `IN_STOCK → RETIRED`, is the planned-refresh and turns-exhausted case: a garment resting on the floor is simply withdrawn. Its cascade recomputes the style's `floor_ready_unit_count` and `active_unit_count`, and from those the `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`. The second branch, `PROCESSING → RETIRED`, is the condemned and lost case: a garment in reconditioning is withdrawn mid-pipeline. Its cascade drives any reverse-logistics job on the unit that sits in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED`, `PROCESSING`, `READY_FOR_PHOTO`, `PHOTOGRAPHED` or `CONDEMNED_HOLD` to `CONDEMNED_HOLD`, and it then recomputes the style's `active_unit_count`, `required_depth_units`, `depth_gap_units` and `replenishment_flag`. This is the only drain for the `CONDEMNED_HOLD` sink described in §3.6: when the unit's job was in `CONDEMNED_HOLD`, retirement also closes the turn, moving the linked reservation `RETURNED → CLOSED`, stamping its `revenue_attributed_cents` from the turn-revenue arithmetic of §3.5 and refreshing the unit's `realized_revenue_cents` — a condemned garment still earned the rental it was paid for. Waitlist promotion is re-evaluated for the style after either branch, since the depth picture has changed. Note that on the condition dimension the unit may be `FLOOR_READY` (a planned refresh) or `CONDEMNED` (unsalvageable damage); retirement moves only the availability dimension and never rewrites `condition_state`.

Both branches share one guard. At rank 45, no reservation whose `allocated_unit_id` is this unit may be in `MATCHED`, `SHIPPED`, `IN_POSSESSION` or `RETURN_INITIATED`, and no damage claim on this unit may be in `OPEN`, `ASSESSED` or `DISPUTED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_UNIT_HAS_ACTIVE_COMMITMENT` (409). A garment that someone is still wearing, still owes a return leg for, or still owes money on cannot be quietly written off. Retirement also leaves the unit-position conservation invariant of §2.7 untouched, because `RETIRED` is itself one of the counted positions.

Retirement can also arrive by cascade rather than by request: as §3.4 records, the second inbound carrier exception at the cap of 2 retires the unit with `retirement_reason` `LOSS` and sets `condition_state` to `CONDEMNED`.

**Listing a retired unit.** An inventory planner creates a `ResaleListing` naming a `rental_unit_id`, an `expected_rental_unit_version`, a `channel` of either `OUTLET` or `PARTNER_RESALE`, and optionally a `supersedes_listing_id`. The listing is created in `DRAFT` and advances `DRAFT → LISTED` inside the same request, stamping `listed_at` and incrementing the unit's `version`; a listing is therefore never observable in `DRAFT`. Its guards run in ladder order. At rank 34, when `supersedes_listing_id` is supplied it must reference a listing on the same `rental_unit_id` whose `state` is `EXPIRED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SUPERSEDE_TARGET` (422) — a supersede chain may only extend from a listing that genuinely ran out its window on the same garment. At rank 35, no other listing on this unit may be in `DRAFT` or `LISTED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (409): one live shop window per garment. At rank 46, the unit's `availability_state` must be `RETIRED`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_NOT_RETIRED` (409). And, as §2.6 sets out, the supplied `expected_rental_unit_version` must still equal the unit's stored `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (412). The listing's `markdown_step` is inherited as the superseded listing's `markdown_step` plus 1, or is 0 for a first listing, which is how the markdown ladder survives across superseding listings.

**Resale pricing.** The dependency here only looks circular: the channel fee percentage is keyed on a price tier, and the floor correction changes the price. The evaluation order is pinned and is executed exactly once, top to bottom, on every listing creation, every reprice and every sale.

Step 1 computes `base_price_cents` as the style's `retail_price_cents`, multiplied by the resale rate for the unit's current `condition_grade` — 0.45 for grade `A`, 0.32 for `B`, 0.20 for `C` and 0.08 for `D` — multiplied by one minus 0.15 times the listing's `markdown_step`, rounded half up. Step 2 derives the channel tier from that Step-1 price and **freezes** it: the tier is `LOW` when `base_price_cents` is below 5,000 cents, `MID` when it is below 20,000 cents, and `HIGH` otherwise. Step 3 reads the fee percentage for the listing's `channel` at that frozen tier: for `OUTLET` it is 0.05 at `LOW`, 0.08 at `MID` and 0.12 at `HIGH`; for `PARTNER_RESALE` it is 0.15 at `LOW`, 0.18 at `MID` and 0.22 at `HIGH`. Step 4 computes `channel_fee_cents` as the Step-1 price multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up. Step 5 computes a provisional net as the Step-1 price minus that fee minus the channel's handling cost, which is 400 cents for `OUTLET` and 900 cents for `PARTNER_RESALE`.

Step 6 is the corrective pass, and there is **at most one** of them. When the provisional net is below the minimum net recovery of 2,000 cents, `list_price_cents` is set to 2,000 plus the channel's handling cost, divided by one minus the fee percentage, rounded half up, and `floor_adjusted` is set to true. Otherwise `list_price_cents` is the Step-1 price and `floor_adjusted` is false. Crucially, this pass reuses the tier frozen at Step 2 and the fee percentage read at Step 3: the tier is **not** re-derived from the corrected price, so a garment whose base price was 4,900 cents keeps its `LOW` fee percentage even if the floor correction lifts its list price past 5,000 cents. Step 7 then restates the money on the final price with the frozen percentage: `channel_fee_cents` becomes `list_price_cents` multiplied by the fee percentage, rounded half up, and `net_recovery_cents` is `list_price_cents` minus that fee minus the handling cost. Rounding half up happens at Steps 1, 4, 6 and 7 and nowhere else. Prior listings on the same unit contribute nothing to the arithmetic except through the `markdown_step` carried down the `supersedes_listing_id` lineage.

**Expiring, marking down and withdrawing.** A planner expires a `LISTED` listing, moving it `LISTED → EXPIRED`. At rank 44 the current logical clock's calendar date must be greater than or equal to the calendar date of `listed_at` plus 30 days, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_OPEN` (409); the boundary is inclusive, so expiry is permitted on exactly the thirtieth day after listing and refused on the twenty-ninth. A planner then reprices an `EXPIRED` listing, and that single request does one of two mutually exclusive things depending on how far down the ladder the listing already is. When `markdown_step` is strictly below the cap of 3, `markdown_step` is incremented by 1, the listing moves `EXPIRED → LISTED`, `listed_at` is re-stamped at the current clock, and `base_price_cents`, `list_price_cents`, `channel_fee_cents`, `net_recovery_cents` and `floor_adjusted` are all recomputed at the new markdown step — this is the bounded markdown cycle `LISTED → EXPIRED → LISTED`. When `markdown_step` already equals 3, the ladder is exhausted and the same request instead moves the listing `EXPIRED → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal; the unit stays `RETIRED` and contributes 0 end-of-life value to realized unit economics, because nothing was ever recovered.

**Recording a sale.** A planner records the sale of a `LISTED` listing by supplying a `sale_price_cents`. At rank 55 that price must be greater than or equal to the listing's `list_price_cents` multiplied by one minus 0.10, rounded half up, otherwise `ERR_SALE_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422): a planner may discount at the point of sale by up to ten percent of the list price and no further. The listing moves `LISTED → SOLD`, `sold_at` is stamped at the current logical clock, and `sale_price_cents` is recorded. Steps 4 through 7 of the pricing arithmetic are then re-run with the list price replaced by `sale_price_cents` and the fee percentage still frozen at its listing-time value, so `channel_fee_cents` and `net_recovery_cents` restate on what the garment actually fetched; `base_price_cents` is left untouched as the historical record of what the markdown ladder asked for. Hop two moves the unit `RETIRED → SOLD`, which is terminal on the availability dimension and increments the unit's `version`. Hop three refreshes the style's end-of-life inputs to realized unit economics. The unit's `realized_revenue_cents` is deliberately **not** touched by a sale: end-of-life money reaches unit economics through the sold listing's `net_recovery_cents`, and adding it to rental revenue as well would count it twice.

### 3.9. Reading the Fleet Back: Personalization, Demand Signals and Per-Garment Economics

Three computed reads close the loop between what happened physically and what the business does next. None of them mutates anything, all of them are derived on demand from the rows that exist at the moment of the read, and two of them carry a `variant` query parameter that selects between two genuinely different definitions of the same number.

**Fit feedback.** After a turn, the member who held it rates it. A `FitFeedback` row is an append-only event with no `updated_at` and no `version`; its `reservation_id`, `member_id` and `style_id` are copied from the reservation rather than supplied. The member supplies a `fit_rating` from `TOO_SMALL`, `SLIGHTLY_SMALL`, `TRUE_TO_SIZE`, `SLIGHTLY_LARGE` and `TOO_LARGE`, an `occasion_match_score` between 1 and 5 inclusive, an optional `keep_intent` flag stating that they would buy the style outright, and an optional free-text `comment` which is null when omitted. Two guards apply in ladder order. At rank 36, the reservation's `state` must be `RETURNED` or `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_FEEDBACK_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409) — a member cannot rate the fit of a garment they have not yet sent back, and rows in `MATCHED`, `SHIPPED`, `IN_POSSESSION`, `RETURN_INITIATED`, `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all refused. At rank 37, no feedback row may already exist for this reservation, otherwise `ERR_FEEDBACK_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409): one rating per turn, so a member cannot stack ratings to move a style's demand score. Filing feedback recomputes the member's `fit_confidence_score` and the style's `demand_score` described in §3.1. Feedback rows may be read individually or listed with filters on `reservation_id`, `member_id`, `style_id` and `fit_rating`.

**Fit confidence.** A member's `fit_confidence_score` is derived from every feedback row that member has ever filed, regardless of style or how old the turn was, taken in ascending `created_at` order. Each `fit_rating` carries a signed size delta: `TOO_SMALL` is −2, `SLIGHTLY_SMALL` is −1, `TRUE_TO_SIZE` is 0, `SLIGHTLY_LARGE` is 1 and `TOO_LARGE` is 2. The mismatch penalty is the sum of the **absolute values** of those deltas across the member's rows, so a garment that ran two sizes small and one that ran two sizes large both count 2 against confidence rather than cancelling each other out. The occasion bonus is the sum, over the member's rows, of `occasion_match_score` minus 3, so a score of 5 contributes positive 2, a score of 3 contributes nothing and a score of 1 contributes negative 2. The keep bonus is 5 for each row whose `keep_intent` is true. The raw score is a baseline of 50, plus 6 times the occasion bonus, plus the keep bonus, minus 8 times the mismatch penalty. The stored `fit_confidence_score` is that raw value clamped at both ends into the range 0 through 100 inclusive: values above 100 are recorded as 100 and values below 0 as 0. There is no rounding, since every term is an integer.

The personalization read returns the member's `member_id`, `fit_confidence_score`, `feedback_count` as the number of rows, `keep_intent_count` as the number of rows whose `keep_intent` is true, and `dominant_fit_rating` as the `fit_rating` value occurring most often across the rows. When two or more ratings tie on count, the winner is the tied rating whose earliest row has the smallest `created_at` — the member's oldest expressed preference breaks the tie. When the member has filed no rows at all, `dominant_fit_rating` is null. The member's own `preferred_categories` and `size_profile` are echoed back unchanged; as §3.2 notes, `size_profile` is a declared free-form string and is never used as an allocation filter, the allocation filter being the reservation's own `requested_size`.

**Damage risk.** A member's damage risk is a weighted score over their finished turns, and the `variant` query parameter chooses the input set. Under `LIFETIME`, which is the default, the scope is every reservation of the member whose `state` is `CLOSED`, with no date filter. Under `ROLLING_12M`, the scope is those `CLOSED` reservations whose `returned_at` falls in the half-open window that begins 365 days before the current logical clock, inclusive, and ends at the current logical clock, exclusive. Reservations in `MATCHED`, `SHIPPED`, `IN_POSSESSION`, `RETURN_INITIATED`, `RETURNED`, `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED` never enter the scope under either variant.

Within that scope, the turn count is the number of reservations in scope; the claims in scope are the damage claims on those reservations whose `state` is `CHARGED` or `WAIVED`, so claims still `OPEN`, `ASSESSED` or `DISPUTED` and claims already `DISMISSED` are all excluded — only settled outcomes, whether the member paid or the waiver did, count against them. The late count is the number of reservations in scope whose `late_days` is greater than zero. The claim rate is the number of claims in scope divided by the turn count, with the divisor floored at 1. The severity index is the sum of severity weights over the claims in scope — 1 for `MINOR`, 2 for `MODERATE`, 3 for `SEVERE` and 4 for `LOSS` — divided by the number of claims in scope, again with the divisor floored at 1. The late rate is the late count divided by the turn count, with the divisor floored at 1. The raw score is 100 multiplied by the sum of three weighted terms: 0.5 times the claim rate, plus 0.3 times the smaller of 1.0 and the severity index divided by 4.0, plus 0.2 times the late rate. `damage_risk_score` is that raw value rounded half up, and rounding happens exactly once, at the very end, on the composed weighted sum — never on the intermediate rates. The score therefore lies between 0 and 100 inclusive. `damage_risk_band` is `LOW` when the score is at most 20, `ELEVATED` when it is at most 49, and `HIGH` otherwise, so 20 is `LOW`, 21 is `ELEVATED`, 49 is `ELEVATED` and 50 is `HIGH`.

The read returns `member_id`, the `variant` in force, `claim_count` as the number of claims in scope, `severity_points` as the unaveraged sum of severity weights over those claims, `late_return_count` as the late count, and the `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band` above. Because `ROLLING_12M` drops an old turn from both the numerator and the denominator, the same stored rows yield a different score under the two variants and can yield a different band. The `LIFETIME` variant is additionally recomputed and persisted onto the member's own `damage_risk_score` and `damage_risk_band` fields on every damage-claim creation and on every dispute resolution, which is why a member row's stored risk can differ from a `ROLLING_12M` read of the same member.

**Per-garment economics.** A style's unit economics are read by an inventory planner or a platform administrator, again with a `variant` query parameter, and the two variants read entirely different data.

The `REALIZED` variant, which is the default, is chronological actuals. Its unit set is every `RentalUnit` of the style in **any** `availability_state`, including `RETIRED` and `SOLD` — a fleet's economics must not improve merely because its worst garments were written off. For each unit, revenue is the unit's `realized_revenue_cents`, itself the sum of `revenue_attributed_cents` over that unit's `CLOSED` reservations; recovery is the sum of `net_fee_cents` over the damage claims on that unit whose `state` is `CHARGED`; end-of-life value is the sum of `net_recovery_cents` over the resale listings on that unit whose `state` is `SOLD`; and servicing is the unit's `cumulative_servicing_cost_cents`. That unit's margin is revenue plus recovery plus end-of-life value, minus the unit's `acquisition_cost_cents`, minus servicing. The unit's margin per turn is its margin divided by its `turns_completed`, with the divisor floored at 1, rounded half up.

The rounding order for the fleet-level figure is **pinned**: round per unit first, then average. `avg_margin_per_turn_cents` is the sum of the per-unit rounded margins-per-turn, divided by the number of units, rounded half up. Summing all unit margins and dividing once by total turns is a different number and is explicitly not what this field means; nor is `avg_margin_per_turn_cents` derived from any of the other averaged fields.

The `REALIZED` response reports `style_id`, the `variant`, `unit_count` as the number of units, and five further averages each computed as a sum over units divided by the unit count and rounded half up: `avg_acquisition_cost_cents` over `acquisition_cost_cents`; `avg_revenue_per_unit_cents` over revenue **plus recovery** for each unit, so charged damage fees are counted as revenue recovered rather than as a separate line; `avg_servicing_cost_per_unit_cents` over servicing; `avg_eol_value_cents` over end-of-life value; and `avg_margin_per_unit_cents` over the per-unit margins. `avg_margin_per_turn_cents` is the pinned value above. When the style has no units at all, `unit_count` is 0 and every field whose name begins with `avg_` is 0; no division by zero occurs.

The `PROJECTED` variant is forward-looking plan arithmetic over a single notional garment, and it reads no `RentalUnit`, `DamageClaim` or `ResaleListing` row whatsoever. Let the planned turns be the style's `expected_rental_turns_per_unit`. The projected unit margin is the reference revenue of 3,200 cents per turn multiplied by the planned turns, plus the style's `planned_eol_value_cents`, minus the style's `acquisition_cost_cents`, minus a flat projected servicing cost of 2,000 cents per turn multiplied by the planned turns. The response reports `unit_count` as exactly 1; `avg_acquisition_cost_cents` as the style's `acquisition_cost_cents`; `avg_revenue_per_unit_cents` as 3,200 multiplied by the planned turns; `avg_servicing_cost_per_unit_cents` as 2,000 multiplied by the planned turns; `avg_eol_value_cents` as the style's `planned_eol_value_cents`; `avg_margin_per_unit_cents` as the projected unit margin; and `avg_margin_per_turn_cents` as the projected unit margin divided by the planned turns, rounded half up. Because the two variants read disjoint inputs, the same stored data yields two different numbers under the same field names, and a planner comparing them is comparing plan against outcome.
