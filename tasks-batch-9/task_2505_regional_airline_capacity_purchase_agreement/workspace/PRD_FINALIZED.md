# Regional CPA Settlement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A capacity purchase agreement (CPA) is the commercial spine of regional airline flying. A mainline partner buys a fixed quantity of departures from a regional carrier, brands them as its own, and pays for them through a monthly settlement that mixes a flat capacity fee with per-block-hour and per-departure rates, an ownership fee for tails the regional itself owns, a set of pass-through cost categories billed at cost, and a performance overlay that can either reward or fine the operator. Every one of those components is computed from operational facts — hours flown, departures completed, bags mishandled, arrivals inside the grace window — that are reported by one party and audited by the other. The result is a settlement process that is far less about arithmetic than about agreeing on which facts count.

This API exists because that agreement is currently reached in spreadsheets. Cancellations are argued over for weeks because nobody has a single authoritative record of whether a given cancellation was the regional carrier's fault or the weather's; pass-through invoices are approved by the same person who filed them; a pilot resignation at a small base silently strands aircraft that the mainline is still paying an ownership fee on; and a corrected cancellation classification arriving after an invoice has been paid leaves two ledgers that disagree by exactly one departure. The system specified here replaces all of that with an explicit, auditable state machine per artefact, derived counters that are always recomputed rather than hand-maintained, and compensating actions that return conserved quantities to their exact pre-event values rather than approximating them.

Everything in this document is mounted under the `/cpa` prefix. All resources are addressed from a flat root — there are no nested collection URLs — and parentage is carried in foreign-key attributes plus filtered list lookups. The service is fully deterministic and reads no wall clock: every timestamp it writes comes from a logical clock that only an administrator can advance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that every endpoint shares: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and audit fields, precision, cascade semantics, concurrency guards, and the order in which rejections are evaluated.

### 2.1. Acting Parties and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. The five roles are `CPAAdministrator`, `MainlineScheduler`, `RegionalOpsController`, `BaseChiefPilot`, and `BrandAuditor`. When the headers are well-formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles map onto the two contracting sides plus two neutral functions. `CPAAdministrator` is the contract administrator: it owns agreement formation, activation and termination, fleet transitions, pass-through reconciliation, scorecard recomputation, invoice issuance and voiding, cancellation finalization, and the logical clock. `MainlineScheduler` is the mainline partner's schedule and negotiation desk: it publishes settlement months and flight legs, classifies cancellations, opens and revises extension offers. `RegionalOpsController` is the regional carrier's operations control: it opens crew bases, registers and inducts tails, hires pilots into the pipeline, reports legs as flown or cancelled, files pass-through estimates and actuals, disputes classifications, counters extension offers, and drives brand remediation. `BaseChiefPilot` moves pilots through training and records attrition. `BrandAuditor` raises brand-standard findings and closes them once remediation has been verified. All five roles may read every collection.

Four of the five roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `CPAAdministrator`, `MainlineScheduler`, `RegionalOpsController`, or `BrandAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the segregation-of-duties rules need it — a pass-through claim stores the identifier that filed its actual in `actual_submitted_by_user_id` and the identifier that adjudicated it in `reconciled_by_user_id`, and those two values are compared to each other, never to a resource identifier.

`BaseChiefPilot` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `BaseChiefPilot`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the `CrewBase` that chief pilot commands, and ownership is checked by comparing the target pilot's `base_id` to `X-User-ID`. A chief pilot acting on a pilot who belongs to a different base is rejected with `ERR_BASE_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403); this check guards both the pilot-advancement and the pilot-attrition actions.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside that inclusive range is a structurally invalid request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the record `id` ascending as the tie-break when two records share a creation timestamp. Newly created records therefore appear last. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered set before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether records remain beyond the returned window.

Every collection additionally accepts filters drawn from its own attributes; those filters are introduced alongside the workflows that create the records in §3.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Version Semantics

Every persisted resource in this service — agreements, crew bases, aircraft, pilots, schedule cycles, flight legs, cancellation claims, pass-through claims, scorecards, invoices, brand audits and extension negotiations — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted unique identifier formatted as a UUID. It carries no type prefix.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the resource was first created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation.

There are no exceptions: no resource in this service omits `updated_at` or `version`. Crucially, a mutation for these purposes includes a change to a *derived* attribute. When a pilot advances and the base's `line_qualified_pilot_count` therefore changes, the crew base has been mutated: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, even though nobody addressed the crew base directly. The same applies to a schedule cycle whose counters move because a leg was completed, to an agreement whose `settled_to_date_usd` moves because an invoice was issued, and to an aircraft that the server grounds on its own initiative. Each affected resource is bumped once per triggering request.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Contract Expiry

The service reads no wall clock. It maintains a single logical current UTC instant, which begins at the fixed value `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the service starts and changes only when an administrator drives it forward through the clock-setting capability, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`. Every timestamp the service writes on its own initiative — `activated_at` on an agreement, `inducted_at` on an aircraft, `closed_at` on a schedule cycle, `issued_final_at` on an invoice, and the `created_at` / `updated_at` of every resource — is read from that logical clock.

Setting the clock also performs one sweep. Immediately after the new instant is installed, every agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `term_end_date` falls strictly before the calendar date of the new logical instant is moved to `EXPIRED`, each such agreement's `version` incrementing by 1. Agreements are swept in ascending `created_at` order so that the observable outcome does not depend on sweep order. Expiry deliberately leaves the agreement's schedule cycles alone: a cycle sitting in `PLANNED` is not touched, but it can never be opened again, because opening requires a governing agreement in `ACTIVE`. The clock endpoint returns no resource payload.

### 2.6. Money, Block Hours and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are decimal values carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding step in this document uses the round-half-up convention. Monetary values supplied by a client must be strictly greater than zero and must not carry more than two decimal places; a violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Block hours are carried to 3 decimal places, must be strictly greater than zero on input, and may not carry more than three decimals. Percentages and per-thousand rates computed by the system are rounded to 2 decimal places. Integer-valued derived quantities are produced with truncation toward zero wherever this document says a value is floored.

Two rounding subtleties matter enough to state globally. First, where a total is formed from several already-rounded two-decimal addends, the addition is exact and no further rounding step is applied. Second, where a per-item money amount is defined, the rounding happens at the item and the *rounded* items are then summed; the alternative of summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is not the behaviour specified here, and it produces a different total in cents.

### 2.7. Derived Fields and Cascade Propagation

A large share of the attributes in this system are derived — they are recomputed by the server from the current state of related records and are never accepted from a client. Derived attributes are always kept immediately consistent: there is no deferred or batched recomputation, so a read that follows a successful write always observes the settled result of every cascade that write triggered.

Cascades in this domain are genuinely multi-hop, and each hop is caused by the previous one rather than being an independent side effect of the original request. Terminating an agreement closes its crew bases, and *because* those bases are now closed, aircraft still awaiting induction at them are withdrawn. A pilot leaving a base lowers that base's qualified headcount, and *because* the headcount fell the base's derived staffing figures are recomputed, and *because* the recomputed allocation dropped below the number of tails assigned there, aircraft are grounded. Finalizing a cancellation classification can move a cycle's counters, which can make that cycle's scorecard stale, which voids that cycle's invoices, which can unwind money that had already been settled and push the cycle back out of settlement. Each entity touched anywhere in such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, and the response to the triggering request reflects the fully settled end state.

### 2.8. Ledger Conservation: The Departure Ledger and Settled Money

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and their conservation is what makes the settlement auditable.

The **departure ledger** of a settlement month is the identity that `departure_ledger_total` must equal `published_departures` at every observable moment. The three ledger positions are `metric_completed_departures`, `cancelled_controllable_count`, and `cancelled_uncontrollable_count`; `metric_completed_departures` is defined as `published_departures` less the two cancellation counters, and `departure_ledger_total` is the sum of all three positions. A cancellation therefore moves one departure from the completed position into a cancellation position without changing the total, and a reinstatement moves it back to the exact value it held before the cancellation. Because the ledger may never go negative, a cancellation that would push the combined cancellation counts past `published_departures` is refused outright with `ERR_DEPARTURE_LEDGER_OVERFLOW` (HTTP 422).

**Settled money** is the identity behind `settled_to_date_usd` on an agreement: it is the sum of `total_usd` over every invoice in `ISSUED` status belonging to any of that agreement's settlement months. Issuing a final invoice adds that invoice's `total_usd` to the figure; voiding an issued invoice subtracts exactly the same amount, returning the figure to its pre-issue value rather than to an approximation of it. The companion counter `open_invoice_count` is the number of invoices in `DRAFT` status across the agreement's settlement months.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Guards on Contract and Cycle Writes

Two write operations in this service can invalidate work a client has already observed, and both are guarded by an optimistic concurrency check against the parent record's `version`.

Opening an extension round against an agreement requires the request to carry `expected_agreement_version`, holding the `version` the client last read from that agreement. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a successful offer legitimately supersedes every standing offer or counter on that agreement to `WITHDRAWN` and advances `extension_round_count`; a writer holding an older agreement version is reasoning about a term sheet that no longer exists.

Creating an invoice against a settlement month requires the request to carry `expected_schedule_cycle_version`, holding the `version` the client last read from that cycle. This field is likewise not optional, and a mismatch with the cycle's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the guard protects the settlement figures: a new invoice supersedes the cycle's standing draft of the same variant, and a client whose view of the cycle predates a cancellation-counter change, a pass-through reconciliation, or an earlier invoice would otherwise bill a settlement the cycle no longer supports.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.10. Resource Addressing and Reference Resolution

Every collection is addressed from the flat `/cpa` root, so each path carries at most one identifier: `agreementId`, `baseId`, `aircraftId`, `pilotId`, `cycleId`, `legId`, `claimId`, `scorecardId`, `invoiceId`, `auditId`, or `negotiationId`. Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces its own 404: `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_BASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PILOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CANCELLATION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PASSTHROUGH_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BRAND_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND` respectively.

Parentage travels in the request body instead of the URL. When a creation request names a related record — `agreement_id`, `assigned_base_id`, `base_id`, `cycle_id`, `aircraft_id`, or `captain_pilot_id` — that reference is resolved before any business rule is evaluated, and a reference that does not resolve yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). This body-reference resolution applies to the creation of crew bases, aircraft, pilots, schedule cycles, flight legs, pass-through claims, and brand audits.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Because both contracting parties transact through the same API, two decisions may never be taken by the party that put them on the table.

Pass-through reconciliation is the first. The identifier recorded in `actual_submitted_by_user_id` when the invoiced actual was filed may not be the `X-User-ID` that later adjudicates the claim, even though both act as `CPAAdministrator`; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Extension acceptance is the second. A round standing in `OFFERED` was authored by the mainline partner, so only `RegionalOpsController` may accept it; a round standing in `COUNTERED` was authored by the regional carrier, so only `MainlineScheduler` may accept it. Acceptance by the authoring side carries the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rejection. Note that this is a stricter rule than the endpoint's role list, which admits both roles: the role list decides whether the caller may attempt the acceptance at all, and the segregation rule then decides whether this particular round is theirs to accept.

### 2.12. Rejection Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, they are evaluated in one fixed order so that the surfaced `error_id` is deterministic. The order is:

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Existence of every identifier in the path, left to right — the resource-specific 404 codes of §2.10.
4. Resolution of every foreign key in the request body — `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Structural validity of the body and query: required fields present, enum members recognised, monetary amounts strictly positive with at most two decimals, block hours strictly positive with at most three decimals, `published_departures` at least 0, `findings_count` at least 1, `pilots_required_per_aircraft` at least 1, `aircraft_allocation_cap` at least 1, and pagination `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. Chief-pilot base ownership on the pilot actions — `ERR_BASE_NOT_OWNED`.
7. Governing agreement in `ACTIVE` status, which is required to induct a tail, to open a settlement month, and to advance a pilot — `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.
8. The optimistic concurrency check of §2.9 — `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`.
9. Whether the target's current state declares the requested transition at all — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
10. Whether the target is already in one of its terminal states — `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.
11. Operational safety rules, in this internal order: aircraft availability (`ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_AVAILABLE`), then captain qualification (`ERR_PILOT_NOT_QUALIFIED`), then base staffing admissibility for induction (`ERR_BASE_UNDERSTAFFED`).
12. Referential business rules, in this internal order: cross-agreement consistency (`ERR_CROSS_AGREEMENT_REFERENCE`), then duplicate pass-through category (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSTHROUGH_CATEGORY`), then scoped natural-key uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`).
13. Temporal geometry, in this internal order: interval ordering (`ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`), then the aircraft turn buffer (`ERR_AIRCRAFT_TURN_CONFLICT`), then the captain's rolling block-hour cap (`ERR_PILOT_DUTY_CAP_EXCEEDED`).
14. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
15. Negotiation-loop bounds — `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` or `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.
16. Remaining operational preconditions, in this internal order: open legs blocking a cycle close (`ERR_CYCLE_HAS_OPEN_LEGS`), scheduled legs blocking a fleet transition (`ERR_AIRCRAFT_HAS_OPEN_LEGS`), missing remediation evidence (`ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`), unverified remediation blocking a finding closure (`ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_VERIFIED`), and an already-final cancellation classification blocking reinstatement (`ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_FINALIZED`).
17. Fiscal rules, in this internal order: the base fleet allocation cap (`ERR_FLEET_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`), invoice variant eligibility for issuance (`ERR_INVOICE_VARIANT_NOT_FINAL`), unreconciled pass-throughs (`ERR_PASSTHROUGH_UNRECONCILED`), a stale scorecard (`ERR_SCORECARD_STALE`), and departure-ledger overflow (`ERR_DEPARTURE_LEDGER_OVERFLOW`).
18. Extension term ordering — `ERR_EXTENSION_TERM_NOT_LATER`.

The practical consequence is that a request failing several of these surfaces only the earliest code in the list. Inducting a tail at a base that is both understaffed and at its allocation cap reports `ERR_BASE_UNDERSTAFFED`, never `ERR_FLEET_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`. Scheduling a leg on a grounded tail with an unqualified captain reports `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. And a request that fails both the concurrency guard and a state check reports the stale-version code, because the concurrency check is evaluated first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Capacity Purchase Agreement

Everything in this system hangs off an **Agreement**, the rate card and term sheet that the two carriers have signed. A `CPAAdministrator` registers one by naming the two parties — `mainline_partner_name` for the partner whose brand the flights carry and `regional_carrier_name` for the operator selling the capacity — and by declaring the five money terms and the term expiry. `fixed_monthly_fee_usd` is the flat monthly capacity fee. `block_hour_rate_usd` is paid per block hour flown and `departure_rate_usd` per completed departure. `aircraft_ownership_fee_usd` is a monthly per-tail fee that is earned only by tails the regional carrier itself owns. `term_end_date` is the contract expiry, expressed as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date. Those four fee and rate values must each be strictly greater than zero.

Two further inputs bound the performance overlay. `incentive_pool_cap_usd` is the hard ceiling on any single month's performance incentive, and `penalty_cap_usd` is the hard floor on any single month's performance penalty, expressed as a positive magnitude. These two are the one exception to the strictly-positive rule for money: each must be at least zero, and a value of zero means the corresponding side of the overlay can never pay out.

A newly registered agreement lands in `DRAFT` with `extension_round_count` at 0, `settled_to_date_usd` at 0.00, `open_invoice_count` at 0, and `activated_at` null. Nothing may operate under it yet. Activation by a `CPAAdministrator` is permitted only from `DRAFT`, and only once at least one crew base has been opened against the agreement with its `lifecycle_status` still `ACTIVE` — an agreement with no live base has nowhere to put aeroplanes, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The `DRAFT → ACTIVE` transition stamps `activated_at` with the logical clock instant and increments the agreement's `version`. The complete `status` vocabulary of an agreement is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `EXTENSION_PENDING`, `EXPIRED`, and `TERMINATED`, of which the last two are terminal.

Termination is the deliberate wind-down, available to a `CPAAdministrator` from either `ACTIVE` or `EXTENSION_PENDING` and recorded with a `termination_reason`. It is refused while any settlement month of the agreement is still `OPEN`; that too surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because a month still accepting operational reports cannot be settled after the contract has been stood down. When it succeeds, the agreement moves to `TERMINATED` and a two-hop cascade runs. First hop: every crew base of the agreement moves `lifecycle_status` from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED`. Second hop, caused by the first: because each of those bases is now `CLOSED`, every aircraft assigned to it that is still sitting in `PENDING_INDUCTION` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Any extension round still standing in `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` at the same time. The agreement's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every base, tail and negotiation the cascade touched.

The third way out of `ACTIVE` is expiry, and it is not something a client requests: as described in §2.5, driving the logical clock past `term_end_date` moves the agreement to `EXPIRED`. Note the asymmetry with termination — expiry runs no cascade at all. Bases stay `ACTIVE`, tails stay where they are, and the only observable consequences are the `status` change, the `version` bump, and the fact that no further cycle of that agreement can be opened.

One scoping rule is worth stating here because it governs the whole of §3: the already-terminal guard that yields `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` applies to the action endpoints of aircraft, pilots, flight legs, cancellation claims, pass-through claims, invoices and extension negotiations. Agreements, crew bases, settlement months, scorecards and brand audits are not in its scope; an inadmissible action on one of those reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` instead. Agreement listings may be narrowed by `status`.

### 3.2. Crew Bases, the Hiring Pipeline and the Staffing Arithmetic

A **CrewBase** is where crews live and where the mainline's fleet allocation is capped. A `RegionalOpsController` opens one against an agreement by supplying the `agreement_id`, a three-letter `station_code`, the contractual `pilots_required_per_aircraft` ratio, and the `aircraft_allocation_cap` — the maximum number of tails the mainline may allocate to that station. The ratio and the cap must each be at least 1, and `station_code` must be unique among the bases of the same `agreement_id`; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409). A fresh base opens with `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE` and every derived figure at zero: `line_qualified_pilot_count`, `in_training_pilot_count`, `assigned_aircraft_count`, `required_pilot_count`, `headcount_shortfall`, `pipeline_shortfall`, and `allocatable_aircraft_count` are all 0, and `staffing_posture` reads `STAFFED`. The only other value `lifecycle_status` ever takes is `CLOSED`, and that value is written exclusively by the agreement-termination cascade of §3.1 — there is no client-facing way to close a base.

**Pilots** are hired into a base's pipeline by a `RegionalOpsController`, who supplies the `base_id`, an `employee_ref` that must be unique within that base, the `seat` the pilot occupies (`CAPTAIN` or `FIRST_OFFICER`), and the `hire_class_date` of the class they joined, as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date. Every new pilot starts in `PIPELINE` with `attrition_destination` null. The full pilot vocabulary is `PIPELINE`, `IN_TRAINING`, `LINE_QUALIFIED`, `ATTRITED_TO_MAINLINE`, and `RESIGNED`, the last two being terminal.

Only two of those five states are counted. `line_qualified_pilot_count` is the number of pilots at the base whose status is `LINE_QUALIFIED`, and `in_training_pilot_count` the number whose status is `IN_TRAINING`. Pilots still in `PIPELINE`, and pilots who have left through either terminal state, are excluded from both counts. The base's `assigned_aircraft_count` is the number of tails whose assigned base is this one and whose status is either `IN_SERVICE` or `GROUNDED_STAFFING` — a tail awaiting induction, transitioning, transitioned or withdrawn does not consume crew.

From those three counts the base derives its whole staffing picture, and it does so twice, on two deliberately different bases. `required_pilot_count` is the number of assigned tails multiplied by `pilots_required_per_aircraft`. The **headcount basis** counts only line-qualified crews: `headcount_shortfall` is the amount by which `required_pilot_count` exceeds `line_qualified_pilot_count`, or 0 when the requirement is already met. The **pipeline basis** additionally credits the training pipeline net of expected attrition: each pilot in training counts as sixty-five hundredths of a line pilot — thirty-five hundredths of the pipeline is discounted for the attrition the base expects before qualification — and the credited contribution is floored to a whole pilot before being added to `line_qualified_pilot_count`. `pipeline_shortfall` is the amount by which `required_pilot_count` exceeds that pipeline-credited availability, or 0 when it does not exceed it.

`staffing_posture` bands the headcount-basis result into three values. It reads `STAFFED` when `headcount_shortfall` is exactly 0; `SHORT` when the shortfall is greater than 0 but no larger than a single aircraft's worth of crew, that is, no larger than `pilots_required_per_aircraft`; and `CRITICAL` when the shortfall exceeds one aircraft's worth of crew. `allocatable_aircraft_count`, by contrast, is a planning figure computed on the pipeline basis: it is the smaller of `aircraft_allocation_cap` and the pipeline-credited availability divided by `pilots_required_per_aircraft` with the quotient floored to a whole aircraft. The two bases disagree whenever anybody is in training, and that disagreement is intentional: admission of a new tail into service and the `staffing_posture` label both use the headcount basis, while the number of tails the base can *hold* — and therefore which tails get grounded or returned to service — uses the pipeline basis. A base can consequently be `SHORT` for induction purposes and still retain its full allocation.

All six derived figures — `required_pilot_count`, `headcount_shortfall`, `pipeline_shortfall`, `staffing_posture`, `allocatable_aircraft_count`, and whichever pilot counter moved — are recomputed on every pilot status change, on every induction of a tail into service, and on every departure of a tail into transition. Crew-base listings may be filtered by `agreement_id` or by `staffing_posture`; pilot listings by `base_id`, `status`, or `seat`.

A `BaseChiefPilot` walks pilots forward one step at a time, naming the destination in `to_status`. The first step, `PIPELINE → IN_TRAINING`, requires that the pilot is in `PIPELINE`, that the acting chief pilot owns the base per §2.1, and that the base's `lifecycle_status` is still `ACTIVE`; a base already closed refuses the advance with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It raises `in_training_pilot_count` by 1 and then recomputes `pipeline_shortfall` and `allocatable_aircraft_count`, which — because the pipeline basis credits trainees — can be enough on its own to bring grounded tails back into service. The second step, `IN_TRAINING → LINE_QUALIFIED`, requires that the pilot is in `IN_TRAINING` and that the chief pilot owns the base; it lowers `in_training_pilot_count` by 1 and raises `line_qualified_pilot_count` by 1, then recomputes `headcount_shortfall`, `pipeline_shortfall`, `staffing_posture` and `allocatable_aircraft_count`, and likewise may restore grounded tails. Both steps require the governing agreement to be `ACTIVE` and increment both the pilot's `version` and the base's `version`. A request naming a `to_status` the pilot cannot reach from where it currently stands is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Attrition is the counter-movement, also driven by a `BaseChiefPilot`, who names a `destination`. A `destination` of `MAINLINE` records the pilot as `ATTRITED_TO_MAINLINE` and writes `attrition_destination` as `MAINLINE`; it is permitted only from `LINE_QUALIFIED`, since only a qualified pilot is a candidate the mainline partner would hire, and an attempt from any other state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `destination` of `OTHER` records the pilot as `RESIGNED` with `attrition_destination` set to `OTHER`, and is permitted from `PIPELINE`, `IN_TRAINING` or `LINE_QUALIFIED`. In every case the chief pilot must own the base. The counter that moves is the one matching the pilot's *prior* status: `line_qualified_pilot_count` falls by 1 for a pilot leaving `LINE_QUALIFIED`, `in_training_pilot_count` falls by 1 for a pilot leaving `IN_TRAINING`, and no counter moves at all for a pilot leaving `PIPELINE`, since pipeline pilots were never counted.

Losing a line-qualified pilot is the sharpest cascade in the crewing domain, and it runs to three hops. Hop one lowers `line_qualified_pilot_count`. Hop two, caused by hop one, recomputes `headcount_shortfall`, `staffing_posture`, `pipeline_shortfall` and `allocatable_aircraft_count` — a base that was `STAFFED` can land in `SHORT` or `CRITICAL` in a single departure. Hop three, caused by hop two, applies when the recomputed `allocatable_aircraft_count` has fallen below `assigned_aircraft_count`: tails at that base are grounded, exactly as many as the difference between the two figures, using the selector described in §3.3. The pilot's `version`, the base's `version`, and the `version` of every tail that was grounded each increment by 1. Resignation from `LINE_QUALIFIED` runs precisely the same three-hop cascade as attrition to the mainline; the two differ only in the terminal status and the recorded `attrition_destination`.

### 3.3. Fleet Registration, Induction, Grounding and Renewal

An **Aircraft** row is one tail flying under one agreement out of one base. A `RegionalOpsController` registers it with the `agreement_id` it flies under, the `assigned_base_id` whose crews operate it, its `tail_number` registration, its certified `seat_count`, and its `ownership` form. `tail_number` must be unique among the tails of the same `agreement_id`, and `seat_count` must be at least 1. `ownership` is one of `REGIONAL_OWNED`, `MAINLINE_OWNED`, or `LEASED`, and only a `REGIONAL_OWNED` tail ever earns the agreement's `aircraft_ownership_fee_usd`. Registration leaves the tail in `PENDING_INDUCTION` with `transition_reason`, `target_ownership`, `grounded_reason` and `inducted_at` all null, and — importantly — leaves the base's counters untouched: a registered tail consumes no crew until it is inducted. The full status vocabulary is `PENDING_INDUCTION`, `IN_SERVICE`, `GROUNDED_STAFFING`, `TRANSITION_PENDING`, `TRANSITIONED` and `WITHDRAWN`, the last two terminal. Fleet listings may be narrowed by `agreement_id`, `assigned_base_id`, or `status`.

Induction is the moment a tail starts consuming crew and earning variable revenue, and it is the one place where the headcount basis of §3.2 gates an operation. A `RegionalOpsController` may move a tail from `PENDING_INDUCTION` to `IN_SERVICE` only when the governing agreement is `ACTIVE`, the assigned base's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`, and two capacity tests both pass. The staffing test asks whether the base would still be crewed after the induction: the number of tails it would then hold, one more than the current `assigned_aircraft_count`, multiplied by `pilots_required_per_aircraft`, must be no greater than `line_qualified_pilot_count`. Trainees do not count here. Failing that test is `ERR_BASE_UNDERSTAFFED` (HTTP 409). The allocation test asks whether the mainline is entitled to put another tail there at all: one more than the current `assigned_aircraft_count` must be no greater than `aircraft_allocation_cap`, and failing it is `ERR_FLEET_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The staffing test is evaluated first. On success `inducted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the base's `assigned_aircraft_count` rises by 1, all of the base's derived staffing figures are recomputed, and both the tail's `version` and the base's `version` increment.

Grounding and restoration are the loop that crew supply drives, and neither is ever requested by a client — both are consequences of a base's staffing recompute. When a recompute leaves `assigned_aircraft_count` greater than `allocatable_aircraft_count`, the server grounds tails: the candidate set is the tails at that base whose status is `IN_SERVICE`, ranked by `created_at` descending so that the most recently inducted tails are grounded first, with `tail_number` in ascending lexicographic order as the tie-break when two tails share a creation timestamp, and the record `id` ascending as the final tie-break should two candidates share both. The server grounds exactly as many tails as the difference between `assigned_aircraft_count` and `allocatable_aircraft_count`. Each grounded tail moves `IN_SERVICE → GROUNDED_STAFFING`, has `grounded_reason` written as `STAFFING_SHORTFALL`, and has its `version` incremented. An empty candidate set is a silent no-op, not an error.

Restoration runs on the same trigger in the opposite direction. When a recompute leaves the number of `IN_SERVICE` tails at the base below `allocatable_aircraft_count`, the server returns grounded tails to service: the candidate set is the tails at that base in `GROUNDED_STAFFING`, ranked by `created_at` ascending so the longest-grounded tails come back first, again with `tail_number` ascending as the tie-break and the record `id` ascending as the final tie-break. The number restored is `allocatable_aircraft_count` less the count of tails currently `IN_SERVICE` at that base. Each restored tail moves `GROUNDED_STAFFING → IN_SERVICE`, has `grounded_reason` cleared back to null, and has its `version` incremented. Because the allocatable figure is computed on the pipeline basis, simply moving a pilot into training can be enough to bring a tail back — `IN_SERVICE → GROUNDED_STAFFING → IN_SERVICE` is a true loop, traversable any number of times as crew supply at the base rises and falls.

Fleet renewal takes a tail out of the paid fleet in two steps, both reserved to a `CPAAdministrator`. The first step moves a tail from either `IN_SERVICE` or `GROUNDED_STAFFING` into `TRANSITION_PENDING`, recording why in `transition_reason` — one of `FLEET_RENEWAL`, `LEASE_EXPIRY`, or `PARTNER_RECALL` — and what the tail will become in `target_ownership`, one of `REGIONAL_OWNED`, `MAINLINE_OWNED`, or `LEASED`. It is refused while the tail still has any leg in `SCHEDULED` status, with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_HAS_OPEN_LEGS` (HTTP 409); an aeroplane cannot be pulled out from under a published schedule. On success `grounded_reason` is cleared to null, the base's `assigned_aircraft_count` falls by 1, and the base's staffing figures are recomputed — which, since the requirement just dropped, will frequently restore other grounded tails at the same base in the same request. Both the tail's `version` and the base's `version` increment. The second step, available only from `TRANSITION_PENDING`, moves the tail to the terminal `TRANSITIONED` status and flips `ownership` to the recorded `target_ownership`; only the tail's `version` moves.

One consequence of these states deserves emphasis because it is where operations and money interact. A tail in `GROUNDED_STAFFING` cannot fly — but it is still counted in `assigned_aircraft_count`, and if it is `REGIONAL_OWNED` it is still counted in the invoice's `ownership_aircraft_count` and still earns its monthly ownership fee. A tail in `TRANSITION_PENDING`, `TRANSITIONED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `PENDING_INDUCTION` is counted in neither.

### 3.4. The Settlement Month: Cycles and Flight Legs

A **ScheduleCycle** is one settlement month. The `MainlineScheduler` publishes it against an `agreement_id`, names the month in `cycle_month` as `YYYY-MM`, and declares in `published_departures` how many departures it is buying — the denominator basis for every performance metric that follows. `cycle_month` must be unique per `agreement_id`, and `published_departures` must be at least 0. A cycle progresses `PLANNED → OPEN → CLOSED → SETTLED`, and can return from `SETTLED` to `CLOSED`; it has no terminal state. Cycle listings may be filtered by `agreement_id` or `status`.

At creation the month's operational counters are all empty — `recorded_completed_legs`, `open_leg_count`, `cancelled_controllable_count`, `cancelled_uncontrollable_count`, `enplaned_bags_total`, `mishandled_bags_total` and `on_time_leg_count` are 0, `credited_block_hours` is 0.000, `estimated_passthrough_total_usd` and `passthrough_settlement_usd` are 0.00, and `closed_at` is null. The two ledger positions, however, are not zero at creation: because `metric_completed_departures` is `published_departures` less the two cancellation counters, and because `departure_ledger_total` is the sum of the three positions, both open at exactly `published_departures`. That is the §2.8 identity holding at the very first observable moment of the month.

Opening the month is a `MainlineScheduler` action permitted only from `PLANNED` and only while the governing agreement is `ACTIVE`; against an agreement that has drifted into `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED`, `DRAFT` or `EXTENSION_PENDING` it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Opening moves the cycle to `OPEN` and increments its `version`, and nothing else.

**FlightLegs** are the operational atoms. The `MainlineScheduler` schedules one by naming the `cycle_id` it is credited to, the `aircraft_id` operating it, the `captain_pilot_id` in command, the mainline-branded `flight_number`, the `origin` and `destination` station codes, the published `scheduled_departure_utc` and `scheduled_arrival_utc` instants, and the `planned_block_hours` the schedule allows. A new leg lands in `SCHEDULED` with `actual_block_hours`, `actual_arrival_utc` and `cancel_cause_code` null, `enplaned_bags` and `mishandled_bags` at 0, `on_time` false, `leg_variable_payment_usd` at 0.00, and `reinstate_count` at 0; the cycle's `open_leg_count` rises by 1. The leg vocabulary is `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, of which only `COMPLETED` is terminal — a cancelled leg can still come back. Leg listings may be narrowed by `cycle_id`, `aircraft_id`, `captain_pilot_id`, or `status`.

Scheduling a leg is the most heavily guarded write in the system. The cycle must be `OPEN`. The tail must be `IN_SERVICE`: a tail in `PENDING_INDUCTION`, `GROUNDED_STAFFING`, `TRANSITION_PENDING`, `TRANSITIONED` or `WITHDRAWN` may not be scheduled, and the refusal is `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). The nominated captain must be `LINE_QUALIFIED`; anyone else earns `ERR_PILOT_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 409). Both the tail and the captain must belong to the same agreement as the cycle — the tail's `agreement_id` and the crew base of the captain must both resolve to the cycle's `agreement_id` — and a mismatch is `ERR_CROSS_AGREEMENT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409). `scheduled_arrival_utc` must be strictly later than `scheduled_departure_utc`, and a non-positive interval is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

Two temporal capacity rules then apply, and they have deliberately different geometry. The first is the **aircraft turn buffer**, and it is pairwise. Treat each leg as the half-open interval that starts at its `scheduled_departure_utc` and runs up to but not including its `scheduled_arrival_utc`, then inflate that interval by thirty-five minutes at each end. The new leg is compared against every other leg on the same `aircraft_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. The pair is acceptable only if they are cleanly separated in one direction or the other: either the new leg departs no earlier than thirty-five minutes after the other leg arrives, or the new leg arrives at least thirty-five minutes before the other leg departs. If neither separation holds for some existing leg, the request is rejected with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_TURN_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The boundary is inclusive on both sides — exactly thirty-five minutes of separation is accepted, and only strictly less than that conflicts.

The second is the **captain's rolling duty cap**, and it is aggregate capacity rather than pairwise geometry. Take the window that begins twenty-four hours before the new leg's `scheduled_departure_utc` and runs up to but not including twenty-four hours after it. Sum `planned_block_hours` across every leg flown by the same `captain_pilot_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and whose own `scheduled_departure_utc` falls inside that window, then add the new leg's `planned_block_hours`. If the resulting total exceeds sixteen block hours — sixteen point zero zero zero, to the three decimals block hours are carried at — the request is rejected with `ERR_PILOT_DUTY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A total of exactly 16.000 is accepted. Because this rule aggregates rather than compares pairs, two legs that never overlap in clock time, and are indeed many hours apart, can still collide through the sum.

Reporting a leg as flown is a `RegionalOpsController` action that moves it `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. It requires the cycle to be `OPEN`, the tail to be `IN_SERVICE` — a grounded or transitioning tail cannot report a completion, again `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_AVAILABLE` — and the reported `actual_arrival_utc` to be strictly later than the leg's `scheduled_departure_utc`, failing which `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The report carries four values: `actual_block_hours`, `actual_arrival_utc`, `enplaned_bags` and `mishandled_bags`. Completion then computes two derived fields on the leg. `on_time` is true when `actual_arrival_utc` is no later than `scheduled_arrival_utc` plus a fourteen-minute grace allowance; the boundary is inclusive, so an arrival exactly fourteen minutes late is on time and an arrival fourteen minutes and one second late is not. `leg_variable_payment_usd` is the leg's own variable earnings: the product of `actual_block_hours` and the agreement's `block_hour_rate_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimal places, plus the agreement's `departure_rate_usd`, which is already carried at two decimals.

Completion also rolls the month's counters forward: `recorded_completed_legs` rises by 1, `open_leg_count` falls by 1, `credited_block_hours` takes on the leg's `actual_block_hours` in addition to what it held, `enplaned_bags_total` and `mishandled_bags_total` absorb the two reported bag counts, and `on_time_leg_count` rises by 1 only when `on_time` came out true. The cancellation counters are untouched, and therefore `metric_completed_departures` and `departure_ledger_total` do not move on a completion — flying a departure is what `published_departures` already assumed. Both the leg's `version` and the cycle's `version` increment.

Cancelling a leg is the other `RegionalOpsController` report, permitted from `SCHEDULED` while the cycle is `OPEN`, and it names a `cause_code` from `CREW_UNAVAILABLE`, `MAINTENANCE`, `WEATHER`, `ATC`, `MAINLINE_REQUEST` or `SECURITY`. Before anything is written, the departure-ledger guard of §2.8 runs: if adding one more cancellation would take the sum of `cancelled_controllable_count` and `cancelled_uncontrollable_count` past `published_departures`, the cancellation is refused with `ERR_DEPARTURE_LEDGER_OVERFLOW` (HTTP 422). On success the leg moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_cause_code` recorded, `open_leg_count` falls by 1, and exactly one cancellation claim is opened for adjudication — described in §3.5. Because that claim is provisionally attributed to the regional carrier, `cancelled_controllable_count` rises by 1 and `metric_completed_departures` and `departure_ledger_total` are recomputed: the metric figure drops by one departure while the ledger total stays exactly equal to `published_departures`. The leg's `version` and the cycle's `version` both increment.

Reinstatement is the compensating action for a cancellation entered in error, and it is what makes the departure ledger conserved rather than merely monotone. A `RegionalOpsController` may move a leg `CANCELLED → SCHEDULED` while the cycle is `OPEN`, provided the tail is still `IN_SERVICE` — the availability rule of `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_AVAILABLE` guards reinstatement just as it guards scheduling and completion — and provided the leg's cancellation claim has not yet been finalized. A claim already in `FINALIZED` blocks the reinstatement with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409), because the two carriers have by then agreed on the attribution and the month's penalty basis is settled. On success `reinstate_count` rises by 1, `cancel_cause_code` is cleared to null, the claim is voided, `open_leg_count` rises by 1, and `cancelled_controllable_count` falls by 1 so that `metric_completed_departures` and `departure_ledger_total` return to their exact pre-cancellation values. Both `version` counters increment. A leg may be cancelled and reinstated repeatedly, and `reinstate_count` records how often.

Closing the month is a `MainlineScheduler` action permitted only from `OPEN`, and only once `open_leg_count` has reached 0 — every leg must have reached either `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. A month with unresolved legs is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_OPEN_LEGS` (HTTP 409). Closing stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, freezes the operational counters, computes the month's pass-through settlement per §3.6, and creates exactly one scorecard in `PUBLISHED` status carrying the full performance computation of §3.7. The cycle's `version` increments. One attribution rule bites at exactly this moment: any cancellation claim of the month that has not yet been finalized is treated as the regional carrier's responsibility for scoring purposes, as §3.5 explains.

### 3.5. Adjudicating Cancellation Controllability

Every cancelled departure raises exactly one **CancellationClaim**, created by the server as part of the cancellation itself — there is no way for a client to author one directly. The claim records the `leg_id` of the cancelled leg and, denormalised from that leg so the month's counters can be evaluated without a join, the `cycle_id` it belongs to. It copies the leg's `cancel_cause_code` into its own `cause_code`, opens with `status` `OPEN`, `classification` null, `dispute_round` 0 and `dispute_note` null. Its `effective_classification` opens as `CONTROLLABLE`. Claim listings may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `leg_id`, `status` or `classification`.

That opening attribution is the commercially significant default of the whole system. `classification` is the mainline partner's stated verdict and takes the value `CONTROLLABLE` or `UNCONTROLLABLE`. `effective_classification` is the attribution the settlement actually uses, and it equals `classification` only once the claim has reached `FINALIZED`; at every earlier point it is pinned to `CONTROLLABLE`, regardless of what the mainline has provisionally said. The burden of proof therefore sits with the regional carrier: an unresolved cancellation is counted against it until the two parties have formally agreed otherwise.

The two month-level counters are defined directly on that pair of attributes. `cancelled_controllable_count` is the number of claims of the month whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CLASSIFIED`, `DISPUTED` or `FINALIZED` and whose `effective_classification` is `CONTROLLABLE`. `cancelled_uncontrollable_count` is the number whose `status` is `FINALIZED` and whose `classification` is `UNCONTROLLABLE`. A claim in `VOIDED` appears in neither, which is precisely how reinstatement returns the ledger to its earlier state. The claim vocabulary is `OPEN`, `CLASSIFIED`, `DISPUTED`, `FINALIZED` and `VOIDED`, the last two terminal.

Classification is the `MainlineScheduler`'s act, admissible from either `OPEN` or `DISPUTED`, and it names the verdict in `classification`. It moves the claim to `CLASSIFIED` and records the verdict — overwriting any previous verdict when the claim is arriving back from a dispute — and it increments only the claim's `version`. It moves no cycle counter whatsoever, because `effective_classification` is still pinned to `CONTROLLABLE` until finalization. Consider a month with one cancellation which the mainline classifies as `UNCONTROLLABLE`: nothing in the month's ledger changes at that moment, and the scoring still treats the departure as the regional's fault.

Disputing is the regional carrier's reply. A `RegionalOpsController` may move a claim `CLASSIFIED → DISPUTED`, supplying its rationale in `dispute_note`, which replaces whatever rationale was recorded before. Each dispute raises `dispute_round` by 1, and the loop is bounded: a claim whose `dispute_round` has already reached two may not be disputed again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). Since classification is readmissible from `DISPUTED`, `CLASSIFIED → DISPUTED → CLASSIFIED` is a genuine bargaining loop, traversable at most twice before the regional carrier is out of objections.

Finalization is where the ledger finally moves, and it is the deepest cascade in the service. A `CPAAdministrator` may finalize a claim only from `CLASSIFIED` and only when `classification` is not null. Hop one sets `status` to `FINALIZED` and copies `classification` into `effective_classification`. Hop two follows from hop one and only bites when the agreed verdict is `UNCONTROLLABLE`: the claim leaves the controllable filter set and joins the uncontrollable one, so `cancelled_controllable_count` falls by 1 and `cancelled_uncontrollable_count` rises by 1, after which `metric_completed_departures` and `departure_ledger_total` are recomputed. Both come out numerically unchanged — one departure has simply moved between two cancellation positions — which is exactly what the §2.8 identity demands. A verdict of `CONTROLLABLE`, by contrast, moves no counter at all, because the claim was already sitting in the controllable set under its pinned attribution.

Hop three follows from hop two, and inherits its conditionality. Because the month's cancellation counters changed, any scorecard of that month still standing in `PUBLISHED` moves to `STALE` — the completion factor it published was computed from counters that no longer hold. Hop four follows from hop three: because that scorecard went stale, every invoice of the month whose status is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` is voided with `void_reason` set to `AUTO_SCORECARD_STALE`. Hop five follows from hop four: for a voided invoice that had been `ISSUED`, the agreement's `settled_to_date_usd` is reduced by that invoice's `total_usd` and the month itself reverts from `SETTLED` back to `CLOSED`; for each voided `DRAFT` invoice, `open_invoice_count` falls by 1. The `version` of the claim, of the month, of the scorecard, of every affected invoice and of the agreement each increment by 1. When the verdict is `CONTROLLABLE`, hops two through five do not run: no counter moved, so no scorecard goes stale and no invoice is voided.

The remaining path out of a claim is voiding, which the server performs on the client's behalf when a cancelled leg is reinstated. A claim in `OPEN`, `CLASSIFIED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `VOIDED`, leaves the controllable filter set so that `cancelled_controllable_count` falls by 1, and has its `version` incremented. All three source states behave identically. A claim already in `FINALIZED` is never voided — the reinstatement that would have caused it is itself refused, as §3.4 describes.

### 3.6. Pass-Through Cost Categories and Their Reconciliation

Three cost categories are billed at cost rather than through the rate card: `FUEL`, `AIRPORT_FEES` and `INSURANCE`. Each is opened as a **PassThroughClaim** by a `RegionalOpsController`, who names the `cycle_id` the cost belongs to, the `category`, and the accrual estimate in `estimated_amount_usd`. At most one live claim may exist per category per month: if another claim for the same `cycle_id` and `category` exists in any status other than `REJECTED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSTHROUGH_CATEGORY` (HTTP 409). A rejected claim, by contrast, frees the category, so a mis-filed category can be re-opened after rejection. A new claim lands in `SUBMITTED` with `actual_amount_usd`, `supporting_doc_ref`, `allowed_amount_usd`, `variance_usd`, `actual_submitted_by_user_id` and `reconciled_by_user_id` all null, and the month's `estimated_passthrough_total_usd` takes on the filed estimate. That running total is the sum of `estimated_amount_usd` over the month's claims whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `ACTUAL_SUBMITTED` or `RECONCILED` — a rejected claim contributes nothing to it. Claim listings may be narrowed by `cycle_id`, `category` or `status`.

Filing the invoiced actual moves the claim `SUBMITTED → ACTUAL_SUBMITTED` and may be done by either a `RegionalOpsController` or a `CPAAdministrator`, provided the month is still `OPEN` or already `CLOSED` — a month that has reached `SETTLED`, or one still sitting in `PLANNED`, does not accept actuals. The filing carries `actual_amount_usd` and the document reference in `supporting_doc_ref`. The server computes `variance_usd` as the actual less the estimate, rounded half-up to two decimal places, which is negative whenever the month came in under accrual. It also records the filer's `X-User-ID` in `actual_submitted_by_user_id`, and it is this recorded identifier that the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.11 later compares against. Only the claim's `version` moves.

Adjudication is a `CPAAdministrator` act, admissible only from `ACTUAL_SUBMITTED`, carrying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` is the same identifier already recorded in `actual_submitted_by_user_id`. Either way the adjudicator's identifier is written to `reconciled_by_user_id`, and both the claim's `version` and the month's `version` increment. The claim vocabulary is `SUBMITTED`, `ACTUAL_SUBMITTED`, `RECONCILED` and `REJECTED`, the last two terminal.

Approval moves the claim to `RECONCILED` and computes `allowed_amount_usd`, the first step of the month's pass-through waterfall. Fuel is passed through uncapped: for `category` `FUEL`, `allowed_amount_usd` is simply `actual_amount_usd`. Every other category is capped at a tolerance above the accrual: for `AIRPORT_FEES` the ceiling is the estimate increased by one tenth, and for `INSURANCE` it is the estimate increased by one twentieth, each ceiling rounded half-up to two decimals, and `allowed_amount_usd` is whichever is smaller, the actual or that ceiling. An airport-fee claim estimated at 40,000.00 and invoiced at 47,000.00 is therefore allowed at 44,000.00, and the 3,000.00 of excess is borne by the regional carrier. Rejection moves the claim to `REJECTED` instead, leaves `allowed_amount_usd` null, and drops the claim out of both the month's `estimated_passthrough_total_usd` and the settlement base described next; both of those month figures are recomputed on the spot.

Whichever way the decision went, the month's `passthrough_settlement_usd` is then rebuilt, in a pinned order of four further steps. Step two forms the **base pass-through total**: the sum of `allowed_amount_usd` over the month's claims whose status is `RECONCILED`, taken in ascending `created_at` order over addends that are already carried at two decimals, so the sum is exact. Step three selects the administrative fee rate from a tier keyed on that base total: three percent when the base total is no more than 100,000.00; two percent when it is more than 100,000.00 but no more than 500,000.00; and one and a half percent when it exceeds 500,000.00. Step four computes the administrative fee as the base total multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Step five sets `passthrough_settlement_usd` to the base total plus that fee.

The pinned order matters at exactly one point, and it is a trap worth stating plainly: the tier is chosen from the step-two base total and is never re-derived from the step-five result. A month whose base total is 99,500.00 selects the three percent rate, adds a 2,985.00 fee, and settles at 102,485.00 — the settlement having crossed the 100,000.00 tier boundary does not retroactively move it to the two percent tier. The same recomputation of `passthrough_settlement_usd` also runs when the month is closed, so a month that is closed with no reconciled claims settles its pass-throughs at 0.00 plus a fee of 0.00.

### 3.7. Performance Scoring, Brand Standards and the Incentive Outcome

Closing a settlement month creates exactly one **Scorecard** against its `cycle_id`, in `PUBLISHED` status with `recompute_count` at 0. A scorecard is only ever `PUBLISHED` or `STALE`; it has no terminal state, and it can move between the two indefinitely. Scorecard listings may be filtered by `cycle_id` or `status`.

Three metrics are computed first. **Completion factor**, published as `completion_factor_pct`, starts from the eligible departure base: `published_departures` less `cancelled_uncontrollable_count`. When that eligible base is zero or below, `completion_factor_pct` is fixed at 100.00 by convention. Otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by the eligible base less `cancelled_controllable_count`, divided by the eligible base, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The asymmetry is the commercial heart of the metric: an uncontrollable cancellation is removed from both the numerator and the denominator, so it neither helps nor hurts, whereas a controllable cancellation is removed from the numerator only and therefore costs the operator directly. And because unfinalized claims are pinned to `CONTROLLABLE` through `effective_classification`, an unresolved dispute scores against the regional carrier at close time.

**On-time arrival**, published as `on_time_arrival_pct`, is one hundred multiplied by `on_time_leg_count` divided by `recorded_completed_legs`, rounded half-up to two decimals, with a pinned convention of 100.00 when `recorded_completed_legs` is 0. Cancelled and still-scheduled legs are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator; only completed legs are scored, and a leg counts as on time exactly on the fourteen-minute inclusive grace boundary of §3.4. **Mishandled bags**, published as `mishandled_bags_per_1000`, is one thousand multiplied by `mishandled_bags_total` divided by `enplaned_bags_total`, rounded half-up to two decimals, with a pinned convention of 0.00 when `enplaned_bags_total` is 0 — a month that carried no bags mishandled none.

Each metric is then banded into integer points. `completion_factor_points` is +3 when `completion_factor_pct` is at least 99.00, 0 when it is at least 97.50 but below 99.00, and −4 when it is below 97.50. `on_time_points` is +2 when `on_time_arrival_pct` is at least 85.00, 0 when it is at least 80.00 but below 85.00, and −3 when it is below 80.00. `bag_points` is +1 when `mishandled_bags_per_1000` is at most 3.00, 0 when it is above 3.00 but at most 5.50, and −2 when it exceeds 5.50. Every band boundary is inclusive on the favourable side: a completion factor of exactly 99.00 earns the full +3, an on-time figure of exactly 80.00 earns 0 rather than −3, and a bag rate of exactly 5.50 earns 0 rather than −2.

A forfeiture branch then couples the three metrics. Missing the completion-factor floor — that is, `completion_factor_points` coming out negative — voids any positive credit earned on the other two metrics, while leaving their negatives intact. Concretely, when `completion_factor_points` is −4, `on_time_points` becomes whichever is smaller, 0 or the banded on-time value, and `bag_points` likewise becomes whichever is smaller, 0 or the banded bag value; an operator that ran a superb on-time month while cancelling too much banks nothing for the punctuality. When `completion_factor_points` is 0 or +3, both other metrics keep their banded values unchanged. `performance_points` is the sum of the three point values after forfeiture, and `gross_performance_usd` is `performance_points` multiplied by a point value of 12,000.00 US dollars, rounded half-up to two decimals and carrying the sign of the point total.

The signed gross figure is then split into the two one-sided money fields, and each is capped. When `gross_performance_usd` is zero or positive, `penalty_usd` is 0.00 and `incentive_usd` is the smallest of three quantities: `gross_performance_usd` itself, the agreement's `incentive_pool_cap_usd`, and a revenue-share ceiling equal to one twentieth of the month's own revenue, rounded half-up to two decimals. That revenue base is the sum of the same two expressions §3.8 defines for an invoice — the fixed component and the actual-basis variable component — evaluated from the current state of the agreement, its fleet and the month's completed legs rather than read off any invoice row, so the scorecard is well defined before any invoice exists. When `gross_performance_usd` is negative, `incentive_usd` is 0.00 and `penalty_usd` is the smaller of the magnitude of the gross figure and the agreement's `penalty_cap_usd`; it is stored as a non-negative magnitude, not as a negative number.

**BrandAudits** feed the last money field on the scorecard. A `BrandAuditor` raises one against a `base_id`, naming the `audit_scope` — one of `UNIFORM`, `CABIN_PRODUCT`, `SERVICE_SCRIPT` or `LIVERY` — the number of findings in `findings_count`, which must be at least 1, and the `severity`, one of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`. A new audit opens with `finding_status` `OPEN`, `remediation_status` `NOT_STARTED` and `evidence_ref` null. The two status attributes are genuinely independent dimensions carried on the same row rather than one combined lifecycle, and they advance separately: `finding_status` runs `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED` and `remediation_status` runs `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS → VERIFIED`. Audit listings may be filtered by `base_id`, `finding_status` or `remediation_status`.

A `RegionalOpsController` acknowledges the finding, moving `finding_status` from `OPEN` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and leaving `remediation_status` untouched. The same role drives the remediation dimension by naming a `to_remediation_status`: `IN_PROGRESS` is admissible only from `NOT_STARTED`, and `VERIFIED` only from `IN_PROGRESS`. Reaching `VERIFIED` additionally requires an `evidence_ref` in the request, and its absence is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); the supplied value is stored on the audit. Remediation never touches `finding_status`, so a carrier may complete and verify its remediation before it has acknowledged the finding, or the other way round. Closing the finding belongs to the `BrandAuditor`: `finding_status` moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → CLOSED`, but only if `remediation_status` has already reached `VERIFIED`. That cross-dimension guard is the one place the two dimensions interact, and violating it is `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Every one of these four acts increments the audit's `version`.

`brand_penalty_usd` is computed from the audits that are still *exposed* on both dimensions at once. An audit is exposed when its base belongs to the same agreement as the month being scored, its `finding_status` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and its `remediation_status` is `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Closing the finding removes the audit from the set; so does verifying the remediation; either alone is sufficient to stop the audit costing money. For each exposed audit, multiply its `findings_count` by its severity weight — 500.00 US dollars per finding for `MINOR`, 2,500.00 for `MAJOR` and 7,500.00 for `CRITICAL` — and sum those products over the exposed audits in ascending `created_at` order. `brand_penalty_usd` is the smaller of that sum rounded half-up to two decimals and a hard ceiling of 25,000.00 US dollars per month. A single `CRITICAL` audit with four findings therefore reaches 30,000.00 raw and is capped to 25,000.00.

Brand activity reaches back into settlement, but asymmetrically, and the asymmetry is deliberate. Raising a new audit puts it into the exposure set, and closing a finding takes it out; each of those two events makes every `PUBLISHED` scorecard belonging to a month of the same agreement go `STALE`, with the invoice-voiding consequences described below. Verifying a remediation also removes the audit from the exposure set for every *future* computation, but it does not by itself mark any scorecard stale. Acknowledging a finding changes no exposure at all, since `ACKNOWLEDGED` is still an exposed value on the finding dimension.

Staleness is how a published scorecard admits that its inputs have moved. A scorecard goes `PUBLISHED → STALE` when the month's `cancelled_controllable_count` or `cancelled_uncontrollable_count` changed after the close, or when a brand audit entered or left the exposure set through creation or finding closure. Going stale voids every invoice of the month whose status is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, with `void_reason` set to `AUTO_SCORECARD_STALE`, and increments the scorecard's `version` — and, through the invoices, unwinds settled money exactly as §3.8 describes.

Republishing is the repair. A `CPAAdministrator` may recompute a scorecard from either `PUBLISHED` or `STALE`. Recomputation rebuilds every metric, every point value, `gross_performance_usd`, `incentive_usd`, `penalty_usd` and `brand_penalty_usd` from the month's current counters and the current exposure set, leaves the scorecard in `PUBLISHED`, raises `recompute_count` by 1 and increments the scorecard's `version`. Recomputing a scorecard whose inputs have not moved is idempotent in every business field: each metric, point and money value keeps the value it already had, and `recompute_count` is the only figure that moves on that self-transition.

### 3.8. Invoicing, Settlement and Compensating Reversals

An **Invoice** is the monthly bill, and it exists in two variants that answer two different questions. A `CPAAdministrator` raises one against a settlement month by naming the `variant` — `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — and supplying `expected_schedule_cycle_version` per §2.9. A `PROVISIONAL` invoice is the cash-flow bill raised while the month is still `OPEN`; a `FINAL` invoice is the settlement bill and requires the month to be `CLOSED`, a request for one against a month that is still `OPEN` being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `FINAL` invoice additionally requires every pass-through claim of the month to have reached a terminal status, either `RECONCILED` or `REJECTED`; while any claim is still `SUBMITTED` or `ACTUAL_SUBMITTED` the request is refused with `ERR_PASSTHROUGH_UNRECONCILED` (HTTP 409). Every invoice is born in `DRAFT` with `void_reason` and `issued_final_at` null, and its statuses are `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` and `VOIDED`, of which `VOIDED` is terminal. Invoice listings may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `status` or `variant`.

Creating an invoice computes every component once, at creation, from the state of the world at that instant; no component and no total is ever recomputed afterwards. Raising a newer invoice for the same month and the same `variant` supersedes the older one: the standing `DRAFT` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `AUTO_SUPERSEDED`. The agreement's `open_invoice_count` is refreshed to reflect the new set of drafts, and the month's `version` increments.

The **fixed component** is common to both variants. `ownership_aircraft_count` is the number of tails under the month's agreement whose `ownership` is `REGIONAL_OWNED` and whose status is `IN_SERVICE` or `GROUNDED_STAFFING`. `fixed_component_usd` is the agreement's `fixed_monthly_fee_usd` plus the product of `aircraft_ownership_fee_usd` and `ownership_aircraft_count`, that product rounded half-up to two decimals before being added.

The **variable component** is where the two variants diverge, and it obeys the per-leg rounding discipline of §2.6. For a single leg, the amount is the product of the leg's block hours and the agreement's `block_hour_rate_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimal places at the leg, plus the agreement's `departure_rate_usd`, which is already two decimals. `variable_component_usd` is the sum of those per-leg amounts across the included legs, taken in ascending `scheduled_departure_utc` order with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break when two legs are published for the same instant. The rounding order is pinned: each leg is rounded to cents and the rounded legs are then added. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different number in cents and is not what this contract specifies. On a `FINAL` invoice the included legs are the month's `COMPLETED` legs and the hours used are each leg's `actual_block_hours` — the same actual basis that `leg_variable_payment_usd` already stores on each leg. On a `PROVISIONAL` invoice the included legs are the month's legs whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and the hours used are each leg's `planned_block_hours`, so still-open flying is paid for in advance on its published plan. Legs in `CANCELLED` are excluded from both bases.

The remaining components follow the variant wholesale. `passthrough_component_usd` is the month's `estimated_passthrough_total_usd` on a `PROVISIONAL` invoice and its reconciled `passthrough_settlement_usd`, administrative fee included, on a `FINAL` one. `incentive_component_usd`, `penalty_component_usd` and `brand_penalty_component_usd` are copied from the month's scorecard — `incentive_usd`, `penalty_usd` and `brand_penalty_usd` respectively — on a `FINAL` invoice, and are all fixed at 0.00 on a `PROVISIONAL` invoice, which deliberately carries no performance overlay at all. `total_usd` is then the fixed component plus the variable component plus the pass-through component plus the incentive component, less the penalty component and less the brand penalty component. Every addend is already carried at two decimals, so the total is exact.

Issuing is the act that moves money, and a `CPAAdministrator` may issue only an invoice that is still `DRAFT`. Four further conditions apply, and each has its own rejection: the `variant` must be `FINAL`, and issuing a provisional bill is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_VARIANT_NOT_FINAL` (HTTP 422); the month must be `CLOSED`; every pass-through claim of the month must be terminal, failing which `ERR_PASSTHROUGH_UNRECONCILED`; and the month's scorecard must be `PUBLISHED`, a scorecard sitting in `STALE` blocking settlement with `ERR_SCORECARD_STALE` (HTTP 409) until it has been recomputed. On success the invoice moves to `ISSUED`, `issued_final_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the month moves `CLOSED → SETTLED`, the agreement's `settled_to_date_usd` takes on this invoice's `total_usd` in addition to what it held, and `open_invoice_count` falls by 1. The invoice's, the month's and the agreement's `version` all increment.

Voiding is the compensating action, and it works from either `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`. An explicit void by a `CPAAdministrator` records `void_reason` as `MANUAL`; the two automatic causes record `AUTO_SUPERSEDED` when a newer invoice displaced a draft and `AUTO_SCORECARD_STALE` when the scorecard cascade of §3.7 invalidated the bill. Voiding a `DRAFT` invoice simply lowers the agreement's `open_invoice_count` by 1. Voiding an `ISSUED` invoice unwinds the settlement precisely: `settled_to_date_usd` is reduced by that invoice's `total_usd`, returning the conserved figure to exactly the value it held before the issue rather than to a recomputed approximation of it, and the month reverts `SETTLED → CLOSED`. `CLOSED → SETTLED → CLOSED` is a revert loop that may be traversed repeatedly, and `settled_to_date_usd` is the quantity it conserves. The invoice's, the month's and the agreement's `version` all increment.

One consequence of computing components at creation is worth spelling out. If the agreement's `block_hour_rate_usd` changes through an accepted extension, invoices already created keep the components they were computed with; the new rate applies only to invoices raised afterwards. If, on the other hand, the operational facts change — a cancellation is finalized as uncontrollable, or a brand audit is raised — the affected invoices are not silently repriced. They are voided outright, and a fresh invoice must be raised against the recomputed month.

### 3.9. Renegotiating the Term

Contract extension is a bounded bargain between the two carriers, conducted through **ExtensionNegotiation** rounds. The `MainlineScheduler` opens a round against an agreement that is `ACTIVE`, supplying `proposed_term_end_date`, `proposed_block_hour_rate_usd`, and the `expected_agreement_version` that §2.9 requires. The proposed term end must be strictly later than the agreement's current `term_end_date`; a proposal that is not an extension is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_TERM_NOT_LATER` (HTTP 422). Opening a round against an agreement that is not `ACTIVE` — including one already sitting in `EXTENSION_PENDING` — is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A successful offer does four things. It creates a negotiation in `OFFERED` status, with `counter_term_end_date` and `counter_block_hour_rate_usd` null, `counter_round` at 0, and `round_index` recording which round of bargaining this is — the agreement's `extension_round_count` after this offer has been counted. It supersedes history: every pre-existing negotiation of the same agreement still standing in `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, so at most one round is ever live. It raises the agreement's `extension_round_count` by 1, a monotone counter that is never decremented no matter how the round ends. And it moves the agreement `ACTIVE → EXTENSION_PENDING`, incrementing its `version`. Negotiation listings may be filtered by `agreement_id` or `status`.

The regional carrier answers with a counter. A `RegionalOpsController` may move a round `OFFERED → COUNTERED` while the agreement is `EXTENSION_PENDING`, supplying `counter_term_end_date` and `counter_block_hour_rate_usd`; the countered term end must likewise be strictly later than the agreement's current `term_end_date`, on pain of `ERR_EXTENSION_TERM_NOT_LATER`. Each counter raises `counter_round` by 1, and the loop is bounded at two: a round whose `counter_round` has already reached two may not be countered again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). The mainline may answer a counter in turn: a `MainlineScheduler` may move a round `COUNTERED → OFFERED` by revising its offer, replacing `proposed_term_end_date` and `proposed_block_hour_rate_usd` with fresh values that must again be strictly later than the current term end, and clearing `counter_term_end_date` and `counter_block_hour_rate_usd` back to null. Revising does not reset `counter_round`, so `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED` is a bargaining loop that closes after at most two counters. Each of these acts increments only the negotiation's `version`.

Acceptance ends the bargain and rewrites the contract. Which side may accept depends on which side put the standing round on the table, per the segregation rule of §2.11: a round in `OFFERED` was authored by the mainline and may be accepted only by `RegionalOpsController`, while a round in `COUNTERED` was authored by the regional carrier and may be accepted only by `MainlineScheduler`. Accepting an `OFFERED` round writes the agreement's `term_end_date` from `proposed_term_end_date` and its `block_hour_rate_usd` from `proposed_block_hour_rate_usd`; accepting a `COUNTERED` round writes them from `counter_term_end_date` and `counter_block_hour_rate_usd` instead. Either way the round moves to the terminal `ACCEPTED` status, the agreement returns `EXTENSION_PENDING → ACTIVE`, and both `version` counters increment. The new rate is prospective only, exactly as §3.8 notes: invoices already created keep the components they were computed with.

Withdrawal is the other way out. A `MainlineScheduler` may withdraw a round standing in either `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED`, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` status and returning the agreement `EXTENSION_PENDING → ACTIVE` with `term_end_date` and `block_hour_rate_usd` untouched. Both `version` counters increment. The same `WITHDRAWN` status is written by the server without a client request in two situations: when a newer offer supersedes the round, and when the agreement is terminated while a round is live. Because the agreement's own `ACTIVE → EXTENSION_PENDING → ACTIVE` cycle is a loop, the same agreement may be renegotiated any number of times, and `extension_round_count` together with each round's `round_index` is the audit trail of how often the parties came back to the table.
