# Hotel Management Agreement & Owner Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A third-party hotel management company operates hotels it does not own. The contract that governs that arrangement — the management agreement — is simultaneously a fee schedule, a budget-approval protocol, a performance covenant, a cash-waterfall instruction and a liability-allocation treaty between two parties whose interests diverge on almost every line. Today that contract lives in a signed PDF and its month-to-month arithmetic lives in spreadsheets maintained by the manager, which means the owner's asset-management team spends its time reconstructing the manager's numbers instead of reviewing them. When a base management fee is computed on one rounding convention and the owner's model uses another, the two sides argue about cents for weeks; when a centralized-services chargeback arrives with no disclosed allocation basis, the owner has no mechanical way to contest it; and when a performance test is failed, nobody can agree on whether the cure right survived.

This API replaces the spreadsheets with a single deterministic system of record. It holds the agreement's economics — the base management fee rate applied to revenue, the two-tier incentive fee measured against an owner priority return, the reserve-for-replacement contribution rate and the cap on centralized-services chargebacks — and it applies them in a pinned order that both parties can audit. Around that core it governs the owner-approved annual operating and capital budget with its revision rounds and deemed-approval deadline, the manager's variance authority above an approved line, the monthly reporting package with its profit-and-loss and its competitive-set market-share benchmark, the annual performance test with cure rights and termination for repeat failure, working-capital funding calls, the cash distribution waterfall, brand-standard cost disputes with the franchisor, and the allocation of employment claims between owner employees and manager employees.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, every derived figure is reproducible: rounding order is contractual, not incidental, and the document states exactly which quantity is rounded and when. Second, every contested figure is traceable: fee accruals, reserve movements and distribution buckets are ledgered rather than overwritten, so an owner who disputes a period can see precisely which accruals were reversed and which cash never moved. All resources described here are mounted under the `/hotel-management` path prefix, with one exception noted in the endpoint table: the platform's logical-clock control endpoint sits at the server root.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. Four roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `HotelManagerOps`, `AssetManager` and `OwnerRep`. A request that omits either header, or that presents an `X-User-Role` value outside those four, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A request whose role is recognised but is not authorised for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

`PlatformAdmin` is the operator of the reporting platform itself: it onboards agreements, activates them, scores performance tests and advances the logical clock. `HotelManagerOps` is the management company's on-property and above-property finance team — the party that builds budgets, files monthly reporting packages, posts chargebacks, executes distributions and elects cures. `AssetManager` is the owner-side asset manager, holding mid-tier variance authority, the right to contest a chargeback, the right to open a brand-standard dispute and the authority to allocate employment claims. `OwnerRep` is the owner's authorised representative and holds final authority: budget decisions, package acceptance, working-capital funding, dispute resolution and termination.

All four roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `HotelManagerOps`, when it is `AssetManager` and when it is `OwnerRep`. What the server does with the identifier is record it where the specification calls for an actor stamp: the submitter of a budget is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`, the actor who decided a budget or a variance request is recorded as `decided_by_user_id`, and the actor who raised a variance request is recorded as `requested_by_user_id`. Those recorded values are used only for the segregation-of-duties comparison described below; they are never resolved to a user record.

Because this is a segregation-of-duties model, two further access rules sit above the plain role check. The actor who decides a matter may not be the actor who raised it: when the deciding `X-User-ID` equals the stored `submitted_by_user_id` of a budget, or the stored `requested_by_user_id` of a variance request, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). And on multi-action endpoints — where a single operation accepts an `action` naming which move is being made — a role may be authorised for the endpoint yet not for the specific action; that combination is refused with `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403). Concretely: contesting a centralized-services chargeback belongs to `AssetManager` alone, while reaffirming or adjusting one belongs to `HotelManagerOps` alone; among the brand-standard dispute moves, a manager response and an escalation to the franchisor belong to `HotelManagerOps`, an owner counter may be made by either `AssetManager` or `OwnerRep`, and resolving with owner funding, resolving as waived, or rescinding a resolution belong to `OwnerRep` alone. Variance decisions carry a third gradation: authority tiers, described in §3.3, are enforced with `ERR_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT` (403).

Read access is uniform. Every one of the four roles may call every list-and-read operation in the system.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List operations are paged through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be below 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` sets how many rows to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, on the budget-revision list operation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).

Unless a specific operation says otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. This ordering is not cosmetic: several of the system's rules — reserve-ledger running balances, variance baseline accumulation, cascade processing order — are defined in terms of `created_at` ascending, and the list responses expose the same sequence the engine uses.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before paging is applied and `has_more` is true whenever rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API persists is addressed by an `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Alongside it, resources carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Most resources also carry `updated_at`, the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation. Management agreements, operating budgets, variance requests, monthly report packages, market benchmarks, centralized service charges, fee accruals, cash distributions, working capital calls, performance tests, brand standard disputes and employment claims all carry the full set.

The one exception is the reserve-for-replacement ledger. A `ReserveLedgerEntry` is append-only and is never modified after it is written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-control operation, whose response reports the new `current_utc` together with the counts of rows the move swept: `deemed_approved_budget_count`, `overdue_call_count`, `lapsed_cure_count`, `deadlocked_dispute_count` and `terminated_agreement_count`. Every deadline in this document — a budget's `owner_decision_deadline_utc`, a package's `due_date_utc`, a call's `due_date_utc`, a test's `cure_deadline_utc`, a dispute's `standstill_end_utc`, an agreement's `termination_effective_utc` — is compared against this clock and against nothing else. Deadlines expressed in days are computed by adding that whole number of days to a base instant.

### 2.5. Cents, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All money in the system is carried as an integer number of cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. There are no fractional-cent balances and no decimal money types. Rates and ratios are decimal numbers: `base_fee_pct` may not exceed 0.10 and is accepted with at most four decimal places, `hotel_basis_share` is a decimal fraction accepted with at most six decimal places, and the other configured rates carry the bounds listed under rule 3 in §2.8.

Two rounding operations appear throughout, and they are not interchangeable. **Round half up** takes the absolute value of the quantity, adds one half, discards the fractional part, and re-applies the original sign; a value of exactly one half cent therefore moves away from zero. **Floor** discards the fractional part outright, which for a non-negative quantity means rounding down. Caps are always floored, so the cap can never be generous by a cent. Fees are always rounded half up.

Where a formula multiplies several quantities, **the order in which rounding is applied is contractual**. Three places in this system deliberately differ from each other and must not be harmonised:

- The actual base management fee on a reported month is rounded **per revenue line and then summed** — three roundings, one addition.
- The budgeted base management fee on an annual budget is rounded **once, on the annual revenue total** — one rounding.
- The annual incentive true-up is rounded **per tier and then summed** — one rounding for the first-tier amount, one for the second-tier amount, then addition.

Derived ratios carry pinned precision as well, and every one of them is rounded **half up** — the same half-up operation defined above, applied at the ratio's stated number of decimal places instead of at whole cents, so a tie at that place moves away from zero. A variance request's `variance_pct` is rounded to six decimal places, a benchmark's `rpi` and a performance test's `rpi_weighted` and `rpi_simple_average` are rounded to two decimal places, and a performance test's `gop_achievement_pct` is rounded to four decimal places. Truncating or flooring a ratio at its pinned precision is not the contractual convention and is not permitted. Every threshold comparison in the performance test is made against the **rounded** figure, never the unrounded intermediate.

### 2.6. Agreement Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the agreement, and both name the same field and the same failure.

The first is creating an operating budget revision under an agreement. That request must carry `expected_agreement_version`, a non-optional integer holding the `version` of the `ManagementAgreement` as the client last observed it. If the value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412). The rule exists because a budget revision legitimately supersedes the live budget for its fiscal year: a writer working from a stale view of the agreement would silently displace a revision it never saw, and the version pin converts that into a deterministic refusal instead.

The second is running the annual incentive true-up for a fiscal year. That request likewise must carry `expected_agreement_version` holding the client's last observed agreement `version`, and a mismatch with the agreement's current `version` is likewise rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412). A true-up run legitimately reverses and re-posts the fiscal year's final incentive accrual, so a writer holding a stale view of the agreement would rewrite the ledger against economics it never observed.

No other operation in the system accepts a version field of any kind. Submitting a budget, deciding a budget, filing a report package, posting a benchmark or a chargeback, reviewing a package, executing a distribution, funding a call, scoring a test, curing, terminating, advancing a dispute and allocating a claim all take no `expected_agreement_version` and perform no version comparison.

Three operations advance the agreement's `version` by one as a declared side effect: activating the agreement, creating a budget revision under it, and running the annual true-up. Because the two version-pinned writes are themselves among those three, a client that creates two budget revisions back to back must re-read the agreement between them.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and the Distribution Hold

A single request in this system routinely mutates more than one row. Those cascades are specified, not incidental, and three conventions govern all of them. First, when a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Second, every row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly as a direct write would, including rows the client never named. Third, a cascade is described in hops, and a later hop fires only because an earlier hop changed something — so where a hop's precondition is not met, that hop and only that hop is skipped.

The most consequential cascade in the system is the **distribution hold**. The agreement carries a computed boolean `distribution_hold`, which is true exactly while the agreement's `status` is `CURE_PERIOD` or `TERMINATION_NOTICED` and false otherwise. When the hold goes up, every `CashDistribution` of the agreement sitting in `CALCULATED` moves to `HELD`; when it comes down again after a completed cure, every `HELD` distribution of that agreement returns to `CALCULATED` with its bucket amounts retained unchanged and no recalculation performed. While the hold is up, execution of a distribution is refused. The hold is the single mechanism by which a performance failure or a termination notice freezes cash without destroying the arithmetic already computed.

45. `payment_amount_cents` is at least `cure_payment_due_cents`; otherwise `ERR_CURE_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/performance-tests/{id}/cure` when the `action` is `PAY_CURE`.
46. The agreement's `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` is at least the dispute's `current_cost_estimate_cents`; otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE_BALANCE` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/brand-disputes/{id}/rounds` when the `action` is `RESOLVE_OWNER_FUNDS` and the `funding_source` is `FFE_RESERVE`.
47. `funded_amount_cents` equals the call's `amount_cents` exactly; otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/working-capital-calls/{id}/fund`.
48. `adjusted_amount_cents` is present and between 0 and the charge's `allocated_amount_cents` inclusive; otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/centralized-charges/{id}/review` when the `action` is `ADJUST`.
49. The `effective_baseline_cents` computed for the variance request is greater than 0; otherwise `ERR_VARIANCE_BASELINE_ZERO` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/budgets/{id}/variance-requests`.
50. The `prorated_budget_agop_cents` computed for the performance test is greater than 0; otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_AGOP_NON_POSITIVE` (422). Applies to `POST /hotel-management/agreements/{id}/performance-tests`.

### 2.9. The Fee Ledger and the Reserve as Append-Only Records

Management fees are never simply "charged". Each one is raised as a `FeeAccrual` row that names the period it belongs to through its `report_package_id`, the `fee_type` it represents — `BASE`, `INCENTIVE` or `CENTRALIZED_SERVICES` — its `variant`, which is `PROVISIONAL` for a monthly accrual and `FINAL` for an annual true-up accrual, and the `fiscal_year` and `period_month` it is booked into. Its `amount_cents` may be negative, which happens only on a `FINAL` true-up that credits the owner. An accrual's `status` runs through `ACCRUED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID`, `REVERSED` and `DISPUTED_HELD`, and once cash reaches it, `paid_amount_cents` records how much settled. A `REVERSED` accrual keeps its `amount_cents` for audit but contributes nothing to any waterfall demand and nothing to any annual sum.

The reserve for replacement is likewise a ledger rather than a balance. Each `ReserveLedgerEntry` names the `agreement_id` whose reserve account it moves, an `entry_type` of `CONTRIBUTION`, `DISBURSEMENT` or `DISBURSEMENT_REVERSAL`, a signed `amount_cents` that is positive for a contribution and for a disbursement reversal and negative for a disbursement, a `source_kind` of `DISTRIBUTION_EXECUTION`, `BRAND_DISPUTE_FUNDING` or `BRAND_DISPUTE_RESCISSION` naming which action produced it, and a `memo` carrying a human-readable provenance note that names the id of the producing resource. Each entry also stores `balance_after_cents`: taking the agreement's entries in `created_at` ascending order, that value is the sum of the `amount_cents` of every entry created strictly before this one, plus this entry's own `amount_cents`. The ledger is append-only and entries are never mutated.

One invariant is asserted after every append and holds at all times: the agreement's `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` equals the signed sum of `amount_cents` across every reserve ledger entry of that agreement. Two other agreement-level aggregates behave the same way. `working_capital_balance_cents` is seeded from `initial_working_capital_cents` at activation and thereafter moves only through funded calls, the first bucket of an executed distribution, a paid cure and employment-claim allocations — and it is the one balance permitted to go negative. `manager_liability_accrued_cents` equals the sum of `manager_share_cents` over the agreement's employment claims whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`, and is never negative.

### 2.10. The Epoch Sweep

Advancing the logical clock is the only way a deadline expires, and one clock move runs a five-step sweep in exactly this order, each step processing its rows in `created_at` ascending order:

1. Every budget in `SUBMITTED` whose `owner_decision_deadline_utc` is strictly before the new clock value becomes `DEEMED_APPROVED`. The count is reported as `deemed_approved_budget_count`.
2. Every working capital call in `REQUESTED` whose `due_date_utc` is strictly before the new clock value becomes `OVERDUE`, with no cash movement. The count is reported as `overdue_call_count`.
3. Every performance test in `FAILED_CURABLE` or `CURE_ELECTED` whose `cure_deadline_utc` is strictly before the new clock value becomes `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED`. The count is reported as `lapsed_cure_count`.
4. Every dispute in `ESCALATED_TO_FRANCHISOR` whose `standstill_end_utc` is at or before the new clock value becomes `DEADLOCKED`. The count is reported as `deadlocked_dispute_count`.
5. Every agreement in `TERMINATION_NOTICED` whose `termination_effective_utc` is at or before the new clock value becomes `TERMINATED`. The count is reported as `terminated_agreement_count`.

The mixture of strict and inclusive comparisons is deliberate. Steps 1 through 3 fire only once the clock has passed the deadline; steps 4 and 5 fire when the clock reaches it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering and Activating a Management Agreement

Everything in this system hangs off a **management agreement**. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying the hotel's trading name as `hotel_name`, the legal name of the owner entity as `owner_entity_name`, the base management fee rate as `base_fee_pct`, the tier-one incentive fee rate as `incentive_fee_pct`, and the annual owner priority return as `owner_priority_cents`. Everything else about the agreement is optional at registration and falls back to a contractual default, and those defaults matter because most agreements in the pilot portfolio are expected to use them.

The optional economics are these. The tier-two incentive rate `incentive_tier2_pct` defaults to 0.15 and may never be set below `incentive_fee_pct`; the annual excess-over-priority amount at which that second tier starts, `incentive_tier2_threshold_cents`, defaults to 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars of excess. The reserve-for-replacement contribution rate `ffe_reserve_pct` defaults to 0.04, four percent of gross revenue. The chargeback cap `centralized_services_cap_pct` defaults to 0.015, one and a half percent of the period's gross revenue. The `working_capital_floor_cents` the owner must keep funded defaults to zero, as does the owner's opening deposit `initial_working_capital_cents`. Governance timings default as follows: `owner_review_window_days` of 30, `max_revision_rounds` of 2, `cure_window_days` of 60 — two months for the manager to elect and pay a cure — `dispute_standstill_days` of 45, `max_dispute_rounds` of 2, `funding_due_days` of 15 and `termination_notice_days` of 90, a full quarter of notice. Performance covenants default to a `gop_test_threshold_pct` of 0.85, an `rpi_test_threshold` of 90.0, a `min_benchmark_months` of 10, a `test_periods_expected` of 12 and a `consecutive_failure_limit` of 2. Employment defaults to an `employee_model` of `MANAGER_EMPLOYEES`, the alternative being `OWNER_EMPLOYEES`, with a `manager_indemnity_cap_cents` of zero.

A newly registered agreement is persisted with `status` `DRAFT` and with every computed aggregate at its opening value: `working_capital_balance_cents` at 0, `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` at 0, `manager_liability_accrued_cents` at 0, `performance_failure_streak` at 0, `distribution_hold` false, and `termination_effective_utc` null. Nothing operational may happen yet.

Activation is the `PlatformAdmin`'s second act and is permitted only while the agreement is `DRAFT`. It moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, writes `initial_working_capital_cents` into `working_capital_balance_cents` — this is the only moment the opening deposit is recognised — sets `distribution_hold` to false, and increments the agreement's `version` by one. From `ACTIVE` the agreement can travel to `CURE_PERIOD` after a curable performance failure, to `TERMINATION_NOTICED` once the owner issues notice, back to `ACTIVE` from `CURE_PERIOD` when a cure completes, and finally to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal: once there, `distribution_hold` remains true and no further operational write is accepted. Any of the twelve operational writes listed as rule 18 in §2.8 attempted while the agreement sits in `DRAFT`, `CURE_PERIOD`, `TERMINATION_NOTICED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Reading a single agreement is available to all four roles and returns the configured economics together with the live computed balances and the current `version` — which is what a client must read before either of the two version-pinned writes described in §2.6.

### 3.2. The Annual Operating and Capital Budget

Each fiscal year the manager proposes a budget, and the owner either approves it, sends it back, or lets the clock approve it by default. A `HotelManagerOps` actor opens a revision by naming the `fiscal_year`, the total budgeted gross revenue as `budgeted_revenue_cents`, the total budgeted operating expenses as `budgeted_operating_expenses_cents` — expenses excluding management fees and centralized services — and the agreement's version as `expected_agreement_version`. Two capital lines are optional and default to zero: `budgeted_capital_ffe_cents` for routine furniture, fixtures and equipment, and `budgeted_capital_brand_cents` for brand-mandated capital. A revision may also carry `supersedes_budget_id`, naming an earlier revision of the same agreement and the same fiscal year that this row replaces; on a first revision the field is null.

At creation, and again at every submit, three amounts are derived from the row's own figures. The budgeted base management fee is the budgeted gross revenue multiplied by the agreement's `base_fee_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents **once on the annual total**, and stored as `budgeted_base_fee_cents`. The budgeted centralized-services charge is taken at the contractual ceiling: the budgeted gross revenue multiplied by `centralized_services_cap_pct`, floored to whole cents, stored as `budgeted_centralized_cents`. The budgeted adjusted gross operating profit, `budgeted_agop_cents`, is the budgeted gross revenue less the budgeted operating expenses, less the budgeted base fee, less the budgeted centralized charge. Note the asymmetry against the monthly actuals in §3.4: the budget rounds the fee once, the actuals round it three times.

Creating a revision has two further consequences. When `supersedes_budget_id` is present, the named row moves to `SUPERSEDED` — from `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, from `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED`, or from `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUPERSEDED` — with no ledger effect in any of the three cases; where the superseded row was `SUBMITTED`, the owner decision pending on it is simply abandoned, and where it was `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the new revision becomes the live budget for the fiscal year. A supersede target belonging to another agreement or another fiscal year, or already sitting in one of the four terminal budget statuses, is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`. Separately, creating a revision increments the agreement's `version` by one.

Submission is available to `HotelManagerOps` from `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and in both cases the agreement must be `ACTIVE`. The submit may optionally carry revised amounts — `budgeted_revenue_cents`, `budgeted_operating_expenses_cents`, `budgeted_capital_ffe_cents`, `budgeted_capital_brand_cents` — and where they are present the derived amounts are recomputed from them. Both origins stamp `submitted_at` with the logical clock, stamp `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and set `owner_decision_deadline_utc` to `submitted_at` advanced by the agreement's `owner_review_window_days`. The `REVISION_REQUESTED` origin additionally increments `revision_round` by one; the `DRAFT` origin does not, leaving `revision_round` at its opening value of 0. Note that a revision cycle never creates a new row — the same budget returns to `SUBMITTED`.

The owner's decision belongs to `OwnerRep` alone and carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, plus an optional `note`. Four conditions guard it beyond the state check: the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, the logical clock must be at or before `owner_decision_deadline_utc` (otherwise `ERR_OWNER_DECISION_WINDOW_CLOSED`), the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and — on the approval branch — no other budget of the same agreement and fiscal year may already hold `APPROVED`, `DEEMED_APPROVED` or `DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD` (otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_YEAR_ALREADY_APPROVED`). An approval moves the row `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and records `decided_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`; that is its entire effect.

A revision request is where the interesting branch lives, because the same `decision` value produces two different destinations selected purely by how many rounds have already been spent. When `revision_round` is strictly below the agreement's `max_revision_rounds`, the row moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and records `decided_by_user_id`; the manager may then revise and resubmit. When `revision_round` has reached or passed `max_revision_rounds`, a further revision request deadlocks: the row moves `SUBMITTED → DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD`, `carryforward_applied` is set to true, and the four budgeted input amounts are **overwritten** from the prior year rather than negotiated further. The carry-forward source is chosen among the same agreement's budgets for the fiscal year immediately before this one whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `DEEMED_APPROVED` or `DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD`; among those candidates the one with the highest `revision_round` wins, and where several share that value the most recently created one wins. Each of the source's `budgeted_revenue_cents`, `budgeted_operating_expenses_cents`, `budgeted_capital_ffe_cents` and `budgeted_capital_brand_cents` is multiplied by a carry-forward factor of 1.03 — a three percent uplift — and rounded half up to whole cents, and the result overwrites the corresponding field on the deadlocked row. If no eligible prior-year candidate exists at all, the last submitted amounts stand unchanged and `carryforward_applied` is left false. Either way, `budgeted_base_fee_cents`, `budgeted_centralized_cents` and `budgeted_agop_cents` are then recomputed from whatever amounts now stand.

The fourth way a budget leaves `SUBMITTED` involves no actor at all. When the logical clock advances strictly past `owner_decision_deadline_utc`, the sweep described in §2.10 moves the row `SUBMITTED → DEEMED_APPROVED`, and `decided_by_user_id` deliberately stays null: a deemed approval is attributed to nobody. `APPROVED`, `DEEMED_APPROVED` and `DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD` are the three **approved-equivalent** statuses, and every downstream rule that asks whether a fiscal year has an approved budget accepts all three; `SUPERSEDED` is terminal but is not approved-equivalent.

Budget revisions can be read individually and listed. The list is filterable by `agreement_id`, by `supersedes_budget_id` — which is how a reader reconstructs a revision chain — by `fiscal_year` and by `status`, and it is the one list operation whose pagination arguments are validated as described in §2.3.

### 3.3. Variance Authority Above an Approved Budget

Once a budget line is approved, the manager may still need to spend beyond it, and the size of the overspend decides who gets to say yes. A `HotelManagerOps` actor raises a variance request against a budget by naming the `category` — `OPERATING`, `CAPITAL_FFE` or `CAPITAL_BRAND` — the incremental spend authority sought as `requested_amount_cents`, and a written `justification`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stamped onto the row as `requested_by_user_id`. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`.

Routing is computed at creation and is order-sensitive. The starting baseline is the budget line matching the category: `budgeted_operating_expenses_cents` for `OPERATING`, `budgeted_capital_ffe_cents` for `CAPITAL_FFE`, `budgeted_capital_brand_cents` for `CAPITAL_BRAND`. To that is added the accumulated authority already granted: the sum of `requested_amount_cents` over sibling variance requests carrying the same `budget_id` and the same `category` whose `status` is `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED` and whose `created_at` is strictly earlier than this row's. The total is stored as `effective_baseline_cents`. If that total is not greater than zero — which happens when a capital line was budgeted at zero and nothing has been approved against it yet — no ratio is defined and the request is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_BASELINE_ZERO`. Otherwise `variance_pct` is the requested amount divided by the effective baseline, rounded to six decimal places.

The two tier tests are then applied to the **rounded** ratio, and both are inclusive at their upper bound. A request whose `variance_pct` is at most 0.05 and whose `requested_amount_cents` is at most 2,500,000 cents — twenty-five thousand dollars — is routed to `MANAGER_DISCRETION`; a ratio of exactly 0.05 therefore stays in the manager's own hands. Failing that, a request whose `variance_pct` is at most 0.15 and whose `requested_amount_cents` is at most 25,000,000 cents — a quarter of a million dollars — is routed to `ASSET_MANAGER`. Everything else, whether by ratio or by absolute size, is routed to `OWNER_REP`. The chosen tier is stored as `approval_level`.

A `MANAGER_DISCRETION` request needs no decision: it is persisted directly in `status` `AUTO_APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id` stays null, and `PENDING` is never observable for it. From that instant it counts toward the `effective_baseline_cents` of every later request in the same category, which is exactly how a manager can walk a series of small variances up a line. Requests routed to `ASSET_MANAGER` or `OWNER_REP` are persisted in `PENDING` and wait.

Deciding a pending request belongs to `AssetManager` and `OwnerRep`, with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `note`. Four guards apply: the request must be `PENDING`, the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`, and the acting role's authority must cover the row's `approval_level` — `AssetManager` covers `ASSET_MANAGER` only, while `OwnerRep` covers both `ASSET_MANAGER` and `OWNER_REP`. An `AssetManager` deciding an `OWNER_REP`-tier request is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT`. Both outcomes — `PENDING → APPROVED` and `PENDING → DENIED` — record `decided_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and nothing else; the difference between them is only that an approval raises the effective baseline for later siblings while a denial does not.

There is a fifth way a pending request dies, and it involves no owner-side judgement: when the owner issues a termination notice, every `PENDING` variance request on any budget of that agreement moves `PENDING → DENIED` with `denied_by_cascade` set to true and `decided_by_user_id` left null, processed in `created_at` ascending order. That flag is the only thing distinguishing a cascade denial from a deliberate one. `AUTO_APPROVED`, `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are all terminal.

Variance requests raised against a budget can be listed by any role, filterable by `category` and by `status`.

### 3.4. The Monthly Reporting Package: Profit-and-Loss, Chargebacks and Fees

The monthly reporting package is the beating heart of the owner-reporting obligation, and it carries two review dimensions that advance independently: a profit-and-loss dimension tracked in `financial_status` and a market-share dimension tracked in `benchmark_status`. Neither waits for the other.

A `HotelManagerOps` actor opens a period against an `ACTIVE` agreement by naming the `fiscal_year` and `period_month` and supplying the period's figures: `rooms_revenue_cents`, `fnb_revenue_cents` and `other_revenue_cents` for the three revenue departments, `operating_expenses_cents` for departmental and undistributed expenses excluding management fees and centralized services, and `closing_cash_available_cents` for the cash sitting in the operating account available to that period's waterfall. The month slot formed by `fiscal_year` and `period_month` is exclusive: a second package for the same slot is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_REPORTED`, while an adjacent month never collides.

Two figures are derived the moment the period is opened, from that package's own inputs and no sibling's. The `gross_revenue_cents` is the sum of the rooms, food-and-beverage and other revenue lines. The `due_date_utc` — the reporting deadline — is the twenty-fifth day of the month following the reported period at midnight UTC; for a period whose `period_month` is 12, it rolls to the twenty-fifth of January in the following calendar year. A freshly opened package sits at `financial_status` `PENDING`, `benchmark_status` `PENDING` and `package_status` `INCOMPLETE`, with `submitted_at` null, `submitted_late` false, `resubmission_count` at 0, `reported_rpi` null, and `allocated_centralized_cents`, `base_fee_cents`, `agop_cents` and `incentive_fee_provisional_cents` all at 0.

Before the period is submitted, the manager posts the centralized-services chargebacks that the management company allocates to the hotel. Each charge names a `service_code` from `RESERVATIONS`, `REVENUE_MANAGEMENT`, `IT_HOSTING`, `PROCUREMENT` and `TALENT`; an `allocation_basis` from `ROOMS_AVAILABLE`, `GROSS_REVENUE`, `HEADCOUNT` and `FLAT`, which is the basis disclosed to the owner for how the hotel's share of the pool was struck; the system-wide `pool_amount_cents` for that service in the period; and the hotel's `hotel_basis_share` of that basis as a decimal fraction. Two booleans complete the payload, both defaulting to false: `allow_deferral`, which decides what happens when the cap bites, and `supporting_detail_provided`, which records whether basis-level supporting detail was attached for the owner. A service code may be charged only once per package; a repeat is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_CODE_ALREADY_CHARGED`. And a charge posted with `allow_deferral` true but `supporting_detail_provided` false is refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_DETAIL_REQUIRED` — the owner will not accept a deferral it cannot audit.

Posting a charge triggers a **full recompute** of the package's allocation, not an incremental one. The period cap is the package's `gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `centralized_services_cap_pct` and floored to whole cents. Every charge on the package participates regardless of its `disclosure_status`, and they are processed in a fixed service priority — `RESERVATIONS` first, then `REVENUE_MANAGEMENT`, then `IT_HOSTING`, then `PROCUREMENT`, then `TALENT` — with `created_at` ascending as a tie-break. Each charge's `requested_amount_cents` is its `pool_amount_cents` multiplied by its `hotel_basis_share`, rounded half up to whole cents. A charge already in `ADJUSTED` keeps its `adjusted_amount_cents` as its `allocated_amount_cents` and still consumes cap headroom; every other charge takes the smaller of its requested amount and the remaining headroom, where the remaining headroom is never treated as less than zero. Its `deferred_amount_cents` is then its requested amount less its allocated amount, and the headroom drops by the allocated amount. Finally the package's `allocated_centralized_cents` is set to the sum of the allocated amounts across all its charges.

What happens when the cap is breached depends entirely on the incoming charge's `allow_deferral` flag, and the two behaviours are mutually exclusive. With `allow_deferral` false, the post is **rejected** with `ERR_CENTRALIZED_CAP_EXCEEDED` whenever cumulative `requested_amount_cents` across the package including the incoming charge would exceed the period cap, and nothing at all is persisted. With `allow_deferral` true, the post **succeeds**: the charge is persisted with its `allocated_amount_cents` truncated to whatever cap remainder was left and `deferred_amount_cents` carrying the rest. Every charge starts life with `disclosure_status` `DISCLOSED` and `adjusted_amount_cents` null. The period's charges can be listed by any role, filterable by `service_code` and `disclosure_status`, which is the owner's window onto chargeback transparency.

Submitting the period's profit-and-loss freezes the numbers. `HotelManagerOps` may submit from `financial_status` `PENDING → SUBMITTED` and, after a dispute, from `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`. Both origins require the agreement to be `ACTIVE` and both require that a budget of the same agreement and `fiscal_year` already holds an approved-equivalent status, failing which the submit is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`. Both origins stamp `submitted_at` with the logical clock and set `submitted_late` to true exactly when that instant is strictly after `due_date_utc` — an instant equal to the deadline is on time. The `DISPUTED` origin additionally increments `resubmission_count` by one; the `PENDING` origin leaves it at zero.

The freeze itself computes four figures in a **pinned order**, because the fees reduce profit while the chargeback cap is keyed on revenue and the dependency would otherwise look circular. First the centralized allocation is settled as described above, producing `allocated_centralized_cents`. Second the base management fee is struck: each of the three revenue lines is multiplied by the agreement's `base_fee_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents **individually**, and the three rounded results are added to give `base_fee_cents`. Summing the revenue first and rounding once is not the contractual order and can differ by a cent, so it is not permitted. Third, `agop_cents` — the adjusted gross operating profit — is the package's `gross_revenue_cents` less its `operating_expenses_cents`, less its `base_fee_cents`, less its `allocated_centralized_cents`, with no intermediate rounding inside that subtraction. The reserve-for-replacement contribution is keyed on gross revenue too, but it is **never** deducted here. Fourth, the monthly slice of the owner priority is the agreement's `owner_priority_cents` divided by twelve and rounded half up to whole cents. Fifth and last, `incentive_fee_provisional_cents` is the package's `agop_cents` less that rounded monthly priority slice, multiplied by the agreement's `incentive_fee_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and floored at zero — a loss month accrues no incentive fee rather than a negative one, and because each month is floored independently, loss months never offset profit months in the provisional accrual.

Each submit raises a fresh set of fee accruals in `ACCRUED`: a `BASE` accrual of `variant` `PROVISIONAL` carrying `base_fee_cents`; a `CENTRALIZED_SERVICES` accrual of `variant` `PROVISIONAL` carrying `allocated_centralized_cents`, omitted entirely when that amount is zero; and an `INCENTIVE` accrual of `variant` `PROVISIONAL` carrying `incentive_fee_provisional_cents`, likewise omitted when zero. A resubmission after a dispute recomputes the fee amounts and creates a whole fresh accrual set rather than editing the old one.

Finally, every transition that touches either status dimension recomputes the roll-up `package_status`, and the four branches are evaluated top to bottom so that contest dominates completion. If `financial_status` is `DISPUTED` or `benchmark_status` is `CHALLENGED`, the roll-up is `CONTESTED`. Otherwise, if both `financial_status` and `benchmark_status` are `ACCEPTED`, it is `COMPLETE`. Otherwise, if either one of them is `ACCEPTED`, it is `PARTIAL`. Otherwise it is `INCOMPLETE`. Packages can be read individually and listed per agreement, filterable by `fiscal_year`, `period_month` and `financial_status`.

### 3.5. Contesting a Centralized-Services Chargeback

Chargeback transparency is enforced through a three-state review cycle on each charge, and the acting roles differ by move. An `AssetManager` contests a charge, moving its `disclosure_status` `DISCLOSED → CONTESTED`; the parent package's `financial_status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` for a contest to be admissible. The contest has teeth: the parent package's `CENTRALIZED_SERVICES` fee accrual, if it sits in `ACCRUED` or `INVOICED`, is moved to `DISPUTED_HELD`, and a package carrying any accrual in `DISPUTED_HELD` cannot have its distribution executed — that attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCRUALS_ON_HOLD`. Contesting a chargeback therefore freezes the whole period's cash, which is precisely the leverage the owner is given.

The manager answers in one of two ways, and both belong to `HotelManagerOps`. **Reaffirming** moves the charge `CONTESTED → DISCLOSED` and releases the held accrual: it returns to `ACCRUED` when the package's `financial_status` is `SUBMITTED`, or to `INVOICED` when the package's `financial_status` is `ACCEPTED`, with its `amount_cents` rewritten to the charge's post-review allocated amount. **Adjusting** moves the charge `CONTESTED → ADJUSTED`, which is terminal, and requires `adjusted_amount_cents` in the payload; the value must be present and between 0 and the charge's current `allocated_amount_cents` inclusive, or the request is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Either review action may carry an optional `note`.

An adjustment cascades in three hops, and each hop fires only because the previous one changed something. First, the charge's `allocated_amount_cents` is replaced by `adjusted_amount_cents` and its `deferred_amount_cents` is recomputed as its `requested_amount_cents` less the new allocated amount. Second, because the charge's allocated amount changed, the package's `allocated_centralized_cents`, its `agop_cents` and its `incentive_fee_provisional_cents` are all recomputed by the same pinned five-step order described in §3.4 — a smaller chargeback means a larger adjusted gross operating profit and therefore a larger provisional incentive fee. Third, because the package's provisional incentive changed, the package's `INCENTIVE` accrual of `variant` `PROVISIONAL` has its `amount_cents` rewritten to the new value, and the `CENTRALIZED_SERVICES` accrual has its `amount_cents` rewritten to the adjusted amount and is returned from `DISPUTED_HELD` to whichever status it held before the contest — `ACCRUED` if the package's `financial_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `INVOICED` if it is `ACCEPTED`. Only after that third hop can the period's cash move again.

### 3.6. Owner Review, Accrual Settlement, the Cash Waterfall and Working-Capital Funding

Owner review is a single operation with two dimensions, and the `dimension` selector — `FINANCIAL` or `BENCHMARK` — decides which of the package's two status fields moves. Only `OwnerRep` may exercise it, the `decision` is either `ACCEPT` or `DISPUTE`, and an optional `note` may accompany either. The named dimension advances; the other is left exactly as it was.

On the financial dimension, both decisions require `financial_status` to be `SUBMITTED` and both tolerate an agreement in `ACTIVE` **or** in `CURE_PERIOD` — the owner is expected to keep reviewing reports while a cure is pending. From there the two branches are disjoint. **Acceptance** moves `financial_status` `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and sets off a four-hop cascade. Because the dimension became `ACCEPTED`, every fee accrual of that package sitting in `ACCRUED` moves to `INVOICED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Because those accruals became `INVOICED`, a `CashDistribution` row is created in `status` `CALCULATED` with its buckets filled by the waterfall described below. Because that distribution may carry an unmet demand, a working capital call is raised when — and only when — its `shortfall_cents` is greater than zero: a call with `reason` `DISTRIBUTION_SHORTFALL`, `amount_cents` equal to the distribution's `shortfall_cents`, `auto_created` set to true and `status` `REQUESTED`. When `shortfall_cents` is zero that fourth hop is skipped entirely and no call appears. The roll-up `package_status` is then recomputed.

**Dispute** on the financial dimension moves `financial_status` `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and does the opposite in every respect: every fee accrual of the package sitting in `ACCRUED` moves to `REVERSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; no `CashDistribution` row is created; no reserve movement is posted; and no working capital call is raised. A reversed accrual keeps its `amount_cents` for audit but stops contributing to any waterfall demand or annual sum. The `package_status` is recomputed, and because a disputed financial dimension dominates the roll-up, the package reads `CONTESTED`. The manager's route back is the resubmission described in §3.4, which increments `resubmission_count` and raises a fresh accrual set.

The waterfall itself is computed once, at the moment the distribution row is created, and its inputs are frozen there. The distribution's `available_cash_cents` is a snapshot of the package's `closing_cash_available_cents` at calculation time. Five bucket demands are computed **before any allocation is made**: the working-capital gap is the agreement's `working_capital_floor_cents` less its `working_capital_balance_cents`, floored at zero so a fully funded account demands nothing; the base-fee demand is the sum of `amount_cents` over the package's `BASE` accruals in `INVOICED`; the reserve demand is the package's `gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `ffe_reserve_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; the owner-priority demand is the agreement's `owner_priority_cents` divided by twelve, rounded half up to whole cents; and the incentive demand is the sum of `amount_cents` over the package's `INCENTIVE` accruals of `variant` `PROVISIONAL` in `INVOICED`. Accruals in `DISPUTED_HELD` and `REVERSED` contribute nothing to any demand, and `CENTRALIZED_SERVICES` accruals are settled outside the waterfall and never form a bucket.

Cash is then allocated in a fixed order, each bucket taking the smaller of the cash still unallocated and its own demand, and each allocation reducing what remains for the next. Bucket one is working-capital restoration, recorded as `wc_restoration_cents`. Bucket two is the base management fee, recorded as `base_fee_paid_cents`. Bucket three is the reserve-for-replacement contribution, recorded as `reserve_contribution_cents`. Bucket four is the owner priority return, recorded as `owner_priority_paid_cents`. Bucket five is the incentive fee, recorded as `incentive_fee_paid_cents`. Whatever survives all five is bucket six, the residual to the owner, recorded as `residual_to_owner_cents`. Finally `shortfall_cents` records the aggregate unmet demand: the sum of the five demands, less the sum of the five amounts actually allocated. Note the ordering consequence the owner should understand: the reserve contribution outranks the owner's own priority return, and the manager's incentive fee ranks below both.

Executing a distribution belongs to `HotelManagerOps` and moves it `CALCULATED → EXECUTED`. Three conditions guard it: the row must be `CALCULATED` — a `HELD` or already-`EXECUTED` row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE` — the agreement's `distribution_hold` must be false, and no accrual of the same package may sit in `DISPUTED_HELD`. Execution moves real money. The agreement's `working_capital_balance_cents` increases by `wc_restoration_cents`. Every working capital call of that agreement whose `reason` is `FLOOR_RESTORATION` and whose `status` is `REQUESTED` moves `REQUESTED → WAIVED_OFFSET` when that restoration amount alone lifts `working_capital_balance_cents` to or above the agreement's `working_capital_floor_cents`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with `funded_amount_cents` left at zero — the call is extinguished by the offsetting distribution rather than funded. The `BASE` and `INCENTIVE` accruals of the package that received cash move `INVOICED → PAID`, each with `paid_amount_cents` set to the amount allocated to its bucket. And a reserve ledger entry is appended with `entry_type` `CONTRIBUTION`, `amount_cents` equal to `reserve_contribution_cents` and `source_kind` `DISTRIBUTION_EXECUTION`, with the agreement's `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` increased by the same amount; that append is skipped when `reserve_contribution_cents` is zero. A `HELD` row that later returns to `CALCULATED` keeps its bucket amounts exactly as computed — the waterfall is never recalculated on release.

Working capital is called in three circumstances, distinguished by the `reason` on the call. A `DISTRIBUTION_SHORTFALL` call is raised automatically by an accepted package as described above. A `CLAIM_ALLOCATION` call is raised automatically by an employment-claim allocation as described in §3.9. A `FLOOR_RESTORATION` call may be raised automatically by an owner-funded brand dispute resolution, or manually by a `HotelManagerOps` actor who supplies the `amount_cents` and the `reason` directly; a manually raised call carries `auto_created` false, while every cascade-raised call carries `auto_created` true. Every call, however raised, has its `due_date_utc` set to the logical clock at the moment of issue advanced by the agreement's `funding_due_days`.

Funding a call belongs to `OwnerRep`, who supplies `funded_amount_cents`. Partial funding is not a concept here: the amount must equal the call's `amount_cents` exactly, and anything else is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A call may be funded from `REQUESTED → FUNDED`, which records the funded amount and increases the agreement's `working_capital_balance_cents` by the call's `amount_cents`, or from `OVERDUE → FUNDED`, which does exactly the same **and** additionally sets `late_funded` to true so the owner's tardiness is on the record. A call that is already `FUNDED` or `WAIVED_OFFSET` cannot be funded again; both are terminal, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CALL_STATE`. The only other way a call moves is the clock: a `REQUESTED` call whose `due_date_utc` the clock has passed becomes `OVERDUE`, with no cash movement whatsoever.

Each period's fee accruals, its calculated distributions, and each agreement's reserve ledger can all be listed by any of the four roles. Accrual lists are filterable by `fee_type`, `variant` and `status`, and they deliberately include `REVERSED` and `DISPUTED_HELD` rows so a disputing owner can see what was unwound. Distribution lists are filterable by `status`. Reserve ledger lists are filterable by `entry_type` and `source_kind` and are returned in append order. Working capital call lists are filterable by `status` and `reason`.

### 3.7. Market Benchmarking, the Annual Performance Test, Cure Rights and Termination

The market-share half of the reporting package tracks how the hotel performs against its competitive set. A `HotelManagerOps` actor posts a benchmark for a period against an `ACTIVE` agreement, naming the hotel's revenue per available room as `hotel_revpar_cents`, the competitive set's as `comp_set_revpar_cents`, the hotel's available room-nights in the period as `rooms_available`, and the provenance as `source` — either `STR_REPORT`, meaning the third-party benchmarking report, or `MANAGER_ESTIMATE`. The row's index `rpi` is the hotel's revenue per available room divided by the competitive set's, multiplied by 100, rounded to two decimal places. Each new benchmark also carries `superseded` false and, through its `report_package_id`, the reporting period it covers.

Posting is admissible from `benchmark_status` `PENDING → SUBMITTED`, which sets the package's `reported_rpi` to the new row's `rpi`, and from `CHALLENGED → SUBMITTED`, which first marks the package's previous non-superseded benchmark with `superseded` true, then creates the new row and replaces the package's `reported_rpi` with the new index. The package's `reported_rpi` is therefore always the simple monthly index of the one non-superseded benchmark, and null while no benchmark exists. This dimension advances whatever the financial dimension happens to be doing, and each posting recomputes `package_status`.

The owner reviews the benchmark dimension through the same review operation with `dimension` set to `BENCHMARK`. Acceptance moves `benchmark_status` `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which makes that month eligible for the annual weighted index used in scoring. A challenge moves `benchmark_status` `SUBMITTED → CHALLENGED`, which excludes the month from the benchmark count until a replacement benchmark has been posted and accepted. Unlike the financial dimension, neither benchmark decision imposes any condition on the agreement's own status — only `benchmark_status` gates them — and both recompute `package_status`. Benchmark rows for a period can be listed by any role, filterable by `source` and by `superseded`, so superseded history remains visible.

Scoring the annual performance test is a `PlatformAdmin` capability, invoked for one agreement and one `fiscal_year`, and it is the most heavily gated computation in the system. The eligible periods are that agreement's report packages for the requested fiscal year whose `financial_status` is `ACCEPTED`; their count is recorded as `accepted_period_count`, and a fiscal year with no accepted period at all is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_PERIODS`. The budget to test against is chosen from that agreement's budgets for the same fiscal year holding `APPROVED`, `DEEMED_APPROVED` or `DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD`; if that candidate set is empty the test is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVED`, and otherwise the candidate with the highest `revision_round` wins, with the most recently created row breaking a tie. That budget's `budgeted_agop_cents` is copied onto the test as `budgeted_agop_snapshot_cents`, and the status it held at that moment is recorded as `budget_source_status`, one of `APPROVED`, `DEEMED_APPROVED` or `DEADLOCK_CARRYFORWARD`.

The snapshot is then pro-rated, because a partial year cannot be judged against a full-year target: `prorated_budget_agop_cents` is the snapshot multiplied by `accepted_period_count` and divided by the agreement's `test_periods_expected`, rounded half up to whole cents. If that pro-rated figure is not greater than zero, no achievement ratio is defined and the test is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_AGOP_NON_POSITIVE`. The actual side is `actual_agop_cents`, the sum of `agop_cents` over the accepted periods, and `gop_achievement_pct` is the actual divided by the pro-rated budget, rounded to four decimal places.

The benchmark leg draws on a deliberately narrow input set: benchmarks sitting on those same accepted periods whose `source` is `STR_REPORT`, whose `superseded` is false, and whose package's `benchmark_status` is `ACCEPTED`. `MANAGER_ESTIMATE` rows never count, and a challenged month never counts. Their number is recorded as `str_month_count`, and if it falls below the agreement's `min_benchmark_months` — ten months out of twelve by default — the test is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BENCHMARK_DATA`. From that set two indices are computed, and only one of them scores. The **scoring** index `rpi_weighted` is 100 multiplied by the sum across those rows of `hotel_revpar_cents` times `rooms_available`, divided by the sum across the same rows of `comp_set_revpar_cents` times `rooms_available`, rounded to two decimal places; because each month is weighted by its available room-nights, a strong month with more room-nights lifts the annual index. The **disclosed** index `rpi_simple_average` is the unweighted mean of the same rows' `rpi` values, rounded to two decimal places; it treats every month equally and therefore differs from the weighted index whenever `rooms_available` varies across months. It is reported for comparison and must never decide the test.

The covenant fails only when **both** legs fail: `gop_achievement_pct` strictly below the agreement's `gop_test_threshold_pct`, and `rpi_weighted` strictly below the agreement's `rpi_test_threshold`. A figure exactly equal to either threshold passes that leg and so saves the test. Where the test does not fail, its `status` is `PASSED`, `streak_after_test` is 0, `cure_payment_due_cents` is 0, `cure_deadline_utc` stays null, and the agreement's `performance_failure_streak` is reset to 0.

Where the test does fail, `streak_after_test` is recorded as the agreement's `performance_failure_streak` plus one, and `cure_payment_due_cents` is the pro-rated budgeted profit less the actual profit, floored at zero. Which failure state results is decided by that streak against the agreement's `consecutive_failure_limit`, and the two branches carry disjoint effects. If the new streak has reached or passed the limit, the test lands in `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED`: the agreement's `performance_failure_streak` is incremented by one, `cure_deadline_utc` stays null because no cure right arises at all, and the agreement's `status` stays `ACTIVE` until the owner chooses to issue notice. If the new streak is still strictly below the limit, the test lands in `FAILED_CURABLE`: `cure_deadline_utc` is set to the scoring instant advanced by the agreement's `cure_window_days`, and the agreement moves `ACTIVE → CURE_PERIOD`, which in turn increments `performance_failure_streak` by one, sets `distribution_hold` to true because the status became `CURE_PERIOD`, and — because the hold went up — moves every `CALCULATED` distribution of that agreement to `HELD` in `created_at` ascending order.

Curing is the manager's privilege and runs in two steps, both through the same operation with an `action` selector and both requiring the logical clock to be at or before `cure_deadline_utc`; a late attempt is refused with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An `ELECT_CURE` action moves the test `FAILED_CURABLE → CURE_ELECTED` and moves no cash — it merely records that the manager intends to cure. A `PAY_CURE` action moves the test `CURE_ELECTED → CURED` and requires `payment_amount_cents` to be at least `cure_payment_due_cents`, refusing anything smaller with `ERR_CURE_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. A sufficient payment writes `cure_paid_cents` as the amount paid and returns the agreement `CURE_PERIOD → ACTIVE`, which resets `performance_failure_streak` to 0, increases `working_capital_balance_cents` by the amount actually paid, sets `distribution_hold` to false, and — because the hold came down — returns every `HELD` distribution of the agreement to `CALCULATED` in `created_at` ascending order with bucket amounts untouched.

A cure right can also simply expire. When the clock passes `cure_deadline_utc`, a test in `FAILED_CURABLE` moves to `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED` and a test in `CURE_ELECTED` moves to `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED` as well — the election lapses without payment. In both cases the agreement stays in `CURE_PERIOD`, and the owner may now issue notice.

Issuing that notice belongs to `OwnerRep`, may carry an optional `reason`, and requires a test in `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED` with the agreement in `ACTIVE` or `CURE_PERIOD`; it moves the test `TERMINATION_TRIGGERED → TERMINATED_NOTICE_ISSUED`. The agreement's move depends on where it stood, and the two branches differ in more than one respect. From `CURE_PERIOD → TERMINATION_NOTICED`: `termination_effective_utc` is set to the current logical clock advanced by the agreement's `termination_notice_days`; `distribution_hold` stays true, since it was already up; every `PENDING` variance request on any budget of the agreement moves to `DENIED` with `denied_by_cascade` true, processed in `created_at` ascending order; and every `SUBMITTED` budget of the agreement moves to `SUPERSEDED`, abandoning the owner decision that was pending. From `ACTIVE → TERMINATION_NOTICED`: `termination_effective_utc` is set the same way; `distribution_hold` is set to true, which was not previously the case; every `CALCULATED` distribution of the agreement moves to `HELD` in `created_at` ascending order; and every `PENDING` variance request moves to `DENIED` with `denied_by_cascade` true in `created_at` ascending order. The asymmetry is deliberate — the `ACTIVE` branch must raise the hold and freeze distributions that the `CURE_PERIOD` branch had already frozen, while the `CURE_PERIOD` branch additionally sweeps away submitted budgets.

Once notice is out, only the clock can finish the job: when it reaches or passes `termination_effective_utc`, the agreement moves `TERMINATION_NOTICED → TERMINATED`, `distribution_hold` stays true, and no further operational write is accepted. `PASSED`, `CURED` and `TERMINATED_NOTICE_ISSUED` are the terminal test outcomes. An agreement's scored tests can be listed by any role, filterable by `fiscal_year` and `status`.

### 3.8. The Annual Incentive True-Up

The monthly incentive fee described in §3.4 is provisional by construction, and the annual true-up is the operation that replaces twelve independent monthly estimates with one properly computed annual figure. A `HotelManagerOps` actor runs it against an `ACTIVE` agreement for a named `fiscal_year`, pinning the agreement with `expected_agreement_version`. It differs from the monthly calculation in three ways that matter, and every one of them can move the number: the annual computation nets loss months against profit months instead of flooring each month at zero, it applies the **full unrounded annual** `owner_priority_cents` instead of twelve separately rounded twelfths, and it adds a second incentive tier that the monthly formula cannot express. The same accepted periods therefore yield a different fee annually than the sum of the monthly provisionals — that divergence is the point of the exercise, not an error.

The eligible periods are that agreement's report packages whose `fiscal_year` matches the request and whose `financial_status` is `ACCEPTED`. Periods in `DISPUTED`, `SUBMITTED` and `PENDING` are all excluded, and a fiscal year with no accepted period is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_PERIODS`. Their count is reported as `accepted_period_count` and `annual_agop_cents` is the sum of `agop_cents` over them — a sum in which a negative month genuinely reduces the annual total.

The fee is then computed in tiers over the excess of annual profit above the owner's full priority. The excess is `annual_agop_cents` less the agreement's `owner_priority_cents`. Where that excess is zero or negative, `final_incentive_fee_cents` is zero. Where it is positive, the first tier's base is the smaller of the excess and the agreement's `incentive_tier2_threshold_cents`, and the second tier's base is the excess less that threshold, floored at zero. `final_incentive_fee_cents` is then the first tier's base multiplied by `incentive_fee_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, **plus** the second tier's base multiplied by `incentive_tier2_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents. Each tier is rounded on its own and the two rounded products are then added; the excess is never multiplied by a blended rate.

What the manager has already collected is `provisional_incentive_total_cents`: the sum of `amount_cents` over that agreement's fee accruals for the same `fiscal_year` whose `fee_type` is `INCENTIVE`, whose `variant` is `PROVISIONAL`, and whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, `INVOICED` or `PAID`. A provisional accrual that was reversed by a disputed package never enters that total, and neither does one sitting in `DISPUTED_HELD`. The settlement figure `true_up_delta_cents` is `final_incentive_fee_cents` less `provisional_incentive_total_cents`, and it is signed: a negative delta — the manager over-collected monthly and owes the difference back — is a legitimate outcome and is persisted as a negative `amount_cents`.

The delta is posted as a new fee accrual with `fee_type` `INCENTIVE`, `variant` `FINAL` and `status` `ACCRUED`, attached to the accepted period with the highest `period_month`, with the most recently created row breaking a tie that the period-uniqueness rule makes unreachable in practice. Because a true-up may legitimately be re-run, any prior `FINAL` accrual of the same agreement and fiscal year is set to `REVERSED` first, so exactly one live `FINAL` accrual exists per fiscal year at any time. That ordering is what makes the operation idempotent in effect: reverse, then post. Finally the agreement's `version` is incremented by one, which is precisely why the operation is version-pinned — a second writer holding the pre-run view would otherwise reverse and re-post against economics it never observed and would be refused with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION`.

The run returns a settlement summary rather than an entity: `fiscal_year`, `accepted_period_count`, `annual_agop_cents`, `final_incentive_fee_cents`, `provisional_incentive_total_cents`, `true_up_delta_cents`, the identifier of the accrual just written as `true_up_accrual_id`, and the identifier of the period it was attached to as `attached_report_package_id`. The `FINAL` accrual it created is visible in that period's accrual list under `variant` `FINAL`, and it settles through the ordinary accrual lifecycle.

### 3.9. Brand Standard Cost Disputes and Employment Claim Liability

A franchise agreement sits above the management agreement, and the two parties do not always agree on what the franchisor's brand standards cost. An `AssetManager` opens a dispute against an `ACTIVE` agreement, naming the requirement at issue as `franchise_requirement_ref`, the manager-side figure as `estimated_cost_cents`, and the franchisor's remediation window as `remediation_start_utc` — inclusive — and `remediation_deadline_utc` — exclusive, and strictly after the start. The dispute opens in `OPENED` with `round` at 0, `current_cost_estimate_cents` seeded from `estimated_cost_cents`, `funding_source` null and `funded_amount_cents` at zero. One structural conflict is refused at creation: a new dispute whose half-open remediation window overlaps the half-open standstill window of an existing `ESCALATED_TO_FRANCHISOR` dispute on the **same** agreement and the **same** `franchise_requirement_ref` is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both windows are half-open, windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending exactly where the other begins — do not overlap and are admissible; only a genuine interior overlap is refused, and a different `franchise_requirement_ref` never conflicts.

The dispute then advances through a single round-posting operation whose `action` selects the move, and the negotiation is a genuine cycle. A `MANAGER_RESPONSE` by `HotelManagerOps` moves `OPENED → MANAGER_RESPONDED` and requires `cost_estimate_cents`, which replaces `current_cost_estimate_cents`; it does not touch `round`. An `OWNER_COUNTER` by either `AssetManager` or `OwnerRep` moves `MANAGER_RESPONDED → OWNER_COUNTERED`, also requires `cost_estimate_cents`, replaces `current_cost_estimate_cents`, and increments `round` by one — only the owner's counter counts a round. A further `MANAGER_RESPONSE` moves `OWNER_COUNTERED → MANAGER_RESPONDED` and again replaces `current_cost_estimate_cents` without touching `round`, so the pair may cycle indefinitely with `round` advancing once per full turn.

Escalation is the manager's exit from a stalled negotiation. An `ESCALATE_TO_FRANCHISOR` action by `HotelManagerOps` moves `OWNER_COUNTERED → ESCALATED_TO_FRANCHISOR`, and it requires `round` to have reached or passed the agreement's `max_dispute_rounds`; a premature attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`. Escalation sets `standstill_start_utc` to the current logical clock and `standstill_end_utc` to that instant advanced by the agreement's `dispute_standstill_days`. During the standstill nothing happens on its own; when the clock reaches or passes `standstill_end_utc` the dispute moves `ESCALATED_TO_FRANCHISOR → DEADLOCKED`, the requirement remains unfunded, and no reserve movement occurs. `DEADLOCKED` is terminal.

Resolution is the owner's decision and comes in two flavours, each reachable from either negotiation state with identical consequences. A `RESOLVE_OWNER_FUNDS` action by `OwnerRep` from `MANAGER_RESPONDED`, or the same action from `OWNER_COUNTERED`, requires a `funding_source` and sets `funded_amount_cents` to `current_cost_estimate_cents` — the figure standing at the moment of resolution, whichever party last moved it. The `funding_source` then decides where the money comes from, and the two branches are mutually exclusive. Under `FFE_RESERVE` the agreement's `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` must be at least `current_cost_estimate_cents`, and a thinner reserve is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE_BALANCE`; a sufficient reserve has a reserve ledger entry appended with `entry_type` `DISBURSEMENT`, `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `current_cost_estimate_cents` and `source_kind` `BRAND_DISPUTE_FUNDING`, and `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` is decreased by that amount. Under `OWNER_CONTRIBUTION` the reserve is untouched — there is no balance requirement at all and no ledger entry — and instead a working capital call is created with `reason` `FLOOR_RESTORATION`, `amount_cents` equal to `current_cost_estimate_cents`, `auto_created` true and `status` `REQUESTED`, which the owner then funds through the ordinary funding route.

A funded resolution can be undone. A `RESCIND_RESOLUTION` action by `OwnerRep` moves `RESOLVED_OWNER_FUNDS → OWNER_COUNTERED`, returning the dispute to live negotiation. Where the resolution drew on the reserve, rescission appends a compensating reserve ledger entry with `entry_type` `DISBURSEMENT_REVERSAL`, `amount_cents` equal to the positive `funded_amount_cents` and `source_kind` `BRAND_DISPUTE_RESCISSION`, and increases `ffe_reserve_balance_cents` by that amount, so the reserve returns to exactly where it stood. In every case `funded_amount_cents` is reset to zero and `funding_source` is reset to null, while `round` is left unchanged — rescinding does not rewind the negotiation count. Because `RESOLVED_OWNER_FUNDS` is reachable and rescindable, it is not terminal.

The other resolution simply drops the requirement. A `RESOLVE_WAIVED` action by `OwnerRep` from `MANAGER_RESPONDED`, or the same action from `OWNER_COUNTERED`, moves the dispute to `RESOLVED_WAIVED` with no cash movement in either case: no reserve entry, no working capital call, no change to `funded_amount_cents` or `funding_source`. `RESOLVED_WAIVED` is terminal. An agreement's disputes can be listed by any role, filterable by `status`.

Employment liability is the second place where the owner-manager split has teeth, and it turns on who employs the hotel's staff. A claim is filed by `HotelManagerOps` against an `ACTIVE` agreement with a `position_class` of `GM`, `EXEC_COMMITTEE` or `LINE_STAFF`, a `claim_amount_cents` greater than zero, and an optional `gross_negligence` flag defaulting to false. Filing copies the agreement's `employee_model` into the claim's `employer_of_record`, so the claim carries the employment arrangement that was in force when it was filed — either `OWNER` or `MANAGER` — and persists the claim in `FILED` with both `owner_share_cents` and `manager_share_cents` at zero.

Allocating the claim belongs to `AssetManager` and requires the agreement to be `ACTIVE` or in `CURE_PERIOD`. The split is computed by walking four conditions in order, and the first that matches decides the manager's share. Where `gross_negligence` is true, the manager bears the whole claim up to the cap: `manager_share_cents` is the smaller of `claim_amount_cents` and the agreement's `manager_indemnity_cap_cents`. This negligence override ignores both the position class and the employee model entirely — a `LINE_STAFF` claim under `OWNER_EMPLOYEES` still lands on the manager when negligence is alleged. Otherwise, where the agreement's `employee_model` is `OWNER_EMPLOYEES`, `manager_share_cents` is zero, because the owner is the employer of record. Otherwise, where the `position_class` is `GM` or `EXEC_COMMITTEE`, the claim is split evenly with the cap applied on top: `manager_share_cents` is the smaller of `claim_amount_cents` divided by two using floor division and `manager_indemnity_cap_cents`, so an odd-cent claim leaves the extra cent with the owner. Otherwise — a `LINE_STAFF` claim under `MANAGER_EMPLOYEES` — `manager_share_cents` is zero. In every branch `owner_share_cents` is `claim_amount_cents` less `manager_share_cents`, which makes the conservation invariant exact: the two shares always sum to `claim_amount_cents`, with no rounding remainder anywhere.

Allocation moves the claim `FILED → ALLOCATED` and cascades in two hops. Because the shares were set, the agreement's `working_capital_balance_cents` is decreased by `owner_share_cents` and its `manager_liability_accrued_cents` is increased by `manager_share_cents`. Then, because that decrease may have pushed the working capital below the covenant, a working capital call is created with `reason` `CLAIM_ALLOCATION`, `amount_cents` equal to `working_capital_floor_cents` less the now-reduced `working_capital_balance_cents`, `auto_created` true and `status` `REQUESTED` — and that second hop is skipped entirely when the balance still meets the floor. The call's amount is measured against the balance **after** the first hop, never before it.

An allocation can be reversed and re-taken. A `REVERSE` action by `AssetManager` moves the claim `ALLOCATED → REVERSED`, increases `working_capital_balance_cents` by `owner_share_cents`, decreases `manager_liability_accrued_cents` by `manager_share_cents` — restoring both balances exactly — and then resets `owner_share_cents` and `manager_share_cents` to zero. A reversed claim contributes nothing to `manager_liability_accrued_cents`, which is always the sum of `manager_share_cents` over claims in `ALLOCATED` alone. A subsequent `ALLOCATE` action moves the claim `REVERSED → ALLOCATED` with exactly the same side-effect set as the first allocation, recomputed against the agreement's configuration **as it stands at the new allocation instant** — so a cap or employee model changed in between produces a different split, and the cycle `ALLOCATED → REVERSED → ALLOCATED` may repeat. Neither state is terminal. Reversal does not retract any working capital call the earlier allocation raised; the call stands and follows its own lifecycle. An agreement's claims and their splits can be listed by any role, filterable by `position_class` and `status`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All paths below are absolute and complete. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer `201 Created`; every other successful call answers `200 OK`, including the action endpoints that transition an existing resource and the true-up run that returns a settlement summary rather than an entity. Where an action endpoint returns the resource it moved, the response `data` carries that resource in full with its recomputed derived fields already applied. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` together with the filters named in the workflow sections above, and each returns `response_type` `"array"`.
