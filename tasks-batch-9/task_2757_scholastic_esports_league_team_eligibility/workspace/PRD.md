# Scholastic Esports League API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

High school esports has outgrown the spreadsheet. A single state-wide scholastic league now coordinates dozens of districts, each with its own policy on which game titles are age-appropriate, how academic standing is measured, how long a coach may contest a reported score, and how much of a team's entry fee the district is willing to subsidize. The league office schedules matches onto shared game-server pools, referees score disputes between rival coaches, investigates account sharing and cheat detections, seeds playoff brackets, and — increasingly — feeds a scholarship showcase pathway that college recruiters are legally constrained in how they may use. Every one of those activities has an eligibility question buried inside it, and every eligibility question has a different owner: a registrar certifies enrollment, an eligibility desk certifies academics, an integrity desk certifies conduct, and a district administrator certifies that the game itself is permissible.

This document specifies the API that carries that whole apparatus. The system's governing concern is **defensible eligibility**: whenever a student takes the field, the league must be able to show who verified what, on which document, against which district's thresholds, and at which moment. That requirement drives three structural commitments. First, verification is deliberately split into independent axes performed by different people, so no single operator can wave a student onto a roster. Second, every derived number the league acts upon — a team's entry fee, its integrity score, its seed, a student's projected scholarship — is recomputed from pinned inputs under a pinned evaluation order, never estimated. Third, every sanction has a documented compensating action, so an overturned appeal restores the league table to exactly the figures it would have held had the sanction never been imposed.

The API is a flat collection of top-level resources. There are no nested resource paths: a roster slot is addressed directly, not underneath its team, and parentage is expressed only by foreign-key fields carried in request bodies and offered as list filters. Time is entirely under test control through a logical clock, because nearly every rule in this specification is a deadline, a window, or an interval.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, temporal semantics, resource identity, concurrency control, rejection precedence, numeric precision, and the league's conservation invariants.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. Once the caller is authenticated, the role must appear in the target operation's authorized-role list, which §4 tabulates; a role acting outside that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `LeagueAdmin`, `DistrictAdmin`, `AthleticDirector`, `Coach`, `EligibilityOfficer`, `IntegrityOfficer`, and `Recruiter`.

- `LeagueAdmin` runs league operations — the title catalogue, seasons, scheduling, seeding, adjudication and the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `LeagueAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, as `reported_by_user_id` on a fair-play case it opens) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `DistrictAdmin` holds district authority over title approvals. When `X-User-Role` is `DistrictAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `District`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `District` compare the resource's district foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A district administrator deciding a title approval must therefore be the district named in that approval's `district_id`.
- `AthleticDirector` runs the school athletics office — coach designation and certification, student records, approval requests, and team withdrawal. When `X-User-Role` is `AthleticDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks compare the governing school foreign key (a team's `school_id`, for instance) to `X-User-ID`.
- `Coach` builds rosters, registers and locks teams, reports results, files disputes, controls broadcasts, and appeals sanctions. When `X-User-Role` is `Coach`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Coach`; ownership checks compare the governing team's `coach_id` to `X-User-ID`, resolved through `RosterSlot.team_id`, through a match's `home_team_id` or `away_team_id`, or through a fair-play case's `team_id`, whichever governs the resource under attack.
- `EligibilityOfficer` staffs the independent eligibility desk that performs the two verification axes on roster slots and posts academic terms. When `X-User-Role` is `EligibilityOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque officer identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `enrollment_verified_by_user_id` or `academics_verified_by_user_id` on the slot it acts on, and compares those recorded values against each other rather than against any entity's `id`.
- `IntegrityOfficer` investigates and adjudicates fair-play cases and decides appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `IntegrityOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque officer identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `investigator_user_id`, `decider_user_id` or `appeal_decider_user_id` on the case, and compares those recorded values against each other, never against an entity `id`.
- `Recruiter` is a college recruiter permitted to log contacts against showcase-nominated students. When `X-User-Role` is `Recruiter`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque recruiter identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is never compared to an entity `id`.

An authenticated, authorized caller in an entity-backed role that reaches for a resource belonging to someone else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This applies to team creation, team registration, roster lock, team withdrawal, roster-slot creation and release, match result reports, dispute filing, broadcast control, fair-play appeals, title-approval creation, title-approval decisions, and coach certification.

Two separations of duty are enforced on `X-User-ID` within the opaque officer space, and both raise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The officer who clears a slot's academics must not be the officer already recorded in that slot's `enrollment_verified_by_user_id`. The officer who decides an appeal must not be the same identifier as that case's `investigator_user_id` or its `decider_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips a number of records from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` reports the size of the full filtered result set before the limit and offset are applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical League Clock

Nothing in this system reads the real wall clock. Every window, deadline, expiry, age computation and elapsed-days credit is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**, whose value at server startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. A league administrator moves the clock with a dedicated administrative capability that accepts a single `current_utc` instant. The clock is monotonic: a requested `current_utc` earlier than the clock's present value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409), while a value equal to the present value is accepted and leaves the clock where it is. Once the clock moves, all subsequent temporal evaluations read the new value.

Datetime serialization is uniform across the API. Every datetime field — inputs, outputs, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and every field whose name ends in `_at` — is serialized as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, in the shape `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`. An input supplied with fewer or more fractional digits is normalized to exactly three digits when it is persisted, so a client that sends a whole-second timestamp reads back a three-digit one.

Four fields are pure dates rather than instants and use the `YYYY-MM-DD` shape with no time component: a student's `birthdate`, a season's `age_cutoff_date`, a school's `enrollment_verified_through`, and a coach's `background_check_expires_on`. When one of these dates is compared against the logical clock, the comparison is made against the clock's calendar date in UTC.

All money in the system is expressed in integer cents. No monetary amount is ever persisted as a floating-point number.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Auditing and Record Versions

Every resource in this API — districts, schools, coaches, students, titles, title approvals, seasons, teams, roster slots, matches and fair-play cases — carries the same four system-managed properties.

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never construct one.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant, with three fractional digits, at which the record was first written. It is taken from the logical clock and never changes afterwards.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant, with three fractional digits, at which the record was last modified. It is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by exactly one on every mutation of that record. A recomputation of a derived field — a team's `starter_slots_used`, `integrity_score` or `seed_points`, a season's `registered_team_count` or `match_point_pool`, a student's `scholarship_award_state` — is a mutation like any other: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the record that changed.

Every entity in this system carries all four properties; none is create-only. When one operation cascades into several records, each touched record independently advances its own `updated_at` and `version`; there is no shared or global version counter.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Two Supersession Points

Two write operations in this system legitimately *supersede* data a client may already have read, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent record's `version`. Every other endpoint in this specification takes no version field at all, and supplying one has no effect.

A result report submitted to `POST /matches/{matchId}/result-reports` must carry `expected_match_version` as a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Match`. The product deliberately allows a coach to re-report a score, and each accepted report supersedes that side's earlier figures and advances the match's `version`. If the supplied `expected_match_version` does not equal the match's current `version`, the report is written against a stale view of the match and would silently overwrite a supersession the reporter never saw; it is therefore rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

An academic posting submitted to `POST /students/{studentId}/academic-postings` must carry `expected_student_version` as a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Student`. A posting shifts the student's latest term into the prior-term register and rewrites the derived standing, so a posting written against a stale view would push an already-superseded term back into the shift register. If `expected_student_version` does not equal the student's current `version`, the posting is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Both checks run after the path and body have been validated but **before** any state precondition on the target is evaluated, because a caller holding a stale view cannot be trusted to have observed the record's current state.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once. The league requires a deterministic answer in that situation, so all checks are arranged in one global ladder and the **first** violated rung decides the response. The ladder is evaluated strictly in the order below; a rung is only reached when every earlier rung passed.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the target operation's role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Ownership.** An entity-backed role acts only on its own resources — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a live record, validated left to right. The first unresolvable segment decides the error, drawn from `ERR_DISTRICT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COACH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROSTER_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FAIR_PLAY_CASE_NOT_FOUND` — each HTTP 404.
5. **Body shape.** Every required field present, every enumerated value inside its declared set, every number inside its declared range, every pure date parsing as `YYYY-MM-DD`, every datetime parsing as ISO 8601, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` not negative — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **District GPA thresholds.** A district's `probation_gpa_floor` is not greater than its `min_gpa` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_GPA_THRESHOLDS`. This rung applies only to district creation.
7. **Distinct match participants.** A match's `home_team_id` differs from its `away_team_id` — otherwise `ERR_SELF_MATCH_FORBIDDEN`. This rung applies only to match scheduling.
8. **Action-conditional field presence,** reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It applies to four operations: fair-play case decisions, dispute actions, match finalization and broadcast control. A `SUSTAIN` decision requires `severity` and a `DISMISS` decision forbids it; a dispute action of `UPHOLD` requires both `corrected_home_games_won` and `corrected_away_games_won` while `REJECT` forbids both; finalizing a contested result requires both `override_home_games_won` and `override_away_games_won` while finalizing an already-decided result forbids both; a broadcast action of `REGISTER` requires `broadcast_school_id`, `broadcast_start_at` and `broadcast_end_at` while `GO_LIVE`, `RESUME` and `END` forbid all three.
9. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body resolves to a live record, reported with the same 404 codes as rung 4. It applies to school creation, coach designation, student enrolment, title-approval creation, season creation, team creation, roster-slot creation, match scheduling, fair-play case creation and showcase nomination.
10. **Polymorphic discriminator agreement.** On fair-play case creation, a `subject_type` of `STUDENT` requires `subject_student_id` present and `subject_match_id` absent, while a `subject_type` of `MATCH_BROADCAST` requires `subject_match_id` present and `subject_student_id` absent — otherwise `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`.
11. **Optimistic concurrency,** as §2.6 describes, reported as `ERR_STALE_VERSION` on result reports and academic postings.
12. **Season stage.** The season is in a stage that permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`. Registration requires `REGISTRATION_OPEN`; a stage advance requires the declared from-stage; a seeding run requires `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`; result reporting requires `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`; server configuration requires `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`.
13. **Seeding freshness** on the advance from `REGULAR_SEASON` to `PLAYOFFS`, which requires `seeding_state` to be `SEEDED` — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_SEEDING_NOT_CURRENT`.
14. **Team from-state.** Registration requires `DRAFT`; roster lock requires `REGISTERED`; withdrawal requires `REGISTERED`, `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `COMPETING`, `SUSPENDED`, `PLAYOFF_SEEDED` or `ELIMINATED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TEAM_STATE`.
15. **Roster openness.** The governing team's status is `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED` — otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED`. It guards roster-slot creation, enrollment verification, academics verification and slot release.
16. **Slot not already released.** The target slot's `participation_status` is not `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_SLOT_RELEASED`. It guards enrollment verification, academics verification and slot release.
17. **Match play and result state,** otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`. Server configuration requires `play_status` `SCHEDULED`; result reporting requires `SERVER_READY`; finalization requires `play_status` `PLAYED` with `result_status` either `CONFIRMED`, `ADJUDICATED` or `CONTESTED`; a forfeit requires `SCHEDULED` or `SERVER_READY`; dispute filing requires `result_status` `CONFIRMED` or `ADJUDICATED`; going live requires `play_status` `SERVER_READY` or `PLAYED`.
18. **Dispute from-state,** otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`. Filing requires `NONE`; `ACCEPT_REVIEW` requires `OPEN`; `REQUEST_INFO`, `UPHOLD` and `REJECT` require `UNDER_REVIEW`; `RESUME_REVIEW` requires `NEEDS_INFO`.
19. **Broadcast from-state,** otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BROADCAST_STATE`. `REGISTER` requires `NONE`; `GO_LIVE` requires `SCHEDULED`; `RESUME` requires `SUSPENDED` together with fewer than three accumulated `spectator_strikes`; `END` requires `LIVE` or `SUSPENDED`.
20. **Fair-play case from-state,** otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Taking a case requires `REPORTED`; a decision requires `INVESTIGATING`; an appeal requires `SUSTAINED`; an appeal decision requires `APPEALED`.
21. **Title-approval from-state,** otherwise `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`. `APPROVE` and `DENY` require `PENDING`; `REVOKE` requires `APPROVED`.
22. **Two-person roster verification** on academics clearance — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
23. **Appeal independence** on the appeal decision — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
24. **Coach certification** is `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_COACH_NOT_CERTIFIED`. It guards both team registration and enrollment verification.
25. **Coach background check** has not lapsed — otherwise `ERR_COACH_BACKGROUND_CHECK_EXPIRED`. It guards both team registration and enrollment verification.
26. **Student enrolment** is `ENROLLED` — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. It guards both roster-slot creation and enrollment verification.
27. **Registrar certification currency** — otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CERTIFICATION_LAPSED`, on enrollment verification.
28. **Document birthdate agreement** — otherwise `ERR_BIRTHDATE_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH`, on enrollment verification.
29. **Publisher age floor** — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_BELOW_TITLE_MIN_AGE`, on enrollment verification.
30. **League age ceiling** — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_OVER_AGE_LIMIT`, on enrollment verification.
31. **Academic standing is evaluable and not ineligible** — otherwise `ERR_ACADEMIC_STANDING_INELIGIBLE`, on academics verification.
32. **Probation starter restriction** — otherwise `ERR_PROBATION_STARTER_RESTRICTED`, on academics verification.
33. **Student fair-play standing** is `CLEAR` — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_FAIR_PLAY_SANCTIONED`. It guards three operations: roster-slot creation, academics verification and showcase nomination.
34. **Title rating within the district ceiling** — otherwise `ERR_TITLE_AGE_RATING_EXCEEDS_DISTRICT_CEILING`, on an approving title decision.
35. **Title approved by the team's district** — otherwise `ERR_TITLE_NOT_DISTRICT_APPROVED`, on team registration.
36. **Student and team share a school** — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, on roster-slot creation.
37. **Coach and team share a school** — otherwise `ERR_COACH_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, on team creation.
38. **Team title matches season title** — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_TITLE_MISMATCH`, on team creation.
39. **Both participants belong to the match's season** — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_SEASON_MISMATCH`, on match scheduling.
40. **The named team participates in the match** — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_MATCH_PARTICIPANT`. It guards result reports, dispute filing and forfeits.
41. **Broadcast school agreement** — otherwise `ERR_BROADCAST_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, on a registering broadcast action.
42. **Case subject belongs to the charged team** — otherwise `ERR_SUBJECT_TEAM_MISMATCH`, on fair-play case creation.
43. **Nominated student is on the nominating team** — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ON_TEAM`, on showcase nomination.
44. **Registration window** — otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
45. **Roster-lock deadline** — otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_DEADLINE_PASSED`.
46. **Match inside the season window** — otherwise `ERR_MATCH_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`.
47. **Server pool capacity** — otherwise `ERR_SERVER_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
48. **Broadcast channel capacity** — otherwise `ERR_BROADCAST_CHANNEL_CONFLICT`.
49. **Dispute window** — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
50. **Recruiting dead period** — otherwise `ERR_RECRUITING_DEAD_PERIOD`.
51. **Clock monotonicity** — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN`.
52. **School code uniqueness within its district** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_NCES_CODE`.
53. **Global title-name uniqueness** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TITLE_NAME`.
54. **Team-name uniqueness within the season** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_NAME`.
55. **One live approval per district and title** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TITLE_APPROVAL`.
56. **One live slot per team and student** — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROSTER_SLOT`.
57. **Starter capacity available** — otherwise `ERR_STARTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
58. **Roster minimum met** — otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. It guards both team registration and roster lock.
59. **Not already nominated** — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_NOMINATED`.
60. **Showcase pathway required for recruiting contact** — otherwise `ERR_SHOWCASE_NOT_NOMINATED`.
61. **Rolling recruiting-contact cap** — otherwise `ERR_RECRUITING_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
62. **A non-empty seed set** — otherwise `ERR_SEED_ORDER_EMPTY`.
63. **District subsidy budget,** evaluated last of all — otherwise `ERR_SUBSIDY_POOL_EXHAUSTED`.

Four precedence pairs are worth calling out because they are the ones implementers most often invert. A registration attempt on a team that is already registered *and* whose district pool is exhausted reports `ERR_INVALID_TEAM_STATE`, because the team from-state rung sits far above the fiscal rung. A dispute filed after the window has closed by a team that is not even a participant reports `ERR_TEAM_NOT_MATCH_PARTICIPANT`, because referential agreement precedes temporal windows. An academics clearance attempted by the same officer who verified enrollment, on a slot whose team has already locked, reports `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED`, because state precedes segregation of duties. And a recruiting contact attempted inside the dead period by a recruiter who has also exhausted the rolling cap reports `ERR_RECRUITING_DEAD_PERIOD`, because the temporal dead-period rung precedes the quota rung.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision Across the League's Ledgers

Four distinct precision regimes coexist in this system, and each is fixed.

Grade-point averages are carried to **three decimal places, rounded half up**. This applies to a district's `min_gpa` and `probation_gpa_floor`, to a posted term's GPA, to a student's `effective_gpa`, and to the snapshot `effective_gpa_at_verification` taken on a roster slot. Standing comparisons are performed on the *rounded* three-decimal value, using "at or above" semantics in both directions.

Seed points are carried to **four decimal places, rounded half up**, and seed comparisons are made at that width.

Integrity scores and the per-case penalties that feed them are carried to **two decimal places, rounded half up**. Penalties are rounded per case and only then summed; the sum is never rounded a second time in a way that could differ from the per-case figures.

Money is carried in **integer cents**, and where a percentage is applied to money the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where several money line items are each subsidized, each line item is rounded to whole cents *before* the line items are summed — §3.6 states the pinned order and why summing first is wrong.

### 2.9. Conservation Invariants and Compensating Actions

The league treats three quantities as conserved, and every operation that moves one of them has a documented counterpart that moves it back. These invariants are not advisory: they must hold after every single request completes.

**Season match-point conservation.** A season's `match_point_pool` always equals the sum of `match_points` across all of that season's teams. It also always equals three points for every match in the season that is `FINALIZED` or `FORFEITED` and has a `winner_team_id`, plus two points for every `FINALIZED` match with no `winner_team_id` whose `result_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ADJUDICATED`. The compensating pairs that keep this true are the dispute-upheld re-award, which reverts the previously credited figures before applying the corrected ones, and the suspension forfeit, whose three-point award is reverted when an appeal restores the suspended team.

**District subsidy conservation.** For every district, the district's remaining `subsidy_pool_cents` plus the sum of `subsidy_granted_cents` across every team fielded by a school in that district always equals the district's `subsidy_pool_initial_cents`, an immutable copy of the pool taken at district creation. The compensating pair is the debit taken at team registration and the restoration performed on team withdrawal.

**Starter-capacity conservation.** For every team, `starter_slots_used` plus `starter_slots_available` always equals the season's `max_starters`. This holds after every roster mutation, including slot creation, slot release, and cascaded ineligibility. The compensating pair is roster-slot creation and roster-slot release.

Cascades are the mechanism by which these invariants survive. A single request may legitimately mutate a chain of records — a sustained case can change a student's fair-play standing, which changes a roster slot's participation, which changes a team's active starter count, which suspends the team, which forfeits its unplayed matches, which changes the season's point pool and staleness, which in turn marks a nominated student's scholarship for recalculation. Each link in such a chain is a real mutation on a real record: it refreshes that record's `updated_at` and increments its `version`. Every cascade in this specification also has an inverse described alongside it, and the inverse must restore exactly the figures that preceded the cascade — no residue, no drift.

### 2.10. Flat Topology and Cross-Entity Resolution

Every collection in this API is mounted at the URL root. There are no nested collection paths: a roster slot, a match and a fair-play case are each addressed directly by their own identifier. Parentage is carried by foreign-key fields inside request bodies, and each such field is offered back as an optional list filter so that the children of a parent remain discoverable — schools by `district_id`; coaches by `school_id`; students by `school_id` or `nominating_team_id`; title approvals by `district_id`, `title_id` or `requested_by_school_id`; seasons by `title_id`; teams by `season_id`, `school_id`, `title_id` or `coach_id`; roster slots by `team_id` or `student_id`; matches by `season_id`, `home_team_id`, `away_team_id`, `broadcast_school_id`, `dispute_filed_by_team_id`, `winner_team_id` or `forfeiting_team_id`; and fair-play cases by `team_id`, `subject_student_id` or `subject_match_id`. Lists additionally filter on the natural status and classification fields — a district's `state_code`, a school's or season's or team's or approval's or case's `status`, a coach's `certification_status`, a student's `academic_standing` or `showcase_status`, a title's `age_rating` or `server_pool_id`, a season's `division`, a slot's `slot_role` or `participation_status`, a match's `play_status`, `result_status`, `dispute_status` or `broadcast_status`, and a case's `case_type`.

Because the topology is flat, several rules require the server to walk a chain of references to find the policy that governs an operation. Three chains recur. A student's governing district is reached through that student's school and that school's `district_id`. A match's dispute window is governed by the **home** side only: the home team's school, and that school's district. A broadcast's cool-down buffer is likewise governed by the district of the school whose channel carries the stream. Where a chain is walked, the resolution is stated explicitly in the workflow that uses it, and no other party's policy is consulted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The District Policy Envelope and the School Registry

Everything downstream of a district inherits that district's policy, so the league office creates districts first and pins their entire rule set at creation. A district carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, both required. Its `max_age_rating` is the highest ESRB rating the district will permit for league titles, drawn from `E`, `E10`, `T` and `M` and defaulting to `T`; these four values form an ordinal scale in which `E` is lowest, then `E10`, then `T`, and `M` is highest. Its `academic_standing_basis` selects which of the two academic evaluation variants applies to its students, either `TERM_SNAPSHOT` (the default) or `ROLLING_CUMULATIVE`. Two GPA thresholds follow: `min_gpa`, the grade-point average at or above which a student is in good standing, defaulting to 2.000, and `probation_gpa_floor`, the average at or above which a student below `min_gpa` is placed on probation rather than declared ineligible, defaulting to 1.750. Both live in the closed range 0.000 through 4.000 with three decimals, and a district whose `probation_gpa_floor` is greater than its `min_gpa` is nonsensical and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GPA_THRESHOLDS`.

The remaining district settings govern windows and budgets. `dispute_window_hours` is how many hours after a result is confirmed a coach may still contest it; it defaults to 48 and must be between 1 and 336 inclusive — that upper bound is two full weeks. `subsidy_pct` is the fraction of each entry-fee line item the district absorbs, defaulting to 0.250 and constrained to the closed range 0.000 through 1.000 at three decimals. `subsidy_pool_cents` is the district's remaining subsidy budget, defaulting to 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — and never negative. At the moment a district is created the server copies that opening figure into `subsidy_pool_initial_cents`, a system-owned field that is never written again and serves as the anchor for the subsidy conservation invariant of §2.9. Recruiting policy contributes four more settings: `recruiting_dead_period_start` and `recruiting_dead_period_end` bracket a blackout interval and are both null when no blackout is configured; `recruiting_contact_cap_per_window` caps contacts per student inside the rolling window and defaults to 3, bounded between 1 and 50 inclusive; and `recruiting_window_days` is that window's length in days, defaulting to 30 and bounded between 1 and 365 inclusive. Finally, `broadcast_buffer_minutes` is the cool-down applied to both ends of a requested broadcast window when the system tests a school channel for conflicts; it defaults to 15 and lies between 0 and 240 inclusive.

Schools are registered underneath a district by supplying that district's identifier as `district_id` together with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and an `nces_code` of 1 to 24 characters. The code is the school's identifier in league paperwork and must be unique among the schools of the same district; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NCES_CODE`. Two operational settings accompany the school. `enrollment_verified_through` is the date, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, through which the registrar certifies the school's enrollment roster, defaulting to `2027-06-30`; once the logical clock's date passes it, the eligibility desk can no longer accept enrollment for that school's students. `concurrent_broadcast_capacity` is how many simultaneous streams the school's channel supports; it defaults to 1 and is bounded between 1 and 4 inclusive. Every new school begins with `status` `ACTIVE`; the enumeration also admits `PROBATION` and `INACTIVE`.

### 3.2. Coaching Credentials, Student Records and Academic Standing

A school designates a coach by supplying `school_id`, the coach's legal `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and an `email` that must be present and must contain an at-sign. A newly designated coach is not yet cleared to do anything consequential: `certification_status` begins at `PENDING`, `background_check_expires_on` is null, and `certified_at` is null. The enumeration for `certification_status` is `PENDING`, `CERTIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`.

Certification is a separate act, performed by the school's athletic director or by the league office, that carries an `action` of either `CERTIFY` or `SUSPEND`. Certifying sets `certification_status` to `CERTIFIED`, writes the supplied `background_check_expires_on` date onto the coach, and stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock. The supplied expiry date is accepted as given — including a date already in the past, which is how the league reproduces a lapsed clearance for audit rehearsals. Suspending sets `certification_status` to `SUSPENDED` and leaves the expiry and the certification timestamp where they are. Two later operations read those two fields together: team registration and enrollment verification both require the governing coach to be `CERTIFIED`, rejecting a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` coach with `ERR_COACH_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and both require `background_check_expires_on` to be on or after the logical clock's current date, rejecting an earlier expiry with `ERR_COACH_BACKGROUND_CHECK_EXPIRED`. The comparison is inclusive: an expiry falling on exactly today's date still clears.

Student records are enrolled by the league office, an athletic director or the eligibility desk, and carry `school_id`, `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a required `birthdate` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `grade_level` between 9 and 12 inclusive. `enrollment_status` is what the registrar reports and defaults to `ENROLLED`, the enumeration also admitting `WITHDRAWN` and `GRADUATED`. `credits_completed` records cumulative credits earned and defaults to 0, never negative. A fresh student record has `academic_standing` `UNEVALUATED`, `fair_play_status` `CLEAR`, `showcase_status` `NONE`, and an empty `recruiting_contact_log`.

Academic history is a **shift register of depth two**. Posting a term supplies `term_label`, `term_gpa` and `term_credits`, plus the `expected_student_version` guard of §2.6. On acceptance the term currently held in `latest_term_gpa` and `latest_term_credits` is displaced into `prior_term_gpa` and `prior_term_credits`; the posted values become the new `latest_term_gpa` and `latest_term_credits`; and `latest_term_label` becomes the posted `term_label`. A term displaced out of the prior slot is gone for good and can never re-enter any calculation. A posting always carries credits greater than zero, so the register never holds a zero-credit term.

Immediately after the shift, the student's standing is re-evaluated under the basis their district declares, resolved through the student's school and that school's district. Under `TERM_SNAPSHOT`, `effective_gpa` is simply the latest term's GPA rounded half up to three decimals, and the prior term is ignored entirely. Under `ROLLING_CUMULATIVE`, the two terms are credit-weighted: multiply the latest term's GPA by the latest term's credits, add the prior term's GPA multiplied by the prior term's credits — treating a null prior GPA and a null prior credit count as zero — then divide that sum by the total of the latest term's credits plus the prior term's credits (again treating a null prior credit count as zero), and round the quotient half up to three decimals. Whichever variant ran is recorded on the student as `standing_basis_applied`, taking the same two values as the district's basis.

The resulting `effective_gpa` then selects a standing: at or above the district's `min_gpa` the student is `GOOD`; otherwise, at or above the district's `probation_gpa_floor` the student is `PROBATION`; otherwise the student is `INELIGIBLE`. All three comparisons use the rounded three-decimal figure. The consequence of the two variants is that an identical transcript can produce different eligibility in different districts. Consider a student whose latest term is a 1.900 average over 6 credits and whose prior term was a 2.400 average over 6 credits. A `TERM_SNAPSHOT` district reads 1.900 and places the student on probation; a `ROLLING_CUMULATIVE` district credit-weights the two terms to 2.150 and places the same student in good standing. That single divergence decides whether the student may hold a starting slot at all.

A posting is not merely bookkeeping. When the recomputed standing lands on `INELIGIBLE`, every roster slot belonging to that student whose academics axis is still `PENDING` and whose team is still open for roster mutation — that is, whose team is in `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED` — moves its academics axis from `PENDING` to `BLOCKED`, takes `ineligibility_reason` `ACADEMIC`, has its participation reconciled to `INELIGIBLE`, and forces a recomputation of that team's `active_starter_count`. Each such slot advances its own `version`. The posting itself advances the student's `version` by one.

### 3.3. The Title Catalogue and the District Approval Pathway

The league office curates the catalogue of playable games. A title carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique across the whole catalogue — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TITLE_NAME` — and a required `publisher`. Its `age_rating` is one of `E`, `E10`, `T` or `M`, defaulting to `T`, on the same ordinal scale the districts use. Its `min_player_age` is the floor the publisher imposes on sanctioned play, defaulting to 13 and bounded between 6 and 21 inclusive. Three fields describe the title's match-server geometry: `server_pool_id` names the shared pool the title runs on and defaults to `POOL-DEFAULT`; `server_pool_capacity` is the number of matches that pool can host at any single instant, defaulting to 1 and bounded between 1 and 8 inclusive; and `match_duration_minutes` is the scheduled length of one match on that title, defaulting to 60 and bounded between 15 and 240 inclusive. That duration is what fixes the interval a scheduled match occupies on the pool.

A title being in the catalogue does not make it playable in a district. An athletic director — or the league office acting on a school's behalf — opens an approval request naming the `district_id` being asked, the `title_id` under review, and the `requested_by_school_id` making the request. The request begins with `status` `PENDING`, with `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` all null. At most one approval per district-and-title pair may sit in `PENDING` or `APPROVED` at a time; a second live request for the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TITLE_APPROVAL`. Note that once an approval reaches `DENIED` or `REVOKED` it no longer blocks a fresh request, because those two states are terminal and are not counted as live.

The district administrator whose `X-User-ID` equals the approval's `district_id` decides it, supplying an `action` and optionally a `decision_note` recording the rationale. An `action` of `APPROVE` moves a `PENDING` approval to `APPROVED`, but only if the title is permissible in that district: the title's `age_rating` must sit at or below the district's `max_age_rating` on the ordinal scale where `E` is 0, `E10` is 1, `T` is 2 and `M` is 3. A title rated above the district's ceiling — an `M`-rated title in a district capped at `T`, for example — is refused with `ERR_TITLE_AGE_RATING_EXCEEDS_DISTRICT_CEILING`. An `action` of `DENY` moves a `PENDING` approval to the terminal `DENIED` state and applies no rating test, since denial is always permissible. An `action` of `REVOKE` moves an `APPROVED` approval to the terminal `REVOKED` state; it likewise applies no rating test. All three actions write `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` — an opaque operator identifier, never an entity identifier — stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock, and advance the approval's `version` by one. Attempting to approve or deny something that is not `PENDING`, or to revoke something that is not `APPROVED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPROVAL_STATE`.

Revocation is deliberately **non-retroactive**. Revoking an approval does not unwind teams that have already registered on that title; it only removes the approval from the live set, so that a team still in `DRAFT` under a school of that district can no longer register. In other words, the registration guard reads only the *current* approval status and never a historical one.

### 3.4. Season Construction and Stage Advancement

A season is the container for one title's competition, and every window, fee, age rule and seeding parameter is fixed when the league office creates it. The season names its `title_id` and carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `division` of either `VARSITY` (the default) or `JV`. Four instants define its calendar. `registration_opens_at` and `registration_closes_at` bracket the team registration window, defaulting to `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` and `2026-09-15T23:59:59.999Z` and both inclusive. `roster_lock_at` is the inclusive deadline by which a coach must lock the roster, defaulting to `2026-09-20T00:00:00.000Z`. `regular_season_end_at` is the inclusive end of the period in which matches may be played, defaulting to `2026-11-30T00:00:00.000Z`.

Eligibility parameters follow. `age_cutoff_date`, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and defaulting to `2026-09-01`, is the date at which a student's age is measured. `max_age` is the league ceiling: a student whose age at the cutoff date is at or above this value is over-age. It defaults to 19 and is bounded between 14 and 25 inclusive. `min_starters` is the minimum number of active starting slots a team needs both to register and to stay unsuspended, defaulting to 1 and bounded between 1 and 5 inclusive; `max_starters` is the ceiling on starting slots a team may hold, defaulting to 3 and bounded between 1 and 8 inclusive. Two fee line-item rates are pinned here too: `base_entry_fee_cents`, the flat per-team charge, defaulting to 12,510 cents, and `per_starter_fee_cents`, the charge levied for each starting slot, defaulting to 3,335 cents. Both are non-negative integers. Seeding is configured by `seeding_basis`, either `MATCH_POINT_BASIS` (the default) or `WIN_PERCENTAGE_BASIS`, and by `playoff_slots`, the number of top seeds that survive a seeding run, defaulting to 2 and bounded between 1 and 16 inclusive.

Five fields on a season are owned by the server. `status` begins at `DRAFT` and walks through `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `REGULAR_SEASON`, `PLAYOFFS` and the terminal `COMPLETE`. `seeding_state` begins at `UNSEEDED` and thereafter alternates between `SEEDED` and `STALE` as results change. `seed_order` begins as an empty array and holds the ordered team identifiers a seeding run produced, its first element being the team whose `seed_position` is 1. `registered_team_count` begins at 0 and counts the season's teams whose status is anything other than `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN`. `match_point_pool` begins at 0 and is the running total of match points awarded in the season, anchoring the conservation invariant of §2.9. A sixth server-owned field, `seeding_basis_applied`, is null until the first seeding run and then records which variant that run actually used.

Stage advancement is a single league-office capability that takes a `to_status` naming the stage to move to, and it advances the season one step at a time. `DRAFT → REGISTRATION_OPEN` requires only that the season currently be in `DRAFT`. `REGISTRATION_OPEN → ROSTER_LOCKED` additionally requires a compound condition over the season's children: at least two of the season's teams must already have reached `ROSTER_LOCKED` themselves, and a season with fewer than two locked teams is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`. Teams still sitting in `REGISTERED` when this advance fires are deliberately left untouched — they missed the lock and will not compete. `ROSTER_LOCKED → REGULAR_SEASON` cascades downward: every team in the season whose status is `ROSTER_LOCKED` automatically transitions to `COMPETING`, each such team advancing its own `version`. `REGULAR_SEASON → PLAYOFFS` requires the season's `seeding_state` to be exactly `SEEDED`; an `UNSEEDED` or `STALE` order may not open a bracket, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEASON_SEEDING_NOT_CURRENT`. `PLAYOFFS → COMPLETE` is the second compound condition over children: every match in the season must have reached `FINALIZED` or `FORFEITED`, and a season with any match still outstanding is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`. Every one of the five advances increments the season's `version` by one, and any attempt to advance from a stage other than the declared from-stage is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`.

### 3.5. Team Formation, Roster Building and Two-Person Eligibility Verification

A team is created in draft by its own coach or by the school's athletic director, naming the `season_id` it will compete in, the `school_id` fielding it, the `title_id` it plays, the `coach_id` designated to run it, and a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique among the teams of that same season — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_NAME`. Two referential agreements are checked at creation: the designated coach must belong to the same school as the team, otherwise `ERR_COACH_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, and the team's `title_id` must be exactly the `title_id` of the season it is joining, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_TITLE_MISMATCH`. A new team's `status` is `DRAFT`; the full lifecycle enumeration is `DRAFT`, `REGISTERED`, `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `COMPETING`, `SUSPENDED`, `PLAYOFF_SEEDED`, `ELIMINATED`, and the terminal pair `WITHDRAWN` and `DISQUALIFIED`. Both `registered_at` and `roster_locked_at` are null at creation. The team's capacity counters are initialised so that the conservation invariant holds from the first instant: `starter_slots_used` is 0 and `starter_slots_available` equals the season's `max_starters`. `active_starter_count` starts at 0, `entry_fee_cents`, `subsidy_granted_cents` and `net_fee_due_cents` at 0, `integrity_score` at 100.00, and `suspension_reason` null — that last field taking one of `FAIR_PLAY_SANCTION`, `ROSTER_MINIMUM_BREACH` or `INTEGRITY_FLOOR_BREACH` while the team is suspended.

Rostering a student is the coach's act. The request names the `team_id`, the `student_id`, and optionally a `slot_role` of either `STARTER` (the default) or `SUB`. Five conditions guard it beyond ownership. The team must still be open for roster work, meaning its status is `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED`, otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED`. The student's `enrollment_status` must be `ENROLLED`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. The student's `fair_play_status` must be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_FAIR_PLAY_SANCTIONED`. The student must be enrolled at the same school as the team, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`. The student must not already hold a slot on this team that has not been released, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROSTER_SLOT`. And when the requested role is `STARTER`, the team must still have starter capacity — `starter_slots_used` strictly below the season's `max_starters`, equivalently at least one `starter_slots_available` — otherwise `ERR_STARTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A fresh slot begins with `verification_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `academic_status` `PENDING`, `participation_status` `PROSPECTIVE`, `released_at` and `ineligibility_reason` null, and both verifier identifiers null; the team's capacity counters are then recomputed.

The slot then advances along **two independent verification axes**, and the order in which they are completed does not matter — a slot may clear academics before enrollment or the other way round.

Axis one is enrollment and age, performed by the eligibility desk with a body carrying `enrollment_document_ref`, the registrar's document reference, and `verified_birthdate`, the date of birth as printed on that document. Nine conditions must hold. The slot must not be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_SLOT_RELEASED`. The team must be in `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED`, otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED`. The student's `enrollment_status` must be `ENROLLED`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. The supplied `verified_birthdate` must equal the student's `birthdate` exactly, otherwise `ERR_BIRTHDATE_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH`. The student's school must still have `enrollment_verified_through` on or after the logical clock's date, otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_CERTIFICATION_LAPSED`. The team's coach must be `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_COACH_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and that coach's `background_check_expires_on` must be on or after the logical clock's date, otherwise `ERR_COACH_BACKGROUND_CHECK_EXPIRED`. Finally the two age bounds are applied to `age_at_cutoff`, the number of completed years from the `verified_birthdate` to the season's `age_cutoff_date`: it must be at or above the title's `min_player_age`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_BELOW_TITLE_MIN_AGE`, and strictly below the season's `max_age`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_OVER_AGE_LIMIT`. The publisher floor is inclusive and the league ceiling is exclusive: a student whose age at the cutoff equals `max_age` is over-age. On success the slot's `verification_status` moves `UNVERIFIED → ENROLLMENT_VERIFIED`, `age_at_cutoff` is pinned to the computed figure, `enrollment_verified_by_user_id` is written from `X-User-ID`, participation is reconciled, the team's roll-ups are recomputed, and the slot's `version` increments by one.

Axis two is academics, performed by a **different** officer. The request carries no body of its own. The slot must not be `RELEASED` and the team must still be in `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the slot's `enrollment_verified_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — this is the two-person rule, and note from §2.7 that a locked roster is reported before the segregation failure when both apply. The student must have at least one posted term, meaning `latest_term_gpa` is not null, otherwise `ERR_ACADEMIC_STANDING_INELIGIBLE`. The standing evaluation of §3.2 then runs afresh against the district's basis, writing the student's `effective_gpa`, `academic_standing` and `standing_basis_applied`; if it yields `INELIGIBLE`, the verification is refused with `ERR_ACADEMIC_STANDING_INELIGIBLE`. If it yields `PROBATION` and the slot's `slot_role` is `STARTER`, the verification is refused with `ERR_PROBATION_STARTER_RESTRICTED` — a student on probation may occupy a `SUB` slot but never a starting one. A `PROBATION` result on a `SUB` slot, and any `GOOD` result, clear the axis. The student's `fair_play_status` must also still be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_FAIR_PLAY_SANCTIONED`. On success the slot's `academic_status` moves `PENDING → CLEARED`, the slot snapshots `standing_at_verification` (one of `GOOD`, `PROBATION` or `INELIGIBLE`) and `effective_gpa_at_verification` (three decimals) from the student's freshly computed figures, writes `academics_verified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, reconciles participation, recomputes the team's `active_starter_count`, and increments both the slot's `version` and the student's `version` by one.

The third axis, `participation_status`, is never written directly — it is **derived**, and reconciled after any write that touches the slot's verification axis, its academics axis, its `released_at`, its `ineligibility_reason`, or the student's `fair_play_status`. The derivation is a strict cascade of four cases in this order. If `released_at` is not null the slot is `RELEASED`. Otherwise, if `ineligibility_reason` is not null the slot is `INELIGIBLE`. Otherwise, if `verification_status` is `ENROLLMENT_VERIFIED` and `academic_status` is `CLEARED`, the slot is `ACTIVE`. In every other case the slot is `PROSPECTIVE`. The `ineligibility_reason` enumeration is `ACADEMIC`, `FAIR_PLAY` and `ENROLLMENT`: an academic posting that drops a still-pending slot to `BLOCKED` writes `ACADEMIC`, a sustained fair-play case against the student writes `FAIR_PLAY`, and `ENROLLMENT` is the reason reserved for a failure on the enrollment axis.

Immediately after each reconciliation the team's three roll-ups are recomputed, in this order. `starter_slots_used` is the count of the team's slots whose `slot_role` is `STARTER` and whose `participation_status` is `PROSPECTIVE`, `ACTIVE` or `INELIGIBLE` — released slots are excluded. `starter_slots_available` is the season's `max_starters` minus `starter_slots_used`. `active_starter_count` is the count of the team's slots whose `slot_role` is `STARTER` and whose `participation_status` is exactly `ACTIVE`.

A coach may release a slot at any time while the team is still in `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED`, optionally recording a `release_note`. Releasing is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED` once the team has locked, and with `ERR_ROSTER_SLOT_RELEASED` if the slot is already released — `RELEASED` is terminal and nothing further can be done to the slot. On success `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `participation_status` becomes `RELEASED`, the team's `starter_slots_used` is decremented and its `starter_slots_available` incremented so their sum still equals `max_starters`, `active_starter_count` is recomputed, and the slot's `version` increments by one.

### 3.6. Registration Economics, Roster Lock and Withdrawal

Registration is the moment a draft team becomes a real league entrant, and it is the coach's own act — `X-User-ID` must equal the team's `coach_id`. Eight conditions gate the `DRAFT → REGISTERED` transition. The team must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TEAM_STATE`. The season must be in `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`. The logical clock must fall at or after `registration_opens_at` and at or before `registration_closes_at`, both bounds inclusive, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The team's coach must be `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_COACH_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and that coach's `background_check_expires_on` must be on or after the clock's date, otherwise `ERR_COACH_BACKGROUND_CHECK_EXPIRED`. An approval must currently exist in `APPROVED` status linking the title the team plays to the district of the team's school — resolved through the team's `school_id` to that school's `district_id` — otherwise `ERR_TITLE_NOT_DISTRICT_APPROVED`; a `PENDING`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED` approval does not qualify. The team's `active_starter_count` must be at least the season's `min_starters`, otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. And the district's remaining `subsidy_pool_cents` must be at least as large as the subsidy this registration would grant, otherwise `ERR_SUBSIDY_POOL_EXHAUSTED` — the last rung on the whole ladder, so any state problem is always reported ahead of a budget problem.

The entry fee is assembled from **line items**, and the rounding order is pinned. The line items are one instance of the season's `base_entry_fee_cents`, plus one instance of the season's `per_starter_fee_cents` for each of the team's starting slots — meaning each slot whose `slot_role` is `STARTER` and whose `participation_status` is `PROSPECTIVE`, `ACTIVE` or `INELIGIBLE`. Substitute slots contribute nothing, and released slots contribute nothing. `entry_fee_cents` is the sum of those line items. The district's subsidy is then computed **per line item**: each line item is multiplied by the district's `subsidy_pct` and that product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and only then are the per-line results added together to give `subsidy_granted_cents`. Rounding the sum instead of the parts is wrong and can differ by one cent for every line item. Take the default figures with a single starting slot: the two line items are 12,510 and 3,335 cents, a quarter of each is 3,128 and 834 cents after rounding, and `subsidy_granted_cents` is therefore 3,962. Summing the two line items to 15,845 first and taking a quarter of that once would give 3,961 — one cent short, and wrong. `net_fee_due_cents` is `entry_fee_cents` less `subsidy_granted_cents`.

Once the fee is pinned, the district's `subsidy_pool_cents` is reduced by exactly the `subsidy_granted_cents` just awarded, keeping the subsidy conservation invariant in balance against the district's `subsidy_pool_initial_cents`. The team's `registered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its status becomes `REGISTERED`, the season's `registered_team_count` is recomputed, and the team's `version` increments by one.

Before the season's deadline the coach locks the roster, moving the team `REGISTERED → ROSTER_LOCKED`. The team must be in `REGISTERED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TEAM_STATE`; the acting coach must own it; the logical clock must be at or before the season's `roster_lock_at`, with locking at the exact deadline millisecond still succeeding and anything later refused with `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_DEADLINE_PASSED`; and the team's `active_starter_count` must again be at least the season's `min_starters`, otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. On success `roster_locked_at` is stamped from the clock and the team's `version` increments by one. From this point the roster is frozen: slot creation, either verification axis, and release are all refused with `ERR_ROSTER_LOCKED`.

Withdrawal is the athletic director's prerogative and belongs to the school — `X-User-ID` must equal the team's `school_id`. It may be exercised from `REGISTERED`, `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `COMPETING`, `SUSPENDED`, `PLAYOFF_SEEDED` or `ELIMINATED`, any other status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_TEAM_STATE`, and it accepts an optional `withdrawal_note`. Withdrawal is the compensating counterpart of registration and unwinds it in full. The district's `subsidy_pool_cents` is increased by the team's `subsidy_granted_cents`, then the team's own `subsidy_granted_cents` and `net_fee_due_cents` are both set to 0, which restores the district's pool to exactly the figure the conservation invariant demands. Every roster slot on the team whose `participation_status` is not already `RELEASED` transitions to `RELEASED`. Every match of the team whose `play_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `SERVER_READY` transitions to `FORFEITED` with `forfeiting_team_id` set to the withdrawing team, carrying the full forfeit award described in §3.7. The season's `registered_team_count` is recomputed, and if the season's `seeding_state` was `SEEDED` it becomes `STALE`. The team's status becomes the terminal `WITHDRAWN` and its `version` increments by one. Each cascaded slot and match advances its own `version` as well.

### 3.7. Match Scheduling, Result Reporting and Dispute Resolution

A match carries **four independent state dimensions**, and understanding that they advance separately is the key to the whole match workflow. `play_status` tracks the play lifecycle through `SCHEDULED`, `SERVER_READY`, `PLAYED` and the two terminal values `FINALIZED` and `FORFEITED`. `result_status` tracks result reporting through `AWAITING_REPORTS`, `PARTIALLY_REPORTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CONTESTED` and `ADJUDICATED`. `dispute_status` tracks contestation through `NONE`, `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `NEEDS_INFO`, `UPHELD` and `REJECTED`. `broadcast_status` tracks streaming through `NONE`, `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `ENDED`. A newly scheduled match sits at `SCHEDULED`, `AWAITING_REPORTS`, `NONE` and `NONE` respectively.

Scheduling is a league-office act naming the `season_id`, the `home_team_id`, the `away_team_id` and the `scheduled_start_at` instant. The two participants must differ, otherwise `ERR_SELF_MATCH_FORBIDDEN`, and both must belong to the same season as the match itself, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_SEASON_MISMATCH`. The server derives `scheduled_end_at` by adding the season's title's `match_duration_minutes` to `scheduled_start_at`, and copies that title's `server_pool_id` onto the match — the pool identifier is a match field precisely so the capacity test has a stable resource to key on. The match must fit inside the season's competitive calendar: `scheduled_start_at` must be at or after the season's `roster_lock_at`, and the derived end must be at or before the season's `regular_season_end_at`. A match falling outside that span is refused with `ERR_MATCH_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`. `region_code` and `patch_version` are null until the server is configured.

**Server pool occupancy** is then tested, and its interval semantics are half-open: a match occupies its pool from `scheduled_start_at` inclusive to `scheduled_end_at` exclusive. The peers considered are every other match sharing the same `server_pool_id` whose `play_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `SERVER_READY` or `PLAYED`; matches that have reached `FINALIZED` or `FORFEITED` release their capacity and are ignored. Concurrency is probed at the candidate's own start instant and at every peer start instant that falls at or after the candidate's start and strictly before the candidate's end. At each probe instant the occupancy is one — the candidate itself — plus the number of peers whose start is at or before that instant and whose end is strictly after it. If the occupancy at any probe instant exceeds the title's `server_pool_capacity`, the scheduling request is refused with `ERR_SERVER_POOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the interval is half-open, **adjacency is legal**: a peer that ends at exactly the candidate's start instant, or begins at exactly the candidate's end instant, does not conflict. With the default `server_pool_capacity` of 1, back-to-back matches on the same pool are therefore schedulable, while an overlap of even one millisecond is not.

Configuring the server is the league office's next step and moves `play_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `SERVER_READY`. It pins the `region_code` and the `patch_version` the match will be played on. The match must be in `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`, and the season must be in `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`.

Both coaches then report the score independently. A report names the `reporting_team_id`, the game counts `home_games_won` and `away_games_won`, and the `expected_match_version` guard of §2.6. The match must be in `SERVER_READY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`; the season must be in `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`; the `reporting_team_id` must be the home or the away side, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_MATCH_PARTICIPANT`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must be the coach of that reporting team, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A report from the home side is stored as `home_reported_home_games_won` and `home_reported_away_games_won`; a report from the away side as `away_reported_home_games_won` and `away_reported_away_games_won`. A repeat report from the same side supersedes that side's earlier pair rather than being rejected — which is exactly why the version guard exists.

The first of the two reports moves `result_status` from `AWAITING_REPORTS` to `PARTIALLY_REPORTED` and does nothing else beyond incrementing the match's `version`. The second report is decided by agreement. If the away side's reported home-game count equals the home side's reported home-game count **and** the away side's reported away-game count equals the home side's reported away-game count, the result is agreed: `result_status` moves `PARTIALLY_REPORTED → CONFIRMED`, the agreed pair is pinned as `home_games_won` and `away_games_won`, `winner_team_id` is set to whichever side won more games and left null on an equal count, `play_status` moves `SERVER_READY → PLAYED`, `result_confirmed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the dispute deadline is computed. If the two pairs differ in either component, the result is contested: `result_status` moves `PARTIALLY_REPORTED → CONTESTED`, `play_status` still moves `SERVER_READY → PLAYED`, no points are awarded, and `home_games_won` and `away_games_won` stay null until an adjudication pins them.

The **dispute deadline** is derived from the home side's district only; the away side's policy never affects it. Walk from the match's `home_team_id` to that team's `school_id`, then to that school's `district_id`, and add that district's `dispute_window_hours` to the match's `result_confirmed_at`. The sum is stored as `dispute_deadline_at`, at millisecond precision with three fractional digits. This computation runs whenever `result_confirmed_at` is set — on confirmation, on an adjudication produced by a finalization override, and on a forfeit.

**Awarding match points** happens whenever a result is decided, and its order is pinned: any re-award first reverts the figures this match previously credited — its own `home_points_awarded` and `away_points_awarded`, and the per-team counters this match contributed — and only then applies the new ones. The award itself is a three-way branch on the pinned game counts. When the home side won more games, the home team is credited 3 points and the away team 0, the home team's `wins` and the away team's `losses` each rise by one. When the away side won more games, the away team is credited 3 points and the home team 0, the away team's `wins` and the home team's `losses` each rise by one. When the counts are equal, each side is credited 1 point and each side's `draws` rises by one. In all three of these non-forfeit cases the game tallies accumulate as well: the home team's `games_won` grows by the match's `home_games_won` and its `games_lost` by the match's `away_games_won`, while the away team's `games_won` grows by `away_games_won` and its `games_lost` by `home_games_won`. Each team's `match_points` grows by the points credited to it, and the season's `match_point_pool` grows by the two credited amounts together. A team's `game_differential` is always its `games_won` less its `games_lost`.

A forfeit is awarded differently. The non-forfeiting side is credited 3 points and the forfeiting side 0; the winning side's `forfeit_wins` rises by one and the forfeiting side's `forfeits_charged` rises by one; and `wins`, `losses`, `draws`, `games_won` and `games_lost` are all left completely untouched. The season's `match_point_pool` therefore grows by exactly 3 for each forfeit. This asymmetry is deliberate and it is what makes the two seeding variants of §3.9 diverge.

Forfeits arise two ways. The league office may charge one directly against a named `forfeiting_team_id`, optionally recording a `reason_note`; the match must be in `SCHEDULED` or `SERVER_READY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`, and the named team must be a participant, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_MATCH_PARTICIPANT`. Alternatively the server produces one on its own initiative whenever a participating team enters `SUSPENDED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `DISQUALIFIED`. Either way, `play_status` becomes `FORFEITED`, `result_status` becomes `ADJUDICATED`, `winner_team_id` becomes the other team, `forfeiting_team_id` is stored, `result_confirmed_at` is stamped, `dispute_deadline_at` is computed, the forfeit award is applied, and the season's `seeding_state` becomes `STALE` if it was `SEEDED`.

Finalization is the league office's closing act on a played match, and its shape depends on where the result stands. From a `CONTESTED` result the caller must supply both `override_home_games_won` and `override_away_games_won`; missing either is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The overrides are pinned as the match's `home_games_won` and `away_games_won`, `winner_team_id` is recomputed from them, `result_status` moves `CONTESTED → ADJUDICATED`, `result_confirmed_at` is restamped and `dispute_deadline_at` recomputed, the point award runs, and `play_status` moves `PLAYED → FINALIZED`. From a result already sitting at `CONFIRMED` or `ADJUDICATED` the override fields must both be absent — supplying either is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the operation simply moves `play_status` from `PLAYED` to `FINALIZED` with no change to the result or the points. A finalization attempt on a match whose `play_status` is not `PLAYED`, or whose `result_status` is none of `CONFIRMED`, `ADJUDICATED` or `CONTESTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`. Either way the match's `version` increments by one.

Filing a dispute belongs to a participating coach. The request names the `filing_team_id`, a `dispute_reason_code` drawn from `SCORE_MISMATCH`, `SERVER_FAULT`, `INELIGIBLE_PLAYER` and `CONDUCT`, and optionally a `dispute_note`. The match's `result_status` must be `CONFIRMED` or `ADJUDICATED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`; the match's `dispute_status` must be `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`, since a match admits only one dispute; the filing team must be a participant, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_MATCH_PARTICIPANT`; and the logical clock must be at or before `dispute_deadline_at`, with filing at the exact deadline millisecond still succeeding and anything later refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Recall from §2.7 that a non-participant filing late hears about the participation problem, not the window. On success `dispute_status` moves `NONE → OPEN` and `dispute_filed_by_team_id` and `dispute_reason_code` are stored.

The league office then works the dispute through a single action-driven capability whose `action` is one of `ACCEPT_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_INFO`, `RESUME_REVIEW`, `UPHOLD` or `REJECT`. `ACCEPT_REVIEW` moves `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW`. `REQUEST_INFO` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → NEEDS_INFO` and may carry an `info_request_note`. `RESUME_REVIEW` moves `NEEDS_INFO → UNDER_REVIEW`. That pair forms a **cycle** which may repeat any number of times before a decision is reached. Any action attempted from a state other than its declared from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`. Every action increments the match's `version` by one.

`REJECT` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and must carry neither corrected count, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Nothing but `dispute_status` changes: the result stands, the points stand, and no team, season or student figure moves.

`UPHOLD` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → UPHELD` and must carry both `corrected_home_games_won` and `corrected_away_games_won`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It then drives a four-link restatement cascade. First, the match's `result_status` becomes `ADJUDICATED`, `home_games_won` and `away_games_won` are replaced by the corrected pair, and `winner_team_id` is recomputed. Second, caused by the first, the point award is re-run in revert-then-apply order on both teams, so `match_points`, `wins`, `losses`, `draws`, `games_won`, `games_lost`, `forfeit_wins` and `forfeits_charged` are all restated from the corrected result, and the season's `match_point_pool` is adjusted by the resulting delta so that the conservation formula of §2.9 still holds exactly. Third, caused by the second, the season's `seeding_state` moves `SEEDED → STALE` and every team in that season that already holds a non-null `seed_position` gets `seed_stale` set to true. Fourth, caused by the third, every student whose `showcase_status` is `NOMINATED` and whose `nominating_team_id` belongs to this season moves `scholarship_award_state` from `CURRENT` to `RECALC_REQUIRED`, because the seed that fed their projection is no longer trustworthy. Every record touched by any link advances its own `updated_at` and `version`.

### 3.8. Broadcasting, Spectator Conduct and Fair-Play Enforcement

Streaming is claimed against a **school's channel**, which is a different kind of resource from the title's server pool — a per-entity channel rather than an aggregate pool — and it deliberately uses the opposite interval semantics. A coach of either participating team claims the channel with an `action` of `REGISTER` accompanied by `broadcast_school_id`, `broadcast_start_at` and `broadcast_end_at`. The match's `broadcast_status` must be `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BROADCAST_STATE`; the acting `X-User-ID` must be the coach of the home or the away team; and the supplied `broadcast_school_id` must be the school of that coach's own participating team, otherwise `ERR_BROADCAST_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`.

The channel conflict test treats the requested window as a **closed** interval and expands it by the governing district's `broadcast_buffer_minutes` on **both** ends — that district being reached through the named school's `district_id`. The peers considered are matches already holding the same school's channel whose `broadcast_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; a broadcast that has `ENDED`, or one that was never registered, frees the channel. A peer conflicts when its `broadcast_start_at` is at or before the expanded candidate's end and its `broadcast_end_at` is at or after the expanded candidate's start. If the number of conflicting peers reaches the school's `concurrent_broadcast_capacity`, the claim is refused with `ERR_BROADCAST_CHANNEL_CONFLICT`. The buffer expands only the candidate; peer windows are compared exactly as stored. The practical consequence is that **adjacency conflicts here**, which is the reverse of the server pool rule: two windows that merely touch collide, any gap of at most `broadcast_buffer_minutes` collides, and only a gap strictly greater than `broadcast_buffer_minutes` clears. On success the three broadcast fields are pinned and `broadcast_status` moves `NONE → SCHEDULED`.

The remaining broadcast actions are simpler. `GO_LIVE` moves `SCHEDULED → LIVE` and additionally requires the match's `play_status` to be `SERVER_READY` or `PLAYED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCH_STATE`. `RESUME` moves `SUSPENDED → LIVE` and requires the match's `spectator_strikes` to be fewer than 3; at three strikes the stream is finished for good and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BROADCAST_STATE`. `END` moves either `LIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to the terminal `ENDED`. `LIVE` and `SUSPENDED` thus form a moderation cycle capped at three strikes. Each of these three actions must omit `broadcast_school_id`, `broadcast_start_at` and `broadcast_end_at`, and each is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BROADCAST_STATE` from any other from-state. Every accepted broadcast action increments the match's `version` by one.

Fair-play cases are the league's conduct docket, and they are **polymorphic**: a case names either a student or a match broadcast as its subject. A case is opened by an integrity officer, the league office, or a coach, and it always charges a `team_id` — the team whose `integrity_score` will absorb the penalty. `subject_type` is either `STUDENT` or `MATCH_BROADCAST` and is required. `case_type` is required and is one of `ACCOUNT_SHARING`, `SMURFING`, `CHEAT_DETECTION` or `SPECTATOR_CONDUCT`. `evidence_ref` is a required evidence bundle reference of 1 to 200 characters. A `STUDENT` case must supply `subject_student_id` and must omit `subject_match_id`; a `MATCH_BROADCAST` case must supply `subject_match_id` and must omit `subject_student_id`. Getting that pairing wrong is `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The subject must also actually belong to the charged team: a named student must hold a non-released roster slot on that team, and a named match must have that team as its home or away side, otherwise `ERR_SUBJECT_TEAM_MISMATCH`. A new case opens in `REPORTED` with `reported_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, `severity` null, `sanction_type` `NONE`, `severity_weight` 0, `applied_penalty` 0, `repeat_index` null, and `appeal_grounds`, `decided_at` and all three later decision identifiers null. The full lifecycle enumeration is `REPORTED`, `INVESTIGATING`, `SUSTAINED`, `APPEALED`, and the three terminal values `DISMISSED`, `SUSTAINED_FINAL` and `OVERTURNED`.

An integrity officer takes the case, optionally recording an `investigation_note`, which moves it `REPORTED → INVESTIGATING` and writes `investigator_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. Any other from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

The officer then decides it, supplying a `decision` of either `SUSTAIN` or `DISMISS` plus an optional `decision_note`. The case must be in `INVESTIGATING`. `SUSTAIN` requires a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `SEVERE`; `DISMISS` requires `severity` to be absent. Violating either is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two branches have deliberately disjoint consequences.

`DISMISS` moves the case `INVESTIGATING → DISMISSED`, writes `decider_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock, leaves `sanction_type` at `NONE`, and changes **nothing else at all** — no student, roster, team, match or season figure moves, and the dismissed case never enters the penalty set.

`SUSTAIN` moves the case `INVESTIGATING → SUSTAINED` and drives a five-link cascade. The first link records the decision — `decider_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the clock — and recomputes the charged team's integrity standing, which also fixes this case's `severity_weight`, `repeat_index`, `applied_penalty` and `sanction_type`. The second link, caused by the first and only when `subject_type` is `STUDENT`, moves that student's `fair_play_status` from `CLEAR` to `SANCTIONED`. The third link, caused by the second, moves every roster slot of that student whose `participation_status` is `ACTIVE` to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` `FAIR_PLAY`, and recomputes each affected team's `active_starter_count`. The fourth link, caused by the third, moves the charged team `COMPETING → SUSPENDED` when any of three things is true: the team's `active_starter_count` has fallen below the season's `min_starters`, the selected `sanction_type` is `TEAM_SUSPENSION` or `DISQUALIFICATION`, or the recomputed `integrity_score` is below 40. The team's `suspension_reason` records which of those it was — `ROSTER_MINIMUM_BREACH`, `FAIR_PLAY_SANCTION` or `INTEGRITY_FLOOR_BREACH` respectively. The fifth link, caused by the fourth, forfeits every match of that suspended team whose `play_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `SERVER_READY`, crediting the opponent 3 points, growing the season's `match_point_pool` by 3 per forfeited match, and setting the season's `seeding_state` to `STALE`. When the case's `subject_type` is `MATCH_BROADCAST` instead, links two through five are replaced by a single effect: the named match's `broadcast_status` moves `LIVE → SUSPENDED` and its `spectator_strikes` rises by one.

**Integrity scoring** works on the set of cases charged to a team whose status is `SUSTAINED` or `SUSTAINED_FINAL`, taken in ascending `created_at` order — the ordering is pinned to `created_at` and never to `id`. Cases in `DISMISSED` or `OVERTURNED` are outside the set entirely. Each case in the set is numbered from 1 in that order, and that number is stored as its `repeat_index`. Each case's `severity_weight` is the sum of two constants: a severity component worth 5 for `MINOR`, 15 for `MAJOR` and 40 for `SEVERE`; and a case-type surcharge worth 5 for `ACCOUNT_SHARING`, 10 for `SMURFING`, 20 for `CHEAT_DETECTION` and 0 for `SPECTATOR_CONDUCT`. The repeat multiplier is 1.0 for the first case in the set, 1.5 for the second, and 2.0 for the third and every case after it. Each case's `applied_penalty` is its `severity_weight` multiplied by its own multiplier, rounded half up to two decimals — the rounding happens per case, never once on the total. The team's total penalty is the sum of those per-case figures.

A clean-conduct credit offsets the penalty. When the set is empty the credit's elapsed-days input is zero. Otherwise take the latest `decided_at` across the set, measure the whole days elapsed from that instant to the logical clock's present value by discarding any partial day, award 5 points for each complete block of 30 such days, and cap the award at 10 points — so a team earns nothing for 29 clean days, 5 points at 30 days, and its maximum 10 points at 60 days and beyond. The team's `integrity_score` is 100.00 minus the total penalty plus that credit, rounded half up to two decimals, then clamped into the closed range 0.00 through 100.00.

Only after `integrity_score` is known is the `sanction_type` for the case being decided chosen, from the enumeration `NONE`, `WARNING`, `STUDENT_SUSPENSION`, `TEAM_SUSPENSION` and `DISQUALIFICATION`. A `SEVERE` severity selects `TEAM_SUSPENSION`. Otherwise a `MATCH_BROADCAST` subject selects `WARNING`. Otherwise the selection is `STUDENT_SUSPENSION`. Two overrides then apply in order: if the recomputed `integrity_score` is below 40 the sanction becomes `TEAM_SUSPENSION` regardless of what was selected, and if the `integrity_score` is exactly 0.00 the sanction becomes `DISQUALIFICATION`.

The charged team's coach may appeal a sustained case, supplying written `appeal_grounds`. The case must be in `SUSTAINED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be the `coach_id` of the case's charged team, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The case moves `SUSTAINED → APPEALED` and **all sanctions remain in force** while the appeal is pending — the case stays in the penalty set and nothing is restored yet.

An independent integrity officer decides the appeal with a `decision` of `AFFIRM` or `OVERTURN` plus an optional `appeal_note`. The case must be in `APPEALED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be neither the case's `investigator_user_id` nor its `decider_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either decision writes `appeal_decider_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and increments the case's `version` by one.

`AFFIRM` moves the case `APPEALED → SUSTAINED_FINAL`. Because the case remains in the sustained set, the team's `integrity_score` does not change and no penalty is re-indexed. If the case's `sanction_type` is `DISQUALIFICATION`, the charged team moves to the terminal `DISQUALIFIED` state, which releases every roster slot on that team not already `RELEASED` and sets the season's `seeding_state` to `STALE` if it was `SEEDED`.

`OVERTURN` moves the case `APPEALED → OVERTURNED` and runs a five-link compensation that is the exact inverse of the sustain cascade. The first link removes the case from the sustained set, which recomputes the team's `integrity_score` and shifts the `repeat_index` of every later sustained case on that team down by one — changing each of their `applied_penalty` figures accordingly — and sets the overturned case's own `sanction_type` back to `NONE`. The second link, caused by the first and only for a `STUDENT` subject, returns that student's `fair_play_status` to `CLEAR`, but only if no other case naming that student remains in `SUSTAINED` or `SUSTAINED_FINAL`. The third link, caused by the second, returns every roster slot of that student whose `participation_status` is `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` `FAIR_PLAY` back to `ACTIVE`, clearing `ineligibility_reason` — provided the slot's own two axes still stand at `ENROLLMENT_VERIFIED` and `CLEARED` — and recomputes each affected team's `active_starter_count`. The fourth link, caused by the third, returns the team `SUSPENDED → COMPETING` once its `active_starter_count` is again at least the season's `min_starters` and no other case against it remains sustained with a `TEAM_SUSPENSION` or `DISQUALIFICATION` sanction; the team's `suspension_reason` is cleared back to null. The fifth link, caused by the fourth, restores every match that was forfeited **solely** because of that suspension: the 3 points credited to the opponent are removed, the winner's `forfeit_wins` and the forfeiting team's `forfeits_charged` are each decremented, the season's `match_point_pool` is reduced by 3 per restored match so it returns to exactly its pre-suspension value, `forfeiting_team_id` is cleared to null, `result_status` returns to `AWAITING_REPORTS`, and both `result_confirmed_at` and `dispute_deadline_at` return to null. Such a match's `play_status` is restored to `SERVER_READY` when its `region_code` had already been pinned and to `SCHEDULED` when it had not. A match forfeited by a direct league-office charge rather than by the suspension is never restored this way. When the overturned case's `subject_type` is `MATCH_BROADCAST`, links two through five are replaced by a single effect: the named match's `spectator_strikes` is reduced by one and its `broadcast_status` returns `SUSPENDED → LIVE`.

### 3.9. Playoff Seeding, Tiebreakers and the Showcase Pathway

Seeding is a league-office run over one season, and it may optionally carry a `basis_override` naming the variant to apply for that run only. When `basis_override` is supplied it takes effect; otherwise the season's own `seeding_basis` applies. Whichever value governed is echoed onto the season as `seeding_basis_applied`, so the same season can be seeded under either variant without editing its stored configuration. The season must be in `REGULAR_SEASON` or `PLAYOFFS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_STAGE`.

The eligible set is the season's teams whose status is `COMPETING`, `PLAYOFF_SEEDED`, `ELIMINATED` or `SUSPENDED`. Teams in `DRAFT`, `REGISTERED`, `ROSTER_LOCKED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `DISQUALIFIED` are excluded from the seed order entirely and keep a null `seed_position`. If the eligible set is empty the run is refused with `ERR_SEED_ORDER_EMPTY`.

A run proceeds in three stages. First, every eligible team's `integrity_score` is recomputed exactly as §3.8 describes, so that the clean-conduct credit reflects the current clock. Second, every eligible team's `seed_points` is computed under the applied variant and stored to four decimals, rounded half up. Under `MATCH_POINT_BASIS` a team's `seed_points` is simply its accumulated `match_points`. Under `WIN_PERCENTAGE_BASIS` the denominator is the team's `wins` plus `losses` plus `draws` — a total that deliberately excludes forfeits, since forfeits touch neither counter — and `seed_points` is the team's `wins` divided by that denominator; when the denominator is zero the team scores 0.0000 and is ordered purely by the tiebreakers. The two variants can invert the standings outright. A team holding two forfeit wins and one genuine loss carries 6 match points and scores 6.0000 under `MATCH_POINT_BASIS`, comfortably ahead of a rival with a single genuine win at 3.0000; under `WIN_PERCENTAGE_BASIS` the same forfeit-fed team scores zero wins out of one counted match, or 0.0000, while the rival scores one out of one, or 1.0000.

Third, the eligible set is sorted. Six keys are applied in strict order, and a later key is consulted only when every earlier key ties. `seed_points` descending comes first. Then `head_to_head_points` descending, which is the points a team earned in `FINALIZED` or `FORFEITED` matches whose *both* participants sit inside the currently tied group — this quantity is recomputed per tied group rather than league-wide. Then `game_differential` descending, that being a team's `games_won` less its `games_lost`. Then `forfeits_charged` ascending, so the team with fewer forfeits ranks higher. Then `integrity_score` descending, compared at two decimals. Finally `created_at` ascending as the last resort; the record `id` is never consulted as a tiebreaker.

The sorted position, counted from 1, becomes each team's `seed_position`, and the ordered list of team identifiers becomes the season's `seed_order`, whose first element is the team at position 1. The season's `seeding_state` becomes `SEEDED`. Every seeded team has its `seed_stale` flag cleared to false and its `version` incremented, and the season's `version` increments by one as well. Teams at a `seed_position` at or below the season's `playoff_slots` take status `PLAYOFF_SEEDED`; the rest take status `ELIMINATED`. Both of those transitions are legal from `COMPETING`, from `PLAYOFF_SEEDED` and from `ELIMINATED`, so a re-run may promote a previously eliminated team or demote a previously seeded one. A team in `SUSPENDED` keeps that status through a run but still receives a `seed_position`.

Seeding freshness is what keeps the bracket honest. Any event that changes a result — a confirmed report, a forfeit, a withdrawal, a disqualification, an upheld dispute — sets the season's `seeding_state` to `STALE` if it was `SEEDED` and raises `seed_stale` on the affected teams. Because the advance into `PLAYOFFS` demands `seeding_state` be exactly `SEEDED`, a stale order forces a fresh run before the bracket can open.

The **showcase pathway** is how a strong season converts into a scholarship projection. The league office nominates a student, naming the `nominating_team_id` whose seed feeds the projection. Three conditions gate it: the student must hold a roster slot on that team that has not been released, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ON_TEAM`; the student's `showcase_status` must currently be `NONE`, since a student may be nominated only once and a repeat is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_NOMINATED`; and the student's `fair_play_status` must be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_FAIR_PLAY_SANCTIONED`. On acceptance `showcase_status` becomes `NOMINATED` and `nominating_team_id` is stored on the student.

The award is then projected in **four pinned steps**, and the tier is never re-derived from the final figure. Step one produces the provisional total. Take a base award of 150,000 cents and multiply it by the academic multiplier for the student's current `academic_standing` — 1.0 for `GOOD`, 0.5 for `PROBATION`, and 0.0 for both `UNEVALUATED` and `INELIGIBLE` — rounding that product half up to whole cents. Then add a seed bonus of 45,000 cents for every rank by which the nominating team's `seed_position` betters fifth place, where a null `seed_position` is treated as fifth place exactly: subtract the seed rank so used from 5, treat any negative result as zero, and multiply by 45,000 cents. The sum is stored as `provisional_award_cents`. Step two chooses `award_tier` from that provisional total alone: at or above 350,000 cents it is `GOLD`, otherwise at or above 200,000 cents it is `SILVER`, otherwise it is `BRONZE`; the enumeration also admits `NONE` for a student who has never been nominated. Step three computes the performance bonus by multiplying the provisional total by the rate for the chosen tier — 5 percent for `BRONZE`, 12 percent for `SILVER`, 20 percent for `GOLD` — and rounding half up to whole cents. Step four sets `scholarship_award_cents` to the provisional total plus that bonus, and sets `scholarship_award_state` to `CURRENT`.

The pinning matters. A student in `GOOD` standing on the top-seeded team reaches a provisional total of 330,000 cents, which lands in the silver band, earning a 39,600-cent bonus and a final `scholarship_award_cents` of 369,600. The stored `award_tier` remains `SILVER` even though the final figure sits inside the gold band, and the bonus is never recomputed at the gold rate. `scholarship_award_state` takes `NONE` before any nomination, `CURRENT` while the nominating team's seeding is fresh, and `RECALC_REQUIRED` once that seeding goes stale — the fourth link of the upheld-dispute cascade in §3.7 being the path that sets it.

Recruiters may then log contacts, and only against students on the pathway. A contact names an `org_code` identifying the recruiting organization, a `channel`, and a `purpose`. The student's `showcase_status` must be `NOMINATED`, otherwise `ERR_SHOWCASE_NOT_NOMINATED`. Two district rules follow, resolved through the student's school to that school's district, and they are evaluated in this order. First, if the district has both a `recruiting_dead_period_start` and a `recruiting_dead_period_end` configured and the logical clock falls inside that interval — with both ends inclusive — the contact is refused with `ERR_RECRUITING_DEAD_PERIOD`. Second, the rolling cap is applied: count the entries already in the student's `recruiting_contact_log` whose `contacted_at` is **strictly after** the instant `recruiting_window_days` before the clock's present value, and if that count has already reached the district's `recruiting_contact_cap_per_window`, refuse with `ERR_RECRUITING_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The window is half-open at its older end: a logged contact whose `contacted_at` falls exactly on the window's opening instant has aged out and is not counted. An accepted contact appends one entry to `recruiting_contact_log` holding `contacted_at` from the logical clock together with the supplied `org_code`, `channel` and `purpose`, and increments the student's `version` by one. Refused attempts are never logged, so a rejected contact leaves the quota exactly where it was.
