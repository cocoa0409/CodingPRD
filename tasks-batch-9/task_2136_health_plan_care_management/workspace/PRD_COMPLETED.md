# Health Plan Care Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Health plans lose money and members in the same place: the weeks after a hospital discharge, the months when a chronic condition quietly worsens, and the moment a member stops answering the phone. This document specifies the backend service that runs a health plan's care management program end to end — identifying which members need a care manager, building and clinically reviewing their care plans, scheduling and documenting every outreach contact, referring unmet social needs to community organizations and chasing those referrals to a closed loop, and finally proving to an accrediting body that the whole apparatus actually worked.

The service is deliberately opinionated about two things that most care management platforms leave to spreadsheets. The first is **stratification**: the same member is scored twice, under a retrospective algorithm that only trusts adjudicated claims and under a forward-looking algorithm that also counts pending claims and booked admissions, and a program declares up front which of the two it believes. The second is **caseload arithmetic**: every enrollment consumes a measurable number of caseload points from the care manager who carries it, those points move whenever risk or outreach intensity moves, and no care manager may be loaded past their declared ceiling by an assignment or a transfer. Together these two mechanisms turn "who should we call, how often, and who has time to call them" from a judgement call into a computation.

All routes described here are mounted under `/care-management`. Every temporal rule — cadence deadlines, transitions-of-care windows, referral loop ages, ROI measurement months — reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the entire system is deterministically testable. The remainder of this document is organised into global contracts that hold everywhere (§2), the business narrative that defines how the domain behaves (§3), the endpoint surface (§4), and the error catalogue (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, empty, or if `X-User-Role` holds a value that is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the caller is identified, the service checks whether that role appears in the list of roles authorised for the requested endpoint; if it does not, the answer is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authentication always precedes authorisation, and both precede any inspection of the request body.

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `CareManager`, `ClinicalReviewer`, `UtilizationAnalyst`, and `CommunityPartner`.

A `ProgramAdmin` runs plan operations: they register members, configure programs, create care managers, register community organizations, reassign enrollments between care managers, and drive the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check applies to a `ProgramAdmin`.

A `CareManager` is the front-line clinician who carries a caseload. When `X-User-Role` is `CareManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CareManager` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a care manager compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a care manager may complete or cancel only a touch whose `care_manager_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, may schedule a touch only against their own `care_manager_id`, and may submit only a care plan whose `authored_by_care_manager_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

A `ClinicalReviewer` approves or returns care plans, and is backed by the very same care-manager roster: when `X-User-Role` is `ClinicalReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CareManager` entity. That shared backing is precisely what makes the segregation-of-duties rule meaningful — a reviewer whose `X-User-ID` equals the plan's `authored_by_care_manager_id` is refusing to review their own work, and the service rejects the attempt with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

A `UtilizationAnalyst` owns the analytics and accreditation function: running stratifications, recording utilization events, producing outcome measurements, and pulling the accreditation file. When `X-User-Role` is `UtilizationAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque analyst identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

A `CommunityPartner` is a community-based organization acting on referrals sent to it. When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CommunityResource`; ownership checks compare a referral's `community_resource_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a partner may accept, decline, or close the loop only on referrals actually addressed to it, and any other attempt yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is envelope metadata rather than a domain instant, and is therefore taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5; every instant stored on or returned inside a resource is taken from the logical clock instead.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; a supplied `limit` must be an integer no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0; a supplied `offset` must be an integer no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint's narrative says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still until it is moved, many records share a `created_at` exactly; ties are broken by order of creation, so records stamped at the same instant come back in the order they were created, and a descending listing reverses that same total order rather than leaving the tie unsettled. Two listings deviate from that default and say so at their point of use: the stratification history for a member is ordered newest first, and the outcome measurements of an enrollment are likewise ordered newest first.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versions

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix of any kind — and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional-second digits. Clients never supply `id` values for the resources they create; a client-supplied `id` is ignored.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the logical-clock instant of every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that record. `Member`, `Program`, `CareManager`, `Enrollment`, `CarePlan`, `CarePlanGoal`, `Intervention`, `Touch`, `SocialNeed`, `CommunityResource` and `Referral` all behave this way.

Three record types are append-only ledgers rather than mutable resources, and they therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. Those three are the risk stratification record, the utilization transition event record, and the outcome measurement record. A stratification is never edited after it is computed; a transition event is a statement about something that already happened; and an outcome measurement is superseded by a newer measurement rather than rewritten. (The `superseded` flag on a measurement is the one exception to immutability, and it is set by the arrival of a newer measurement of the same method rather than by any client edit.)

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service maintains a logical clock, and every deadline, window, age, and cadence rule in this specification reads that clock rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ProgramAdmin` may advance or rewind the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the instant to move to; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes nothing retroactively — records already stamped keep their stamps, already-computed scores keep their values, and only future evaluations see the new instant.

### 2.6. Order of Validation

When a request could fail several checks at once, the service reports the failure that comes first in the following fixed ladder. The ordering is normative: a request that violates two rules always surfaces the earlier one.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation** — the caller's role is listed on the endpoint, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Request shape** — on every write endpoint, each required input field is present, each enum-valued field holds a declared member of its enumeration, each integer or number field is of the correct type and satisfies its declared range constraint, and any instant-valued field such as `scheduled_end_at` or `service_end_at` parses as an ISO 8601 instant. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
4. **Pagination shape** — on every list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` are checked as described in §2.3, yielding `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
5. **Path resolution** — every identifier embedded in the request path is resolved from left to right, and the first segment that resolves to nothing decides the error. The service reports the not-found code named for the failing segment: `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARE_MANAGER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GOAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOCIAL_NEED_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMUNITY_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSITION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
6. **Body reference resolution** — identifiers supplied inside the request body are resolved next, in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list, using the same catalogue of not-found codes. Enrollment creation resolves `member_id`, then `program_id`, then `care_manager_id`. Care plan creation resolves `enrollment_id`. Touch creation resolves `enrollment_id`, then `care_manager_id`. Social need creation resolves `enrollment_id`. Referral creation resolves `social_need_id`, then `community_resource_id`. Transition event creation resolves `member_id`, then `index_admission_event_id`. Enrollment transfer resolves `to_care_manager_id`.
7. **Resource ownership** — for the identity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` must equal the resource's owning foreign key as described in §2.1, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This applies to touch creation, touch completion, touch cancellation, care plan submission, referral decisions and referral loop closure.
8. **Segregation of duties** — a review decision by a `ClinicalReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the plan's `authored_by_care_manager_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Source state** — the target resource must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. The reported code names the resource and the state it should have been in: `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_GOAL_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_PLANNED`, `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SENT`, `ERR_REFERRAL_LOOP_NOT_CLOSABLE` or `ERR_SOCIAL_NEED_NOT_REFERABLE`, each HTTP 409.
10. **Record version agreement** — the concurrency check of §2.8, yielding `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
11. **Cross-entity state gates** — a touch may be scheduled only on an enrollment that has a care plan in `ACTIVE` status; a care plan may be submitted, and may be approved on review, only while its enrollment's `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`, a `REQUEST_REVISION` decision carrying no such gate; and an intervention may be created or completed only while its owning plan is `ACTIVE`. A failure of any of these five gates is `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) where the missing element is the active plan, and `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` where the missing element is the active enrollment.
12. **Safety and consent rules** — consent (`ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED`), documentation (`ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`), credential authorisation (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED`) and state licensure (`ERR_STATE_LICENSURE_MISMATCH`), evaluated in that relative order.
13. **Temporal rules** — window ordering (`ERR_TOUCH_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_INVALID`), then window recency (`ERR_TOUCH_WINDOW_IN_PAST`, `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_IN_PAST`), then touch readiness (`ERR_TOUCH_NOT_YET_DUE`), then measurement-window emptiness (`ERR_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD_EMPTY`).
14. **Referential business rules** — contracting status (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_CONTRACTED`), then domain and geography match (`ERR_RESOURCE_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`), then duplicate enrollment (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT`), then same-manager transfer (`ERR_TRANSFER_TO_SAME_MANAGER`), then the graduation precondition (`ERR_GOALS_NOT_MET`), then stratification presence (`ERR_STRATIFICATION_REQUIRED`), then program tier minimum (`ERR_RISK_TIER_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`).
15. **Interval and uniqueness constraints** — care-manager calendar conflict (`ERR_CARE_MANAGER_SLOT_CONFLICT`), then duplicate open referral (`ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_REFERRAL`), then community resource concurrency (`ERR_RESOURCE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).
16. **Fiscal and capacity ceilings, evaluated last** — caseload ceiling (`ERR_CASELOAD_WOULD_EXCEED_CAPACITY`), then care-manager capacity posture on a new touch (`ERR_CARE_MANAGER_OVER_CAPACITY`), then the program staff-minute budget (`ERR_PROGRAM_STAFF_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`), and finally the empty auto-assignment pool (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CARE_MANAGER`).

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Units

Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Money is carried everywhere in whole cents as an integer; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no currency conversion. Caseload point values — a care manager's `capacity_points`, an enrollment's `acuity_weight_points`, and a manager's aggregated `assigned_caseload_points` — are carried to one decimal place. Risk scores are integers in the range 0 through 100, produced by a single rounding step applied at the very end of the computation rather than by rounding each contributing term. Engagement scores are likewise integer percentages produced by one final rounding step. The compliance rate of the accreditation file carries one decimal place. The return-on-investment ratio carries two decimal places.

A rule that recurs throughout the financial narrative and is worth stating once, globally: **rounding happens at the smallest declared unit and the rounded values are then summed — never the reverse.** An intervention's cost is rounded once when the intervention is created and that stored figure is what later aggregations add up; a projected member-month cost is rounded once per member-month and the monthly figures are then combined. Summing unrounded quantities and rounding the total afterwards is not the declared order and produces different cents figures.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Enrollment Writes

Two write operations on an enrollment require the client to declare which version of the enrollment it believes it is acting on. Both take a mandatory input field named `expected_enrollment_version`, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that enrollment. It is not an optional field, and a request that omits it is rejected as a malformed request.

Reassigning an enrollment to a different care manager is the first. The transfer moves caseload points off one manager's ledger and onto another's, so a client working from an out-of-date view would be moving points off a manager that no longer holds the enrollment. If `expected_enrollment_version` does not equal the enrollment's current `version`, the transfer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and neither ledger moves.

Recording an outcome measurement is the second. A new measurement supersedes the prior measurement of the same method for that enrollment, so a re-measurement taken from a stale view would silently overwrite a newer reading whose measured window has already moved on. Here too, a `expected_enrollment_version` that does not equal the enrollment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and no measurement row is appended.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Activation, disenrollment, touch completion, plan review and every other mutation are guarded by their state preconditions alone.

### 2.9. Caseload Ledger Discipline

A care manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is never adjusted by deltas. Whenever anything happens that could change it, the figure is recomputed from scratch as the full sum of `acuity_weight_points` over every enrollment that names that manager in `care_manager_id` and whose `enrollment_status` is either `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, rounded to one decimal place. Enrollments in `GRADUATED` or `DISENROLLED` are outside the summed set, which is exactly what makes the ledger conserve when an enrollment ends. The manager's `active_enrollment_count` is a separate counter, tracking only enrollments whose `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`, and it too is recomputed as a full recount rather than by deltas. A recomputation that leaves all three of `assigned_caseload_points`, `capacity_status` and `active_enrollment_count` exactly as they were is not a mutation of the manager record at all: its `updated_at` and `version` are left untouched, and only a recomputation that actually moves one of the three refreshes them.

Immediately after every recomputation of `assigned_caseload_points`, the manager's `capacity_status` is refreshed: it becomes `OVER_CAPACITY` when `assigned_caseload_points` is strictly greater than `capacity_points`, and `OPEN` otherwise. Equality is `OPEN` — a manager loaded exactly to their ceiling is still open for business.

### 2.10. Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this system touch more than one record. When they do, every affected record has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, including records that the caller never named — a goal that goes at risk because of an escalated social need, a plan that suspends because a member stopped answering, an intervention voided because its plan closed. Cascades run to completion within the request that triggered them, and the response returned to the caller reflects the settled state, not an intermediate one. Where a cascade has an ordering that matters, the narrative in §3 states the hops explicitly and that ordering is normative.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Member Registry, Consent, and the Utilization Feed

Everything in this system begins with a member. Plan operations register one by supplying the plan's own `external_member_id` — the health-plan member number, which must be non-empty and unique across all members, a collision being reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` once every field of the request has passed its own shape check — together with the member's `full_name`, their `birth_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form, and their `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the US state of residence. Two further attributes may be supplied at registration and default sensibly when they are not: `chronic_condition_count`, an integer no smaller than zero counting the member's active chronic conditions, which defaults to 0; and `social_data_consent_status`, which is either `GRANTED` or `REVOKED` and defaults to `GRANTED`. A newly registered member has no stratification history, so all four of `latest_concurrent_score`, `latest_concurrent_tier`, `latest_prospective_score` and `latest_prospective_tier` start out null.

Consent is not a one-time decision. Either plan operations or the member's care manager may toggle it later by supplying a replacement `social_data_consent_status`, which overwrites the stored value and increments the member's `version`. Consent matters because it gates the moment social-need data would leave the plan: a member whose consent reads `REVOKED` cannot have a referral sent on their behalf, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED`. Revoking consent does not retract referrals already sent.

The utilization feed is the second stream of member data. Plan operations and the analytics function both record utilization events, each naming the `member_id` it belongs to, an `event_type` drawn from `INPATIENT_ADMISSION`, `ED_VISIT`, `OBSERVATION_STAY`, `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` and `DISCHARGE`, the `occurred_at` instant, and the `facility_name` that is carried into the accreditation file. Two optional attributes shape the financial and analytic treatment of the event: `episode_cost_cents`, the allowed cost of the episode in whole cents, defaulting to 0; and `adjudication_status`, either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, defaulting to `OPEN`. `CLOSED` means the claim has been adjudicated, and only `CLOSED` events enter the retrospective stratification variant and every outcome-measurement member-month cost. A `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` is by nature an event whose `occurred_at` sits in the future.

Discharges are a special case in two ways. A `DISCHARGE` event carries no episode cost of its own — any `episode_cost_cents` supplied on a discharge is discarded and the stored figure is 0 — and it must name a `discharge_disposition`, one of `HOME`, `SNF`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `AMA` or `EXPIRED`, its absence being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; every non-discharge event leaves that attribute null, a disposition supplied there being checked against the same enumeration and then discarded. A discharge may also point back at the admission it closes through `index_admission_event_id`, a reference to the earlier event record; for any event type other than `DISCHARGE` that reference is still resolved — an identifier naming no event is `ERR_TRANSITION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` — and then discarded, so the stored value is null. Recording a discharge is furthermore the one utilization event with a downstream consequence: it opens a transitions-of-care obligation on every one of the member's active enrollments, and the count of enrollments it escalated is recorded on the event itself as `toc_triggered_enrollment_count`, which is 0 for every non-discharge event. That cascade is described in §3.6.

### 3.2. Risk Stratification: One Member, Two Scores

Stratification is the gatekeeper for program eligibility, and it is deliberately run in two flavours. Plan operations or the analytics function requests a stratification by naming a `variant`, either `CONCURRENT` or `PROSPECTIVE`. The result is an immutable record carrying the `member_id` it was computed for, the `variant` used, an `as_of_date` stamped as the calendar date of the logical clock at computation time, the component point totals, the final `score` and `tier`, and `included_event_count` — the number of utilization events that actually contributed utilization points under this variant.

**Assembling the event set.** Consider the logical clock instant at the moment of computation. The retrospective window covers the 180 days immediately before that instant, including the instant 180 days earlier and excluding the computation instant itself. The forward window covers the 90 days immediately after, excluding the computation instant and including the instant 90 days later. Only utilization events belonging to this member are considered at all, and `DISCHARGE` events never earn points under either variant. Under `CONCURRENT`, an event is included when its `event_type` is `INPATIENT_ADMISSION`, `ED_VISIT` or `OBSERVATION_STAY`, its `adjudication_status` is `CLOSED`, and its `occurred_at` falls inside the retrospective window; `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` records are excluded entirely. Under `PROSPECTIVE`, the same three retrospective event types are included whether their `adjudication_status` is `CLOSED` or `OPEN`, and to those are added every `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` whose `occurred_at` falls inside the forward window. The consequence is intended: a member with pending claims and a booked admission scores strictly lower under `CONCURRENT` than under `PROSPECTIVE`.

**Weighting for recency.** Each included event contributes a base point value determined by its type — 20 points for an `INPATIENT_ADMISSION`, 8 for an `ED_VISIT`, 5 for an `OBSERVATION_STAY`, and 20 for a `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` — multiplied by a recency factor. A `SCHEDULED_ADMISSION` always carries a recency factor of 1.0, because a booked admission is not aged. For every other included event, compute its age as the number of days from `occurred_at` to the computation instant, retaining the fractional part rather than truncating it; when that age is 90 days or less the recency factor is 1.0, and when it is greater than 90 days the factor is one half. An event dated exactly 90 days before the computation instant therefore still earns its full weight. Summing base points multiplied by recency factor across the whole included set gives `utilization_points`, carried to one decimal place.

**The static and social components.** `chronic_points` is the member's `chronic_condition_count` multiplied by 6 points per condition. `social_need_points` counts the member's open social needs — every social need whose `status` is `IDENTIFIED`, `REFERRED` or `UNMET_ESCALATED` and whose owning enrollment belongs to this member and has an `enrollment_status` of `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` — multiplies that count by 4 points per need, and then caps the product at 12 points, so no member can earn more than three needs' worth of social points however many are open.

**Combining.** `raw_score` is `utilization_points` plus `chronic_points` plus `social_need_points`, carried to one decimal place with no intermediate rounding of the individual terms. The final `score` is that raw figure rounded half up to a whole number and then capped at 100, so a raw figure of 130.4 reports as 100. The `tier` bands the score: `HIGH` when the score is 85 or more, `RISING` when the score is at least 60 but below 85, `MODERATE` when the score is at least 35 but below 60, and `LOW` when the score is below 35.

Appending a stratification refreshes the member's cached view and increments the member's `version`. If the run used the `CONCURRENT` variant, `latest_concurrent_score` and `latest_concurrent_tier` take the new record's `score` and `tier`; if it used `PROSPECTIVE`, `latest_prospective_score` and `latest_prospective_tier` do. In each case "latest" means the record for that variant with the newest `as_of_date`, and where two records share an `as_of_date`, the one created most recently. Reading a member's stratification history returns the records newest `as_of_date` first, and the history may be narrowed to a single `variant`.

### 3.3. Programs, Care Managers, and the Enrollment Decision

A **program** is the policy object that decides who may enrol, who may carry them, how often they are contacted, and what the plan is willing to spend. Plan operations creates one with a display `name` and a required configuration block: `min_risk_tier`, the lowest tier eligible to enrol, drawn from `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `RISING` and `HIGH`; `stratification_variant`, either `CONCURRENT` or `PROSPECTIVE`, naming which of the two scores this program believes; `required_credential_levels`, a non-empty subset of `RN`, `LCSW` and `CHW` listing the licensure classes permitted to carry the program's enrollments; `staff_minute_budget`, the ceiling on staff time the program may commit and deliver; `hourly_cost_cents`, the loaded staff cost per hour in whole cents; and `cohort_benchmark_pmpm_cents`, a matched-cohort benchmark per-member-per-month cost in whole cents that is consulted by exactly one of the two outcome-measurement methods. Those three money-and-budget figures are each a required integer no smaller than zero. Three cadence and compliance intervals are optional integers that default: `care_plan_review_interval_days` defaults to 90, `toc_outreach_hours` defaults to 48, and `referral_loop_days` defaults to 30. A brand-new program starts with both of its staff-time ledgers, `committed_staff_minutes` and `delivered_staff_minutes`, at 0.

A **care manager** is created by plan operations with a `full_name`, a `credential_level` of `RN`, `LCSW` or `CHW`, a non-empty `licensed_state_codes` array of two-letter state codes, and `capacity_points`, a strictly positive ceiling carried to one decimal place on how much caseload weight they may hold. A new care manager starts with `assigned_caseload_points` at 0.0, `capacity_status` at `OPEN`, and `active_enrollment_count` at 0. The roster may be listed and narrowed by `capacity_status` or by `credential_level`, and the member registry may be narrowed by `state_code`.

**Caseload weight** is the currency that ties these two objects together. Each enrollment consumes a weight computed from two factors: the risk tier frozen onto the enrollment and the outreach intensity currently applied to it. The tier contributes 1 point for `LOW`, 2 for `MODERATE`, 4 for `RISING` and 7 for `HIGH`. The cadence multiplier is 1.0 at `STANDARD` intensity, 1.5 at `INTENSIVE`, and 2.0 at `TOC_INTENSIVE`. The enrollment's `acuity_weight_points` is the tier points multiplied by the cadence multiplier, rounded half up to one decimal place. A rising-risk member under standard outreach therefore weighs 4.0; escalate that same member to transitions-of-care intensity and they weigh 8.0.

Either plan operations or a care manager may enrol a member, supplying `member_id` and `program_id`, and optionally `care_manager_id`. Before anything is created, the request must clear a series of gates, and §2.6 rather than the order of this narrative decides which failure is reported: when a `care_manager_id` is named, that manager's credential and licensure are tested first at rung 12, then the three gates of this paragraph at rung 14, and the caseload ceiling last of all at rung 16. The member may not already hold another enrollment in the same program whose `enrollment_status` is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`; a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT`. The member must have at least one stratification whose `variant` equals the program's `stratification_variant`, or the request fails with `ERR_STRATIFICATION_REQUIRED`. That stratification's tier must rank at or above the program's `min_risk_tier`, ranking the tiers `LOW` below `MODERATE`, `MODERATE` below `RISING` and `RISING` below `HIGH`; a member who ranks strictly lower is turned away with `ERR_RISK_TIER_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`.

When the request names a `care_manager_id` explicitly, three further gates apply to that named manager — the first two of them ahead of the three gates just described, and the third only after them. Their `credential_level` must be one of the program's `required_credential_levels`, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. The member's `state_code` must appear in the manager's `licensed_state_codes`, else `ERR_STATE_LICENSURE_MISMATCH`. And the manager's current `assigned_caseload_points` plus the weight this new enrollment would consume must not exceed their `capacity_points`; equality is accepted, and only a strict overshoot is refused, with `ERR_CASELOAD_WOULD_EXCEED_CAPACITY`. An enrollment created this way records `assignment_reason` as `EXPLICIT`.

**Auto-assignment.** Omitting `care_manager_id` hands the choice to the selector, which is fully deterministic and consults no random property — in particular, no identifier value participates in the filter, the ranking, or the tie-break. The candidate weight used throughout is the tier points for the enrollment's frozen tier multiplied by 1.0, rounded to one decimal place, because a newly created enrollment always begins at `STANDARD` cadence. The selector first filters the entire care-manager roster down to those whose `credential_level` is one of the program's `required_credential_levels`, whose `licensed_state_codes` contains the member's `state_code`, whose `capacity_status` is currently `OPEN`, and whose `assigned_caseload_points` plus the candidate weight is no greater than their `capacity_points`. Surviving candidates are then ranked by headroom — `capacity_points` minus `assigned_caseload_points`, compared at one decimal place — from largest headroom to smallest. Where two candidates have identical headroom, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins. The first-ranked candidate becomes the enrollment's `care_manager_id` and `assignment_reason` is recorded as `AUTO_SELECTED`. If the filter leaves no candidate at all, nothing is created and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CARE_MANAGER`.

However the manager is chosen, creation freezes two snapshots that never move again: `risk_tier_at_enrollment` takes the `tier` and `risk_score_at_enrollment` takes the `score` of the member's latest stratification under the program's `stratification_variant`. The new enrollment begins with `enrollment_status` `PENDING` and `engagement_status` `UNREACHED` — two dimensions that advance on entirely disjoint triggers, so an enrollment's engagement state is never a function of its lifecycle state and vice versa, with the single proviso that engagement transitions are only recorded while `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`. Its `cadence_tier` and `cadence_tier_snapshot` both read `STANDARD`, its `pre_toc_cadence_tier` is null, its `acuity_weight_points` is computed at standard cadence, and the owning manager's `assigned_caseload_points` and `capacity_status` are recomputed accordingly. Enrollments may later be listed and narrowed by `member_id`, `program_id`, `care_manager_id`, `enrollment_status` or `engagement_status`.

### 3.4. Activation, Reassignment, and the End of an Enrollment

An enrollment sits in `PENDING` until someone starts the clock on it. Either plan operations or a care manager may activate it, moving `enrollment_status` from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE`; attempting this on an enrollment in any other state yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_PENDING`. Activation stamps `activated_at` with the logical-clock instant and then does five things in the member's file. It sets the outreach intensity: `cadence_tier` becomes `INTENSIVE` when `risk_tier_at_enrollment` is `HIGH`, and `STANDARD` otherwise, and `cadence_tier_snapshot` is set to that same value, opening the first engagement measurement window. It recomputes `acuity_weight_points` from the tier points and the new cadence multiplier. It then recomputes the owning manager's `assigned_caseload_points` as a full sum and refreshes their `capacity_status` from that new figure — and note carefully that activation performs **no** capacity precheck of its own, so activating a high-risk enrollment may itself be the event that flips its owner to `OVER_CAPACITY`. It increments the manager's `active_enrollment_count` by one. Finally it projects `next_touch_due_at` from a base instant equal to `activated_at`, running the very same three-adjustment projection that §3.6 step 6 describes rather than a simpler one of its own — with the counters where a freshly activated enrollment leaves them, no adjustment applies and the interval is simply the one implied by the cadence tier just chosen — and resets the engagement counters: `engagement_score` to 0 and `reached_touch_count` to 0. The enrollment's `version` increases by one.

Reassignment is the exclusive province of plan operations. A transfer names a `to_care_manager_id` and, as §2.8 requires, an `expected_enrollment_version`. The enrollment must be in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` — a `GRADUATED` or `DISENROLLED` enrollment cannot be reassigned, and the attempt returns `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The target's `credential_level` must be one of the program's `required_credential_levels`, and the member's `state_code` must appear in the target's `licensed_state_codes` — the same two gates that govern an explicit assignment, reported as `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED` and `ERR_STATE_LICENSURE_MISMATCH`, and evaluated at rung 12 ahead of the same-manager test. Only then must the target genuinely be a different manager than the enrollment's current `care_manager_id`, else `ERR_TRANSFER_TO_SAME_MANAGER`. And the target's `assigned_caseload_points` plus this enrollment's current `acuity_weight_points` must be no greater than their `capacity_points`, with equality accepted and only a strict overshoot rejected as `ERR_CASELOAD_WOULD_EXCEED_CAPACITY`.

An accepted transfer rewrites `care_manager_id` to the target and records `assignment_reason` as `TRANSFERRED`. The enrollment's weight leaves the previous manager's ledger and joins the target's, and both managers have their `capacity_status` refreshed from their new sums. When the enrollment's `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`, both managers' `active_enrollment_count` figures are adjusted as well — one down, one up. And because the previous manager is no longer the one making the calls, every touch on this enrollment that is still `SCHEDULED` and still names the previous manager moves from `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, with a `cancel_reason` of `CARE_MANAGER_TRANSFER`, releasing those calendar intervals. `enrollment_status` itself is unchanged by a transfer; the observable movement is the ownership field and the two ledgers. The enrollment's `version` increases by one.

Ending an enrollment requires a `reason`, one of `GOALS_MET`, `LOST_CONTACT`, `MEMBER_REQUEST`, `INELIGIBLE` or `DECEASED`, supplied by plan operations or the care manager. The enrollment must still be in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`; one already `GRADUATED` or `DISENROLLED` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` before the reason is looked at at all. The reason decides the destination. `GOALS_MET` graduates the enrollment into `GRADUATED`; every other reason disenrolls it into `DISENROLLED`. Both destinations stamp `disenrolled_at` with the logical-clock instant, store the supplied reason in `disenrollment_reason`, null out both `next_touch_due_at` and `toc_due_at`, and increment `version`.

A `PENDING` enrollment can only be disenrolled, never graduated: a member who was never activated has no active care plan whose goals could have been met, so a `PENDING` enrollment ended with a reason of `GOALS_MET` is refused with `ERR_GOALS_NOT_MET`. The `PENDING` ending is otherwise quiet — there are no touches to cancel and no plan to close. The enrollment's stored `acuity_weight_points` is left exactly as it was, but the enrollment has now left the set of `PENDING` and `ACTIVE` enrollments that the ledger sums, so the manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is recomputed downward and their `capacity_status` refreshed.

Ending an `ACTIVE` enrollment for any reason other than `GOALS_MET` runs a four-hop cascade. First, every touch on this enrollment still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `ENROLLMENT_ENDED`, freeing the care manager's calendar intervals. Second, the enrollment's care plan — the one whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — moves to `CLOSED` and takes a `closed_at` stamp. Third, caused by that closure, every goal under that plan whose `status` is `OPEN` or `AT_RISK` moves to `CLOSED_UNMET`; a goal that was `AT_RISK` additionally has its `barrier_flag` cleared back to false and drops the plan's `at_risk_goal_count` by one, and the plan's `compliance_posture` is recomputed once the cascade has settled — a `CLOSED` plan is never `DEFICIENT`, so it reads `COMPLIANT` when no goal is left at risk. Fourth, caused by those goal closures, every intervention under those goals whose `status` is still `PLANNED` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `PLAN_CLOSED`, and each one releases its `staff_minutes` back from the program's `committed_staff_minutes`. Alongside the cascade, the manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is recomputed downward, their `capacity_status` refreshed, and their `active_enrollment_count` reduced by one.

Graduation is the same shape with a deliberately narrower body. Ending an `ACTIVE` enrollment with `GOALS_MET` first demands proof: the enrollment must have a care plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, that plan must carry at least one goal, and every one of those goals must be in `MET`. A plan with no goals at all, or with any goal outside `MET`, fails this check with `ERR_GOALS_NOT_MET`. When the proof holds, `enrollment_status` becomes `GRADUATED`, `disenrollment_reason` records `GOALS_MET`, every `SCHEDULED` touch on the enrollment moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `ENROLLMENT_ENDED`, and the `ACTIVE` care plan moves to `CLOSED`. No goal is reopened and no intervention is voided on this path, for the simple reason that no goal is left in `OPEN` or `AT_RISK` to close. The manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is recomputed downward, `capacity_status` refreshed, and `active_enrollment_count` reduced by one.

### 3.5. The Care Plan: Authoring, Goals, Interventions, and Clinical Review

Only a care manager may author a care plan, and doing so requires nothing more than the `enrollment_id` the plan will govern and a `title` that is carried verbatim into the accreditation file. The acting care manager's `X-User-ID` is recorded on the plan as `authored_by_care_manager_id`, which is the anchor for both the ownership check on submission and the segregation-of-duties check on review. A fresh plan opens in `DRAFT` with `plan_version_no` at 1, `revision_count` at 0, `at_risk_goal_count` at 0, `compliance_posture` at `COMPLIANT`, and `review_decision`, `revision_note`, `reviewed_by_care_manager_id`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `closed_at` and `next_review_due_at` all null. Plans may later be listed and narrowed by `enrollment_id`, by `authored_by_care_manager_id`, by `reviewed_by_care_manager_id`, or by `status`.

**Goals** are added to a plan while it is still in `DRAFT`; an attempt to add a goal to a plan in any other status is refused with `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`. Each goal names the `care_plan_id` it belongs to, a `domain`, a free-text `description` stating the goal, and a `target_date` — an ISO 8601 calendar date the goal aims to be met by. The `domain` enumeration is `CLINICAL`, `MEDICATION`, `BEHAVIORAL`, `FOOD`, `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORTATION` and `UTILITIES`. The last four of those are deliberately the same values a social need may carry, which is the mechanism by which an escalated social need finds the goals it should put at risk. A new goal opens in `OPEN` with `barrier_flag` false and both `planned_intervention_count` and `completed_intervention_count` at 0. A plan's goals may be listed and narrowed by `status` or by `domain`.

**Interventions** are the unit of work and the unit of cost. A care manager plans one under a goal by supplying a `modality` — `PHONE`, `HOME_VISIT`, `EDUCATION`, `REFERRAL_COORDINATION` or `MEDICATION_REVIEW` — and `staff_minutes`, a strictly positive integer count of planned staff time. The intervention records the `goal_id` it belongs to, opens in `PLANNED`, and has `delivered_at` and `void_reason` null. Two gates guard creation, and §2.6 fires them in the reverse of the order they are narrated in. The goal itself must be in `OPEN` or `AT_RISK`; a goal already `MET` or `CLOSED_UNMET` takes no new work, and the attempt returns `ERR_GOAL_NOT_OPEN` at rung 9, before the owning plan is consulted at all. Only then must that plan be in `ACTIVE` status, because work is only planned against an approved plan; otherwise `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` at rung 11.

The fiscal gate is the program's staff-time budget. Adding up the program's current `committed_staff_minutes`, its current `delivered_staff_minutes`, and the new intervention's `staff_minutes` must produce a figure no larger than the program's `staff_minute_budget`; equality fits, and only a strict overshoot is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STAFF_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On acceptance, the intervention's `cost_cents` is computed once and frozen: multiply `staff_minutes` by the program's `hourly_cost_cents`, divide by 60, and round half up to whole cents. A later change to the program's `hourly_cost_cents` never restates an intervention's stored cost. Creation adds the intervention's `staff_minutes` to the program's `committed_staff_minutes` and increments the goal's `planned_intervention_count` by one. The interventions under a goal may be listed and narrowed by `status`.

Completing an intervention is how work becomes delivered. The intervention must be in `PLANNED` — a second completion, or a completion of something already voided, returns `ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_PLANNED` — and its plan must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Completion sets `status` to `COMPLETED`, stamps `delivered_at` with the logical clock, moves the intervention's `staff_minutes` out of the program's `committed_staff_minutes` and into its `delivered_staff_minutes`, decrements the goal's `planned_intervention_count` by one and increments its `completed_intervention_count` by one.

Goal completion is derived, never declared. When a completion leaves the goal with `planned_intervention_count` at 0 and `completed_intervention_count` of at least 1, the goal itself moves to `MET`. A goal in `OPEN` simply becomes `MET`. A goal in `AT_RISK` becomes `MET` too, and additionally clears its `barrier_flag` back to false, decrements the owning plan's `at_risk_goal_count` by one, and has the plan's `compliance_posture` recomputed. There is no endpoint that sets a goal to `MET` directly.

Voiding is the compensating action for planning work that will not happen. A care manager voids a `PLANNED` intervention by supplying a `void_reason` — free text, drawn from no enumeration, which must be present and non-empty or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the intervention's own `PLANNED` state is the only other gate, so unlike creation and completion a void imposes no `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` check and work may be voided off a plan that has since left `ACTIVE`. The intervention moves to `VOIDED`, the reason is stored, and its `staff_minutes` is released from the program's `committed_staff_minutes` while `delivered_staff_minutes` is left untouched. The goal's `planned_intervention_count` drops by one. Critically, and in deliberate contrast to completion, **no goal auto-transition fires from a void** — a goal whose last planned intervention was voided rather than completed stays exactly where it was, even though its `planned_intervention_count` has reached 0.

**Submission and review.** The plan's author, and only the author, may submit it for clinical review; a care manager whose `X-User-ID` differs from `authored_by_care_manager_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The plan must be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`. It must then carry at least one goal — a goal-less plan is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested after the `DRAFT` check and before the enrollment is consulted. And the owning enrollment's `enrollment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A successful submission moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_REVIEW` and stamps `submitted_at`.

Review is the exclusive act of a `ClinicalReviewer`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION` and, on the revision branch, a `revision_note`. The reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_care_manager_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — rung 8, and therefore ahead of any inspection of the plan's status. Only then must the plan be in `PENDING_REVIEW`, else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_IN_REVIEW`.

On `APPROVE`, the owning enrollment must additionally still be `ACTIVE`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Approval moves the plan from `PENDING_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock, sets `review_decision` to `APPROVED`, records `reviewed_by_care_manager_id` as the reviewer's `X-User-ID`, and computes `next_review_due_at` as `approved_at` advanced by the program's `care_plan_review_interval_days` days. The plan's `compliance_posture` is then recomputed.

On `REQUEST_REVISION`, the `revision_note` must be present and non-empty; that is a request-shape rule sitting at rung 3, so a missing or blank note answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the plan identifier is even resolved. This branch, unlike `APPROVE`, imposes no gate at all on the owning enrollment's status. The plan moves from `PENDING_REVIEW` back to `DRAFT`, closing the revision cycle. `review_decision` records `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the supplied note is stored in `revision_note`, `reviewed_by_care_manager_id` records the reviewer's `X-User-ID`, `plan_version_no` increases by one, `revision_count` increases by one, and `submitted_at` is cleared back to null so the plan reads as unsubmitted again. The cycle `DRAFT` → `PENDING_REVIEW` → `DRAFT` may repeat as many times as the reviewer insists, each pass advancing both counters.

**Compliance posture** is a derived summary of the plan's health, recomputed whenever anything that feeds it changes. It reads `DEFICIENT` whenever the plan's `status` is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise it reads `AT_RISK` whenever `at_risk_goal_count` is 1 or more. Otherwise it reads `COMPLIANT`.

### 3.6. Outreach: Scheduling Touches, Measuring Engagement, and Adjusting Cadence

A **touch** is one scheduled contact attempt occupying a slot on a care manager's calendar. Scheduling one requires the `enrollment_id` being contacted, the `care_manager_id` whose calendar it occupies, a `touch_type` of `ROUTINE`, `TOC` or `ASSESSMENT`, a `modality` of `PHONE`, `VIDEO` or `HOME_VISIT`, and the half-open calendar window: `scheduled_start_at`, which is the inclusive start, and `scheduled_end_at`, which is the exclusive end. Plan operations may book on anyone's calendar; a care manager may book only their own, so a `CareManager` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the `care_manager_id` in the body is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Touches may later be listed and narrowed by `enrollment_id`, `care_manager_id`, `status` or `touch_type`.

Five further gates guard a booking. The enrollment must have a care plan in `ACTIVE` status — there is no outreach against an unapproved plan — else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The named care manager's `credential_level` must be one of the program's `required_credential_levels`, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. The window must be well formed, with `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, else `ERR_TOUCH_WINDOW_INVALID`; and it must not be in the past, meaning `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the logical clock's current instant, else `ERR_TOUCH_WINDOW_IN_PAST`. The calendar must be free, as described immediately below. And the named care manager's `capacity_status` must read `OPEN`; a manager already carrying more caseload points than their ceiling takes no new bookings, and the attempt returns `ERR_CARE_MANAGER_OVER_CAPACITY`.

**Calendar geometry.** A care manager can be in exactly one place at a time, and the plan requires documentation time between contacts. Conflict detection therefore expands the *candidate* window by 15 minutes on each side — 15 minutes earlier than `scheduled_start_at` and 15 minutes later than `scheduled_end_at` — and tests that expanded window against every existing touch on the same `care_manager_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, each taken in its raw unexpanded form. The candidate conflicts with an existing touch when the expanded start is earlier than that touch's `scheduled_end_at` and that touch's `scheduled_start_at` is earlier than the expanded end. Any conflict rejects the booking with `ERR_CARE_MANAGER_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Because the comparison is strict on both sides, back-to-back booking is rejected: a candidate beginning at the exact instant an existing touch ends is expanded 15 minutes backwards and therefore overlaps it. The smallest accepted gap is exactly 15 minutes — a candidate beginning exactly 15 minutes after an existing touch's end is accepted. Touches in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` release their interval entirely and are never occupants.

**Cancelling** a scheduled touch is available to its care manager and to plan operations, and takes a `cancel_reason` — free text that must be present and non-empty, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The touch moves from `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, the reason is stored, and the calendar interval is released so that a later booking whose buffered window overlaps it is accepted. Cancellation is engagement-neutral: no engagement counter changes, no cadence recomputation fires, and the enrollment is untouched. A touch already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled again, and the attempt returns `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

**Completion** is the single richest operation in the system, and only the touch's own care manager may perform it. The touch must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_SCHEDULED`, and its `scheduled_start_at` must already have been reached on the logical clock — completing early returns `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_YET_DUE`. The caller supplies an `outcome` of either `REACHED` or `NOT_REACHED`, and a `documentation_note`. Accreditation requires a contact note for every conversation that actually happened, so a completion carrying `REACHED` without a present, non-empty `documentation_note` is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`; a `NOT_REACHED` completion needs no note, and whatever note it does supply is stored verbatim. Those two checks do not fire in the order just narrated: the documentation rule sits at rung 12 and the readiness rule at rung 13, so a premature `REACHED` completion carrying no note reports `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_TOUCH_NOT_YET_DUE`.

An accepted completion then runs six steps, **in exactly this order**. The order is normative because two of the steps read values that a later step overwrites.

*Step 1 — record the touch.* `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `outcome` takes the supplied value, and the `documentation_note` is stored.

*Step 2 — move the outreach counters on the enrollment.* When the `outcome` is `REACHED`, the enrollment's `last_reached_touch_at` is set to this touch's `completed_at`, its `consecutive_failed_outreach_count` is reset to 0, and its `reached_touch_count` increases by one; additionally, when the touch's `touch_type` is `TOC` and the enrollment has an open `toc_due_at` whose instant is at or after this `completed_at`, the transitions-of-care obligation is cleared as described later in this subsection. When the `outcome` is `NOT_REACHED`, `consecutive_failed_outreach_count` increases by one and `reached_touch_count` is left unchanged.

*Step 3 — recompute the engagement score.* The measurement window is the 90 days ending at the current logical-clock instant, excluding the instant 90 days earlier and including the current instant. The expected number of contacts in that window comes from the enrollment's `cadence_tier_snapshot` — and from the snapshot only, never from the live `cadence_tier` and never from the value step 5 is about to write. The contact interval implied by a snapshot of `STANDARD` is 30 days, of `INTENSIVE` is 14 days, and of `TOC_INTENSIVE` is 7 days. Divide 90 by that interval, discard any fractional part, and take the larger of the result and 1; that is the expected contact count. The achieved count is the number of touches on this enrollment whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `outcome` is `REACHED`, and whose `completed_at` falls inside the window — cancelled touches and `NOT_REACHED` completions never enter that numerator. `engagement_score` is then 100 multiplied by the achieved count, divided by the expected count, rounded half up to a whole percentage, and capped at 100.

*Step 4 — settle the engagement dimension.* `engagement_status` is chosen top-down from the counters produced by step 2 and the score produced by step 3, with the first matching clause winning. If `consecutive_failed_outreach_count` is 3 or more, the enrollment becomes `DISENGAGED`. Otherwise, if `reached_touch_count` is 0, it reads `UNREACHED`. Otherwise, if `engagement_score` is 60 or more, it becomes `ENGAGED`. Otherwise it becomes `AT_RISK`. This dimension is cyclic in both directions: `UNREACHED` → `ENGAGED`, `UNREACHED` → `AT_RISK`, `UNREACHED` → `DISENGAGED`, `ENGAGED` → `AT_RISK`, `AT_RISK` → `ENGAGED`, `AT_RISK` → `DISENGAGED`, `DISENGAGED` → `ENGAGED` and `DISENGAGED` → `AT_RISK` are all reachable, and none of them disturbs the enrollment's `enrollment_status`, which stays `ACTIVE` throughout.

Two of those movements carry a cascade onto the care plan. Entering `DISENGAGED` — from `UNREACHED` or from `AT_RISK` alike — moves the enrollment's care plan from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, whose `compliance_posture` consequently reads `DEFICIENT`. Leaving `DISENGAGED` — whether the enrollment lands in `ENGAGED` or in `AT_RISK` — moves that same plan from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, and its `compliance_posture` is recomputed, reading `AT_RISK` when the plan still has one or more goals in `AT_RISK` and `COMPLIANT` when it does not.

*Step 5 — reassign the cadence tier.* `cadence_tier` is chosen top-down, the first matching clause winning, reading the `engagement_status` that step 4 just produced. When the enrollment has an open `toc_due_at`, the tier is `TOC_INTENSIVE`. Otherwise, when `engagement_status` is `DISENGAGED`, the tier is `INTENSIVE`. Otherwise, when `risk_tier_at_enrollment` is `HIGH`, the tier is `INTENSIVE`. Otherwise the tier is `STANDARD`. Whenever this step actually changes the tier, `acuity_weight_points` is recomputed from the tier points and the new cadence multiplier, the owning care manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is recomputed as a full sum, and their `capacity_status` is refreshed.

*Step 6 — project the next contact deadline.* The base instant is the enrollment's `last_reached_touch_at` when that is not null, and `activated_at` otherwise. The starting interval is the number of days implied by the **current** `cadence_tier` produced by step 5 — 30 days at `STANDARD`, 14 at `INTENSIVE`, 7 at `TOC_INTENSIVE`. Three adjustments are then applied in exactly this order. First, escalation: when `consecutive_failed_outreach_count` is 2 or more, the interval becomes the larger of 3 days and half the starting interval with any fractional part discarded. Second, relaxation: when `engagement_status` is `ENGAGED` and `consecutive_failed_outreach_count` is 0, the interval becomes the smaller of 45 days and the starting interval plus 7 days. Third, the transitions-of-care clamp, applied last so that it dominates both adjustments above: when the enrollment has an open `toc_due_at` whose instant is still ahead of the logical clock, the interval becomes the smaller of its current value and 2 days. `next_touch_due_at` is the base instant advanced by the resulting whole number of days, with the time-of-day component of the base preserved exactly.

Both the touch's `version` and the enrollment's `version` increase by one on a completion.

### 3.7. Transitions of Care: The Discharge Obligation

The weeks after a discharge are where care management earns its budget, so a discharge imposes a hard, time-boxed outreach obligation. Recording a utilization event whose `event_type` is `DISCHARGE` fires a cascade on every enrollment of that member whose `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE`. Enrollments in `PENDING`, `GRADUATED` or `DISENROLLED` are untouched, and enrollments belonging to other members are irrelevant.

The cascade runs in four hops on each affected enrollment. Hop one sets the deadline and raises the intensity: `toc_due_at` becomes the discharge event's `occurred_at` advanced by the program's `toc_outreach_hours` hours, the enrollment's current `cadence_tier` is stashed in `pre_toc_cadence_tier` so it can be restored later, and both `cadence_tier` and `cadence_tier_snapshot` are set to `TOC_INTENSIVE`. Hop two is caused by that tier change: `acuity_weight_points` is recomputed as the enrollment's tier points multiplied by the transitions-of-care cadence multiplier of 2.0, rounded to one decimal place — so a `RISING` enrollment that weighed 4.0 now weighs 8.0. Hop three is caused by hop two: the owning care manager's `assigned_caseload_points` is recomputed as the full sum over their `PENDING` and `ACTIVE` enrollments. Hop four is caused by hop three: the manager's `capacity_status` becomes `OVER_CAPACITY` when that new sum is strictly greater than their `capacity_points`, which as a side consequence removes them from the auto-assignment candidate pool for any subsequent enrollment. Alongside the four hops, `next_touch_due_at` is reprojected — and because the transitions-of-care clamp is applied last in that projection, the resulting interval is at most 2 days. The discharge event's own `toc_triggered_enrollment_count` is incremented once for each enrollment it escalated, and each affected enrollment's `version` increases by one.

Note what this cascade does *not* do. It never changes `enrollment_status`, which remains `ACTIVE` throughout; the escalation is a self-loop whose observable effects are the deadline, the two cadence fields and the caseload ledgers. It also refreshes `cadence_tier_snapshot`, which is the only event besides activation that does so — an ordinary cadence change during touch completion leaves the snapshot alone, which is what keeps the engagement denominator stable across a measurement window.

Discharging the obligation requires a genuine conversation, on time. When a touch whose `touch_type` is `TOC` is completed with an `outcome` of `REACHED`, and the enrollment has an open `toc_due_at`, and the touch's `completed_at` is at or before that `toc_due_at`, the obligation is satisfied: the touch's `satisfied_toc_obligation` flag — false on every other touch — is set to true, `toc_due_at` is cleared to null, `cadence_tier` and `cadence_tier_snapshot` are both restored to the value stashed in `pre_toc_cadence_tier`, and `pre_toc_cadence_tier` returns to null. The restoration lowers `acuity_weight_points` again, which recomputes the owning manager's `assigned_caseload_points` and refreshes their `capacity_status`. A `ROUTINE` or `ASSESSMENT` touch never satisfies the obligation however well it goes, a `NOT_REACHED` outcome never satisfies it, and a `TOC` touch completed after `toc_due_at` has passed does not satisfy it either — in that last case the obligation simply stays open, the enrollment stays at `TOC_INTENSIVE`, and the accreditation file will record the miss.

### 3.8. Social Needs, Community Referrals, and the Closed Loop

Food, housing, transportation and utilities are the four social domains this program addresses, and a care manager records one as a **social need** against any `enrollment_id` — whatever that enrollment's `enrollment_status`, and whoever carries it, no ownership or lifecycle gate applying here — naming its `domain` — `FOOD`, `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORTATION` or `UTILITIES` — and its assessed `severity` of `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`. The need opens in `IDENTIFIED` with `escalation_count` at 0, `identified_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `resolved_at` null. Needs may be listed and narrowed by `enrollment_id`, by `domain` or by `status`.

**Community resources** are the organizations on the receiving end. Plan operations registers one with a `name`, a non-empty `served_domains` array drawn from the same four social domains, a non-empty `service_state_codes` array of two-letter state codes, and `concurrent_slot_capacity`, an integer of at least 1 declaring how many referrals may have overlapping service intervals at once. An optional `contract_status` of `CONTRACTED`, `NOT_CONTRACTED` or `SUSPENDED` defaults to `CONTRACTED`. A new organization starts with `open_referral_count` and `closed_loop_count` both at 0; thereafter the first counts its referrals in `SENT` or `ACCEPTED` and the second counts those in `CLOSED_MET` or `CLOSED_UNMET`.

**Sending a referral** is a care manager's act, and it names the `social_need_id` being referred out, the `community_resource_id` receiving it, and a half-open service interval given by `service_start_at`, the inclusive start, and `service_end_at`, the exclusive end. The sending care manager's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `sent_by_care_manager_id`. Referrals may be listed and narrowed by `social_need_id`, `community_resource_id`, `sent_by_care_manager_id` or `status`.

A referral must clear a long gate list before it is persisted, and §2.6 rather than the order of this paragraph decides which failure is reported. The need itself must be referable before anything else is examined: its `status` must be `IDENTIFIED` or `UNMET_ESCALATED`, else `ERR_SOCIAL_NEED_NOT_REFERABLE` at rung 9, ahead of consent, the service window and every referential rule below. The member's `social_data_consent_status` must read `GRANTED`, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED` — this is the point at which social data would leave the plan. The service interval must be well formed, with `service_end_at` strictly after `service_start_at`, else `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_INVALID`, and must not be in the past, meaning `service_start_at` is at or after the logical clock, else `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_IN_PAST`. The receiving organization's `contract_status` must be `CONTRACTED`; a `NOT_CONTRACTED` or `SUSPENDED` organization receives nothing new, and the attempt returns `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_CONTRACTED`. The need's `domain` must appear in the organization's `served_domains` and the member's `state_code` must appear in its `service_state_codes`, else `ERR_RESOURCE_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`. And the need may not already carry another referral in `SENT` or `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_REFERRAL`.

**Resource concurrency.** The organization's capacity is checked on plain half-open intervals with no buffer of any kind, which is deliberately different geometry from the care-manager calendar. The occupants are the referrals against the same `community_resource_id` whose `status` is `SENT` or `ACCEPTED`; referrals in `DECLINED`, `CLOSED_MET` or `CLOSED_UNMET` have released their slot. The instants probed are the candidate's own `service_start_at` together with each occupant's `service_start_at` that falls strictly inside the candidate's interval. At each probe instant, count the candidate itself as one, add every occupant whose `service_start_at` is at or before the probe and whose `service_end_at` is strictly after it, and if that total ever exceeds the organization's `concurrent_slot_capacity`, reject the referral with `ERR_RESOURCE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the intervals are half-open, an occupant whose `service_end_at` equals the candidate's `service_start_at` never counts, so exact adjacency is always accepted no matter how small the capacity.

A persisted referral opens in `SENT` with `sent_at` stamped from the logical clock, `decision_at`, `decline_reason`, `outcome_note`, `closed_at` and `loop_closure_hours` all null. It increments the organization's `open_referral_count` by one, and it moves the social need to `REFERRED` — from `IDENTIFIED` on a first attempt, or from `UNMET_ESCALATED` when a previously failed loop is being tried again.

**The decision fork.** Only the receiving organization decides, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and only on a referral in `SENT` — anything else is `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SENT`. `ACCEPT` moves the referral to `ACCEPTED` and stamps `decision_at`; the slot occupation is retained, because both `SENT` and `ACCEPTED` occupy, and `open_referral_count` is unchanged. `DECLINE` requires a present, non-empty `decline_reason` — a request-shape rule at rung 3, so a missing or blank reason answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the referral identifier is resolved — moves the referral to `DECLINED`, stamps `decision_at`, stores the reason, and — diverging sharply from the accept branch — releases the slot immediately and decrements the organization's `open_referral_count` by one, so a referral whose interval overlaps the declined one is accepted from that moment on.

**Closing the loop.** Both branches converge on closure, which supplies a `resolution` of `SERVICE_DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_NOT_DELIVERED` together with a present, non-empty `outcome_note`, a missing or blank note being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 3. A referral in `ACCEPTED` is closed by the receiving organization; a referral in `DECLINED` is closed by a care manager, who is the one who knows the service never happened — and the ownership check of rung 7 binds only a `CommunityPartner` caller, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the referral's `community_resource_id`, while any `CareManager` may close a referral whether or not they are the one who sent it. The closability gate reads the supplied `resolution` as well as the status, and admits exactly the three paths enumerated below: `ACCEPTED` under either resolution, and `DECLINED` under `SERVICE_NOT_DELIVERED` only. Everything else — a referral still in `SENT`, one already `CLOSED_MET` or `CLOSED_UNMET`, or a `DECLINED` referral offered `SERVICE_DELIVERED` — returns `ERR_REFERRAL_LOOP_NOT_CLOSABLE`. Every closure stamps `closed_at` and computes `loop_closure_hours` as the whole number of hours from `sent_at` to `closed_at`, truncating any partial hour toward zero, and increments the organization's `closed_loop_count` by one.

The three closure paths differ in what else they do. From `ACCEPTED` with `SERVICE_DELIVERED`, the referral becomes `CLOSED_MET`, the organization's `open_referral_count` drops by one, and the social need moves from `REFERRED` to `RESOLVED` with `resolved_at` stamped. From `ACCEPTED` with `SERVICE_NOT_DELIVERED`, the referral becomes `CLOSED_UNMET`, the organization's `open_referral_count` likewise drops by one, and the social need moves from `REFERRED` to `UNMET_ESCALATED`, starting the barrier cascade below. From `DECLINED` with `SERVICE_NOT_DELIVERED`, the referral also becomes `CLOSED_UNMET` and the need also escalates, but `open_referral_count` is **not** decremented a second time, because the decline already released that slot.

**The barrier cascade.** A need entering `UNMET_ESCALATED` runs three hops. Hop one sets the need's `status` to `UNMET_ESCALATED` and increases its `escalation_count` by one. Hop two, caused by hop one, finds every goal whose `domain` equals this need's `domain`, whose `status` is `OPEN`, and which sits on the `ACTIVE` care plan of this need's enrollment, and moves each from `OPEN` to `AT_RISK` with `barrier_flag` set to true. Hop three, caused by hop two, increases that plan's `at_risk_goal_count` by one for each such goal and sets its `compliance_posture` to `AT_RISK` where it had been `COMPLIANT`.

The reverse runs when the need is finally met. A need moving from `REFERRED` to `RESOLVED` returns every goal in the same `domain` on the same enrollment that currently sits in `AT_RISK` back to `OPEN`, clearing its `barrier_flag` to false — but only when no other social need on that enrollment in the same `domain` remains in `UNMET_ESCALATED`. Each restored goal decrements the plan's `at_risk_goal_count` by one, and the plan's `compliance_posture` is recomputed afterwards. The `UNMET_ESCALATED` → `REFERRED` path exists precisely so that a second referral attempt can be made against a different organization.

### 3.9. Proving It Worked: Outcome Measurement and the Accreditation File

The analytics function is the only role permitted to record an outcome measurement, and it does so against a single enrollment, naming a `method` of `PRE_POST` or `MATCHED_COHORT` and, as §2.8 requires, the `expected_enrollment_version` it last observed. The resulting record carries the `enrollment_id` measured, the `method` used, a `measurement_end_at` stamped from the logical clock at the moment of measurement, and the whole cost picture described below. Measurements may be listed for an enrollment newest first, optionally narrowed by `method`.

**The measured window.** The intervention period begins at the enrollment's `activated_at` and ends at `measurement_end_at`. Only *whole calendar months entirely inside that period* count: a month qualifies when its first instant is at or after the period's start and its last instant is at or before the period's end, so a partial month at either edge is dropped entirely. The number of qualifying months is `intervention_month_count`. If that count is zero — the enrollment was activated too recently for a single whole month to have elapsed, or, its `activated_at` being null, was never activated at all — nothing is recorded and the request is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD_EMPTY`. Beyond that there is no gate on the enrollment's `enrollment_status`: a `GRADUATED` or `DISENROLLED` enrollment measures exactly as an `ACTIVE` one does.

**What a month costs.** The actual cost of any month is the sum of `episode_cost_cents` over the member's utilization events whose `adjudication_status` is `CLOSED` and whose `occurred_at` falls at or after that month's start and strictly before the next month's start. Events still in `OPEN` are ignored on both sides of the comparison, throughout the entire measurement.

**Projecting what the month would have cost.** This is where the two methods diverge. Under `PRE_POST`, the baseline window is the 12 whole calendar months immediately preceding the month in which `activated_at` falls, ending where that month begins. Sum `episode_cost_cents` over the member's `CLOSED` utilization events inside that baseline window, divide by 12, and round half up to whole cents: that is `baseline_pmpm_cents`. Multiply that figure by a trend factor of 1.03 and round half up to whole cents to obtain `monthly_projected_cost_cents`. Under `MATCHED_COHORT`, the member's own history is ignored entirely: `baseline_pmpm_cents` is recorded as 0, and `monthly_projected_cost_cents` is the program's `cohort_benchmark_pmpm_cents` multiplied by a risk factor keyed to the enrollment's `risk_tier_at_enrollment` — 0.6 for `LOW`, 1.0 for `MODERATE`, 1.4 for `RISING` and 2.1 for `HIGH` — rounded half up to whole cents. For identical inputs the two methods report different `gross_savings_cents`, different `net_savings_cents` and a different `roi_ratio`, which is the point of offering both.

**The arithmetic, with its rounding order pinned.** `monthly_projected_cost_cents` is rounded once, per the paragraph above, and that single rounded figure is what every subsequent multiplication and subtraction uses. `projected_cost_cents` is `monthly_projected_cost_cents` multiplied by `intervention_month_count`. `actual_cost_cents` is the sum of the monthly actual costs across the qualifying months. `gross_savings_cents` is the sum, taken month by month across those same months, of `monthly_projected_cost_cents` less that month's actual cost — a figure that may legitimately be negative when the member cost more during the program than the projection expected. Summing unrounded monthly projections and rounding the total afterwards is not the declared order and produces a different cents figure.

**What the program spent.** `program_cost_cents` is the sum of the stored `cost_cents` values of every intervention in `COMPLETED` status that hangs beneath one of this enrollment's care plans, added exactly as each was stored at creation time. Interventions in `PLANNED` or `VOIDED` are excluded, and the figure is never recomputed from aggregate staff minutes. `net_savings_cents` is `gross_savings_cents` less `program_cost_cents`. Finally `roi_ratio` is `net_savings_cents` divided by `program_cost_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places — and is recorded as null when `program_cost_cents` is 0, rather than as an infinity or an error.

Recording a new measurement marks every prior measurement for the same `enrollment_id` and the same `method` with `superseded` set to true; a freshly written measurement always reads `superseded` false. A `PRE_POST` measurement never supersedes a `MATCHED_COHORT` one or vice versa. Recording a measurement increases the enrollment's `version` by one.

**The accreditation file** is a computed read available to plan operations and the analytics function, and it is the artifact an accrediting body actually inspects. It evaluates one program's *active* enrollments — those whose `enrollment_status` is `ACTIVE` — and nothing else; `PENDING`, `GRADUATED` and `DISENROLLED` enrollments are outside the audit sample entirely. The sample is examined in ascending `created_at` order. The response reports the `program_id` it covers, an `evaluated_at` instant taken from the logical clock, `active_enrollment_count` as the size of the sample, `enrollments_with_deficiency_count` as the number of sampled enrollments carrying at least one deficiency, `compliance_rate_pct`, a `deficiency_counts` object giving the number of sampled enrollments exhibiting each code — always carrying all six keys, a code no sampled enrollment exhibits reporting 0 rather than being omitted, and each enrollment contributing at most 1 to a code however many times it exhibits it — and `deficient_enrollment_ids` listing, in that same ascending `created_at` order, the identifiers of the enrollments that carry at least one code.

Six deficiency codes may be raised against a sampled enrollment. `CARE_PLAN_MISSING` is raised when no care plan on that enrollment is in `ACTIVE` status. `CARE_PLAN_STALE` is raised when a care plan on the enrollment is in `ACTIVE` status and its `next_review_due_at` is earlier than the current logical-clock instant. `TOUCH_OVERDUE` is raised when the enrollment's `next_touch_due_at` is not null and is earlier than that instant. `TOC_OUTREACH_MISSED` is raised when the enrollment's `toc_due_at` is not null and is earlier than that instant. `REFERRAL_LOOP_OPEN` is raised when a referral reached through the enrollment's social needs is still in `SENT` or `ACCEPTED` and its `sent_at` advanced by the program's `referral_loop_days` days is earlier than that instant. `MISSING_CONTACT_NOTE` is raised when a touch on the enrollment is in `COMPLETED` status with an `outcome` of `REACHED` and yet its `documentation_note` is null or empty.

`compliance_rate_pct` is the share of the sample that is entirely clean: subtract `enrollments_with_deficiency_count` from `active_enrollment_count`, multiply by 100, divide by `active_enrollment_count`, and round half up to one decimal place. When `active_enrollment_count` is 0 the rate is pinned to 100.0 rather than dividing by zero — an empty audit sample is vacuously compliant.
