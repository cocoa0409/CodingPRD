# Direct-to-Consumer Hearing Aid & Remote Fitting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Selling a hearing aid directly to a consumer without an audiologist in the room is only lawful when three things are simultaneously true: the buyer's self-administered screening produced no finding that legally demands a licensed evaluation, the buyer's state of residence permits over-the-counter sale and (where relevant) remote telehealth fitting, and the money that changes hands when the buyer sends the device back obeys that same state's trial-and-restocking arithmetic. This service is the regulatory and financial spine behind that promise. It carries a consumer from an in-app hearing screening, through candidacy scoring and — when a red flag appears — a mandatory referral to a licensed audiologist, into an order that pins its governing state policy at checkout, a delivery that starts a statutory trial clock, remote programming appointments with a hearing professional, dome-and-tip fit exchanges, mail-in repairs, loss-and-damage warranty claims, insurance and flexible-spending reimbursement paperwork, a subscription care plan, and staged firmware update campaigns.

Two properties dominate every design decision. The first is **auditability**: every forward-moving action is gated by an explicit regulatory predicate, and every cent that moves must be reconstructible after the fact from stored counters rather than recomputed from live configuration. A state regulator amending its restocking rule next month must not retroactively change the refund owed on an order placed today, which is why an order captures a versioned snapshot of its governing policy at checkout and settles against that snapshot forever. The second is **arithmetic determinism**: the same returned device yields two materially different refunds depending on which restocking variant its state prescribes, and the difference is sensitive to the order in which values are rounded. Rounding order is therefore normative in this document, not incidental.

Nine operator and consumer roles share the surface, and several of the most consequential actions are deliberately split between two people who must not be the same person: the compliance officer who files a medical-disqualification override cannot approve it, the reimbursement specialist who submits a claim cannot approve its payment, and an audiologist who voided a patient's screening for data quality cannot later be the one who clinically clears the referral that screening triggered. Engineers reading this document should treat those separations, the cascades that fan out from a single red-flag finding, and the conservation invariants on cash and inventory as the load-bearing requirements — the rest of the surface exists to serve them.

All paths in this document are relative to the API mount point `/hearing-care`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or blank, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine role names below, spelled exactly, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the acting role is permitted on the endpoint but the endpoint is not listed for that role, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `Patient`, `HearingProfessional`, `LicensedAudiologist`, `CareOpsAgent`, `ReimbursementSpecialist`, `RepairTechnician`, `FirmwareEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin`.

Three of these are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Patient` compare the resource's `patient_id` field to `X-User-ID`, including transitively through `device_order_id`, `trial_period_id` or `device_id` when the target resource has no direct `patient_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `HearingProfessional`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's `provider_id` field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `LicensedAudiologist`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Provider`, with the same `provider_id` comparison. The distinction between the two provider roles is clinical scope, not identity resolution: a caller may only act as `LicensedAudiologist` when the `Provider` row named by `X-User-ID` has `provider_type` equal to `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST`.

If an entity-backed role's `X-User-ID` matches no row of its backing entity, or matches a `Patient` whose `status` is `DEACTIVATED` or a `Provider` whose `status` is `OFFBOARDED`, the request fails with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 401). A `Patient` acting on a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403); a `HearingProfessional` or `LicensedAudiologist` acting on a session or referral whose `provider_id` is not the caller is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROVIDER` (HTTP 403); and a caller whose `Provider.provider_type` is `TELECARE_FITTER` attempting a clearance determination or a screening invalidation is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The remaining six roles — `CareOpsAgent`, `ReimbursementSpecialist`, `RepairTechnician`, `FirmwareEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin` — use **opaque** identities. For each of these, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a `ReimbursementClaim`, and as `filed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a `ComplianceOverride` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded values are what make segregation of duties enforceable (§2.10).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or an `offset` below 0 is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); both must be integers.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative in §3 pins a different ordering for a computed collection. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting index), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is below `total_count`).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource exposes an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Every other instant-valued field the API emits is serialized in exactly that same form — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, the three fractional digits always present even when the instant carries no sub-second part — including the logical-clock stamps listed below and every stored or computed instant such as `expires_at`, `started_at`, `nominal_end_utc`, `effective_end_utc`, `return_deadline_utc`, `warranty_expires_at`, `cycle_start_utc`, `cycle_end_utc`, `scheduled_start_utc`, `scheduled_end_utc`, `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc` and the `pause_start_utc`/`pause_end_utc` members of a trial's `pause_intervals`. Almost every resource additionally exposes `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation and formatted identically, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every mutation of that row. The single exception is `RedFlagFinding`: a finding is an immutable audit event, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a finding, and no endpoint mutates one after emission. Every other entity in the system — `StatePolicy`, `Patient`, `ScreeningSession`, `Provider`, `ProviderReferral`, `RemoteFittingSession`, `DeviceOrder`, `Device`, `TrialPeriod`, `ReturnAuthorization`, `FitExchange`, `InventoryPool`, `RepairOrder`, `WarrantyClaim`, `ReimbursementClaim`, `CarePlanSubscription`, `FirmwareCampaign`, `FirmwareRollout` and `ComplianceOverride` — carries all four common properties.

All time in this system is read from a **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that instant until an administrator moves it. `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` accepts a `to_iso8601` value and sets the clock to that instant. The clock is the only source for every field this document calls a "logical-clock instant": `submitted_at`, `scored_at`, `detected_at`, `issued_at`, `completed_at`, `delivered_at`, `shipped_at`, `received_at`, `settled_at`, `closed_at`, `triaged_at`, `installed_at`, `filed_at`, `decided_at`, `deactivated_at` and `waiver_acknowledged_at`.

Three deadline-bearing entities expire **lazily**: a `ProviderReferral` whose `expires_at` the clock has reached, a `ReturnAuthorization` whose `return_deadline_utc` the clock has passed, and a `TrialPeriod` whose `effective_end_utc` the clock has passed all transition on the next read or write that touches them, not at the moment the clock advanced. Reading a single trial (`GET /trial-periods/{id}`) additionally recomputes `days_used`, `paused_days` and `effective_end_utc` before returning, so a read is never stale with respect to the clock.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Regulatory Version Pinning

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. For `POST /patients/{patient_id}/compliance-overrides/{override_id}/approve`, the `patient_id` segment is resolved first and the `override_id` segment second; the first segment that names no existing row produces `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) without the later segments being consulted. The same left-to-right rule governs `/patients/{patient_id}/screening-sessions`, `/trial-periods/{trial_period_id}/return-authorizations`, `/device-orders/{device_order_id}/fit-exchanges`, `/firmware-campaigns/{campaign_id}/rollouts`, `/firmware-campaigns/{campaign_id}/manual-enrollments` and `/care-plan-subscriptions/{subscription_id}/change-tier`, whose nested segments are named `patient_id`, `trial_period_id`, `device_order_id`, `campaign_id` and `subscription_id` respectively; a single-resource path segment is always spelled `id`. A foreign key supplied in a request body rather than a path — `state_policy_id`, `patient_id`, `provider_id`, `device_order_id`, `device_id`, `provider_referral_id`, `repair_order_id`, `care_plan_subscription_id`, `originating_repair_order_id`, `appeal_of_claim_id`, `firmware_rollout_id`, `supersedes_session_id` — that resolves to no row produces `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Regulatory configuration is **version-pinned at checkout**. When an order is placed, the server copies the purchaser's governing policy identity and content onto the order: `state_policy_id`, `pinned_state_policy_version` (the policy's `version` as observed at that moment), `pinned_trial_days` (a copy of the policy's `trial_period_days`) and `pinned_restocking_mode` (a copy of the policy's `restocking_fee_mode`). Amending a policy through `PATCH /state-policies/{id}` increments the policy's own `version` by one and does **not** touch the pinned fields of any existing order. Soft-retiring a policy through `DELETE /state-policies/{id}` sets `is_retired` to true and increments `version`; retired policies remain readable and remain pinned on existing orders, but assigning one to a new or amended patient (`POST /patients`, `PATCH /patients/{id}`) or placing a new order against one (`POST /patients/{patient_id}/device-orders`) is rejected with `ERR_STATE_POLICY_RETIRED` (HTTP 409). Every restocking computation reads the pinned policy row at the pinned version; live edits made after checkout are excluded from the arithmetic.

### 2.6. Money, Precision and the Cash-Conservation Ledger

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents. Wherever a computation produces a fractional value, it is rounded **half up**. Two non-monetary quantities carry declared precision as well: `candidacy_risk_score`, `advisory_weight_total`, `utilization_pct` and `failure_rate_pct` are all carried to one decimal place, rounded half up. Day counts and minute counts are whole numbers; unless a specific rule says "rounded up", they are truncated toward zero (floor).

Rounding **order** is normative and differs by calculation. In restocking settlement, each component is rounded to the cent independently and only then summed; summing first and rounding the total is forbidden and produces a different number of cents. In candidacy scoring, rounding is applied exactly once at the very end, never to an intermediate term. In warranty deductibles, rounding is applied to the multiplicative part only, and the integer surcharge is added afterwards. In care-plan billing, rounding applies to the loyalty discount and then to the proration, in that order. The relevant §3 narratives state each of these explicitly.

Every `DeviceOrder` carries four cash counters — `captured_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `retained_cents` and `open_balance_cents` — and they satisfy a **cash-conservation identity** at every observable moment: the amount captured equals the amount refunded plus the amount lawfully retained plus the open balance still attributable to a kept device. `open_balance_cents` is defined as `captured_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus `retained_cents`. Issuing and then voiding a return authorization leaves the identity untouched. Waiving previously retained restocking moves value from `retained_cents` into `refunded_cents` without changing `captured_cents`. Similarly, every `ReimbursementClaim` satisfies a **benefit-conservation identity**: `insurance_paid_cents` plus `fsa_paid_cents` plus `patient_responsibility_cents` always equals `eligible_amount_cents`, in every state including denial and void. And every `InventoryPool` satisfies an **inventory-conservation identity**: `on_hand_units` plus `reserved_units` plus `dispatched_units` always equals `total_units_received`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency: Stale-Version Guards

Four write endpoints are guarded against a client acting on a parent record it has not observed in its current form. In each case the guard field is a required (non-optional) member of the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the named parent, and a mismatch with that parent's current `version` rejects the request. Endpoints not named here take no version field.

- `POST /trial-periods/{trial_period_id}/return-authorizations` requires `expected_trial_period_version` matching the target `TrialPeriod`'s current `version`. If another writer has advanced the trial's version since the client last read it — a superseding authorization after a void, or a granted goodwill extension that shifted `effective_end_utc` — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a writer working from the older view would snapshot `days_used_at_issue` from a superseded picture of the trial.
- `POST /device-orders/{device_order_id}/fit-exchanges` requires `expected_device_order_version` matching the target `DeviceOrder`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A completed exchange legitimately rewrites `dome_size` and `tip_type`, so a writer holding an earlier view would reserve against the wrong source pool and break inventory conservation.
- `POST /care-plan-subscriptions/{subscription_id}/change-tier` requires `expected_subscription_version` matching the target `CarePlanSubscription`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A billing-cycle run, an SLA credit posted by a closed repair, and a waived no-show fee all legitimately move `credit_balance_cents` and the cycle window, so a proration computed from an earlier view would be measured against a superseded cycle boundary.
- `POST /firmware-campaigns/{campaign_id}/manual-enrollments` requires `expected_device_version` matching the target `Device`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful install rewrites `firmware_version` and a warranty replacement rewrites `status` and `replaced_by_device_id`, so an enrollment computed from an earlier view would pin a `from_version_observed` that no longer matches the unit.

### 2.8. Cascades, Derived Fields and Immediate Consistency

All cascades run synchronously inside the request that triggers them; nothing is deferred to a background worker. When a transition dirties a derived field, that field is recomputed before the response is emitted. The derived fields that behave this way are `Patient.candidacy_risk_score`, `Patient.candidacy_risk_band`, `Patient.candidacy_risk_variant`, `Patient.advisory_weight_total`, `Patient.open_mandatory_flag_count`, `Patient.screening_attempt_count`, `Patient.lifetime_return_count`, `ScreeningSession.attempt_ordinal`, `Provider.booked_minutes_on_focus_date`, `Provider.utilization_pct`, `Provider.completed_session_count`, `ProviderReferral.expires_at`, `ProviderReferral.reschedule_count`, `ProviderReferral.cleared_finding_count`, `RemoteFittingSession.duration_minutes`, `DeviceOrder.tax_cents`, `DeviceOrder.total_cents`, the four order cash counters, `Device.warranty_expires_at`, `Device.age_months_at_last_claim`, `Device.loss_damage_claims_approved`, `TrialPeriod.nominal_end_utc`, `TrialPeriod.effective_end_utc`, `TrialPeriod.paused_days`, `TrialPeriod.days_used`, the `ReturnAuthorization` settlement outputs, `FitExchange.exchange_ordinal`, `FitExchange.exchange_fee_cents`, the four `InventoryPool` counters, `RepairOrder.sla_days`, `RepairOrder.turnaround_days`, `RepairOrder.sla_credit_cents`, `RepairOrder.pauses_trial`, `WarrantyClaim.device_age_months`, `WarrantyClaim.claim_ordinal`, `WarrantyClaim.deductible_cents`, `WarrantyClaim.deductible_paid_cents`, the `ReimbursementClaim` allocation buckets and `documentation_complete` and `info_request_count`, the `CarePlanSubscription` charge fields and `cycle_ordinal` and `dunning_attempts`, `FirmwareCampaign.rollout_count`, `FirmwareCampaign.installed_count`, `FirmwareCampaign.failure_rate_pct`, `FirmwareRollout.selection_rank` and `FirmwareRollout.attempt_count`.

Two multi-hop cascades are named throughout this document because their later hops are the easiest to overlook.

**The return-settlement cascade** fires when a care operator settles an inspected return authorization. Hop 1 moves the `TrialPeriod` from `RETURN_INITIATED` to `RETURN_SETTLED` and increments `Patient.lifetime_return_count`. Hop 2 moves the `DeviceOrder`'s `fulfillment_status` to `RETURNED` and its `payment_status` to either `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED`, updating `refunded_cents`, `retained_cents` and `open_balance_cents`. Hop 3 sets the `Device.status` to `RETIRED` while retaining its `device_order_id` for audit. Hop 4 moves every `CarePlanSubscription` attached to that order to `CANCELLED` and forfeits its `credit_balance_cents` by setting it to zero. Hop 5 moves every `FirmwareRollout` on that device whose status is `PENDING` or `OFFERED` to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` and recomputes the owning campaign's `rollout_count`, `installed_count` and `failure_rate_pct`.

**The medical-hold cascade** fires when a scored screening drives a patient into `REFERRAL_REQUIRED`. Hop 1 opens a `ProviderReferral` in `OPEN`. Hop 2 moves every `DeviceOrder` of that patient whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM` or `READY_TO_SHIP` to `MEDICAL_HOLD` with `medical_hold_reason` set to `MANDATORY_RED_FLAG`. Hop 3 moves every `RemoteFittingSession` in `SCHEDULED` whose `session_type` is `INITIAL_FIT` or `FOLLOW_UP` and which references one of those orders to `CANCELLED`, with `no_show_charged_cents` forced to 0 — a medical hold never costs the patient a fee. Hop 4 recomputes `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` and `utilization_pct` for every provider whose bookings hop 3 released.

Mutating a resource always refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`; a cascade that mutates a second resource refreshes that second resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version` too, which is precisely why the stale-version guards in §2.7 can detect an unobserved cascade.

### 2.9. Request Evaluation Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the rule with the lowest priority number below wins and its error is the one returned. The ordering is deliberate for a regulated medical-device channel: authentication first, then authorization, then existence, then request shape, then stale-version detection, then state legality, then the business bands in the order medical safety and state regulation, constraint and temporal, capacity and quota, and finally fiscal — a patient must never be told "payment declined" for an order that was medically prohibited in the first place.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the nine declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an existing backing row (a `Patient` or a `Provider`) that is neither `DEACTIVATED` nor `OFFBOARDED`. This applies to every endpoint called as `Patient`, `HearingProfessional` or `LicensedAudiologist` — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (401).
4. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
5. An acting `Patient`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `patient_id` of the targeted resource, directly or transitively through `device_order_id`, `trial_period_id` or `device_id`. This applies to every `Patient`-callable endpoint that targets an existing resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
6. An acting `HearingProfessional` or `LicensedAudiologist` `X-User-ID` equals the targeted `RemoteFittingSession.provider_id` or `ProviderReferral.provider_id`. This applies to `POST /remote-fitting-sessions/{id}/start`, `POST /remote-fitting-sessions/{id}/complete`, `POST /remote-fitting-sessions/{id}/no-show` and `POST /provider-referrals/{id}/determination` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROVIDER` (403).
7. The acting `Provider`'s `provider_type` is `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST` for clearance and invalidation operations, namely `POST /provider-referrals/{id}/determination` and `POST /screening-sessions/{id}/invalidate` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
8. Segregation of duties on approval: the approving `X-User-ID` differs from `ReimbursementClaim.submitted_by_user_id`, and the approving `ComplianceOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the `filed_by_user_id` of the compliance override. This applies to `POST /reimbursement-claims/{id}/decide` and `POST /patients/{patient_id}/compliance-overrides/{override_id}/approve` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. Segregation of duties on clinical clearance: the clearing provider is not the provider who invalidated the referral's `triggering_screening_session_id`. This applies to `POST /provider-referrals/{id}/determination` and to `POST /remote-fitting-sessions` when `session_type` is `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` (403).
10. Every path-segment resource exists, validated left to right, on every endpoint with path parameters — otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
11. Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row, on every POST and PATCH endpoint that accepts foreign keys — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
12. A referenced `StatePolicy` has `is_retired` false when it is being newly assigned, on `POST /patients`, `PATCH /patients/{id}` and `POST /patients/{patient_id}/device-orders`; already-pinned retired policies stay valid on existing orders — otherwise `ERR_STATE_POLICY_RETIRED` (409).
13. An `InventoryPool` matching the requested `model`, `dome_size` and `tip_type` triple exists, on `POST /device-orders/{id}/confirm-fit` and `POST /device-orders/{device_order_id}/fit-exchanges` — otherwise `ERR_INVENTORY_POOL_NOT_FOUND` (404).
14. `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0, on every list endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
15. Every required body field is present with its declared type and inside its declared numeric bounds, on every POST and PATCH endpoint — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). This also covers a `scheduled_end_utc` that is not strictly after `scheduled_start_utc`, and any negative money or day count.
16. Every supplied enum value is a member of its field's declared value set, on every POST and PATCH endpoint with enum inputs — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422).
17. Natural keys are unused — `Patient.email`, `Provider.npi`, `Device.serial_number`, `InventoryPool.sku`, `FirmwareCampaign.campaign_code`, `StatePolicy.state_code`, and the server-minted `ReturnAuthorization.rma_code` — on `POST /patients`, `POST /providers`, `POST /devices`, `POST /inventory-pools`, `POST /firmware-campaigns` and `POST /state-policies` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409).
18. `expected_trial_period_version` equals `TrialPeriod.version`, on `POST /trial-periods/{trial_period_id}/return-authorizations` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TRIAL_VERSION` (412).
19. `expected_device_order_version` equals `DeviceOrder.version`, on `POST /device-orders/{device_order_id}/fit-exchanges` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (412).
20. `expected_subscription_version` equals `CarePlanSubscription.version`, on `POST /care-plan-subscriptions/{subscription_id}/change-tier` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` (412).
21. `expected_device_version` equals `Device.version`, on `POST /firmware-campaigns/{campaign_id}/manual-enrollments` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (412). Stale-version detection deliberately runs **before** state legality, so a stale writer is told about staleness rather than about a state it cannot yet see.
22. The targeted entity's current state is a declared origin state for the requested transition, on every action endpoint that drives a state transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
23. The targeted entity is not already in a terminal state for its machine, on every action endpoint that drives a state transition — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409).
24. The sibling entity named by the transition's precondition is in an allowed state — the order's `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED` before an `INITIAL_FIT` session may start, the campaign's `status` is `ROLLING` before a rollout may be offered or installed, and the order's trial is not `RETURN_SETTLED` before a suspended plan may be reinstated. This applies to `POST /remote-fitting-sessions/{id}/start`, `POST /firmware-rollouts/{id}/offer`, `POST /firmware-rollouts/{id}/install` and `POST /care-plan-subscriptions/{id}/reinstate` — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_NOT_READY` (409).
25. The pinned policy's `otc_sales_permitted` is true, on `POST /patients/{patient_id}/device-orders` — otherwise `ERR_OTC_SALES_PROHIBITED_IN_STATE` (422).
26. The patient's `age_years` is at least the policy's `min_age_years`, on `POST /patients/{patient_id}/device-orders` and `POST /device-orders/{id}/confirm-fit` — otherwise `ERR_AGE_BELOW_STATE_MINIMUM` (422).

Four dual-failure scenarios are worth stating explicitly because implementers commonly get them backwards. A billing-cycle run against a cancelled subscription with a declining instrument violates both rule 22 and rule 62; rule 22 wins, so the caller sees `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A fit confirmation for a patient with an open mandatory red flag whose model-dome-tip triple has no pool row violates both rule 28 and rule 13; rule 13 wins, so the caller sees `ERR_INVENTORY_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, because referential existence is checked before the safety band. A return request made after `effective_end_utc` on an order whose device is currently in repair violates both rule 34 and rule 40; rule 34 wins, so the caller sees `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And a patient cancelling another patient's shipped exchange violates both rule 5 and rule 22; rule 5 wins, so the caller sees `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three separations are enforced structurally rather than by policy documentation. First, a medical-disqualification override is dual-control: the `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `filed_by_user_id` cannot be the one whose `X-User-ID` would be recorded as `approved_by_user_id`, on either the approve or the reject path. Second, a reimbursement claim's `submitted_by_user_id` cannot equal the `X-User-ID` deciding it, on approve and on deny alike. Third, a `LicensedAudiologist` who invalidated a screening session for data quality is permanently excluded from the referral triggered by that session — both from being selected or booked for its evaluation and from recording its determination. The server therefore records which provider performed each screening invalidation, and consults that record whenever a referral names that session as its `triggering_screening_session_id`.

### 2.11. Status Codes

Creation endpoints return 201 with the newly created resource. Every other successful operation, including action endpoints that drive a state transition, returns 200 with the affected resource. Errors carry the HTTP status listed against their `error_id` in §5.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Patient Under a State Policy

Regulatory configuration is the root of everything else, so a jurisdiction must be described before a resident of it can be registered. A platform operator creates a policy row identified by a `state_code`, a two-character uppercase US state code that is globally unique. The policy declares whether over-the-counter self-fit devices may be sold to residents (`otc_sales_permitted`, defaulting to true) and whether a remote programming session may be conducted by an out-of-state provider (`telehealth_fitting_permitted`, also defaulting to true). It sets the `min_age_years` floor for candidacy, which defaults to 18, and the statutory `trial_period_days` minimum, which must be at least 30 and at most 90. It declares whether the patient must acknowledge a medical-evaluation waiver before ordering (`waiver_required`, default false), how many days a licensed-provider clearance stays valid (`referral_validity_days`, default 180 — a little under six months), and the lifetime `max_loss_damage_claims` ceiling per device, which defaults to 2.

The commercially interesting part of a policy is its return economics. `restocking_fee_mode` is one of `FLAT_PCT`, `PRORATED_DAILY` or `PROHIBITED`. Under a percentage regime the policy's `restocking_fee_pct` — a fraction between 0 and 0.20 inclusive, carried to four decimal places and defaulting to 0 — is the share of the device subtotal the seller may keep. Under a daily regime the policy's `restocking_daily_rate_cents` — a non-negative integer, defaulting to 0 — is what may be kept for each counted trial day. Either way, `restocking_fee_cap_cents` (non-negative, defaulting to 0) is an absolute ceiling on what a single return may retain, where a stored 0 means "no cap at all" rather than "retain nothing". `shipping_refundable` decides whether the outbound shipping charge comes back to the patient on a trial return, and it defaults to false. A policy is never hard-deleted; retiring one sets `is_retired` (a system-managed flag defaulting to false), leaving it queryable and leaving it in force on any order that already pinned it.

Registering a patient supplies the governing `state_policy_id` along with `email`, `full_name` and `age_years`. The email is globally unique and compared case-insensitively; the declared age must be between 0 and 120 inclusive. The server denormalizes `state_code` onto the patient from the chosen policy and refreshes that copy whenever `state_policy_id` changes on an amendment. A freshly registered patient's `status` is `REGISTERED`, `candidacy_risk_score` is 0, `candidacy_risk_band` is `LOW`, `candidacy_risk_variant` is `PROVISIONAL_SELF_REPORTED`, `advisory_weight_total` is 0, `open_mandatory_flag_count` is 0, `lifetime_return_count` is 0, `screening_attempt_count` is 0, and both `waiver_acknowledged_at` and `deactivated_at` are null. Where the governing policy demands it, a patient acknowledges the medical-evaluation waiver in a dedicated self-service action that stamps `waiver_acknowledged_at` with the current logical clock.

A patient's candidacy `status` moves through `REGISTERED`, `SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS`, `CANDIDATE_APPROVED`, `REFERRAL_REQUIRED`, `PROVIDER_CLEARED`, `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED` and the terminal `DEACTIVATED`. Deactivation is available to a platform operator from `REGISTERED`, from `CANDIDATE_APPROVED` and from `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED`, and in all three cases requires that the patient hold no `DeviceOrder` whose `fulfillment_status` is anything other than `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED`. All three set `deactivated_at` to the current logical clock; the `CANDIDATE_APPROVED` origin additionally invalidates every screening still in `DRAFT` by moving it to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` set to `DATA_QUALITY`.

### 3.2. Self-Administered Screening, Red Flags and Candidacy Scoring

A patient starts a screening themselves. Starting one from `REGISTERED` requires that the patient's governing policy is not retired, moves the patient from `REGISTERED` to `SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS`, and creates a `ScreeningSession` in `DRAFT` with `attempt_ordinal` 1. Because `screening_attempt_count` counts only sessions that reached `SCORED` or `INVALIDATED`, opening a draft leaves that counter alone. On a retake the payload carries `supersedes_session_id`, naming the earlier session this attempt replaces; a first attempt leaves it null. In general, a session's `attempt_ordinal` is one more than the number of prior sessions for the same `patient_id` ordered by `created_at` ascending, and is always at least 1.

Filling in a draft records five measurements and moves the session from `DRAFT` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and may only be done by the patient who owns it: the four-frequency `pure_tone_avg_db` (between 0 and 120 inclusive, one decimal place), the absolute left/right `asymmetry_db` difference (same range and precision), the questionnaire's `self_reported_difficulty_score` (an integer from 0 to 40 inclusive, higher meaning more perceived difficulty), `speech_in_noise_pct` (percent words correct on the in-app task, 0 to 100 inclusive, one decimal place), and the array of declared `symptom_codes`, which defaults to empty.

Submitting the session requires all five measurement fields to be non-null and the patient to be in `SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS`; it stamps `submitted_at`, advances the session `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED`, and then — inside the very same request — scores it and advances `SUBMITTED → SCORED`, stamping `scored_at`. Scoring runs a **finding detection table in exactly this order**, appending a `RedFlagFinding` for each condition that holds, each finding carrying a `code`, a `severity` and a `weight`, and each stamped with `detected_at` from the logical clock. A declared symptom of sudden loss emits `SUDDEN_UNILATERAL_LOSS` at severity `MANDATORY_REFERRAL` with weight 0.0. A declared drainage symptom emits `ACTIVE_DRAINAGE`, mandatory, weight 0.0. A declared vertigo symptom emits `VERTIGO`, mandatory, weight 0.0. A declared deformity symptom emits `VISIBLE_DEFORMITY`, mandatory, weight 0.0. A declared pain symptom emits `EAR_PAIN` at severity `ADVISORY` with weight 1.5. A declared one-ear tinnitus symptom emits `UNILATERAL_TINNITUS`, advisory, weight 1.0. A declared wax symptom emits `IMPACTED_CERUMEN`, advisory, weight 1.0. Then, if the patient's `age_years` is strictly below the policy's `min_age_years`, the table emits `AGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, mandatory, weight 0.0. If `pure_tone_avg_db` is at least 71.0 — the boundary itself counts as severe — it emits `SEVERE_LOSS_THRESHOLD`, mandatory, weight 0.0. Finally, if `asymmetry_db` is at least 15.0 — again inclusive — it emits `SUDDEN_UNILATERAL_LOSS`, mandatory, weight 0.0; when both the sudden-loss symptom and the asymmetry threshold are present, exactly one finding with that code is emitted, not two, because findings are deduplicated on `code`. Every `RedFlagFinding` records the `screening_session_id` that produced it and leaves `cleared_by_referral_id` null while the finding is open. Findings from other sessions never contribute to this session's outcome; the input set is exactly the submitting session's own fields plus the patient's `age_years` and their policy's `min_age_years`.

The session's `screening_outcome` — `PENDING` until scoring, then one of `PASS`, `ADVISORY_PASS` or `RED_FLAG` — follows from two aggregates. The mandatory count is the number of emitted findings at severity `MANDATORY_REFERRAL`. The advisory sum is the total of the `weight` values of the emitted advisory findings, rounded half up to one decimal place **before** any comparison. If the mandatory count is greater than zero, the outcome is `RED_FLAG`. Otherwise, if the advisory sum is at least 2.5 — the threshold is inclusive, so a pain finding plus a wax finding, totalling 2.5, is a red flag — the outcome is also `RED_FLAG`. Otherwise, if the advisory sum is greater than zero, the outcome is `ADVISORY_PASS`. Otherwise, with an empty finding set, the outcome is `PASS`.

A `PASS` or `ADVISORY_PASS` moves the patient `SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS → CANDIDATE_APPROVED`, provided there are no mandatory findings, the advisory sum is below 2.5, and the patient's `age_years` is at least the policy's `min_age_years`. That transition recomputes `candidacy_risk_score` under the `PROVISIONAL_SELF_REPORTED` variant, sets `candidacy_risk_variant` to `PROVISIONAL_SELF_REPORTED`, derives `candidacy_risk_band` from the new score, refreshes `advisory_weight_total`, sets `open_mandatory_flag_count` to 0, and increments `screening_attempt_count`.

Candidacy risk is a weighted composite of the patient's latest non-invalidated screening — specifically the session with `status` `SCORED` ordered by `scored_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, with `INVALIDATED` sessions excluded entirely. Four normalized terms are formed. The pure-tone term is `pure_tone_avg_db` divided by 90, capped at 1.0. The difficulty term is `self_reported_difficulty_score` divided by 40. The speech-in-noise term is 1.0 minus `speech_in_noise_pct` divided by 100, so poorer word recognition raises risk. The asymmetry term is `asymmetry_db` divided by 25, capped at 1.0. The weighted base is 100 multiplied by the sum of 0.40 times the pure-tone term, 0.25 times the difficulty term, 0.20 times the speech-in-noise term and 0.15 times the asymmetry term. Under the provisional variant — written by a screening submit that approved candidacy, before any provider has examined the patient — the score is that base rounded half up to one decimal place, with red-flag evidence excluded entirely and no attenuation applied. Under the provider-adjusted variant — written by a licensed clearance — the base is first attenuated to 85 percent of itself, then increased by 6.0 points for each mandatory finding the clearing referral retired and by 2.0 points for each unit of the patient's `advisory_weight_total` as observed when the referral was issued; the resulting figure is capped at 100.0 and then rounded half up to one decimal place. Rounding happens exactly once, at the end, and never to an intermediate term. Identical screening measurements therefore yield a different score under the two variants, which is intended. The `candidacy_risk_band` is `LOW` when the score is strictly below 35.0, `MODERATE` when it is at least 35.0 and strictly below 65.0, and `HIGH` when it is at least 65.0.

A session leaves the pipeline in three other ways. A licensed audiologist may void an `IN_PROGRESS` session for data quality, moving it to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_reason` `DATA_QUALITY`, provided the acting provider's `provider_type` is `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST`, their `status` is `ACTIVE`, and their `licensed_state_codes` contains the patient's `state_code`; an optional `reason_note` may accompany the action, the invalidating provider's identity is recorded for the later conflict-of-interest test, and the patient's `advisory_weight_total` and `open_mandatory_flag_count` are recomputed. A `SCORED` session becomes `INVALIDATED` when a newer session names it through `supersedes_session_id` or when a compliance override is approved, with `invalidated_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED` or `PROVIDER_OVERRIDE` respectively; either way, that session's still-open findings stop contributing to `advisory_weight_total`. And a `DRAFT` session becomes `INVALIDATED` with reason `DATA_QUALITY` as part of the patient-deactivation cascade. `INVALIDATED` is terminal.

Two patient-level counters are worth restating because they gate ordering. `open_mandatory_flag_count` is the number of findings at severity `MANDATORY_REFERRAL` whose `cleared_by_referral_id` is still null, counted across every screening session whose `status` is not `INVALIDATED`. `advisory_weight_total` is the sum of `weight` over findings at severity `ADVISORY` whose `cleared_by_referral_id` is null, restricted to the latest non-invalidated session, carried to one decimal place.

### 3.3. Referral, Clearance and Dual-Control Overrides

A `RED_FLAG` outcome drives the patient into `REFERRAL_REQUIRED` — reachable from `SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS`, from `CANDIDATE_APPROVED` when a later attempt scores `RED_FLAG` and supersedes the previously scored session, and from `PROVIDER_CLEARED` when a new session produces at least one mandatory finding that is not already cleared. All three run the same medical-hold cascade described in §2.8, and the `CANDIDATE_APPROVED` origin additionally marks the superseded session `INVALIDATED` with reason `SUPERSEDED`. Hop 1 of that cascade opens a `ProviderReferral` in `OPEN`, populating `reason_codes` with the `code` values of the triggering findings ordered by their `detected_at` ascending, naming the `triggering_screening_session_id`, stamping `issued_at` from the logical clock, and computing `expires_at` as `issued_at` plus the patient's policy `referral_validity_days` days. A fresh referral has `provider_id` null, `reschedule_count` 0, `clearance_outcome` `PENDING` and `cleared_finding_count` 0.

Booking the evaluation moves the referral `OPEN → SCHEDULED` and stamps its `provider_id` with the booked provider. That booking requires the clock to be strictly before `expires_at`, the selected provider to have `provider_type` `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST` and `status` `ACTIVE` with the patient's `state_code` present in `licensed_state_codes`, and the selected provider not to be the one who invalidated the referral's `triggering_screening_session_id`. When the booking omits a provider, the server selects one: candidates are the providers whose `provider_type` is `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `licensed_state_codes` contains the patient's `state_code`, who did not perform the invalidation of the triggering session, and for whom the daily-capacity gate of §3.4 accepts the candidate slot. Providers with `provider_type` `TELECARE_FITTER`, providers in `SUSPENDED` or `OFFBOARDED`, and providers unlicensed in the patient's state are excluded. Candidates are ranked by their utilization percentage — 100 multiplied by `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` divided by `daily_capacity_minutes`, rounded half up to one decimal place before comparison — ascending, so the least-loaded audiologist is picked first, with `created_at` ascending and then `npi` ascending as tie-breaks; `id` is never used in the ordering. An empty candidate set is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PROVIDER`.

Should the evaluation appointment collapse — a no-show or a cancellation — the referral cycles `SCHEDULED → OPEN`, incrementing `reschedule_count` and clearing `provider_id` so a different audiologist may be booked. The cycle is capped: a booking attempted when `reschedule_count` has already reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_MAX_RESCHEDULES_EXCEEDED`. Completing the evaluation appointment moves the referral `SCHEDULED → EVALUATED` (requiring the session to be in progress and the referral not yet expired) and leaves `clearance_outcome` at `PENDING` awaiting the formal determination.

The determination itself is the clinical hinge of the whole system. Recording it supplies a `clearance_outcome` of `CLEARED` or `NOT_CLEARED` and optionally a free-text `clearance_note`, and may only be done by the audiologist whose `id` is the referral's `provider_id`, who must additionally not have invalidated the triggering session. A `CLEARED` determination moves the referral `EVALUATED → CLEARED` and requires the clock to be strictly before `expires_at`; it stamps `cleared_by_referral_id` on every still-open finding whose `code` appears in the referral's `reason_codes`, increments the referral's `cleared_finding_count` accordingly, recomputes the patient's `open_mandatory_flag_count` (which should now be zero) and `advisory_weight_total`, recomputes `candidacy_risk_score` under the `PROVIDER_ADJUSTED` variant and sets `candidacy_risk_variant` to `PROVIDER_ADJUSTED`, moves the patient `REFERRAL_REQUIRED → PROVIDER_CLEARED`, and releases every one of that patient's held orders from `MEDICAL_HOLD` back to `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM` with `medical_hold_reason` cleared — no inventory moves on release, because the reservation was already given back when the hold was applied.

A `NOT_CLEARED` determination moves the referral `EVALUATED → NOT_CLEARED` and drives the patient `REFERRAL_REQUIRED → MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED`. Every one of that patient's orders sitting in `MEDICAL_HOLD` moves to `CANCELLED`; each such order's `payment_status` moves `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED` if nothing was captured, or `CAPTURED → REFUNDED` with `refunded_cents` set to the full `captured_cents` and `retained_cents` set to 0 — a regulatory full refund retains nothing whatsoever. Every care plan attached to those orders moves to `CANCELLED`.

Referrals also die of old age and of oversight. An `OPEN` referral whose `expires_at` the clock has reached becomes `EXPIRED` on the next read or write touching it, leaving the patient in `REFERRAL_REQUIRED` but now free to retake a screening. A `SCHEDULED` referral that expires likewise becomes `EXPIRED`, and additionally cancels the linked evaluation session and recomputes that provider's `booked_minutes_on_focus_date`. A compliance officer may withdraw an `OPEN` referral, optionally with a `reason_note`, provided no session referencing it is in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; the referral becomes `WITHDRAWN`. `CLEARED`, `NOT_CLEARED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal. Once no referral for the patient remains in `OPEN`, `SCHEDULED` or `EVALUATED`, the patient may start a retake, which moves them `REFERRAL_REQUIRED → SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS` and creates a session whose `supersedes_session_id` is the latest `SCORED` session — the re-screen loop that lets a patient re-enter candidacy without any operator intervention.

A patient who has been medically disqualified can only re-enter screening through **dual control**. A compliance officer files a `ComplianceOverride` against the patient, supplying a required `justification` of between 20 and 2000 characters that is retained for audit. Filing requires the patient to be in `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED`, creates the override in `PENDING`, stamps `filed_at` from the logical clock, records the acting operator identity as the immutable `filed_by_user_id`, and leaves `approved_by_user_id` and `decided_at` null. A **different** compliance officer then approves it: approval requires the override to be `PENDING`, the acting operator identity to differ from `filed_by_user_id`, and the patient still to be `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED`. Approval moves the override `PENDING → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, moves the patient `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED → SCREENING_IN_PROGRESS`, invalidates the patient's latest `SCORED` screening with `invalidated_reason` `PROVIDER_OVERRIDE` and clears that session's findings, resets `open_mandatory_flag_count` to 0 and `advisory_weight_total` to 0.0, and creates a fresh screening in `DRAFT` whose `attempt_ordinal` is one more than `screening_attempt_count`. Rejection, likewise reserved for an officer other than the filer and optionally carrying a `reason_note`, moves the override `PENDING → REJECTED` and stamps `decided_at`; the patient stays `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED` and every held order stays held. `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

### 3.4. Remote Fitting: Capacity, Slot Geometry and Attendance

A provider is onboarded with a `provider_type` of either `TELECARE_FITTER` (which conducts remote programming) or `LICENSED_AUDIOLOGIST` (which additionally clears referrals), a globally unique ten-digit `npi`, a `full_name`, a non-empty array of two-character uppercase `licensed_state_codes`, a `daily_capacity_minutes` figure greater than 0 and at most 600 (ten hours), and an optional `buffer_minutes` gap of between 0 and 60 that defaults to 10. A new provider's `status` is `ACTIVE`; only `ACTIVE` providers may take new bookings, and the lifecycle also admits `SUSPENDED` and `OFFBOARDED`. Three counters are derived: `booked_minutes_on_focus_date`, `utilization_pct` and `completed_session_count`, all starting at 0. Providers may be listed with a `licensed_state_code` filter that matches a single code inside the array.

A patient books a `RemoteFittingSession` by supplying `patient_id`, a `session_type` of `INITIAL_FIT`, `FOLLOW_UP`, `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` or `TROUBLESHOOT`, a `scheduled_start_utc` and a `scheduled_end_utc`, and optionally a `provider_id`, a `device_order_id`, a `provider_referral_id` and a `programming_profile` such as `MILD_SLOPING_V3`. A `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` booking names the `provider_referral_id` it satisfies and leaves `device_order_id` null; the other session types name the order whose device is being programmed and leave `provider_referral_id` null. The booked interval is half-open from `scheduled_start_utc` inclusive to `scheduled_end_utc` exclusive; `duration_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between the two and must be at least 15 and at most 120. Creation confirms the slot in the same request, so a new session lands in `SCHEDULED` rather than lingering in `REQUESTED`; the machine's initial state is nonetheless `REQUESTED`, which is where a rebooked session returns to. Booking requires the provider to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the patient not to be `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED`.

Two geometric checks guard a booking. **Slot overlap** widens only the *candidate* interval by the provider's `buffer_minutes` on both sides — the candidate is treated as starting `buffer_minutes` earlier and ending `buffer_minutes` later — and compares that widened candidate against each existing session of the same provider whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, using those existing sessions' un-widened half-open intervals. A conflict exists when the widened candidate start is strictly before the existing end **and** the existing start is strictly before the widened candidate end; both comparisons are strict, so exact adjacency at the buffered boundary — a widened candidate start falling precisely on an existing end — is accepted. Sessions in `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` or `REQUESTED` occupy no interval at all. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

**Daily capacity** is an aggregate rather than a point check. The focus date is the UTC calendar date of the candidate's `scheduled_start_utc`, and `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over that provider's sessions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` and whose own `scheduled_start_utc` falls on that same UTC date. Sessions in `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` or `REQUESTED` are excluded, and a session that runs past midnight is attributed entirely to the date of its start. The booking is accepted when the already-booked minutes plus the candidate's `duration_minutes` is at most `daily_capacity_minutes`; exact equality is accepted, so a provider with 480 minutes of capacity may take a 60-minute session on top of 420 already booked but not on top of 421. Exceeding the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. After every accepted booking, and after every transition that moves a session into or out of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, the provider's `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` and `utilization_pct` are recomputed, the latter as 100 multiplied by `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` divided by `daily_capacity_minutes`, rounded half up to one decimal place.

State regulation reaches into booking twice. When the patient's policy has `telehealth_fitting_permitted` false, the booked provider must be licensed in the patient's own `state_code`, otherwise the booking is rejected with `ERR_TELEHEALTH_NOT_PERMITTED_IN_STATE`. For a `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` booking the state-licensure requirement holds regardless of the telehealth flag, and its failure is `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE`.

A provider starts the appointment inside its window, moving it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`; the clock must be at or after `scheduled_start_utc` and strictly before `scheduled_end_utc`, and for an `INITIAL_FIT` the linked order's `fulfillment_status` must already be `DELIVERED`. Starting changes no derived field. Completing it moves the session `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, requires a non-null `programming_profile` for `INITIAL_FIT`, `FOLLOW_UP` and `TROUBLESHOOT` types (the profile may also be supplied on the completion call itself), stamps `completed_at`, and increments the provider's `completed_session_count`. For an `INITIAL_FIT`, completion sets the linked order's device to `WITH_PATIENT` if it is not already there. For a `REFERRAL_EVALUATION`, completion drives the referral `SCHEDULED → EVALUATED`.

Attendance failures cost money, but only when the patient holds a care plan. Recording a no-show requires the clock to be at or after `scheduled_end_utc` and the session to be `SCHEDULED`; it moves the session `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` and sets `no_show_charged_cents` to 2,500 cents (twenty-five dollars) when the patient holds a plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`, and to 0 otherwise. The charge is drawn against the plan's `credit_balance_cents` first, and only the shortfall is charged on the next cycle. The provider's `booked_minutes_on_focus_date` is recomputed, and for a `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` the referral is driven `SCHEDULED → OPEN`. A no-showed session can be reopened by the patient for rescheduling, moving it `NO_SHOW → REQUESTED` and clearing `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` pending a new slot, provided the patient is neither `MEDICALLY_DISQUALIFIED` nor `DEACTIVATED`.

Cancellation takes three forms. A patient (or a care operator) cancelling a `SCHEDULED` session before its start instant moves it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and sets `no_show_charged_cents` to 1,000 cents (ten dollars) when the cancellation lands within 24 hours of `scheduled_start_utc` and the patient holds a plan in `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`, and to 0 otherwise; provider capacity and utilization are recomputed, and a `REFERRAL_EVALUATION` drives its referral back to `OPEN`. Cancelling a session still in `REQUESTED` moves it `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` with no capacity recomputation at all, because an unscheduled session occupies no interval. And the medical-hold cascade cancels `SCHEDULED` sessions on its own initiative, always with `no_show_charged_cents` forced to 0 and with the provider's booked minutes recomputed. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal session states.

### 3.5. Ordering, Fit Confirmation, Inventory and Shipment

Placing an order supplies the `model` (`NEO`, `LINK` or `FLEX`), the `dome_size` (`S`, `M`, `L` or `XL`), the `tip_type` (`OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `POWER`), a `list_price_cents` greater than 0, and optionally a `discount_cents` (at least 0 and at most `list_price_cents`, defaulting to 0), an `accessory_subtotal_cents` for chargers and extra dome kits (at least 0, defaulting to 0) and a `shipping_cents` charge (at least 0, defaulting to 1,495 cents — $14.95). Checkout pins the regulatory snapshot described in §2.5 and then composes the money. The device subtotal is `list_price_cents` minus `discount_cents`. The taxable base is that subtotal plus `accessory_subtotal_cents`; shipping is deliberately not taxable. `tax_cents` is the taxable base multiplied by the sales-tax rate of 0.0725 (7.25 percent), rounded half up — this is the single rounding in the whole composition. `total_cents` is the device subtotal plus `accessory_subtotal_cents` plus `shipping_cents` plus `tax_cents`. All four cash counters — `captured_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `retained_cents` and `open_balance_cents` — start at 0, and no other order's figures participate. A new order's `payment_status` is `PENDING_AUTH`, its `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM`, its `delivered_at` and `medical_hold_reason` are null.

An order carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and they advance under separate triggers. The payment dimension runs through `PENDING_AUTH`, `AUTHORIZED`, `CAPTURED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED`, `AUTH_FAILED` and `VOIDED`, with `REFUNDED`, `AUTH_FAILED` and `VOIDED` terminal. The fulfillment dimension runs through `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM`, `READY_TO_SHIP`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`, `MEDICAL_HOLD`, `RETURN_IN_TRANSIT`, `RETURNED` and `CANCELLED`, with `RETURNED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `MEDICAL_HOLD` is reachable from two forward states and is left only by clearance or cancellation.

An administrator drives the payment processor's answer by simulating it against a named `device_order_id` with an `outcome`. An authorization outcome moves `PENDING_AUTH → AUTHORIZED` with no cash movement, leaving `captured_cents` at 0. A declined outcome moves `PENDING_AUTH → AUTH_FAILED` and moves the fulfillment dimension to `CANCELLED`.

Fit confirmation is where medical, regulatory, inventory and payment gates converge. A care operator confirming an order requires the patient's `status` to be `CANDIDATE_APPROVED` or `PROVIDER_CLEARED`, the patient's `open_mandatory_flag_count` to be 0, the patient's `waiver_acknowledged_at` to be non-null whenever the pinned policy's `waiver_required` is true, an `InventoryPool` matching the order's `model`, `dome_size` and `tip_type` triple to exist with `on_hand_units` of at least 1, and the order's `payment_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`. Confirmation moves the fulfillment dimension `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM → READY_TO_SHIP`, reserves a unit by decrementing that pool's `on_hand_units` by one and incrementing its `reserved_units` by one, assigns an `IN_STOCK` device of the same model by moving that device to `ASSIGNED` and writing the order's identity into the device's `device_order_id`, and increments the order's `version` by one.

Inventory pools are the conserved substrate underneath all of this. A pool is keyed by a globally unique `sku` formatted as the model, dome size and tip type joined by hyphens, and repeats `model`, `dome_size` and `tip_type` as its own fields. Its four counters — `on_hand_units` (unreserved and physically available), `reserved_units` (held by exchanges in `RESERVED` or `SHIPPED`), `dispatched_units` (delivered to patients) and `total_units_received` (the cumulative number ever added) — all start at 0, the first two may never go negative, and the identity of §2.6 holds before and after every operation that touches them. Restocking adds a supplied number of `units` to both `on_hand_units` and `total_units_received` and increments the pool's `version`. Discontinuing a pool sets `is_discontinued` to true; a discontinued pool refuses new reservations, rejected as `ERR_POOL_DISCONTINUED`, but stays queryable.

Devices are minted independently of orders, with a globally unique `serial_number`, a `model` and an optional `firmware_version` that defaults to `4.0.0`. A device's `status` runs through `IN_STOCK`, `ASSIGNED`, `WITH_PATIENT`, `IN_REPAIR`, `REPLACED`, `LOST` and `RETIRED`, and is only ever mutated as a side effect of some other machine's transition — there is no endpoint whose purpose is to set it. While `IN_STOCK` a device's `device_order_id` is null. Its `shipped_at`, `warranty_expires_at` and `replaced_by_device_id` are null until the relevant event occurs, and `age_months_at_last_claim` and `loss_damage_claims_approved` start at 0.

Shipping the order is a joint transition across both dimensions. It requires `payment_status` to be `AUTHORIZED` and an assigned device in `ASSIGNED` to exist, and requires the patient's `open_mandatory_flag_count` still to be 0. It moves fulfillment `READY_TO_SHIP → SHIPPED` and payment `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED`, sets `captured_cents` to `total_cents` with `refunded_cents` and `retained_cents` at 0 and `open_balance_cents` equal to `total_cents`, stamps the device's `shipped_at` from the logical clock and sets its `warranty_expires_at` to `shipped_at` plus 730 days (two years), moves the reserved unit onward by decrementing `reserved_units` and incrementing `dispatched_units`, and increments the order's `version`.

A carrier scan against a named `device_order_id` with an `event` of `DELIVERED` moves fulfillment `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`, stamps `delivered_at`, sets the device to `WITH_PATIENT`, creates the trial period described in §3.6 in `ACTIVE` with `started_at` equal to `delivered_at`, and activates any care plan attached to that order which is still `TRIALING`. A scan with `event` `RETURN_PICKED_UP` moves fulfillment `DELIVERED → RETURN_IN_TRANSIT` and advances the matching return authorization `ISSUED → IN_TRANSIT`, and requires such an authorization in `ISSUED` to exist.

Cancelling an unshipped order is available to a care operator with an optional `reason_note`. From `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM` it requires `payment_status` to be `PENDING_AUTH` or `AUTHORIZED` and simply voids the authorization with no inventory movement. From `READY_TO_SHIP` it requires `payment_status` `AUTHORIZED`, releases the reservation by decrementing `reserved_units` and incrementing `on_hand_units`, returns the assigned device to `IN_STOCK` and clears its `device_order_id`, and advances payment `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED`; inventory conservation holds before and after. From `MEDICAL_HOLD` — reached either by a compliance-driven disqualification or by an operator cancellation — the order moves to `CANCELLED`, its payment dimension moves `AUTHORIZED → VOIDED` or `CAPTURED → REFUNDED` as a regulatory full refund, and any attached care plan is cancelled. When an order is cancelled for a regulatory reason while a trial is running, that trial moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, voiding any authorization still in `ISSUED` and cancelling any attached plan.

The medical-hold cascade's second hop deserves a note on inventory. An order held from `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM` merely gains `medical_hold_reason` `MANDATORY_RED_FLAG` and triggers the session-cancellation hop. An order held from `READY_TO_SHIP` does the same **and** releases its inventory reservation — decrementing `reserved_units`, incrementing `on_hand_units` — and returns the assigned device to `IN_STOCK` with its `device_order_id` cleared. That asymmetry is why release from `MEDICAL_HOLD` back to `PENDING_FIT_CONFIRM` performs no inventory movement: there is nothing left to give back. Beyond `MANDATORY_RED_FLAG`, `medical_hold_reason` may also read `POLICY_OTC_PROHIBITED` or `WAIVER_MISSING`.

A dedicated computed read on an order exposes its cash-conservation breakdown, its position in the trial, and its fit-exchange history in one response.

### 3.6. The Trial Clock, Returns, Restocking Settlement and Fit Exchanges

Delivery starts the statutory trial. A `TrialPeriod` is created with `device_order_id` and a denormalized `patient_id` (kept for return-history counting), `status` `ACTIVE`, `started_at` equal to the order's `delivered_at`, `nominal_end_utc` equal to `started_at` plus the order's `pinned_trial_days` days, an empty `pause_intervals` array, `paused_days` 0, `days_used` 0 and `extension_days_granted` 0. The trial lifecycle admits `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `RETURN_INITIATED`, `RETURN_SETTLED`, `EXPIRED_KEPT` and `VOIDED`, of which the last three are terminal.

Day counting is deliberately generous to the patient. The counting window ends at the earlier of the current logical clock and the trial's `effective_end_utc`. Pause intervals are **closed** ranges from a `pause_start_utc` to a `pause_end_utc`; an open pause, whose `pause_end_utc` is still null, is treated as running to the end of the counting window. Intervals are sorted by `pause_start_utc` ascending with their insertion order in the array as the tie-break, then merged: when the next interval's start is at or before the running interval's end, the two merge and the running end becomes the later of the two ends — touching endpoints therefore merge rather than double-count. `paused_days` is the total merged paused duration in seconds divided by 86,400 seconds (24 hours) and floored. Elapsed days are the counting-window end minus `started_at`, in seconds, divided by 86,400 and floored, so a span of exactly 86,400 seconds counts as one day while 86,399 counts as zero. `days_used` is the elapsed days minus `paused_days`, floored at 0. And `effective_end_utc` — the deadline that actually gates a return — is `nominal_end_utc` plus `paused_days` days plus `extension_days_granted` days. Only this trial's own `started_at`, `pause_intervals`, `nominal_end_utc` and `extension_days_granted` participate; the whole computation is clipped at `effective_end_utc`. A care operator may grant goodwill days by adding a supplied `extension_days` count to `extension_days_granted`, which must stay between 0 and 30 inclusive; the grant recomputes `effective_end_utc` and increments the trial's `version` by one, and it is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` once the clock has passed `effective_end_utc`. While the window is still open, a trial in any status other than `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the terminal `RETURN_SETTLED`, `EXPIRED_KEPT` and `VOIDED` report `ERR_INVALID_STATE` here too rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, because the grant declares `ACTIVE` and `PAUSED` as its origin set.

A depot receipt of a device that is under trial pauses the clock, moving the trial `ACTIVE → PAUSED` and appending a new interval whose start is the current logical clock and whose end is still null. Two guards apply. No second pause may be opened while one is still open — the open pause blocks it, rejected with `ERR_PAUSE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` — while intervals that merely touch at their endpoints are legitimate. And the trial carries an aggregate budget: once `paused_days` has reached 60, which is inclusive, no new pause may open, rejected with `ERR_PAUSE_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. Returning the repaired unit closes the open interval at the current logical clock and moves the trial `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, recomputing `paused_days`, `days_used` and `effective_end_utc`. The pause-and-resume cycle repeats once per repair.

When the clock passes `effective_end_utc` with no authorization in `ISSUED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RECEIVED` or `INSPECTED`, the trial moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED_KEPT`, freezing `days_used` at the value computed at `effective_end_utc` — the device is now the patient's property. A trial that is `PAUSED` when the clock passes `effective_end_utc` moves `PAUSED → EXPIRED_KEPT`, first closing the open interval at `effective_end_utc` and then freezing `days_used`.

A patient initiates a return, which moves the trial `ACTIVE → RETURN_INITIATED`. The request carries `expected_trial_period_version` per §2.7. It requires the clock to be at or before `effective_end_utc`, no repair on the order's device to be in `KIT_SENT`, `RECEIVED` or `IN_REPAIR`, and no exchange on the order to be in `RESERVED` or `SHIPPED`. The pinned `restocking_fee_mode` is *not* a gate: a `PROHIBITED` mode yields a waived settlement, never a rejection. Initiation creates a `ReturnAuthorization` in `ISSUED` with a globally unique human-facing `rma_code`, a `days_used_at_issue` snapshot of the trial's `days_used` at that instant, a `return_deadline_utc` of the current clock plus 14 days (two weeks), `restocking_variant` `WAIVED`, and zeros in `restocking_fee_cents`, `non_refundable_shipping_cents` and `refund_amount_cents`; `received_at`, `settled_at` and `inspection_grade` are null. It increments the trial's `version` by one and the order's `version` by one. Authorization status runs `ISSUED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED`, `REFUND_SETTLED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED`, of which the last three are terminal.

A carrier pickup scan moves the authorization `ISSUED → IN_TRANSIT` and requires the clock to be at or before `return_deadline_utc`. Warehouse receipt moves it `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` and stamps `received_at`, again requiring the clock to be at or before `return_deadline_utc`. Inspection supplies an `inspection_grade` of `RESELLABLE`, `REFURBISHABLE` or `DAMAGED` and moves the authorization `RECEIVED → INSPECTED`; a `RESELLABLE` grade credits the unit straight back to stock by decrementing the pool's `dispatched_units` and incrementing its `on_hand_units`, while a `REFURBISHABLE` or `DAMAGED` grade credits nothing, the unit having left the conserved pool total through a subsequent restock adjustment instead. An `ISSUED` authorization whose `return_deadline_utc` the clock has passed becomes `EXPIRED`, reverting the trial `RETURN_INITIATED → ACTIVE` when the trial's `effective_end_utc` has not yet passed, and driving it to `EXPIRED_KEPT` when it has. A care operator may void an `ISSUED` authorization with an optional `reason_note`, which is a compensating action: the authorization becomes `VOIDED` with `restocking_fee_cents` and `refund_amount_cents` set to 0, the trial reverts `RETURN_INITIATED → ACTIVE` (which requires the clock still to be at or before `effective_end_utc`), the trial's `version` increments, and the order's cash-conservation identity is exactly what it was before the issue.

**Restocking settlement** runs at inspection and is the most rounding-sensitive computation in the system. Its inputs are the authorization's own `days_used_at_issue` snapshot and `inspection_grade`, the order's `pinned_restocking_mode`, `pinned_trial_days`, `captured_cents` and `shipping_cents`, and the **pinned** policy row read at the order's `pinned_state_policy_version`; live policy edits made after checkout are excluded. Step one forms two components: the device component is `list_price_cents` minus `discount_cents`, and the accessory component is `accessory_subtotal_cents`. Step two selects the variant, testing in this order: if the pinned mode is `PROHIBITED` the variant is `WAIVED`; otherwise if `days_used_at_issue` is 0 the variant is `WAIVED` — a same-day return is never charged restocking; otherwise if the pinned mode is `FLAT_PCT` the variant is `FLAT_PCT`; otherwise it is `PRORATED_DAILY`. The chosen variant is stored on `restocking_variant`.

Step three computes per-component restocking, **rounding each component to the cent before anything is summed**. Under `FLAT_PCT`, the device portion is the device component multiplied by the policy's `restocking_fee_pct`, rounded half up; the accessory portion is the accessory component multiplied by `restocking_fee_pct` and again by an accessory factor of 0.5 — accessories restock at half the device rate — rounded half up. Under `PRORATED_DAILY`, the device portion is the policy's `restocking_daily_rate_cents` multiplied by the lesser of `days_used_at_issue` and the order's `pinned_trial_days`, and the accessory portion is 0. Under `WAIVED`, both portions are 0.

Step four adds a **third, independently rounded component**: a damage surcharge. A `DAMAGED` grade surcharges the device component multiplied by 0.15, rounded half up; a `REFURBISHABLE` grade surcharges the device component multiplied by 0.05, rounded half up; a `RESELLABLE` grade surcharges nothing. This surcharge applies **even under the `WAIVED` variant** — a state's prohibition on restocking fees does not shield a patient from damage they caused. Step five sums the three already-rounded components and applies the cap: when the pinned policy's `restocking_fee_cap_cents` is 0 the raw sum stands unchanged; otherwise `restocking_fee_cents` is the lesser of the raw sum and the cap. Summing the components first and rounding the total once is explicitly forbidden and yields a different number of cents. Step six sets `non_refundable_shipping_cents` to 0 when the pinned policy's `shipping_refundable` is true, and to the order's `shipping_cents` otherwise. Step seven sets `refund_amount_cents` to the order's `captured_cents` minus `restocking_fee_cents` minus `non_refundable_shipping_cents`, floored at 0. For a whole-order return, `refund_amount_cents` plus `restocking_fee_cents` plus `non_refundable_shipping_cents` equals `captured_cents` exactly.

Settling an inspected authorization moves it `INSPECTED → REFUND_SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at`, and runs the five-hop return cascade of §2.8. It requires `refund_amount_cents` to have been computed and the order's `payment_status` to be `CAPTURED`. On the payment dimension, a settlement whose `restocking_fee_cents` plus `non_refundable_shipping_cents` exceeds 0 with a positive `refund_amount_cents` moves `CAPTURED → PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, adding `refund_amount_cents` to `refunded_cents` and adding the retained restocking plus non-refundable shipping to `retained_cents`, then recomputing `open_balance_cents` — which should reach 0 on a whole-order return. A settlement where the variant is `WAIVED` and `refund_amount_cents` equals `captured_cents`, or a regulatory full refund on disqualification, moves `CAPTURED → REFUNDED` with `refunded_cents` equal to `captured_cents`, `retained_cents` 0 and `open_balance_cents` 0.

A compliance officer may afterwards waive retained restocking, optionally with a `reason_note`, provided the settled authorization's `restocking_fee_cents` is above 0 and the clock is within 30 days of `settled_at`. This compensating action moves the payment dimension `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED`, refunding the retained amount so that `refunded_cents` equals `captured_cents`, `retained_cents` becomes 0 and `open_balance_cents` becomes 0, and rewriting the authorization's `restocking_variant` to `WAIVED` with `restocking_fee_cents` and `non_refundable_shipping_cents` set to 0 and `refund_amount_cents` set to `captured_cents`. Cash conservation holds before and after.

**Fit exchanges** let a patient trade dome size or tip type without returning the device. A request names the `to_dome_size` and `to_tip_type` wanted, carries `expected_device_order_version` per §2.7, and may set `allow_backorder`. It requires the order's `fulfillment_status` to be `DELIVERED`, the requested pair to differ from the order's current `dome_size` and `tip_type` pair (otherwise `ERR_SAME_FIT_CONFIGURATION`), the target pool to exist with `is_discontinued` false and `on_hand_units` of at least 1, and either the order's trial to be `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` or the patient to hold a care plan in `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`. Reservation happens in the same request, so an accepted request lands in `RESERVED`: the server writes `to_inventory_pool_id` and `from_inventory_pool_id`, decrements the target pool's `on_hand_units` and increments its `reserved_units`, computes `exchange_fee_cents`, and increments the order's `version` by one. When the target pool is empty and the caller did not opt into a backorder, the request instead lands in `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `POOL_DEPLETED` and no inventory movement; the other possible rejection reasons an exchange may record are `SAME_CONFIGURATION` and `TRIAL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The exchange lifecycle is `REQUESTED`, `RESERVED`, `SHIPPED`, `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED`, with the last three terminal alongside `COMPLETED`.

The exchange fee rewards patients who are still inside their trial. `exchange_ordinal` is one more than the number of prior exchanges on the same order whose `status` is `SHIPPED` or `COMPLETED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and is always at least 1; exchanges in `REQUESTED`, `RESERVED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` never count toward it. If the order's trial is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, the fee is 0 — exchanges are always free inside the trial. Otherwise, if `exchange_ordinal` is at most 2, the fee is 0, so the first two post-trial exchanges are free as well. Otherwise, if the patient holds a plan whose `plan_tier` is `PREMIUM` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`, the fee is 1,900 cents multiplied by 0.5, rounded half up — a premium subscriber pays half. Otherwise the fee is the full 1,900 cents ($19.00). A non-zero fee is drawn against the plan's `credit_balance_cents` first, and only the shortfall is charged on the next cycle.

A care operator ships the replacement domes and tips, moving the exchange `RESERVED → SHIPPED` with no counter change — the unit stays in `reserved_units` until the patient confirms. The owning patient then confirms the new configuration fits, moving the exchange `SHIPPED → COMPLETED`: the order's `dome_size` and `tip_type` are rewritten to the requested pair and the order's `version` increments, the target pool's `reserved_units` decrements while its `dispatched_units` increments, and the source pool's `dispatched_units` decrements while its `on_hand_units` increments as the outgoing configuration returns to stock. Inventory conservation holds for each pool before and after. Cancellation, optionally with a `reason_note`, is a compensating action available to the patient from `RESERVED` and to a care operator from `SHIPPED` (the latter requiring the order's trial not to be `RETURN_SETTLED`): the target pool's `reserved_units` decrements and its `on_hand_units` increments, restoring the pre-request counters exactly, and any `exchange_fee_cents` that was charged is credited back to the plan's `credit_balance_cents`.

### 3.7. Repairs, Warranty Claims and Device Replacement

A repair is opened against a `device_id` with a reported `fault_code` of `NO_SOUND`, `INTERMITTENT`, `BATTERY`, `MOISTURE`, `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` or `FIRMWARE_BRICK`. Creation denormalizes the owner's `patient_id` and the governing `care_plan_subscription_id` (null when the patient holds no plan), and computes two derived fields. `sla_days` is 5 when a plan is linked whose `plan_tier` is `PREMIUM`, 10 when the linked plan's tier is `STANDARD`, and 15 when there is no plan at all; the same 15-day fallback applies to newly opened repairs once a plan has been suspended. `pauses_trial` is true when the device's order has a trial whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, and such a repair suspends trial day counting. A new repair's `status` is `KIT_REQUESTED`, with `received_at`, `closed_at` null, `turnaround_days` 0 and `sla_credit_cents` 0. The lifecycle admits `KIT_REQUESTED`, `KIT_SENT`, `RECEIVED`, `IN_REPAIR`, `REPAIRED`, `UNREPAIRABLE`, `RETURNED_TO_PATIENT`, `ESCALATED_TO_WARRANTY` and `CANCELLED`; the last three are terminal.

A care operator dispatches the mail-in kit, moving the repair `KIT_REQUESTED → KIT_SENT` and requiring the device to be `WITH_PATIENT`. A depot technician records receipt, moving it `KIT_SENT → RECEIVED`, again requiring the device to be `WITH_PATIENT`; receipt stamps `received_at` (which starts the SLA clock), sets the device to `IN_REPAIR`, recomputes `sla_days` from the plan tier, and when `pauses_trial` is true drives the trial `ACTIVE → PAUSED` under the pause guards of §3.6. Bench work begins with a transition `RECEIVED → IN_REPAIR` that changes no derived field.

Resolution forks. A supplied `resolution` of `REPAIRED` moves the repair `IN_REPAIR → REPAIRED` — branch A, the repairable path, which never creates a warranty claim — and requires either that `fault_code` is not `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` or that the patient has already paid a warranty deductible. A `resolution` of `UNREPAIRABLE` moves the repair `IN_REPAIR → UNREPAIRABLE` — branch B — and sets the device to `REPLACED` pending a warranty determination.

Branch A closes by returning the unit, moving `REPAIRED → RETURNED_TO_PATIENT`. Closure stamps `closed_at`, computes `turnaround_days` and `sla_credit_cents`, credits the linked plan's `credit_balance_cents` by the credit amount when a plan exists, sets the device back to `WITH_PATIENT`, and — when this repair paused the trial — drives the trial `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, extending `effective_end_utc` by the accumulated `paused_days`. Branch B closes by escalating, moving `UNREPAIRABLE → ESCALATED_TO_WARRANTY`, which requires either that the device's `warranty_expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock or that the `fault_code` is `FIRMWARE_BRICK`. Escalation performs the same `closed_at`, `turnaround_days`, `sla_credit_cents` and plan-credit work as branch A, likewise drives a paused trial back to `ACTIVE`, and additionally creates a `WarrantyClaim` in `FILED` whose `claim_kind` is `DEFECT` and whose `originating_repair_order_id` names this repair. Branch A never creates a claim; that is the whole difference in the fork's side-effect sets. A repair may also be cancelled with an optional `reason_note` — by the patient from `KIT_REQUESTED` or by a care operator from `KIT_SENT` — and in neither case is there any trial adjustment or SLA credit, because no depot receipt occurred.

The **SLA credit** is a decay function on lateness. `turnaround_days` is the whole number of days between `received_at` and `closed_at`, floored. Days over is `turnaround_days` minus `sla_days`, floored at 0 — closing exactly on the SLA day yields zero. The multiplier is 2.0 when a plan is linked whose `plan_tier` is `PREMIUM` and 1.0 otherwise. `sla_credit_cents` is the days over multiplied by 500 cents per day and by that multiplier, rounded half up, and then capped at 5,000 cents ($50.00) — the cap is applied **after** the multiplied product has been rounded. Only this repair's `received_at` and `closed_at` and its linked plan's `plan_tier` participate, and a repair that ended in `CANCELLED` never produces a credit.

A `WarrantyClaim` may also be filed directly against a `device_id` with a `claim_kind` of `LOSS`, `DAMAGE` or `DEFECT`, optionally naming an `originating_repair_order_id`; a directly filed loss claim leaves that reference null. Filing denormalizes the claimant's `patient_id` and lands the claim in `FILED`, with `device_age_months` 0, `deductible_cents` 0, `deductible_paid_cents` 0, `replacement_device_id` null, `denial_reason` null and `triaged_at` null. The lifecycle runs `FILED`, `TRIAGED`, `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`, `APPROVED_REPAIR`, `DENIED`, `FULFILLED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `FULFILLED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal. The owning patient may withdraw a claim still in `FILED`, moving it `FILED → WITHDRAWN` with no counter changes; a withdrawn claim does not count toward `claim_ordinal`.

A depot technician triages the claim, moving it `FILED → TRIAGED` and requiring the device not to be `RETIRED`. Triage stamps `triaged_at`, computes `device_age_months` and `claim_ordinal`, mirrors the age onto the device's `age_months_at_last_claim`, and computes the deductible. `device_age_months` is the whole number of months between the device's `shipped_at` and the logical clock, floored. `claim_ordinal` is one more than the number of prior claims on the same `device_id` whose `status` is `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`, `APPROVED_REPAIR` or `FULFILLED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and is at least 1; withdrawn claims are excluded from the count.

The **deductible** is tiered on device age and scaled by claim nature. An age of at most 12 months — inclusive — takes a multiplier of 0.5; an age from 13 through 24 months takes 1.0; an age above 24 months takes 1.5. The kind factor is 0.0 for `DEFECT`, 0.75 for `DAMAGE` and 1.0 for `LOSS`, so a manufacturing defect is never charged a deductible. The ordinal surcharge is 0 when `claim_ordinal` is 1 and 5,000 cents ($50.00) otherwise. `deductible_cents` is the base of 9,900 cents ($99.00) multiplied by the age multiplier and by the kind factor, rounded half up, with the integer surcharge added afterwards — the rounding touches the multiplicative part only.

A compliance officer decides the claim, supplying a `decision` and, on a denial, a `denial_reason`. A `REPLACE` decision moves the claim `TRIAGED → APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`; it requires, for a `LOSS` or `DAMAGE` claim, that the device's `loss_damage_claims_approved` is strictly below the policy's `max_loss_damage_claims`, and requires either that `warranty_expires_at` is strictly after the clock or that `claim_kind` is `LOSS`. The replace branch mints a new device in `IN_STOCK`, assigns it to the same `device_order_id`, writes the new unit's identity into the old device's `replaced_by_device_id` and moves the old device to `REPLACED` — or to `LOST` when `claim_kind` is `LOSS` — and writes `replacement_device_id` on the claim. The replacement's `firmware_version` is `4.0.0`, so any still-pending rollout on the old device moves to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE`. A `REPAIR` decision moves the claim `TRIAGED → APPROVED_REPAIR`; it requires `claim_kind` to be `DEFECT` or `DAMAGE` and `warranty_expires_at` to be strictly after the clock. The repair branch creates a new repair in `KIT_REQUESTED` for the same device, copying `fault_code` from the originating repair or using `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE` for a damage claim, and leaves `replacement_device_id` null — no replacement unit is minted. A `DENY` decision moves the claim `TRIAGED → DENIED` and sets `denial_reason` to whichever predicate failed: `WARRANTY_EXPIRED`, `CLAIM_CAP_REACHED`, `NOT_COVERED_FAULT` or `DEVICE_RETIRED`.

The patient pays the deductible by supplying an `amount_cents` that is added to `deductible_paid_cents`. Both approval branches then rejoin at fulfilment, but with different preconditions. From `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`, fulfilment requires only that `deductible_paid_cents` equals `deductible_cents`; it moves the claim to `FULFILLED`, sets the replacement device to `WITH_PATIENT` with `shipped_at` stamped from the logical clock and `warranty_expires_at` copied from the **original** device — a replacement does not extend the warranty — and recomputes the original device's `loss_damage_claims_approved`, which counts claims on that device whose `status` is `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT` or `FULFILLED` and whose `claim_kind` is `LOSS` or `DAMAGE`. From `APPROVED_REPAIR`, fulfilment requires both that `deductible_paid_cents` equals `deductible_cents` **and** that the repair created by the decision has reached `RETURNED_TO_PATIENT`; it moves the claim to `FULFILLED` with no device replacement.

### 3.8. Care Plans, Billing Cycles and Reimbursement Paperwork

A care plan is enrolled against a `patient_id` and a `device_order_id` with a `plan_tier` of `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, a `base_fee_cents` greater than 0, and an optional `firmware_auto_update_enabled` consent flag that defaults to true. A new plan's `status` is `TRIALING` and its `cycle_ordinal` is 0, with `cycle_start_utc` and `cycle_end_utc` unset, `last_charge_cents`, `service_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents`, `credit_balance_cents` and `dunning_attempts` all 0. The lifecycle admits `TRIALING`, `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE`, `SUSPENDED` and the terminal `CANCELLED`. Delivery of the attached order moves the plan `TRIALING → ACTIVE`, setting `cycle_start_utc` to the order's `delivered_at`, `cycle_end_utc` to `cycle_start_utc` plus 30 days, and `cycle_ordinal` to 0.

The **cycle charge** looks circular — the service fee depends on the subtotal tier, and the loyalty discount depends on the fee-inclusive base — so its evaluation order is normative and runs in exactly five steps. Step one: the subtotal is `base_fee_cents`, plus a premium surcharge of 2,000 cents ($20.00) when `plan_tier` is `PREMIUM`. Step two: `service_fee_cents` is 0 when that subtotal is at least 9,900 cents ($99.00) — the threshold is inclusive, so a subtotal of exactly 9,900 waives the fee — and 495 cents ($4.95) otherwise. Step three: the fee-inclusive base is the subtotal plus `service_fee_cents`. Step four: the loyalty rate is 0.15 when `cycle_ordinal` is at least 12, otherwise 0.10 when `cycle_ordinal` is at least 6, otherwise 0.0; `loyalty_discount_cents` is the lesser of 1,500 cents ($15.00) and the fee-inclusive base multiplied by that rate, rounded half up. Step five: `last_charge_cents` is the fee-inclusive base minus `loyalty_discount_cents`, floored at 0. Testing the **discounted** amount against the service-fee waiver threshold is forbidden and produces a different total. Only this subscription's own `base_fee_cents`, `plan_tier`, `cycle_ordinal` and `credit_balance_cents` participate.

Credits settle **after** step five, never inside it. The applied credit is the lesser of `credit_balance_cents` and `last_charge_cents`; the amount actually due is `last_charge_cents` minus that applied credit, and `credit_balance_cents` is reduced by the applied credit. When a tier change is requested mid-cycle, the charge is recomputed with the **new** tier, the remaining days are the seconds from the current logical clock to `cycle_end_utc` divided by 86,400 and rounded **up** to a whole day, and the prorated charge is `last_charge_cents` multiplied by those remaining days divided by 30, rounded half up. The tier change carries `plan_tier` and `expected_subscription_version` and increments the plan's `version`.

Running a billing cycle is an administrator action carrying a `charge_outcome` and an optional `pay_from_credit` flag. It requires the clock to be at or after `cycle_end_utc`. On success the plan self-loops `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`: the five-step computation writes `service_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `last_charge_cents`, credit is consumed, `cycle_ordinal` increments, `cycle_start_utc` advances to the old `cycle_end_utc` and `cycle_end_utc` becomes the new `cycle_start_utc` plus 30 days. When `pay_from_credit` is true but `credit_balance_cents` is below the computed charge, the run is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. On a declined instrument, with `last_charge_cents` exceeding `credit_balance_cents`, the plan moves `ACTIVE → PAST_DUE` with `dunning_attempts` set to 1 and — importantly — `cycle_ordinal` **not** incremented, which is why a past-due episode never advances the loyalty tier. The run itself nonetheless *fails* with `ERR_PAYMENT_DECLINED`: the freshly computed `service_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `last_charge_cents`, the `PAST_DUE` status and `dunning_attempts` are committed to the row before that error response is emitted, and none of it is rolled back. A declining retry below behaves the same way — its `dunning_attempts` increment, and any suspension and suspension cascade the increment triggers, persist alongside the error.

The patient drives recovery by retrying the charge with a `charge_outcome`. A succeeding retry moves the plan `PAST_DUE → ACTIVE`, increments `cycle_ordinal`, advances the cycle window and resets `dunning_attempts` to 0; it requires `dunning_attempts` to be below 3. A declining retry self-loops `PAST_DUE → PAST_DUE` and increments `dunning_attempts`, likewise requiring the counter to be below 3 beforehand. The third failure — the increment that brings `dunning_attempts` to 3 — moves the plan `PAST_DUE → SUSPENDED`. Suspension moves every rollout in `PENDING` or `OFFERED` on the device belonging to this plan's order to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE`, unless the owning campaign's `is_mandatory_safety` is true, and drops the SLA for newly opened repairs back to the 15-day no-plan fallback. A care operator may reinstate a suspended plan, moving it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, resetting `dunning_attempts` to 0 and re-basing the cycle window on the current logical clock; reinstatement requires the order's trial not to be `RETURN_SETTLED`.

Cancellation reaches `CANCELLED` from `ACTIVE` and `PAST_DUE` at the patient's request (or a care operator's, optionally with a `reason_note`) and from `SUSPENDED` at a care operator's request, and also arrives as hop 4 of the return-settlement cascade. In every case the same two effects apply: every rollout in `PENDING` or `OFFERED` on this order's device moves to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` with the owning campaign's counters recomputed, and `credit_balance_cents` is forfeited by being set to 0.

Reimbursement paperwork is a separate, polymorphic workflow. A claim is drafted against a `patient_id` with a `subject_kind` of `DEVICE_ORDER`, `REPAIR_ORDER` or `CARE_PLAN`, a `claimed_amount_cents` greater than 0, and exactly the one subject reference that matches — `device_order_id`, `repair_order_id` or `care_plan_subscription_id` — with the other two left null; supplying the wrong combination is `ERR_SUBJECT_KIND_MISMATCH`. An appeal additionally names `appeal_of_claim_id`, the denied claim it re-opens. The draft may carry `document_codes` (defaulting to an empty array) and three declared financial inputs: `insurance_benefit_cap_cents`, `insurance_benefit_used_cents` and `fsa_balance_cents`, each a non-negative integer defaulting to 0. Creation computes `documentation_complete`, which is true exactly when `document_codes` is a superset of the required set for the claim's `subject_kind` — `INVOICE`, `AUDIOGRAM_OR_WAIVER`, `PROOF_OF_PAYMENT` and `LMN` for a `DEVICE_ORDER`, and `INVOICE` and `PROOF_OF_PAYMENT` for the other two kinds. A new claim's `status` is `DRAFT`, its allocation buckets and `info_request_count` are 0, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `denial_reason` are null. The lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `PAID` and `VOIDED`, with `PAID` and `VOIDED` terminal.

A reimbursement specialist submits the claim, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Submission requires `documentation_complete` to be true, requires the subject entity to exist and agree with `subject_kind`, and for a `DEVICE_ORDER` subject requires the order's `payment_status` to be `CAPTURED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. It records the acting operator identity as `submitted_by_user_id` and runs the **benefit waterfall**. Step one computes `eligible_amount_cents`: it is 0 outright when `documentation_complete` is false; for a `DEVICE_ORDER` subject it is the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and the order's `captured_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus `retained_cents` floored at 0; for a `REPAIR_ORDER` subject it is the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and a repair cap of 50,000 cents ($500.00); for a `CARE_PLAN` subject it is the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and the plan's `last_charge_cents` multiplied by 12 cycles. The remaining steps fill three ordered buckets with no proportional splitting: `insurance_paid_cents` is the lesser of the eligible amount and the declared `insurance_benefit_cap_cents` minus `insurance_benefit_used_cents` floored at 0; what remains after insurance is the eligible amount minus `insurance_paid_cents`; `fsa_paid_cents` is the lesser of that remainder and `fsa_balance_cents`; and `patient_responsibility_cents` is the eligible amount minus `insurance_paid_cents` minus `fsa_paid_cents`, absorbing any remainder by construction. The bucket order is fixed — insurance benefit, then flexible spending, then patient responsibility — and sibling claims on the same subject never reduce the buckets, because duplicates are refused by validation instead.

Review is a loop. Moving the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` changes no allocation. A specialist may request more documentation, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` with a supplied `requested_document_codes` list and incrementing `info_request_count`, which must be below 2 beforehand. The patient responds by supplying `document_codes` and optionally revised `insurance_benefit_used_cents` and `fsa_balance_cents`, which merges the new codes, requires `documentation_complete` to hold afterwards, moves the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and **re-runs the waterfall with the updated inputs** — the allocation may legitimately change. The cycle from review through an information request and back is capped at two requests.

Deciding the claim supplies a `decision` and, on denial, a `denial_reason`. Both branches require the acting operator identity to differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. An approval moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id`, and requires `claimed_amount_cents` to be at most `eligible_amount_cents`; benefit conservation holds. A denial moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets `denial_reason` to `DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, `NOT_COVERED`, `AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_ELIGIBLE` or `DUPLICATE_SUBJECT`, and zeroes `insurance_paid_cents` and `fsa_paid_cents` while setting `patient_responsibility_cents` to the whole `eligible_amount_cents`, so the conservation identity still holds. A denied claim re-enters review through the appeal loop: a new claim naming it through `appeal_of_claim_id` inherits its `subject_kind` and subject reference and drives the original `DENIED → UNDER_REVIEW`, so only one of the pair can ultimately be paid; the appeal is refused when the appealed claim's `denial_reason` is `DUPLICATE_SUBJECT` or when an appeal already exists. Payment moves an approved claim `APPROVED → PAID`, requires `approved_by_user_id` to be non-null and the sum of `insurance_paid_cents` and `fsa_paid_cents` to be above 0, and renders the claim immutable. A compliance officer may void a claim from `SUBMITTED` or from `APPROVED`, optionally with a `reason_note`; either void zeroes `insurance_paid_cents` and `fsa_paid_cents` and sets `patient_responsibility_cents` to `eligible_amount_cents`, leaving benefit conservation unchanged.

### 3.9. Firmware Campaigns and Rollout Mechanics

A firmware engineer drafts a campaign with a globally unique `campaign_code`, a targeted `model` drawn from the same `NEO`, `LINK` and `FLEX` set an order, a device and an inventory pool name, an exact `from_version` a device must currently run to be eligible, the `to_version` this campaign installs, and a maintenance window bounded by `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. The window is a **closed** interval: rollout offers may only be emitted while the clock is at or between the two endpoints inclusive. Optional inputs are `batch_size` (between 1 and 100, defaulting to 3), `max_concurrent_installs` (between 1 and 50, defaulting to 2) and `is_mandatory_safety` (defaulting to false), which when true overrides subscriber opt-out. A new campaign's `status` is `DRAFT` with `rollout_count`, `installed_count` and `failure_rate_pct` all 0; `failure_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by failed rollouts divided by the sum of installed and failed rollouts, to one decimal place rounded half up, and reads 0 when that denominator is 0. The lifecycle admits `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ROLLING`, `PAUSED`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, of which the last two are terminal.

Scheduling moves the campaign `DRAFT → SCHEDULED` and enforces three conditions: `window_start_utc` must be strictly before `window_end_utc`, `to_version` must differ from `from_version`, and no other campaign for the same `model` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ROLLING` or `PAUSED` may occupy an intersecting window. **Window collision uses non-strict comparisons on both sides**: a conflict exists when this campaign's `window_start_utc` is at or before the other's `window_end_utc` and the other's `window_start_utc` is at or before this campaign's `window_end_utc`. Touching endpoints therefore collide — a window starting at the exact instant another ends is a conflict, which is the opposite of the strict, buffer-widened session geometry in §3.4. Campaigns in `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` occupy no window, and only same-model campaigns can collide. A collision is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Advancing the campaign pumps a batch. The first advance moves it `SCHEDULED → ROLLING` and requires the clock to be at or after `window_start_utc` and at or before `window_end_utc`; each subsequent advance self-loops `ROLLING → ROLLING`, requiring the count of rollouts in `INSTALLING` to be strictly below `max_concurrent_installs` and the clock to be at or before `window_end_utc`. Each advance selects a **cohort**. A device is a candidate when its `status` is `WITH_PATIENT`, its `model` equals the campaign's `model`, its `firmware_version` equals the campaign's `from_version` exactly, no repair on that device is in `KIT_SENT`, `RECEIVED` or `IN_REPAIR`, no rollout for **this** campaign on that device is already in `PENDING`, `OFFERED`, `INSTALLING` or `INSTALLED`, and either the campaign's `is_mandatory_safety` is true or the plan attached to that device's order exists with `status` in `ACTIVE` or `TRIALING` and with `firmware_auto_update_enabled` true. Devices in `IN_STOCK`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_REPAIR`, `REPLACED`, `LOST` or `RETIRED` are excluded, while a device carrying only a `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE`, `FAILED` or `ROLLED_BACK` rollout for this campaign remains selectable again. Candidates are ordered by `warranty_expires_at` ascending — the units closest to falling out of warranty are updated first — with `created_at` ascending and then `serial_number` ascending as tie-breaks; `id` never participates in the ordering. The first `batch_size` candidates are taken, and for each one a `FirmwareRollout` is created in `PENDING` with `selection_rank` counting from 1 in that order and with `from_version_observed` captured from the device's current `firmware_version`. An empty candidate set is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DEVICES`. Each advance recomputes `rollout_count`, and subsequent advances also recompute `installed_count` and `failure_rate_pct`.

An engineer may enrol a single device by hand, naming a `device_id` and carrying `expected_device_version` per §2.7. The enrolment creates a rollout in `PENDING` whose `selection_rank` is one more than the campaign's current `rollout_count` and whose `from_version_observed` is the device's current `firmware_version`, increments the device's `version` by one, and recomputes `rollout_count`. Manual enrolment respects subscriber consent: unless the campaign's `is_mandatory_safety` is true, the device's order must carry a care plan in `ACTIVE` or `TRIALING` whose `firmware_auto_update_enabled` is true, and a plan that is absent, in any other status, or opted out is refused with `ERR_AUTO_UPDATE_NOT_CONSENTED` — here one check covers both the plan's state and the flag, unlike the offer path where the two are separate (see §2.9 rules 24 and 33). Nothing is required of the campaign itself: a manual enrolment is accepted whatever `status` the campaign holds, and it neither requires nor consults the maintenance window.

Every rollout row names its owning campaign through `firmware_campaign_id` and its target unit through `device_id`, and rollouts may be listed filtered by either. A rollout's own lifecycle runs `PENDING`, `OFFERED`, `INSTALLING`, `INSTALLED`, `FAILED`, `ROLLED_BACK` and `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE`, with `INSTALLED`, `ROLLED_BACK` and `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` terminal; `attempt_count` starts at 0, and `failure_code` and `installed_at` are null. An engineer offers the update to the subscriber, moving the rollout `PENDING → OFFERED`; this requires the campaign to be `ROLLING`, the clock to be inside the closed maintenance window, and — unless the campaign's `is_mandatory_safety` is true — two separate things of the device's plan, tested in this order: first the plan must exist in `ACTIVE` or `TRIALING`, a sibling-entity precondition whose failure is `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_NOT_READY` under §2.9 rule 24, and only then must that plan's `firmware_auto_update_enabled` be true, whose failure is `ERR_AUTO_UPDATE_NOT_CONSENTED` under rule 33. The patient starts the install, moving `OFFERED → INSTALLING` and incrementing `attempt_count`; this requires the count of the campaign's rollouts in `INSTALLING` to be strictly below `max_concurrent_installs` and the device to be `WITH_PATIENT`.

Install telemetry arrives from the device via an administrator-injected report naming a `firmware_rollout_id`, an `outcome` and an optional `failure_code`. A success moves the rollout `INSTALLING → INSTALLED`, and requires the device's current `firmware_version` still to equal `from_version_observed` — any drift is refused with `ERR_FIRMWARE_VERSION_DRIFT`. Success sets the device's `firmware_version` to the campaign's `to_version`, stamps `installed_at`, and recomputes the campaign's `installed_count` and `failure_rate_pct`. A failure moves the rollout `INSTALLING → FAILED`, sets `failure_code` to `VERSION_DRIFT`, `POWER_LOSS`, `CHECKSUM` or `TIMEOUT`, and recomputes `failure_rate_pct`; when that rate rises above 25.0 percent the campaign is driven `ROLLING → PAUSED` automatically. A failed rollout may be retried by the patient, moving it `FAILED → INSTALLING` and incrementing `attempt_count`, provided `attempt_count` is below 3 and the campaign is `ROLLING`; a third failure is not retried. An engineer may instead roll a failed install back, moving it `FAILED → ROLLED_BACK` when `attempt_count` has reached 3 or when `failure_code` is `CHECKSUM`; the rollback is a compensating action restoring the device's `firmware_version` to `from_version_observed`, and when the unit remains unusable it also creates a repair in `KIT_REQUESTED` with `fault_code` `FIRMWARE_BRICK`.

Rollouts also die of ineligibility. A `PENDING` rollout moves to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` when the device leaves `WITH_PATIENT` or its plan leaves `ACTIVE` and `TRIALING` — the paths being the return-settlement cascade, plan cancellation, plan suspension and campaign abort — and the campaign's `rollout_count` and `failure_rate_pct` are recomputed. An `OFFERED` rollout moves to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` on plan cancellation, plan suspension or campaign abort, but only when the campaign's `is_mandatory_safety` is false, with the campaign counters likewise recomputed.

Finally, the campaign itself can be paused, resumed, completed or aborted by its engineer. Pausing moves it `ROLLING → PAUSED`, leaving every `PENDING` rollout exactly where it is and blocking further batch selection; the same transition happens automatically when `failure_rate_pct` climbs above 25.0 after a failure. Resuming moves it `PAUSED → ROLLING` and requires `failure_rate_pct` to be at most 25.0 and the clock to be at or before `window_end_utc`. Completing moves it `ROLLING → COMPLETED` and requires no rollout to remain in `PENDING`, `OFFERED` or `INSTALLING`; it freezes `installed_count` and `failure_rate_pct`. Aborting from `PAUSED` moves every rollout in `PENDING` or `OFFERED` to `CANCELLED_INELIGIBLE` while leaving `INSTALLED` rollouts intact; aborting from `SCHEDULED` requires `rollout_count` to be 0 and changes no rollout at all, which is the empty-set edge of the same action.
