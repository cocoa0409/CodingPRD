# Recipe Costing & Menu Engineering API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multi-unit restaurant group lives or dies on two numbers that nobody in the building can currently produce reliably: what a plate actually costs to make, and what the menu mix is doing to contribution margin. Today the culinary team keeps recipe specifications in spreadsheets, the finance team keeps distributor price sheets in email attachments, and the store managers count inventory into a form that nobody reconciles against what the recipes say should have been consumed. When a distributor raises the price of beef in one region, nobody knows which of the two hundred plated recipes just became unprofitable, which sub-recipes those recipes inherit their cost from, or which limited-time offer was launched on figures that are now three price feeds out of date.

This document specifies the Recipe Costing & Menu Engineering service, an API mounted under `/culinary` that makes the whole chain explicit and auditable. It holds the recipe tree — plated recipes built from ingredient lines and from other recipes as sub-recipes — and it holds the yield and shrink factors that separate the edible-portion quantity a chef specifies from the as-purchased quantity a buyer must actually order. It ingests distributor price feeds region by region, publishes them as a dated event, and propagates the consequences of that publication upward through the recipe graph to every menu item whose plate cost just became untrustworthy. On top of that spine it layers the commercial workflows the culinary and finance functions actually argue about: plate costing with regional freight and waste allowances, theoretical-versus-actual food cost variance for a period, menu-engineering classification of every item into stars, plowhorses, puzzles and dogs, price change proposals modelled against elasticity assumptions and market caps, allergen and nutrition roll-ups that must reach the printed menu correctly, and limited-time offers with a post-promotion profitability verdict.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below and should be read as non-negotiable. The first is that **no cost figure is ever silently recomputed**. A change to a specification, a component, or a published price marks the affected costs as stale and stops; an explicit roll-up call is what produces new numbers, and operations that consume cost figures refuse to run against stale ones. This makes the system's arithmetic reproducible and makes every invalidation visible to the client in the same response that caused it. The second is that **there is no floating-point arithmetic anywhere in this domain**. Money is carried as whole cents or whole micro-dollars, ratios as whole basis points, energy as whole micro-kcal and sodium as whole micrograms, so that two independent implementations of the same roll-up agree to the last unit.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based and mandatory on every endpoint. Each request must present two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they act under. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or present but containing nothing except whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five declared role names, or names a role that the targeted endpoint does not list as authorised, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. The five role names, spelled exactly as they must appear in the header, are `PlatformAdmin`, `RecipeDeveloper`, `CulinaryDirector`, `CostAnalyst` and `UnitManager`.

`PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator: it owns the store registry, shares the ingredient-catalogue creation surface with culinary R&D, and is the only role permitted to drive the logical clock. It has no authority over recipes, costs, menus or approvals. `RecipeDeveloper` is culinary R&D: it authors recipes and their component graphs, creates menu items, drafts price change proposals and specification change orders, and plans promotions — it submits work for approval and can never approve it. `CulinaryDirector` is the approval authority: it activates and retires recipes, ingredients and menu items, approves or rejects price change proposals and specification change orders, rolls an approved specification change back, and launches and decides the fate of promotions. `CostAnalyst` owns cost and menu engineering: it loads and publishes distributor feeds, runs every roll-up and plate-cost recomputation, applies approved price changes, drains specification propagation, and owns the period-review lifecycle together with promotion costing and post-promotion review. `UnitManager` is a store manager whose only write capability is recording physical inventory counts for their own store's period reviews. Every role in the system may read: all five roles are authorised on every read endpoint.

Role identity binds `X-User-ID` to the domain differently per role. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `RecipeDeveloper`, `CulinaryDirector` or `CostAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a price change proposal or a specification change order — but never compares it against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `UnitManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Unit`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Unit` compare the resource's own unit reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `UnitManager` writing a count line into a period review is permitted only when that review's `unit_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. A `CostAnalyst` may write count lines into any store's review and is not subject to that comparison.

Segregation of duties is enforced independently of role membership. Whenever an operator submits a price change proposal or a specification change order, their `X-User-ID` is recorded on the record as `submitted_by_user_id`. The subsequent approval must come from a different operator: if the approver's `X-User-ID` equals the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403, even when that operator legitimately holds the `CulinaryDirector` role. Holding an approving role is necessary but not sufficient; not having authored the record is the second condition.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty characters drawn from upper-case letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's real UTC clock at the moment the response is built, and it is the one timestamp anywhere in this API that does **not** come from the logical clock of §2.4 — it therefore advances between two requests even when the logical clock has not moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows in the response; when omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the first returned row; when omitted it defaults to 0, and it must not be negative. A `limit` outside the accepted range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific list endpoint states a different ordering, collections are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock of §2.4 only moves when a `PlatformAdmin` explicitly advances it, a great many rows share one identical `created_at`, so every list ordering in this API resolves whatever ties remain after its stated sort keys by the order in which the rows were created — earlier-created row first, even where the leading sort key runs descending. A list is therefore fully determined even when every row in it was created inside a single logical instant. Several lists in this domain do state a different ordering — price lines for an ingredient come back oldest effective window first, period reviews come back with the most recently ending period first, promotions come back window-start first — and each such deviation is described with the workflow that needs it.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, the Audit Stamp and the Logical Clock

Every resource this API persists — stores, ingredients, price feeds, price lines, recipes, recipe components, prep guides, menu items, price change proposals, specification change orders, period reviews, mix lines, count sheet lines and promotion campaigns — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured decoration; a recipe's `id` and a menu item's `id` are indistinguishable in shape.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent modification, in the same format.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by exactly one on every mutation of the row.

The `version` counter is the load-bearing part of that quartet, and it is deliberately generous about what counts as a mutation. A write that changes only a derived field is still a mutation: when a price feed publication flips a recipe's `cost_state` without touching a single component, the recipe's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed. When a specification change order opens against a recipe and pushes that recipe's `open_spec_change_count` up by one, the recipe's `version` increments — which is exactly why a second order authored against the earlier read will be refused. Every cascade hop described anywhere in this document increments the `version` of every row it touches, and refreshes that row's `updated_at`.

Time in this system is a server-held logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Before the first clock write the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it forward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint; the clock is strictly monotonic, so an instant earlier than the current reading is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` and HTTP 422. Advancing the clock modifies no entity and fires no cascade, and because it has no resource to return it answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` object rather than any representation of the clock — it only changes what "now" means for the price windows, cooling-off intervals, promotion windows and effective dates described below. Every timestamp the system writes itself (`retired_at`, `published_at`, `last_rollup_at`, `removed_at`, `approved_at`, `applied_at`, `closed_at`, `reviewed_at`, `last_price_change_at`) is the logical clock's reading at the moment of the write. Instants the *client* supplies and the system stores — a feed's `feed_date`, a price line's `effective_from` and `effective_to`, a review's `period_start` and `period_end`, a campaign's `window_start` and `window_end`, and a proposal's `effective_from` — are normalised to UTC when they are accepted and are read back in exactly the same three-fractional-digit form as the timestamps above, whatever offset or fractional precision they were written with.

### 2.5. Integer Arithmetic, Units of Measure and Rounding

There is no floating-point arithmetic in this domain, and no monetary value is ever transported as a decimal. Four unit systems are in play and every field name states which one it belongs to:

- **Micro-dollars** (`_micros` suffix on a money field): one US dollar is 1000000 micro-dollars and one cent is 10000 micro-dollars. Pack costs, unit costs, extended component costs, batch costs, portion costs and plate costs are carried this way.
- **Whole cents** (`_cents` suffix): menu prices, plate cost in cents, contribution margins, gross sales, theoretical and actual food cost and every promotion figure.
- **Basis points** (`_bps` suffix): one percent is 100 basis points, so 10000 basis points is exactly one whole. Yields, shrink factors, food cost ratios, mix shares, elasticity assumptions, price deltas, variances and every threshold expressed as a proportion are carried this way.
- **Micro-kcal and micrograms**: energy is carried as micro-kcal, where one kilocalorie is 1000000 micro-kcal; sodium is carried as whole micrograms.

Three rounding behaviours are used, and which one applies is always stated at the point of use:

- **Round half up** is the default for every division that produces a reported figure. For a non-negative numerator, divide the numerator by the divisor to obtain a whole quotient and a remainder; if twice the remainder is greater than or equal to the divisor, add one to the quotient, otherwise keep it. For a negative numerator the magnitude is rounded by exactly that rule and the negative sign is then restored, so a value sitting exactly halfway always rounds away from zero.
- **Ceiling** is used in exactly one place: converting an edible-portion quantity into the as-purchased quantity that must be bought. You cannot buy a fraction of a base unit and expect the yield to cover the specification, so the quotient is rounded up whenever any remainder exists.
- **Floor** is used in exactly two places: an ingredient's combined net yield, and a count sheet line's expected usage. In both, the whole quotient is kept and any remainder is discarded, whatever its size — these two figures are never rounded up and never rounded half up.

Because these rules are applied at every intermediate step rather than only at the end, the **order of rounding is part of the specification**, not an implementation detail. Wherever a calculation below says a figure is rounded before being summed, or that one factor is applied before another with a rounding in between, collapsing the steps into a single expression produces a different — and wrong — answer.

### 2.6. Cost Freshness: the STALE and FRESH Contract

The system never recomputes a cost behind the client's back. Each recipe carries a `cost_state` that is either `STALE` or `FRESH`, and each menu item carries a `plate_cost_state` with the same two values. A newly created recipe starts `STALE`; a newly created menu item starts `STALE`. A state becomes `FRESH` only as the immediate result of a successful roll-up call on that specific row, and it reverts to `STALE` the moment anything the last roll-up consumed changes underneath it — a component added or removed, a quantity re-specified, a distributor price published, or a sub-recipe's own cost going stale.

When a cost goes stale the previously computed figures are **not** cleared. A recipe that flips from `FRESH` to `STALE` keeps the `batch_cost_micros` and `portion_cost_micros` the last roll-up wrote, and every component keeps the `unit_cost_micros_used`, `pack_base_units_snapshot` and `extended_cost_micros` it was last given. The figures are simply no longer trustworthy, and the system says so through the state flag rather than by zeroing data. Alongside the flag each recipe records the `cost_basis` its current figures were produced under and the `last_rollup_at` instant they were produced at; both are null before the first roll-up. A menu item records the same `cost_basis` for its plate cost.

Invalidation propagates in the same request that causes it, and it propagates **upward** through the recipe graph. A recipe holding an active sub-recipe component whose child recipe just went stale is itself stale, and that reasoning repeats transitively up the graph until it reaches recipes that were already stale or that consume nothing newly stale. An already-`STALE` recipe stops the chain at that node — it does not need flipping and its parents are not revisited on its account. From every plated recipe that goes stale, the invalidation continues sideways into the menu items backed by that recipe: their `plate_cost_state` flips to `STALE` and their `margin_class` resets to `UNCLASSIFIED`, because a menu-engineering classification computed against a plate cost nobody has recomputed is worse than no classification at all. Two qualifications on that sideways step are stated precisely by the workflows that perform it, and this paragraph is deliberately the weaker summary of them. First, an item whose `plate_cost_state` is already `STALE` is normally passed over rather than rewritten a second time (§3.2, §3.3), the one exception being the menu items of the very recipe a specification change order changed or rolled back, which are rewritten unconditionally because they must take that recipe's new allergen and sodium figures whatever state they were already in (§3.6). Second, one flip to `STALE` propagates nowhere at all: a recipe's own retirement sets its `cost_state` to `STALE` as pure bookkeeping and walks neither parents nor menu items (§3.4). Operations that consume a cost figure — recipe review, recipe activation, plate costing, menu-item activation, proposal submission, proposal approval and promotion costing — refuse to run against a stale one and report `ERR_COST_STALE` with HTTP 409.

### 2.7. Expected-Version Guards on Specification and Promotion Writes

Four write operations in this API take a client-supplied expected version of a **parent** row and refuse to proceed when it no longer matches. These are the only optimistic-concurrency guards in the system; no other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and clients must not send one. In every one of the four cases the field is a required plain integer carrying no range constraint of its own: only a non-integer value (or an absent one) is the malformed request of §2.10 position 3, while a zero or a negative expected version is perfectly well formed, simply cannot equal a stored `version` — which starts at 1 and only ever climbs — and is therefore answered by the mismatch below with HTTP 412 rather than by HTTP 400.

- `POST /culinary/recipes/{recipe_id}/components` requires `expected_recipe_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Recipe`. If it does not equal the recipe's stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A state precondition cannot substitute here: appending a second, different component to a recipe is entirely legitimate product behaviour, so the only thing distinguishing an informed append from one authored against a specification the client can no longer see is whether the expected version still matches.
- `DELETE /culinary/recipe-components/{recipe_component_id}` requires `expected_recipe_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Recipe` that owns the component. A mismatch with the recipe's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Removing a second, different component is likewise legitimate, so the stale-version comparison is the only way to detect that the caller decided against a specification they are no longer looking at.
- `POST /culinary/spec-change-orders` requires `expected_recipe_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Recipe` the order targets. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Because opening an order itself increments the recipe's `version` (it moves `open_spec_change_count`), a second order authored from the same read of the recipe is guaranteed to be caught by this guard; a genuinely sequential second order, authored after re-reading the recipe, passes.
- `POST /culinary/lto-campaigns` requires `expected_menu_item_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `MenuItem`. A mismatch with the menu item's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MENU_ITEM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A single menu item may legitimately carry several campaigns over disjoint windows, so the create cannot be gated on a state precondition; the stale expected version is what proves the promotion was planned against a price or a plate cost that has since moved.

Note the deliberate asymmetry with price change proposals: creating a proposal takes **no** expected-version field, because the conflict a proposal create guards against is a second live proposal on the same item, which is a state precondition rather than a stale read.

### 2.8. Regional Cost and Price Parameters

Every store, every price feed, every price line, every menu item and every promotion is stamped with a `region_code` drawn from exactly four values: `NE`, `SE`, `MW` and `WEST`. Four region-indexed parameter sets hang off that code and are referenced throughout §3.

The **freight uplift** applied when a portion cost becomes a plate cost is 600 basis points in `NE`, 250 basis points in `SE`, 0 basis points in `MW` and 450 basis points in `WEST`; that is `REGION_FREIGHT_UPLIFT_BPS`. The **market cap multiplier** used when modelling a price change is 11500 basis points in `NE`, 10500 in `SE`, 11000 in `MW` and 12000 in `WEST`; that is `REGION_CAP_MULTIPLIER_BPS`. The **competitor price index**, expressed in whole cents, is 1750 in `NE`, 1450 in `SE`, 1500 in `MW` and 1900 in `WEST`; that is `REGION_COMPETITOR_INDEX_CENTS`.

One region is singled out as the costing reference. Ingredient unit costs consumed by any roll-up are always selected from price rows whose `region_code` is `MW` — the `REFERENCE_REGION_CODE`. A recipe's cost is therefore a single group-wide number regardless of who eats the plate, and regional differences enter only later, at the plate-cost step, through the freight uplift above. Feeds for the other three regions are still loaded, published and visible; they simply do not feed a recipe roll-up.

### 2.9. Allergen Bitmasks and Nutrition Roll-Up Units

Allergens are carried as a single integer bitmask so that a set union is a bitwise OR and a "declared covers actual" test is a bitwise AND. Exactly eight bits are defined: milk is 1, egg is 2, gluten is 4, peanut is 8, soy is 16, fish is 32, shellfish is 64 and tree nut is 128. A mask therefore ranges from 0 (no allergen) through 255 (all eight). This convention applies to an ingredient's own `allergen_mask`, to the mask a recipe rolls up across its whole component tree, to the copy a menu item holds, and to the `declared_allergen_mask` printed on the menu.

Per-portion nutrition figures travel with the same integer discipline as everything else: `kcal_per_base_unit_micros` and `kcal_per_portion_micros` are micro-kcal, and `sodium_mcg_per_base_unit` and `sodium_mcg_per_portion` are whole micrograms.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Validation Ordering

Requests are validated in one fixed order, and the **first** rule that fails determines the response; no later rule is evaluated and no partial mutation is persisted. The order below is exhaustive for this API. Where an operation-specific narrative in §3 mentions several checks, they are evaluated in this global order regardless of the order the narrative happens to introduce them in.

1. Both auth headers present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and is authorised on the target endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. Request body and query string parse; every required field is present; every field has its declared type; every enum value is a declared member; every integer respects its declared range; every ISO 8601 instant is well formed; `limit` is within 1 through 100 and `offset` is not negative; and where both bounds of a window are present, `period_start` is strictly before `period_end` and `window_start` is strictly before `window_end` — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Several readings of that rule are fixed explicitly. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so what such a request reports is its missing required fields rather than a parse failure, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself malformed. An explicit `null` is treated exactly as an absent field: a required field is reported missing, and an optional field falls back to its documented default. An identifier field carrying the empty string is malformed rather than merely unmatched, and so is reported here rather than by one of the 404 rules below. Declared types are checked without any coercion — a numeric string is never accepted as an integer, and a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared. An instant may be written with a `Z` suffix or with a numeric UTC offset and is normalised to UTC, and one carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. `limit` and `offset` must each be a base-10 integer before their ranges are examined. And a filter query parameter whose value falls outside the enumeration that filter draws from is rejected here rather than silently matching nothing, whereas a query parameter this API does not define for the endpoint is ignored. The two window comparisons named above are the only ordering comparisons made between a pair of supplied instants; a price line's `effective_from` and `effective_to` are deliberately not compared, so a line may be loaded with an `effective_to` at or before its `effective_from`.
4. A `unit_id` in the path or body identifies an existing store — `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. This applies to the single-store read and to period-review creation.
5. An `ingredient_id` in the path or body identifies an existing ingredient — `ERR_INGREDIENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404, on every endpoint that takes one in either place.
6. A `price_feed_id` in the path identifies an existing feed — `ERR_PRICE_FEED_NOT_FOUND`, 404, on every feed-scoped endpoint.
7. A `recipe_id` in the path or body — including the `child_recipe_id` of a sub-recipe component — identifies an existing recipe — `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND`, 404. This covers every recipe-scoped endpoint plus menu-item creation, specification-change-order creation and component creation.
8. A `recipe_component_id` in the path, or a `target_component_id` in the body, identifies an existing component — `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404, on the component read, the component removal and specification-change-order creation.
9. A `menu_item_id` in the path or body identifies an existing menu item — `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, 404. This covers every menu-item-scoped endpoint plus proposal creation, promotion creation and mix-line loading.
10. A `proposal_id` in the path identifies an existing price change proposal — `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
11. A `spec_change_order_id` in the path identifies an existing specification change order — `ERR_SPEC_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
12. A `period_review_id` in the path identifies an existing period review — `ERR_PERIOD_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
13. An `lto_campaign_id` in the path identifies an existing promotion campaign — `ERR_LTO_NOT_FOUND`, 404.
14. A `UnitManager` writing a count line targets a review whose `unit_id` equals their `X-User-ID` — `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
15. `expected_recipe_version` equals the stored recipe `version` — `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION`, 412, on component creation, component removal and specification-change-order creation.
16. `expected_menu_item_version` equals the stored menu-item `version` — `ERR_STALE_MENU_ITEM_VERSION`, 412, on promotion creation.
17. The recipe whose component set is being edited directly is `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_MUTABLE`, 409, on component creation and component removal.
18. The recipe referenced by the operation is in a status the operation allows — `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on recipe review submission, recipe activation, recipe retirement, menu-item activation, specification-change-order creation and specification-change-order approval. The same gate also rejects a review submission on a recipe with no active components, because an empty specification is not reviewable in any status.
19. The feed being written to or published is `DRAFT` and has at least one line before publication — `ERR_FEED_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`, 409, on feed line creation and feed publication.
20. The menu item is in a status the operation allows — `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on menu-item activation, proposal submission, promotion costing, recipe retirement and mix-line loading.
21. The price change proposal is in a status the operation allows — `ERR_PROPOSAL_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on proposal editing, submission, revision request, approval and application.
22. The specification change order's `approval_status` is the one the operation requires — `ERR_SCO_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on order submission, approval, rejection, propagation and rollback.
23. The period review is in a status the operation allows — `ERR_REVIEW_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on mix-line loading, count-line loading, classification and closing.
24. The promotion campaign is in a status the operation allows — `ERR_LTO_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on campaign costing, launch, close-review and decision.
25. Every count sheet line of the review being closed has `sync_state` equal to `CURRENT` — `ERR_COUNT_SHEET_OUT_OF_DATE`, 409.
26. The cost figures the operation consumes are not stale — `ERR_COST_STALE`, 409 — on recipe roll-up, recipe review submission, recipe activation, plate-cost recomputation, menu-item activation, proposal submission, proposal approval and promotion costing.
27. The approver's `X-User-ID` differs from the record's `submitted_by_user_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, on proposal approval and specification-change-order approval. This sits deliberately **below** the state and freshness gates, so approving a record that is in the wrong state, or whose recipe has left `ACTIVE`, or whose plate cost went stale, reports that problem even when the approver also happens to be the submitter.
28. The component targeted by the operation is still `ACTIVE` — `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_REMOVED`, 409 — on component removal, specification-change-order creation, submission and approval.
29. The requested propagation target is not already reflected in `propagation_status` — `ERR_PROPAGATION_ALREADY_SYNCED`, 409.
30. Every active sub-recipe component of the recipe being activated points at a child recipe that is itself `ACTIVE` — `ERR_SUBRECIPE_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409.
31. No ingredient the operation depends on is `RETIRED` — `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED`, 422 — on ingredient retirement (so a second retirement is refused rather than silently repeated), component creation, recipe activation, specification-change-order approval of an ingredient addition, and feed publication.
32. The menu item's `declared_allergen_mask` covers every bit of the backing recipe's rolled-up `allergen_mask` — `ERR_ALLERGEN_UNDECLARED`, 422, on menu-item activation.
33. A kids-menu item's backing recipe is within the sodium ceiling — `ERR_NUTRITION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422, on menu-item activation.
34. A sub-recipe component does not close a cycle in the recipe graph — `ERR_RECIPE_CYCLE`, 422, on component creation.
35. The resulting depth of the owning recipe and of every recipe that transitively contains it stays within the depth ceiling — `ERR_RECIPE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, 422, on component creation and recipe activation.
36. The submitted ingredient `sku` is unused — `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, 409, on ingredient creation.
37. The submitted `code` is unused among entities of the same type — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, 409, on store creation, recipe creation and menu-item creation.
38. The menu item has no other live price change proposal — `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_OPEN`, 409, on proposal creation.
39. The revision cycle terminates rather than raising a fiscal error: below the revision ceiling a revision request reopens the proposal, at exactly the ceiling the same call rejects it outright, and any further revision request against the now-rejected proposal is reported by the status gate at position 21 as `ERR_PROPOSAL_INVALID_STATE`, 409.
40. The review has at least one mix line before classification — `ERR_EMPTY_MIX`, 422.
41. The review has at least one count sheet line before closing — `ERR_EMPTY_COUNT_SHEET`, 422.
42. A proposal's `effective_from` respects the cooling-off interval since the item's last applied price change — `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_COOLING_OFF`, 409, on proposal submission.
43. Launching would not exceed the per-region ceiling on overlapping launched campaigns — `ERR_LTO_WINDOW_CAPACITY`, 409.
44. The logical clock has reached the instant the operation waits for — `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, 409 — a proposal's `effective_from` for application, a campaign's `window_end` for close-review.
45. No published line's window intersects an existing published window for the same ingredient and region — `ERR_PRICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409, on feed publication.
46. The requested clock instant is at or after the current logical clock — `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`, 422.
47. The proposed price is within the effective cap price — `ERR_MARKET_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 422, on proposal submission.
48. The proposed price leaves at least the margin floor above the plate cost — `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`, 422, on proposal submission.
49. Every ingredient component of the recipe being rolled up resolves to a unit cost under the requested basis, after the weighted-average fallback has been attempted — `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_PRICE`, 422.

Two consequences of that ordering deserve calling out because they are easy to get backwards. First, positions 15 and 16 sit **above** every state gate: a stale expected version is reported even when the target recipe is also in a non-mutable status, so a component append against an `ACTIVE` recipe with a stale expected version yields `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION`, not `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_MUTABLE`. Second, position 47 sits above position 48: a proposal that breaches both the market cap and the margin floor reports `ERR_MARKET_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

When a path names several resources — a feed and its lines, a recipe and its prep guides, a review and its count lines — identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the response reports the first identifier in the path that matches nothing. A request for the lines of a non-existent feed therefore reports `ERR_PRICE_FEED_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches any body-level *identifier* check. That precedence is absolute **within the identifier rules**: every identifier carried in the path is resolved before any identifier carried in the body, so positions 4 through 13 order the body identifiers of one request among themselves rather than promoting a body identifier over a path identifier. It does not, however, lift identifier resolution above the three rules that precede it, and that is the limit of the phrase "absolute" here. Positions 1, 2 and 3 are evaluated first on every endpoint without exception, and nothing named in the path is looked up until all three have passed: a call carrying no or unusable auth headers reports `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` or `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even when every identifier in its path matches nothing; a write whose body is malformed — a missing required field, a wrongly typed one, an out-of-range integer, a badly formed instant, a window whose bounds are the wrong way round, or a field combination the stated `component_type` or `change_type` forbids — reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the path identifier matches nothing; and a list read whose `limit`, `offset` or enum filter is unacceptable likewise reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before its path identifier is resolved. The ordering that holds everywhere is therefore: rule 1, then rule 2, then the whole of rule 3, then the path identifiers left to right, then the body identifiers in the order positions 4 through 13 give them, then every rule from position 14 downward. Loading a price line that names a non-existent ingredient into a non-existent feed therefore reports `ERR_PRICE_FEED_NOT_FOUND`, adding a component that names a non-existent ingredient to a non-existent recipe reports `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND`, and recording a count line for a non-existent ingredient against a non-existent review reports `ERR_PERIOD_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Where several identifiers do sit in the body of one request — a specification change order's `recipe_id`, `target_component_id` and `ingredient_id` — the numbering above governs, so the `ingredient_id` is resolved before the `recipe_id` and the `recipe_id` before the `target_component_id`. Two rejections happen even earlier than rule 1, because they occur before any endpoint has been selected and therefore before any role can be authorised against one: a request to a path this API does not define at all is answered `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, and a request to a defined path using a method that path does not support is answered `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405. These are the only two situations in which that error id does not carry the HTTP 400 recorded for it in §5, and both are reported regardless of whether the auth headers were present.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Purchasing Catalogue: Stores and Ingredients

Everything else in this system hangs off two flat registries. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each store by supplying a `code`, a `name` and a `region_code`. The store code is a human-readable identifier of three to twelve characters drawn only from upper-case letters, digits and the hyphen, and it must be unique across all stores; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The `name` is a display string of one to eighty characters. The store's `region_code` is one of `NE`, `SE`, `MW` or `WEST`, and it is what a store manager's inventory counts are ultimately scoped by.

Ingredients are the purchasing leaves of the recipe graph and may be registered either by a `RecipeDeveloper` or by a `PlatformAdmin`. Registration supplies a `sku` — the distributor-independent purchasing identifier, three to twenty characters of upper-case letters, digits and hyphens, unique across all ingredients, with a repeat refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` — together with a `name` of one to eighty characters and a `base_uom` that fixes the unit every quantity for that ingredient will be expressed in: `GRAM`, `MILLILITER` or `EACH`. Purchasing arrives in packs, so registration also states `pack_base_units`, the number of base units in one purchased pack and at least one; it is the divisor that turns a delivered pack cost into a per-base-unit cost.

Yield is where a chef's specification and a buyer's order diverge, and two basis-point figures capture it. `trim_yield_bps` is the butchery or trim yield: how many edible-portion units survive per 10000 as-purchased units, so a value of 6800 means a third of the mass is lost to bone and trim. It ranges from 1 through 10000. `cook_shrink_bps` is the further loss during cooking, stated as basis points lost out of 10000 and ranging from 0 through 9999, and it is applied *on top of* the trim yield rather than added to it. From those two the system derives `net_yield_bps`, the combined yield actually used to convert an edible-portion specification into an as-purchased quantity: multiply `trim_yield_bps` by the difference between 10000 and `cook_shrink_bps`, divide by 10000, and discard any fractional part. The derived value is always at least 1. Any `net_yield_bps` a client supplies at registration is ignored — the server computes it.

Registration also captures the food-safety and nutrition payload the recipe tree will roll up: the ingredient's own `allergen_mask` as an integer from 0 through 255 under the bit convention of §2.9, its `kcal_per_base_unit_micros` as a non-negative micro-kcal figure, and its `sodium_mcg_per_base_unit` as a non-negative microgram figure. Every new ingredient starts with `status` set to `ACTIVE` and `retired_at` null.

Retirement is a purchasing decision, and a `CulinaryDirector` makes it. Retiring an ingredient moves its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED` — a terminal state — writes `retired_at` from the logical clock and increments the ingredient's `version`. What retirement deliberately does **not** do is touch a single cost figure: no specification and no published price changed, so every recipe's `cost_state` and every menu item's `plate_cost_state` are left exactly as they were, and every published price row for that ingredient remains usable. A roll-up that reaches a retired ingredient prices it perfectly normally. What retirement blocks is future commitment: a retired ingredient may not be added as a component, its presence in a recipe blocks that recipe's activation, and a feed quoting it cannot be published. Because `RETIRED` is terminal, a second retirement attempt on an already retired ingredient is refused with `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED` rather than treated as a harmless repeat. Independently of that lifecycle, an ingredient carries one further read surface: every count sheet line ever recorded against it is listable across all period reviews at once, in `created_at` ascending order, optionally narrowed to a single `sync_state` of `CURRENT` or `OUT_OF_DATE`.

### 3.2. Distributor Price Feeds and the Five-Hop Publication Cascade

Prices reach the system in batches. A `CostAnalyst` opens a feed by naming the `distributor_code` that submitted it — one of `BROADLINE`, `PRODUCE` or `PROTEIN` — the `region_code` every line in the feed applies to, and the `feed_date` the distributor states as its publication date — itself a required ISO 8601 instant, and one the system never compares against the logical clock or against the feed's own lines, so a feed may legitimately be dated in the past or in the future. A new feed opens with `status` `DRAFT`, `line_count` 0, `published_at` null and its three cascade counters — `superseded_price_count`, `affected_recipe_count` and `affected_menu_item_count` — all 0. Nothing a draft feed contains has any effect on any cost.

Each price line names an `ingredient_id`, a `pack_cost_micros` of at least one micro-dollar (the delivered cost of a single pack), an inclusive `effective_from` instant and an optional `effective_to` instant that marks the exclusive end of the price's validity; omitting `effective_to`, or sending it as null, makes the window open-ended. Four values on the line are server-owned and any client-supplied value for them is ignored: the line's `price_feed_id` is taken from the feed it is being loaded under, its `region_code` and `distributor_code` are copied from that feed, and its `pack_base_units_snapshot` is frozen from the ingredient's current `pack_base_units` at the moment the line is created. That snapshot, not the ingredient's live pack size, is the divisor every later roll-up uses when it reads this price. A new line's `status` is `PENDING`. Loading a line increments the feed's `line_count` and its `version`. Lines may only be added while the feed is `DRAFT`; adding one to an already published feed is refused with `ERR_FEED_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`, as is a second publication attempt on the same feed.

Publication is the event that makes prices real, and it is guarded four ways. The feed must still be `DRAFT` and must hold at least one line — an empty feed cannot publish, and that is reported as `ERR_FEED_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. Every line of the feed must reference an ingredient whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a single retired ingredient anywhere in the feed refuses the whole publication with `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED`. And no line may collide with a price window that is already in force.

Collision is judged on half-open windows — the interval runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive — and is evaluated per ingredient and region pair against rows whose `status` is already `PUBLISHED`. Three readings follow from that, and all three matter. A new line whose `effective_from` falls exactly on an existing bounded row's `effective_to` is *adjacent*, not overlapping, and is accepted. A new line whose `effective_from` falls strictly after an open-ended published row's `effective_from` does not collide either: it *truncates* that predecessor. A new line whose `effective_from` is at or before an existing row's `effective_from`, or falls strictly inside a bounded existing window, is a genuine collision and the publication is refused with `ERR_PRICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

Once those gates pass, publication fires a strictly sequential five-hop cascade in which each hop's inputs are precisely the rows the previous hop changed.

The **first hop** flips the feed's `status` to `PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, and moves every one of the feed's lines from `PENDING` to `PUBLISHED`. In the same hop, taking the feed's lines in the order they were loaded, for each newly published line any existing `PUBLISHED` row **belonging to some other feed** that covers the same ingredient and region, whose `effective_to` is null and whose `effective_from` is earlier than the new line's `effective_from`, is truncated in place: its `effective_to` becomes the new line's `effective_from` and its `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Rows loaded under the feed now publishing are never candidates for this truncation, and are equally never candidates for the collision gate above, since at gate time they are all still `PENDING`: two lines of one feed quoting the same ingredient and region therefore both publish and both stay exactly as they were written, neither refusing the other nor truncating the other. The feed's `superseded_price_count` records how many rows were superseded this way.

The **second hop** exists because a price for some ingredient became published. Every recipe that holds an active ingredient component pointing at that ingredient, and whose `cost_state` is currently `FRESH`, flips to `STALE`. Its cost figures are untouched and still show what the last roll-up wrote.

The **third hop** exists because those recipes went stale. Every recipe holding an active sub-recipe component whose child is one of the newly stale recipes also flips its `cost_state` to `STALE`, and that reasoning repeats transitively up the recipe graph until no further parent changes. The feed's `affected_recipe_count` records the number of distinct recipes flipped across the second and third hops together — a recipe that was already `STALE` is neither counted nor used as a starting point for further propagation.

The **fourth hop** exists because plated recipes went stale. Every menu item whose backing recipe is one of them, and whose `plate_cost_state` is currently `FRESH`, flips to `STALE`; the feed's `affected_menu_item_count` records how many. The **fifth hop** exists because those menu items' plate costs went stale: each of them has its `margin_class` reset to `UNCLASSIFIED`, and each also drags every promotion campaign booked against it whose `status` is `COSTED` back to `PLANNED` with its three costing figures cleared, by the reverse edge described in §3.9. A menu item that was already `STALE` is left entirely alone — it is not counted in `affected_menu_item_count`, its `margin_class` is not rewritten, and its campaigns are not disturbed. Every entity touched in any of the five hops has its `version` incremented.

Price history is observable. The full set of price rows quoted for one ingredient — across every feed and every region — is readable as a list ordered by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, optionally narrowed by `status` or by `region_code`. This is the only way to watch the publish-and-truncate behaviour above take effect, since a superseded row's rewritten `effective_to` and flipped `status` are visible nowhere else. The lines belonging to one feed are separately readable in `created_at` ascending order, optionally narrowed by `status`.

### 3.3. Composing the Recipe Graph and Rolling Up Its Cost

A `RecipeDeveloper` creates a recipe by supplying a `code` (three to twenty characters of upper-case letters, digits and hyphens, unique across recipes, with a repeat refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), a `name` of one to eighty characters, a `recipe_type`, a `base_uom` of `GRAM`, `MILLILITER` or `EACH`, a `batch_yield_base_units` of at least one, and a `portions_per_batch` of at least one. The `recipe_type` is either `SUB_RECIPE`, meaning the recipe may be consumed as a component of other recipes, or `PLATED`, meaning it may back a menu item. The `base_uom` describes this recipe's own batch yield, and it is the unit a parent recipe consumes a sub-recipe in. `batch_yield_base_units` is how many base units of finished product one batch produces and is the divisor used when a parent costs a sub-recipe line; `portions_per_batch` is how many portions one batch produces and is the divisor for portion cost and for per-portion nutrition.

A new recipe opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `cost_state` `STALE`, a null `cost_basis` and a null `last_rollup_at`, and with `batch_cost_micros`, `portion_cost_micros`, `allergen_mask`, `kcal_per_portion_micros`, `sodium_mcg_per_portion`, `depth`, `component_count` and `open_spec_change_count` all at 0. Because the cost starts stale, a roll-up is unavoidable before the recipe can be sent for review.

**Components.** The component set is directly editable only while the recipe's `status` is `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; an attempt against an `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED` recipe is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_MUTABLE`, and specification changes to a live recipe must instead go through the change-order workflow of §3.6. Adding a component names a `component_type` of either `INGREDIENT` or `SUB_RECIPE`. An `INGREDIENT` line must carry `ingredient_id` and must leave `child_recipe_id` null; a `SUB_RECIPE` line must carry `child_recipe_id` and must leave `ingredient_id` null. Every line states `ep_quantity_base_units`, the edible-portion quantity of at least one that this recipe's specification calls for, expressed in the referenced ingredient's or child recipe's own `base_uom`, and a `sequence_no` of at least one that fixes the deterministic evaluation order of components inside the recipe, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. `sequence_no` values carry no uniqueness constraint within a recipe: a second line repeating a `sequence_no` already in use is accepted rather than refused, which is exactly why that tie-break is specified at all. The add also carries `expected_recipe_version` as described in §2.7.

From the edible-portion quantity the system derives `ap_quantity_base_units`, the as-purchased quantity that must actually be bought. For an `INGREDIENT` line, multiply `ep_quantity_base_units` by 10000, divide by the ingredient's `net_yield_bps`, and round the quotient *up* to the next whole base unit whenever there is any remainder. For a `SUB_RECIPE` line the as-purchased quantity simply equals `ep_quantity_base_units`, because a sub-recipe batch has no trim or shrink of its own — the losses were already booked inside the child. A brand-new line has `status` `ACTIVE`, a null `removed_at`, and `unit_cost_micros_used`, `pack_base_units_snapshot` and `extended_cost_micros` all still at 0 until a roll-up fills them in.

Three constraints police what may be added. An ingredient whose `status` is `RETIRED` may not be added at all — `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED`. A sub-recipe line whose `child_recipe_id` is the owning recipe itself, or is any recipe that already reaches the owning recipe through active components, would make the recipe graph cyclic and is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_CYCLE`; because that gate exists, every roll-up walk in this system is guaranteed to terminate and never needs a visited-set escape hatch. And the resulting `depth` of the owning recipe, together with the depth of every recipe that transitively contains it, must stay at four or below — a five-level nest is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. Those three are the whole of it. In particular a `SUB_RECIPE` line's `child_recipe_id` is constrained by neither the child's `recipe_type` nor the child's `status`, so a `PLATED` recipe may be consumed as a component and a child that is still `DRAFT`, or one that has already been retired, may be named — the requirement that every sub-recipe child be `ACTIVE` is enforced only later, when the owning recipe is activated.

Adding a component is a specification change, so it invalidates costs: the owning recipe recomputes `component_count`, `allergen_mask`, `kcal_per_portion_micros`, `sodium_mcg_per_portion` and `depth` from the new component set, sets its `cost_state` to `STALE` and increments its `version`; and every menu item backed by that recipe flips its `plate_cost_state` to `STALE` with `margin_class` reset to `UNCLASSIFIED`. The upward walk of §2.6 runs in the same request, so every recipe that reaches this one through active sub-recipe components also flips `cost_state` to `STALE` transitively and increments its `version` — with its own five derived fields left alone, since its component set did not change — and the menu items of those ancestors are invalidated alongside the menu items of the edited recipe itself. On the menu-item side only an item whose `plate_cost_state` is currently `FRESH` is touched at all: an already-`STALE` item is left entirely alone, keeping its `version`, whatever `margin_class` a classification run last wrote and its promotions undisturbed, while each item that does flip drags every promotion campaign booked against it whose `status` is `COSTED` back to `PLANNED` by the reverse edge of §3.9. Two things a direct component edit deliberately does **not** do: it does not re-copy the recipe's recomputed `allergen_mask` and `sodium_mcg_per_portion` onto its menu items — those two copies move only when a plate cost is recomputed or when the change-order cascade of §3.6 carries them, so an item may legitimately advertise the mask of a specification that has since changed underneath it — and it does not touch the prep guide's `sync_state` or any count sheet line's `sync_state`, both of which are disturbed only by the change-order workflow of §3.6, never by an edit made directly while the recipe is `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW`.

Removal is a soft delete performed by a `RecipeDeveloper` and, like the add, is only permitted while the recipe is `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and only with a matching `expected_recipe_version`. The targeted line's `status` becomes `REMOVED` and its `removed_at` is stamped from the logical clock; the removed row is returned in the response. A removed line keeps its historical `extended_cost_micros` for audit and contributes nothing to any future roll-up. The owning recipe recomputes the same five derived fields from the remaining active components, goes `STALE` and bumps its `version`, and the same menu-item invalidation follows. Targeting a line that is already `REMOVED` is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_REMOVED`. `component_count` is by definition the number of components of the recipe whose `status` is `ACTIVE`.

Components are readable individually — showing the `unit_cost_micros_used` and `pack_base_units_snapshot` the last roll-up applied alongside the resulting `extended_cost_micros` — and as a filtered list. The list can be narrowed by owning recipe with `recipe_id`, by `child_recipe_id` to discover which recipes consume a given sub-recipe, by `ingredient_id` to discover which recipes consume a given ingredient, and by `status`; it is ordered `sequence_no` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. The three identifier filters are compared literally and are never themselves resolved against the store, so a `recipe_id`, `child_recipe_id` or `ingredient_id` that names nothing at all returns an empty page with `total` 0 rather than a not-found error.

**The cost roll-up.** A `CostAnalyst` asks for a recipe's cost to be recomputed, optionally naming a `basis`. The basis is either `LATEST_PUBLISHED` or `WEIGHTED_AVG_90D`, defaults to `LATEST_PUBLISHED` when omitted, and is recorded on the recipe as `cost_basis`. The call is gated on the recipe's own `status` at no point — the recipe-status rule at position 18 of §2.10 does not list the roll-up — so a `DRAFT`, an `UNDER_REVIEW`, an `ACTIVE` and even a `RETIRED` recipe may all be rolled up, and a successful run leaves a retired recipe `FRESH` again with a freshly stamped `last_rollup_at`.

Children come first. Every active sub-recipe component's child recipe must already have `cost_state` `FRESH` *and* a `cost_basis` equal to the basis this roll-up was asked for; if any child fails either test the whole call fails with `ERR_COST_STALE`. Children are never recomputed implicitly — a caller who wants a fresh three-level tree rolls up the deepest level first and works upward.

Under `LATEST_PUBLISHED`, each ingredient component's unit cost comes from exactly one price row, chosen from the rows whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose ingredient matches the component, whose `region_code` is the reference region `MW`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the current logical clock, and whose `effective_to` is either null or strictly after the current logical clock. Surviving candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending and then `created_at` ascending, and the first row wins; should two candidates still tie on both keys the earlier-created row wins, so the selection stays single-valued even when two lines of one feed quote the same ingredient, the same region and the same `effective_from` inside a single logical instant. The component's `unit_cost_micros_used` becomes that row's `pack_cost_micros` and its `pack_base_units_snapshot` becomes that row's own `pack_base_units_snapshot`.

Under `WEIGHTED_AVG_90D` the unit cost is instead a duration-weighted average over every `PUBLISHED` row for that ingredient in region `MW` whose validity window intersects the ninety days ending at the current logical clock — that is, the interval from ninety days before the clock, inclusive, to the clock itself, exclusive. Each candidate's weight is the number of *whole* twenty-four-hour days of intersection, truncated to an integer, and a candidate with zero whole days of intersection is dropped entirely. For each surviving candidate, express its price per base unit as its `pack_cost_micros` multiplied by 1000000, divided by its `pack_base_units_snapshot`, rounded half up. Multiply each candidate's per-base-unit figure by its weight, sum those products, sum the weights, divide the first sum by the second and round half up: that quotient is the component's `unit_cost_micros_used`. Because the figure is now expressed per million base units, the component's `pack_base_units_snapshot` is set to 1000000 so that the per-component arithmetic below is unchanged. Should every candidate be dropped, the variant falls back to the `LATEST_PUBLISHED` selection for that component; should that selection also find nothing, the roll-up fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_PRICE`.

With unit costs in hand, each active component's `extended_cost_micros` — its own contribution to the batch — is computed individually. For an `INGREDIENT` component, take `ap_quantity_base_units` (recomputed as the ceiling of `ep_quantity_base_units` multiplied by 10000 and divided by the ingredient's `net_yield_bps`), multiply by `unit_cost_micros_used`, divide by `pack_base_units_snapshot`, and round half up to whole micro-dollars. For a `SUB_RECIPE` component, `ap_quantity_base_units` equals `ep_quantity_base_units`, `unit_cost_micros_used` is set to the child recipe's `batch_cost_micros` and `pack_base_units_snapshot` to the child's `batch_yield_base_units`; the extended cost is then `ep_quantity_base_units` multiplied by the child's `batch_cost_micros`, divided by the child's `batch_yield_base_units`, rounded half up.

The recipe's `batch_cost_micros` is the sum of `extended_cost_micros` over its active components, and `portion_cost_micros` is `batch_cost_micros` divided by `portions_per_batch`, rounded half up. **The rounding order here is pinned to round-then-sum and is part of the contract:** each component's extended cost is rounded to whole micro-dollars first, and the already-rounded values are then added. Accumulating the exact fractional products and rounding once at the end is explicitly *not* the specification, and the two orders disagree whenever two or more components leave fractional remainders. Because `portions_per_batch` is always at least one, the portion division never divides by zero. Finally the roll-up sets `cost_state` to `FRESH`, stamps `last_rollup_at` from the logical clock, increments the recipe's `version` and increments the `version` of every component it wrote to. A recipe with no active components at all rolls up legitimately: `batch_cost_micros` and `portion_cost_micros` are both 0 and the recipe is nonetheless marked `FRESH`.

**Allergens, nutrition and depth.** The same roll-up call also refreshes the recipe's food-safety and nutrition profile by walking the active component set depth-first from the target recipe. The `allergen_mask` is the bitwise union of every active ingredient component's own `allergen_mask` and every active sub-recipe component's child `allergen_mask` — which means a plated recipe inherits allergens it never references directly, and an ingredient carrying a mask of 0 leaves the parent's mask unchanged, so removing such an ingredient does not necessarily change the mask at all. A child's contribution is the mask this very walk recomputes from the child's own live active component set, not the `allergen_mask` currently persisted on the child recipe row, so a parent's union is right even when the child is `STALE` or has never been rolled up once.

Energy is accumulated per batch. Each active ingredient component contributes its `ap_quantity_base_units` multiplied by the ingredient's `kcal_per_base_unit_micros`; each active sub-recipe component contributes `ep_quantity_base_units` multiplied by the child's own batch energy total, divided by the child's `batch_yield_base_units`, rounded half up. Sodium is accumulated by exactly the same shape from each ingredient's `sodium_mcg_per_base_unit`. The reported `kcal_per_portion_micros` is the batch energy total divided by `portions_per_batch`, rounded half up, and `sodium_mcg_per_portion` is the batch sodium total divided by `portions_per_batch`, rounded half up.

`depth` measures nesting: a recipe with no active sub-recipe component — all ingredient leaves — has a depth of 0; otherwise its depth is one more than the greatest depth among the children of its active sub-recipe components.

The same allergen, nutrition, depth and component-count arithmetic is also available as a **computed read** on any recipe. That read recalculates all of it from the live active component graph and returns it in the recipe's own response shape, but it persists nothing whatsoever and it works even when `cost_state` is `STALE` — which is precisely how a caller distinguishes what the graph says today from what the last roll-up persisted.

### 3.4. Review, Activation, Prep Guides and Retirement of a Recipe

A recipe reaches production through a review cycle that a `RecipeDeveloper` opens and a `CulinaryDirector` closes. Submitting a recipe for review moves it from `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and — this is the cyclic part — an already-`ACTIVE` recipe whose specification has changed is submitted for review by exactly the same action, moving `ACTIVE` to `UNDER_REVIEW` so that the component set becomes directly editable again. Both submissions enforce the same four preconditions. The recipe must be in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE`. The recipe must have at least one active component, and an empty specification is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE` as well, because a recipe with nothing in it is not a reviewable specification in any status. The recipe's `cost_state` must be `FRESH`, and a stale cost is refused with `ERR_COST_STALE`. And its `depth` must be within the four-level ceiling. Note the ordering carefully: the empty-specification refusal is part of the recipe-status gate at position 18 of §2.10 and therefore **outranks** the freshness gate at position 26 — a brand-new recipe with no components and a stale cost reports `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_COST_STALE`. A successful submission increments the recipe's `version`.

Activation is the `CulinaryDirector`'s decision and moves `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE`. Five things must hold. The recipe must actually be `UNDER_REVIEW`. Its `cost_state` must be `FRESH`, since activating on figures nobody recomputed is exactly the failure mode this system exists to prevent — otherwise `ERR_COST_STALE`. No active ingredient component may reference an ingredient whose `status` is `RETIRED` — otherwise `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED`. Every active sub-recipe component's child recipe must itself be `ACTIVE`, so a plated recipe can never go live on a sub-recipe that is still in draft — otherwise `ERR_SUBRECIPE_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the recipe's `depth` must be at four or below — otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`.

Activation is also where the kitchen-facing document is born. If no prep guide yet exists for the recipe, activation mints one with `revision_no` 1 and `sync_state` `CURRENT`, freezing three figures from the recipe as it now stands — `batch_yield_base_units_snapshot`, `portions_per_batch_snapshot` and `component_count_snapshot` — and recording `synced_spec_version` as the recipe's `version` *after* this transition's increment. Each prep guide also carries the `recipe_id` of the recipe it documents. If a prep guide already exists, activation leaves it entirely untouched, **including its `sync_state`**: re-activating a recipe after a specification change never silently marks an out-of-date prep guide as current again. That resynchronisation is a separate, explicit act described in §3.6. Activation increments the recipe's `version`. Prep guides are readable as a list per recipe, which will contain either zero rows or exactly one, and it is the only place the minted revision and the `OUT_OF_DATE` flag can be observed.

Retirement is the terminal move and is also the `CulinaryDirector`'s. An `ACTIVE` recipe may be retired only when nothing live still depends on it. If any menu item backed by this recipe is in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, the retirement is refused with `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`; the item must first be suspended or retired in its own right. If any active component whose `child_recipe_id` is this recipe belongs to a recipe in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ACTIVE`, the retirement is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE`. When retirement does succeed it freezes the recipe's cost figures where they stand, sets `cost_state` to `STALE` — retired figures are by definition no longer maintained — and increments the recipe's `version`. That flip is the one transition to `STALE` in this system that does **not** propagate: retirement walks no parents and touches no menu item, so a menu item that was suspended while holding a `FRESH` plate cost keeps that `plate_cost_state`, its `margin_class`, its promotions and its `version` exactly as they were when the recipe behind it retires. Nothing is lost by that, because the gates above guarantee no `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ACTIVE` recipe and no `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` menu item was still depending on the recipe.

### 3.5. Menu Items: From Portion Cost to Plate Cost

A menu item is the sellable face of a plated recipe in one region. A `RecipeDeveloper` creates it with a `code` (three to twenty characters of upper-case letters, digits and hyphens, unique across menu items, with a repeat refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), a `name` of one to eighty characters, a `recipe_id` that must reference a recipe whose `recipe_type` is `PLATED`, a `region_code` selecting the freight uplift, competitor index and cap multiplier that will apply, a `current_price_cents` of at least one whole cent, a `target_food_cost_bps` between 500 and 9000 basis points inclusive that is the item's food-cost ambition and is later read by the price-cap relief rule, a `declared_allergen_mask` between 0 and 255 stating what the printed menu warns about, and an optional `is_kids_menu` boolean that defaults to false when omitted. The `PLATED` requirement carries no error id of its own: a `recipe_id` that resolves to a `SUB_RECIPE` recipe is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400, reported after the recipe has been resolved at position 7 of §2.10 and before the duplicate-`code` comparison at position 37. The backing recipe's `status` is not examined at creation at all, so an item may legitimately be created on a `PLATED` recipe that is still `DRAFT`, or on one that has already been retired; every status question is deferred to activation.

Every new item opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `plate_cost_state` `STALE`, `margin_class` `UNCLASSIFIED`, a null `cost_basis`, a null `last_price_change_at`, `is_lto` false, and `open_lto_count`, `plate_cost_micros`, `plate_cost_cents`, `food_cost_bps`, `contribution_margin_cents`, `allergen_mask` and `sodium_mcg_per_portion` all at 0. Items can be listed with filters on `recipe_id`, `region_code`, `status` and `margin_class`, ordered `created_at` ascending. A `status` runs over `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED`, where `RETIRED` is terminal.

**Plate costing** is a `CostAnalyst` operation and it is deliberately *not* gated on the item's own status: a `DRAFT` item must be able to obtain a fresh plate cost, because a fresh plate cost is a precondition of activation. What it does require is that the backing recipe's `cost_state` be `FRESH`, failing which the call reports `ERR_COST_STALE`; the recipe's `cost_basis` is copied onto the item as its own `cost_basis` so that a reader always knows which pricing basis the plate cost rests on.

The arithmetic proceeds in four ordered steps, each rounding half up to whole micro-dollars before the next step consumes it. First, take the recipe's `portion_cost_micros` and apply the freight uplift for the item's `region_code`: multiply by the sum of 10000 and the region's uplift in basis points (600 for `NE`, 250 for `SE`, 0 for `MW`, 450 for `WEST`), then divide by 10000. Second, apply the plate waste allowance of 200 basis points to *that* result: multiply by 10200 and divide by 10000. Third, compute the garnish allowance as 150 basis points of the waste-adjusted figure — multiply by 150 and divide by 10000 — and then take whichever is larger, that proportional amount or the flat garnish floor of 8000 micro-dollars. Fourth, `plate_cost_micros` is the waste-adjusted figure plus the garnish allowance.

That step order is load-bearing. The uplift and the waste factor are applied *sequentially with a rounding between them*, so collapsing them into one multiplier built from the sum of the two basis-point figures produces a different and wrong answer. The garnish floor makes the plate cost a non-linear function of the portion cost: below a certain portion cost the garnish contributes the flat floor and above it the proportional term, so scaling a recipe's cost does not scale its plate cost.

From `plate_cost_micros` three reported figures follow. `plate_cost_cents` is `plate_cost_micros` divided by 10000, rounded half up. `food_cost_bps` is `plate_cost_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `current_price_cents`, rounded half up. `contribution_margin_cents` is `current_price_cents` minus `plate_cost_cents`, and it is **not** clamped — a plate that costs more than it sells for reports a negative contribution margin, which is information the menu engineers want rather than an error. The run also copies the recipe's `allergen_mask` onto the item's own `allergen_mask` and the recipe's `sodium_mcg_per_portion` onto the item's `sodium_mcg_per_portion`, sets `plate_cost_state` to `FRESH` and increments the item's `version`. `margin_class` is left exactly as it was: plate costing is not classification.

**Activation** belongs to the `CulinaryDirector` and admits an item to the menu from either `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED` — the `SUSPENDED` arm is what closes the cycle back to `ACTIVE` after a retired promotion. From any other status the request is refused with `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`. Four further conditions apply identically to both arms. The backing recipe's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE`. The item's `plate_cost_state` must be `FRESH`, otherwise `ERR_COST_STALE`. Every allergen bit set in the backing recipe's rolled-up `allergen_mask` must also be set in the item's `declared_allergen_mask` — the declared mask must cover the recipe's mask, including bits the recipe inherited only through sub-recipes and never referenced directly — and a single undeclared bit refuses activation with `ERR_ALLERGEN_UNDECLARED`. Finally, when `is_kids_menu` is true, the backing recipe's `sodium_mcg_per_portion` must be at most 640000 micrograms — that is, 640 milligrams of sodium per portion — and exceeding it is refused with `ERR_NUTRITION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; when `is_kids_menu` is false the sodium ceiling is not enforced at all. A successful activation increments the item's `version` and changes nothing else.

The reverse move, `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, is never requested directly. It happens only as a cascade from the retirement arm of a closed promotion, described in §3.9.

Every mix line ever recorded for one menu item, across every period review, is readable as a list ordered newest review first — the owning review's `period_end` descending, then the owning review's own creation order where two reviews share a `period_end`, and only then each line's creation order inside one review, so the lines belonging to one review always stay contiguous rather than interleaving with another review's. That list is how a price-change projection's baseline unit volume can be independently verified.

### 3.6. Specification Change Orders: Approval, the Four-Hop Cascade, Rollback and Propagation

Once a recipe is `ACTIVE`, its component set is frozen against direct edits. The only way to change it is a specification change order — a reviewable, snapshotted, reversible instruction that carries its own approval trail. Each order tracks **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `approval_status` runs `DRAFT`, `PENDING`, `APPROVED` and then either `REJECTED` or `ROLLED_BACK`, both terminal. `propagation_status` runs `NOT_STARTED`, then `PREP_GUIDE_SYNCED` or `COUNTS_SYNCED`, then `FULLY_PROPAGATED`. The propagation dimension only ever advances while `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, and a rollback resets the propagation dimension to `NOT_STARTED` without disturbing the approval dimension's terminal value.

A `RecipeDeveloper` opens an order against a recipe that must be `ACTIVE` — any other status is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE` — and must supply `expected_recipe_version`, because the whole point of an order is that it targets a specification the author has actually read. The `change_type` selects one of three shapes, and each shape fixes which of the optional fields must be present and which must be null. `COMPONENT_QTY` re-quantifies an existing line: it requires `target_component_id` and `new_ep_quantity_base_units` (at least one base unit) and requires `ingredient_id` to be null. `COMPONENT_ADD` appends an ingredient line: it requires `ingredient_id` and `new_ep_quantity_base_units` and requires `target_component_id` to be null. `COMPONENT_REMOVE` retires an existing line: it requires `target_component_id` and requires both `ingredient_id` and `new_ep_quantity_base_units` to be null. Creating the order increments the recipe's `open_spec_change_count` and its `version` — which is exactly why a second order authored against the same read of the recipe is rejected by the version guard. The new order opens with `approval_status` `DRAFT`, `propagation_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and null `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `rejection_reason`, `created_component_id`, `approved_at` and every `pre_change_` snapshot.

**Submission** moves `DRAFT` to `PENDING` and is a `RecipeDeveloper`'s act — the role is what is checked and never the individual, so any `RecipeDeveloper` may submit an order another one opened, and it is the caller of *this* request whose identity the approval's segregation-of-duties comparison later rules out. For a `COMPONENT_QTY` or `COMPONENT_REMOVE` order the target component must still have `status` `ACTIVE`; if it was removed in the meantime the submission is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_REMOVED`. Submission records the caller as `submitted_by_user_id` and increments the order's `version`.

**Rejection** moves `PENDING` to `REJECTED` and is the `CulinaryDirector`'s. It stores the supplied `rejection_reason` — a required string carrying no length constraint at all, so an empty string is accepted and recorded verbatim — decrements the recipe's `open_spec_change_count` and increments the recipe's `version`. Critically it leaves the recipe specification completely untouched and the recipe's `cost_state` exactly as it was — a rejected order costs the recipe nothing but a version bump — and it leaves `propagation_status` at `NOT_STARTED` forever, since there is nothing to propagate.

**Approval** moves `PENDING` to `APPROVED`, belongs to the `CulinaryDirector`, and is the most consequential operation in the system. Its preconditions are checked in a fixed order. The order must be `PENDING`. The owning recipe must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_INVALID_STATE` — and this check happens **before** the segregation-of-duties comparison, so an order against a recipe that has since been suspended into review reports the recipe-status error even when the approver is also the submitter. Then the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then the target component (for `COMPONENT_QTY` and `COMPONENT_REMOVE`) must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_REMOVED`. Then, for `COMPONENT_ADD` only, the referenced ingredient's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_INGREDIENT_RETIRED`.

Before it changes anything, approval takes its snapshots: `pre_change_batch_cost_micros` from the recipe's `batch_cost_micros`, `pre_change_allergen_mask` from the recipe's `allergen_mask`, `pre_change_recipe_version` from the recipe's `version`, and `pre_change_ep_quantity_base_units` from the target component's `ep_quantity_base_units` — which is null for a `COMPONENT_ADD`, since there is no prior line. It also sets `approved_by_user_id` to the caller and `approved_at` to the current logical-clock instant.

Approval then fires a four-hop cascade, each hop caused by the previous one.

**Hop 1 — the specification edit.** For `COMPONENT_QTY`, the target component's `ep_quantity_base_units` becomes `new_ep_quantity_base_units` and its `ap_quantity_base_units` is recomputed from that new figure by the same ceiling rule that governs any ingredient line. For `COMPONENT_ADD`, a new component is minted with `component_type` `INGREDIENT`, the supplied `ingredient_id`, a null `child_recipe_id`, `ep_quantity_base_units` equal to `new_ep_quantity_base_units`, `ap_quantity_base_units` derived by the ceiling rule, `sequence_no` equal to the recipe's current `component_count` plus one, `status` `ACTIVE`, a null `removed_at`, and `extended_cost_micros`, `unit_cost_micros_used` and `pack_base_units_snapshot` all 0 — the new line carries no costing figures whatsoever until a roll-up gives it some; the new component's identifier is recorded in `created_component_id` so a rollback can retire exactly that line and no other. For `COMPONENT_REMOVE`, the target component's `status` becomes `REMOVED` and its `removed_at` is stamped. **No cost figure is recomputed by this transition.** The `extended_cost_micros` of every existing line and the recipe's `batch_cost_micros` are left exactly as the last roll-up wrote them. Approval changes the specification and reports that the money is now unknown; it does not compute the new money.

**Hop 2 — the recipe and its ancestors.** Because the component set changed, the owning recipe recomputes `component_count`, `allergen_mask`, `kcal_per_portion_micros`, `sodium_mcg_per_portion` and `depth` from the new component set, flips `cost_state` to `STALE`, decrements `open_spec_change_count` and increments `version`. Then, because a child recipe's cost went stale, every recipe holding an active sub-recipe component whose `child_recipe_id` is a recipe just flipped to `STALE` also flips `cost_state` to `STALE` and increments `version`, repeated transitively up the recipe graph until no further parent changes. Those ancestors' own component sets did not change, so their `allergen_mask`, `kcal_per_portion_micros`, `sodium_mcg_per_portion`, `depth` and `component_count` are left exactly as they were. Hop 2 also enqueues the propagation work: it flips the recipe's prep guide `sync_state` to `OUT_OF_DATE`, and it flips `sync_state` to `OUT_OF_DATE` on every count sheet line that belongs to a period review whose `status` is not `CLOSED` and whose `ingredient_id` is consumed by an active component of this recipe — meaning named directly by one of this recipe's own active `INGREDIENT` components, and deliberately not reached transitively through one of its sub-recipes, so this set is narrower than the set of ingredients the §3.8 explosion would attribute to the recipe. Closed reviews are historical records and are never touched.

**Hop 3 — the menu items.** Because the recipe's allergen roll-up and cost state changed, every menu item whose `recipe_id` is this recipe copies the recipe's new `allergen_mask` and new `sodium_mcg_per_portion`, flips `plate_cost_state` to `STALE`, resets `margin_class` to `UNCLASSIFIED` and increments `version`. Menu items backed by one of the *transitively* staled ancestor recipes are treated differently and the difference matters: they flip `plate_cost_state` to `STALE`, reset `margin_class` to `UNCLASSIFIED` and increment `version`, but they **keep** their existing `allergen_mask` and `sodium_mcg_per_portion`, because the ancestor recipes' own roll-ups did not move. The asymmetry runs one step further, into what happens when a menu item is already `STALE`. A menu item of the changed recipe is rewritten whether or not its `plate_cost_state` was already `STALE` — the allergen and sodium copies have to move regardless, so it takes the state, the `margin_class` reset, the `version` increment and the hop-4 promotion revert either way. A menu item of a merely ancestral recipe is touched only when its `plate_cost_state` is currently `FRESH`; an already-`STALE` one keeps its `version`, its `margin_class` and its `COSTED` promotions entirely undisturbed.

**Hop 4 — the promotions.** Because those menu items' plate costs went stale, every promotion campaign for them whose `status` is `COSTED` reverts to `PLANNED` and clears `promo_plate_cost_cents`, `promo_food_cost_bps` and `forecast_contribution_cents` to 0. A promotion priced against a plate cost that no longer holds is returned to planning rather than left quietly wrong.

**Rollback** moves `APPROVED` to `ROLLED_BACK` and is the `CulinaryDirector`'s compensating action; the order need only be `APPROVED`, and the propagation dimension's position is irrelevant. It restores the specification exactly. For `COMPONENT_QTY` it writes `pre_change_ep_quantity_base_units` back onto the target component and recomputes `ap_quantity_base_units` from it. For `COMPONENT_ADD` it sets the component named by `created_component_id` to `status` `REMOVED`. For `COMPONENT_REMOVE` it sets the target component's `status` back to `ACTIVE` and clears `removed_at` to null. It then restores the recipe's `batch_cost_micros` from `pre_change_batch_cost_micros` and `allergen_mask` from `pre_change_allergen_mask`, recomputes `component_count`, `kcal_per_portion_micros`, `sodium_mcg_per_portion` and `depth` from the restored component set, sets `cost_state` to `STALE` and increments the recipe's `version`; and every recipe that reaches this recipe through active sub-recipe components again flips `cost_state` to `STALE` and increments `version`, transitively, with their own roll-up figures untouched. Every menu item of the rolled-back recipe has its `allergen_mask` and `sodium_mcg_per_portion` copied back from the restored recipe and flips `plate_cost_state` to `STALE`, `margin_class` to `UNCLASSIFIED` and `version` up by one; menu items of transitively staled ancestors flip state, class and version without touching their allergen or sodium copies. That menu-item pass and the promotion pass behind it are hop 3 and hop 4 exactly as approval performs them, with the same already-`STALE` asymmetry: a menu item of the rolled-back recipe takes the allergen and sodium rewrite, the `margin_class` reset, the `version` increment and the `COSTED`-promotion revert whether or not its `plate_cost_state` was already `STALE`, while a menu item of a merely ancestral recipe is left wholly undisturbed unless its `plate_cost_state` is currently `FRESH`. Finally, rollback resets `propagation_status` to `NOT_STARTED`, flips the prep guide's `sync_state` back to `OUT_OF_DATE`, and flips the affected count sheet lines of every non-closed review back to `OUT_OF_DATE` — the propagation work has to be done again against the restored specification.

Because approval never rewrote any `extended_cost_micros`, restoring the component rows and those two snapshots leaves intact the conserved identity that a recipe's `batch_cost_micros` equals the sum of `extended_cost_micros` over its active components. Rollback returns the cost basis to precisely the state the last roll-up produced; it does not invent one. What rollback does *not* restore is the recipe's `cost_state`: the recipe is left `STALE`, because the version history it has been through means nobody has recomputed the figures since.

**Propagation** is the `CostAnalyst`'s and drains one target at a time. The request names a `target` of either `PREP_GUIDE` or `COUNT_SHEETS`. The order's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_SCO_INVALID_STATE`. The named target must not already be reflected in `propagation_status`, otherwise `ERR_PROPAGATION_ALREADY_SYNCED` — each target may be drained exactly once per order. Four transitions cover the grid: from `NOT_STARTED`, a `PREP_GUIDE` target advances to `PREP_GUIDE_SYNCED` and a `COUNT_SHEETS` target advances to `COUNTS_SYNCED`; from `PREP_GUIDE_SYNCED`, a `COUNT_SHEETS` target advances to `FULLY_PROPAGATED`; and from `COUNTS_SYNCED`, a `PREP_GUIDE` target advances to `FULLY_PROPAGATED`. Either order of draining reaches `FULLY_PROPAGATED`, and the two targets are genuinely independent — draining one never performs the other's work.

The `PREP_GUIDE` target re-issues the kitchen document: the recipe's prep guide has its `sync_state` set to `CURRENT`, its `revision_no` incremented by one, its `batch_yield_base_units_snapshot`, `portions_per_batch_snapshot` and `component_count_snapshot` refreshed from the recipe as it now stands, and its `synced_spec_version` set to the recipe's current `version`. The prep guide's `version` and the order's `version` each increment by one.

The `COUNT_SHEETS` target refreshes the physical-inventory expectations: for every count sheet line whose `sync_state` is `OUT_OF_DATE` and that belongs to a period review whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, `expected_base_units` is recomputed by the expected-usage explosion of §3.8 against the current specification, `sync_state` is set to `CURRENT`, `variance_base_units` is recomputed as `used_base_units` minus the new `expected_base_units`, and `synced_spec_version` is set to the recipe's current `version`. Every touched count sheet line has its `version` incremented, as does the order.

Orders are readable individually — showing both dimensions, every snapshot and the `created_component_id` a `COMPONENT_ADD` produced — and as a list filterable by `recipe_id`, `target_component_id`, `ingredient_id`, `approval_status` and `propagation_status`, ordered `created_at` ascending.

### 3.7. Price Change Proposals and the Elasticity Projection

Menu prices are not edited in place. A price move is a proposal that carries its own forward-looking projection, is reviewed by someone other than its author, and is applied only once its effective date arrives. Proposal `status` runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, then either `REVISION_REQUESTED` (which returns to `SUBMITTED`), `APPROVED` and finally `EFFECTIVE`, or `REJECTED`; `EFFECTIVE` and `REJECTED` are terminal. Every state gate on a proposal reports `ERR_PROPOSAL_INVALID_STATE`: editing and submitting require `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, requesting a revision and approving require `SUBMITTED`, and applying requires `APPROVED`.

A `RecipeDeveloper` opens a proposal with `menu_item_id`, a `proposed_price_cents` of at least one cent, an `effective_from` instant, and an `elasticity_bps` between 0 and 50000 inclusive. That elasticity is supplied as a **positive magnitude** — the basis points of unit decline per 10000 basis points of price increase — because units always move against price and the projection supplies the sign itself; a caller must never encode the direction by making it negative. The target item may have no other proposal whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`; a second live proposal is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_OPEN`. Note that creation carries **no** expected-version field, because the conflict it guards against is a second live proposal, which is a state precondition rather than a stale read. Creation is also deliberately ungated on the item's own status: a proposal may be drafted against a `DRAFT` item and will simply fail at submission. The new proposal opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, null `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `last_revision_reason` and `applied_at`, and no projection at all — every projection field (`baseline_units`, `price_delta_bps`, `projected_unit_delta_bps`, `projected_units`, `projected_food_cost_bps`, `market_cap_price_cents`, `effective_cap_price_cents` and `projected_contribution_delta_cents`) is reported as null rather than as 0 until the first submission writes it.

A `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` proposal may be edited by a `RecipeDeveloper`, who may supply any subset of `proposed_price_cents`, `effective_from` and `elasticity_bps`; omitted fields are left alone. An edit increments the proposal's `version` and, importantly, leaves every projection field exactly as the last submission left it. Projections are only ever rewritten by a submission.

**Submission** is the calculation event, and it applies identically to both of its arms — the first submission out of `DRAFT` and every resubmission out of `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Both arms re-evaluate the same five preconditions against the proposal's current values and the item's current state. The target item's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`. The item's `plate_cost_state` must be `FRESH`, otherwise `ERR_COST_STALE` — a projection against an unknown plate cost is meaningless. When the item's `last_price_change_at` is not null, the proposal's `effective_from` must be at least 14 whole days after it, otherwise `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_COOLING_OFF`; when `last_price_change_at` is null there is no cooling-off restriction at all. The `proposed_price_cents` must not exceed `effective_cap_price_cents` as computed below, otherwise `ERR_MARKET_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And `proposed_price_cents` minus the item's `plate_cost_cents` must be at least the 300-cent margin floor, otherwise `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`. Both cap and floor are evaluated from the freshly computed projection, so the projection is computed first and the gates read the figures it just produced; a submission that fails either gate persists nothing at all, leaving the proposal's status, its `submitted_by_user_id` and whatever projection its last successful submission wrote exactly as they were. A successful submission writes every projection field, sets `submitted_by_user_id` to the caller — re-stamping it on a resubmission — and increments the proposal's `version`.

The projection is evaluated in exactly seven steps, each consuming only the outputs of the steps above it. **Step one** computes `price_delta_bps`: take the difference between `proposed_price_cents` and the item's `current_price_cents`, multiply the absolute value of that difference by 10000, divide by `current_price_cents`, round half up, and then re-apply the sign of the original difference. The magnitude is therefore always rounded half up *away from zero*, for a cut as much as for a rise. **Step two** computes `projected_unit_delta_bps`: multiply the absolute value of `price_delta_bps` by `elasticity_bps`, divide by 10000, round half up, and give the result the *opposite* sign to `price_delta_bps`. A price rise therefore yields a negative unit delta and a price cut yields a positive one. **Step three** computes `market_cap_price_cents` as the smaller of two independent ceilings: the competitor-index arm, which is the item's regional `REGION_COMPETITOR_INDEX_CENTS` multiplied by its regional `REGION_CAP_MULTIPLIER_BPS`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up; and the single-step arm, which is the item's `current_price_cents` multiplied by 11200 — that is, 10000 plus the 1200-basis-point single-increase ceiling — divided by 10000 and rounded half up. **Step four** establishes the volume baseline. `baseline_units` is the `units_sold` of the mix line belonging to the most recent closed period review that contains a line for this menu item, where "most recent" ranks reviews by `period_end` descending and breaks ties by `created_at` ascending, first row winning; the search spans every closed review in the system and is deliberately **not** scoped to any one store's `unit_id`, and where the winning review happens to carry more than one line for the same menu item it is the earliest-created of those lines that is read. When no such line exists anywhere, `baseline_units` is the default baseline of 1000 units. Then `projected_units` is `baseline_units` multiplied by the sum of 10000 and `projected_unit_delta_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up, and floored at zero — that clamp only bites when the unit delta is below negative 10000 basis points, which a large increase under a high elasticity can produce. **Step five** computes `projected_food_cost_bps` as the item's `plate_cost_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `proposed_price_cents`, rounded half up — computed from the **proposed** price and never from any capped price. **Step six** computes `effective_cap_price_cents`: the relief is 300 basis points when `projected_food_cost_bps` is strictly greater than the item's `target_food_cost_bps` and zero otherwise, and the effective cap is `market_cap_price_cents` multiplied by the sum of 10000 and that relief, divided by 10000, rounded half up. **Step seven** computes `projected_contribution_delta_cents` as `projected_units` multiplied by the difference between `proposed_price_cents` and `plate_cost_cents`, minus `baseline_units` multiplied by the difference between `current_price_cents` and `plate_cost_cents`; it may be negative, and a price rise that loses enough volume genuinely does produce a negative figure.

That order is pinned and the apparent circularity is deliberate. The cap relief depends on the projected food cost, the projected food cost depends on the proposed price, and the proposed price is what the cap constrains. The loop is broken by evaluating the food cost against the raw proposed price in step five and only then widening the cap in step six. The cap is never fed back into the food-cost step, and the cap comparison at submission is made against the relieved `effective_cap_price_cents`, never against the un-relieved `market_cap_price_cents`. Two proposals identical in every respect but their item's `target_food_cost_bps` can therefore land on opposite sides of the cap, and re-ordering these two steps flips both outcomes.

**Requesting a revision** is the `CulinaryDirector`'s and is a single operation with two outcomes, selected by the proposal's own `revision_round`. It requires a `SUBMITTED` proposal and carries a `last_revision_reason` — a required string carrying no length constraint at all, so an empty string is accepted and recorded verbatim. While `revision_round` is strictly below the two-round ceiling, the call moves the proposal to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_round` by one, stores `last_revision_reason` and increments `version` — the author may then patch and resubmit. When `revision_round` has already reached two, the very same call instead moves the proposal to `REJECTED`, stores `last_revision_reason`, leaves `revision_round` unchanged and increments `version` — the revision loop is bounded, and the third revision request terminates the proposal rather than reopening it. Any further revision request against the now-rejected proposal is a plain state violation reported as `ERR_PROPOSAL_INVALID_STATE`.

**Approval** moves `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED` and belongs to the `CulinaryDirector`. Two conditions beyond the status gate apply, in this order: the item's `plate_cost_state` must still be `FRESH`, otherwise `ERR_COST_STALE`, because a plate-cost invalidation since submission voids the whole projection; and only then must the caller's `X-User-ID` differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A self-approval of a proposal whose item has gone stale therefore reports the stale-cost error, not the duties error. Approval sets `approved_by_user_id` and increments `version`.

**Application** moves `APPROVED` to `EFFECTIVE` and is the `CostAnalyst`'s. The current logical clock must have reached `effective_from`; earlier than that the call is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. That clock comparison is the only precondition beyond the proposal's own `APPROVED` status: application re-examines neither the item's `status` nor its `plate_cost_state`, so a proposal approved while its item was `ACTIVE` and `FRESH` still applies after the item has been suspended or its plate cost has gone stale — position 20 of §2.10 lists proposal *submission* and not proposal application, and position 26 likewise. Application stamps `applied_at` with the current logical clock and then rewrites the item: `current_price_cents` becomes `proposed_price_cents`, `last_price_change_at` becomes the current logical clock, and `food_cost_bps` and `contribution_margin_cents` are recomputed from the *unchanged* `plate_cost_cents` against the new price by the same two formulas plate costing uses. The item's `plate_cost_state` stays `FRESH`, because the plate cost itself did not move — only the price did. But `margin_class` is reset to `UNCLASSIFIED`, because a menu-engineering classification computed at the old price no longer describes this item. The item's `version` increments by one. Proposals are readable individually and as a list filterable by `menu_item_id` and `status`, ordered `created_at` ascending.

### 3.8. Period Reviews: Menu Mix Engineering, Physical Counts and Variance

A period review is one store's retrospective on one window of trading. A `CostAnalyst` opens it with a `unit_id`, a `period_start` and a `period_end` that must be strictly after the start, and a `basis` of either `SALES_MIX_BASED` or `USAGE_BASED`. The window is closed at the start and open at the end. The `basis` is fixed at creation and can never be changed, because it decides which theoretical food cost the close computes. The review opens in `status` `OPEN` with `total_units_sold`, `total_gross_sales_cents`, `popularity_threshold_bps`, `margin_threshold_cents`, `theoretical_food_cost_cents`, `actual_food_cost_cents` and `variance_bps` all 0, a null `variance_flag` until the close writes one, and a null `closed_at`. `status` runs `OPEN`, `MIX_LOADED`, `CLASSIFIED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal. Reviews are listed ordered `period_end` descending then `created_at` ascending — exactly the ranking a price-change projection uses to pick its baseline — with filters on `unit_id`, `status` and `basis`.

**Loading the sales mix** is the `CostAnalyst`'s. Each mix line names a `menu_item_id` and carries `units_sold` and `gross_sales_cents`, both zero or greater. The item must exist and its `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`: a review may load any item that was on the menu during the period, including one that a retired promotion has since suspended. The line freezes `plate_cost_cents_snapshot` from the item's `plate_cost_cents` at the moment of loading, and every later classification and close values that line with the snapshot rather than with the item's live plate cost. Loading a line recomputes the review's `total_units_sold` and `total_gross_sales_cents` from all of its mix lines and increments the review's `version`. The **first** line auto-advances the review from `OPEN` to `MIX_LOADED` without any explicit request; later lines leave it in `MIX_LOADED`. A line's `mix_pct_bps`, `cm_per_unit_cents` and `margin_class` are all written later by the classification run. Mix lines are listed per review in `created_at` ascending order.

**Recording physical counts** may be done by a `CostAnalyst` for any review, or by a `UnitManager` — but only for a review whose `unit_id` matches that manager's own identity, a mismatch being refused with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Each count line names an `ingredient_id` and carries `opening_base_units`, `received_base_units` and `counted_base_units`, all zero or greater. From those, `used_base_units` is `opening_base_units` plus `received_base_units` minus `counted_base_units`, floored at zero so an impossible count never reports negative consumption. Creation also derives `expected_base_units` from the currently loaded mix by the explosion described next, sets `variance_base_units` to `used_base_units` minus `expected_base_units`, sets `sync_state` to `CURRENT`, and records `synced_spec_version` from the version of the recipe specification the expectation was computed against — concretely, the `version` of the first recipe found that names this ingredient directly in one of its own active `INGREDIENT` components, searching the review's mix lines in `created_at` ascending order and, inside each mix line, that line's backing recipe first and then its sub-recipes breadth-first with siblings in `sequence_no` order. When no mix line's recipe consumes the ingredient at all — a review with no mix lines yet included — there is no specification to name and `synced_spec_version` is 0. `valued_usage_micros` stays 0 until the close run values the line. Recording a count line also increments the owning review's `version`, even though it moves none of the review's own figures. Count lines are listed per review in `created_at` ascending order, filterable by `sync_state`.

The **expected-usage explosion** answers, for one ingredient and one review, how much of that ingredient the period's sales *should* have consumed. It is built from a per-portion figure carried in micro-units — one millionth of a base unit — so that the divisions by portions and yields do not lose resolution before the units are multiplied out. For a given recipe and ingredient, accumulate over the recipe's active components. An ingredient component on that very ingredient contributes its `ap_quantity_base_units` multiplied by 1000000, divided by the recipe's `portions_per_batch`, rounded half up. A sub-recipe component contributes the child recipe's own per-batch accumulation for the same ingredient — the identical accumulation performed on the child without the portions divisor — multiplied by the component's `ep_quantity_base_units` and by 1000000, all divided by the product of the child's `batch_yield_base_units` and the parent's `portions_per_batch`, and rounded half up; that whole thing is evaluated as one integer expression with a single rounding at the end, not as a chain of separately rounded divisions. Then `expected_base_units` is the sum, over every mix line of the review, of that line's `units_sold` multiplied by the per-portion micro figure for the menu item's backing recipe, divided by 1000000 and taken to the floor. An ingredient that no mix line's recipe consumes therefore has `expected_base_units` 0 and a `variance_base_units` equal to the whole of `used_base_units`, and a review with no mix lines yields `expected_base_units` 0 for every count line. Ingredients that reach a plate only through a sub-recipe are picked up by this explosion exactly like directly referenced ones.

Valuation of a count line is `used_base_units` multiplied by the selected price row's `pack_cost_micros`, divided by **that same price row's** `pack_base_units_snapshot`, rounded half up; the divisor is the pack size the chosen price was quoted against, and a count line carries no pack snapshot of its own. The price row is selected exactly as the recipe roll-up selects it under `LATEST_PUBLISHED` — and note that **both** review bases use `LATEST_PUBLISHED` prices for this valuation; the review's `basis` selects the theoretical figure, not the valuation of actual usage. When no price row is selectable for that ingredient at all — nothing published for it in the reference region, or nothing whose effective window covers the current logical clock — the line is valued at 0 rather than the close being refused: this valuation never reports `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_PRICE`, which is a recipe roll-up error only.

**Classification** is the `CostAnalyst`'s menu-engineering run. It is reachable from `OPEN` and from `MIX_LOADED`, but a review still in `OPEN` has by definition no mix lines and therefore always fails with `ERR_EMPTY_MIX`; the state gate admits it so that the empty-mix error rather than a state error is what the caller sees. On a review with at least one line it computes `popularity_threshold_bps` as the popularity factor of 7000 basis points divided by the number of mix lines, rounded half up — so a two-line review sets the bar at 3500 basis points and a five-line review at 1400. Then, per line, `mix_pct_bps` is `units_sold` multiplied by 10000 and divided by the review's `total_units_sold`, rounded half up; and `cm_per_unit_cents` is `gross_sales_cents` divided by `units_sold` and rounded half up, minus that line's `plate_cost_cents_snapshot` — it is 0, with no division performed at all and the `plate_cost_cents_snapshot` not subtracted either, when `units_sold` is 0, which is a perfectly legitimate line since mix-line creation admits a `units_sold` of 0. `mix_pct_bps` is likewise 0 for every line of a review whose `total_units_sold` is 0, again without dividing. Then `margin_threshold_cents` is the units-weighted average unit contribution margin: sum each line's `units_sold` multiplied by its `cm_per_unit_cents`, divide by `total_units_sold`, round half up — and it too is 0 when `total_units_sold` is 0.

A line is popular when its `mix_pct_bps` is greater than **or equal to** `popularity_threshold_bps`, and high-margin when its `cm_per_unit_cents` is greater than **or equal to** `margin_threshold_cents`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a line sitting exactly on a threshold falls on the favourable side of it. The quadrant follows: `STAR` when popular and high-margin, `PLOWHORSE` when popular but not high-margin, `PUZZLE` when high-margin but not popular, and `DOG` when neither. A single-line review whose line sold anything at all always yields a `STAR`, because its mix share is 10000 basis points against a 7000-point bar and its contribution margin *is* the weighted average; the one exception is a lone line with `units_sold` 0, whose mix share is 0 against that bar and whose 0 contribution margin merely ties the 0 threshold, so it comes out a `PUZZLE`. Classification writes both thresholds onto the review and all three computed fields onto every line, copies each line's `margin_class` onto its menu item and increments each of those items' `version`, and increments the review's `version`. Nothing forbids a review from carrying two mix lines for the same menu item, and when it does each line is classified in its own right while the item itself ends up holding the class of the last such line in `created_at` order.

**Closing** the review is the `CostAnalyst`'s final act on a `CLASSIFIED` review. It requires at least one count line, otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_COUNT_SHEET`, and it requires every count line of the review to have `sync_state` `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_COUNT_SHEET_OUT_OF_DATE` — a review cannot be closed on expectations computed against a specification that has since changed.

The close writes `valued_usage_micros` on every count line by the valuation rule above and increments each of those lines' `version` — under either `basis`, since the actual figure is always built from the counts — and then computes `theoretical_food_cost_cents` according to the review's `basis`. Under `SALES_MIX_BASED` it is the sum over the mix lines of `units_sold` multiplied by `plate_cost_cents_snapshot`; that basis therefore inherits the regional freight uplift, the plate waste factor and the garnish allowance that plate costing baked into each snapshot, and it values the period at the plate cost as of the moment each mix line was loaded rather than at today's prices. Under `USAGE_BASED` it is computed from the count lines instead: for each line take `expected_base_units` multiplied by the selected price row's `pack_cost_micros`, divided by that same price row's `pack_base_units_snapshot`, rounded half up to micro-dollars; sum those; divide by 10000 and round half up to cents. A line for which no price row is selectable contributes 0 to that sum, exactly as it contributes 0 to the actual figure. That basis values the exploded ingredient quantities at today's published prices and contains no uplift, no waste factor and no garnish allowance, so for the same mix it is structurally lower than `SALES_MIX_BASED`. The same underlying data can therefore land on opposite sides of the tolerance depending only on the review's basis, which is precisely why the basis is fixed at creation.

`actual_food_cost_cents` is the sum of `valued_usage_micros` over the review's count lines, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. When `theoretical_food_cost_cents` is 0 the review reports `variance_bps` 0 and `variance_flag` `NO_BASIS` — there is nothing to be a percentage of. Otherwise take the difference of actual minus theoretical, multiply its absolute value by 10000, divide by `theoretical_food_cost_cents`, round half up, and re-apply the sign of the difference: `variance_bps` is positive when actual exceeds theoretical and negative when it falls short. `variance_flag` is `OVER_TOLERANCE` when the **absolute** value of `variance_bps` exceeds the 300-basis-point tolerance, and `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` otherwise. Both an overrun and an underrun of the same magnitude are out of tolerance: an unexplained favourable variance means the count is wrong, not that the kitchen is unusually efficient. The close stamps `closed_at` with the current logical clock, sets `status` to `CLOSED` and increments the review's `version`. A closed review is immutable, and its count lines are never again flipped `OUT_OF_DATE` by any later specification change.

### 3.9. Limited-Time Offers: Costing, Launch Capacity and the Post-Promotion Verdict

A limited-time offer promotes one menu item at a promotional price over a bounded window, and it is held to account afterwards. Campaign `status` runs `PLANNED`, `COSTED`, `LAUNCHED`, `CLOSED` and then branches to either `RENEWED` or `RETIRED`, both terminal; `COSTED` also has a reverse edge back to `PLANNED`. Campaigns are listed ordered `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending, filterable by `menu_item_id`, `region_code` and `status`.

A `RecipeDeveloper` plans a campaign with `menu_item_id`, a `window_start`, a `window_end` strictly after it, a `promo_price_cents` of at least one cent, a `forecast_units` of at least one — the denominator of forecast attainment, which is why zero is not allowed — and an `expected_menu_item_version`, since planning a promotion against a menu item someone else has just re-priced is exactly the race worth losing loudly. The window is closed at the start and open at the end. The campaign copies its `region_code` from the menu item, so it occupies that region's concurrent-promotion capacity, and it increments the item's `open_lto_count` and `version`. It opens in `PLANNED` with `verdict` `PENDING`, a null `reviewed_at`, and `promo_plate_cost_cents`, `promo_food_cost_bps`, `forecast_contribution_cents`, `actual_units`, `actual_gross_sales_cents`, `promo_support_cost_cents`, `promo_contribution_cents` and `forecast_attainment_bps` all 0. Planning examines neither the item's `status` nor its `plate_cost_state`: a campaign may be booked against a `DRAFT`, a `SUSPENDED` or even a `RETIRED` item, and against a plate cost that is `STALE`, because position 20 of §2.10 lists promotion *costing* and not promotion planning, and position 26 does the same. A plan is only a plan; it is the costing run below that refuses.

**Costing** moves `PLANNED` to `COSTED` and is the `CostAnalyst`'s. The backing menu item's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_ITEM_INVALID_STATE`, and its `plate_cost_state` must be `FRESH`, otherwise `ERR_COST_STALE`. The run freezes `promo_plate_cost_cents` from the item's `plate_cost_cents`; computes `promo_food_cost_bps` as `promo_plate_cost_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `promo_price_cents`, rounded half up; and computes `forecast_contribution_cents` as `forecast_units` multiplied by the difference between `promo_price_cents` and `promo_plate_cost_cents`. Neither figure is constrained: a promotional price below the plate cost yields a negative `forecast_contribution_cents` and a `promo_food_cost_bps` above 10000, and the costing run still succeeds. Deciding whether a loss-leader is worth running is the director's judgement, not a validation. Costing increments the campaign's `version`.

The **reverse edge** from `COSTED` back to `PLANNED` is never requested directly. It fires as a cascade, and precisely once for every menu-item rewrite that one of the invalidation passes described elsewhere performs on the backing item — never on its own account and never at any other moment. Exactly three passes can reach a campaign: a price feed publication's fourth and fifth hops (§3.2), a direct component add or removal on a recipe that is still `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` (§3.3), and a specification change order's approval or rollback (§3.6), the last being the typical case and the only one that reaches an item through an arbitrarily deep chain of ancestor recipes. In the first two passes, and in the third for an item backed only by a *transitively* staled ancestor recipe, the campaign moves only where the item's `plate_cost_state` was `FRESH` and therefore actually flipped; an item that was already `STALE` is passed over entirely and its `COSTED` campaigns keep their frozen figures. The one situation in which the revert fires without anything flipping is a menu item of the very recipe a change order changed or rolled back: that item is rewritten unconditionally, so even when it was already `STALE` its `COSTED` campaigns still revert. And one flip to `STALE` never reaches a campaign at all — a recipe's own retirement (§3.4) sets that recipe's `cost_state` to `STALE` while walking neither parents nor menu items, so no plate cost and no promotion moves with it. It clears `promo_plate_cost_cents`, `promo_food_cost_bps` and `forecast_contribution_cents` back to 0 and increments the campaign's `version`, so a campaign can never be launched on figures nobody has recomputed. Recovering means recomputing the recipe's cost, recomputing the item's plate cost, and costing the campaign again.

**Launching** moves `COSTED` to `LAUNCHED` and is the `CulinaryDirector`'s. The current logical clock must still be before `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_LTO_INVALID_STATE` — a promotion whose window has already elapsed cannot be launched. And the number of campaigns already in `LAUNCHED` that share this campaign's `region_code` and whose window intersects this campaign's window must be fewer than two, otherwise `ERR_LTO_WINDOW_CAPACITY`. Intersection is judged on the half-open windows, so a campaign starting exactly when another ends does not intersect it and does not consume capacity; the limit is on genuinely overlapping promotions in one region, and campaigns in other regions or in other statuses are never counted. Those two conditions and the campaign's own `COSTED` status are the whole of it: launching re-examines neither the backing item's `status` nor its `plate_cost_state`, so a campaign whose item has been suspended since it was costed still launches — position 20 of §2.10 does not list the launch, and a campaign still sitting in `COSTED` is itself the evidence that no invalidation has touched the plate cost its figures were frozen from. Launching sets the item's `is_lto` to true and increments the item's `version`, and increments the campaign's `version`.

**The post-promotion review** moves `LAUNCHED` to `CLOSED` and is the `CostAnalyst`'s. The current logical clock must have reached `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`: a promotion is judged only after it is over. The request supplies `actual_units` and `actual_gross_sales_cents`, both zero or greater. From those, `promo_support_cost_cents` is `actual_gross_sales_cents` multiplied by the 300-basis-point support charge, divided by 10000, rounded half up — the marketing cost of running the promotion. `promo_contribution_cents` is `actual_gross_sales_cents`, minus `actual_units` multiplied by `promo_plate_cost_cents`, minus `promo_support_cost_cents`; note that it is valued at the plate cost the *costing run froze*, not at whatever the item's plate cost is today. `forecast_attainment_bps` is `actual_units` multiplied by 10000, divided by `forecast_units`, rounded half up.

The `verdict` becomes `PROFITABLE` only when **both** conditions hold: `promo_contribution_cents` is at least the minimum contribution of 0 cents, and `forecast_attainment_bps` is at least the minimum attainment of 8000 basis points. Otherwise it becomes `UNPROFITABLE`. Both must hold because a promotion that made money on a fraction of its forecast volume cannibalised the base menu rather than growing it, and should not be renewed. A campaign with zero actual units and zero gross sales has a contribution of exactly 0, which satisfies the contribution floor, but an attainment of 0 basis points, which does not — so it is `UNPROFITABLE`. The review stamps `reviewed_at`, sets the item's `is_lto` back to false, decrements the item's `open_lto_count`, increments the item's `version`, and increments the campaign's `version`.

**The decision** is the `CulinaryDirector`'s and forks from `CLOSED` on a `decision` of either `RENEW` or `RETIRE`. The `decision` is a request-body input only: the campaign stores no attribute of that name, and which arm was taken is afterwards read from the campaign's own `status` (`RENEWED` versus `RETIRED`). The two arms share no side effect beyond the version bump.

The `RENEW` arm requires the campaign's `verdict` to be `PROFITABLE`; renewing an unprofitable promotion is refused with `ERR_LTO_INVALID_STATE`. It moves the campaign to `RENEWED` and mints a successor campaign for the same `menu_item_id` in `status` `PLANNED`, whose `window_start` is this campaign's `window_end`, whose `window_end` is this campaign's `window_end` plus the length of this campaign's window — so the successor is the same duration, immediately following, and abutting rather than overlapping — whose `promo_price_cents` is the same, whose `forecast_units` is this campaign's `actual_units` (the promotion's own outcome becomes the next forecast), and whose `region_code` is copied from the menu item. It increments the item's `open_lto_count` and `version`, and the item stays `ACTIVE` and sellable throughout.

The `RETIRE` arm has no verdict precondition at all — a profitable promotion may perfectly well be retired. It moves the campaign to `RETIRED`, mints no successor campaign whatsoever, and instead sets the backing menu item's `status` to `SUSPENDED`, resets its `margin_class` to `UNCLASSIFIED`, sets its `is_lto` to false and increments its `version`. The suspension follows the `RETIRE` arm itself and never consults the `verdict`: retiring a `PROFITABLE` campaign suspends the item exactly as retiring an `UNPROFITABLE` one does. Bringing the item back to the menu afterwards is the separate activation described in §3.5, which will re-check the recipe status, the plate-cost freshness, the allergen coverage and the sodium ceiling from scratch.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create new, stand-alone resources (a store, an ingredient, a price feed, a recipe, a menu item, a proposal, a change order, a period review, a promotion campaign) or add new, distinct resources to a collection (a price line on a feed, a component on a recipe, a mix line or count line on a review) return a `201 Created` status code. Operations that modify an existing resource — every state transition, every recomputation, every propagation, the soft removal of a component and the logical-clock advance — return a `200 OK` status code, as do all read operations.
