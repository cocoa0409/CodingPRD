# Certified Payroll & Prevailing Wage Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Public works contracting sits on a paperwork obligation that never stops arriving: every week, for every tier of every project, a contractor must assemble a certified payroll, sign a statement of compliance under penalty of perjury, and file it into a project-specific reporting portal before that portal's window closes. On the other side of the same transaction, an awarding agency and a labor-standards enforcement office must read those filings, compare them against the wage determination that governed the locality on the day the work was performed, interview workers on site to see whether the paper matches reality, and — when it does not — compute back wages, collect restitution, and escalate toward debarment. Today that cycle is run on spreadsheets, scanned PDFs and email threads, which means the arithmetic that decides whether a worker was underpaid is neither reproducible nor auditable.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that cycle. The service owns the authoritative wage arithmetic: it decides the required hourly basic rate and fringe amount for every reported worker-week, decides how much of a contractor's fringe benefit contribution may be credited against the fringe obligation, decides whether the week's apprentice hours stayed inside the ratio the apprenticeship program permits, and derives the resulting make-whole deficiency in whole cents. It also owns the compliance posture that those numbers imply — freezing payroll operations on a project that carries a critical finding, suspending a subcontract tier whose contractor drifts into a debarment proposal, and refusing to close out a project whose restitution ledger has not been settled to zero.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **every derived quantity is reproducible**: calculations run in a pinned order, rounding happens at a pinned point, and where two defensible ways of computing the same figure exist, the service stores both and names which one is authoritative. Second, **every ledger is conserved and every ledger movement is compensable**: accepting a payroll adds its hours to a tier's certified-hours total and rescinding that acceptance removes exactly the same hours; disbursing a restitution payment debits a finding's outstanding obligation and reversing that payment restores exactly the amount that was debited. All collections in this API are peers mounted directly beneath the service root — agency-side records such as wage determinations, reporting portals, findings and interviews are not children of contractor-side records such as payrolls, workers and restitution payments, and neither side owns the other.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participants, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not listed as an authorized role for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before anything else: they are the first and second gates on every request in the system.

The five roles are `PortalAdmin`, `PayrollPreparer`, `CertifyingOfficial`, `AgencyComplianceOfficer` and `LaborStandardsAuditor`.

A `PortalAdmin` is the system operator. Portal admins maintain reporting portals, wage determinations and their rate schedules, apprenticeship program registrations, contractor records, contractor staff rosters, projects, contract tiers, and the service's logical clock. A `PayrollPreparer` is a contractor staff member who assembles the weekly certified payroll, registers workers and fringe benefit plans, and schedules and disburses restitution payments — but who may never sign the statement of compliance for a payroll they assembled. A `CertifyingOfficial` is the contractor staff member authorized to sign that statement and to file responses to findings raised against their firm's payrolls. An `AgencyComplianceOfficer` is the awarding-agency reviewer who picks up submitted payrolls, accepts or returns them, rescinds acceptances, approves or rejects fringe benefit plans, adjudicates contractor responses to findings, reverses restitution disbursements, and reinstates suspended tiers. A `LaborStandardsAuditor` is the enforcement investigator who schedules, conducts and cancels on-site interviews, raises and withdraws findings, and issues formal debarment decisions.

Two of these roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PayrollPreparer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ContractorStaff` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ContractorStaff` or a `Contractor` compare that resource's owning contractor against the acting staff row's `contractor_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CertifyingOfficial`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `ContractorStaff` row, resolved and ownership-checked the same way. In both cases, if no `ContractorStaff` row exists with that identifier the request fails with `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and if the staff row resolves but belongs to a contractor other than the one that owns the targeted assignment, submission, finding or payment, the request fails with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The other three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PortalAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier — but the server does record it: accepting a payroll writes the acting identifier into that submission's `accepted_by_user_id`, and that stored value is later compared against the acting identifier on finding adjudication to enforce segregation of duties. When `X-User-Role` is `LaborStandardsAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `raised_by_user_id` on every finding that auditor raises, and never compared to any entity's `id`.

A third authorization layer applies only to the two contractor-side roles: the acting staff row's declared `capability`. A staff row whose `capability` is `PREPARER` may perform preparation work only; one whose `capability` is `CERTIFIER` may certify and respond only; one whose `capability` is `BOTH` may do either. A preparation request from a staff row lacking `PREPARER` or `BOTH`, or a certification or finding-response request from a staff row lacking `CERTIFIER` or `BOTH`, is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_CAPABILITY_MISSING` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters: `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; where two rows carry the same creation instant the ordering remains stable but no additional guarantee is made.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the request's filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. A `limit` or `offset` outside its permitted range is a body-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, list endpoints expose filters that let a caller walk relationships in reverse. Determinations may be filtered by `locality_code`, `status` and `superseded_by_id`; rate rows within a determination by `classification_code` and `zone_code`; contractors by `parent_contractor_id`, `debarment_status` and `registration_status`; staff rosters by `capability`; fringe plans by `approval_status` and `plan_type`; projects by `wage_determination_id`, `reporting_portal_id`, `status`, `posture` and `locality_code`; contract tiers by `project_id`, `contractor_id`, `upper_tier_assignment_id`, `status` and `tier`; workers by `employer_contractor_id`, `apprentice_program_id` and `worker_number`; payroll submissions by `contract_assignment_id`, `project_id`, `workflow_status`, `compliance_status`, `week_ending_date`, `prepared_by_staff_id`, `certified_by_staff_id`, `superseded_submission_id` and `is_late`; payroll lines by `payroll_submission_id`, `worker_id`, `fringe_plan_id` and `classification_code`; findings by `project_id`, `payroll_submission_id`, `worker_id`, `source_interview_id`, `status`, `severity` and `finding_type`; restitution payments by `finding_id`, `worker_id` and `status`; and interviews by `project_id`, `contract_assignment_id`, `worker_id`, `compared_submission_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Clients never supply either value.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. The resources that carry `updated_at` and `version` are the wage determination, the contractor, the apprenticeship program, the fringe benefit plan, the project, the contract assignment, the worker, the payroll submission, the on-site interview, the finding and the restitution payment.

Four resource kinds are deliberately lighter: the reporting portal, the wage rate row, the contractor staff row and the payroll line carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and nothing in this specification increments a version on them — when a payroll line's computed fields are rewritten by the wage engine, it is the owning payroll submission's `version` that advances, not the line's.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Before any clock call has been made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PortalAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response is a clock state object reporting `current_utc` — the clock's value after the move — and `contractors_rescored`, the count of contractors whose debarment risk was recomputed as a consequence of the move. Every instant the service writes itself (`statement_signed_at`, `submitted_at`, `disbursed_at`, `last_finding_at`, `response_due_at`, and the derived `filing_due_at`) is read from or derived from this logical clock, and all instants are stored and compared to millisecond resolution in UTC.

### 2.5. Precision: Whole Cents, Two-Decimal Hours and Rounding Direction

All monetary amounts in this system are integers in whole cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in any response. Wherever a monetary computation produces a non-integral intermediate — dividing an annual contribution by an hour count, scaling a journeyworker rate by an apprentice percentage, multiplying a per-hour deficiency by a fractional hour count, or applying the overtime factor — the result is rounded half up to a whole cent, meaning a value exactly at the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

All hour quantities carry two decimal places. Hours supplied by a client with more than two decimal places are a body-shape violation and are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Where hours are derived — the permitted apprentice hour allowance for a trade in a week — the derivation is rounded half up to two decimal places. Where hours are accumulated into a ledger, they are added and subtracted exactly, with no rounding applied to the accumulation itself.

Counts and scores are plain integers with no rounding: a debarment risk score is an integer clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100, and the decay-period count inside that score is taken by discarding any fractional period rather than rounding it.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity Ordering

Nested paths are resolved left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request naming a project that does not exist fails with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; a contract assignment, with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a payroll submission, with `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`; a finding, with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`; a restitution payment, with `ERR_RESTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`; an interview, with `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; a wage determination, with `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`; a contractor, with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`; a fringe benefit plan, with `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; and an apprenticeship program, with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. All ten are HTTP 404.

Only after every path segment resolves does the service look at foreign keys carried in the request body, and those are resolved in the order the body fields are declared: `contract_assignment_id`, then `prepared_by_staff_id`, then `superseded_submission_id`, then `worker_id`, then `fringe_plan_id`, then `wage_determination_id`, then `reporting_portal_id`, then `upper_tier_assignment_id`, then `contractor_id`, then `payroll_submission_id`, then `source_interview_id`. An unresolvable body reference produces the 404 belonging to its target kind — `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Tiers and Findings

Exactly two write operations in this system require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a parent record before it writes. Both are cases where the state machine itself permits a second write, so nothing but a version check can tell the caller that the record moved underneath them.

`POST /payroll-submissions` requires a non-optional `expected_contract_assignment_version` field carrying the `version` of the `ContractAssignment` named in `contract_assignment_id` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the assignment's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSIGNMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters most for correction filings: a payroll that supersedes a `RETURNED` row is a legitimate second create for the same week, so the state machine does not forbid it, and the version check is the only signal that another correction was already filed, that the tier was suspended, or that a payroll was accepted since the preparer last looked.

`POST /findings/{id}/response` requires a non-optional `expected_finding_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `Finding` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the finding's current `version`, the response filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each response legitimately overwrites the previous `response_narrative` and advances `response_round`, so the state machine permits repeated filings; the version check surfaces the fact that the finding changed under the author — a reviewer decision landed, or a restitution disbursement moved the outstanding balance — before that overwrite is accepted.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field of any kind. In particular, adding a payroll line, finalizing, certifying, accepting, returning, rescinding, disbursing, reversing, conducting an interview and adjudicating a response all proceed without any client-supplied version.

### 2.8. The Validation Precedence Ladder

Because many operations are guarded by several checks at once, the order in which those checks run is part of the contract. When a request would fail more than one check, the service reports the failure from the highest rung of the following ladder that the request trips, and stops there. Rungs 1 through 7 apply to every request that reaches them; the remaining rungs apply only where noted.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint role authorization — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Path-segment existence, left to right, as described in §2.6 — the ten 404 identifiers.
4. Body foreign-key existence, in body-field declaration order, as described in §2.6 — the nine body-reference 404 identifiers.
5. Ownership for the entity-backed roles: the acting `ContractorStaff` row's `contractor_id` must equal the contractor of the contract assignment that owns the targeted payroll submission, finding response or restitution payment — `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. This rung guards nine operations: opening a payroll submission, adding a payroll line, finalizing, certifying, filing a finding response, scheduling a restitution payment, disbursing a restitution payment, registering a worker, and declaring a fringe benefit plan.
6. Staff capability, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_STAFF_CAPABILITY_MISSING`. This rung guards five operations: opening a payroll submission, adding a payroll line, finalizing and certifying, plus filing a finding response; the first three require `PREPARER` or `BOTH`, and the last two require `CERTIFIER` or `BOTH`.
7. Request-body shape and field-level constraints — required fields present, enum values in range, integers non-negative where a non-negative integer is required, hour values within two decimal places, and timestamps parseable — `ERR_VALIDATION`.
8. State legality: the target resource must currently sit in a state the requested transition declares as a from-state. The identifier depends on the resource: `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INTERVIEW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATUS_TRANSITION`, all HTTP 409. Two extra conditions live on this rung: when a finding is raised, the cited submission's `workflow_status` must be one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED` or `RETURNED`; and when an interview is conducted, exactly one `ACCEPTED` submission on the interview's tier must cover `window_start`.
9. The project posture gate: the owning project's `posture` must not be `FROZEN` — `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). This rung guards five operations: opening a payroll submission, adding a payroll line, finalizing, certifying, and advancing a project to `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`.
10. Optimistic concurrency, as described in §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_ASSIGNMENT_VERSION` on payroll creation and `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION` on response filing, both HTTP 412. Note the consequence of this rung's position: a stale version is reported only after state legality and the posture gate have both passed.
11. Segregation of duties on certification — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409), on certification only.
12. Segregation of duties on adjudication — `ERR_SOD_REVIEW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), on response review only.
13. Notarization — `ERR_NOTARIZATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), on certification only.
14. The debarment and registration gate — `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DEBARRED` (HTTP 409). This rung guards three operations: creating a contract tier on a project, activating a tier, and reinstating a suspended tier.
15. Apprenticeship program registration — `ERR_APPRENTICE_PROGRAM_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line and on finalizing.
16. Apprentice trade alignment — `ERR_APPRENTICE_TRADE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line and on finalizing.
17. Apprentice period within the published scale — `ERR_APPRENTICE_PERIOD_OUT_OF_SCALE` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line and on finalizing.
18. Wage rate coverage — `ERR_NO_WAGE_RATE_FOR_CLASSIFICATION` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line and on finalizing.
19. Fringe plan ownership — `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_FOREIGN` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line.
20. Fringe plan usability — `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422), on adding a payroll line.
21. Upper-tier participation — `ERR_UPPER_TIER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), on creating a contract tier and on activating one.
22. The payroll week must end on a Saturday — `ERR_WEEK_ENDING_NOT_SATURDAY` (HTTP 422), on opening a payroll submission.
23. The payroll week must be over — `ERR_PAYROLL_WEEK_NOT_ENDED` (HTTP 422), on finalizing.
24. Payroll week occupation on the tier — `ERR_PAYROLL_WEEK_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on opening a payroll submission.
25. Governing-period occupation on the locality — `ERR_DETERMINATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on activating a determination.

### 2.9. Derived Counters, Compliance Posture and Cascade Discipline

A substantial part of this system's value is that agency staff can read a project or a contractor row and see, without running a query, how much unresolved exposure it carries. Those summary fields are maintained by the service, never written by a client, and recomputed synchronously as part of whichever request caused them to change. Nothing in this system is eventually consistent: by the time a response is returned, every counter, posture and ledger the request touched has already been brought up to date, and every touched row's `version` has advanced.

On a project, `open_finding_count` is the number of findings recorded against that project whose `status` is `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`; `open_critical_finding_count` is the subset of those whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`; `outstanding_restitution_cents` is the sum of `restitution_outstanding_cents` over that same set of findings; and `suspended_assignment_count` is the number of contract assignments on the project whose `status` is `SUSPENDED`. On a contract assignment, `open_finding_count` counts the findings in those same three non-terminal statuses that cite a payroll submission belonging to that assignment. On a contractor, `open_critical_finding_count` counts findings against that contractor whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is one of the same three, and `outstanding_restitution_cents` sums `restitution_outstanding_cents` over every finding against that contractor whose `status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`. A determination's `rate_count` is the number of wage rate rows attached to it, and a worker's `interview_count` is the number of interviews on that worker whose `status` is `CONDUCTED_MATCHED` or `CONDUCTED_DISCREPANT`. Findings on other projects of the same contractor never contribute to a project's counters, and findings on other contractors never contribute to a contractor's.

A project's `posture` is derived from those counters, and it is the master gate on payroll activity. It is evaluated in a pinned order in which the first matching step wins. Step one: if the project has at least one open critical finding, the posture is `FROZEN`. Step two: otherwise, if the project's outstanding restitution is strictly greater than five thousand dollars — that is, more than 500,000 cents — the posture is `FROZEN`; an outstanding balance of exactly 500,000 cents does not freeze the project. Step three: otherwise, if the project's open finding count is two or more, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; exactly two open findings does restrict. Step four: otherwise, if at least one assignment on the project is suspended, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Step five: otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Posture is re-evaluated whenever a finding is created or changes status, whenever a restitution payment is disbursed or reversed, whenever a contract assignment changes status, and whenever a project status advance is requested.

Cascades in this system may run several hops deep, and each hop is attributed to the event that caused it rather than to the original API call. Activating a superseding wage determination, for example, drives its predecessor to `SUPERSEDED` (hop one), that supersede re-points awarded projects (hop two), and those re-points force a recomputation of required rates on the unfiled payrolls of the re-pointed projects (hop three). Raising a critical finding suspends the cited tier, and that suspension in turn re-derives the parent project's suspended-assignment count and posture. Every row touched by any hop has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed exactly as if it had been the direct target of a request.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers and Compensating Actions

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and both have a forward movement and an exactly compensating reverse movement.

The first is hours. A contract assignment's `cumulative_certified_hours` must at all times equal the sum of `total_hours_reported` over the payroll submissions on that assignment whose `workflow_status` is `ACCEPTED`. Submissions in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RETURNED` contribute nothing at all. Acceptance adds the submission's `total_hours_reported` to the tier's running total; rescinding that acceptance subtracts the same figure, restoring the exact pre-acceptance value. Both movements are exact additions and subtractions of two-decimal hour quantities with no rounding. Because a `RETURNED` row never contributed to the total in the first place, accepting the correction filing that supersedes it adds those hours exactly once.

The second is restitution cents. For every finding whose `status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, the finding's `restitution_required_cents` must at all times equal its `restitution_outstanding_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` over the restitution payments on that finding whose `status` is `DISBURSED`. Payments in `PENDING`, `VOIDED` or `REVERSED` contribute zero to the disbursed side of that identity. Disbursing a payment reduces the finding's outstanding balance by the payment's `amount_cents` and increases the owning assignment's `cumulative_restitution_cents` by the same amount; reversing a disbursed payment increases the outstanding balance and reduces the assignment's cumulative restitution by that same amount, returning both to their exact pre-disbursement values. Voiding a payment that was still `PENDING` moves neither side of the identity, because no funds had entered the ledger. An assignment's `cumulative_restitution_cents` is therefore the sum of `amount_cents` over the `DISBURSED` restitution payments on findings whose cited submission belongs to that assignment.

### 2.11. Interval Conventions

Three different intervals appear in this specification, and each has its own inclusivity, so they are stated once here and referenced from the workflows that use them.

A **payroll week** is closed on both ends. A submission whose `week_ending_date` is a given Saturday covers the interval running from six days before that Saturday through the Saturday itself, with both endpoints included. Two consecutive weekly payrolls therefore share no day and do not collide.

A determination's **governing period** is half-open: it runs from `effective_date` inclusive up to `expiry_date` exclusive, and when `expiry_date` is null the period is open-ended. A determination whose `effective_date` falls exactly on another determination's `expiry_date` does not collide with it, while one whose `effective_date` falls strictly inside another's span does.

An **interview window** is half-open: it runs from `window_start` inclusive up to `window_end` exclusive.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Publishing Wage Determinations and Their Rate Schedules

Everything in this system is anchored to a published wage decision. A `PortalAdmin` records one by supplying its `decision_number` — the published decision identifier such as `CA20260014` — together with a `modification_number` giving the modification sequence of that decision, a `locality_code` naming the county the decision governs, an `effective_date`, and optionally an `expiry_date`. The `modification_number` defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative, and the pair of `decision_number` and `modification_number` must be unique across the system. A `locality_code` is uppercase and between 4 and 24 characters. When an `expiry_date` is supplied it must fall strictly after the `effective_date`. The new determination begins life with `status` `DRAFT`, a `rate_count` of 0, and a `version` of 1.

A determination is worthless until it carries a rate schedule. While the determination is still `DRAFT`, the admin attaches rate rows to it, one per craft classification and travel zone. Each row names the `classification_code` the rate applies to — a craft code such as `ELEC-INSIDE-JW` — and the `trade_code` giving the trade family used later for apprentice-ratio grouping, both uppercase, with the trade family between 2 and 12 characters. Each row also carries a `zone_code`, which is one of `BASE`, `ZONE1` or `ZONE2` and defaults to `BASE`; a `base_hourly_rate_cents` giving the required straight-time basic hourly rate, which must be at least 1 cent; a `fringe_hourly_rate_cents` giving the required hourly fringe amount, which may be zero but not negative; a `zone_adjustment_cents` added to the basic rate when the project's travel zone matches this row's zone, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative; and an `apprentice_scale_pct` array. The combination of owning determination, `classification_code` and `zone_code` must be unique, so a determination carries at most one row per classification per zone. The `apprentice_scale_pct` array holds between 1 and 8 integer percentages of the basic rate, indexed so that the first entry is apprentice period 1; every entry must be between 30 and 100 inclusive, and the sequence must be non-decreasing from first to last. Each rate row added advances the owning determination's `rate_count` by one and its `version` by one. Attempting to attach a rate row to a determination that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`.

Activation by a `PortalAdmin` moves a determination `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. The determination must currently be `DRAFT` and must carry at least one rate row; a determination whose `rate_count` is still 0 cannot be activated, and both failures report `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`. The activation request may optionally name a `supersedes_determination_id`. When it does, that predecessor must currently be `ACTIVE`, must govern the same `locality_code`, and must have an `effective_date` strictly earlier than the activating determination's own — a predecessor that is not `ACTIVE`, belongs to another locality, or is not strictly earlier is likewise rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`.

Localities may not be governed twice at once. Before activation completes, the service compares the activating determination's governing period — half-open, as described in §2.11 — against the governing period of every other determination in the same locality whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`. If any of those periods intersects the activating one, the activation is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, unless the intersecting determination is precisely the one named as `supersedes_determination_id`. Because both periods are half-open, a determination whose `effective_date` sits exactly on another's `expiry_date` — whether that expiry was declared at creation or written into the row by an earlier supersede — passes cleanly, while one whose `effective_date` falls strictly inside another's span does not.

When activation succeeds it sets `status` to `ACTIVE` and advances the determination's `version` by one. Where a predecessor was named, the same act drives that predecessor `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`: the predecessor's `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_id` is set to the successor's `id`, its `expiry_date` is overwritten with the successor's `effective_date`, and its `version` advances by one. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — a determination never leaves it. Note the deliberate asymmetry here: a determination whose declared `expiry_date` has already slipped past the logical clock still keeps `status` `ACTIVE` and still governs its locality until some successor actually supersedes it. Expiry alone changes nothing.

The supersede then cascades twice more, and both hops are attributed to the supersede rather than to the activation. First, every project whose `wage_determination_id` points at the superseded determination and whose `status` is still `AWARDED` is re-pointed to the value of `superseded_by_id`, with that project's `version` advancing by one; projects that have already moved past `AWARDED` keep the determination they were awarded under. Second, on each re-pointed project, every payroll submission whose `workflow_status` is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` has the wage arithmetic on all of its lines redone against the successor's rate rows: each line's `required_base_cents`, `required_fringe_cents`, `creditable_fringe_cents` and `line_underpayment_cents` are recomputed, and the submission's `straight_time_underpayment_cents` and `make_whole_underpayment_cents` are refreshed from the new line values. Submissions further along than `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` are left exactly as filed. Callers can trace this history afterwards, because the successor link is queryable in both directions: `superseded_by_id` reads as a filter that finds the successors of a determination.

### 3.2. Contractors, Staff Rosters, Apprenticeship Programs and Workers

Contractors are registered by a `PortalAdmin` with a `legal_name`, which must be unique across the system and between 2 and 120 characters, and a `dir_registration_number` giving the firm's public-works contractor registration number, which must also be unique. A firm that is a subsidiary may declare its corporate parent through `parent_contractor_id`, which points at another contractor row and is left null for an independent firm; the corporate children of a contractor are found by filtering on that same field. The request may set `registration_status`, which is either `REGISTERED` or `LAPSED` and defaults to `REGISTERED`. A newly registered contractor always starts with `debarment_status` `CLEAR`, a `debarment_risk_score` of 0, a null `last_finding_at`, and zeroed `open_critical_finding_count` and `outstanding_restitution_cents`.

Each contractor carries a staff roster. A `PortalAdmin` adds a staff member by supplying a `full_name` — the name that will be printed on the statement of compliance — and a `capability` of `PREPARER`, `CERTIFIER` or `BOTH`, optionally accompanied by a `notary_reference_number` recording the member's notary commission reference. That commission reference matters only at certification time, and only on projects whose portal demands a notarized statement. Staff rows are lightweight records: they carry `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from §2.4's common properties, and the roster is browsable filtered by `capability`.

Apprenticeship programs are registered separately, because a single program sponsors apprentices across many contractors. A program carries a `registration_number`, unique across the system; a `sponsor_name` identifying the joint apprenticeship committee or employer that sponsors it; a `trade_code`, uppercase and 2 to 12 characters, which must line up with the `trade_code` on the wage rate rows the program's apprentices will be paid under; a `registration_status` of `REGISTERED`, `PROVISIONAL` or `LAPSED`, defaulting to `REGISTERED`; an `allowed_apprentice_hours_per_100_journey` between 1 and 100, expressing how many apprentice hours the program permits for every 100 journeyworker hours of the same trade within a single payroll week; and a `period_count` between 1 and 8 giving the number of apprentice periods the program defines. Only a program in `REGISTERED` standing supports apprentice-rate payroll lines — `PROVISIONAL` and `LAPSED` programs do not — and a `PortalAdmin` may move a program between the three standings at any time, which advances the program's `version` by one.

Workers are registered by a `PayrollPreparer` rather than by the agency, since the employing firm owns the payroll identity. Each worker names an `employer_contractor_id` giving the employer of record, a `worker_number` — the payroll identifier printed on the weekly certified payroll in place of a social security number, unique within that employer — a `full_name` as it is reported on certified payroll, and a `default_classification_code` giving the classification the worker is normally reported under. An apprentice additionally names an `apprentice_program_id` identifying the enrolling program and an `apprentice_period`, a one-based index selecting an entry from the published apprentice scale; both are null for a journeyworker. The period must be between 1 and 8, and when a program is named it must not exceed that program's `period_count`. A newly registered worker starts with an `interview_count` of 0. Because worker registration is an ownership-checked operation, the acting preparer's staff row must belong to the contractor named as `employer_contractor_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`.

### 3.3. Fringe Benefit Plans and the Two Annualization Denominators

Prevailing-wage obligations may be discharged partly in cash and partly through bona fide benefit contributions, and the arithmetic that converts an annual plan contribution into an hourly credit is where most disputes in this domain start. A `PayrollPreparer` declares a plan against their own contractor with a `plan_name`, unique within that contractor; a `plan_type` of `BONA_FIDE_TRUST`, `UNFUNDED` or `CASH_IN_LIEU`; an `annual_contribution_cents` giving the total contributed over the annualization year, which may be zero but not negative; an `annual_total_hours` counting every hour worked by covered employees in that year across both public and private work; and an `annual_public_works_hours` counting only the subset performed on covered public works. Both hour figures must be strictly greater than zero and carry two decimal places, and the public-works figure may not exceed the total. A newly declared plan starts with `approval_status` `PENDING_APPROVAL`.

Declaring a plan computes and stores **both** defensible hourly credits at once, from identical inputs. The **annualized-all-hours** figure is stored in `annualized_credit_cents` and is obtained by dividing the plan's `annual_contribution_cents` by its `annual_total_hours` and rounding the quotient half up to a whole cent. The **covered-hours-only** figure is stored in `claimed_credit_cents` and is obtained by dividing the same `annual_contribution_cents` by `annual_public_works_hours` alone, again rounded half up to a whole cent. A plan whose `plan_type` is `CASH_IN_LIEU` skips both divisions: both `annualized_credit_cents` and `claimed_credit_cents` are set to 0, because cash in lieu of benefits is reported as cash on the payroll line and never as a plan credit.

The distinction between the two figures is not cosmetic. Because covered public-works hours can never exceed total hours, the covered-hours-only figure is always greater than or equal to the annualized figure, and is strictly greater whenever the contractor also performs private work. The **annualized-all-hours figure is authoritative for compliance**: it, and only it, is what the wage engine may credit against a fringe obligation. The covered-hours-only figure is the contractor-facing number the plan sponsor asserts; it is surfaced when a contractor's plans are listed, side by side with the authoritative figure, and it may never be used to reduce a computed wage deficiency.

An `AgencyComplianceOfficer` then rules on the plan by supplying a `decision`, which on this endpoint is required and must be exactly `APPROVE` or `REJECT` — any other string, including `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, is a validation failure carrying `ERR_VALIDATION` that leaves the plan untouched — and which sets `approval_status` to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` respectively, re-runs both credit computations from the stored inputs, and advances the plan's `version` by one. Only an `APPROVED` plan yields a non-zero credit on a payroll line. A plan still sitting in `PENDING_APPROVAL` credits nothing, but it is not an error to name it on a line; a plan that has been `REJECTED` may not be named on a line at all, and doing so is refused with `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`.

### 3.4. Awarding a Project and Building Its Tier Structure

Before a project can exist, the reporting portal it will file into must be registered. A `PortalAdmin` registers a portal with a `portal_code` — a short code such as `LCPTRACKER_CA`, unique across all portals and between 3 and 40 characters — and a `submission_format` describing the wire format the portal accepts for a weekly certified payroll, which is one of `WH347_PDF`, `XML_LCP` or `CSV_FLAT`. The portal's timing and notarization settings are covered in §3.7, where they govern the filing deadline.

A project is created by a `PortalAdmin` with a `project_name`, unique and between 2 and 120 characters; an `awarding_agency` naming the public body that awarded the contract; a `locality_code` giving the county of the work site; a `zone_code` of `BASE`, `ZONE1` or `ZONE2`, defaulting to `BASE`, which selects which travel-zone rate row will apply to the project's payroll; a `wage_determination_id` locking a determination to the project at award; a `reporting_portal_id` naming the portal that will receive its weekly certified payroll; a `funding_source` of `FEDERAL_DAVIS_BACON`, `STATE_ONLY` or `DUAL`, declaring which prevailing-wage regime governs; and a `contract_award_date` giving the award instant. The project's `locality_code` must equal the `locality_code` of the determination it locks, and the determination's `effective_date` must fall at or before the `contract_award_date`; a project failing either alignment is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new project begins at `status` `AWARDED` with `posture` `OPEN` and every derived counter at 0.

Tiers are added to the project one at a time. Each tier names the `contractor_id` performing the work and a `subcontract_amount_cents` giving the value of that tier's scope, which must be at least 1 cent. The prime contractor's tier omits `upper_tier_assignment_id` entirely and receives a computed `tier` of 0; a subcontract names the assignment that awarded it through `upper_tier_assignment_id` and receives a `tier` one greater than that upper tier's, up to a maximum depth of 4. The lower tiers awarded by a given tier are found by filtering on that same upper-tier field. A named upper tier must belong to the same project, or the request is refused with `ERR_UPPER_TIER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Tier creation is also gated on the firm's standing: the named contractor must have `debarment_status` `CLEAR` and `registration_status` `REGISTERED`, and a firm that is `PROPOSED` or `DEBARRED`, or whose registration has gone `LAPSED`, cannot be brought onto a project at all — the attempt fails with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DEBARRED`. A newly created tier sits at `status` `INVITED` with `version` 1 and all its ledgers at zero.

Activating a tier is a `PortalAdmin` action that moves it `INVITED → ACTIVE`. The tier must currently be `INVITED`; the contractor must be `CLEAR` and `REGISTERED` at that moment, not merely at creation time, and a firm whose standing has slipped in the interim is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DEBARRED`; the owning project must be either `AWARDED` or `ACTIVE`; and when the tier has an upper tier, that upper tier must itself be `ACTIVE` at this moment, or the activation fails with `ERR_UPPER_TIER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Subcontract tiers therefore come online strictly top-down. Success sets `status` to `ACTIVE` and advances the tier's `version` by one.

Once at least one tier-0 assignment on the project is `ACTIVE`, a `PortalAdmin` may advance the project `AWARDED → ACTIVE` by supplying a `to_status` of `ACTIVE`. An advance attempted while no prime tier is active, or from any status other than `AWARDED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATUS_TRANSITION`. When physical work finishes, the same operator advances the project `ACTIVE → SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` with a `to_status` of `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE`; this requires the project to be `ACTIVE` and its `posture` not to be `FROZEN`, and a frozen project is held back with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. Final closeout — `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE → CLOSED` — is described in §3.9, because its preconditions are enforcement conditions rather than construction milestones.

Tiers do not merely move forward. A tier that is `ACTIVE` is driven `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` by the system whenever a finding with `severity` `CRITICAL` reaches `OPEN` against one of that tier's payroll submissions, and whenever the tier's contractor reaches `debarment_status` `DEBARRED`. Suspension advances the tier's `version` by one and then, as a consequence of the suspension itself, forces the parent project to recompute `suspended_assignment_count` and re-evaluate `posture`, advancing the project's `version` by one. An `AgencyComplianceOfficer` may later return a tier `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` by supplying a `reason`; the tier must be `SUSPENDED`, its `open_finding_count` must be exactly 0, and its contractor's `debarment_status` must be back to `CLEAR` — a firm that is still `PROPOSED` or `DEBARRED` blocks reinstatement with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DEBARRED`, and a request against a tier that is not `SUSPENDED` fails with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. Reinstatement advances the tier's `version` and again recomputes the project's suspended-assignment count and posture. This cycle between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` may repeat without limit over the life of a tier. `TERMINATED` is the one state a tier never leaves.

### 3.5. Assembling the Weekly Certified Payroll

A weekly certified payroll belongs to one tier and one payroll week. A `PayrollPreparer` opens one by naming the `contract_assignment_id` whose payroll it is, the `week_ending_date` closing the seven-day payroll week, the `prepared_by_staff_id` identifying the staff member who assembled it, and the `expected_contract_assignment_version` described in §2.7. Two optional fields complete the payload: `superseded_submission_id`, naming the returned filing this one replaces, and `no_work_performed`, a flag that defaults to false and asserts the tier performed no covered work in the week. A new submission is created with `workflow_status` `DRAFT` and `compliance_status` `UNVERIFIED`, its `certified_by_staff_id`, `statement_signed_at`, `submitted_at` and `accepted_by_user_id` all null, and its computed totals at zero; creating it also advances the owning tier's `version` by one.

The `week_ending_date` must be a Saturday at exactly midnight UTC — `00:00:00.000Z` — and any other instant, including a Saturday with a non-zero time component, is refused with `ERR_WEEK_ENDING_NOT_SATURDAY`. Only one live payroll may occupy a given week on a given tier. The service compares the new submission's covered week, closed on both ends as described in §2.11, against the covered week of every existing submission on the same tier whose `workflow_status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ACCEPTED`; if the two intervals share any instant the create is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_WEEK_OVERLAP`. Rows in `RETURNED` are the exception: a returned filing releases its week, so a correction may be filed for it.

Correction filings are how a returned payroll is fixed, because `RETURNED` is terminal for the row it lands on. When `superseded_submission_id` is supplied it must name a submission on the same `contract_assignment_id`, carrying the same `week_ending_date`, and currently in `workflow_status` `RETURNED`; a target on another tier, for another week, or in any other filing state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUPERSEDE_TARGET`. Note also the interaction with §2.7: because returning a payroll advances the tier's `version`, a preparer who read the tier before the return must re-read it, or their correction filing will be rejected as stale.

Payroll lines are added to a `DRAFT` submission one worker at a time. Each line names the `worker_id` it reports and the `classification_code` actually worked that week, which need not equal the worker's `default_classification_code`. Hours are split across `straight_hours`, which must be at least 0 and at most 40, and `overtime_hours`, which must be at least 0 and has no upper bound; both carry two decimal places. Cash rates come in as `base_rate_paid_cents`, the straight-time cash hourly rate actually paid, and `overtime_rate_paid_cents`, the overtime cash hourly rate actually paid, which defaults to 0 and is disregarded when the line reports no overtime hours; neither may be negative. Fringe reporting uses `fringe_cash_paid_cents`, the hourly fringe amount paid to the worker in cash, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative, and optionally `fringe_plan_id`, naming the benefit plan whose hourly credit is claimed on this line — left null when the entire fringe obligation was discharged in cash. A `reported_as_apprentice` flag, defaulting to false, declares that the contractor is claiming the apprentice scale for this line. Every computed field on a fresh line — `required_base_cents`, `required_fringe_cents`, `creditable_fringe_cents`, `reclassified_journey_hours` and `line_underpayment_cents` — stays at 0 until the payroll is finalized. Adding a line advances the owning submission's `version` by one.

Several checks fire as each line is added. A submission may carry at most one line per worker, so a second line for the same `worker_id` on the same submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WORKER_LINE`. A named `fringe_plan_id` must belong to the contractor of the line's tier — naming another firm's plan is `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_FOREIGN` — and must not be in `approval_status` `REJECTED`, which is `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. Fringe may not be counted twice: a line may not simultaneously name a plan whose `plan_type` is `CASH_IN_LIEU` and report a `fringe_cash_paid_cents` greater than 0, and a line that does both is refused with `ERR_FRINGE_DOUBLE_COUNT`. A wage rate row must exist for the combination of the project's locked determination, the line's `classification_code`, and the project's `zone_code`; when none does, the line is refused with `ERR_NO_WAGE_RATE_FOR_CLASSIFICATION`. And when `reported_as_apprentice` is true, three apprentice conditions are checked immediately: the worker's `apprentice_program_id` must resolve to a program whose `registration_status` is `REGISTERED`, or the line is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_PROGRAM_NOT_REGISTERED`; that program's `trade_code` must equal the `trade_code` of the matched wage rate row, or the line is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_TRADE_MISMATCH`; and the worker's `apprentice_period` must be no greater than both the length of the matched row's `apprentice_scale_pct` array and the program's `period_count`, or the line is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_PERIOD_OUT_OF_SCALE`. All four of the referential checks in this paragraph's second half — program registration, trade alignment, period within scale, and rate coverage — are re-run at finalization, because a program's standing or a determination's rate schedule may have moved between line entry and filing.

### 3.6. Finalization: The Wage Engine

Finalization is where the service earns its keep. A `PayrollPreparer` finalizes a submission that is still `DRAFT`, and doing so runs the complete wage arithmetic, writes every computed field on the submission and its lines, and advances the filing axis `DRAFT → PENDING_CERTIFICATION` while the compliance axis moves off `UNVERIFIED`.

Four preconditions guard the act. The submission must currently be `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. The payroll week must be over: the logical clock must read at or after the submission's `week_ending_date`, and an earlier clock is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_WEEK_NOT_ENDED`. The line inventory must be internally consistent: either the submission carries at least one payroll line and `no_work_performed` is false, or it carries zero lines and `no_work_performed` is true — a zero-line submission with the flag false, and a submission carrying lines with the flag true, are both refused with `ERR_EMPTY_PAYROLL`. And the surrounding structure must permit filing: the project's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`, and the owning tier's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.

The four computations then run in a pinned order that must not be varied, because each consumes what the previous one wrote: first the apprentice-ratio evaluation, then the required prevailing rate per line, then the creditable fringe per line, then the underpayment arithmetic. Alongside them the submission's `total_hours_reported` is set to the sum, over every line, of that line's `straight_hours` plus its `overtime_hours`, and its `filing_due_at` is derived from the project's portal as described in §3.7. Finalization advances the submission's `version` by one.

**Step one — the apprentice ratio.** Lines are grouped by trade family, where a line's trade family is the `trade_code` of the wage rate row matched for it in step two's lookup. The ratio is evaluated strictly per submission and per week; lines belonging to other submissions of the same week or the same tier are never pooled in. Within each trade family, the apprentice hour total is the sum of `straight_hours` plus `overtime_hours` over the lines whose `reported_as_apprentice` is true, and the journeyworker hour total is the same sum over the lines whose `reported_as_apprentice` is false. The permitted ratio for that family is the smallest `allowed_apprentice_hours_per_100_journey` among the distinct apprenticeship programs referenced by that family's apprentice lines — the most restrictive program in the crew governs the whole crew, and because a minimum is order-independent no tie-break is needed. The permitted apprentice hours for the family are then the journeyworker hour total multiplied by that permitted ratio and divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. The excess for the family is the apprentice hour total minus the permitted hours when that difference is positive, and zero otherwise; an apprentice total exactly equal to the permitted hours is compliant, not excessive. The submission's `excess_apprentice_hours` is the sum of the per-family excesses across all trade families present on the payroll.

Excess hours must be repriced, and which lines absorb them is deterministic. The candidate lines for a trade family are that family's lines whose `reported_as_apprentice` is true, ordered by descending total hours — straight plus overtime — with ties broken by ascending `created_at` on the payroll line. Walking that order, each candidate absorbs into its `reclassified_journey_hours` either the whole remaining excess or its own total hours, whichever is smaller, until the family's excess is exhausted. This allocation makes one pass and one pass only, using the journeyworker hour total as it stood *before* any reclassification. It is tempting to argue that repriced hours enlarge the journeyworker pool, which would enlarge the permitted apprentice allowance, which would shrink the excess — the service deliberately does not chase that loop. There is no second pass and no fixed-point iteration: reclassified hours remain counted as apprentice hours for ratio purposes and are treated as journeyworker hours for wage-pricing purposes only.

**Step two — the required prevailing rate.** For each line, the service locates the single wage rate row belonging to the project's locked `wage_determination_id` whose `classification_code` equals the line's `classification_code` and whose `zone_code` equals the project's `zone_code`. Rows on determinations that have been superseded are excluded from this lookup even if the project once pointed at them, and when no such row exists the finalization is refused with `ERR_NO_WAGE_RATE_FOR_CLASSIFICATION`. From the matched row, the journeyworker basic rate is that row's `base_hourly_rate_cents` plus its `zone_adjustment_cents`. A line whose `reported_as_apprentice` is false takes that journeyworker basic rate as its `required_base_cents` unchanged. A line whose `reported_as_apprentice` is true instead looks up the worker's enrolling program — a missing program, or one whose `registration_status` is not `REGISTERED`, is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_PROGRAM_NOT_REGISTERED`; a program whose `trade_code` differs from the matched row's `trade_code` is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_TRADE_MISMATCH` — and then selects the percentage sitting at the position in the matched row's `apprentice_scale_pct` array given by the worker's `apprentice_period`, counting the first entry as period 1. When no entry exists at that position the finalization is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_PERIOD_OUT_OF_SCALE`. The line's `required_base_cents` is then the journeyworker basic rate multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, and rounded half up to a whole cent. In every case — apprentice or journeyworker — the line's `required_fringe_cents` is the matched row's `fringe_hourly_rate_cents` in full. This is the pinned interpretation and it is worth stating flatly: the apprentice scale discounts the basic rate only, and an apprentice is owed the complete fringe amount an experienced journeyworker in the same classification is owed.

**Step three — the creditable fringe.** A line that names no `fringe_plan_id` has a plan credit of zero. A line that names one resolves the plan and checks that its `contractor_id` matches the contractor of the line's tier, refusing with `ERR_FRINGE_PLAN_FOREIGN` when it does not. From there the credit follows three cases: a plan whose `approval_status` is anything other than `APPROVED` contributes zero; an `APPROVED` plan whose `plan_type` is `UNFUNDED` also contributes zero, because an unfunded promise is not a bona fide contribution; and an `APPROVED` plan of any other type contributes its stored `annualized_credit_cents` — the authoritative annualized-all-hours figure from §3.3, never the contractor-facing `claimed_credit_cents`. The line's `creditable_fringe_cents` is then the smaller of that plan credit and the line's own `required_fringe_cents`. That cap is deliberate and it is absolute: fringe credit in excess of the determination's fringe requirement is discarded at this point and can never be carried over to offset a shortfall in the basic rate. Cash fringe is treated differently — `fringe_cash_paid_cents` is not capped here and counts in full toward the total hourly rate the worker actually received. Plans that a contractor holds but that a line does not name contribute nothing to that line.

**Step four — the underpayment.** Each line is priced in four hour buckets, because reclassified hours are owed the journeyworker rate while the remaining hours are owed the apprentice-scaled rate, and overtime is owed one and a half times the basic component either way. Reclassified hours are drawn from straight time first: the reclassified straight-time hours are the smaller of the line's `reclassified_journey_hours` and its `straight_hours`, and the reclassified overtime hours are whatever reclassified hours remain after that. The line's still-scaled straight hours are its `straight_hours` less the reclassified straight-time hours, and its still-scaled overtime hours are its `overtime_hours` less the reclassified overtime hours.

Against those buckets the service sets what was paid and what was required. The total straight-time hourly compensation the worker actually received is the line's `base_rate_paid_cents` plus its `fringe_cash_paid_cents` plus its `creditable_fringe_cents`; the total overtime hourly compensation received is the line's `overtime_rate_paid_cents` plus the same two fringe components. On the required side, the scaled straight-time requirement is `required_base_cents` plus `required_fringe_cents`; the journeyworker straight-time requirement replaces the scaled basic rate with the full journeyworker basic rate — the matched row's `base_hourly_rate_cents` plus its `zone_adjustment_cents` — and adds the same `required_fringe_cents`. The scaled overtime requirement is `required_base_cents` multiplied by the overtime factor of one and a half, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus `required_fringe_cents` added afterwards; the journeyworker overtime requirement is the full journeyworker basic rate multiplied by that same factor of one and a half, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus `required_fringe_cents` added afterwards. The fringe component is never multiplied by the overtime factor — that too is a pinned interpretation.

Each of those four requirements yields an hourly deficiency: the requirement minus the corresponding amount received when that difference is positive, and zero when the worker was paid at or above the requirement. Overpayment in one bucket never offsets underpayment in another. The line's straight-time deficiency in cents is then the scaled straight-time hourly deficiency multiplied by the still-scaled straight hours, plus the journeyworker straight-time hourly deficiency multiplied by the reclassified straight-time hours, with that sum rounded half up to a whole cent. The line's overtime deficiency in cents is the scaled overtime hourly deficiency multiplied by the still-scaled overtime hours, plus the journeyworker overtime hourly deficiency multiplied by the reclassified overtime hours, again rounded half up to a whole cent. The line's `line_underpayment_cents` is the sum of those two rounded figures. Rounding happens exactly once per line per hour category and never anywhere else in this step: the alternative of accumulating exact per-hour products across every line and rounding once at the very end is explicitly rejected, since it can differ from the pinned order by as much as one cent per line.

Rolling those figures up gives the submission's two totals, both of which are stored. `straight_time_underpayment_cents` is the sum of the per-line straight-time deficiencies only — the preliminary screen the portal shows, which omits every overtime deficiency and is therefore strictly smaller than the full figure whenever any line carries overtime hours together with an overtime deficiency. `make_whole_underpayment_cents` is the sum of the per-line `line_underpayment_cents` values, and it is the authoritative make-whole total: it drives the compliance axis and it is the ceiling against which a finding's back-wage demand is validated. Lines belonging to a superseded — that is, `RETURNED` — submission for the same week are excluded from both totals; only the lines of the submission being finalized participate.

A read-only summary exposes both variants side by side for any submission, echoing the `payroll_submission_id`, reporting `straight_time_underpayment_cents` and `make_whole_underpayment_cents`, the `variant_delta_cents` between them — the make-whole total minus the straight-time total — plus the submission's `excess_apprentice_hours` and the `line_count` of payroll lines it carries.

Finalization closes by moving the compliance axis, which is a second, independent state field on the very same submission row. Both `make_whole_underpayment_cents` and `excess_apprentice_hours` being exactly zero drives the axis `UNVERIFIED → COMPLIANT`. A `make_whole_underpayment_cents` greater than zero, or an `excess_apprentice_hours` greater than zero, or both, drives it `UNVERIFIED → DEFICIENT` instead. The two axes evolve independently from this point onward: a submission can be `ACCEPTED` on the filing axis while cycling through `DEFICIENT`, `RESTITUTION_PENDING` and `REMEDIED` on the compliance axis, and the compliance axis has no terminal state at all.

### 3.7. Signing the Statement of Compliance and Filing to the Portal

Every project files into exactly one reporting portal, and it is the portal that decides when a weekly payroll becomes late. Beyond the `portal_code` and `submission_format` introduced in §3.4, a portal declares a `cutoff_offset_days` — the whole number of days after the payroll week's end at which its filing window closes, which must be between 1 and 21 — and a `grace_hours` value, the extra hours appended to that cutoff instant before a filing counts as late, which defaults to 0 and may not exceed 72, so a portal can extend its own deadline by at most three further days. A portal also declares `requires_notarized_statement`, a flag defaulting to false which, when true, makes the notary reference mandatory at signing time.

Finalization writes the deadline onto the submission. A submission's `filing_due_at` is its `week_ending_date` advanced by the portal's `cutoff_offset_days` whole days and then by the portal's `grace_hours` hours. Because `week_ending_date` is always a Saturday at exactly midnight UTC, this instant is exact to the millisecond. Only the portal the project pointed at is consulted, and only the submission's own week-ending instant; changing a portal's settings after a submission has been finalized does not retroactively move that submission's `filing_due_at`.

Signing is a single act that both executes the statement of compliance and transmits the payroll, and it is reserved to a `CertifyingOfficial`. The submission must currently be in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`; any other filing state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. The acting staff row must have a `capability` of `CERTIFIER` or `BOTH`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_STAFF_CAPABILITY_MISSING`, and it must belong to the contractor of the submission's tier, failing which it is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. The project's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the filing is held with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`.

The heart of this operation is segregation of duties. The staff row signing the statement of compliance must not be the staff row named in the submission's `prepared_by_staff_id`. A firm whose single employee holds `capability` `BOTH` therefore cannot self-certify their own work: the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Two distinct people, or at minimum two distinct staff rows, are structurally required to move a payroll from assembly to filing.

Two optional payload fields complete the request. `notary_reference_number` carries the notary commission reference; when the project's portal has `requires_notarized_statement` true, this field must be present *and* must equal the acting staff member's own stored `notary_reference_number`, and an absent or non-matching value is refused with `ERR_NOTARIZATION_REQUIRED`. `apprentice_ratio_exception_note` carries the contractor's written explanation for exceeding the apprentice ratio; when the submission's `excess_apprentice_hours` is greater than zero this note must be present and non-empty, and its absence is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_RATIO_EXCEEDED`. A submission with exactly zero excess apprentice hours needs no note at all. Note the design intent: an over-ratio week is not blocked from being filed, it is blocked from being filed silently.

There is also an outer boundary past which a portal simply will not accept paper. At signing time the logical clock must read at or before the submission's `week_ending_date` advanced by ninety days; a clock reading exactly at that hard-close instant is still accepted, while anything beyond it is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Note the difference between this and lateness — lateness is recorded, not refused.

A successful signing writes `certified_by_staff_id` with the acting staff row's identifier, sets `statement_signed_at` and `submitted_at` to the current logical clock instant, stores the supplied `notary_reference_number` on the submission when one was given, and sets `is_late` to true exactly when `submitted_at` falls strictly after `filing_due_at`. A submission transmitted at the deadline instant precisely is on time, not late. The filing axis then advances `PENDING_CERTIFICATION → SUBMITTED` and the submission's `version` advances by one.

### 3.8. Agency Review, Acceptance and the Certified-Hours Ledger

Filed payroll waits in `SUBMITTED` until an `AgencyComplianceOfficer` picks it up, which advances the filing axis `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and takes no payload at all; a pickup attempted against a submission in any other filing state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. The submission's `version` advances by one.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the officer has two exits. Acceptance advances the axis `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED` and requires no payload, but it is gated on the compliance axis: a submission whose `compliance_status` is `DEFICIENT` may not be accepted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNDERPAYMENT_UNRESOLVED`. A submission in `COMPLIANT`, `RESTITUTION_PENDING` or `REMEDIED` may be accepted, as may one still in `UNVERIFIED` — though in practice finalization always moves it off that state. Acceptance records the acting officer's `X-User-ID` in the submission's `accepted_by_user_id`, which is the value that will later block that same officer from adjudicating a finding raised against this very payroll. It also adds the submission's `total_hours_reported` to the owning tier's `cumulative_certified_hours`, advancing both the submission's `version` and the tier's `version` by one.

Return is the other exit: an officer supplying a `return_reason` advances the axis `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED`. `RETURNED` is terminal for the row — a returned payroll is never re-edited and never re-filed. Correction happens only by opening a fresh submission that names this row through `superseded_submission_id`, as described in §3.5. Returning advances the submission's `version` by one and, importantly, also advances the owning tier's `version` by one, precisely so that a preparer holding a stale reading of the tier is forced to re-read it before the superseding filing is accepted.

Acceptance is reversible, and the reversal is a true compensating action. An `AgencyComplianceOfficer` supplying a `rescission_reason` — which must be present — moves the axis `ACCEPTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, clears `accepted_by_user_id` back to null, and subtracts the submission's `total_hours_reported` from the tier's `cumulative_certified_hours`, restoring exactly the value that stood before acceptance. Both the submission's `version` and the tier's `version` advance by one. One condition protects the audit record: rescission is refused when any on-site interview has already been compared against this submission — that is, when an interview names it through `compared_submission_id` and sits in `CONDUCTED_MATCHED` or `CONDUCTED_DISCREPANT` — because an auditor's observation was measured against this filing and that comparison cannot be un-made. Such an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`, as is any rescission aimed at a submission that is not currently `ACCEPTED`. The `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED → UNDER_REVIEW` cycle may repeat, and the certified-hours ledger described in §2.10 stays exact across every traversal.

### 3.9. On-Site Interviews and the Audit Fork

Paper compliance and site reality are two different things, which is why a `LaborStandardsAuditor` goes to the job site and talks to workers. Scheduling an interview names the `project_id` whose site will be visited, the `contract_assignment_id` giving the tier the worker was performing under, the `worker_id` to be interviewed, and the interview window as `window_start` and `window_end`, half-open per §2.11. A new interview starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` and occupies a buffered window on the worker.

That buffer is what keeps auditors from double-booking a worker. The requested window is expanded by a full hour — sixty minutes — on each side, and the expanded span must not intersect the *unexpanded* window of any other interview on the same `worker_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONDUCTED_MATCHED` or `CONDUCTED_DISCREPANT`. Because the comparison is made against the expanded span, two windows that merely touch end-to-end still collide, and the request is refused with `ERR_INTERVIEW_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Interviews in `CANCELLED` do not occupy anything: cancelling frees the window, and a new interview on the same worker may then take an overlapping or buffer-adjacent slot.

Conducting an interview records what the auditor saw — an `observed_classification_code` naming the classification the worker was actually performing, and an `observed_hourly_rate_cents` giving the cash rate the worker stated — and then forks on whether that observation agrees with the paper. The interview must be `SCHEDULED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVIEW_STATE`, and the logical clock must read at or after `window_start`. The service then looks for the payroll the observation should be measured against: on the interview's tier, exactly one submission must have `workflow_status` `ACCEPTED` and a covered week — closed on both ends per §2.11 — containing `window_start`. That row becomes the interview's `compared_submission_id`; when no such row exists, or when more than one does, the conduct is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`.

The **matched** branch is taken when the compared submission carries a payroll line for the interviewed worker whose `classification_code` equals the `observed_classification_code` and whose `base_rate_paid_cents` is greater than or equal to the `observed_hourly_rate_cents`. It writes `observed_classification_code`, `observed_hourly_rate_cents` and `compared_submission_id`, moves the interview `SCHEDULED → CONDUCTED_MATCHED`, advances the worker's `interview_count` by one, and advances the interview's `version` by one. It raises no finding and leaves the compared submission's compliance axis exactly where it was.

The **discrepant** branch is taken when any of three conditions holds: the compared submission has no payroll line for the interviewed worker at all, or its line for that worker carries a `classification_code` different from the `observed_classification_code`, or its line's `base_rate_paid_cents` is strictly less than the `observed_hourly_rate_cents`. It writes the same three observation fields, moves the interview `SCHEDULED → CONDUCTED_DISCREPANT`, advances the worker's `interview_count` by one, and advances the interview's `version` by one — and then does strictly more. It auto-creates a finding whose `project_id` is the interview's project, whose `payroll_submission_id` is the compared submission, whose `worker_id` is the interviewed worker, whose `source_interview_id` is this interview, whose `finding_type` is `MISCLASSIFICATION`, whose `severity` is `MAJOR`, whose `restitution_required_cents` is 0, and whose `status` is `OPEN`. That finding reaching `OPEN` then cascades exactly as any auditor-raised finding does: the compared submission's compliance axis moves `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` or `REMEDIED → DEFICIENT`, and the project, tier and contractor counters are all recomputed. Findings created this way carry a non-null `source_interview_id` and are found by filtering on it; findings an auditor raises directly leave it null.

Cancellation is the third exit from `SCHEDULED`. An auditor supplying a `cancellation_reason` moves the interview `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and advances its `version` by one; an attempt to cancel an interview that has already been conducted or cancelled is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVIEW_STATE`. All three of `CONDUCTED_MATCHED`, `CONDUCTED_DISCREPANT` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.10. Findings, Responses and the Adjudication Loop

A `LaborStandardsAuditor` raises a finding against a specific weekly filing by naming the `project_id` the finding is recorded against, the `payroll_submission_id` it cites, optionally the `worker_id` affected — left null for a finding against the submission as a whole — a `finding_type` of `UNDERPAYMENT`, `MISCLASSIFICATION`, `APPRENTICE_RATIO`, `FRINGE_CREDIT` or `MISSING_PAYROLL`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and a `restitution_required_cents` giving the total back-wage obligation the finding imposes, which may be zero but not negative. The cited submission's `workflow_status` must be one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED` or `RETURNED` — payroll still in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` has not yet been filed and cannot be cited — and a citation against either of those two states is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. When the `finding_type` is `UNDERPAYMENT`, the demanded `restitution_required_cents` may not exceed the cited submission's computed `make_whole_underpayment_cents`; the service's own authoritative arithmetic is the ceiling on the demand, and a larger figure is refused with `ERR_RESTITUTION_ABOVE_COMPUTED_UNDERPAYMENT`.

Raising a finding is the most far-reaching single act in the system. The new row starts at `status` `OPEN` with `restitution_outstanding_cents` equal to its `restitution_required_cents`, a `response_round` of 0, a null `response_narrative`, a `raised_by_user_id` holding the acting auditor's `X-User-ID`, and a `response_due_at` set to the current logical clock instant advanced by thirty days. From there five things happen. The cited submission's compliance axis moves `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` or `REMEDIED → DEFICIENT` — a submission that had already been remedied is knocked back into deficiency by a fresh finding, which is precisely what makes that axis cyclic. The contractor's `last_finding_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, and its `open_critical_finding_count`, `outstanding_restitution_cents` and `debarment_risk_score` are recomputed, which may drive that contractor `CLEAR → PROPOSED` and from there suspend every one of its `ACTIVE` tiers on every project. The tier's `open_finding_count` is recomputed, and when the new finding's `severity` is `CRITICAL` that tier is additionally driven `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. The project's `open_finding_count`, `open_critical_finding_count`, `outstanding_restitution_cents` and `suspended_assignment_count` are recomputed and its `posture` re-evaluated per §2.9 — which means a single critical finding freezes the project outright, immediately blocking further payroll work on every tier of it. Every row touched along that chain has its `version` advanced.

The contractor answers through a `CertifyingOfficial`, who supplies a `response_narrative` together with the `expected_finding_version` required by §2.7. The finding must be in `OPEN` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`; any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. The acting staff row must belong to the contractor of the cited submission's tier, or the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`, and must hold `capability` `CERTIFIER` or `BOTH`, or it is refused with `ERR_STAFF_CAPABILITY_MISSING`. A successful filing writes the `response_narrative` — overwriting whatever text a previous round had left there — advances `response_round` by one, moves the finding `OPEN → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, and advances the finding's `version` by one.

Adjudication is an `AgencyComplianceOfficer` action carrying a `decision` and an optional `reviewer_note`. The finding must be in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`. Before either outcome is evaluated, segregation of duties is enforced: the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `accepted_by_user_id` recorded on the cited submission, so the officer who accepted a payroll may never adjudicate a finding raised against it, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SOD_REVIEW_CONFLICT`. A `decision` to reject moves the finding `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_REJECTED` and advances its `version` by one, which reopens the contractor's ability to file a further response. That revision loop — `RESPONSE_REJECTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_REJECTED` — may repeat without bound, and `response_round` is the running count of how many rounds have been filed.

A `decision` to accept is the exit. It requires the finding's `restitution_outstanding_cents` to be exactly 0 — the agency does not close a finding whose back wages are still owed, and an outstanding balance blocks acceptance with `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. Acceptance moves the finding `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_ACCEPTED`, advances its `version` by one, and then unwinds the exposure the finding created: the project's `open_finding_count`, `open_critical_finding_count` and `outstanding_restitution_cents` are recomputed and its `posture` re-derived, advancing the project's `version`; the tier's `open_finding_count` is recomputed, advancing the tier's `version`; the contractor's `open_critical_finding_count` and `debarment_risk_score` are recomputed, which may drive that contractor `PROPOSED → CLEAR`; and the cited submission's compliance axis may advance `DEFICIENT → REMEDIED` or `RESTITUTION_PENDING → REMEDIED`. Both of those compliance moves require the same two conditions: every finding citing that submission must be in `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and the sum of their `restitution_outstanding_cents` must be exactly 0.

Withdrawal is the auditor's own exit. Supplying a `withdrawal_reason`, an auditor moves a finding `OPEN → WITHDRAWN` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED → WITHDRAWN`, provided no restitution payment on that finding has reached `DISBURSED` — money that has already moved cannot be wished away, and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. Withdrawal sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, forces `restitution_outstanding_cents` to 0, advances the finding's `version` by one, sets every restitution payment on the finding that is still `PENDING` to `VOIDED`, and then recomputes the project's finding counters, outstanding restitution and `posture`, the tier's `open_finding_count`, and the contractor's `open_critical_finding_count` and `debarment_risk_score`. `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal states of a finding.

### 3.11. Restitution, Debarment Risk and Project Closeout

Back wages are paid worker by worker. A `PayrollPreparer` schedules a payment against a finding by naming the `worker_id` who is the payee and an `amount_cents` of at least 1 cent. A ceiling applies at scheduling time: the sum of `amount_cents` over the payments already on that finding whose `status` is `PENDING` or `DISBURSED`, plus the new amount, must not exceed the finding's `restitution_required_cents`, and a request that would push the committed total past the obligation is refused with `ERR_RESTITUTION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`. A scheduled payment starts at `status` `PENDING` with a null `disbursed_at` and a null `reversal_reason`, and it deliberately leaves the finding's outstanding balance untouched — scheduling is a promise, not a movement.

Disbursement is the movement, and it too is a `PayrollPreparer` action taking no payload. The payment must be `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`; the finding must be in `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`; and the payment's `amount_cents` must not exceed the finding's current `restitution_outstanding_cents`, or it is refused with `ERR_RESTITUTION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`. Success moves the payment `PENDING → DISBURSED` and stamps `disbursed_at` with the current logical clock instant. It then reduces the finding's `restitution_outstanding_cents` by the paid amount, increases the owning tier's `cumulative_restitution_cents` by the same amount, and recomputes the contractor's `outstanding_restitution_cents` and `debarment_risk_score`, advancing the `version` of the payment, the finding, the tier and the contractor by one each.

Two further consequences follow from where the balance lands. When the disbursement drives the finding's outstanding balance to exactly zero while its `finding_type` is `UNDERPAYMENT` and it sits in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, the finding auto-advances `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` without any agency action — full payment of an underpayment demand settles it — and that auto-acceptance in turn advances the cited submission's compliance axis to `REMEDIED` when no other finding on that submission remains non-terminal, with the project's counters and `posture` recomputed behind it. When instead an outstanding balance remains above zero, the cited submission's compliance axis moves `DEFICIENT → RESTITUTION_PENDING`, recording that money has started to flow but the obligation is not yet satisfied.

Disbursements are reversible by an `AgencyComplianceOfficer`, and the same operation covers two materially different cases selected by the payment's current state. A payment that is `DISBURSED` and whose finding is not `WITHDRAWN` moves `DISBURSED → REVERSED` with the supplied `reversal_reason`, which must be present: the finding's `restitution_outstanding_cents` is increased by the payment's `amount_cents`, the tier's `cumulative_restitution_cents` is decreased by the same amount, the contractor's `outstanding_restitution_cents` is recomputed, and all four rows — payment, finding, tier, contractor — have their `version` advanced by one. If that finding had auto-advanced to `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` solely because its balance hit zero, it rolls back to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`; the cited submission's compliance axis then returns to `RESTITUTION_PENDING` when at least one `DISBURSED` payment still exists on any finding citing that submission, and to `DEFICIENT` when none does. A payment that is still `PENDING`, by contrast, moves `PENDING → VOIDED` with the same required `reversal_reason`, leaves the finding's outstanding balance, the tier's cumulative restitution and the contractor's outstanding restitution all completely unchanged because no funds had moved, and advances only the payment's own `version` by one. A payment already in `REVERSED` or `VOIDED` cannot be reversed again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`; both are terminal.

Debarment risk is the running summary of a contractor's exposure, recomputed whenever a finding is created, whenever a finding changes status, whenever a restitution payment is disbursed or reversed, and whenever the logical clock is moved — the clock move re-scores every contractor whose `last_finding_at` is not null and reports how many were re-scored. The score starts from the findings against that contractor's tiers whose `status` is `OPEN`, `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`; findings that reach `RESPONSE_ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` drop out of the calculation entirely and stop contributing any weight at all. Each such finding whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` contributes forty points, each `MAJOR` fifteen points, and each `MINOR` eight points. A further twenty-five points are added — once, not per finding — when the contractor's `outstanding_restitution_cents` has reached five thousand dollars, that is 500,000 cents or more. Against that raw total the service applies a decay for good behaviour: when `last_finding_at` is null the decay is zero, and otherwise it is five points for each complete four-week period — each full 28 days — that has elapsed between `last_finding_at` and the current logical clock instant, with any partial period discarded rather than rounded up. The contractor's `debarment_risk_score` is the raw total less that decay, clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100.

The score drives the contractor's own state machine. A contractor in `CLEAR` whose recomputed score reaches seventy is driven `CLEAR → PROPOSED`; the threshold is inclusive, so a score of exactly 70 does propose debarment. That transition advances the contractor's `version` by one and, as a consequence of the proposal itself, drives every one of that contractor's `ACTIVE` tiers on every project through `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. A contractor in `PROPOSED` whose recomputed score falls below seventy — because findings were accepted or withdrawn, or because decay accumulated as the clock advanced — is driven `PROPOSED → CLEAR`, advancing its `version` by one. That `CLEAR → PROPOSED → CLEAR` cycle may be traversed repeatedly by the same firm.

Only a `LaborStandardsAuditor` can make the proposal final. Supplying a `to_status` and a `reason`, the auditor either debars a contractor that is currently `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → DEBARRED`, or restores that contractor to `CLEAR`. Both outcomes require the firm to be in `PROPOSED`: a `CLEAR` firm cannot be debarred directly and a `DEBARRED` firm cannot be restored, and either attempt fails the state-legality rung of §2.8. A `to_status` of `DEBARRED` performs the debarment; a `to_status` of `CLEAR` performs the restoration, which is the same `PROPOSED → CLEAR` transition the risk score can trigger on its own. Debarment sets `debarment_status` to `DEBARRED`, advances the contractor's `version` by one, sets every one of that contractor's tiers whose `status` is `INVITED` or `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with each tier's `version` advancing, and then recomputes `suspended_assignment_count` and `posture` on each affected project. `DEBARRED` is terminal: a debarred firm never returns to `CLEAR` and can never be brought onto another tier.

Closeout is the last act, and it is where the ledgers are proven. A `PortalAdmin` supplying a `to_status` of `CLOSED` moves a project `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE → CLOSED`, and the project must currently be `SUBSTANTIALLY_COMPLETE` — any other origin is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATUS_TRANSITION`. Three conditions must hold together: the project's `open_finding_count` must be 0, its `outstanding_restitution_cents` must be 0, and no payroll submission on any of its tiers may sit in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RETURNED`. Any of the three failing refuses the closeout with `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_BLOCK_CLOSEOUT` — note that a `RETURNED` payroll blocks closeout even though it is terminal on its own axis, because the correction it invites was never filed. A successful closeout sets `status` to `CLOSED`, resets `posture` to `OPEN`, advances the project's `version` by one, and drives every tier on the project whose `status` is `INVITED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to `TERMINATED`, advancing each of those tiers' `version` by one. `CLOSED` is terminal for the project.
