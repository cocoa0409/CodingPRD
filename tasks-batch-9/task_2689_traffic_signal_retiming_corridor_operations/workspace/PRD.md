# Traffic Signal Retiming & Corridor Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A city traffic operations division runs signal retiming as a recurring program rather than as a one-off project. Every year a fixed engineering budget has to be pointed at the corridors that will repay it: the ones whose timing plans are oldest, whose traffic volumes have shifted the most, and whose citizens complain the loudest. Today that decision is made in spreadsheets, the before-and-after studies that justify it live in field notebooks, and the pedestrian-clearance arithmetic that keeps the division out of an accessibility lawsuit is redone by hand every time a split changes. The consequence is not merely administrative: a corridor can be signed off as retimed while one of its intersections is running a cycle length its local controller was never allowed to serve, and nobody notices until a complaint arrives.

This document specifies the Traffic Signal Retiming & Corridor Operations API, the system of record for that program. It carries the signal inventory (controller family, detection health, communication connectivity, pedestrian equipment), the corridors assembled from that inventory, the screening and selection arithmetic that turns plan age, volume change and complaint history into a single comparable priority score, the turning movement counts and floating-car travel time runs that constitute the before and after studies, the timing plans themselves — cycle length, splits and offsets by time of day, coordinated against the corridor master — the pedestrian timing compliance record, transit and emergency vehicle priority configuration with its conflict ranking and per-cycle second budget, the field observations and fine-tuning loop that follow implementation, and the delay, stops and arrivals-on-green measurements that close the loop.

Two properties dominate the design. First, **the arithmetic is the contract**: a coordinated cycle, a rescaled split, a required pedestrian change interval and a priority score must be reproducible to the last second and the last hundredth, because engineers, citizens and auditors all read the same numbers. Second, **the division separates authorship from approval**: whoever drafts a timing plan or proposes a priority treatment may not be the engineer who certifies or approves it. Every endpoint in this specification is mounted under the `/signal-ops` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including requests to the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they act in. If either header is absent or blank, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the six roles enumerated below, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401), also 401 rather than 403 — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a permission shortfall. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not among those authorised for the target endpoint in §4, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are:

- **`SignalOpsAdmin`** — the traffic operations division administrator. Owns the signal inventory, corridor creation and membership, corridor screening and the selection decision, the project phase gates (starting and completing fine tuning, closing or abandoning a project), priority deployment and suspension, and the logical clock.
- **`TimingEngineer`** — develops the timing. Opens retiming projects, advances them out of data collection, runs the corridor coordination pipeline, drafts timing plans and phase splits, adjusts splits, submits plans for certification, proposes priority configurations, and implements the retiming in the field.
- **`CertifyingEngineer`** — the independent reviewer. Certifies or returns submitted timing plans, waives failing pedestrian compliance checks with written justification, approves or rejects priority configurations, and produces and certifies performance evaluations.
- **`FieldTechnician`** — collects field data: turning movement counts, floating-car travel time runs, and field observations during fine tuning.
- **`CitizenLiaison`** — the public-information officer. Registers citizen requesters and moves complaints through triage, linkage to a retiming project, resolution and reopening.
- **`PublicRequester`** — a member of the public acting on their own behalf.

Five of these roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `SignalOpsAdmin`, `TimingEngineer`, `CertifyingEngineer`, `FieldTechnician` or `CitizenLiaison`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the workflow demands accountability — as `authored_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a timing plan, `waived_by_user_id` on a pedestrian compliance check, `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a priority configuration, `observer_user_id` on a field observation, and `certified_by_user_id` on a performance evaluation — and it is compared against those recorded values by the segregation-of-duties rule.

`PublicRequester` is the one **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `PublicRequester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Requester` row; ownership checks compare a resource's requester foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A `PublicRequester` reading a requester row, listing that requester's complaints, or filing a complaint must be acting on their own row — the requester identified in the path, and the `requester_id` supplied when filing, must equal `X-User-ID`. Otherwise the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `CitizenLiaison` faces no such restriction and may read and file on behalf of any requester.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against the identity that authored the artefact under review, and it guards exactly three operations. Certifying a timing plan and waiving one of that plan's pedestrian compliance checks are refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the plan's `authored_by_user_id`; approving or rejecting a priority configuration is refused with the same code when the acting `X-User-ID` equals that configuration's `proposed_by_user_id`. Returning a submitted plan to draft is deliberately outside this gate, because a return withdraws rather than blesses the work.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the pagination window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string, with no type prefix and no embedded domain meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied value is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was first stored.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. Three record types are deliberately append-only and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at`, with both `updated_at` and `version` absent from their payloads entirely: corridor membership rows, turning movement counts and travel time runs. Nothing in this system mutates a membership row, a count or a run after creation, so neither field would ever change and neither is exposed.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads real wall-clock time. All age arithmetic, all freshness windows and every timestamp the server stamps for itself — a complaint's `received_at`, a travel time run's `recorded_at`, an intersection's `last_retimed_at`, a corridor's `screened_at` and a project's `last_tuning_completed_at` — read a server-held **logical clock**. On startup that clock stands at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it stays there until an administrator moves it.

A `SignalOpsAdmin` moves the clock by supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`. The clock is monotonic: a target instant strictly earlier than the clock's current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to its current value is permitted. Moving the clock never mutates a stored row and never increments any `version`; ages and window comparisons simply produce different answers the next time they are evaluated.

### 2.6. Nested Retrieval and Path Resolution

The URL surface mixes flat collections with reverse-reachability sub-resources, and no path nests more than one child segment below a parent. Identifiers embedded in a path are resolved strictly **left to right**, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing decides the response: a missing corridor yields `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_FOUND`, a missing intersection `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing retiming project `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing timing plan `ERR_TIMING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, a missing phase split `ERR_PHASE_SPLIT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing pedestrian compliance check `ERR_PED_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, a missing priority configuration `ERR_PRIORITY_CONFIG_NOT_FOUND`, a missing complaint `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing requester `ERR_REQUESTER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing field observation `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing performance evaluation `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`. All eleven are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a **request body** are resolved after every path identifier has been resolved, and they raise the same 404 codes. Body-borne foreign keys appear when enrolling an intersection in a corridor, opening a retiming project against a corridor, drafting a timing plan against a project and an intersection, proposing a priority configuration against an intersection, logging a field observation against a project and an intersection, filing a complaint against a requester and a corridor, and linking a complaint to a project.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When a single request breaks several rules, exactly one error comes back, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in the fixed order below, and **the first failure wins** — a later check never masks an earlier one, and an earlier check never defers to a "more interesting" later one.

1. Both headers present and non-blank — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The acting role is authorised for the target endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. Segregation of duties, on plan certification, pedestrian-check waiver and priority-configuration approval only — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
5. Requester ownership, on reading a requester row, listing a requester's complaints, and filing a complaint — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
6. Path identifiers resolve, left to right — the eleven 404 codes of §2.6.
7. Body foreign keys resolve — the 404 codes for corridor, intersection, project and requester.
8. Request-body shape: required fields present, declared types honoured, numeric ranges respected, enum values inside their declared sets — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
9. Pagination parameters in range, on list endpoints — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
10. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — the 409 state codes (`ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_SCREENED`, `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_SELECTED`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_DATA_COLLECTION`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IMPLEMENTED`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_FIELD_TUNING`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_EVALUATION`, `ERR_PROJECT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_COORDINATED`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_PED_CHECK_NOT_FAILING`, `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PROPOSED`, `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_DEPLOYED`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_PROVISIONAL`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_OBSERVATIONS_UNRESOLVED`).
11. Optimistic concurrency: the supplied parent version matches — `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INTERSECTION_VERSION`.
12. Accessible-signal consistency on intersection registration and update — `ERR_APS_STANDARD_MISMATCH`.
13. Operational posture gates — `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CERTIFICATION` on plan certification, `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DEPLOYMENT` on priority deployment.
14. Pedestrian compliance gate on plan submission — `ERR_PED_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.
15. Minimum-green gate on plan submission — `ERR_MIN_GREEN_VIOLATION`.
16. Before-study completeness — `ERR_BEFORE_STUDY_INCOMPLETE`.
17. Plan coverage — `ERR_PLAN_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`.
18. Complaint-to-project corridor agreement — `ERR_COMPLAINT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`.
19. After-study availability on evaluation certification — `ERR_NO_AFTER_STUDY`.
20. Study-window freshness on the advance out of data collection — `ERR_STUDY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
21. Time-of-day window overlap on plan submission — `ERR_TOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
22. Travel-time-run spacing — `ERR_RUN_SPACING_VIOLATION`.
23. Field-observation timing window — `ERR_OBSERVATION_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.
24. Local cycle ceiling — `ERR_CYCLE_EXCEEDS_LOCAL_MAX`.
25. Approach saturation — `ERR_OVERSATURATED_APPROACH`.
26. Priority rank uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIORITY_RANK`.
27. Phase-number uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PHASE_NUMBER`.
28. Corridor-membership uniqueness and gap-free sequencing — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CORRIDOR_MEMBER`, then `ERR_SEQUENCE_INDEX_GAP`.
29. A non-empty eligible member set on corridor screening — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEMBERS`.
30. The selection score threshold — `ERR_SCORE_BELOW_SELECT_THRESHOLD`.
31. No active project already on the corridor — `ERR_CORRIDOR_PROJECT_ACTIVE`.
32. The corridor is selected before a project may open on it — `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_SELECTED`.
33. The fine-tuning round ceiling — `ERR_TUNING_ROUND_LIMIT`.
34. Requester email uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUESTER_EMAIL`.
35. The priority second budget can fund the deployment — `ERR_PRIORITY_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Because the state check (rung 10) precedes the concurrency check (rung 11), a request that is both aimed at a wrongly-stated resource and carrying a stale parent version reports the state error, not the stale-version error. And because the request-shape check (rung 8) precedes every business rule, a body whose `tod_start_min` is not strictly less than its `tod_end_min` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and never reaches the overlap rule.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Units

Wherever a value is described as rounded to a number of decimal places, the rounding method is **round half up**: a digit sequence exactly halfway between two representable values goes to the larger magnitude. Percentages, mean travel times, average plan ages and the corridor priority score are all carried to **2 decimal places**. A count's critical flow ratio is carried to **4 decimal places**. Cycle lengths, splits, offsets and priority extension seconds are whole seconds. Two places in the system deliberately do **not** round: plan age in days truncates downward to a whole day, and the priority second budget takes the floor of its fraction, so neither can ever overstate. Two places deliberately round **up**: a recommended cycle length is raised to the next whole multiple of five seconds, and a required pedestrian change interval is raised to the next tenth of a second.

Rounding happens exactly where each calculation in §3 says it happens, and nowhere else. Intermediate components of a score are never individually rounded before being combined; a single rounding step is applied to the result. Conversely, the mean travel times stored on a performance evaluation are rounded **before** they are fed into the delay-reduction percentage, so that the percentage is reproducible from the two persisted means.

Distances are feet, speeds are feet per second, cycle lengths and intervals are seconds, time-of-day boundaries are whole minutes after local midnight, and volumes are vehicles per hour.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Two create operations mutate a parent aggregate as a side effect, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one; omitting it is a request-shape failure.

Drafting a timing plan requires `expected_project_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the retiming project the plan belongs to. If that value does not equal the project's current `version` — because some other writer has advanced the project since the client read it, for example by drafting the plan that legitimately supersedes the one this client still believes is current — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The same field and the same rejection code guard the creation of a provisional performance evaluation for a project.

Proposing a priority configuration requires `expected_intersection_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target intersection. A mismatch against the intersection's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTERSECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because an intersection's `version` moves whenever its deployed coordinated cycle, and therefore its per-cycle priority second budget, is recomputed; a configuration proposed against a stale budget view would be claiming extension seconds that no longer exist, so the write is refused outright rather than silently re-ranked.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three must not be expected to carry one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.10. Operational Posture: The Gate on Signal Work

Every intersection carries a derived **operational posture** that summarises whether the division may safely act on its timing. It is computed from exactly two of the intersection's own fields — `comm_status` and `detection_status` — and never from any child row, and it is re-derived on every read and on every write that materialises the intersection. If communication is `OFFLINE`, or detection has `FAILED`, the posture is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if communication is `INTERMITTENT`, or detection is `PARTIAL` or `NONE`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. In every remaining case the posture is `READY`.

Posture is consumed in four distinct ways, and the three sets involved are not interchangeable. Screening eligibility, before-study completeness, plan coverage and the corridor coordination participant set all admit both `READY` and `RESTRICTED` members and drop only `BLOCKED` ones. Certifying a timing plan requires the plan's intersection to be anything other than `BLOCKED`, failing with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CERTIFICATION` when it is. Deploying a priority configuration is stricter still: it requires `READY` exactly, and a `RESTRICTED` intersection is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DEPLOYMENT`. And the programming variant of corridor scoring ignores posture altogether, deliberately admitting `BLOCKED` members that the screening variant excluded.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system touch more than one aggregate. When they do, the whole set of effects is applied within the triggering request, in the order this document pins, and every touched row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — including rows the caller never named. Where a cascade processes a set of rows, the processing order is stated explicitly (by sequence position, by time-of-day start, or by `created_at` ascending) so that the resulting numbers are reproducible; a row's `id` is never used as an ordering key.

Cascades are also atomic with respect to their own guards. When a cascade link's precondition fails, the entire triggering request fails and **nothing** is written — not the row the caller was creating, and not the earlier links' effects.

### 2.12. Conserved Quantities and Compensation

Three balances in this system are invariants rather than conveniences, and every operation that disturbs one is paired with an operation that restores it.

The **split-second invariant**: once a timing plan has left `DRAFT`, the sum of its phases' effective splits equals its operating cycle length exactly, with no drift. The plan carries both totals — `drafted_split_total_sec`, the sum of the drafted splits, and `effective_split_total_sec`, the sum of the effective splits — so the invariant is observable without re-adding the phases.

The **priority budget invariant**: at every observable moment, an intersection's `granted_priority_extension_total_sec` plus its `unallocated_priority_budget_sec` equals its `priority_budget_sec`. Suspending a deployed configuration returns its granted seconds to the unallocated pool rather than discarding them.

The **complaint partition**: every complaint of a corridor sits in exactly one of three buckets — `complaint_open_count`, `complaint_linked_count` and `complaint_terminal_count` — and those three always sum to `complaint_total_count`. Any transition that moves a complaint between buckets decrements one and increments another in the same operation, so the partition is never briefly wrong.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Signal Inventory

Everything in the retiming program hangs off the inventory, so the inventory is where a `SignalOpsAdmin` starts. Registering a signalized intersection requires a `location_name` — a human-readable cross-street label of 1 to 120 characters that may not be blank — and a `controller_type` naming the cabinet family installed in the field, one of `TS2_CABINET`, `ATC_CABINET` or `LEGACY_170`. Everything else about the intersection is optional at registration and takes a documented default. `detection_status` records vehicle-detection health as last field-verified and defaults to `FULL`, the other admissible values being `PARTIAL`, `FAILED` and `NONE`. `comm_status` records connectivity back to the central signal system and defaults to `ONLINE`, the alternatives being `INTERMITTENT` and `OFFLINE`. `max_cycle_length_sec` is the locally adopted ceiling on cycle length at this intersection; it defaults to 150 seconds and must lie between 60 and 240 seconds inclusive.

Three fields describe the pedestrian environment. `aps_installed` is true when an Accessible Pedestrian Signal — the audible and tactile equipment a blind pedestrian relies on — is present, and defaults to false. `walking_speed_standard` names the walking speed used to size pedestrian clearance and defaults to `STD_3_5_FPS`, the alternative being `EXTENDED_3_0_FPS`. `pedestrian_recall` is true when the pedestrian phase is served on recall every cycle, whether or not anybody pushed the button, and defaults to false. The accessible-signal rule binds the first two together: an intersection with `aps_installed` true must carry `walking_speed_standard` `EXTENDED_3_0_FPS`, and a registration that pairs `aps_installed` true with `STD_3_5_FPS` is rejected with `ERR_APS_STANDARD_MISMATCH`.

Updating an intersection is how detection and communication health, and the pedestrian configuration, stay current: an administrator may revise `detection_status`, `comm_status`, `aps_installed`, `walking_speed_standard`, `pedestrian_recall` and `max_cycle_length_sec`, each independently optional. The accessible-signal rule is re-evaluated against the *resulting* pair, and it is enforced in both directions on an update. Turning `aps_installed` on while `walking_speed_standard` would remain `STD_3_5_FPS` is `ERR_APS_STANDARD_MISMATCH`; so is clearing `aps_installed` while `walking_speed_standard` would remain `EXTENDED_3_0_FPS`. The two fields must move in the same request. Every accepted update re-derives `operational_posture` from the new health values and increments the intersection's `version`.

Two derived fields are visible from the moment an intersection is registered. `operational_posture` is the `READY` / `RESTRICTED` / `BLOCKED` gate described in §2.10. `timing_plan_age_days` reports how stale the intersection's deployed timing is, in whole days: while `last_retimed_at` is still null — meaning no timing plan for this intersection has ever reached `DEPLOYED` — the age reads exactly 3650 days, a synthetic "ten years old" that guarantees a never-retimed intersection outranks any genuinely dated one. Once `last_retimed_at` holds an instant, the age is the whole number of seconds between it and the logical clock's current value, divided by 86400 and truncated downward to a whole day; a gap of one second short of two full days reports 1, not 2. Notice what the age is *not*: it is derived from `last_retimed_at` alone and never inspects timing plan rows.

Four more fields on an intersection are maintained by operations described later but are worth naming here because they appear on every read. `deployed_coordinated_cycle_sec` is the `cycle_length_sec` of this intersection's `DEPLOYED` timing plan holding the lowest `tod_start_min`, and is null while no plan of this intersection is `DEPLOYED`. `priority_budget_sec` is the per-cycle green-extension budget available to priority requests, `granted_priority_extension_total_sec` is the sum of `granted_extension_sec` across this intersection's priority configurations whose `deployment_status` is `DEPLOYED`, and `unallocated_priority_budget_sec` is the slack between them. All three start at 0 for a freshly registered intersection, because it has no deployed cycle yet.

The inventory listing accepts four filters — `controller_type`, `comm_status`, `detection_status` and `operational_posture` — alongside the usual pagination. Because an intersection sits at the centre of the reference graph rather than at the top of a tree, five reverse-reachability listings hang off it: the corridor membership rows that enrol it in corridors; the timing plans controlling it, filterable by `status` and `tod_period`; the priority configurations installed there, filterable by `priority_type`, `approval_status` and `deployment_status`; the field observations logged at it, filterable by `resolution_status`; and the turning movement counts collected at it, filterable by `study_role` and `period`.

### 3.2. Corridor Assembly, Screening and Program Selection

A **corridor** is the unit the retiming program budgets against. Creating one requires a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, unique across corridors, and a `jurisdiction_class` — `ARTERIAL`, `COLLECTOR` or `LOCAL` — which is the functional classification that later multiplies the corridor's score. `annual_volume_change_pct` is the signed year-over-year traffic volume change in percent; it defaults to 0, must lie between -100.0 and 400.0 inclusive, and only its absolute magnitude ever enters scoring, so a 30 percent collapse in volume and a 30 percent surge are equally strong evidence that the timing no longer fits. A new corridor starts at `selection_status` `UNSCREENED` with `member_count` 0, every complaint counter 0, `priority_score` 0, `priority_score_variant` null and `screened_at` null.

Intersections are enrolled one at a time, in order along the corridor. Each enrolment names the `intersection_id` and the `sequence_index` — the 1-based position along the corridor in the primary progression direction — plus `distance_from_prev_ft`, the centreline distance in feet from the member one position earlier, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Sequencing is kept gap-free by construction: the supplied `sequence_index` must equal the corridor's current `member_count` plus 1, and anything else is refused with `ERR_SEQUENCE_INDEX_GAP`. An intersection already enrolled in that corridor is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CORRIDOR_MEMBER`; the uniqueness is on the pairing of corridor and intersection, so the same intersection may legitimately belong to several corridors. The first member of a corridor has nothing before it, so a `distance_from_prev_ft` other than exactly 0 at `sequence_index` 1 is an invalid request. Each accepted enrolment recomputes `member_count` as the number of membership rows pointing at the corridor and increments the corridor's `version`.

**Screening** is the recurring act of re-measuring a corridor against the program's criteria, and it is deliberately re-runnable: a corridor arrives at `SCREENED` from `UNSCREENED` the first time, from `DEFERRED` in a later program cycle, and from `SELECTED` when a previous project has finished and the corridor is thrown back into the pool, so `UNSCREENED → SCREENED`, `DEFERRED → SCREENED` and `SELECTED → SCREENED` are all legal. All three arrivals share two guards: the corridor must have at least one member, and at least one of those members' intersections must have an `operational_posture` of `READY` or `RESTRICTED`, failing which the screen is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEMBERS` — a corridor with zero members and a corridor whose every member is `BLOCKED` both fail on exactly this code. Screening from `SELECTED` carries one additional guard: the corridor must not still have a retiming project whose status is anything other than `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, failing which the screen is refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_PROJECT_ACTIVE`.

A successful screen rebuilds the corridor's whole derived surface in one pass. `member_count` is recounted. The four complaint bucket counters are re-derived: `complaint_total_count` counts every complaint filed against the corridor regardless of status or age; `complaint_open_count` counts those in `OPEN` or `TRIAGED`; `complaint_linked_count` counts those in `LINKED`; and `complaint_terminal_count` counts those in `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`. `complaint_count_12mo` is recomputed as the trailing-twelve-month complaint pressure — the number of the corridor's complaints whose `received_at` falls inside the **closed** interval that begins 365 days before the logical clock's current value and ends at that current value, counting only complaints in `OPEN`, `TRIAGED`, `LINKED` or `RESOLVED`. Complaints in `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` are excluded from this window count, on the reasoning that a complaint the liaison judged groundless is not evidence of a timing problem; and a complaint whose `received_at` falls exactly on the lower bound, to the second, **is** counted, while one second earlier is not. Then `avg_plan_age_days` and `priority_score` are recomputed under the screening variant, `priority_score_variant` is set to `SCREENING`, `screened_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current value, and the corridor's `version` is incremented.

`avg_plan_age_days` is the mean staleness of the corridor's timing, and it is the one number in the system whose definition depends on *why* it is being computed. The corridor's membership rows are enumerated in `sequence_index` ascending order and filtered down to an included set; each included member contributes its intersection's `timing_plan_age_days`; the mean is their sum divided by their count, rounded half up to 2 decimal places **once**, applied to the mean and never to the individual ages. An empty included set yields 0.00. Under the **screening variant** the included set is the members whose intersection posture is `READY` or `RESTRICTED`, so `BLOCKED` members are dropped from both the numerator and the denominator — the division does not want a dark intersection's decade-old timing dragging a live corridor to the top of the list. Under the **programming variant** every member is included regardless of posture, because once money is being committed the corridor must be judged as it actually stands, `BLOCKED` intersections and all. The two variants produce genuinely different numbers whenever at least one member is `BLOCKED` and its age differs from the eligible members' mean.

The `priority_score` turns four inputs into one comparable number between 0 and 100. The **age component** is the corridor's `avg_plan_age_days` divided by an age saturation of 1825 days — five years, the point past which additional staleness earns no more credit — capped at 1. The **volume component** is the absolute value of `annual_volume_change_pct` divided by a volume saturation of 25.0 percent, capped at 1. The **complaint component** is `complaint_count_12mo` divided by a complaint saturation of 20 complaints, capped at 1. Those three are combined with fixed weights: 0.45 on age, 0.35 on volume and 0.20 on complaints. The weighted sum is then multiplied by the class multiplier for the corridor's `jurisdiction_class` — 1.25 for `ARTERIAL`, 1.00 for `COLLECTOR` and 0.80 for `LOCAL` — the product is capped at 1, multiplied by 100.0, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. No component and no intermediate product is ever rounded on its own; the single rounding step is the last one. Because the score is a pure function of `avg_plan_age_days` and three static inputs, whichever average-age variant ran is what determines the score, and `priority_score_variant` records which one it was: `SCREENING` or `PROGRAMMING`.

The **selection decision** is where a screened corridor either enters the program or waits for the next cycle. The acting administrator supplies a `decision` of `SELECT` or `DEFER`, optionally accompanied by a `rationale`. Either decision requires the corridor to be in `SCREENED`; from any other selection status the request is refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_SCREENED`. Both decisions recompute `complaint_count_12mo`, `avg_plan_age_days` and `priority_score` under the **programming variant** before anything else is decided, set `priority_score_variant` to `PROGRAMMING`, and increment the corridor's `version`. A successful `SELECT` moves the corridor `SCREENED → SELECTED`; a `DEFER` moves it `SCREENED → DEFERRED`. `SELECT` then imposes two further guards: the freshly recomputed programming-variant score must be at least the select threshold of 50.00 — exactly 50.00 is accepted, 49.99 is not — failing which the decision is refused with `ERR_SCORE_BELOW_SELECT_THRESHOLD`; and the corridor must have no retiming project whose status is anything other than `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_PROJECT_ACTIVE`. A refused `SELECT` writes **nothing at all**: the corridor keeps its screening-variant `priority_score`, its `priority_score_variant` of `SCREENING`, its `selection_status` of `SCREENED` and its previous `version`, exactly as if the request had never been made. `DEFER` carries no threshold and no project guard; a deferred corridor sits at `DEFERRED` until it is screened again, and `SCREENED → DEFERRED → SCREENED` is a legal loop that a corridor may traverse many times. There is no terminal selection status — a corridor is perpetually re-screenable.

The corridor listing may be narrowed by `selection_status` and by `jurisdiction_class`. Alongside its own read, a corridor exposes three reverse-reachability listings: its membership rows in sequence order, the retiming projects on it filterable by `status`, and the complaints filed against it filterable by `status` and `category`.

### 3.3. Citizen Feedback: Requesters, Complaints and the Bucket Partition

Complaint history is one of the three inputs to the priority score, which makes citizen feedback a first-class part of the program rather than a courtesy inbox. A `CitizenLiaison` registers a **requester** with a `display_name` of 1 to 80 non-blank characters and an `email` that must contain an `@` character and must be unique across all requesters; a second registration using an email another requester already holds, compared without regard to letter case, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUESTER_EMAIL`. `contact_pref` records how the division may follow up and defaults to `EMAIL`, the alternatives being `PHONE` and `NONE`. A new requester's `open_complaint_count` starts at 0; thereafter it always equals the number of that requester's complaints sitting in `OPEN`, `TRIAGED` or `LINKED`. A requester's own row and their complaint list, the latter filterable by `status`, are readable by a `CitizenLiaison` for anybody and by a `PublicRequester` only for themselves.

Filing a complaint names the `requester_id` behind it and the `corridor_id` it is about, a `category` from `COORDINATION`, `PEDESTRIAN`, `DETECTION` and `TIMING_OTHER`, and a `narrative` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters in the citizen's own words. The server stamps `received_at` with the logical clock's current value — this is the instant the trailing-twelve-month window later measures against — sets `status` to `OPEN`, leaves `linked_project_id` null and `reopen_count` at 0, refreshes the corridor's `complaint_total_count` and `complaint_open_count`, and increments the requester's `open_complaint_count` by 1. Either a `CitizenLiaison` or the `PublicRequester` themselves may file, the latter only with their own `requester_id`.

**Triage** is the liaison's first judgement. Supplying a `disposition` of `TRIAGE`, optionally with a `note`, moves the complaint `OPEN → TRIAGED`; supplying `NO_ACTION` moves it `OPEN → CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, which is the complaint machine's only terminal state. Either disposition requires the complaint to be in `OPEN`; from anywhere else the request is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`, and that same code covers every mis-stated complaint action — linking from outside `TRIAGED`, resolving from outside `LINKED`, reopening from outside `RESOLVED`. Both dispositions refresh the corridor's four bucket counters and the requester's `open_complaint_count`, and increment the complaint's `version`. The arithmetic differs between them in a way worth internalising: `TRIAGED` and `OPEN` both live in the open bucket, so a triage leaves `complaint_open_count` and the requester's `open_complaint_count` numerically unchanged, whereas `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` lives in the terminal bucket, so a no-action closure moves one unit from `complaint_open_count` to `complaint_terminal_count` and decrements the requester's `open_complaint_count` by 1.

**Linking** ties a triaged complaint to the work that will address it. The liaison supplies the `project_id` of a retiming project, which must belong to the very corridor the complaint names — a project on a different corridor is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_PROJECT_MISMATCH` — and which must not already be `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, a terminal project being refused with `ERR_PROJECT_TERMINAL`. A successful link moves the complaint `TRIAGED → LINKED`, writes `linked_project_id`, moves one unit from `complaint_open_count` to `complaint_linked_count`, and increments the complaint's `version`. The requester's `open_complaint_count` does not move, because `LINKED` is still one of the three statuses that count as open from the citizen's point of view.

**Resolution** is only available once the work has actually been measured: the project named by `linked_project_id` must be in `EVALUATION` or `CLOSED`, and a project still in data collection, plan development, implementation or fine tuning is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_EVALUATION`. The liaison may attach a `resolution_note`. A successful resolve moves the complaint `LINKED → RESOLVED`, moves one unit from `complaint_linked_count` to `complaint_terminal_count`, decrements the requester's `open_complaint_count` by 1, and increments the complaint's `version`.

Complaints come back. A citizen who reports that the problem persists has their complaint **reopened**, optionally with a `reason`: the complaint moves `RESOLVED → OPEN`, `linked_project_id` is cleared to null, `reopen_count` is incremented by 1, one unit moves from `complaint_terminal_count` back to `complaint_open_count`, the requester's `open_complaint_count` is incremented by 1, and the complaint's `version` is incremented. Two further routes back to `OPEN` are driven by project and corridor events rather than by the liaison, and both are described where they are triggered: a `LINKED` complaint whose project is abandoned returns to `OPEN` with `linked_project_id` cleared and `reopen_count` incremented, and a `TRIAGED` coordination complaint whose corridor is auto-deferred returns to `OPEN` with `reopen_count` incremented. Because a complaint may cycle through `OPEN` any number of times, `reopen_count` is the honest measure of how often the division has failed to satisfy this citizen.

### 3.4. Opening a Retiming Project and Completing the Before Study

A `TimingEngineer` opens a **retiming project** against a corridor by naming its `corridor_id`, the `study_window_start_at` instant from which before-study data is considered fresh, an optional `study_window_days` giving the length of that freshness window in whole days (defaulting to 180 and constrained to between 30 and 730 inclusive), and the `lead_engineer_user_id` — 1 to 64 non-blank characters — identifying the engineer accountable for the work. The corridor must be in `SELECTED`; any other selection status is refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_SELECTED`. The corridor must also not already carry a project whose status is anything other than `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, a second live project being refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_PROJECT_ACTIVE`. A new project starts in `DATA_COLLECTION` with `tuning_round` 0 and `last_tuning_completed_at` null, and its `eligible_member_count` is computed as the number of the corridor's members whose intersection posture is `READY` or `RESTRICTED`.

Data collection has two halves, both filled in by a `FieldTechnician`. **Turning movement counts** are recorded against an intersection and carry the `project_id` of the study they belong to, a `study_role` of `BEFORE` or `AFTER`, the `count_date` instant the count was collected in the field, a `period` of `AM_PEAK`, `MID_DAY` or `PM_PEAK`, and `phase_volumes` — an object mapping phase number to that phase's critical lane volume in vehicles per hour. `phase_volumes` must hold between 1 and 8 entries, its keys must be the decimal strings `1` through `8`, and its values must be integers between 0 and 2000 inclusive; an absent, empty or non-object `phase_volumes`, or one holding a negative volume, is an invalid request.

Each count immediately yields two derived numbers. Its `critical_flow_ratio_sum` is the sum of all its phase volumes divided by a saturation flow of 1800 vehicles per hour per lane; the comparison below is made on that exact quotient, and the value persisted on the count is that quotient rounded half up to 4 decimal places. A count whose critical flow ratio reaches the oversaturation ratio of 0.95 describes an approach that cannot be fixed by retiming — a sum of exactly 0.95 is already too much, and is rejected together with anything above it — and the recording is refused with `ERR_OVERSATURATED_APPROACH`. Otherwise the count's `recommended_cycle_sec` is computed Webster-style: the total lost time is the number of phase entries multiplied by 4.0 seconds of lost time per phase; the raw cycle is one and a half times that total lost time, plus 5.0, all divided by one minus the critical flow ratio sum; that raw cycle is then raised to the next whole multiple of 5 seconds (a raw cycle already an exact multiple of 5 stays where it is); the result is reduced to the intersection's `max_cycle_length_sec` if it exceeds it; and finally, if what remains is below the floor of 60 seconds, it is raised to 60. The clamping order matters: the local ceiling is applied to the rounded-up value, and the 60-second floor is applied last and therefore wins over a ceiling below 60.

**Travel time runs** are the floating-car half of the study, recorded against the project. Each run carries a `study_role` of `BEFORE` or `AFTER`, a `direction` of `NB`, `SB`, `EB` or `WB`, the `started_at` instant the probe vehicle entered the corridor, the end-to-end `duration_sec` (greater than 0 and no more than 3600), the `stops_count` of full stops observed (0 or more), the `arrivals_on_green_count` of signal arrivals that met a green indication, and `total_arrivals`, the number of signal arrivals observed on the run, which must be at least 1 and at least as large as `arrivals_on_green_count`. The server stamps `recorded_at` with the logical clock's current value; that stamp, not `started_at`, is what the certified performance variant later filters on.

Runs of the same project, in the same `direction` and with the same `study_role` must be genuinely separate passes, so a spacing rule applies. Treat each run as occupying the **closed** interval from its `started_at` to its `started_at` plus its `duration_sec`. A new run is accepted only when the gap between its interval and every stored run's interval, measured on whichever side the new run falls, is at least 120 seconds: a gap of exactly 120 seconds is accepted, a gap of 119 seconds is refused, and overlapping intervals or intervals that merely touch at an endpoint — a gap of zero — are refused. The rejection is `ERR_RUN_SPACING_VIOLATION`. Runs in different directions, or with different study roles, or on different projects, never constrain each other.

**Advancing** the project out of data collection moves it `DATA_COLLECTION → PLAN_DEVELOPMENT` and is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_DATA_COLLECTION` from any other status. Three substantive gates apply. Every one of the corridor's members whose intersection posture is `READY` or `RESTRICTED` must have at least one `BEFORE` turning movement count recorded for this project, and the project must hold at least one `BEFORE` travel time run; either shortfall is refused with `ERR_BEFORE_STUDY_INCOMPLETE`. And the study data must still be fresh: the logical clock's current value must be strictly earlier than `study_window_start_at` advanced by `study_window_days` whole days. The deadline instant itself already counts as expired — at exactly that instant the advance is refused with `ERR_STUDY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, while one second earlier it is accepted. A successful advance recomputes `eligible_member_count` and increments the project's `version`.

A project exposes six reverse-reachability listings: its turning movement counts filterable by `study_role`, its travel time runs filterable by `study_role` and `direction`, its timing plans filterable by `status`, its field observations filterable by `resolution_status`, the complaints linked to it filterable by `status`, and its performance evaluations filterable by `status`.

### 3.5. Plan Development: Drafting Timing Plans and Phase Splits

Once a project is in `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`, its `TimingEngineer` drafts one **timing plan** per intersection per time-of-day window. A draft names the `project_id` it belongs to and the `intersection_id` it controls, a `tod_period` label from `AM_PEAK`, `MID_DAY`, `PM_PEAK` and `OFF_PEAK`, and the window itself: `tod_start_min`, the inclusive start expressed in whole minutes after local midnight between 0 and 1439, and `tod_end_min`, the exclusive end between 1 and 1440. Windows never wrap around midnight, so `tod_end_min` must be strictly greater than `tod_start_min`; a body that violates this is an invalid request and never reaches any business rule. The drafted `cycle_length_sec` must lie between 60 and 240 seconds and must additionally not exceed the target intersection's `max_cycle_length_sec`, a drafted cycle above the local ceiling being refused with `ERR_CYCLE_EXCEEDS_LOCAL_MAX`. `coordination_ref` defaults to `MASTER`, meaning the plan will take the corridor coordinated cycle and an offset; the alternative, `LOCAL_FREE`, means the plan runs free of the corridor master and keeps an offset of 0. Drafting also requires `expected_project_version`, the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.9, whose mismatch is `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`. Drafting against a project in any status other than `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`.

A new plan starts in `DRAFT` on the workflow axis and `PENDING` on the pedestrian-compliance axis, with `offset_sec` 0, `return_count` 0, `certified_by_user_id` null, and both split totals at 0. Two writes happen at creation that matter much later: `drafted_cycle_length_sec` snapshots the supplied `cycle_length_sec`, preserving the engineer's original intent so that a later compensation can restore it exactly, and `authored_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, becoming the comparand for segregation of duties. Creation also increments the parent project's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a second client holding a stale `expected_project_version` is turned away.

Re-drafting is allowed but is not additive. When a plan is created for a `project_id`, `intersection_id` and `tod_period` combination that already carries a plan still in `DRAFT`, that earlier draft transitions `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` and has its own `version` incremented — the engineer's newest thinking replaces the old, and `SUPERSEDED` is the plan machine's terminal state. If, however, the same combination already carries a plan that has moved past drafting — one in `COORDINATED`, `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `DEPLOYED` — the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TOD_PLAN`, because superseding work that has already been coordinated or blessed would silently discard a review.

**Phase splits** give the plan its internal structure. A phase split belongs to exactly one plan, carries that plan's `timing_plan_id`, and may only be added while the plan is still in `DRAFT`; adding one to a plan that has moved on is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`. Each split names its `phase_number`, a NEMA phase number between 1 and 8 that must be unique within the plan — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PHASE_NUMBER` — and its `split_sec`, the drafted split in whole seconds inclusive of green, yellow and red clearance, which must be at least 5 and no more than 240. `min_green_sec` is the minimum green the controller must serve for the phase, at least 1 and no more than 60 seconds. `yellow_sec` is the yellow change interval, between 3.0 and 6.0 seconds, and `red_clearance_sec` is the all-red clearance interval, between 0.0 and 6.0 seconds.

Four further fields on a split describe pedestrian service. `is_pedestrian_phase` defaults to false and is true when the phase serves a crossing. `walk_sec` is the WALK interval provided, `ped_clear_sec` is the pedestrian change (flashing DON'T WALK) interval provided, and `crosswalk_length_ft` is the curb-to-curb crossing distance; all three default to 0 and may not be negative, but each of the three must be strictly greater than 0 when `is_pedestrian_phase` is true, and a pedestrian phase supplied with any of them left at 0 is an invalid request. While the plan is still in `DRAFT` a split's `effective_split_sec` simply mirrors its `split_sec`; adding a split recomputes the plan's `drafted_split_total_sec` and `effective_split_total_sec` and increments the plan's `version`.

An engineer refining a draft may adjust a split's `split_sec`, `min_green_sec`, `yellow_sec`, `red_clearance_sec`, `walk_sec`, `ped_clear_sec` and `crosswalk_length_ft`, each independently optional; whether the phase serves pedestrians is fixed at creation and is not adjustable. The adjustment is likewise only available while the parent plan is in `DRAFT`, and is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT` otherwise. Every accepted adjustment re-mirrors `effective_split_sec` onto the new `split_sec`, recomputes both of the plan's split totals, and increments the plan's `version`.

### 3.6. The Corridor Coordination Pipeline

Coordination is the single heaviest operation in the system: one request by the project's `TimingEngineer` takes every `DRAFT` plan of the project `DRAFT → COORDINATED`, and along the way it computes recommended cycles, the corridor's coordinated cycle, the rescaled splits, the progression offsets and the pedestrian compliance verdict — **in that order, which is pinned**. Evaluating pedestrian minimums before the splits have been rescaled would compare them against drafted rather than operating splits and can flip a verdict from failing to passing, so the order is part of the contract, not an implementation detail.

Before any of it runs, five gates are checked. The project must be in `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`. Every timing plan belonging to the project must be in `DRAFT`; the presence of any plan further along is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, because a mixed-status project cannot be coordinated coherently. The project must hold at least one timing plan at all, an empty plan set being an invalid request. Every eligible member intersection that has a `DRAFT` plan must have at least one `BEFORE` turning movement count for this project, failing which the whole call is refused with `ERR_BEFORE_STUDY_INCOMPLETE`. And no count feeding the pipeline may have a critical flow ratio at or above 0.95, such an approach being refused with `ERR_OVERSATURATED_APPROACH`.

Exactly one turning movement count per intersection feeds the pipeline, and which one is chosen is fully determined: among the counts for this project at this intersection with `study_role` `BEFORE`, take the one with the greatest `count_date`; if two share that `count_date`, take the one with the greatest `created_at`; if they still tie, prefer `PM_PEAK` over `AM_PEAK`, and `AM_PEAK` over `MID_DAY`. That count's `recommended_cycle_sec` — computed exactly as described in §3.4 for the count itself — is the intersection's ask.

The **corridor coordinated cycle** is then derived by constraint propagation over the participating members. Participants are the corridor's membership rows, enumerated in `sequence_index` ascending order, restricted to members whose intersection posture is `READY` or `RESTRICTED` **and** which have at least one `DRAFT` plan for this project whose `coordination_ref` is `MASTER`. If that participant set turns out empty, nothing is coordinated: there is no coordinated cycle, and every plan keeps the cycle length its engineer drafted. Otherwise the coordinated cycle is the largest of the participants' recommended cycles, raised to the next whole multiple of 5 seconds. That candidate is then tested against the hard ceiling, which is the **smallest** `max_cycle_length_sec` among the participants: a coordinated cycle above that ceiling cannot be served by every intersection on the corridor, so the whole request is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_EXCEEDS_LOCAL_MAX` and nothing is written.

With the coordinated cycle in hand, every `DRAFT` plan whose `coordination_ref` is `MASTER` has its `cycle_length_sec` overwritten with that coordinated value. `drafted_cycle_length_sec` is deliberately left alone — it is the memory of what the engineer asked for, and the return and reopen compensations described later restore `cycle_length_sec` from it. A plan whose `coordination_ref` is `LOCAL_FREE` keeps its own drafted cycle untouched.

**Split rescaling** then fits each plan's phases to whatever cycle that plan is now operating. For a given plan, its phase splits are enumerated in `phase_number` ascending order and their `split_sec` values are summed into `drafted_split_total_sec`. Each phase's `effective_split_sec` is then the phase's `split_sec` multiplied by the plan's operating cycle length, divided by that drafted total, rounded half up to a **whole second** — each phase individually, before any summation. Because rounding each phase independently rarely lands on the cycle exactly, a residual is computed as the operating cycle length minus the sum of the rounded effective splits; the residual may be positive or negative. That single residual is added to exactly one phase: the phase with the greatest `min_green_sec`, and if several tie on that, the one with the lowest `phase_number`. This is the "coordinated phase", the one the corridor's progression is built around and the one most able to absorb a second either way. After the residual is applied, the plan's `effective_split_total_sec` is the sum of the phases' effective splits, which now equals the plan's `cycle_length_sec` exactly. Two tempting alternatives are wrong and must not be used: summing the unrounded scaled values and rounding the total, and truncating every phase and then spreading the whole remainder across phases. Both produce different second-by-second splits from the same inputs.

**Offset assignment** places each plan in the progression. A plan whose `coordination_ref` is `LOCAL_FREE` gets an `offset_sec` of 0 and nothing more happens. For a plan under the corridor master, walk the corridor's membership rows in `sequence_index` ascending order and add up `distance_from_prev_ft` over every member whose sequence position is less than or equal to the sequence position of this plan's intersection — including `BLOCKED` members, because distance along the street is geometry and does not care about detector health. Divide that cumulative distance in feet by a progression speed of 44.0 feet per second (30 miles per hour) to get a cumulative travel time in seconds, round that half up to a whole second, and only then take the remainder of dividing it by the plan's `cycle_length_sec`. The rounding happens before the modulo, never after. The first member of a corridor therefore always receives an offset of 0, since it has no distance ahead of it.

Pedestrian timing is evaluated next, against the freshly rescaled effective splits, and §3.7 describes it in full. The pipeline then sets each plan's `ped_compliance_state`: `COMPLIANT` when every compliance check the pipeline created for that plan came out passing — which includes the case of a plan with no pedestrian phases at all, for which no checks are created and the condition holds vacuously — and `NON_COMPLIANT` when at least one came out failing. Finally each plan's `status` becomes `COORDINATED`, each touched plan's `version` is incremented by 1, and the project's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.7. Pedestrian Timing Compliance and Waivers

Pedestrian timing is where the division's exposure is greatest, so the compliance evaluation leaves a permanent, auditable row rather than a boolean. The coordination pipeline creates one **pedestrian compliance check** for every phase split of every plan whose `is_pedestrian_phase` is true, in `phase_number` ascending order, and each check records both what was required and what the plan actually provides. A check carries the `timing_plan_id` of the plan it evaluated and the `phase_number` of the split it evaluated, so a reader can line the verdict up against the timing without guessing.

The required numbers come from the intersection, not from the plan. `walking_speed_fps` is the walking speed applied: 3.5 feet per second when the intersection's `walking_speed_standard` is `STD_3_5_FPS`, and 3.0 feet per second when it is `EXTENDED_3_0_FPS`. `required_ped_clear_sec` is the crossing distance `crosswalk_length_ft` divided by that walking speed, raised **up** to the next tenth of a second — a crossing that computes to 12.41 seconds requires 12.5, and one that computes to exactly 12.4 requires 12.4. `required_walk_sec` is the minimum WALK interval and depends on the accessible-signal equipment: 7.0 seconds where `aps_installed` is true, and 4.0 seconds where it is not, on the reasoning that a pedestrian relying on an audible signal needs longer to register the indication and start walking. `required_phase_length_sec` is the total the phase must accommodate: the required WALK interval plus the required pedestrian change interval plus the phase's `yellow_sec` plus its `red_clearance_sec`.

Against those three requirements the check records three provided values. `provided_ped_clear_sec` snapshots the split's `ped_clear_sec`, `provided_walk_sec` snapshots its `walk_sec`, and `provided_phase_length_sec` snapshots its `effective_split_sec` — the **post-rescale** operating split, never the drafted one. The three sub-checks are then evaluated in a pinned order and the first failure decides the outcome. If the provided pedestrian change interval is less than the required one, the `result` is `FAIL` with `failure_reason` `PED_CLEAR_SHORT`. Otherwise, if the provided WALK interval is less than the required one, the `result` is `FAIL` with `failure_reason` `WALK_SHORT`. Otherwise, if the provided phase length is less than the required phase length, the `result` is `FAIL` with `failure_reason` `PHASE_TOO_SHORT`. If none of the three falls short, the `result` is `PASS` and `failure_reason` is null. Every one of the three comparisons treats equality as satisfactory: a provided value exactly equal to its requirement passes.

A failing check is not necessarily fatal, but overriding it costs a signature. A `CertifyingEngineer` may **waive** a check, moving its `result` `FAIL → WAIVED`, and must supply a `waiver_justification` in writing; the server records that text and stamps `waived_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Three guards apply: the check must currently be `FAIL`, and one already passing or already waived is refused with `ERR_PED_CHECK_NOT_FAILING`; the plan the check belongs to must still be in `COORDINATED`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_COORDINATED`; and the acting engineer must not be the plan's author, a self-waiver being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `PASS` and `WAIVED` are both terminal check results; only `FAIL` can still move.

The waiver bubbles up, but only when the plan is fully clean. After a waiver is applied, if the plan no longer holds any check whose `result` is `FAIL`, the plan's `ped_compliance_state` becomes `WAIVED` and the plan's own `version` is incremented. If other failing checks remain, the plan stays `NON_COMPLIANT` and its version does not move on account of this waiver. This is the clearest illustration of the plan's two independent state axes: the compliance axis can travel `NON_COMPLIANT → WAIVED` while the workflow axis stands perfectly still at `COORDINATED`, and — as the next subsection shows — the workflow axis can travel `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` without the compliance axis moving at all. A plan's compliance checks are listable, filterable by `result`.

### 3.8. Certification, Return and Field Implementation

**Submission** hands a coordinated plan to the reviewer, and it is the point at which four independent obligations are tested. The plan must be in `COORDINATED`, and from any other status the submission is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_COORDINATED`. Its `ped_compliance_state` must be `COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED`; a plan still `PENDING` or still `NON_COMPLIANT` is refused with `ERR_PED_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Every one of the plan's phase splits must satisfy the minimum-green obligation — the phase's `effective_split_sec` must be at least the sum of its `min_green_sec`, its `yellow_sec` and its `red_clearance_sec`, with equality acceptable — and any phase falling short is refused with `ERR_MIN_GREEN_VIOLATION`. And the plan's time-of-day window must not collide with another window already live at the same intersection.

That last rule is a **half-open** interval test with no buffer whatsoever. The plan's window runs from `tod_start_min` inclusive to `tod_end_min` exclusive, and it is compared against the window of every *other* plan on the same `intersection_id` whose status is `COORDINATED`, `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `DEPLOYED`. Any intersection of those two half-open ranges is refused with `ERR_TOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; but adjacency is explicitly fine — one plan ending at minute 540 and another starting at minute 540 do not overlap, and no separation is required between them. Contrast this deliberately with the travel-time-run spacing rule of §3.4, which uses closed intervals and demands a 120-second buffer: the two temporal rules in this system are different on purpose, and applying either one's semantics to the other is a defect. A successful submission moves the plan `COORDINATED → SUBMITTED` and increments its `version`.

**Certification** is the independent blessing. The reviewing `CertifyingEngineer` must not be the plan's author, must find the plan in `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and must find the plan's intersection in a posture other than `BLOCKED`, since certifying timing for an intersection the central system cannot even reach, or whose detection has failed outright, is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CERTIFICATION`. Certification moves the plan `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `certified_by_user_id`, and increments the plan's `version`. Note that a `RESTRICTED` intersection may be certified; only `BLOCKED` bars it.

**Returning** a plan is certification's mirror image, and it is a full compensation rather than a status change. The reviewer must supply a `reason`, and the plan must be in `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The plan then moves `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` and every write the coordination pipeline made to it is undone: `cycle_length_sec` is restored from `drafted_cycle_length_sec`; every phase split's `effective_split_sec` is restored to its own `split_sec`, processed in `phase_number` ascending order; `offset_sec` returns to 0; `ped_compliance_state` returns to `PENDING`; `effective_split_total_sec` is restored to `drafted_split_total_sec`; and every pedestrian compliance check belonging to the plan is **deleted** outright, since the verdicts described timing that no longer exists. `return_count` is incremented by 1 and so is the plan's `version`. The split-second invariant must hold with zero drift after a return: the plan is byte-for-byte back in the state a fresh draft would be in.

**Implementation** is the moment the paper becomes signal timing in the field, and it fires the largest cascade in the system. The project's `TimingEngineer` may implement only from `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT` — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_PLAN_DEVELOPMENT` — and only when coverage is complete: every one of the corridor's members whose intersection posture is `READY` or `RESTRICTED` must have at least one `CERTIFIED` plan under this project, and any gap is refused with `ERR_PLAN_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. When those hold, five linked effects are applied in order, all within the one request.

First, the project moves `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT → IMPLEMENTED` and its `version` is incremented. Second, because the project entered `IMPLEMENTED`, every timing plan of the project in `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → DEPLOYED`, processed in a fully pinned order: by the plan's intersection's corridor sequence position ascending, and within one intersection by `tod_start_min` ascending. Third, because each of those plans entered `DEPLOYED`, two things follow for each. Any *other* plan for the same `intersection_id` and the same `tod_period` that was already `DEPLOYED` — the older plan, the one with the strictly earlier `created_at` — moves `DEPLOYED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `version` incremented; an intersection therefore never has two deployed plans for one time-of-day label. And the plan's intersection is refreshed: `last_retimed_at` takes the logical clock's current value, `timing_plan_age_days` consequently becomes 0, `deployed_coordinated_cycle_sec` is re-derived as the `cycle_length_sec` of the deployed plan holding the lowest `tod_start_min`, the three priority-budget fields are recomputed from that new cycle as §3.9 describes, and the intersection's `version` is incremented.

Fourth, because those intersections' `last_retimed_at` values changed, every corridor that holds a membership row for any touched intersection recomputes its `avg_plan_age_days` and its `priority_score` under the **programming variant**, sets `priority_score_variant` to `PROGRAMMING` and increments its `version`; corridors are processed in `created_at` ascending order. A corridor whose recomputed score falls below the defer threshold of 35.00 **and** whose `selection_status` is `SCREENED` is auto-deferred, moving `SCREENED → DEFERRED`: the program has just fixed the very staleness that put it in the queue, so it no longer belongs there. A corridor at `UNSCREENED`, `SELECTED` or already `DEFERRED` is left alone regardless of its score, and a `SCREENED` corridor scoring exactly 35.00 or above is likewise left at `SCREENED`.

Fifth, because those corridors entered `DEFERRED`, their coordination complaints are requeued. For each auto-deferred corridor, every complaint whose `status` is `TRIAGED` and whose `category` is `COORDINATION` moves `TRIAGED → OPEN`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with `reopen_count` incremented by 1 and `version` incremented by 1. Complaints of any other category are untouched, and so are complaints already in `OPEN`, `LINKED`, `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`. The corridor's bucket counters are re-derived after the requeue and come out numerically unchanged, because `TRIAGED` and `OPEN` both sit in the open bucket; the affected requesters' `open_complaint_count` values are unchanged for exactly the same reason. The point of the requeue is not accounting — it is that a coordination complaint against a corridor the division has just decided not to retime must go back in front of the liaison rather than sit quietly in a queue that no longer leads anywhere.

### 3.9. Transit and Emergency Vehicle Priority

Priority treatments let a bus or an ambulance buy extra green, and because green is a zero-sum quantity within a cycle, the division rations it. A **priority configuration** is proposed by a `TimingEngineer` against a single intersection, naming the `intersection_id`, a `priority_type` of `TSP` for transit signal priority or `EVP` for emergency vehicle preemption, the `requested_max_extension_sec` of green extension the treatment asks for each cycle (at least 1 and no more than 40 seconds), an optional `max_early_green_sec` allowance for truncating the preceding red (defaulting to 0 and no more than 20 seconds), and a `conflict_resolution_rank` between 1 and 9 where a lower rank is served first. Proposal also requires `expected_intersection_version`, whose mismatch is `ERR_STALE_INTERSECTION_VERSION`.

Ranks are unique per intersection **and per priority type**, but only among configurations that are not suspended: a rank already held at the same `intersection_id` for the same `priority_type` by a configuration whose `deployment_status` is anything other than `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIORITY_RANK`. A suspended configuration releases its rank for reuse, and the same rank number may legitimately be held once for `TSP` and once for `EVP` at the same intersection. A new configuration starts with `approval_status` `PROPOSED`, `deployment_status` `NOT_DEPLOYED` and `granted_extension_sec` 0; `proposed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the parent intersection's `version` is incremented by 1.

A priority configuration carries **two independent state axes** on one row. The approval axis moves `PROPOSED → APPROVED` or `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and never moves again — `REJECTED` is terminal — while the deployment axis moves `NOT_DEPLOYED → DEPLOYED`, `DEPLOYED → SUSPENDED` and `SUSPENDED → DEPLOYED` as operations require. The deployment axis may only leave `NOT_DEPLOYED` once the approval axis has reached `APPROVED`, but no deployment move ever changes `approval_status`.

Approval is the `CertifyingEngineer`'s call and, like plan certification, is barred to the proposer. The engineer supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `note`. The configuration must be in `PROPOSED` on the approval axis; anything else is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PROPOSED`. An `APPROVE` writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `approved_by_user_id`; a `REJECT` writes no approver. Both increment the configuration's `version`.

**Deployment** is a `SignalOpsAdmin` operation with four gates and a budget test. The configuration must be `APPROVED` on the approval axis, otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_APPROVED`. Its deployment axis must be at `NOT_DEPLOYED` or `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_DEPLOYED` — redeploying an already deployed configuration is refused on that code. The intersection's posture must be exactly `READY`; both `RESTRICTED` and `BLOCKED` are refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_DEPLOYMENT`, because priority logic that fires against failed detection is a safety problem rather than a service improvement. And the intersection must already carry at least one `DEPLOYED` timing plan, since there is no cycle to extend otherwise; the absence of one is refused with `ERR_PLAN_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`.

The budget itself is derived from the cycle. An intersection's `priority_budget_sec` is one fifth — 20 percent — of its `deployed_coordinated_cycle_sec`, taken as a floor so the budget can never exceed the fraction, and it is 0 whenever no plan of the intersection is deployed and therefore no coordinated cycle exists. That budget is then handed out by a **waterfall** over the intersection's configurations whose `deployment_status` is `DEPLOYED`, in a strictly pinned order: all `EVP` configurations first, then all `TSP` configurations; within one priority type by `conflict_resolution_rank` ascending; and among configurations sharing a rank, by `created_at` ascending. A row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Walking that order, each configuration's `granted_extension_sec` is the smaller of its own `requested_max_extension_sec` and whatever remains of the budget, and the remainder shrinks by the amount granted, so an early emergency treatment can legitimately starve a later transit one. Every configuration that is not `DEPLOYED` has a `granted_extension_sec` of 0. The intersection's `granted_priority_extension_total_sec` is the sum of the granted extensions, and its `unallocated_priority_budget_sec` is the budget minus that sum.

A deployment that would grant zero seconds is not allowed to proceed quietly. When the intersection's `unallocated_priority_budget_sec` is already 0 at the moment of the deploy, the waterfall can give the newly deployed configuration nothing at all, so the request is refused with `ERR_PRIORITY_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` and **nothing is written** — no deployment status change, no rewritten grants, no version bumps. This applies to both `TSP` and `EVP` configurations; in particular an `EVP` configuration must always come out of the waterfall with a granted extension greater than zero, since an emergency treatment that cannot actually preempt is worse than none. A successful deploy sets `deployment_status` to `DEPLOYED`, re-runs the whole waterfall for that intersection and rewrites `granted_extension_sec` on **every** deployed configuration there — not only on the one just deployed — recomputes `priority_budget_sec`, `granted_priority_extension_total_sec` and `unallocated_priority_budget_sec`, increments the intersection's `version`, and increments the configuration's `version`.

**Suspension** is deployment's compensation. It requires the deployment axis to be at `DEPLOYED`, otherwise `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_DEPLOYED`, and the administrator may attach a `reason`. The configuration moves to `SUSPENDED` and its `granted_extension_sec` is zeroed; the waterfall is then re-run over the intersection's remaining deployed configurations, which is what returns the released seconds to whoever was starved before; the three intersection budget fields are recomputed; and both the intersection's and the configuration's `version` are incremented. Because the budget is re-derived rather than adjusted, the priority budget invariant of §2.12 holds after a suspension exactly as it did before.

### 3.10. Fine Tuning, Performance Measurement and Project Disposition

An implemented retiming is not a finished retiming. A `SignalOpsAdmin` starts the observation phase by supplying an `action` of `START`, which moves the project `IMPLEMENTED → FIELD_TUNING`; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IMPLEMENTED`. The same `action` field carries `COMPLETE` to end the phase, described at the end of this subsection.

During `FIELD_TUNING` a `FieldTechnician` logs **field observations**, each naming the `project_id` and the `intersection_id` observed, the `tod_period` in effect, the `observed_at` instant the observation was made, and a structured `finding_code` from `QUEUE_SPILLBACK`, `EARLY_RETURN_TO_GREEN`, `PED_CALL_MISSED`, `DETECTION_FAULT`, `PROGRESSION_BREAK` and `NONE`. The optional `requires_adjustment` flag, false by default, is the consequential one: it declares that the finding demands a timing change. The server stamps `observer_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and sets `resolution_status` to `OPEN`. Observations may only be logged while the project is in `FIELD_TUNING`, and are otherwise refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_FIELD_TUNING`. The `observed_at` instant must fall inside the tuning window: at or after the project's most recent entry into `FIELD_TUNING`, and at or before the logical clock's current value. An observation dated before tuning began, or dated in the future, is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.

An observation flagged `requires_adjustment` fires a three-link cascade on top of its own creation. The plan to reopen is the `DEPLOYED` timing plan matching the observation's `intersection_id` and `tod_period`; if more than one matches, the one with the greatest `created_at` is selected. That plan moves `DEPLOYED → DRAFT` and is compensated exactly as a returned plan is: `cycle_length_sec` is restored from `drafted_cycle_length_sec`, `offset_sec` returns to 0, `ped_compliance_state` returns to `PENDING`, every phase split's `effective_split_sec` is restored to its `split_sec`, `effective_split_total_sec` is restored to `drafted_split_total_sec`, every pedestrian compliance check of the plan is deleted, and the plan's `version` is incremented. Because that plan left `DEPLOYED`, the project itself moves `FIELD_TUNING → PLAN_DEVELOPMENT` with `tuning_round` incremented by 1 and its `version` incremented; this second link fires only if a matching deployed plan was actually found and reopened. And because the project left `FIELD_TUNING`, every performance evaluation of the project still in `PROVISIONAL` moves `PROVISIONAL → INVALIDATED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each with its `version` incremented — measurements taken against timing that has just been withdrawn cannot be allowed to stand.

The tuning loop is bounded. `tuning_round` counts completed loops back into plan development, and the loop may run at most three times: if incrementing `tuning_round` would carry it past 3, the entire observation request is refused with `ERR_TUNING_ROUND_LIMIT` and **no field observation row is created at all** — the reopen, the project transition and the invalidation are all abandoned along with it. A project that has exhausted its rounds must be finished on the timing it has.

Each observation is closed out by marking its `resolution_status` as `ADDRESSED` or `DISMISSED`; either a `FieldTechnician` or a `SignalOpsAdmin` may do so, and the observation must still be `OPEN`, a second resolution being refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_OPEN`. Completing the tuning phase — the `COMPLETE` action — requires the project to be in `FIELD_TUNING`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_FIELD_TUNING`, and requires every field observation of the project to have been resolved one way or the other; a project still holding an `OPEN` observation is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATIONS_UNRESOLVED`. Completion moves the project `FIELD_TUNING → EVALUATION`, stamps `last_tuning_completed_at` with the logical clock's current value, and increments the project's `version`. That stamp is the cutoff the certified performance variant filters on, which is why it is written here and nowhere else.

**Performance evaluations** are produced by a `CertifyingEngineer` and carry the `project_id` they measure. Creation is deliberately permitted while the project is in either `FIELD_TUNING` or `EVALUATION`, so that a provisional read-out can be taken mid-tuning to decide whether more tuning is worthwhile; any other project status is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_EVALUATION`. Creation requires `expected_project_version` and is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` on a mismatch. A new evaluation is `PROVISIONAL` with `metric_variant` `PROVISIONAL_UNWEIGHTED`, and it supersedes its predecessor: any existing `PROVISIONAL` evaluation of the same project moves `PROVISIONAL → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented. Creation also increments the project's `version` by 1.

Every evaluation stores the same nine measurements, all derived from the project's travel time runs. `mean_travel_time_before_sec` and `mean_travel_time_after_sec` are the mean corridor travel times over the included `BEFORE` and `AFTER` runs, each rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `delay_reduction_pct` is 100.0 multiplied by the difference between the before mean and the after mean, divided by the before mean, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — and it is computed from the two **already-rounded** means, so the persisted means are exactly the numbers the percentage came from. `total_stops_before` and `total_stops_after` are the sums of `stops_count` over the included before and after runs, and `stops_reduction_pct` is 100.0 multiplied by their difference divided by `total_stops_before`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; when `total_stops_before` is 0 the reduction reads 0.00 rather than failing. `aog_pct_before` is 100.0 multiplied by the sum of `arrivals_on_green_count` over the included before runs divided by the sum of `total_arrivals` over those same runs, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `aog_pct_after` is the same quantity over the included after runs. `included_after_run_count` reports how many `AFTER` runs actually entered the computation under the active variant. If the project has no `BEFORE` run at all the computation fails with `ERR_BEFORE_STUDY_INCOMPLETE`; if it has no included `AFTER` run the computation fails with `ERR_NO_AFTER_STUDY`.

Which runs are included, and how a mean is defined, both depend on the variant — and this is the single most important asymmetry in the measurement story. Under the **provisional variant**, every run with `study_role` `AFTER` is included regardless of when it was recorded, and a mean is the plain arithmetic mean: the sum of the runs' `duration_sec` divided by the number of runs, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Under the **certified variant**, an `AFTER` run is included only when its `recorded_at` is at or after the project's `last_tuning_completed_at`, so runs invalidated by the last round of fine tuning are dropped from the after mean, from `total_stops_after` and from `aog_pct_after` alike; and a mean becomes an **arrivals-weighted** mean — the sum over the included runs of `duration_sec` multiplied by `total_arrivals`, divided by the sum of `total_arrivals`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The weighting applies to the `BEFORE` mean as well as the `AFTER` mean, and `BEFORE` runs are never filtered by `recorded_at` under either variant. Identical run data therefore yields different certified numbers than provisional ones even when the two inclusion sets happen to coincide.

**Certifying** an evaluation is what makes those numbers official. The evaluation must be `PROVISIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_PROVISIONAL`. Its project must be in `EVALUATION` — a provisional read-out taken mid-tuning cannot be certified until tuning is finished — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_EVALUATION`. The project's `last_tuning_completed_at` must not be null, and a request against a project with no completed tuning is an invalid request. And at least one `AFTER` run must have a `recorded_at` at or after `last_tuning_completed_at`; without one the certification is refused with `ERR_NO_AFTER_STUDY`. Certification sets `metric_variant` to `CERTIFIED_WEIGHTED`, **recomputes every metric field** under the certified variant rather than reusing the provisional numbers, sets `certified_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the evaluation `PROVISIONAL → CERTIFIED`, and increments its `version`. `CERTIFIED`, `INVALIDATED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal evaluation states.

**Closing** a project is the administrator's last act, and the `disposition` field decides which of two very different endings applies. A `disposition` of `CLOSED` requires the project to be in `EVALUATION`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_IN_EVALUATION`, and requires at least one `CERTIFIED` performance evaluation to exist for it, the absence of one being refused with `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`. The project moves `EVALUATION → CLOSED`, its `version` is incremented, and every complaint whose `linked_project_id` is this project and whose status is `LINKED` moves `LINKED → RESOLVED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; each such complaint moves one unit from its corridor's `complaint_linked_count` to `complaint_terminal_count` and decrements its requester's `open_complaint_count` by 1. The citizens who complained get their answer as a side effect of the division closing its books.

A `disposition` of `ABANDONED` is the other ending, and it is only available before the retiming ever reached the street: the project must be in `DATA_COLLECTION` or `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT`, so the legal moves are `DATA_COLLECTION → ABANDONED` and `PLAN_DEVELOPMENT → ABANDONED`, whose side-effect sets are identical to one another in every particular. Any other status — including `IMPLEMENTED`, `FIELD_TUNING`, `EVALUATION` and the two terminal statuses themselves — is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_TERMINAL`, which is also the code returned for any attempted write against a project already `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`. Abandonment supersedes the unfinished work and requeues the citizens: every timing plan of the project whose status is `DRAFT`, `COORDINATED`, `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; and every complaint whose `linked_project_id` is this project and whose status is `LINKED` moves `LINKED → OPEN` with `linked_project_id` cleared to null and `reopen_count` incremented by 1. That complaint cascade is the exact compensation of the link operation — one unit moves from the corridor's `complaint_linked_count` back to its `complaint_open_count`, restoring the partition to its pre-link values — while the requester's `open_complaint_count` is left unchanged, because `LINKED` was already counted in that requester's open bucket. Both dispositions accept an optional `note`, and both increment the project's `version`. Abandonment applies to complaints of every `category`, unlike the auto-defer requeue of §3.8, which touches only `COORDINATION` complaints.
