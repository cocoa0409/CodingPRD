# Dental Sleep Medicine API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnoea is diagnosed by a physician and treated, in a large and growing share of cases, by a dentist. That split is the whole problem this platform exists to solve. A sleep physician runs a home sleep apnoea test or an attended polysomnogram, reads it, and refers the patient to a dental sleep provider for a custom mandibular advancement device. The dentist takes records, commissions the device from a dental laboratory, delivers it, and then advances it a fraction of a millimetre at a time over months of chairside visits until the patient stops snoring or stops tolerating the advancement. Somewhere near the end of that process the patient is re-tested with the device in the mouth, and the dentist owes the referring physician a written efficacy report — which the physician, and only the physician, may sign. Until that signature lands, the clinical loop is open, and an open loop is not a billable course of therapy.

Layered over the clinical story is a documentation regime that is entirely medical, not dental. Oral appliance therapy is durable medical equipment: it is paid by the patient's **medical** benefit, it requires a standard written order signed by the ordering physician and supported by a recent face-to-face encounter, it usually requires prior authorisation, it requires proof of delivery on file, and continued-use billing requires objective adherence data pulled off a microrecorder embedded in the device. Devices break, wear out, get lost, and occasionally get recalled by their manufacturer — each of which has its own paperwork, its own warranty arithmetic, and its own effect on what may still be billed. Payers audit. When a payer opens an audit on a patient, every clinical and billing write for that patient stops until the audit closes.

This document specifies the REST API behind that practice. It is written for the engineer who must implement it, and it assumes no prior knowledge of dental sleep medicine — every threshold, every deadline, every fee and every rounding rule is stated here in full. The API is mounted at the server root: every business collection is a top-level resource joined to its neighbours by foreign keys, and no resource is nested inside another in its URL. Section 2 fixes the system-wide contracts — envelope, pagination, identity, precision, and the strictly ordered validation ladder that every request walks. Section 3 tells the business story, workflow by workflow. Sections 4 and 5 are the endpoint and error catalogues.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Care Team Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the actor is writing. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below spelled exactly, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint (see the table in §4), the answer is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `SleepPhysician`, `Dentist`, `LabTechnician`, `BillingSpecialist`, `ClinicCoordinator` and `Patient`.

- `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform and doubles as the payer's adjudication desk and the manufacturer's safety office. It registers providers and payers, decides prior authorisations, adjudicates claims, registers manufacturing lots and issues recalls, opens and closes payer audits, and drives the logical clock.
- `SleepPhysician` is the only role permitted to order and result a sleep study, to refer a patient for oral appliance therapy, to write and sign a standard written order, and to sign off an efficacy report.
- `Dentist` is the treating dental sleep provider. The dentist responds to referrals, captures records, commissions and receives laboratory work, fabricates and delivers the device, titrates it, declares plateau, drafts and submits efficacy reports, opens and closes repairs, remediates device recalls and performs periodic recall checks.
- `LabTechnician` is the dental laboratory. Its single write capability is shipping the finished case.
- `BillingSpecialist` files prior authorisations and medical claims, submits them and appeals them, and never touches clinical state.
- `ClinicCoordinator` is front-desk staff: registers patients, captures proof of delivery, and enters adherence paperwork.
- `Patient` may attest to his own wear time and read his own records.

**Identity binding.** When `X-User-Role` is `SleepPhysician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's `ordering_physician_id` or `referring_physician_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Dentist`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Provider`, compared against `treating_dentist_id` or `performed_by_dentist_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `LabTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is again the `id` of the acting `Provider`, compared against `lab_provider_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`, and ownership checks compare the resource's `patient_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `BillingSpecialist` or `ClinicCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

**Care-team scope.** Beyond the endpoint-level role check, an actor must belong to the care team of the patient whose row is being touched. This test guards *reads as well as writes* on every patient-scoped endpoint, and a failure is `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM` (HTTP 403). A `Dentist` is in scope for a patient when some `Referral` names him as its `treating_dentist_id` in *any* status, `ISSUED` included — without that clause he could never answer the very referral that admits him to the care team. A `SleepPhysician` is in scope for a patient he has either ordered a `SleepStudy` for or referred. A `LabTechnician` is in scope only for `LabOrder` rows naming him as `lab_provider_id`. A `Patient` is in scope only for rows that resolve to his own `patient_id`. `ClinicCoordinator`, `BillingSpecialist` and `PlatformAdmin` are unscoped and never fail this test.

Scope has to start somewhere, and the bootstrap is deliberate: when a `SleepPhysician` orders a sleep study or writes a referral for a patient he has never touched, he establishes his own scope by naming himself in `ordering_physician_id` or `referring_physician_id` respectively. A physician who names a *different* provider in that slot on either of those two creates is refused here, with `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM`, before any provider-suitability rule is consulted.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Collections, Pagination and Filtering

Every collection endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters *before* the page window is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`.

**Every list endpoint sorts by `created_at` ascending.** Where two rows share the same `created_at` value, the row the server created first is returned first; insertion order is the tie-break, and it applies equally to the "newest row" selections described in §3 (for example, the newest objective adherence download on an appliance, or the newest signed written order for a patient and item), which read the same ordering in reverse.

Filters, when several are supplied, combine conjunctively: a row must match all of them. The filter sets are: patients by `medical_payer_id` and `dental_payer_id`; sleep studies by `patient_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `appliance_id`, `study_purpose` and `status`; referrals by `patient_id`, `referring_physician_id`, `treating_dentist_id`, `diagnostic_study_id` and `status`; written orders by `patient_id`, `ordering_physician_id` and `status`; prior authorisations by `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `referral_id` and `status`; lab orders by `referral_id`, `lab_provider_id` and `status`; appliances by `patient_id`, `lab_order_id`, `lot_id`, `replaces_appliance_id`, `written_order_id` and `lifecycle_status`; titration visits by `appliance_id`, `performed_by_dentist_id` and `status`; adherence downloads by `appliance_id` and `source`; efficacy reports by `patient_id`, `referral_id`, `appliance_id`, `efficacy_study_id` and `status`; claims by `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `appliance_id`, `prior_auth_id`, `written_order_id`, `repair_order_id` and `status`; and audit holds by `patient_id`, `payer_id` and `status`. Providers, payers and appliance lots have no collection endpoint — they are read one at a time by id.

A filter value that is not a member of the enumeration it filters on is **not** an error. Enum-membership enforcement is reserved for the request-body fields exhaustively listed in §2.8, and query-string filters are not among them. A list request filtering on an unrecognised status, purpose, source or lifecycle value therefore succeeds with an empty page: `data` is an empty array, `total_count` is 0 and `has_more` is false. The same holds for a syntactically valid identifier filter that matches no row.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Dates and the Logical Clock

Every persisted row carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused. Client-supplied ids are ignored. All rows also carry `created_at`. Every row except one additionally carries `updated_at` and `version`: `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by whole steps on mutation as described in §2.5, and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation. The single exception is `AdherenceDownload`, which is an immutable observation: an adherence download carries `id` and `created_at` only — it has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its serialized form.

**One uniform rendering rule governs every instant-valued field in every response.** `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp` and the clock's own `current_utc` are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Caller-supplied instants are **re-rendered canonically, never echoed verbatim**: a client that sets the clock to `2026-06-01T12:00:00Z` reads `2026-06-01T12:00:00.000Z` back, and a client that supplies additional precision has it truncated to three fractional digits in the response. `created_at` and `updated_at` are real server-time instants recording when the row was written; they are the ordering key for collections and for the newest-row selections of §3, and they are independent of the business clock described next.

Date-valued fields are a different type and are rendered differently: they are plain ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component, no `T`, and no zone suffix, and a caller-supplied calendar date comes back exactly as the same calendar date. The date-valued fields are `date_of_birth`, `performed_on`, `cpap_documented_on`, `diagnosis_valid_until`, `order_date`, `f2f_encounter_date`, `signed_on`, `submitted_on`, `decided_on`, `approval_valid_until`, `promised_ship_date`, `shipped_on`, `recall_issued_on`, `delivered_on`, `pod_signed_on`, `warranty_end_date`, `useful_lifetime_end_date`, `plateau_declared_on`, `next_recall_due_on`, `scheduled_for`, `applied_on`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `due_by`, `physician_decision_on`, `date_of_service`, `reported_on`, `returned_on`, `opened_on` and `closed_on`. Any of them supplied in a request body must parse as `YYYY-MM-DD` or the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

**The logical clock.** This API never reads wall-clock time for business purposes. It maintains a logical clock which, before the first time anyone sets it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` and which never falls back to wall-clock time thereafter. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Wherever this document says "the clock date", it means the UTC calendar date of the logical clock at the moment the request is processed. Every deadline, validity window, warranty boundary, titration interval and recall posture in this specification is evaluated against that clock, and every system-stamped business date — a submission date, an application date, a plateau date, a remediation date — is taken from it. Setting the clock also re-evaluates the patient-recall posture of every appliance in therapy, as §3.7 describes.

### 2.5. Serialized Representations and Version Semantics

A resource is always serialized in full. Every field the entity owns appears in its representation on every read and on every write that returns it, including computed fields that are still unset — an unset computed field is present with a JSON `null` value rather than omitted. Alongside the entity's own fields, each representation carries `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`, with the single exception noted in §2.4.

- A `Provider` exposes `npi`, `full_name`, `provider_type`, `state_license`, `dme_supplier_number`, `status` and `open_efficacy_loop_count`.
- A `Payer` exposes `name`, `payer_kind`, `prior_auth_required`, `useful_lifetime_months`, `coinsurance_pct`, `appeal_limit`, `residual_ahi_target` and `pct_reduction_target`.
- A `Patient` exposes `mrn`, `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, `medical_payer_id`, `dental_payer_id`, `deductible_remaining_cents`, `epworth_score` and `records_frozen`.
- A `SleepStudy` exposes `patient_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `appliance_id`, `modality`, `study_purpose`, `performed_on`, `respiratory_events`, `recording_minutes`, `sleep_minutes`, `artifact_pct`, `attended`, `severity_index`, `severity_band`, `repeat_count` and `status`.
- A `Referral` exposes `patient_id`, `referring_physician_id`, `treating_dentist_id`, `diagnostic_study_id`, `cpap_history`, `cpap_nights_used`, `cpap_documented_on`, `severity_band_at_referral`, `eligibility_basis`, `diagnosis_valid_until`, `loop_status` and `status`.
- A `WrittenOrder` exposes `patient_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `item_hcpcs`, `order_date`, `f2f_encounter_date`, `signed_on`, `f2f_age_days` and `status`.
- A `PriorAuth` exposes `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `referral_id`, `requested_hcpcs`, `submitted_on`, `decided_on`, `approval_valid_until`, `denial_reason`, `appeal_attempts` and `status`.
- A `LabOrder` exposes `referral_id`, `lab_provider_id`, `appliance_kind`, `capture_method`, `max_protrusive_mm`, `retruded_position_mm`, `promised_turnaround_days`, `submitted_on`, `promised_ship_date`, `shipped_on`, `turnaround_on_time`, `remake_count` and `status`.
- An `ApplianceLot` exposes `manufacturer`, `lot_code`, `material`, `notice_reference`, `recall_severity`, `recall_issued_on`, `affected_device_count`, `remediated_count`, `pending_remediation_count`, `retired_count` and `status`.
- An `Appliance` exposes `patient_id`, `lab_order_id`, `lot_id`, `replaces_appliance_id`, `serial_number`, `has_microrecorder`, `appliance_kind`, `hcpcs_code`, `max_protrusive_mm`, `delivered_on`, `written_order_id`, `pod_signed_on`, `pod_method`, `warranty_end_date`, `useful_lifetime_end_date`, `starting_advancement_mm`, `current_advancement_mm`, `applied_visit_count`, `plateau_declared_on`, `next_recall_due_on`, `recall_cycle_index`, and all three of its state axes — `lifecycle_status`, `device_hold_status` and `recall_status`.
- A `TitrationVisit` exposes `appliance_id`, `performed_by_dentist_id`, `visit_seq`, `scheduled_for`, `requested_increment_mm`, `side_effect_severity`, `applied_increment_mm`, `applied_on`, `days_since_prior_applied` and `status`.
- An `AdherenceDownload` exposes `appliance_id`, `source`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `nights_recorded`, `nights_at_or_above_min_hours`, `mean_hours_per_worn_night`, `rate_of_window`, `rate_of_recorded` and `compliant` — plus `id` and `created_at`, and nothing else.
- An `EfficacyReport` exposes `patient_id`, `referral_id`, `appliance_id`, `efficacy_study_id`, `baseline_index`, `residual_index`, `pct_reduction`, `meets_absolute_target`, `meets_relative_target`, `outcome`, `due_by`, `submitted_on`, `physician_decision_on`, `dispute_reason`, `revision_count` and `status`.
- A `Claim` exposes `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `appliance_id`, `prior_auth_id`, `written_order_id`, `repair_order_id`, `claim_type`, `hcpcs_code`, `date_of_service`, `billed_cents`, `allowed_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, `payer_paid_cents`, `adjustment_cents`, `denial_error_id`, `appeal_attempts` and `status`.
- A `RepairOrder` exposes `appliance_id`, `loaner_appliance_id`, `reason`, `reported_on`, `labor_minutes`, `returned_on`, `in_warranty`, `billable`, `labor_units`, `repair_charge_cents`, `resets_useful_lifetime` and `status`.
- An `AuditHold` exposes `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `opened_on`, `closed_on`, `scope_note` and `status`.

Two endpoints return something other than an entity. Setting the logical clock returns an object whose only field is `current_utc`. The adherence summary of an appliance returns an object carrying `appliance_id`, `objective_rate_of_window`, `objective_rate_of_recorded`, `objective_compliant`, `attested_rate_of_window`, `attested_compliant`, `window_start` and `window_end`; §3.7 says which download each figure is taken from.

**Version-bump granularity is one increment per row per request.** A request that mutates a row raises that row's `version` by exactly 1, no matter how many of its fields changed, how many sub-objects of the same row were touched, or how many separate rules inside the request wrote to it. When a request cascades into other rows, each *distinct row* that is actually written is likewise raised by exactly 1 — never once per affected field and never once per cascade level. A row that a request merely reads, or that a rule considers and leaves unchanged, is not bumped at all and its `updated_at` is not refreshed.

The representation returned by a mutating request is projected **after** every write of that request has been committed. The `version` and `updated_at` in a create or action response are therefore the post-write values: a create returns `version` 1, and an action that bumps a row from `version` 4 returns `version` 5, never the value the row carried when the handler began.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder

Every request walks a single, strictly ordered ladder of 35 rungs. **The rungs are evaluated in ascending order and evaluation stops at the first rung that fails**; that rung's error identifier and HTTP status are the response, and no later rung is consulted. A request that would fail several rungs therefore reports only the lowest-numbered one, and no test of a later rung's behaviour is meaningful on a request that already fails an earlier one. The rungs, in order:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-roles list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every id embedded in the URL resolves to a live row, validated left to right, so that in a multi-segment path the leftmost unresolvable id is the one reported. A `patient_id` gives `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `provider_id` gives `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`; a `referral_id` gives `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`; an `appliance_id` gives `ERR_APPLIANCE_NOT_FOUND`; and every other path id — `payer_id`, `study_id`, `written_order_id`, `prior_auth_id`, `lab_order_id`, `lot_id`, `visit_id`, `adherence_download_id`, `report_id`, `claim_id`, `repair_order_id` and `audit_hold_id` — gives `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. All five are HTTP 404, and no other id routes to `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Body-reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, validated in the order the fields are declared in the endpoint's input list, so the first unresolvable reference in that order is the one reported. `patient_id` gives `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`; `provider_id`, `referring_physician_id`, `treating_dentist_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `lab_provider_id` and `performed_by_dentist_id` give `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`; `referral_id` gives `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`; `appliance_id`, `replaces_appliance_id` and `loaner_appliance_id` give `ERR_APPLIANCE_NOT_FOUND`; and `payer_id`, `diagnostic_study_id`, `efficacy_study_id`, `written_order_id`, `prior_auth_id`, `lab_order_id`, `lot_id` and `repair_order_id` give `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. All HTTP 404.
5. **The global write gate — payer audit hold.** When the patient in scope has `records_frozen` true, the request is refused with `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` (409). See the gate paragraph below.
6. **Care-team scope.** As specified in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM` (403). Applies to every patient-scoped endpoint, reads included.
7. **Required fields and JSON types.** Every field marked required in the endpoint's input list is present and carries the declared JSON type — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
8. **Enum membership.** The exhaustively listed enum-bearing fields of §2.8 carry a declared member — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422).
9. **Numeric and calendar domains.** `max_protrusive_mm` lies between 4.00 and 12.00 inclusive and is a multiple of 0.25; `requested_increment_mm` lies between 0.25 and 2.00 inclusive and is a multiple of 0.25; `epworth_score` lies between 0 and 24 inclusive; `artifact_pct` lies between 0 and 100 inclusive; `sleep_minutes` does not exceed `recording_minutes`; `billed_cents` is at least 0; `labor_minutes` is at least 0; `coinsurance_pct` lies between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive; `nights_at_or_above_min_hours` does not exceed `nights_recorded`, which in turn does not exceed 30 nights; the adherence window spans exactly 30 inclusive nights; `f2f_encounter_date` is not later than `order_date`; `deductible_remaining_cents` is at least 0; `respiratory_events`, `recording_minutes` and `sleep_minutes` are each at least 0; `mean_hours_per_worn_night` lies between 0 and 24 inclusive; `promised_turnaround_days` lies between 1 and 60 inclusive; and every date field parses as `YYYY-MM-DD`. A violation is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
10. **Unique clinical identifiers.** `npi`, `mrn`, `lot_code` and `serial_number` do not collide — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (409). See §2.9.
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_appliance_version`, `expected_patient_version` or `expected_referral_version` matches the current `version` of the parent named in §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to the creation of titration visits, written orders and efficacy reports.
12. **State preconditions.** First the path resource's own `status` must admit the transition being requested; then the governing parent's status must admit it. A titration apply requires the appliance's `lifecycle_status` to be `DELIVERED` or `IN_TITRATION`; a plateau declaration requires `IN_TITRATION`; an efficacy-report submission requires `TITRATION_PLATEAU`; a physician response requires the report to be `SUBMITTED`; a lab-order creation, a lab-order submission and a prior-authorisation creation each require the referral to be `ACCEPTED`; an appliance creation requires the lab order to be `RECEIVED_ACCEPTED`; a proof-of-delivery capture requires the appliance to be `DELIVERED`; a recall check requires `ACTIVE_THERAPY`; a repair-order creation requires the appliance to be past `DELIVERED` and neither `RETIRED` nor `REPLACED`; and a claim submission requires the claim to be `DRAFT`. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
13. **The device gate — lot recall hold.** When the appliance in scope carries `device_hold_status` `RECALL_HOLD`, the request is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_RECALL_HOLD` (409). See the gate paragraph below.
14. **Segregation of duties.** On referral creation, `treating_dentist_id` must differ from `referring_physician_id`. On a physician response to an efficacy report, the acting `X-User-ID` must be the referral's `referring_physician_id`; if it is instead the referral's `treating_dentist_id` — exactly what happens when a treating dentist presents the `SleepPhysician` role header to sign off his own titration — the answer is `ERR_SELF_REFERRAL` (422), not `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM`, because the treating dentist genuinely passes the rung-6 care-team test. Any *other* provider id fails rung 6 first with `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM`.
15. **Provider slot suitability.** `referring_physician_id` and `ordering_physician_id` must name a provider whose `provider_type` is `SLEEP_PHYSICIAN`; `treating_dentist_id` and `performed_by_dentist_id` must name a `DENTIST`; `lab_provider_id` must name a `DENTAL_LAB`. In all five slots the named provider's `status` must also be `ACTIVE` — a `SUSPENDED` provider is refused with this same identifier. A violation is `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to referral creation, sleep-study creation, written-order creation, lab-order creation and titration-visit creation.
16. **Study validity.** A referenced `SleepStudy` must be in status `DIAGNOSTIC_VALID` — an `ORDERED` study that was never resulted, or a `NON_DIAGNOSTIC` one that failed its modality quality gate, may neither ground a referral nor ground an efficacy report. A violation is `ERR_STUDY_NON_DIAGNOSTIC` (422). Applies to referral creation and efficacy-report creation.
17. **Diagnosis present.** The study grounding a referral must carry an actual diagnosis: its `study_purpose` is `DIAGNOSTIC` and its `severity_band` is `MILD`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`. A violation is `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to referral creation.
18. **Diagnosis currency — mutate then reject.** When the clock date is later than the referral's `diagnosis_valid_until`, the referral is expired and the request refused with `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_EXPIRED` (422). See the mutate-then-reject paragraph below. Applies to a dentist's referral response, to lab-order creation and to prior-authorisation creation.
19. **Medical necessity.** The severity-by-CPAP-history fork of §3.3 admits an eligibility basis — otherwise `ERR_CPAP_TRIAL_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to referral creation.
20. **One live device per patient.** Both appliance creation and appliance delivery are refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_APPLIANCE_EXISTS` (409) when the patient already holds a different live device, as §3.5 details.
21. **Titration interval.** At least 14 whole days separate this application from the appliance's most recent applied or reduced visit — otherwise `ERR_TITRATION_INTERVAL` (422). Evaluated only when `side_effect_severity` is `NONE` or `MILD`.
22. **Protrusive ceiling.** The bounded increment must not carry `current_advancement_mm` past the appliance's `max_protrusive_mm` — otherwise `ERR_PROTRUSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Evaluated only when `side_effect_severity` is `NONE` or `MILD`.
23. **Plateau prerequisites.** A plateau declaration requires at least 3 applied visits, and an efficacy-report creation requires the appliance to be in `TITRATION_PLATEAU` or `EFFICACY_PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_PLATEAU_REQUIRED` (422).
24. **Cross-provider loop closure.** A claim of `claim_type` `CONTINUED_USE` may be submitted only when the appliance's referral has `loop_status` `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_EFFICACY_LOOP_OPEN` (409). Applies to claim submission for that claim type only.
25. **Medical-benefit routing.** `payer_id` must equal the patient's `medical_payer_id` and that payer's `payer_kind` must be `MEDICAL` — otherwise `ERR_DENTAL_PAYER_ROUTING` (422). Applies to claim creation, claim submission and prior-authorisation creation.
26. **Standard written order present.** A `SIGNED` `WrittenOrder` exists for this patient whose `item_hcpcs` equals the appliance's `hcpcs_code` at delivery, or the claim's `hcpcs_code` at claim submission — otherwise `ERR_SWO_MISSING` (422). Applies to appliance delivery, and to claim submission for `claim_type` `INITIAL_DEVICE` or `REPLACEMENT`.
27. **Order predates delivery.** That order's `order_date` is not later than the appliance's `delivered_on` — otherwise `ERR_SWO_AFTER_DELIVERY` (422). Same two endpoints and same claim types as rung 26.
28. **Face-to-face currency.** That order's `f2f_age_days` is at most 180 — otherwise `ERR_F2F_ENCOUNTER_STALE` (422). Same two endpoints and same claim types as rung 26. Rungs 26, 27 and 28 fire in that order and never collapse into one another.
29. **Prior authorisation — mutate then reject.** When the payer requires it, an `APPROVED` and unexpired `PriorAuth` must back the claim — otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED` (422). See the mutate-then-reject paragraph below. Applies to claim submission.
30. **Proof of delivery.** `pod_signed_on` is not null and `pod_method` is set — otherwise `ERR_POD_MISSING` (422). Applies to claim submission for `claim_type` `INITIAL_DEVICE`, `REPLACEMENT` or `CONTINUED_USE`.
31. **Useful lifetime elapsed.** A payer-funded replacement is admissible only when `date_of_service` falls strictly after the predecessor appliance's `useful_lifetime_end_date` — otherwise `ERR_USEFUL_LIFETIME_NOT_ELAPSED` (422). Applies to claim submission for `claim_type` `REPLACEMENT`.
32. **Repair billability.** A repair claim requires the linked repair order's `billable` to be true — otherwise `ERR_REPAIR_NOT_BILLABLE` (422). Applies to claim submission for `claim_type` `REPAIR`.
33. **Objective adherence and live recall.** A continued-use claim requires the newest objective adherence download on the appliance to be compliant and requires `recall_status` not to be `LAPSED` — otherwise `ERR_ADHERENCE_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to claim submission for `claim_type` `CONTINUED_USE`.
34. **Loaner availability.** The requested `loaner_appliance_id` is not occupied by an overlapping repair interval — otherwise `ERR_LOANER_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to repair-order creation.
35. **Appeal cap.** `appeal_attempts` on the prior authorisation, or on the claim, is strictly below the payer's `appeal_limit` before a further appeal is accepted — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to prior-authorisation appeal and claim appeal.

**Rung 5 is a global write gate.** When the patient in scope has at least one `AuditHold` in status `OPEN` — which is what `records_frozen` records — every mutating endpoint whose subject resolves to that patient is refused at rung 5, **pre-empting rungs 6 through 35 entirely**. No care-team check, no field validation, no state check and no business rule runs; the only possible answer is `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` (409). The patient in scope is resolved through the row being written: directly for `Patient`, `Referral`, `SleepStudy`, `WrittenOrder`, `PriorAuth`, `EfficacyReport` and `Claim`; through its referral for a `LabOrder`; through its appliance for a `TitrationVisit`, an `AdherenceDownload` and a `RepairOrder`; and through its own `patient_id` for an `Appliance`. Exactly seven mutations are exempt and remain available while a patient is frozen: opening an audit hold, closing an audit hold, registering an appliance lot, issuing a lot recall, registering a provider, registering a payer, and setting the logical clock. Every `GET` endpoint is exempt.

**Rung 13 is a second, narrower gate over the device-facing block.** When the appliance in scope carries `device_hold_status` `RECALL_HOLD`, every titration, plateau, delivery, proof-of-delivery, efficacy-report, repair-order, recall-check and claim write that touches it is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_RECALL_HOLD` (409), **pre-empting rungs 14 through 35**. The only writes that remain open on a held device are the remediation that clears the hold, and the read endpoints. One pre-emption in the other direction is designed in: on a titration *apply*, rung 13 is unreachable, because the recall cascade has already moved every `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` visit on that appliance to `VOIDED_BY_HOLD` and rung 12 therefore rejects first with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The rung is reached instead through titration-visit creation, appliance delivery, repair-order creation and claim submission, whose subjects the cascade does not void.

**The two mutate-then-reject rungs.** Rung 18 and rung 29 are the only rungs in this API that commit a write and *then* raise an error. The write is not rolled back: the caller receives a 4xx response and the row named in the rung has genuinely changed state.

At rung 18, when the clock date is later than the referral's `diagnosis_valid_until` and the referral is in status `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`, three things happen in order. First the write: the referral's `status` is persisted as `EXPIRED` and its `version` rises by 1, and — on the `ACCEPTED` arm — every `LabOrder` on that referral still in `RECORDS_CAPTURED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. Then the rejection: `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_EXPIRED` (422). And third, on any *later* call against a referral that is **already** `EXPIRED`, this rung is never reached at all: rung 12's state gate rejects first — a dentist's response requires an `ISSUED` referral, and lab-order and prior-authorisation creation each require an `ACCEPTED` one — so the answer becomes `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), no second write occurs, and the referral's `version` is not bumped a second time.

At rung 29 the third clause resolves the other way, and the difference matters. When the payer's `prior_auth_required` is true and the claim's linked authorisation is missing or in any status other than `APPROVED`, the claim submission is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED` (422) and **no write occurs**. When the authorisation is `APPROVED` but the clock date has passed its `approval_valid_until`, the write happens first — the authorisation's `status` is persisted as `EXPIRED` and its `version` rises by 1 — and then the same `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED` (422) is raised. On a later submission against an authorisation that is **already** `EXPIRED`, nothing pre-empts this rung, because the authorisation is not the path resource of a claim submission and rung 12 there examines only the claim's own `DRAFT` status; rung 29 therefore fires again, raises the same `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED` (422), and this time performs **no** write and does **not** bump the authorisation's `version`.

Three orderings in the middle of the ladder are deliberate and must not be rearranged. Rung 14 sits above rung 15 because no single provider can be both a `SLEEP_PHYSICIAN` and a `DENTIST`: were suitability evaluated first, a referral naming the same provider in both slots would always answer `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH` and `ERR_SELF_REFERRAL` would be unreachable on referral creation. Rung 16 sits above rung 17 because a `NON_DIAGNOSTIC` study also has a null `severity_band`: were the band checked first, `ERR_STUDY_NON_DIAGNOSTIC` would be unreachable. And rung 24 is deliberately *not* applied to the recall check, because that endpoint's rung-12 precondition already demands `ACTIVE_THERAPY`, a state reachable only through loop closure — a loop-open clause there would be permanently masked.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three creates in this API legitimately supersede a row somebody else may be holding open, and all three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency against a named parent. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional** input: omitting it is a rung-7 `ERR_VALIDATION` (400), and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a rung-11 rejection with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field.

- **Creating a `TitrationVisit`** requires `expected_appliance_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Appliance`. If the appliance's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). The guard exists because visit creation supersedes any visit still `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` on that appliance: a writer holding a stale view of the appliance would silently discard a colleague's open visit.
- **Creating a `WrittenOrder`** requires `expected_patient_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Patient`. A mismatch with the patient's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). A new standard written order for the same `item_hcpcs` supersedes the prior `DRAFT` or `SIGNED` order, so a stale writer would invalidate an order that was just issued from a newer clinical picture.
- **Creating an `EfficacyReport`** requires `expected_referral_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Referral`. A mismatch with the referral's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). A new report supersedes a `DISPUTED` report on the same referral, so a stale writer would replace a revision the physician has already acted on.

Note the feedback loop this creates. Each of these three creates itself bumps the parent it is guarded by — visit creation raises `Appliance.version`, written-order creation raises `Patient.version`, report creation raises `Referral.version` — so a client that creates two rows in a row must re-read the parent between them, and any *other* write that touches the parent invalidates a version the client is holding.

### 2.8. Enumerations and Their Error Routing

Exactly the following request-body fields route an unrecognised value to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 422), and **no other field in this API routes there**:

`provider_type` (`SLEEP_PHYSICIAN`, `DENTIST`, `DENTAL_LAB`); a provider's `status` at registration (`ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`); `payer_kind` (`MEDICAL`, `DENTAL`); `modality` (`HSAT`, `PSG`); `study_purpose` (`DIAGNOSTIC`, `EFFICACY`); `cpap_history` (`NONE`, `FAILURE`, `INTOLERANCE`, `REFUSAL`); `item_hcpcs` (`E0486`, `A9270`, `K0739`); `requested_hcpcs` (`E0486`, `A9270`, `K0739`); `denial_reason` (`NOT_MEDICALLY_NECESSARY`, `MISSING_DOCUMENTATION`, `NON_COVERED_ITEM`); `appliance_kind` (`CUSTOM_FABRICATED`, `PREFABRICATED`); `capture_method` (`PVS_IMPRESSION`, `DIGITAL_SCAN`); `recall_severity` (`CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II`, `CLASS_III`); `pod_method` (`IN_PERSON`, `SHIPPED`); `side_effect_severity` (`NONE`, `MILD`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE`); `source` (`OBJECTIVE_MICRORECORDER`, `PATIENT_ATTESTED`); `claim_type` (`INITIAL_DEVICE`, `REPAIR`, `REPLACEMENT`, `CONTINUED_USE`); a claim's `hcpcs_code` (`E0486`, `A9270`, `K0739`); and `reason` on a repair order (`DEFECT`, `WEAR`, `ABUSE`, `LOSS`).

Six action verbs carry their own body enums, and they route here too: the `decision` a dentist gives when answering a referral (`ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`); the `fit_outcome` a dentist gives when receiving a lab case (`ACCEPTED`, `REMAKE_REQUIRED`); the `decision` recorded on a prior authorisation (`APPROVED`, `DENIED`); the `outcome` recorded when adjudicating a claim (`PAID`, `DENIED`); the `decision` a physician gives on an efficacy report (`SIGNED`, `DISPUTED`); and the `outcome` given when completing a repair order (`REPAIRED`, `NOT_COVERED`).

Any *other* malformed input is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) at rung 7 or rung 9 and never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`: a wrong JSON type, a number outside its declared domain, a malformed date, an unknown field, and every free-text field — including `remediation_action`, `narrative`, `dispute_reason`, `scope_note`, `material`, `notes`, `reason` supplied alongside a referral decision, `state_license`, `dme_supplier_number`, `manufacturer`, `notice_reference`, `lot_code`, `serial_number`, `full_name`, `name`, `mrn`, `npi` and `denial_error_id`. Computed and system-owned enumerations — every `status`, plus `severity_band`, `severity_band_at_referral`, `eligibility_basis`, `loop_status`, `outcome` on an efficacy report, `lifecycle_status`, `device_hold_status` and `recall_status` — are never accepted from a client at all and so never reach rung 8.

### 2.9. Unique Clinical Identifiers

Four identifiers are unique, each within its own entity, and a collision on any of them is `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (HTTP 409): a provider's `npi`, a patient's `mrn`, an appliance lot's `lot_code` and an appliance's `serial_number`. Comparison is exact string equality and is case-sensitive, so `LOT-88a` and `LOT-88A` are distinct lot codes. An `npi` must additionally be exactly ten digits, which is a rung-9 domain check answering `ERR_VALIDATION`, not a uniqueness failure. No other field in this API is unique, and no other field routes to `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

### 2.10. Arithmetic Precision, Rounding and the Three Conservation Invariants

Every rounding in this specification is **round half up**: at the stated precision, a discarded remainder of exactly one half raises the retained digit. Precision differs by quantity and is stated wherever a formula appears, but the schedule is: an apnoea-hypopnoea index (`severity_index`, `baseline_index`, `residual_index`) is carried to 1 decimal place; `pct_reduction`, `rate_of_window` and `rate_of_recorded` are carried to 4 decimal places; every millimetre quantity (`max_protrusive_mm`, `starting_advancement_mm`, `current_advancement_mm`, `requested_increment_mm`, `applied_increment_mm`, `retruded_position_mm`) is carried to 2 decimal places and every *stored* advancement is an exact multiple of 0.25 mm; every money field is a whole number of cents; and every count, day span and month span is a whole number. Date arithmetic in whole days is plain calendar addition. Date arithmetic in whole months preserves the day of the month and clamps to the last day of a shorter target month — twelve months after 2028-02-29 is 2029-02-28.

Three conservation invariants hold at every observable instant, and no operation in this API may break them.

**Conservation 1 — protrusive range.** An appliance's `current_advancement_mm` always equals its `starting_advancement_mm` plus the sum of `applied_increment_mm` over exactly those `TitrationVisit` rows on that appliance whose `status` is `APPLIED` or `REDUCED`; and it always lies between 0.00 and the appliance's `max_protrusive_mm` inclusive. Visits in `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED_BY_HOLD` contribute exactly 0.00. A visit that is held and later applied must leave the sum untouched by its held leg alone.

**Conservation 2 — money.** For every `Claim` in status `PAID`, `allowed_cents` equals `payer_paid_cents` plus `patient_responsibility_cents`, and `billed_cents` equals `allowed_cents` plus `adjustment_cents`. A claim in `DENIED` or `DENIED_FINAL` leaves `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents` and `payer_paid_cents` all null and moves no deductible. An appeal that is later paid applies the deductible exactly once, against the balance observed at the paying adjudication.

**Conservation 3 — recalled device population.** From the instant a recall is issued and forever afterwards, a lot's `affected_device_count` equals its `remediated_count` plus its `pending_remediation_count`, and the `retired_count` bucket is disjoint from all three. The recalled population is frozen at issuance: retiring a device *after* the recall was issued does not move it out of `pending_remediation_count`, so that device must still be remediated before the lot can close.

### 2.11. Cascades and Cross-Entity Consistency

Several operations in this API reach well beyond the row they name. Wherever a cascade writes to a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` rises by exactly 1 — once for the whole request, as §2.5 requires — and the cascade is atomic with the operation that triggered it: either every level lands or the request fails and none does. Three cascades matter, and each is specified in full in §3.

The deepest is the lot recall, five levels deep: the lot's own posture changes, every non-retired device in the lot goes on safety hold, every open titration visit on those devices is voided, a device left with no open visit while under titration is suspended, every open efficacy report on those devices is withdrawn and its referral's loop re-opens, and every draft continued-use claim on those devices is voided. The physician's signature on an efficacy report is three levels deep: the referral closes, the appliance enters active therapy, and the patient-recall cadence starts. A repair opened for a lost device is three levels deep: the repair closes itself as not covered, the appliance retires, and every draft continued-use claim on it is voided.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Cast: Providers, Payers, Patients, and the Payer's Audit

Nothing clinical can happen until the platform knows who is involved. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each participant as a `Provider` carrying an `npi` — the National Provider Identifier, exactly ten digits and unique across all providers — together with a `full_name` and a `provider_type` of `SLEEP_PHYSICIAN`, `DENTIST` or `DENTAL_LAB`. Two optional attributes round out the record: `state_license`, which defaults to the literal string `UNVERIFIED` when the registrant does not supply one, and `dme_supplier_number`, the Medicare durable-medical-equipment supplier number, which defaults to null and which only a provider of type `DENTIST` is expected to hold. A provider's participation `status` is settable at registration and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the alternative is `SUSPENDED`. A suspended provider may not be named in any of the five provider slots this API recognises — `referring_physician_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `treating_dentist_id`, `lab_provider_id` and `performed_by_dentist_id` — on any new row, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`, the same identifier that a type mismatch carries. (Where the provider record itself describes this restriction it names only four of the slots, omitting `ordering_physician_id`; the ladder rung that enforces it covers all five, and the ladder is the enforcement path.)

Each provider also carries a computed workload figure, `open_efficacy_loop_count`, initialised to 0 at registration. It counts the `EfficacyReport` rows whose referring physician is this provider and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` — the reports the physician still owes an answer on, or has bounced back and is waiting to see again — and it is recomputed on every transition of any efficacy report naming that physician.

Payers are registered with equal ceremony, and a payer is far more than a name: it is a small policy document. `name` and `payer_kind` are required, the latter being `MEDICAL` or `DENTAL`. Everything else has a default which the registrant may override: `prior_auth_required` defaults to true and decides whether an approved authorisation must back a device claim; `useful_lifetime_months` defaults to 60 whole months and is the reasonable useful lifetime of a covered oral appliance, counted from the day the device was delivered; `coinsurance_pct` defaults to 0.20 and is the member's share of the allowed amount after the deductible, expressed as a fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive; `appeal_limit` defaults to 2 and caps appeals on an authorisation and, separately and independently, on a claim; `residual_ahi_target` defaults to 5.0 and is the absolute residual-index success target; and `pct_reduction_target` defaults to 0.50 and is the proportional-reduction success target. Oral appliance therapy is a medical benefit, and a payer whose `payer_kind` is `DENTAL` may never be the payer of a claim.

A `ClinicCoordinator` or a `PlatformAdmin` registers the patient. A `Patient` carries an `mrn` — the medical record number, unique across all patients — plus `full_name` and `date_of_birth`, and binds two benefits: `medical_payer_id`, the medical benefit that adjudicates oral appliance claims, and the optional `dental_payer_id`, recorded for completeness and defaulting to null. The medical benefit is expected to name a payer whose `payer_kind` is `MEDICAL`; no ladder rung enforces that at registration, because the medical-benefit routing rung guards claims and prior authorisations rather than patient registration, so a patient bound to the wrong kind of payer is discovered later, when the first authorisation or claim is refused with `ERR_DENTAL_PAYER_ROUTING`. Two further inputs shape the money and the clinical fork downstream: `deductible_remaining_cents`, the unmet medical deductible in cents, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `epworth_score`, the Epworth Sleepiness Scale score captured at intake, defaulting to 0 and confined to the range 0 through 24 inclusive. Finally, every patient carries `records_frozen`, computed and initialised to false.

That last flag is the switch behind the global write gate. When a payer decides to audit a patient's records, a `PlatformAdmin` opens an `AuditHold` naming the `patient_id` under audit, the `payer_id` conducting it, and an optional free-text `scope_note` describing what is being examined. The hold's `opened_on` is stamped from the clock date and its `status` starts `OPEN`; `closed_on` stays null until the audit ends. Creating the hold recomputes the patient's `records_frozen` to true — which is itself a write to the patient row, so the patient's `version` rises by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes — and from that instant every mutating endpoint whose subject resolves to that patient answers `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` and nothing else. Closing the audit moves the hold `OPEN → CLOSED`, stamps `closed_on` from the clock date, and recomputes `records_frozen` back to false **only when no other hold on that patient remains `OPEN`** — two overlapping audits require two closures before the patient thaws. Opening and closing an audit are themselves exempt from the freeze, as are lot registration, recall issuance, provider registration, payer registration and setting the clock; every other mutation waits.

### 3.2. Diagnosing Obstructive Sleep Apnoea

A `SleepPhysician` opens the clinical record by ordering a study. The order names the `patient_id`, the `ordering_physician_id` — which must be the acting physician himself, and must be an `ACTIVE` provider of type `SLEEP_PHYSICIAN` — a `modality` of `HSAT` (an unattended home sleep apnoea test) or `PSG` (a polysomnogram), and a `study_purpose` of `DIAGNOSTIC` or `EFFICACY`. An efficacy study is a re-test performed with a device in the mouth, so it must name the `appliance_id` it is testing; a diagnostic study must not name one, and supplying `appliance_id` on a diagnostic order, or omitting it on an efficacy order, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new study begins in status `ORDERED` with `repeat_count` 0 and every recording field null.

When the recording is scored, the ordering physician — and only the ordering physician; another provider is outside care-team scope and gets `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM` — posts the results: `performed_on`, the calendar date the recording was made; `respiratory_events`, the count of scored apnoeas plus hypopnoeas over the whole recording; `recording_minutes`, the total monitored minutes; and optionally `sleep_minutes`, the scored total sleep time; `artifact_pct`, the percentage of the recording rejected as artifact, defaulting to 0; and `attended`, whether a technologist was present, defaulting to false.

**The severity index is computed from a denominator that depends entirely on the modality.** For an `HSAT`, the denominator is `recording_minutes` divided by 60 — a home test cannot score sleep, so every monitored minute counts, and `sleep_minutes` is ignored even when it is supplied. For a `PSG`, the denominator is `sleep_minutes` divided by 60, because an attended study scores true sleep time. In both branches `severity_index` is `respiratory_events` divided by that denominator in hours, rounded half up to 1 decimal place, and a denominator of zero or less is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Because scored sleep time is always the smaller number, the PSG arm produces the higher index from the same event count, and can land the same patient in a stricter severity band. Consider 90 respiratory events. Scored as an `HSAT` over 480 recording minutes, the denominator is 8.0 hours and the index is 90 divided by 8.0, which is 11.25 and rounds half up to **11.3**. Scored as a `PSG` with 360 sleep minutes, the denominator is 6.0 hours and the index is exactly **15.0** — the identical event count, one band higher.

**Whether the recording is diagnostic at all is a second, independent modality fork.** An `HSAT` is diagnostic when `recording_minutes` is at least 240 and `artifact_pct` is at most 20; attendance is irrelevant to it. A `PSG` is diagnostic when `sleep_minutes` is at least 180 and `attended` is true; artifact is irrelevant to it, so a polysomnogram with an `artifact_pct` of 90 is still diagnostic if it scored enough sleep with a technologist present. Both boundaries are inclusive: exactly 240 recording minutes passes, 239 does not; exactly 180 sleep minutes passes, 179 does not; exactly 20 percent artifact passes, 20.1 does not.

The two branches of the outcome deserve equal attention. On the passing branch the study moves `ORDERED → DIAGNOSTIC_VALID`, `severity_index` is stored, and `severity_band` is derived from it: at least 30.0 is `SEVERE`, at least 15.0 but below 30.0 is `MODERATE`, at least 5.0 but below 15.0 is `MILD`, and anything below 5.0 is `NONE`. Every band boundary is inclusive on its lower edge. On the failing branch the study moves `ORDERED → NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, and `severity_index` is **still computed and stored** — the arithmetic is not conditional on quality — but `severity_band` is set to null, and a study in this state may never ground a referral or an efficacy report.

A non-diagnostic recording does not require a new study row. The physician re-orders on the same row, which moves it `NON_DIAGNOSTIC → ORDERED`, raises `repeat_count` by 1, and clears `performed_on`, `respiratory_events`, `recording_minutes`, `sleep_minutes`, `artifact_pct`, `attended`, `severity_index` and `severity_band` all back to null. The re-order optionally supplies `modality`, which is how a failed home test is escalated to an attended polysomnogram without losing its identity. `DIAGNOSTIC_VALID` is terminal; there is no path back out of it.

### 3.3. The Referral, Its Currency, and the Medical-Necessity Fork

With a diagnosis in hand, the `SleepPhysician` refers the patient to a dentist. The referral names the `patient_id`, the `referring_physician_id` (himself, `ACTIVE`, of type `SLEEP_PHYSICIAN`), the `treating_dentist_id` (`ACTIVE`, of type `DENTIST`, and never the same provider as the referring physician), and the `diagnostic_study_id` carrying the diagnosis. Three optional fields document the patient's history with positive airway pressure: `cpap_history`, one of `NONE`, `FAILURE`, `INTOLERANCE` or `REFUSAL` and defaulting to `NONE`; `cpap_nights_used`, the nights of documented use during the trial, defaulting to 0 and never negative, and meaningful only when the history is `FAILURE` or `INTOLERANCE`; and `cpap_documented_on`, the calendar date the failure, intolerance or refusal was documented.

Creation snapshots `severity_band_at_referral` from the grounding study — a later re-read of that study can never change what the referral was admitted on — and initialises `loop_status` to `OPEN` and `status` to `ISSUED`.

**Diagnosis currency depends on modality, and the two windows are far apart.** A referral grounded on an `HSAT` carries a `diagnosis_valid_until` of the study's `performed_on` plus 365 days; grounded on a `PSG`, it is `performed_on` plus 1095 days. The boundary date itself is still current — the referral expires only when the clock date is *later* than `diagnosis_valid_until`. A home test performed on 2025-06-01 therefore stays current through 2026-06-01 and expires on 2026-06-02, while a polysomnogram performed on the very same day stays current through 2028-05-31 (the third year of that span contains the 2028 leap day) — the identical referral is current under one modality and long expired under the other.

**Medical necessity forks on the severity band, and the arms are not symmetrical.** A band of `NONE`, or a null band, means there is nothing to treat and the request is refused with `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_REQUIRED`. For a `SEVERE` band, oral appliance therapy is second-line: positive airway pressure must have been tried and failed, so all three of the following must hold — `cpap_history` is `FAILURE`, `INTOLERANCE` or `REFUSAL`; the trial was long enough, meaning either the history is `REFUSAL` (a patient who refuses outright never accumulates nights) or `cpap_nights_used` is at least 30; and the documentation is fresh, meaning `cpap_documented_on` is not null and the whole number of days from that date to the clock date is at most 730. If any of the three fails, the referral is refused with `ERR_CPAP_TRIAL_REQUIRED`; if all three hold, `eligibility_basis` is set to `CPAP_INTOLERANT_SEVERE`. For a `MODERATE` band, oral appliance therapy is first-line and **no** positive-airway-pressure documentation is required at all: `eligibility_basis` is set to `FIRST_LINE_MILD_MODERATE` and no further test runs. For a `MILD` band the same basis applies, but only if the patient is symptomatic or has some pressure history: either `epworth_score` is at least 10, or `cpap_history` is anything other than `NONE`. A mild patient with an `epworth_score` of 9 and no pressure history is refused with `ERR_CPAP_TRIAL_REQUIRED`, while the identical payload under a `MODERATE` band is admitted without comment.

The treating dentist answers with a `decision` of `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reason`. Only the provider named as `treating_dentist_id` may answer; the patient's records must not be frozen; and, on the accepting arm, the clock date must not have passed `diagnosis_valid_until`. Both arms are ordinary mutations: `ISSUED → ACCEPTED` and `ISSUED → DECLINED` each raise the referral's `version` by 1 and refresh `updated_at`. The declining arm cascades to nothing, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight — a lab order can only be opened against an `ACCEPTED` referral, so an `ISSUED` referral has no dependants to unwind. `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `CLOSED` are the referral's three terminal states.

Expiry is the referral's third exit, and it is one of the two rungs in this API that writes before it refuses. When a dentist answers a referral, or a dentist opens a lab order against it, or a billing specialist files an authorisation against it, and the clock date has passed `diagnosis_valid_until`, the referral is persisted as `EXPIRED` with its `version` raised by 1 — and on the `ACCEPTED` arm every lab order on that referral still in `RECORDS_CAPTURED` is cancelled at the same moment — and only then is the caller refused with `ERR_DIAGNOSIS_EXPIRED`. On any subsequent attempt the referral is already `EXPIRED`, the state gate rejects first with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and no second expiry write and no second version bump occurs. §2.6 states the full precedence.

### 3.4. The Paperwork the Medical Benefit Demands

Durable medical equipment is not paid on clinical merit alone. Two documents must exist before the money moves, and both are the physician's or the biller's work rather than the dentist's.

The first is the **standard written order**. A `SleepPhysician` issues one naming the `patient_id`, the `ordering_physician_id` (himself), the `item_hcpcs` being ordered — `E0486` for a custom-fabricated appliance, `A9270` for a prefabricated one, or `K0739` for repair labour — the `order_date` on which it was written, and the `f2f_encounter_date` of the face-to-face encounter that supports it. Creation computes `f2f_age_days` as the whole number of days from `f2f_encounter_date` to `order_date`; a negative result means the encounter is dated after the order, which is nonsense and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The order begins in `DRAFT`.

Issuing a written order is guarded against a stale view of the patient, because it supersedes its predecessor: any earlier order for the same `patient_id` and the same `item_hcpcs` that is still `DRAFT` or `SIGNED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and the patient's own `version` rises by 1. The request must therefore carry `expected_patient_version` matching the patient's current `version`, as §2.7 specifies. One qualification applies to the signed arm: an older `SIGNED` order may be superseded only when no `Appliance` references it as its `written_order_id` — a device already delivered against an order keeps that order alive as its evidence. Where that qualification bites, no error identifier is defined; the older signed order simply remains `SIGNED` and the new order is created alongside it.

The ordering physician then signs, supplying `signed_on`, which must not be earlier than `order_date` (an earlier signature date is a calendar-domain violation answering `ERR_VALIDATION`). Signing moves the order `DRAFT → SIGNED` and persists `signed_on`; only a `SIGNED` order can support a delivery or a device claim, and the patient's records must not be frozen at the moment of signing. Where a delivery or a claim looks for its supporting order, it looks among that patient's orders whose `item_hcpcs` matches the item in question and takes the newest `SIGNED` one, ordered by `signed_on` descending and then by `created_at` descending.

The second document is the **prior authorisation**. A `BillingSpecialist` files one naming the `patient_id`, the `payer_id` — which must be the patient's own `medical_payer_id`, and whose `payer_kind` must be `MEDICAL`, on pain of `ERR_DENTAL_PAYER_ROUTING` — the `referral_id` whose therapy is being authorised, and the `requested_hcpcs` code among `E0486`, `A9270` and `K0739`. The referral must be `ACCEPTED`, and the expiry rung of §3.3 is evaluated against it here too. The row is created already in `SUBMITTED`, with `submitted_on` stamped from the clock date and `appeal_attempts` at 0.

A `PlatformAdmin` records the payer's answer with a `decision`, optionally a `denial_reason` and optionally a `decided_on`. On the approving arm, `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`: `decided_on` is set to the supplied date, or to the clock date when none is supplied, and `approval_valid_until` is computed as `decided_on` plus 90 days. On the denying arm, `SUBMITTED → DENIED`: `denial_reason` is required — one of `NOT_MEDICALLY_NECESSARY`, `MISSING_DOCUMENTATION` or `NON_COVERED_ITEM`, with its absence answering `ERR_VALIDATION` — and `decided_on` is set the same way, while `approval_valid_until` stays null.

Denial is not the end. A `BillingSpecialist` appeals with a free-text `narrative`, moving the authorisation `DENIED → APPEALED` and raising `appeal_attempts` by 1; the appeal is accepted only while `appeal_attempts` is strictly below the payer's `appeal_limit`, and the patient's records must not be frozen. From `APPEALED` the payer may approve — `APPEALED → APPROVED`, refreshing `decided_on` and recomputing `approval_valid_until` from the new decision date — or deny again. The second denial forks on the count: while `appeal_attempts` is still strictly below `appeal_limit`, the row returns to `DENIED` with `decided_on` refreshed and the cycle may run again; once `appeal_attempts` equals `appeal_limit`, the row lands in `DENIED_FINAL` instead, and no further appeal is accepted. With the default `appeal_limit` of 2, an authorisation can be denied, appealed, denied, appealed, and denied finally. A further appeal against a `DENIED_FINAL` authorisation answers `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` — the identifier this specification explicitly nominates for that case, which requires the state gate to treat both `DENIED` and `DENIED_FINAL` as appealable postures and to let the appeal cap decide. The friction is worth stating plainly: a stricter reading of the state gate, under which only `DENIED` admits an appeal, would answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE` from `DENIED_FINAL` and would leave the nominated identifier unreachable there. `DENIED_FINAL` and `EXPIRED` are the authorisation's terminal states.

An approved authorisation does not stay approved forever. Its `approval_valid_until` is enforced at claim submission, where an expired approval is written out as `EXPIRED` before the submission is refused — the second of this API's two mutate-then-reject rungs, specified in full in §2.6.

### 3.5. From Impression to Device in the Mouth

Once a referral is `ACCEPTED`, the `Dentist` takes records and opens a lab work order. The order names the `referral_id`, the `lab_provider_id` (an `ACTIVE` provider of type `DENTAL_LAB`), the `appliance_kind` — `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` or `PREFABRICATED`, which fixes the item code, the price and the warranty — and the `capture_method`, either `PVS_IMPRESSION` for a physical impression or `DIGITAL_SCAN`. Two measurements come off the articulator: `max_protrusive_mm`, the maximum protrusive range in millimetres, which must lie between 4.00 and 12.00 inclusive and be an exact multiple of 0.25, and which becomes the hard physical ceiling for every advancement that follows; and the optional `retruded_position_mm`, the retruded contact position reference, defaulting to 0.00. The lab commits to a turnaround with `promised_turnaround_days`, defaulting to 14 and confined to 1 through 60 inclusive. The order opens in `RECORDS_CAPTURED`, with `remake_count` at 0.

Submitting the case moves it `RECORDS_CAPTURED → IN_FABRICATION`, stamps `submitted_on` from the clock date, and computes `promised_ship_date` as `submitted_on` plus `promised_turnaround_days`; the submission may supply a fresh `promised_turnaround_days` to override the one captured at records. The laboratory then ships, supplying `shipped_on` — and only the provider named as `lab_provider_id` may do so. Shipping moves the order `IN_FABRICATION → SHIPPED` and sets `turnaround_on_time` to true exactly when `shipped_on` is not later than `promised_ship_date`, and to false otherwise.

The dentist tries the case in and answers with a `fit_outcome` — and it must be the dentist the referral names as its `treating_dentist_id`, no other. The two answers are mirror images of one another. `ACCEPTED` moves the order `SHIPPED → RECEIVED_ACCEPTED`, which is terminal and which is the only state from which a device may be founded. `REMAKE_REQUIRED` moves it `SHIPPED → REMAKE_REQUIRED` and clears `shipped_on` and `turnaround_on_time` back to null, because the shipment that was measured no longer counts. From `REMAKE_REQUIRED` the dentist submits again — the same capability, with the referral still required to be `ACCEPTED` — which returns the order to `IN_FABRICATION`, raises `remake_count` by 1, resets `submitted_on` to the current clock date and recomputes `promised_ship_date` from it. A case can go round that loop as many times as the fit demands. The order's other terminal state is `CANCELLED`, reached only as the cascade of a referral expiring while the order still sits in `RECORDS_CAPTURED`; no device may be founded on a cancelled order.

**Fabricating the device.** The dentist creates the `Appliance` from an accepted lab order and a manufacturing lot, supplying `lab_order_id`, `lot_id`, a `serial_number` unique across all appliances, an optional `has_microrecorder` defaulting to true, and an optional `replaces_appliance_id` naming the predecessor device this one succeeds. Creation copies `patient_id` from the lab order's referral, and `appliance_kind` and `max_protrusive_mm` from the lab order itself, and derives `hcpcs_code`: `E0486` for a `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` device, `A9270` for a `PREFABRICATED` one. The new device starts on all three of its axes at once — `lifecycle_status` `FABRICATED`, `device_hold_status` `NONE`, `recall_status` `NOT_SCHEDULED` — with `applied_visit_count` and `recall_cycle_index` at 0 and every date field null. When `replaces_appliance_id` is supplied, the predecessor moves `ACTIVE_THERAPY → REPLACED`, and as it leaves therapy its `recall_status` is reset to `NOT_SCHEDULED` and its `next_recall_due_on` is cleared to null.

**Only one device may be live in a patient's mouth.** Both fabrication and delivery are refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_APPLIANCE_EXISTS` when the patient already holds a *different* appliance whose `lifecycle_status` is `DELIVERED`, `IN_TITRATION`, `TITRATION_SUSPENDED`, `TITRATION_PLATEAU`, `EFFICACY_PENDING` or `ACTIVE_THERAPY`. The single exception is a declared replacement: the fabrication request supplies, or the delivery's row already carries, a `replaces_appliance_id` naming that very device, and that device is itself in `ACTIVE_THERAPY` or `RETIRED`. Guarding both operations rather than only one is deliberate — it lets a clinic fabricate a spare and hold it in `FABRICATED` indefinitely as a loaner, while still ensuring that only one device is ever actually in the patient's mouth.

**Delivery** is where the documentation regime first bites. The dentist supplies `delivered_on` and the `written_order_id` presented at the chair, and four things are checked in ladder order: the appliance must not be on a recall hold; a `SIGNED` written order must exist for this patient whose `item_hcpcs` equals the appliance's `hcpcs_code`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SWO_MISSING`; that order's `order_date` must not be later than `delivered_on`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SWO_AFTER_DELIVERY`; and that order's `f2f_age_days` must be at most 180, failing which the answer is `ERR_F2F_ENCOUNTER_STALE`. Exactly 180 days old still qualifies. The three checks fire in that order and never collapse into one another — a claim whose device was delivered before its order was written is refused for the ordering, not for the absence.

A successful delivery moves the device `FABRICATED → DELIVERED`, persists `written_order_id` on the appliance, and computes three things. `warranty_end_date` is `delivered_on` plus 36 whole months. `useful_lifetime_end_date` is `delivered_on` plus the medical payer's `useful_lifetime_months`, 60 months by default. And the initial advancement is set — **by a formula that differs entirely by device class**. For a `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` device the raw target is 60 percent of `max_protrusive_mm`. For a `PREFABRICATED` device it is the smaller of a fixed 4.00 mm and 0.50 of `max_protrusive_mm` — half the measured range. In both arms the rounding order is pinned: the raw target is first divided by the mechanical grain of 0.25 mm, that quotient is rounded half up to a whole number of grains, and the result is multiplied back by 0.25 — scale first, round once, never round the millimetre figure directly. A custom device on a measured range of 8.00 mm therefore starts at 4.75 mm (0.60 of 8.00 is 4.80; 4.80 divided by 0.25 is 19.2; rounded to 19 grains; 19 times 0.25 is 4.75), while a prefabricated device on the identical records starts at 4.00 mm (the smaller of 4.00 and half of 8.00 is 4.00, which is already 16 whole grains). A custom device on a 10.50 mm range starts at 6.25 mm (6.30 is 25.2 grains, rounded to 25). `current_advancement_mm` is initialised equal to `starting_advancement_mm`.

Proof of delivery is captured separately, by the `Dentist` or the `ClinicCoordinator`, while the device is in `DELIVERED`: `pod_signed_on`, the date the patient signed, and `pod_method`, either `IN_PERSON` or `SHIPPED`. Capturing it persists both fields and moves no state axis at all. Without it, no device claim of any kind may be submitted.

### 3.6. Titration at the Chair

Advancement happens a visit at a time, and each visit is a row of its own. The `Dentist` schedules one naming the `appliance_id`, the `performed_by_dentist_id` (an `ACTIVE` provider of type `DENTIST`), the `scheduled_for` date, and the `requested_increment_mm` he intends to apply — between 0.25 and 2.00 inclusive, and an exact multiple of 0.25. Creating a visit assigns `visit_seq`, the one-based ordinal of this visit among all visits on that appliance ordered by `created_at` ascending, and it **supersedes** any visit on the same appliance still in `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`, moving each to `SUPERSEDED`. Because that quietly discards a colleague's open visit, the create is guarded: it must carry `expected_appliance_version` matching the appliance's current `version`, and creating the visit raises that `version` by 1. Scheduling a visit carries **no** precondition on the appliance's `lifecycle_status`: the rung-12 state precondition of §2.6 governs the titration *apply*, not the create, so a visit may be scheduled against an appliance in any lifecycle state — `FABRICATED` included — provided the ladder's other rungs, the recall gate of rung 13 among them, are satisfied. A visit begins in `SCHEDULED` with `applied_increment_mm`, `applied_on` and `days_since_prior_applied` all null.

At the chair, the dentist applies the visit by reporting `side_effect_severity` — the joint, tooth and bite symptoms the patient describes. **That single field decides which of three outcomes the visit takes, and the three branches differ in almost every particular.**

When the report is `NONE` or `MILD`, the visit resolves to `APPLIED`. The requested advancement is first bounded at the per-visit ceiling of 1.00 mm, so a request for 1.50 mm becomes a bounded 1.00 mm. Two rungs then guard it, in this order: at least 14 whole days must separate this application from the appliance's most recent `APPLIED` or `REDUCED` visit, measured from that visit's `applied_on` to the current clock date, with the boundary inclusive — exactly 14 days passes, 13 does not, and where there is no prior applied or reduced visit the interval does not apply — failing which the answer is `ERR_TITRATION_INTERVAL`; and `current_advancement_mm` plus the bounded increment must not exceed the appliance's `max_protrusive_mm`, with equality permitted and one grain over refused, failing which the answer is `ERR_PROTRUSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The ceiling is a hard stop and never a silent clamp: a device sitting at 7.50 mm against an 8.00 mm ceiling refuses a bounded 1.00 mm outright rather than applying 0.50 mm, though it accepts a request of exactly 0.50 mm and lands on 8.00 mm. On success, `applied_increment_mm` is set to the bounded value, `applied_on` is stamped from the clock date, `days_since_prior_applied` records the whole days from the prior applied or reduced visit's `applied_on` to this one (null for the first such visit on the device), and the appliance's `current_advancement_mm` and `applied_visit_count` are recomputed. The first visit ever to resolve to `APPLIED` or `REDUCED` also moves the device `DELIVERED → IN_TITRATION`.

When the report is `MODERATE`, the visit resolves to `HELD` — a deferral, not an advancement. `applied_increment_mm` is set to 0.00 and, crucially, `applied_on` **stays null**, so a held visit never starts the titration interval clock and the next visit is measured from whatever applied or reduced visit came before. `scheduled_for` is pushed forward by 14 days, and `applied_visit_count` is **not** incremented — a held visit does not count toward the plateau requirement. Neither the interval rung nor the ceiling rung is evaluated on this branch. A held visit may be applied again later, and if the symptoms persist it simply holds again: the same visit moves `HELD → HELD`, pushing `scheduled_for` forward by a further 14 days each time, indefinitely, without ever consuming protrusive range. From `HELD` the other two answers behave exactly as they do from `SCHEDULED`: `NONE` or `MILD` resolves it to `APPLIED` under both guarding rungs, and `SEVERE` resolves it to `REDUCED`.

When the report is `SEVERE`, the visit resolves to `REDUCED` — the device is backed off. `applied_increment_mm` is set to the negative of the smaller of 0.50 mm and the appliance's current `current_advancement_mm`, which floors the result so that advancement can never drop below 0.00: a device at 4.75 mm falls to 4.25 mm, while a device at only 0.25 mm falls to 0.00 and no further. `applied_on` is stamped and the visit counts toward `applied_visit_count` exactly as an applied visit does. The protrusive-ceiling rung is not evaluated here, because a reduction can never breach a ceiling. Here the specification contains a friction point that downstream readers should adjudicate rather than assume: the ladder rung governing the titration interval states that it is evaluated **only** when the report is `NONE` or `MILD`, with the explicit rationale that backing a patient off a painful advancement must never have to wait — yet the state-machine description of the `SCHEDULED → REDUCED` edge also lists the fourteen-day interval among its preconditions. The two readings differ exactly on a severe reduction requested within 14 days of the last applied visit: one admits it, the other refuses it with `ERR_TITRATION_INTERVAL`. The `HELD → REDUCED` edge lists no interval precondition at all.

`APPLIED`, `REDUCED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED_BY_HOLD` are all terminal for a visit. The appliance's running total obeys Conservation 1 of §2.10 at every instant: `current_advancement_mm` is `starting_advancement_mm` plus the sum of `applied_increment_mm` over exactly the `APPLIED` and `REDUCED` visits on that device, taken in `created_at` order, with held, scheduled, superseded and voided visits contributing exactly 0.00; and `applied_visit_count` is the size of that same set.

**Declaring plateau** ends titration. The dentist declares it with no request body at all; the device must be `IN_TITRATION`, must not be on a recall hold, and must have at least 3 applied visits behind it — `applied_visit_count` of 3 is enough and 2 is not, and a shortfall answers `ERR_PLATEAU_REQUIRED`. Declaring moves the device `IN_TITRATION → TITRATION_PLATEAU`, stamps `plateau_declared_on` from the clock date, and moves every visit on that appliance still `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` to `SUPERSEDED`.

### 3.7. Proving the Therapy Works: Wear Time, the Efficacy Loop, and Recall

**Adherence** is measured over a rolling window of exactly 30 nights. A `Dentist`, a `ClinicCoordinator` or the `Patient` himself records one by naming the `appliance_id`, the `source` of the figures, the `window_start` and `window_end` dates bounding the window, `nights_recorded` (nights on which the device reported any wear at all), `nights_at_or_above_min_hours` (nights whose wear duration reached 4.0 hours) and `mean_hours_per_worn_night` (the mean wear hours across the recorded nights, between 0 and 24 inclusive). The window is inclusive of both ends and must span exactly 30 nights, so `window_end` is `window_start` plus 29 days — 2026-06-01 through 2026-06-30 is a valid window and anything else is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. `nights_at_or_above_min_hours` may not exceed `nights_recorded`, which in turn may not exceed 30. A download is an immutable observation: it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, and it is never edited after the fact.

The `source` is `OBJECTIVE_MICRORECORDER` when the figures were pulled off the chip embedded in the device, or `PATIENT_ATTESTED` when the patient reported them. A device whose `has_microrecorder` is false can never produce an objective download, and an attempt to record one against such a device is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**Two adherence rates are computed from the same numerator, and they disagree by design.** `rate_of_window` divides `nights_at_or_above_min_hours` by the full 30-night window, rounded half up to 4 decimal places: this is the payer's figure, and nights on which the device was never worn at all count against the patient. `rate_of_recorded` divides the same numerator by `nights_recorded`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places: this is the clinic's figure, reported back to the patient as a measure of wear quality on the nights he did wear it, and it is null when `nights_recorded` is 0. Consider 20 qualifying nights out of 24 recorded: the payer's figure is 20 divided by 30, which is **0.6667**, while the clinic's is 20 divided by 24, which is **0.8333**. The two agree only when `nights_recorded` equals 30 — 21 qualifying nights out of 30 recorded gives **0.7000** on both. `compliant` is derived from `rate_of_window` and **never** from `rate_of_recorded`: it is true exactly when `rate_of_window` is at least 0.70. On the worked pair above, the 20-of-24 download is not compliant while the 21-of-30 download is, even though the first looks far better to the clinic.

The adherence summary for an appliance reports both readings side by side, from the newest download of *each* source taken independently by `created_at` descending. It returns `appliance_id`, then `objective_rate_of_window`, `objective_rate_of_recorded` and `objective_compliant` from the newest objective download, then `attested_rate_of_window` and `attested_compliant` from the newest attested download, and finally the `window_start` and `window_end` of the newest objective download when one exists and of the newest attested download otherwise. Any figure with no download behind it is null.

**The efficacy report** is the document that closes the cross-provider loop. The `Dentist` drafts it naming the `referral_id` it closes, the `appliance_id` under test, and the `efficacy_study_id` — which must be a study whose `study_purpose` is `EFFICACY`, whose `status` is `DIAGNOSTIC_VALID`, and whose own `appliance_id` equals the report's. The appliance must be in `TITRATION_PLATEAU` or `EFFICACY_PENDING`, on pain of `ERR_PLATEAU_REQUIRED`, and a study that never passed its quality gate is refused with `ERR_STUDY_NON_DIAGNOSTIC`. Because a new report supersedes any `DISPUTED` report on the same referral — moving it to `SUPERSEDED` — the create carries `expected_referral_version` and raises the referral's `version` by 1. Creation copies `patient_id` from the referral and computes `due_by` as the appliance's `plateau_declared_on` plus 30 days: the deadline the dentist owes the referring physician. That deadline is informational; what the billing ladder actually enforces later is loop closure, not timeliness.

**The outcome is judged against two independent criteria, and the interesting cases are the ones that satisfy exactly one.** `baseline_index` is the severity index of the referral's diagnostic study and `residual_index` that of the efficacy study. `pct_reduction` is `baseline_index` minus `residual_index`, divided by `baseline_index`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The absolute criterion, `meets_absolute_target`, is true when `residual_index` is at most the payer's `residual_ahi_target` — 5.0 by default — regardless of how far the patient came. The relative criterion, `meets_relative_target`, is true when `pct_reduction` is at least the payer's `pct_reduction_target` — 0.50 by default — **and** `residual_index` is below 10.0; the second clause stops a spectacular percentage drop from certifying a patient who is still frankly apnoeic. When both criteria hold the `outcome` is `COMPLETE`; when exactly one holds it is `PARTIAL`; when neither holds it is `FAILED`. A baseline of 40.0 falling to a residual of 8.0 gives a `pct_reduction` of 0.8000: the absolute criterion fails (8.0 is above 5.0) but the relative one holds (0.8000 is at least 0.50, and 8.0 is below 10.0), so the outcome is `PARTIAL`. A baseline of 30.0 falling to 4.0 gives 0.8667 and satisfies both, so it is `COMPLETE`. A baseline of 20.0 falling to 12.0 gives 0.4000 and satisfies neither, so it is `FAILED`.

Submitting the report sends it to the physician. The acting dentist must be the referral's `treating_dentist_id`, the efficacy study must be valid, and the patient's records must not be frozen. On the first submission the report moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, `submitted_on` is stamped from the clock date, the appliance moves `TITRATION_PLATEAU → EFFICACY_PENDING`, and the referring physician's `open_efficacy_loop_count` is recomputed. The same capability re-submits a disputed report, moving it `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`, raising `revision_count` by 1 and refreshing `submitted_on`. A friction point lives here and should be adjudicated rather than assumed: the state gate describes an efficacy-report submission as requiring the appliance to be in `TITRATION_PLATEAU`, which is exactly the state a first submission leaves behind — a re-submission after a dispute finds the appliance in `EFFICACY_PENDING`, or in `IN_TITRATION` when the dispute was on a `PARTIAL` outcome — while the `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` edge itself names only the treating-dentist check among its preconditions. Read strictly against the appliance state, the revision cycle would be unreachable.

The physician answers with a `decision`, and the two answers diverge sharply. **On `SIGNED`**, the acting `X-User-ID` must be the referral's `referring_physician_id` and must not be its `treating_dentist_id` — a treating dentist presenting the physician role header to sign off his own titration is refused with `ERR_SELF_REFERRAL`, while any other provider is out of care-team scope and is refused earlier with `ERR_NOT_CARE_TEAM`. The report moves `SUBMITTED → SIGNED`, `physician_decision_on` is stamped from the clock date, and a three-level cascade runs: the referral moves `ACCEPTED → CLOSED` and its `loop_status` becomes `CLOSED`; the appliance moves `EFFICACY_PENDING → ACTIVE_THERAPY`; and the patient-recall cadence starts, with `recall_status` moving `NOT_SCHEDULED → NOT_DUE` and `next_recall_due_on` set to the clock date plus 90 days while `recall_cycle_index` stays at 0. The physician's `open_efficacy_loop_count` is recomputed. **On `DISPUTED`**, a `dispute_reason` must be supplied — free text, and its absence is `ERR_VALIDATION` — the report moves `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`, `physician_decision_on` is stamped, and the referral's `loop_status` stays `OPEN`, so nothing downstream unlocks. The appliance's fate on this branch depends on the outcome under dispute: when `outcome` is `PARTIAL`, the appliance moves `EFFICACY_PENDING → IN_TITRATION` and `plateau_declared_on` is cleared to null, forcing the dentist to titrate further and re-declare plateau; when `outcome` is `COMPLETE` or `FAILED`, the appliance stays in `EFFICACY_PENDING`. The physician's `open_efficacy_loop_count` is recomputed on this branch too, since a disputed report is still an open loop. `SIGNED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are the report's terminal states.

**The patient-recall cadence** is the appliance's third state axis and it runs on its own clock. Activation sets `next_recall_due_on` to 90 days after the date therapy became active — measured from activation, not from delivery. Every time a `PlatformAdmin` sets the logical clock, the posture of every appliance in `ACTIVE_THERAPY` is re-evaluated, and the lapse test is applied **first**: when the clock date has reached or passed `next_recall_due_on` plus 30 grace days, `recall_status` becomes `LAPSED`; otherwise, when the clock date has reached or passed `next_recall_due_on`, it becomes `DUE`. Both boundaries are inclusive. Because the lapse edge is tested first, a single large clock jump past the grace boundary takes an appliance straight from `NOT_DUE` to `LAPSED`, skipping `DUE` entirely.

The `Dentist` clears the posture by performing a recall check, optionally recording free-text `notes`. The appliance must be in `ACTIVE_THERAPY`. From either `DUE` or `LAPSED` the check returns `recall_status` to `NOT_DUE` and raises `recall_cycle_index` by 1, and the next check falls 365 days later. On the lapsed arm the specification is explicit that the next due date is recomputed **from the date the check was actually performed, not from the missed due date**, so a late check pushes the whole cadence out rather than compressing it — and the calculation that governs both arms, together with its stated rationale, measures every subsequent cycle from the date the previous check was performed. The friction to record is that the description of the `DUE → NOT_DUE` edge instead says the 365 days advance from the completed due date; the two readings coincide only when a check is performed exactly on its due date. A check performed on 2026-09-05 against a due date of 2026-08-30 therefore schedules the next check on 2027-09-05 under the calculation's rule and on 2027-08-30 under the edge's phrasing.

### 3.8. Billing the Medical Benefit

A `BillingSpecialist` drafts the claim. It names the `patient_id`, the `payer_id`, the `appliance_id` being billed, a `claim_type` of `INITIAL_DEVICE`, `REPAIR`, `REPLACEMENT` or `CONTINUED_USE`, the `hcpcs_code` being billed, the `date_of_service`, and `billed_cents`, the amount billed in whole cents and never negative. Three optional references carry the supporting paperwork: `prior_auth_id`, `written_order_id`, and `repair_order_id`, the last of which is set only on a repair claim. The medical-benefit routing rung bites already at draft time — a claim naming the patient's `dental_payer_id`, or any payer whose `payer_kind` is `DENTAL`, or a medical payer that is not this patient's `medical_payer_id`, is refused with `ERR_DENTAL_PAYER_ROUTING` before the row is ever created. For an `INITIAL_DEVICE` or a `REPLACEMENT` claim, `date_of_service` must equal the appliance's `delivered_on`; no dedicated identifier is declared for that mismatch, so it surfaces as a calendar-domain `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new claim begins in `DRAFT` with `appeal_attempts` at 0 and every money field except `billed_cents` null.

**Submission is where the whole durable-medical-equipment documentation ladder runs**, rungs 24 through 33, in strict order and stopping at the first failure. In sequence: a continued-use claim requires the appliance's referral to have `loop_status` `CLOSED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_EFFICACY_LOOP_OPEN`; the payer routing is re-checked (`ERR_DENTAL_PAYER_ROUTING`); an initial-device or replacement claim requires a `SIGNED` written order matching the claim's `hcpcs_code` (`ERR_SWO_MISSING`), whose `order_date` does not follow the delivery (`ERR_SWO_AFTER_DELIVERY`) and whose `f2f_age_days` is at most 180 (`ERR_F2F_ENCOUNTER_STALE`); every claim type requires an approved, unexpired authorisation when the payer's `prior_auth_required` is true (`ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED`, with the expiry write described in §2.6); an initial-device, replacement or continued-use claim requires proof of delivery on file, meaning both `pod_signed_on` and `pod_method` are set (`ERR_POD_MISSING`); a replacement claim requires `date_of_service` strictly after the predecessor appliance's `useful_lifetime_end_date`, the end date itself still counting as inside the lifetime (`ERR_USEFUL_LIFETIME_NOT_ELAPSED`); a repair claim requires the linked repair order's `billable` to be true (`ERR_REPAIR_NOT_BILLABLE`); and a continued-use claim requires the newest objective adherence download on the appliance — the newest row whose `source` is `OBJECTIVE_MICRORECORDER`, by `created_at` descending — to have `compliant` true and requires the appliance's `recall_status` not to be `LAPSED` (`ERR_ADHERENCE_NOT_MET`). A patient-attested download, however flattering its figures, never satisfies that last rung. The claim's own `status` must be `DRAFT`, the patient must not be frozen, and the appliance must not be on a recall hold.

Submission moves the claim `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and resolves the fee basis, which is fixed by the item code rather than negotiated. `E0486`, the custom-fabricated appliance, has a fee basis of 185000 cents. `A9270`, the prefabricated appliance, is statutorily non-covered and has a fee basis of 0 cents. `K0739`, repair labour, takes its basis from the linked repair order's `repair_charge_cents`. From that basis, `allowed_cents` is the smaller of `billed_cents` and the fee basis, and `adjustment_cents` — the contractual write-off — is `billed_cents` minus `allowed_cents`. A custom device billed at 200000 cents is allowed 185000 with 15000 written off. A prefabricated device billed at 45000 cents is allowed **0**, with the entire 45000 landing in `adjustment_cents`; the claim is still perfectly submittable and adjudicable, because non-coverage is a coverage outcome and not an error. A repair claim billed at 9000 cents against a repair order charging 7500 is allowed 7500 with 1500 written off.

Adjudication is the `PlatformAdmin`'s, and the two outcomes are not symmetrical in what they write. **On `PAID`** the money waterfall runs in a pinned order: the deductible first, then coinsurance on the remainder, rounded exactly once. `deductible_applied_cents` is the smaller of `allowed_cents` and the patient's `deductible_remaining_cents` at this instant. The coinsurance base is `allowed_cents` minus `deductible_applied_cents`, and `coinsurance_cents` is the payer's `coinsurance_pct` multiplied by that base, rounded half up to whole cents. `patient_responsibility_cents` is `deductible_applied_cents` plus `coinsurance_cents`, and `payer_paid_cents` is `allowed_cents` minus `patient_responsibility_cents`. Finally the ledger syncs: the patient's `deductible_remaining_cents` decreases by `deductible_applied_cents`. Work an allowed amount of 185000 cents against a patient with 50000 cents of deductible left and a 0.20 coinsurance: the deductible takes 50000, the base is 135000, coinsurance is 27000, the patient owes **77000** and the payer pays 108000, and the patient's remaining deductible falls to 0. The order matters and the alternative is explicitly rejected: rounding coinsurance on the full allowed amount and then adding the deductible would give 87000, which is not what this API computes. **On `DENIED`** the picture is the mirror image: `denial_error_id` is persisted — the identifier the submission would have raised, supplied by the adjudicator — and **no money field is written at all**, leaving `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents` and `payer_paid_cents` null and moving no deductible. A denial lands in `DENIED_FINAL` rather than `DENIED` when `appeal_attempts` already equals the payer's `appeal_limit`.

The claim's appeal cycle mirrors the authorisation's. A `BillingSpecialist` appeals a denied claim with a free-text `narrative`, moving it `DENIED → APPEALED` and raising `appeal_attempts` by 1, permitted only while `appeal_attempts` is strictly below the payer's `appeal_limit` and while the patient is not frozen; beyond that the answer is `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. From `APPEALED` the payer may pay — computing the money legs exactly as on the first paying adjudication, from the deductible balance observed at *that* instant, so a deductible consumed by another claim in the meantime is not double-counted — or deny again, returning to `DENIED` while attempts remain and landing in `DENIED_FINAL` once `appeal_attempts` equals `appeal_limit`. `PAID`, `DENIED_FINAL` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

`VOIDED` is reached only by cascade, and only for a `DRAFT` claim of type `CONTINUED_USE`: either the lot recall of §3.9 or the loss of the device it bills. A voided claim can never be submitted; the biller must draft a fresh row.

### 3.9. When Devices Fail: Repairs, Warranty, Loaners and Lot Recalls

A `Dentist` opens a `RepairOrder` naming the `appliance_id`, a `reason`, the `reported_on` date the problem surfaced, an optional `labor_minutes` of bench labour defaulting to 0, and an optional `loaner_appliance_id` — a spare device issued to the patient while the case is at the laboratory. The state gate requires the appliance to be past `DELIVERED` and neither `RETIRED` nor `REPLACED`; the state machine states the admissible set explicitly as `ACTIVE_THERAPY`, `IN_TITRATION`, `TITRATION_PLATEAU` or `EFFICACY_PENDING`. The two phrasings differ on exactly one state, `TITRATION_SUSPENDED`, and the difference is visible in the response: under the enumerated set a repair opened on a suspended device is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, whereas under the looser phrasing it passes the state gate and is refused one rung later, by the device gate, with `ERR_DEVICE_RECALL_HOLD`. This is a friction point for downstream adjudication, not a settled question.

**Whether the repair is billable turns on the reason code and the warranty clock together.** `in_warranty` is true only when `reason` is `DEFECT` *and* `reported_on` is not later than the appliance's `warranty_end_date`, which sits 36 months after delivery. `billable` is the logical opposite of `in_warranty` for the `DEFECT` and `WEAR` reasons, while `ABUSE` and `LOSS` are always billable. On the in-warranty branch nothing is charged: `labor_units` is 0, `repair_charge_cents` is 0, and a repair claim raised against that order is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_NOT_BILLABLE`, because the manufacturer covers it. On the billable branch, labour is billed in whole quarter-hours: `labor_units` is `labor_minutes` divided by 15 and rounded **up** to the next whole unit, the uncapped charge is `labor_units` multiplied by 2500 cents per unit, and `repair_charge_cents` is the smaller of that and a cap of half the custom-appliance fee schedule — 0.50 of 185000 cents, rounded half up, which is 92500 cents. Forty minutes of labour is therefore 3 units and 7500 cents. At 555 minutes the charge is exactly 37 units and 92500 cents; at 556 minutes it is 38 units, 95000 cents uncapped, and still 92500 cents after the cap binds. `resets_useful_lifetime` is false on every repair, in warranty or out: **no repair ever moves `useful_lifetime_end_date` or `warranty_end_date`.** Only a payer-funded replacement restarts the useful-lifetime clock, and it does so by delivering a new device with a new lifetime of its own.

The reason code also decides, at the instant of creation, where the case goes. For `DEFECT`, `WEAR` and `ABUSE` the order moves `OPEN → IN_LAB` immediately and the loaner interval, if a loaner was named, opens on that device. For `LOSS` the order moves `OPEN → CLOSED_NOT_COVERED` immediately instead: `billable` is set to true and `repair_charge_cents` to 0, because a lost device is replaced rather than repaired, and a two-level cascade runs — the appliance is retired, resetting its `recall_status` to `NOT_SCHEDULED` and clearing `next_recall_due_on`, and every `DRAFT` claim of type `CONTINUED_USE` on that appliance is voided. The retirement edge is declared out of `ACTIVE_THERAPY` and, identically, out of `IN_TITRATION`, where any visit still `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` on the device also moves to `SUPERSEDED`; a device lost mid-titration is retired just the same. A second friction point belongs here: repair creation is admitted from `TITRATION_PLATEAU` and `EFFICACY_PENDING` as well, yet no retirement edge is declared out of either of those two states, so the destination of a `LOSS` repair opened against a plateaued or efficacy-pending device is not settled by the specification.

A loaner may serve only one repair at a time. Occupancy is a half-open interval running from the repair's `reported_on` up to but not including its `returned_on`; a repair still open occupies its loaner from `reported_on` onward without end. A new repair naming a loaner whose interval would overlap is refused with `ERR_LOANER_UNAVAILABLE`. Because the interval excludes its closing date, a device freed on a given date may be issued again to a repair reported on exactly that date.

Completing a repair takes an `outcome`, and the two answers differ in what they leave behind. `REPAIRED` moves the order `IN_LAB → CLOSED_REPAIRED`, stamps `returned_on` from the clock date — which closes the loaner interval and frees the loaner device — and leaves `resets_useful_lifetime` false and the appliance's dates untouched. `NOT_COVERED` moves the order `IN_LAB → CLOSED_NOT_COVERED`, stamps `returned_on` the same way, and sets `repair_charge_cents` to 0; no repair claim may be created against an order closed that way. Both closed states are terminal.

**Lot recalls are the deepest cascade in this API.** A `PlatformAdmin` registers each manufacturing lot with a `manufacturer`, a `lot_code` unique across all lots, and an optional `material` defaulting to `NYLON_COPOLYMER`. A lot opens in `ACTIVE` with `affected_device_count`, `remediated_count`, `pending_remediation_count` and `retired_count` all 0.

Issuing a recall against an `ACTIVE` lot requires a `notice_reference` — the manufacturer's or regulator's notice identifier — and a `recall_severity` of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`. The lot moves `ACTIVE → RECALL_ISSUED` with `recall_issued_on` stamped from the clock date, and the counters are computed over every appliance carrying that `lot_id`: `affected_device_count` is the number whose `lifecycle_status` is neither `RETIRED` nor `REPLACED`, `retired_count` is the number whose `lifecycle_status` is one of those two, `remediated_count` starts at 0, and `pending_remediation_count` starts equal to `affected_device_count`. Note that the calculation partitions the population at the instant of issuance while the lot record's own description of `retired_count` speaks of counting at remediation time; the frozen-at-issuance reading is the one Conservation 3 of §2.10 depends on, and the divergence is recorded here rather than resolved.

Then the cascade runs, five levels deep. **Level 1:** every appliance in the lot whose `lifecycle_status` is neither `RETIRED` nor `REPLACED` moves `device_hold_status` `NONE → RECALL_HOLD`. **Level 2:** every titration visit on those appliances still in `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` moves to `VOIDED_BY_HOLD`. **Level 3:** an appliance left with no `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` visit remaining, and which was in `IN_TITRATION`, moves `IN_TITRATION → TITRATION_SUSPENDED`. **Level 4:** every efficacy report on those appliances in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and its referral's `loop_status` returns to `OPEN` — with the referring physician's `open_efficacy_loop_count` recomputed for the reports that were `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`. **Level 5:** every claim on those appliances with `claim_type` `CONTINUED_USE` and `status` `DRAFT` moves to `VOIDED`. A device already `RETIRED` or `REPLACED` is untouched by level 1 and by everything below it.

Remediation is the dentist's, one device at a time, recording a free-text `remediation_action`. The appliance's lot must be in `RECALL_ISSUED` and the appliance must be on a `RECALL_HOLD` — remediation is the single write that a held device still accepts. It moves `device_hold_status` `RECALL_HOLD → NONE` and, when the lifecycle axis was suspended, moves `TITRATION_SUSPENDED → IN_TITRATION` on the same call. On the lot, `remediated_count` rises by 1 and `pending_remediation_count` falls by 1; when `pending_remediation_count` reaches 0 the lot moves `RECALL_ISSUED → REMEDIATION_COMPLETE`, which is terminal, and the lot stops blocking anything. Because the affected population was frozen at issuance, a device that retires *after* the recall still has to be remediated before the lot can close.
