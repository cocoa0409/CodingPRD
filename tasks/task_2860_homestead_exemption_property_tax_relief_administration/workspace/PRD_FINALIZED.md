# Homestead Exemption Relief API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

County assessors administer property tax relief under a body of statute that is generous in intent and unforgiving in detail. A homestead exemption is worth real money to a household, which is precisely why the eligibility record — who owns the parcel, who sleeps in it, what the household earned, whether the claimant is old enough or disabled enough or farming the land — has to be reconstructed from registries that disagree with one another, and why an exemption granted on a false residency claim has to be clawed back years later with penalty and interest. Today the Exemptions Unit performs this work across disconnected systems: intake clerks key applications into one database, analysts maintain the parcel roll in another, fraud referrals arrive as spreadsheets, and appeal hearings are docketed on paper. The consequence is not merely inefficiency; it is a value roll that nobody can reproduce, because no single record explains why a parcel's taxable value is what it is.

This document specifies the Homestead Exemption & Property Tax Relief Administration API, the system of record for the Exemptions Unit. It governs the full arc of a relief claim: intake and submission, residency and ownership verification against external registries, determination across five statutory classifications, the minting and continuing supervision of exemption grants, valuation freezes and their portability between properties, retroactive refund claims bounded by statutory lookback, fraud investigation with penalty and back-tax recovery, and the notice, appeal and hearing rights that attach to every adverse action. Every collection is mounted at the root of the API — there is no versioned or domain prefix — and peer resources are joined by explicit identifiers carried in request bodies rather than by nesting in URL paths.

Two properties drive the design. The first is **reproducibility**: a parcel's assessed, exempt and taxable values are never entered by hand but derived, on every event that could change them, from the parcel's published valuation inputs and the set of grants currently reducing it. The second is **segregation of duties**: the clerk who intakes an application may not determine it, the investigator who substantiates a fraud case may not collect on it, and the reviewer whose determination was appealed may not sit as the hearing officer. These two properties, taken together, mean that a large share of this specification is about *ordering* — which check runs before which, which value is read before which is written — and implementers should treat every stated evaluation order as a hard requirement rather than an incidental detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelope and pagination contracts, timekeeping, arithmetic discipline, the parcel value invariant, duty separation, stale-version preconditions, and the fixed order in which a request is screened before any business logic runs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. A request missing either header, or presenting an `X-User-Role` that is not one of the nine roles enumerated below, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is well formed but is not among the roles authorised for the addressed operation (§4 lists the authorised roles for each) is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.

The nine roles are `Taxpayer`, `ProgramAdministrator`, `AssessorAnalyst`, `IntakeClerk`, `ExemptionsReviewer`, `FraudInvestigator`, `RecoveryOfficer`, `HearingClerk`, and `HearingOfficer`. Their responsibilities divide as follows. A `Taxpayer` is the property owner or applicant; they may read only their own records, answer information requests on their own applications, file retroactive claims, and file appeals. A `ProgramAdministrator` configures statutory relief programs and operates the test-driving clock and registry-injection capabilities. An `AssessorAnalyst` maintains the parcel roll and the ownership and occupancy registry and runs the annual assessment roll. An `IntakeClerk` opens taxpayer accounts, applications and income certifications, records verification checks, and submits applications for review — but may never determine one. An `ExemptionsReviewer` determines applications, mints and ports grants, takes compliance actions on grants, and decides retroactive claims. A `FraudInvestigator` opens fraud cases from address and registration cross-matches and adjudicates them, but may never record the money movement on a case they substantiated. A `RecoveryOfficer` collects, waives and writes off recovery assessments raised by substantiated cases. A `HearingClerk` opens hearing dockets and schedules appeals onto docket slots, and may never decide an appeal. A `HearingOfficer` hears scheduled appeals and issues the decision that grants, denies, dismisses or continues them.

Only one role is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Taxpayer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TaxpayerAccount`; ownership checks compare that value against the taxpayer identifier reachable from the addressed resource. Resolution is direct for a taxpayer account and its sub-resource reads, and indirect otherwise: through an application's `taxpayer_id`, through a grant's `taxpayer_id`, through a retroactive claim's grant to that grant's `taxpayer_id`, through a notice's `taxpayer_id`, and through an appeal's `taxpayer_id`. When the resolved taxpayer identifier differs from `X-User-ID`, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This ownership resolution governs every request a `Taxpayer` makes; it is declared explicitly for answering an information request, filing a retroactive claim, filing an appeal, reading a taxpayer account, listing exemption grants, and listing a taxpayer's notices. When a `Taxpayer` lists exemption grants, any taxpayer filter naming somebody else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and an unfiltered listing is scoped to the acting taxpayer's own grants.

The other eight roles — `ProgramAdministrator`, `AssessorAnalyst`, `IntakeClerk`, `ExemptionsReviewer`, `FraudInvestigator`, `RecoveryOfficer`, `HearingClerk` and `HearingOfficer` — carry opaque operator identifiers. For each of these, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The server does record such identifiers where a transition writes one — `intake_user_id` and `determined_by_user_id` on an application, `opened_by_user_id` and `substantiated_by_user_id` on a fraud case, `decided_by_user_id` on an appeal — and it compares an opaque identifier only against another opaque identifier already recorded on the resource, for the duty-separation purposes described in §2.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside that inclusive range is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `offset` parameter is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond it.

Each list endpoint also accepts a set of equality filters over its own columns, described with the workflow that produces those rows in §3: ownership records may be narrowed by `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `occupancy_type` and `status`; exemption applications by `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `program_id`, `status` and `tax_year`; verification checks by `source` and `result`; income certifications by `certification_year` and `status`; exemption grants by `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `application_id`, `program_id`, `ported_from_grant_id` and `benefit_status`; retroactive claims by `status`; fraud cases by `taxpayer_id`, `grant_id`, `investigation_status` and `recovery_status`; notices by `notice_type`, `subject_id` and `subject_type`; and appeals by `notice_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `docket_id` and `status`. Every filter is optional and filters combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties

Every resource the API persists carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, generated by the server and never supplied by a client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the row was first written.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every subsequent mutation, including mutations that only rewrite derived fields — a parcel whose exempt and taxable values are recomputed because a grant became active has been mutated, and its `version` advances accordingly. `ReliefProgram`, `Parcel`, `TaxpayerAccount`, `OwnershipRecord`, `ExemptionApplication`, `IncomeCertification`, `ExemptionGrant`, `RetroactiveClaim`, `FraudCase`, `Notice`, `HearingDocket` and `Appeal` all behave this way.

The single exception is `VerificationCheck`. A verification check is an immutable event: once recorded it is never rewritten, superseded checks are retained alongside their successors, and the row carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a verification check — they are not present-and-null, they simply do not appear in the resource.

### 2.5. The Logical Assessment Clock

The system does not read wall-clock time. Every instant it stamps and every deadline it evaluates come from a logical clock that begins at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and only ever moves when a `ProgramAdministrator` sets it, by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. There is no fallback to real time at any point.

The clock is what stamps `checked_at` on a verification check, `mailed_at` on a notice, and `filed_at` on an appeal, all as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. It is also the reference for every date comparison: whether a certification's `recertification_due_date` has passed at annual-roll time, and whether an appeal was filed at or before a notice's `appeal_deadline`. Calendar-date fields — `date_of_birth`, `deed_recorded_date`, `occupancy_start_date`, `occupancy_end_date`, `claimed_occupancy_date`, `recertification_due_date` and `docket_date` — are plain ISO 8601 dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component; a malformed date or instant anywhere in a payload is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer numbers of **whole cents**, and every field holding one is named with a `_cents` suffix. All rates, shares and multipliers are integer **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is one whole unit: `relief_pct_bps` of 10000 exempts the entire assessed basis, `assessment_cap_pct_bps` of 200 is a two percent annual cap, and `ownership_pct_bps` of 5000 is a one-half undivided interest.

Because every stored amount is an integer cent, rounding happens only where a calculation divides. Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the method is **round half up** to a whole cent: a residue of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero. Each calculation in §3 pins the exact point at which its single rounding occurs, and those pinned points are normative. In particular, calculations that sum a per-year series round each year's term to whole cents *before* summing — summing exact products and rounding the total once produces cent-level differences and is wrong. Conversely, calculations that describe a chain of multiplications ending in one rounding must not round intermediate steps, because an intermediate rounding changes which side of a ceiling comparison the value falls on. Ratio comparisons — the income tier bands, for instance — are performed as exact integer comparisons by cross-multiplication, never by computing a decimal ratio first.

Monetary and basis-point values appear in API responses as JSON numbers, never strings. A negative amount or a negative basis-point value where the field is declared non-negative is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.7. The Parcel Value Decomposition

A parcel's taxable basis is never entered; it is derived. The derivation is a single named procedure that runs in four ordered steps, and it is the only writer of `capped_value_cents`, `assessed_value_cents`, `exempt_value_cents`, `taxable_value_cents` and `exemption_status` on a `Parcel`, and of `applied_exempt_value_cents` on an `ExemptionGrant`.

**Step one — the assessment cap.** The number of elapsed years is the parcel's `tax_year` minus its `base_year`, or zero if that difference would be negative. The `capped_value_cents` is then the `base_year_value_cents` multiplied by the quantity ten thousand plus `assessment_cap_pct_bps` times the elapsed-year count, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent. This is the only rounding anywhere in the procedure. Growth is therefore simple, not compounded: a parcel whose base year is four years behind its tax year and whose cap is the default two percent per year grows its base-year value by eight percent in total. When `assessment_cap_pct_bps` is omitted at parcel creation it defaults to 200, and it may be set anywhere from 0 through 2000 inclusive.

**Step two — the assessed basis.** The assessed basis starts as the smaller of the parcel's `market_value_cents` and its `capped_value_cents`. The system then looks for a valuation freeze: among the grants on this parcel whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `frozen_value_cents` is not null, the **controlling freeze** is the one whose program has the lowest `priority_rank`, and where two programs tie on rank, the grant with the earlier `created_at`. If such a grant exists, the assessed basis becomes the smaller of the value computed so far and that controlling grant's `frozen_value_cents`. The result is written to `assessed_value_cents`. Note the order: the cap applies first, and the freeze applies to the already-capped figure.

**Step three — the exemption waterfall.** Only grants whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` participate. They are ordered by their program's `priority_rank` ascending, with earlier `created_at` breaking ties; the grant's own `id` never participates in the ordering. Beginning with the whole `assessed_value_cents` as the remaining basis, each grant in turn absorbs the smaller of its own `benefit_amount_cents` and the remaining basis. That absorbed figure is written to the grant's `applied_exempt_value_cents` and subtracted from the remaining basis. A grant whose benefit exceeds what is left absorbs only the remainder, and every grant after it in the ordering absorbs zero. The sum of all absorbed amounts becomes the parcel's `exempt_value_cents`, and `taxable_value_cents` is `assessed_value_cents` less `exempt_value_cents`. The invariant that exempt plus taxable equals assessed holds after every run, and `taxable_value_cents` is therefore never negative.

**Step four — the derived posture.** When nothing was absorbed, `exemption_status` becomes `UNEXEMPTED`. When the absorbed total equals `assessed_value_cents` exactly, it becomes `FULLY_EXEMPT`. Otherwise it becomes `PARTIALLY_EXEMPT`.

Grants in any status other than `ACTIVE` — `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED`, `TRANSFERRED` or `LAPSED` — are excluded from both the freeze selection and the waterfall, and an excluded grant's `applied_exempt_value_cents` is reset to 0. That exclusion is what makes suspension immediately expensive for a taxpayer and immediately visible on the roll.

The decomposition re-runs on every event that changes a parcel's published values or its `ACTIVE` grant set: when an application is approved and mints a grant; on every change of a grant's `benefit_status` in either direction, whether `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, or a move to `REVOKED`, `LAPSED` or `TRANSFERRED`; on a portability transfer, where it runs for the source parcel and the destination parcel alike; and for every parcel rolled forward by the annual assessment roll. Each such run is a mutation of the parcel: it refreshes the parcel's `updated_at` and increments the parcel's `version` by exactly 1. Where a single request touches two parcels, each parcel's `version` increments by 1.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties Across the Exemptions Unit

Four hand-offs in this system are duty-separated, and in each the server compares the acting `X-User-ID` against an operator identifier already recorded on the resource. A mismatch is required; equality is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Determination is barred to the intake clerk: a determination whose acting `X-User-ID` equals the application's `intake_user_id` is refused. Deciding a retroactive claim is barred to the reviewer who determined the application behind the claimed grant: the acting identifier is compared against the `determined_by_user_id` of that application. Recording a recovery action — collection or abatement — is barred to the investigator who substantiated the case, by comparison against the case's `substantiated_by_user_id`. And deciding an appeal is barred to the operator who took the noticed action being challenged: for a denial notice that is the application's `determined_by_user_id`, and for a recovery-assessment notice it is the case's `substantiated_by_user_id`.

None of these comparisons involves a taxpayer identity, and none of them compares an operator identifier to an entity `id`; opaque identifiers are only ever compared against other opaque identifiers stored on the record.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Preconditions

Two write operations require the client to prove it has read the current state of a parent record before it acts. Each takes a non-optional version field in its request body, and a mismatch against the parent's stored `version` is rejected before any business rule is evaluated.

A determination request — `POST /exemption-applications/{id}/determine` — must carry `expected_parcel_version` equal to the current `version` of the parcel named by the application's `parcel_id`. If it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that approving an application mints a grant that rewrites the parcel's whole value decomposition, and several classifications may legitimately stack on one parcel; a determination computed against a superseded view of the parcel's assessed and exempt values would produce a benefit amount that never existed. The check forces the reviewer to re-read the parcel before deciding.

A portability transfer — `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/port` — must carry `expected_grant_version` equal to the current `version` of the source grant addressed in the path. If it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Partial ports are legitimate and each one reduces the source grant's remaining portable balance, so a transfer sized from a stale view of the grant would allocate frozen value that an earlier port already carved out. The check forces the second transfer to re-read the remaining balance first.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to any other operation neither require nor accept `expected_parcel_version` or `expected_grant_version`, and no other operation performs a stale-version comparison.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Screened

Many operations in this system can fail for several reasons at once, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the fixed ascending order below, and the **first** failure decides the response; later checks are not evaluated at all. Where a rung lists several error identifiers, the one reported is the one matching the resource or condition that actually failed.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation for the addressed operation — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Taxpayer ownership of the addressed resource — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, as described in §2.1. Declared for `POST /exemption-applications/{id}/respond-info`, `POST /retroactive-claims`, `POST /appeals`, `GET /taxpayer-accounts/{id}`, `GET /exemption-grants` and `GET /taxpayer-accounts/{id}/notices`. When the addressed or referenced resource does not exist at all, ownership cannot be evaluated and the not-found error from rung 6 or rung 7 is returned instead.
4. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, as described in §2.8. Applies to `POST /exemption-applications/{id}/determine`, `POST /retroactive-claims/{id}/decide`, `POST /fraud-cases/{id}/recovery-actions` and `POST /appeals/{id}/decide`.
5. Request-body shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: required fields present, integers where integers are required and non-negative where declared non-negative, enum members drawn from the declared sets, ISO 8601 dates and instants well formed, and a pagination `limit` inside the inclusive range 1 through 100. Applies to every endpoint.
6. Path identifiers resolve, left to right — `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAXPAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FRAUD_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DOCKET_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter. When a path carries more than one identifier, they are resolved in the order they appear in the path and the first unresolvable one decides the error.
7. Body foreign keys resolve — the same not-found family plus `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, covering `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `program_id`, `grant_id`, `notice_id`, `docket_id` and `destination_parcel_id`. Applies to `POST /ownership-records`, `POST /exemption-applications`, `POST /income-certifications`, `POST /retroactive-claims`, `POST /fraud-cases`, `POST /appeals`, `POST /appeals/{id}/schedule` and `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/port`.
8. Stale-version preconditions — `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` on determination and `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` on a portability transfer, as described in §2.9.
9. The target is in a state that admits the requested transition — `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_GRANT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_ASSESSED`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_SCHEDULED` or `ERR_DOCKET_CLOSED`. Applies to every action endpoint.
10. The taxpayer is not barred — `ERR_TAXPAYER_BARRED`, on `POST /exemption-applications/{id}/submit`.
11. No fraud hold — `ERR_FRAUD_HOLD_ACTIVE`, on `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/port`, `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/compliance-actions` and `POST /retroactive-claims/{id}/decide`.
12. The destination parcel of a transfer is owned and occupied by the grant's taxpayer — `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_OWNED`, on `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/port`.
13. Verified ownership and occupancy support the claim — `ERR_OWNERSHIP_NOT_VERIFIED`, on an approving determination.
14. Residency cross-matching succeeded — `ERR_RESIDENCY_VERIFICATION_FAILED`, on an approving determination.
15. Programme-specific eligibility — `ERR_EXEMPTION_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, on an approving determination.
16. Occupancy interval geometry on the parcel — `ERR_OCCUPANCY_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, on `POST /ownership-records`.
17. One primary residence per taxpayer — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIMARY_RESIDENCE`, on `POST /ownership-records`.
18. Hearing interval geometry on the docket — `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_CONFLICT`, on `POST /appeals/{id}/schedule`.
19. Appeal timeliness and appealability — `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED` or `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_APPEALABLE`, on `POST /appeals`.
20. A retroactive claim has at least one eligible year — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLAIM_YEARS`, on `POST /retroactive-claims`.
21. The requested start year is inside the statutory lookback — `ERR_STATUTORY_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDED`, on `POST /retroactive-claims`.
22. Claim-year exclusivity — `ERR_CLAIM_YEARS_OVERLAP`, on `POST /retroactive-claims`.
23. No competing live application for the same parcel, programme and tax year — `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION`, on `POST /exemption-applications/{id}/submit`.
24. No competing live grant for the same parcel, programme and effective tax year — `ERR_DUPLICATE_GRANT`, on an approving determination.
25. Information requests are bounded — `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED`, on a determination requesting information.
26. Continuances are bounded — `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT_REACHED`, on an appeal decision that continues the hearing.
27. Docket capacity — `ERR_DOCKET_CAPACITY_REACHED`, on `POST /appeals/{id}/schedule`.
28. At most one appeal per notice — `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`, on `POST /appeals`.
29. Income certification is on file where the programme requires one — `ERR_INCOME_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, on an approving determination and on a reinstating compliance action.
30. Household income within the programme ceiling — `ERR_INCOME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on an approving determination.
31. Countable assets within the programme ceiling — `ERR_ASSET_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on an approving determination.
32. The portability allocation is positive — `ERR_PORTABLE_BALANCE_EXHAUSTED`, on `POST /exemption-grants/{id}/port`.
33. A recovery payment does not exceed the outstanding balance — `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, on a paying recovery action.
34. The substantiation risk threshold is met — `ERR_RISK_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, on a substantiating fraud-case decision.

One ordering inside rung 9 deserves emphasis, because it is easy to get backwards. For grants, the terminal-status check runs first: any compliance action or portability transfer addressed to a grant whose `benefit_status` is `TRANSFERRED`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` is rejected with `ERR_GRANT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and so is an approving retroactive-claim decision whose claimed grant sits in one of those three statuses. Only after a grant has been shown to be non-terminal does the system report `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE` (a portability transfer against a `SUSPENDED` grant) or `ERR_GRANT_NOT_SUSPENDED` (a reinstatement against an `ACTIVE` grant). A terminal grant therefore never yields either of those two errors.

### 2.11. The Conserved Relief Ledger

Each `TaxpayerAccount` carries a three-field money ledger that is the system's audit answer to the question "how much relief did this household actually receive?". `lifetime_refund_cents` accumulates the `total_payable_cents` of that taxpayer's approved retroactive claims, less any amount removed when such a claim is later reversed. `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` accumulates the amounts collected against that taxpayer's fraud cases, less any collections written back when a case is abated. `net_relief_cents` is `lifetime_refund_cents` minus `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents`, and it is re-derived every single time either component changes — never stored independently, never patched by hand. All three initialise to 0 when the account is opened.

Because the derived figure is recomputed rather than adjusted, compensating events restore it exactly. When an abatement writes off a recovery, the collected total drops by precisely what had been collected on that case and the net figure returns to its pre-assessment value; when a fraud substantiation reverses an approved refund, the refund total drops by precisely that claim's `total_payable_cents`. Every one of these writes is a mutation of the taxpayer account and advances its `version`.

The account's `status` is likewise derived from the taxpayer's compliance posture rather than set directly. An account opens in `GOOD_STANDING`. It becomes `FLAGGED` while any fraud case naming that taxpayer has an open investigation, and returns to `GOOD_STANDING` when no such case remains open. A substantiated fraud finding moves the account to `BARRED`, which is what later blocks new applications for that taxpayer.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Roll: Programmes, Parcels and the Ownership Registry

Nothing can be claimed until the statutory catalogue exists. A `ProgramAdministrator` registers each **relief programme** with its `program_code` — one of `HOMESTEAD_BASIC`, `SENIOR_FREEZE`, `VETERAN_DISABILITY`, `DISABILITY_INCOME` or `AGRICULTURAL_USE`, unique across programmes — a human-readable `name`, and the parameters that make the programme calculable. `relief_pct_bps` is the share of the assessed basis the programme exempts before any ceiling, expressed in basis points anywhere from 1 through 10000 inclusive. `max_exempt_value_cents` is the hard ceiling on the value one parcel may absorb from this programme and must not be negative. `priority_rank` places the programme in the parcel waterfall, from 1 through 99 inclusive and unique across programmes, with the lower rank applying first. `statutory_lookback_years` bounds retroactive reach at anywhere from 0 through 5 full tax years inclusive. `penalty_pct_bps` and `interest_pct_bps_per_year` — both non-negative — drive the recovery assessment when a grant under this programme turns out to have been improperly claimed. A programme is created with `status` set to `OPEN`; the enumeration also admits `CLOSED`, and only an `OPEN` programme accepts new submissions.

Four booleans and two ceilings decide how much scrutiny a programme carries, and each defaults to the permissive value when omitted. `income_limit_cents` is the annual household income ceiling and defaults to null, meaning the programme is not income-tested; `asset_limit_cents` is the countable-asset ceiling and likewise defaults to null, meaning no asset test. `requires_income_certification` defaults to false; when true, a certified income record for the application's tax year is a precondition of approval. `recertification_required` defaults to false; when true, the household must recertify income every year or the annual roll will suspend the grant. `freeze_enabled` defaults to false; when true, an approved grant freezes the parcel's assessed value at the figure observed at approval, and that frozen value becomes portable.

The parcel roll is the `AssessorAnalyst`'s responsibility. Creating a **parcel** takes its `apn` (the assessor parcel number, unique across the roll), a `situs_address_normalized` string that has been upper-cased and had its internal whitespace collapsed — this is the form every registry cross-match compares against — a `county_zone` of `NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST` or `WEST`, the `tax_year` the published values belong to (from 2015 through 2100 inclusive), the `base_year` the base-year value was established in (which must not be later than `tax_year`), the `base_year_value_cents` the assessment cap grows from, the current appraised `market_value_cents`, and optionally the `assessment_cap_pct_bps`. Both money figures must be non-negative. Creation immediately runs the value decomposition of §2.7 against an empty active-grant set, so a new parcel is published with its `capped_value_cents` and `assessed_value_cents` computed, `exempt_value_cents` at 0, `taxable_value_cents` equal to the assessed basis, and `exemption_status` reading `UNEXEMPTED`.

External data sources are represented by injected registry records, appended through an administrator-only registry-injection capability so that cross-matching has something to read. Each injected record names the `taxpayer_id` it describes, the `source` it came from, the `record_effective_date` it took effect on, and — depending on the source — either an `address_normalized` value or a `reported_income_cents` figure. Records are immutable and additive; the response echoes a `record_id` alongside the `taxpayer_id`, `source` and `record_effective_date`. When a cross-match later consults a source, it reads the record with the greatest `record_effective_date` for that taxpayer-and-source pair, breaking ties in favour of the most recently inserted record.

**Ownership and occupancy** is where residency verification gets its evidence. An `AssessorAnalyst` ingests an ownership record naming the `parcel_id` and the `taxpayer_id` it links, the `ownership_pct_bps` undivided share that taxpayer holds (from 1 through 10000 inclusive, and the figure that later pro-rates every benefit), the `deed_recorded_date`, the `occupancy_type` claimed for the interval, the inclusive `occupancy_start_date`, the `source_system` the record came from, and optionally an `occupancy_end_date`. Occupancy is one of `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE`, `SECONDARY`, `RENTAL` or `AGRICULTURAL`. The source is one of `DEED_REGISTRY`, `FIELD_INSPECTION` or `TAXPAYER_AFFIDAVIT`, and it decides trust: a record ingested from `DEED_REGISTRY` or `FIELD_INSPECTION` is created with `status` `VERIFIED`, while a record resting on a `TAXPAYER_AFFIDAVIT` is created `UNVERIFIED`. Only a `VERIFIED` record can support an approval. The third status, `SUPERSEDED`, is written when a later record closes this one out.

Occupancy intervals are **half-open**: an interval runs from its `occupancy_start_date` inclusive to its `occupancy_end_date` exclusive, and a null `occupancy_end_date` means the interval is still open-ended. When an end date is supplied it must fall strictly after the start date. Two geometry rules follow. A new `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` interval must not overlap an existing `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` interval on the same parcel; because the semantics are half-open, touching endpoints are fine — a record ending on the day the next one begins does not overlap — but any genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Separately, nobody may have two primary residences at once: a new `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` interval must not overlap another `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` interval held by the same taxpayer on a *different* parcel, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIMARY_RESIDENCE`. The parcel-level check is evaluated before the taxpayer-level one.

Ingestion also maintains history automatically. When an open-ended predecessor record exists for the same `parcel_id` and `taxpayer_id`, that predecessor is truncated — its `occupancy_end_date` is set to the new record's `occupancy_start_date` — and its `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. This happens whatever the predecessor's `occupancy_type` and whatever the new record's. Should more than one open-ended predecessor exist for that pair, the one truncated is the one with the greatest `occupancy_start_date`, with the earlier `created_at` breaking a tie.

Supersession can cost a taxpayer their exemption. If the newly ingested record carries an `occupancy_type` of `SECONDARY` or `RENTAL`, and the record it supersedes was the very record that supported an `ACTIVE` grant on that parcel for that taxpayer, and that grant's `exemption_type` is not `AGRICULTURAL_USE`, then the grant loses its factual basis. The consequences cascade in order: the predecessor record is truncated and marked `SUPERSEDED`; the grant's `benefit_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `OCCUPANCY_LOST`; the grant's departure from the active set re-runs the parcel value decomposition, rewriting `exempt_value_cents`, `taxable_value_cents` and the `applied_exempt_value_cents` of the grants that remain, re-deriving `exemption_status` and incrementing the parcel's `version` by 1; and a notice of type `SUSPENSION` is mailed to the taxpayer against that grant, appealable, with its `appeal_deadline` set 30 days past the `mailed_at` instant.

### 3.2. Intake: Accounts, Applications and Income Certifications

An `IntakeClerk` opens a **taxpayer account** for each claimant, recording the `full_name`, the `date_of_birth` as a calendar date (this is the figure the senior age test reads), the `ssn_last4` — exactly four digits of the taxpayer identification number, used for registry cross-matching — the `mailing_address_normalized` address of record, and optionally the `disability_rating_pct_bps`. The disability rating is an adjudicated service-connected rating in basis points from 0 through 10000 inclusive; it defaults to 0 and, for veteran claims only, it multiplies the benefit. The account opens in `GOOD_STANDING` with `lifetime_refund_cents`, `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` and `net_relief_cents` all at 0, as described in §2.11.

Creating an **exemption application** is likewise clerk work. The payload names the `parcel_id` the exemption is claimed on, the `taxpayer_id` of the claimant, the `program_id` of the classification applied for, the `tax_year` claimed (2015 through 2100 inclusive), the `claimed_occupancy_date` the claimant asserts occupancy began, and the `claimed_address_normalized` address the claimant asserts as their primary residence — the string that registry cross-matching will be compared against. The application is created in `DRAFT` with `info_request_round` at 0, `determined_by_user_id`, `denial_reason` and `resulting_grant_id` all null, and `intake_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`. That stamp is the duty-separation bar of §2.8: whoever keyed the application is thereafter disqualified from determining it.

Submission moves the application from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and only from `DRAFT` — a submission attempt against any other status is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT`. Three further conditions apply. The programme applied for must still be `OPEN`. The claimant's account must not be `BARRED`; both `GOOD_STANDING` and `FLAGGED` accounts may submit, but a barred taxpayer is turned away with `ERR_TAXPAYER_BARRED` — being flagged for an open investigation slows a household down, being barred by a substantiated finding stops it. And no other application may already be live for the same `parcel_id`, `program_id` and `tax_year` combination, where "live" means an application whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION`. A withdrawn `DRAFT` or a `DENIED` predecessor does not block a fresh claim. Submission is a mutation of the application and increments its `version` by 1; verification is deliberately *not* run here, because the clerk or reviewer records each cross-match explicitly.

Income-tested programmes need an **income certification** on file. A clerk files one against a `taxpayer_id` for a `certification_year` (2015 through 2100 inclusive), declaring the `household_income_cents` earned in that year, the `countable_asset_cents` held outside the residence, the `household_size` (from 1 through 20 inclusive members), and the `recertification_due_date`, an inclusive calendar date by which the next certification must be on file. Both money figures must be non-negative. The certification is created with `status` `PENDING`, its `relief_tier` reading `NOT_ASSESSED` and its `relief_multiplier_bps` at 0; the tier remains unassessed until a determination consumes the record. The full tier enumeration is `NOT_ASSESSED`, `TIER_FULL`, `TIER_HIGH` and `TIER_PARTIAL`, and the multiplier is always one of 0, 5000, 7500 or 10000 basis points. A certification's lifecycle admits four states in all — `PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, `LAPSED` and `REVOKED` — and both `LAPSED` and `REVOKED` are terminal.

### 3.3. Cross-Matching Against External Registries

Verification is recorded one source at a time. Either an `IntakeClerk` or an `ExemptionsReviewer` records a **verification check** against an application by naming a single `source`: `VOTER_REGISTRY`, `DMV`, `USPS_NCOA`, `INCOME_DOR` or `DEED_REGISTRY`. Everything else about the check is computed by the server, and the resulting row is immutable — checks accumulate rather than overwrite, which is what lets an investigation later replay the whole history. Each check records the `checked_at` instant from the logical clock, the `expected_value` the application asserted, the `observed_value` the registry actually held, and a `result`.

The comparison depends on the source. For `VOTER_REGISTRY`, `DMV`, `USPS_NCOA` and `DEED_REGISTRY`, the server takes the latest injected registry record for the pair of the application's `taxpayer_id` and the named source, and compares its address against the application's `claimed_address_normalized`. For `INCOME_DOR` the comparison is monetary: the server selects the income certification whose `taxpayer_id` matches the application's claimant and whose `certification_year` equals the application's `tax_year` and whose `status` is either `PENDING` or `CERTIFIED`, choosing the row with the greatest `created_at` and breaking a tie in favour of the greatest `version`, and compares that certification's `household_income_cents` against the income the revenue department reported. Agreement writes `result` `MATCH`; disagreement writes `MISMATCH`. `NO_RECORD` is written when no registry record exists at all for that taxpayer-and-source pair, and also when the source is `INCOME_DOR` and no income certification satisfies the selector above — there is then nothing to compare the reported income against, and `observed_value` is the empty string.

Two transitions ride on the act of recording a check. The **first** check recorded for an application auto-transitions that application from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and increments its `version` by 1; recording a second or later check leaves the status untouched at `UNDER_REVIEW`. Separately, an `INCOME_DOR` check whose `result` is `MATCH` certifies income: the very certification the comparison selected moves from `PENDING` to `CERTIFIED` and its `version` increments by 1. A `MISMATCH` or `NO_RECORD` result from `INCOME_DOR` leaves the certification `PENDING`, which later blocks approval of any income-tested programme.

The two sources that gate approval are `VOTER_REGISTRY` and `DEED_REGISTRY`. For each of those two independently, at least one check row must exist for the application and the **latest** row for that source — latest by `checked_at` descending, with the later `created_at` breaking a tie — must read `MATCH`. A `MISMATCH` that was subsequently corrected by a fresh check therefore does not block approval; a `MISMATCH` or `NO_RECORD` standing as the latest word on either source does, and the approval is refused with `ERR_RESIDENCY_VERIFICATION_FAILED`. The `DMV`, `USPS_NCOA` and `INCOME_DOR` sources never gate approval directly — their contribution is to fraud screening, described in §3.8.

### 3.4. Determination: Approval, Denial and the Request for Information

Determination is the pivot of the whole system, and it belongs exclusively to an `ExemptionsReviewer` acting on an application whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW` — any other status is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. Every determination request carries an `outcome` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_INFO`, together with the `expected_parcel_version` stale-version proof described in §2.9; a denial may also carry a `denial_reason` note. Whatever the outcome, the acting reviewer must not be the clerk recorded in `intake_user_id`, and the application's `version` increments by 1.

The mildest outcome is a **request for information**, which returns the application from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED` and increments its `info_request_round` by 1. A reviewer may do this at most twice: the round counter must be below 2 when the request is issued, so the third attempt is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED`. A request for information leaves `determined_by_user_id` alone — no determination has been made — and, unlike an approval, it does **not** increment the parcel's `version`, because no grant and no value has changed. The taxpayer closes the cycle by answering: a `Taxpayer` acting on their own application supplies a fresh `claimed_address_normalized` and `claimed_occupancy_date`, which replace the values previously asserted, and the application returns from `INFO_REQUESTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` with its `version` incremented by 1. Answering an application that is not in `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`.

A **denial** records the supplied `denial_reason` on the application, stamps `determined_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and moves the status to `DENIED`. Two things then follow in order: a notice is created with `notice_type` `DENIAL`, `subject_type` `EXEMPTION_APPLICATION`, `subject_id` equal to the application's own identifier, `taxpayer_id` copied from the application, `mailed_at` stamped from the logical clock and `appealable` true; and that notice's `appeal_deadline` is computed as its `mailed_at` instant plus a thirty-day appeal window, which is what later decides whether an appeal against the denial is timely. As with a request for information, a denial does not increment the parcel's `version`. A denial is not the end of the road: should a hearing officer later grant an appeal against that denial notice, the application returns from `DENIED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, its `denial_reason` is cleared back to null and its `determined_by_user_id` is cleared back to null as well, so the reviewer whose denial was reversed is no longer recorded as the decider, and the application's `version` increments by 1. The `info_request_round` counter, however, is preserved across such a reopening — a reopened application that had already used both information requests cannot ask for a third. `DENIED` is therefore not a terminal status for an application; `APPROVED` is the only terminal one, and an approved application is never re-determined.

An **approval** is the only path that mints a grant, and it must clear a long line of gates. In the order they are evaluated: verified ownership, then residency cross-matching, then programme-specific eligibility, then grant exclusivity, then the certification requirement, then the income ceiling, then the asset ceiling.

The ownership gate requires a `VERIFIED` ownership record linking the application's `taxpayer_id` to its `parcel_id`, whose `occupancy_type` is what the programme demands — `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` for `HOMESTEAD_BASIC`, `SENIOR_FREEZE`, `VETERAN_DISABILITY` and `DISABILITY_INCOME`, and `AGRICULTURAL` for `AGRICULTURAL_USE` — and whose half-open interval covers January 1 of the application's `tax_year`. Absent such a record, the approval is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_NOT_VERIFIED`. Residency cross-matching is the `VOTER_REGISTRY` and `DEED_REGISTRY` latest-check rule of §3.3, refused with `ERR_RESIDENCY_VERIFICATION_FAILED`. Programme eligibility depends on the classification: a `SENIOR_FREEZE` claim requires the claimant to reach the age of 65 on or before January 1 of the claimed `tax_year`, measured from `date_of_birth`; a `VETERAN_DISABILITY` claim requires a `disability_rating_pct_bps` strictly greater than 0; an `AGRICULTURAL_USE` claim requires the agricultural occupancy record just described. A failure here is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Grant exclusivity forbids approval when a grant already exists for the same `parcel_id`, `program_id` and `effective_tax_year` whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — a suspended grant still occupies the slot — and the collision is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_GRANT`.

The remaining three gates concern income. When the programme's `requires_income_certification` is true, a certification must exist for the claimant with `certification_year` equal to the application's `tax_year` and `status` `CERTIFIED`; otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_INCOME_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Where more than one such certification exists, the one with the greatest `created_at` is the one consumed. From it the system derives the **income relief tier** by exact integer comparison, never by computing a decimal ratio: when twice the certified `household_income_cents` is no greater than the programme's `income_limit_cents` — that is, when income is at or below half the ceiling — the tier is `TIER_FULL` and the `relief_multiplier_bps` is 10000; otherwise, when four times the income is no greater than three times the limit — income at or below three quarters of the ceiling — the tier is `TIER_HIGH` at 7500 basis points; otherwise, when the income is no greater than the limit itself, the tier is `TIER_PARTIAL` at 5000 basis points; and when the income strictly exceeds the limit the approval is refused with `ERR_INCOME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Every band boundary is inclusive at its upper edge, so income exactly equal to the ceiling yields `TIER_PARTIAL`, and income one cent above the ceiling is rejected. The asset test is a separate gate evaluated only after the tier has resolved: when the programme declares an `asset_limit_cents`, a certified `countable_asset_cents` strictly greater than that limit is refused with `ERR_ASSET_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. When a programme is not income-tested, no certification is consumed and the grant's `relief_multiplier_bps` is simply 10000.

With every gate cleared, the system computes the grant's **benefit amount** in five exact-arithmetic steps followed by a single rounding. First, the statutory share: the parcel's `assessed_value_cents` — as it stands after the cap-and-freeze step of the decomposition for the current tax year — multiplied by the programme's `relief_pct_bps` and divided by ten thousand. Second, the income tier: that figure multiplied by the grant's `relief_multiplier_bps` and divided by ten thousand, where the multiplier is 10000 for any programme that is not income-tested. Third, the disability adjustment: the running figure multiplied by the claimant's `disability_rating_pct_bps` and divided by ten thousand when and only when the programme's `program_code` is `VETERAN_DISABILITY`, and multiplied by ten thousand basis points — that is, left unchanged — for every other classification. Fourth, the programme ceiling: the running figure is replaced by the smaller of itself and `max_exempt_value_cents`. Fifth, the ownership share: the result is multiplied by the grant's `ownership_pct_bps_snapshot` and divided by ten thousand. Only then, sixth, is the figure rounded half up to a whole cent and written to `benefit_amount_cents`. No intermediate step is rounded — the ceiling comparison in step four is made against the unrounded value — and the order of steps four and five is pinned: the ceiling applies *before* the ownership pro-ration, never after, because applying it afterwards would hand a fractional owner a larger benefit than the statute allows.

The approval then writes the grant and cascades outward. A new grant row is created with `benefit_status` `ACTIVE`, its `application_id`, `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id` and `program_id` copied from the application, its `ported_from_grant_id` null (this grant was determined, not transferred), its `exemption_type` snapshotting the programme's `program_code` at approval, its `effective_tax_year` set to the application's `tax_year`, its `ownership_pct_bps_snapshot` taken from the `ownership_pct_bps` of the verified ownership record that satisfied the gate, its `relief_multiplier_bps` taken from the resolved income tier or 10000, its `first_eligible_tax_year` set to the calendar year of that verified record's `occupancy_start_date` — the floor a retroactive claim may later reach back to — and its `benefit_amount_cents` as just computed. When the programme has `freeze_enabled` true, both `frozen_value_cents` and `portable_balance_cents` are set to the parcel's `assessed_value_cents` as observed at approval; for a programme without a freeze both fields remain null. The application's `resulting_grant_id` is stamped with the new grant's identifier, mirroring the grant's own `application_id`, and `determined_by_user_id` records the deciding reviewer.

Three further consequences follow, in order. The new grant entering the active set re-runs the parcel value decomposition, rewriting `capped_value_cents`, `assessed_value_cents`, `exempt_value_cents`, `taxable_value_cents` and the `applied_exempt_value_cents` of every `ACTIVE` grant on that parcel. The rewritten exempt total re-derives the parcel's `exemption_status` and increments the parcel's `version` by 1. And where an income certification was consumed, that certification's `relief_tier` and `relief_multiplier_bps` are written from the tier just resolved — the certification is stamped with the finding, which is why the same record read after determination is no longer `NOT_ASSESSED`.

### 3.5. The Supervised Life of a Grant

A grant is not a certificate filed and forgotten; it is a live claim on the roll that the county keeps testing. Its `benefit_status` moves through five values — `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED`, `TRANSFERRED` and `LAPSED` — of which the last three are terminal, and only an `ACTIVE` grant enters the value waterfall. While suspended, a grant records why in its `suspension_reason`, which is one of `FRAUD_INVESTIGATION`, `RECERTIFICATION_LAPSE` or `OCCUPANCY_LOST` and is null whenever the grant is not suspended.

Three events suspend a grant, and each writes its own reason. Opening a fraud investigation against the grant suspends it with `FRAUD_INVESTIGATION` (§3.8). Ingesting an ownership record that supersedes the supporting occupancy with a secondary or rental use suspends it with `OCCUPANCY_LOST` (§3.1). And the annual roll suspends it with `RECERTIFICATION_LAPSE` when the household's income recertification has gone missing. In all three cases the mechanics are identical: the grant leaves the active set, the parcel value decomposition re-runs for `parcel_id`, the parcel's `exemption_status` is re-derived and its `version` increments by 1, and — for the fraud and occupancy paths — an appealable `SUSPENSION` notice is mailed against the grant with a thirty-day appeal deadline.

Reinstatement is the reverse edge, and it belongs to an `ExemptionsReviewer` taking a compliance action naming an `action` of `REINSTATE` and optionally a `reason`. The grant must currently be `SUSPENDED`: an attempt against an `ACTIVE` grant is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_SUSPENDED`, and one against a grant already in a terminal status is refused with `ERR_GRANT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, which as §2.10 explains is evaluated first. No fraud case naming that taxpayer may have an open investigation, or the request is refused with `ERR_FRAUD_HOLD_ACTIVE`. And where the grant was suspended for `RECERTIFICATION_LAPSE`, the household must have put a `CERTIFIED` income certification back on file whose `certification_year` is at least the grant's `effective_tax_year`; without one the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_INCOME_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. A successful reinstatement returns `benefit_status` to `ACTIVE`, clears `suspension_reason` back to null, and re-runs the parcel value decomposition so the restored benefit reappears in `exempt_value_cents`, with the parcel's `exemption_status` re-derived and its `version` incremented by 1.

The same compliance capability, with an `action` of `REVOKE`, ends a grant outright. Revocation is admissible from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED`, moves `benefit_status` to the terminal `REVOKED` and clears `suspension_reason`. On the direct path the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the identifier of the fraud investigator who opened any case on this grant. Revocation re-runs the parcel value decomposition, increments the parcel's `version` by 1, and mails a notice with `notice_type` `REVOCATION` and `subject_type` `EXEMPTION_GRANT` against the grant, whose `appeal_deadline` again sits thirty days past `mailed_at`.

The **annual assessment roll** is the county's yearly sweep, run by an `AssessorAnalyst` against a `target_tax_year`. It does three things in order and reports how much of each it did, returning the `target_tax_year` alongside a `parcels_rolled_count`, a `certifications_lapsed_count`, a `grants_suspended_count` and a `grants_lapsed_count`. First, every parcel whose `tax_year` is earlier than the target has its `tax_year` set to the target and its value decomposition re-run — which is where the assessment cap earns another year of growth — with each rolled parcel's `version` incrementing by 1. Second, income certifications go stale: a `CERTIFIED` certification whose `recertification_due_date` falls strictly before the logical clock's calendar date, and for whose household no `CERTIFIED` certification exists with a `certification_year` at or beyond the target year, becomes `LAPSED`. Third, that lapse propagates: every `ACTIVE` grant of that household under a programme whose `recertification_required` is true is suspended with `suspension_reason` `RECERTIFICATION_LAPSE`, which in turn re-runs the value decomposition of each affected parcel and increments each parcel's `version` by 1.

Patience is finite. A grant already sitting `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `RECERTIFICATION_LAPSE` when a *second* roll finds the household still without a `CERTIFIED` certification for the new target year moves to the terminal `LAPSED` status, with `suspension_reason` cleared, and the value decomposition re-runs once more for its parcel with the parcel's `version` incrementing by 1. Unlike suspension and revocation, lapsing mails no notice — the household was warned by the suspension notice a roll earlier. A `LAPSED` grant is final; the household's route back onto the roll is a fresh application.

Reads exist for every one of these outcomes, because most grants in this system are created or mutated by cascade rather than by a direct request. A grant may be read individually, and grants may be listed and narrowed by `parcel_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `application_id`, `program_id`, `ported_from_grant_id` or `benefit_status`, which is how a caller discovers the grant a determination minted, the child grants a portability transfer carved out, or the set of suspensions a roll produced. A taxpayer's income certifications are likewise listable so that cascade-certified and cascade-lapsed rows can be found, and an application's verification checks are listable so that the whole cross-match history — including superseded rows — can be replayed.

### 3.6. Portability: Moving a Valuation Freeze to a New Home

A household that has held a frozen assessment for years and then moves should not lose the benefit of that freeze, and statute lets them carry it across. Only grants under a freeze-enabled programme have anything to carry: at approval such a grant recorded both `frozen_value_cents`, the assessed value locked in at that moment, and `portable_balance_cents`, initially the same figure, representing how much of the freeze has not yet been transferred away. For a grant under a programme without a freeze, both fields are null and portability is meaningless.

An `ExemptionsReviewer` transfers a freeze by naming the `destination_parcel_id`, the `transfer_value_cents` they wish to move, and the `expected_grant_version` proof of §2.9. The source grant must be `ACTIVE` — a transfer attempt against a `SUSPENDED` grant is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and one against a `TRANSFERRED`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` grant with `ERR_GRANT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — and its `portable_balance_cents` must not be null. No fraud case naming the grant's taxpayer may have an open investigation, or the transfer is refused with `ERR_FRAUD_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The destination must genuinely be the household's new home: a `VERIFIED` ownership record with `occupancy_type` `PRIMARY_RESIDENCE` must link the grant's `taxpayer_id` to the destination parcel and cover the transfer date, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_OWNED`.

The amount actually moved is the smallest of three figures: the `transfer_value_cents` requested, the source grant's remaining `portable_balance_cents`, and the destination parcel's `assessed_value_cents`. That third ceiling is read from the destination parcel **before** the new grant is inserted, so the freeze is capped by the destination's pre-transfer basis; re-reading it afterwards would let the freeze cap itself and is wrong. Since every term is already a whole number of cents, no rounding occurs. If the resulting allocation is zero or less — the minimum meaningful allocation being one cent — the transfer is refused with `ERR_PORTABLE_BALANCE_EXHAUSTED`.

A successful transfer creates a new grant on the destination parcel. Its `ported_from_grant_id` points at the source grant, making the portability chain traceable; its `frozen_value_cents` is the allocated amount and its own `portable_balance_cents` is 0, so a ported freeze cannot be ported onward; its `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE`. It carries forward the source grant's `application_id`, `taxpayer_id`, `program_id`, `exemption_type`, `effective_tax_year`, `first_eligible_tax_year` and `relief_multiplier_bps`, takes its `ownership_pct_bps_snapshot` from the `ownership_pct_bps` of the destination's verified primary-residence record, names the destination in its `parcel_id`, and has its `benefit_amount_cents` computed by the six-step benefit formula of §3.4 against the destination parcel. Step one of that formula reads the destination's **pre-transfer** assessed basis — the very quantity that supplied the third allocation ceiling above, read before the new grant is inserted — and not the possibly lower basis the closing decomposition of this same request leaves on the destination once the freeze it has just received applies. The figure is written once, when the child grant is inserted, and no later decomposition rewrites it.

On the source side, `portable_balance_cents` is reduced by the allocated amount and the source grant's `version` increments by 1. When the reduction leaves the balance at exactly 0 the whole freeze has been moved, and the source grant's `benefit_status` becomes the terminal `TRANSFERRED`, removing it from its parcel's active set. A **partial** transfer — one whose allocation is smaller than the balance — leaves the source grant `ACTIVE` with a reduced `portable_balance_cents`, and the household may transfer the remainder later. Finally, the value decomposition re-runs for both parcels, the source losing a grant and the destination gaining one, and each parcel's `version` increments by 1.

One quantity is conserved by construction and worth stating as an invariant: for any freeze-enabled grant, its remaining `portable_balance_cents` plus the sum of `frozen_value_cents` across every child grant that names it in `ported_from_grant_id` always equals its original `frozen_value_cents`. Frozen value is moved, never created.

### 3.7. Retroactive Claims and Refund Settlement

An exemption discovered late is worth money for the years it should have applied to, and a **retroactive claim** is how a household asks for it. Either the `Taxpayer` themselves or an `IntakeClerk` on their behalf files a claim naming the `grant_id` the benefit is claimed under, the `filing_tax_year` the claim is filed in, and the `requested_start_tax_year` the claimant asks to be refunded from. Both years fall between 2015 and 2100 inclusive, and the requested start must not be later than the filing year.

Filing resolves the eligible window first. The last eligible year is always the `filing_tax_year` minus one, because the current year is served by the grant itself and needs no refund; it is written to `last_eligible_tax_year`. The first eligible year is the **greatest** of three floors: the filing year minus the programme's `statutory_lookback_years`, the grant's own `first_eligible_tax_year`, and the `requested_start_tax_year` — whichever binds hardest wins — and it is written to `first_eligible_tax_year`. The window is a closed interval including both ends, and `eligible_year_count` is the last eligible year minus the first, plus one. Should the first eligible year come out later than the last, no year survives and the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLAIM_YEARS`. Two related guards apply at filing: a `requested_start_tax_year` earlier than the filing year minus the programme's `statutory_lookback_years` is refused with `ERR_STATUTORY_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDED`, and a claim whose eligible interval intersects the eligible interval of an existing `PENDING` or `APPROVED` claim on the same grant — closed-interval intersection, so sharing even a single year collides — is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_YEARS_OVERLAP`.

Filing then computes the **provisional gross** figure. Each eligible year's tax saving is the grant's `benefit_amount_cents` multiplied by the county mill rate of 125 basis points — one and a quarter percent of exempted value — divided by ten thousand and rounded half up to a whole cent. That per-year rounding happens before anything is added together; the sum of the per-year savings across every year in the window is written to `gross_refund_cents`. At filing the claim's `status` is `PENDING`, and the provisional variant deliberately ignores the household's outstanding recovery balance and adds no interest, leaving `offset_applied_cents`, `net_refund_cents`, `refund_interest_cents` and `total_payable_cents` all at 0.

Adjudication belongs to an `ExemptionsReviewer`, who supplies an `action` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and optionally a `note`. The claim must still be `PENDING`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDING`. The reviewer must not be the one whose `determined_by_user_id` is recorded on the application behind the claimed grant. Approval additionally requires that the grant still support a refund: its `benefit_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, since a `REVOKED`, `TRANSFERRED` or `LAPSED` grant cannot — such a claim is refused with `ERR_GRANT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — and no fraud case naming the grant's taxpayer may have an open investigation, or approval is refused with `ERR_FRAUD_HOLD_ACTIVE`.

Approval runs the **final** refund variant, whose six steps must be evaluated in the order given. Step one recomputes the per-year savings and their gross sum exactly as at filing. Step two reads the household's outstanding recovery: the sum, across every fraud case of that taxpayer whose `recovery_status` is `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, of that case's `total_assessed_cents` less its `recovery_paid_cents`. Cases in `NONE`, `PAID` or `ABATED` contribute nothing, and the figure is read as frozen at substantiation — recovery interest is never re-accrued during claim adjudication. Step three runs the offset waterfall: the amount available to absorb starts as the smaller of the gross refund and the outstanding recovery, and the years are walked in **ascending** tax-year order so the oldest year absorbs first; each year absorbs the smaller of the amount still available and that year's saving, and what remains of that year's saving after absorption is its refundable remainder. The total absorbed across all years is written to `offset_applied_cents`. Step four computes statutory refund interest on the un-offset remainders only: for each year, the refundable remainder multiplied by the refund interest rate of 400 basis points per year — four percent — multiplied by the number of years between that tax year and the `filing_tax_year`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent, with each year rounded before summing. Step five sums the refundable remainders into `net_refund_cents` and the per-year interest amounts into `refund_interest_cents`. Step six adds those two into `total_payable_cents`. Interest is computed after the offset and never on an absorbed amount; reversing the offset and interest steps would inflate the payable figure and is explicitly not the pinned order. For identical inputs, therefore, the final variant yields a strictly smaller principal than the provisional one whenever the household owes an outstanding recovery.

Approval then moves money in four ordered cascades. The claim becomes `APPROVED`. The taxpayer's `lifetime_refund_cents` rises by the claim's `total_payable_cents`, and the changed component re-derives `net_relief_cents`. Where `offset_applied_cents` is greater than 0, the absorbed amount is credited against the household's outstanding fraud cases in ascending `assessment_tax_year` order, with the earlier `created_at` breaking a tie: each case's `recovery_paid_cents` rises and its `recovery_status` advances to `PARTIALLY_PAID` or, when the credit closes the balance exactly, to `PAID`; the taxpayer's `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` rises by the total credited and `net_relief_cents` is re-derived again. Finally a notice with `notice_type` `REFUND_DETERMINATION` and `subject_type` `RETROACTIVE_CLAIM` is mailed against the claim with `appealable` **false** — an approved refund is not an adverse action and there is nothing to challenge.

A denial is simpler and, unlike an approval, is appealable. The claim becomes `DENIED`, its `net_refund_cents`, `refund_interest_cents` and `total_payable_cents` all stay at 0, and a `REFUND_DETERMINATION` notice is mailed against the claim with `appealable` true and an `appeal_deadline` thirty days past `mailed_at`. Both `DENIED` and `REVERSED` are terminal for a claim; the fourth status, `REVERSED`, is reached only by the fraud cascade described next.

### 3.8. Fraud Detection, Recovery Assessment and Collection

Exemption fraud in this domain is nearly always a residency lie, and it shows up as disagreement between registries. A `FraudInvestigator` opens a **fraud case** naming the `taxpayer_id` under investigation, the `grant_id` alleged to have been improperly claimed, the `improper_start_tax_year` and `improper_end_tax_year` bounding the years alleged to be improper (the start must not be later than the end), and the `assessment_tax_year` the recovery will be assessed in. The case is created with `opened_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and with two status fields set independently: `investigation_status` at `OPEN` and `recovery_status` at `NONE`. A newly opened case has no money on it and no replayed score: `back_tax_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `interest_cents`, `total_assessed_cents`, `recovery_paid_cents` and `adjudication_risk_score` all start at 0, and `substantiated_by_user_id` starts null. Only adjudication writes the score, and only substantiation writes the ledger.

That pair is the unusual shape of this entity, and it matters. `investigation_status` walks the adjudication axis through `OPEN`, `SUBSTANTIATED` and `CLEARED`; `recovery_status` walks the money axis through `NONE`, `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID` and `ABATED`. The two advance on separate axes on the same row: a substantiated case keeps that `investigation_status` forever while its `recovery_status` walks from `ASSESSED` through `PARTIALLY_PAID` to `PAID`, or jumps to `ABATED`. A cleared case is finished; so is a substantiated case whose recovery has reached `PAID` or `ABATED`. Abatement settles money, not guilt — `investigation_status` remains `SUBSTANTIATED` after an abatement — and collecting money never touches the adjudication axis.

Opening a case is itself an enforcement act. The addressed grant is immediately suspended with `suspension_reason` `FRAUD_INVESTIGATION`, which re-runs the parcel value decomposition, re-derives the parcel's `exemption_status` and increments the parcel's `version` by 1; the taxpayer's account `status` becomes `FLAGGED`; and an appealable `SUSPENSION` notice with `subject_type` `EXEMPTION_GRANT` is mailed against the grant with an `appeal_deadline` thirty days past `mailed_at`.

Opening also computes a **risk score**, written to `screening_risk_score`. The score combines four factors, each of which is worth either 100 or nothing, weighted in basis points that sum to ten thousand: a registration address mismatch against the voter registry or the DMV carries 3500 basis points; multiple concurrent homestead claims by the same taxpayer on distinct parcels carry 3000; a postal forwarding order carries 1500; and income under-reporting against the revenue department record carries 2000. Each factor's value is multiplied by its weight, the four products are added, the total is divided by ten thousand, and the result is rounded half up to a whole integer, yielding a score between 0 and 100 inclusive. The evidence for the mismatch factors is the verification-check log of *every* application belonging to that taxpayer — not merely the application behind the alleged grant — where an address mismatch is a `MISMATCH` result from `VOTER_REGISTRY` or `DMV`, a postal forwarding order is a `MISMATCH` from `USPS_NCOA`, and income under-reporting is a `MISMATCH` from `INCOME_DOR`. A `NO_RECORD` result never counts as a mismatch. The multiple-claims factor is different in kind: it fires when the taxpayer holds grants whose `exemption_type` is `HOMESTEAD_BASIC` and whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` across at least two distinct parcels.

The score is computed twice in a case's life, by two variants that differ **only** in how they read the check log. The screening variant, run at case opening, collapses the log to the latest check per source — latest by `checked_at` descending, with the later `created_at` breaking a tie — and treats a source as mismatched only when that latest check reads `MISMATCH`. A subsequent `MATCH` therefore hides an earlier `MISMATCH`, so a corrected registry record drives the screening score down. The adjudication variant, run at case decision, instead walks every check ever recorded in `checked_at` ascending order and latches a mismatch permanently, superseded rows included; it therefore yields a strictly higher score than the screening variant whenever a mismatch was later corrected. Neither variant consults any row's `id`.

Adjudication belongs to a `FraudInvestigator` supplying an `action` of `SUBSTANTIATE` or `CLEAR` and optionally `findings`. The case's `investigation_status` must still be `OPEN`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. Either action recomputes the adjudication-variant score and writes it to `adjudication_risk_score`, so even a cleared case keeps the replayed figure for the audit record. Substantiation additionally demands that the replayed score be at least the substantiation threshold of 60 — below that the request is refused with `ERR_RISK_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` — and that the `improper_end_tax_year` not fall after the `assessment_tax_year`.

**Clearing** a case compensates the suspension it caused: the suspended grant returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` cleared, the parcel value decomposition re-runs and the parcel's `version` increments by 1, and the taxpayer's account returns to `GOOD_STANDING` provided no other case of theirs still has an open investigation. `recovery_status` stays at `NONE` — a cleared case never assesses anything.

**Substantiating** a case stamps `substantiated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — the bar that later keeps the same investigator away from the money — and then assesses the recovery. The assessment walks every tax year in the closed interval from `improper_start_tax_year` through `improper_end_tax_year` and computes three components for each year, in this pinned order and with each rounded to whole cents *before* anything is summed. That year's back tax is the grant's `benefit_amount_cents` as stored at substantiation, multiplied by the county mill rate of 125 basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. That year's penalty is derived from the **already rounded** back tax of that same year, multiplied by the programme's `penalty_pct_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. That year's interest is likewise derived from the already rounded back tax, multiplied by the programme's `interest_pct_bps_per_year`, multiplied by the number of whole years from that tax year to the `assessment_tax_year`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — simple accrual, never compounded, so the year immediately before the assessment year accrues exactly one year of interest and the assessment year itself accrues none. The three per-year series are then summed into `back_tax_cents`, `penalty_cents` and `interest_cents`, and their total into `total_assessed_cents`. Deriving the penalty from an unrounded back tax, or summing exact products and rounding once at the end, produces cent-level differences and is wrong. The `total_assessed_cents` figure is frozen at this moment and is never re-accrued afterwards, however long collection takes.

Substantiation then cascades widely, in order. The recovery ledger opens: `back_tax_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `interest_cents` and `total_assessed_cents` are written and `recovery_status` advances from `NONE` to `ASSESSED`. The alleged grant — already `SUSPENDED` since the case was opened — is forced to `REVOKED`. The revoked grant re-runs the parcel value decomposition, rewriting `exempt_value_cents` and `taxable_value_cents` and incrementing the parcel's `version` by 1. Every `APPROVED` retroactive claim on that grant becomes `REVERSED`, which reduces the taxpayer's `lifetime_refund_cents` by that claim's `total_payable_cents` and re-derives `net_relief_cents`. The taxpayer's account `status` becomes `BARRED`. Every `CERTIFIED` income certification of that taxpayer whose `certification_year` falls inside the closed improper-year interval becomes `REVOKED`, with its `relief_tier` and `relief_multiplier_bps` preserved for audit rather than reset. And an appealable notice with `notice_type` `RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT` and `subject_type` `FRAUD_CASE` is mailed against the case, with an `appeal_deadline` thirty days past `mailed_at`.

Collection is a `RecoveryOfficer`'s work and is deliberately kept away from the investigator: a recovery action whose acting `X-User-ID` equals the case's `substantiated_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The action names either `PAY` with an `amount_cents`, or `ABATE`, optionally with a `reason`. In both cases the case's `recovery_status` must be `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; a case still at `NONE` has no assessment to act on and a case already `PAID` or `ABATED` has a closed balance, and either is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_ASSESSED`.

A payment must not exceed the outstanding balance, which is `total_assessed_cents` less `recovery_paid_cents`; an excess is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A payment below the outstanding balance raises `recovery_paid_cents` by the `amount_cents` paid and leaves `recovery_status` at `PARTIALLY_PAID`; a payment exactly equal to the outstanding balance brings `recovery_paid_cents` up to `total_assessed_cents` and advances `recovery_status` to `PAID`. Either way the taxpayer's `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` rises by the same amount and `net_relief_cents` is re-derived, and `investigation_status` is untouched.

An **abatement** writes off the whole assessed balance: `recovery_status` becomes `ABATED` and `recovery_paid_cents` is reset to 0. The compensation is exact — the taxpayer's `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` is reduced by precisely the amount previously collected on this case, and `net_relief_cents` is re-derived back to its pre-assessment figure.

### 3.9. Notices, Appeal Rights and the Hearing Docket

Every adverse action in this system generates a **notice**, and no request creates one directly — notices are always the by-product of the action they describe. A notice records the `taxpayer_id` of the noticed party, a `notice_type` of `DENIAL`, `SUSPENSION`, `REVOCATION`, `RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT` or `REFUND_DETERMINATION`, the `mailed_at` instant taken from the logical clock, a short machine-generated `body_summary` describing the noticed action, and a polymorphic subject. The subject is a pair: `subject_type` names the entity kind, and `subject_id` resolves against that kind — to an exemption application when `subject_type` is `EXEMPTION_APPLICATION`, to an exemption grant when `EXEMPTION_GRANT`, to a retroactive claim when `RETROACTIVE_CLAIM`, and to a fraud case when `FRAUD_CASE`. Notices are readable as a list per taxpayer, narrowable by `notice_type`, `subject_type` and `subject_id`, which is how a household discovers the deadlines attached to actions taken against it.

Appealability is recorded on the notice itself. The `appealable` flag is true for every notice except one case: a `REFUND_DETERMINATION` notice issued on an *approved* retroactive claim is not appealable, because a granted refund is not an adverse action. Where a notice is appealable, its `appeal_deadline` is its `mailed_at` instant plus a thirty-day appeal window, and the boundary is inclusive — an appeal filed at exactly the deadline instant is still timely, while one filed strictly after it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`. An attempt to appeal a notice whose `appealable` flag is false is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_APPEALABLE`.

A `Taxpayer` files an **appeal** against their own notice by supplying the `notice_id` and a statement of `grounds`. The appeal is created with `status` `FILED`, its `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, its `taxpayer_id` copied from the notice, its `continuance_count` at 0, and its `docket_id`, `hearing_start_utc`, `hearing_end_utc` and `decided_by_user_id` all null. Only one appeal may exist per notice; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`.

Hearings are held on dockets opened by a `HearingClerk`. A **hearing docket** names its `docket_date`, the inclusive earliest hearing start instant `session_start_utc`, the inclusive latest hearing end instant `session_end_utc`, and a `capacity` from 1 through 20 inclusive limiting how many appeals may hold slots on it. A docket opens with `status` `OPEN` and `scheduled_appeal_count` at 0. That count is derived rather than incremented blindly: it is the number of appeals whose `docket_id` is this docket and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `GRANTED`, `DENIED` or `DISMISSED`. A decided appeal keeps consuming its slot; only a continued appeal releases one. A clerk may also close a docket, optionally recording a `reason`: the docket's `status` becomes `CLOSED` and its `version` increments by 1, appeals already holding slots keep them and remain `SCHEDULED` with the count unchanged, and every later attempt to schedule onto that docket is refused with `ERR_DOCKET_CLOSED`.

Scheduling an appeal onto a docket is also clerk work, naming the `docket_id` and the requested `hearing_start_utc` and `hearing_end_utc`. The appeal must be `FILED` or `CONTINUED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The docket must be `OPEN`. The requested start must fall strictly before the requested end, and an end that is not strictly after the start is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The requested interval must fit inside the docket's session window, with both bounds inclusive — a hearing may begin exactly at `session_start_utc` and end exactly at `session_end_utc`. It must also keep clear of every other slot already held on that docket by **more than** a fifteen-minute buffer on each side: expanding the requested interval by fifteen minutes at each end must not touch another slot's interval, so a gap of exactly fifteen minutes collides. A geometry failure — whether an interval outside the session window or too close to a neighbour — is refused with `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Finally, the docket's `scheduled_appeal_count` must still be below its `capacity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DOCKET_CAPACITY_REACHED`. On success the appeal's `docket_id`, `hearing_start_utc` and `hearing_end_utc` are written, its `status` becomes `SCHEDULED`, and the docket's `scheduled_appeal_count` is re-derived one higher with the docket's `version` incremented by 1. Re-docketing an appeal that had been continued works identically and against the same interval, buffer, capacity and session-window conditions, moving it from `CONTINUED` back to `SCHEDULED` and re-deriving the new docket's count.

A `HearingOfficer` decides a `SCHEDULED` appeal — any other status is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_SCHEDULED` — by supplying an `action` of `GRANT`, `DENY`, `DISMISS` or `CONTINUE`, optionally with `findings`. Every decision records the acting `X-User-ID` in `decided_by_user_id`, and no decision may be taken by the operator who took the noticed action being challenged: for a `DENIAL` notice that is the application's `determined_by_user_id`, and for a `RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT` notice it is the case's `substantiated_by_user_id`.

A **continuance** postpones rather than decides: `continuance_count` increments by 1, the appeal's `docket_id`, `hearing_start_utc` and `hearing_end_utc` are all reset to null, its `status` becomes `CONTINUED`, and the released slot re-derives the former docket's `scheduled_appeal_count` one lower, freeing the buffer window for another appeal. An appeal may be continued at most twice: the counter must be below 2 when the continuance is issued, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT_REACHED`. A `DISMISS` decision is used when the appellant fails to appear at the reserved interval; it and a `DENY` decision both leave the noticed action standing exactly as it was, and both are terminal for the appeal, as is `GRANTED`.

Granting an appeal is the system's compensation mechanism, and what it undoes depends on the notice's `subject_type`. On a `RECOVERY_ASSESSMENT` notice, the case's `recovery_status` becomes `ABATED` and its `recovery_paid_cents` resets to 0; the abatement returns the `REVOKED` grant to `ACTIVE`; the reactivated grant re-runs the parcel value decomposition and the parcel's `version` increments by 1; and the taxpayer's `lifetime_recovery_collected_cents` drops by the amount that had been collected, with `net_relief_cents` re-derived. On a `DENIAL` notice, the `DENIED` application returns to `UNDER_REVIEW` and its `determined_by_user_id` is cleared to null, putting the claim back in front of a different reviewer. On a `REVOCATION` notice, the `REVOKED` grant returns to `ACTIVE` and the parcel value decomposition re-runs with the parcel's `version` incrementing by 1. In every case the docket's `scheduled_appeal_count` is unchanged — the hearing was held, and the slot stays consumed.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that modify an existing resource, including every action that drives a state transition, every adjudication and decision, the annual assessment roll, and setting the logical clock, return `200 OK`. Reads return `200 OK`.
