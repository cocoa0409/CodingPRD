# Historic Rehab Tax Credit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Rehabilitating a certified historic building for federal tax credit is one of the most paperwork-dense transactions in real estate development. A single project runs a two-agency review gauntlet — a State Historic Preservation Office recommendation followed by a binding National Park Service determination — three times over, once for the evaluation of the building's significance, once for the rehabilitation plan, and once for the completed work. In parallel, the developer must keep a ledger clean enough to prove that qualified rehabilitation expenditures incurred inside a fixed measuring window exceeded the building's adjusted basis, must structure an investor vehicle that does not itself disqualify the credit, must obtain reviewer concurrence for every material construction change, and must then survive five years of compliance monitoring during which any lapse claws part of the credit back.

This document specifies an API that carries that whole apparatus. The system is a compliance-and-audit platform first: it records who decided what, when, and on which version of the underlying facts, and it refuses any write that would let a decision rest on a stale or unproven premise. It is a ledger platform second: qualified and non-qualified costs are segregated line item by line item, the credit is converted into a ratable five-year schedule, and every claim and every recapture assessment moves money between accounts that must always reconcile back to the same total.

Two design commitments shape everything below. First, certification and compliance are **independent dimensions** of a project — a project can be deep into its monitoring period while its part-by-part certification is still incomplete, and neither dimension's transitions write the other's state. Second, no reviewer, developer, monitor, or investor administrator may act outside a narrowly drawn operation set; in particular the reviewer who recommends on an application may never be the reviewer who decides it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, audit fields, precision, cascade semantics, concurrency, and the order in which a request is rejected.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity that principal is acting in. A request that omits `X-User-ID` or supplies it empty — a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the body is parsed. A request whose declared role is not among the roles authorized for the target operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and so is a request that omits `X-User-Role` altogether or declares a value that is not one of the six roles at all.

The system recognizes exactly six roles: `Developer`, `ShpoReviewer`, `NpsReviewer`, `ComplianceMonitor`, `InvestorAdmin`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

- A `Developer` is the rehabilitation taxpayer. When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Developer record; ownership checks on any resource that references a Developer compare the resource's developer-reference field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a write against a project, a part application, a cost line item, an amendment, or a covenant of that project succeeds only when the project's `developer_id` equals `X-User-ID`; otherwise the write is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. The `Developer` alone opens projects, revises their basis and measuring period, declares placed-in-service, creates part applications and submits or resubmits them, records cost line items, and creates and submits amendments. It shares the recording of properties, the classification of costs, the admission of investor interests, and the creation and attestation of covenants; it may read everything.
- A `ShpoReviewer` is a State Historic Preservation Office reviewer. When `X-User-Role` is `ShpoReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Reviewer record, whose `agency` must be `SHPO`; ownership checks compare an application's `shpo_reviewer_id` to `X-User-ID`. Issuing the SHPO recommendation on an assigned application is exclusive to this role; it shares the raising of standards findings and the recording of a concurrence decision on an amendment, and it reads everything except the developer identity record and the investor-side and ledger resources — neither the project-scoped listings of investor interests, credit claims, recapture events and covenants nor the single-resource reads of those same four entities are open to it.
- An `NpsReviewer` is a National Park Service reviewer. When `X-User-Role` is `NpsReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting Reviewer record, whose `agency` must be `NPS`; the determination it issues is stamped onto the application's `nps_reviewer_id`. Issuing the binding determination is exclusive to this role; it shares finding creation and amendment concurrence with `ShpoReviewer`, and it has the same read scope.
- A `ComplianceMonitor` watches the five-year recapture period. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceMonitor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`, and a monitor is deliberately not scoped to a single developer. Reporting recapture events is exclusive to this role; it shares cost reclassification (including the audit disallowance of locked costs), covenant creation, and covenant attestation, and it may read everything.
- An `InvestorAdmin` administers the investor side of the structure. When `X-User-Role` is `InvestorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting InvestorInterest record, which scopes the caller to exactly one project: the interest's `project_id`. Making the pass-through election and opening and filing credit claims are exclusive to this role. Because the identity is the interest itself, a claim action succeeds only when the acting interest's `project_id` equals the claim's `project_id` and the interest's `status` is `ADMITTED`; a mismatch or a `DISQUALIFIED` interest is refused with `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED` (HTTP 403). It also shares the admission of investor interests and may read developers, properties, projects together with their computed QRE summary, part applications, and the investor and ledger sub-resources — but not reviewer identities, cost line items, findings or amendments. The one-project scoping binds its writes only: its reads are not narrowed to the acting interest's own project.
- A `PlatformAdmin` seeds identities and drives the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. Registering developer and reviewer identities and setting the logical clock are exclusive to this role; it shares the recording of properties and may read everything. It holds no domain decision rights whatsoever: it can neither review, nor classify costs, nor claim credits.

Two further authorization rules deserve emphasis, because they are role checks rather than business checks and therefore fire early. A reviewer's `agency` must match the decision being recorded — a Reviewer whose `agency` is `NPS` cannot issue an SHPO recommendation, a Reviewer whose `agency` is `SHPO` cannot issue a determination, and on an amendment concurrence the acting agency selects which branch of the concurrence fork the decision lands on. Any of these mismatches yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Separately, segregation of duties forbids the reviewer who recommended on an application from also deciding it: when `X-User-ID` equals the application's `shpo_reviewer_id`, the determination is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read — like every other timestamp this system emits — from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from the host wall clock, so it does not advance from one request to the next unless the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a value that does not parse as a base-ten integer at all; `offset` carries no upper bound, and either parameter supplied as an empty string reads as its default.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by order of creation, earliest first — a tie-break that decides most orderings in practice rather than none, because the logical clock stands still unless it is explicitly moved.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`: a server-generated UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, in the same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every change to any of the resource's properties — including changes the server makes to computed fields as a side effect of an operation on some other resource. That holds for every field the server stores; a field that is instead derived at read time out of other rows changes without being a mutation of the row that reports it, and the property's `open_project_count` described in §3.1 is the only such field in this specification. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row; it is the value optimistic concurrency control is checked against. Properties, projects, part applications, standards findings, cost line items, amendments, investor interests, credit claims, recapture events, and compliance covenants all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Two identity records are exceptions. A Developer record and a Reviewer record carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations, because these rows are seeded once and never mutated by any operation in this specification.

All timestamps and all "current date" comparisons in this system read from a **logical clock** rather than the host wall clock. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it by supplying `to_iso8601` — any ISO 8601 instant, whose trailing `Z` or explicit offset is normalised to UTC and which is read as UTC when it carries no offset at all — and the response reports the resulting clock as `current_utc`, the sole field of that response's `data`. Whenever the clock is rendered into a timestamp field it carries exactly three fractional digits, any finer precision in the instant it was set to being truncated rather than rounded. The clock only ever moves forward: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a successful clock move mutates no entity — it only changes what subsequent requests observe. Every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `certified_at`, `submitted_at`, `review_window_end`, `decision_at`, `election_at`, and `filed_at` value is taken from this clock, and so is every comparison phrased against "the logical clock date". This clock control is the sole endpoint exempt from the `X-User-ID` requirement, and it is equally tolerant of a request that declares no `X-User-Role` at all: it refuses only a caller that declares some role other than `PlatformAdmin`, with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field holding one is named with a `_cents` suffix. Amounts are never stored or returned as decimal dollars, and no intermediate monetary result is allowed to carry a fractional cent into a persisted field.

Three rounding disciplines apply, and each is attached to exactly one place:

- **Truncation toward zero (floor).** The developer-fee cap is floored to a whole cent — the fractional part is discarded, never rounded up. The count of full years elapsed in a recapture assessment is likewise floored, and the master-lease safe-harbor term is floored to a whole number of years.
- **Round half up.** The per-year ratable credit amount and the recapture obligation amount are rounded half up to a whole cent, meaning a remainder of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. The lease-term component of an investor risk score is rounded half up to a whole point.
- **No rounding at all.** Cost aggregation, the substantial-rehabilitation threshold comparison, and claim settlement operate purely on whole-cent integers, so no rounding step exists in them; the total claimable credit is an exact integer product of an already-rounded per-year amount.

Percentages are handled separately from money. `credit_allocation_pct` is a number strictly greater than 0 and at most 100, carrying at most two decimal places. `recapture_pct` is a server-computed number quantised to two decimal places before it is stored. Both are emitted as ordinary JSON numbers rather than as fixed-point strings, so trailing zeros do not survive the round trip: a `credit_allocation_pct` supplied as `25` or as `25.00` is echoed as `25.0`, a full recapture percentage reads `1.0`, and a step of one remaining compliance year reads `0.2`. `disqualification_risk_score` and `max_investor_risk_score` are integers in the inclusive range 0 to 100.

### 2.6. The Two Independent State Dimensions of a Project

A project row carries two enumerated state fields that advance on separate axes and never write each other.

`certification_status` is the part-by-part review dimension. It begins at `PRE_CERTIFICATION` and moves to `PART1_CERTIFIED`, then `PART2_APPROVED`, then `PART3_CERTIFIED` as the corresponding part application is approved; a denial of the first Part 1 application drives it to the terminal value `DECERTIFIED`. Nothing else advances it — not a cost entry, not a covenant, not a claim.

`compliance_status` is the post-certification monitoring dimension. It begins at `NOT_MONITORED` and moves to `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING` when the building is declared placed in service; it oscillates to `CURE_PENDING` while a covenant is breached or a recapture balance is outstanding, and back to `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING` when both conditions clear. It ends at one of two absorbing values: `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` when the five-year period completes clean, or `RECAPTURED` when ownership of the building transfers inside the period. Once either absorbing value is reached, the dimension never leaves it.

The independence is deliberate and load-bearing. Monitoring begins at the placed-in-service declaration, which is legal from `PART2_APPROVED` onward, so a project can legitimately sit at `PART2_APPROVED`/`IN_SERVICE_MONITORING` — certification incomplete, monitoring already running. Whenever the compliance posture has to be re-derived, it is derived from facts alone, in this precedence order: if `placed_in_service_date` is null the posture is `NOT_MONITORED`; otherwise, if any recapture event of the project has `event_type` of `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` the posture is `RECAPTURED`; otherwise, if any covenant of the project is `BREACHED` or `recapture_balance_cents` is greater than zero the posture is `CURE_PENDING`; otherwise, if every covenant of the project is `COMPLIANT`, the monitoring end date has been reached or passed on the logical clock date, and `recapture_balance_cents` is zero, the posture is `COMPLIANT_CLOSED`; otherwise the posture is `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING`. The monitoring end date is `placed_in_service_date` advanced by five years' worth of days, counting a year as 365 days. This derivation is never run spontaneously and never on a read: it is performed only as a hop of the operations §§3.7 through 3.9 describe, and each such hop fires only from the origin posture that hop names. A posture already settled therefore does not move merely because the logical clock has since crossed the monitoring end date, and the `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` step in particular is reached only through a covenant attestation and is measured against that attestation's `period_end_date` rather than against the clock. `RECAPTURED` and `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` are absorbing and are never left once entered. The certification dimension is not an input to it at any step.

### 2.7. Cascade Propagation and Version Accounting

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one row. A determination on a part application can move the property's evaluation outcome, the project's certification dimension, and the classification of every cost line item on the project. An amendment concurrence can resolve a finding, decrement its application's unresolved counter, complete that application, and advance the project. A covenant attestation can move the project's compliance dimension and re-score every admitted investor interest.

The rule for all of them is uniform: **every row a cascade touches is a mutation of that row.** Its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one, exactly as if a client had written to it directly, and no client request against that row is required for this to happen. A cascade that touches four rows produces four version increments, one per row. A cascade step whose condition is not satisfied does not fire and therefore does not bump the version of the row it would have touched — for example, a determination on a Part 2 application never touches the property, so the property's `version` is unchanged by it.

Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggers them, and the response reflects the fully settled world: a caller reading the response of an operation sees post-cascade values, never an intermediate state. Where a chain of steps is order-sensitive — recompute totals, then re-test, then re-derive the schedule; or offset the recapture balance, then mutate the ledger, then settle events, then re-derive posture — the order stated in the relevant workflow narrative is normative, because a different order produces different numbers.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Project Record

Two write operations require the client to prove it has observed the current state of the project before it acts. Both take `expected_project_version` as a required, non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target project.

The first is `POST /part-applications`. Creating a part application against a project whose `version` has advanced since the client last read it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the serialization point for the supersede rule: another writer may already have created an application for the same part number, which legitimately supersedes the earlier one, so the current writer's picture of the project — including which application is the live one — can no longer be trusted.

The second is `PATCH /projects/{id}`. Revising the adjusted basis or the measuring period on a project whose `version` has advanced since the client last read it is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A basis revision supersedes the prior basis outright, so it cannot be re-derived from a stale read: the substantial-rehabilitation threshold the client believed it was moving away from may already have changed underneath it.

In both cases the check is `expected_project_version` against the project's current `version` at the moment of the write, and only exact equality passes. The field is first validated as a non-negative integer, so a missing, non-integer or negative value is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` schema violation in band 3 of §2.9 rather than an `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`. No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect on the outcome.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in ascending precedence bands, and the first violated check decides the response:

1. **Caller identity.** `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This runs before role resolution and before any body parsing, and applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control.
2. **Role authorization.** The resolved role of the caller is among the roles authorized for the endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Because authorization precedes schema validation, an unauthorized caller who also sends a malformed body still receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request schema.** Types, enum membership, numeric ranges, date formats, and required-field presence, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Path existence.** Each `{id}` in the path resolves to an existing row of its entity, else that entity's own not-found error (404). Path existence resolves before any body reference, so an unknown path id always yields the path entity's 404 rather than a body reference error.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves to an existing row of its declared parent entity, else that parent entity's not-found error (404).
6. **Ownership and actor identity.** Developer ownership of the target project (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), segregation of duties on a determination (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), reviewer agency match (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), and investor-interest standing (`ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED`) — all 403. Ownership is checked after existence, so a developer touching another developer's project gets 403 while an unknown project id gets 404. Inside this band on a determination, the identity-reuse check runs **before** the agency check: a Reviewer whose id equals the application's `shpo_reviewer_id` receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` whatever its agency, so the agency check only ever reports on a reviewer that did not recommend on this application.
7. **Reviewer eligibility.** The reviewer being assigned is credentialled and of the right agency, else `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
8. **Entity state.** The requested transition is declared from the target's current state, else that entity's invalid-state error (`ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, all 409), together with the other state-shaped preconditions: `ERR_CREDIT_CLAIM_ALREADY_FILED`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`, `ERR_COST_ITEM_LOCKED` (409) and `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). This band also catches idempotent replays — a second request for the same action on an already-advanced row.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_project_version` equals the project's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412). Placing this after existence, role, ownership and state means a stale version never masks a more specific error.
10. **Business safety.** Sequencing, uniqueness of certification, cross-entity prerequisites, category eligibility, structure coherence and risk: `ERR_PART_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`, `ERR_PART_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`, `ERR_PRESERVATION_STANDARD_VIOLATION`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_FINDING_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NONQUALIFIED_CATEGORY`, `ERR_STRUCTURE_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DISQUALIFIED_PARTY`, `ERR_DISQUALIFICATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, `ERR_PASS_THROUGH_ELECTION_REQUIRED`.
11. **Temporal.** Overlap, window and calendar checks: `ERR_PROPERTY_MEASURING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CAPACITY`, `ERR_OUTSIDE_MEASURING_PERIOD`, `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW`, `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_RECAPTURE_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`, and the covenant-window coherence check that reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
12. **Fiscal.** Aggregate allocation and the substantial-rehabilitation test last of all: `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` and `ERR_SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_NOT_MET`.

Two consequences are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. A cost in a statutorily non-qualified category that was also incurred outside the measuring period reports `ERR_NONQUALIFIED_CATEGORY`, not `ERR_OUTSIDE_MEASURING_PERIOD`, because the safety band precedes the temporal band. And an attempt to re-file an already-filed claim on a project whose substantial test has since failed reports `ERR_CREDIT_CLAIM_ALREADY_FILED`, not `ERR_SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_NOT_MET`, because the state band precedes the fiscal band.

### 2.10. Reference Resolution and Sub-Resource Reads

Identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. In a path, the leftmost `{id}` is resolved first: a request for the cost line items, investor interests, credit claims, recapture events, compliance covenants, or QRE summary of a project that does not exist fails with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` before anything else is examined, and a request for the findings of an application that does not exist fails with `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Body references follow the same discipline, resolved in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's request body. When a project is opened with both an unknown `developer_id` and an unknown `property_id`, the response is `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_FOUND`, because `developer_id` is listed first; only when the developer resolves does the property reference get examined and possibly report `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`. The same left-to-right rule governs `project_id` and `part_application_id` on an amendment, `part_application_id` on a finding, `project_id` on a cost line item, an investor interest, a claim, a recapture event, or a covenant, `shpo_reviewer_id` on a submission (`ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND`), `addresses_finding_id` on an amendment and each id in `waived_finding_ids` on a determination (`ERR_STANDARD_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`).

The read-only sub-resource listings under a project accept their own filters in addition to `limit` and `offset`: the cost line item listing filters by `classification` and `category`; the investor interest listing by `kind` and `status`; the credit claim listing by `status` and `tax_year`; the recapture event listing by `event_type` and `status`; the covenant listing by `covenant_type` and `status`. The findings listing under an application filters by `severity`, `resolution_state` and `standard_number`. The top-level project listing filters by `developer_id`, `property_id`, `certification_status` and `compliance_status`; the top-level part application listing filters by `project_id`, `part_number`, `status`, `shpo_reviewer_id`, `nps_reviewer_id` and `supersedes_application_id`; the top-level amendment listing filters by `project_id`, `part_application_id`, `addresses_finding_id` and `status`. Every one of these filters is optional, and omitting all of them lists everything in scope. The three filters that name an integer field — `tax_year`, `part_number` and `standard_number` — must parse as base-ten integers, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; every other filter is matched by exact equality against the stored value and is *not* checked against the enumeration it draws from, so an unrecognised value is not a schema violation at all but simply matches nothing and yields an empty page whose `total_count` is zero. A filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent.

### 2.11. Dates, Windows and Interval Semantics

Dates supplied by clients are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; a malformed date is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three distinct interval conventions coexist in this domain and must not be conflated.

**The measuring period is half-open.** It runs from `measuring_period_start_date` inclusive to `measuring_period_end_date` exclusive. A cost incurred exactly on the start date is inside the period; a cost incurred exactly on the end date is outside it. Two measuring periods overlap precisely when the first period's start precedes the second period's end **and** the second period's start precedes the first period's end; periods that merely touch, where one period's end date equals the other's start date, do not overlap.

**A reviewer's review window is closed.** It runs from the date of `submitted_at` through the date of `review_window_end`, both endpoints included. Two closed windows overlap precisely when the first window's start is on or before the second window's end **and** the second window's start is on or before the first window's end — so two windows that share only a single endpoint date **do** overlap. This is the exact opposite of the measuring-period convention, and it is intentional.

**A covenant monitoring window is inclusive at both ends.** It runs from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date`, and `window_end_date` must fall strictly after `window_start_date`; a window that fails this coherence check is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Calendar arithmetic comes in two flavours. Advancing by **months** preserves the day of month: the exclusive end of the measuring period is `measuring_period_start_date` advanced by `measuring_period_months` calendar months, landing on the same day of month — or, when the target month is too short to hold that day, on that month's last day instead. Advancing by **days** is plain day counting, and where a duration is expressed in years for day arithmetic — the five-year compliance period, the elapsed time in a recapture assessment — a year counts as exactly 365 days.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Seeding the Cast: Taxpayers, Reviewers and Buildings

Before any rehabilitation can be recorded, three kinds of identity must exist. A `PlatformAdmin` registers the taxpayer that will own the rehabilitation by supplying its `legal_name`, the `taxpayer_id_last4` — exactly four characters, digits only, being the tail of the taxpayer identification number — and its `entity_type`, which is one of `C_CORP`, `S_CORP`, `PARTNERSHIP`, or `LLC`. A `contact_email` of at most 320 characters may be supplied for review correspondence and may be omitted; when it is supplied it must contain an at-sign, and when it is omitted the developer's representation still carries the field, holding null. The `legal_name` runs from 1 to 120 characters and may not be blank.

Reviewer identities are registered the same way and by the same role. A reviewer carries a `full_name` of 1 to 80 characters, an `agency` of either `SHPO` or `NPS`, a `state_code` giving the two-letter uppercase USPS code of the reviewer's office, and a `credential_status` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may alternatively be `SUSPENDED`. Only an `ACTIVE` reviewer may be assigned to an application or may decide one; a `SUSPENDED` reviewer is inert.

Buildings are recorded by either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `Developer`. A property carries the `name` under which the building or complex appears on its nomination (1 to 120 characters, non-blank), its `street_address` (1 to 160 characters), its `city` (1 to 80 characters), its `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), and its `year_built`, which must fall in the inclusive range 1600 through 2010 and which feeds the evaluation of significance. Its register posture at intake is `listing_status`, defaulting to `NOT_LISTED` and otherwise one of `INDIVIDUAL_LISTED`, `DISTRICT_CONTRIBUTING`, or `DISTRICT_NONCONTRIBUTING`.

A newly recorded property has not yet been evaluated: its `evaluation_status` is `UNEVALUATED`, its `certified_at` is null, and its `open_project_count` is zero. The evaluation outcome is never supplied by a client — it becomes `CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`, with `certified_at` stamped from the logical clock, when the building's Part 1 significance application is approved, and `NON_CERTIFIED` when that Part 1 application is denied. A building whose `listing_status` is `DISTRICT_NONCONTRIBUTING` can never be certified: any attempt to put an application on such a property into review is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`. The `open_project_count` counts the property's projects whose certification dimension is any of `PRE_CERTIFICATION`, `PART1_CERTIFIED`, `PART2_APPROVED`, or `PART3_CERTIFIED`; opening a project raises it by one, and a project driven to `DECERTIFIED` drops out of it. It is derived from the project set each time the property is read rather than stored on the property row, so it moves without the property being mutated: opening a project on a building, or decertifying one, leaves that building's `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were, and only an evaluation-outcome change ever mutates a property.

### 3.2. Opening a Rehabilitation Project and Fixing the Measuring Period

A `Developer` opens a project on a recorded building, naming the building through `property_id` and itself through `developer_id`, which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID` — a developer cannot open a project in another taxpayer's name. The project carries an internal `project_name` of 1 to 120 characters and elects its investor vehicle up front through `structure_type`, which is either `MASTER_LEASE` or `SINGLE_TIER`. This election is what later constrains which kind of investor party may be admitted.

The fiscal frame of the project is set at the same moment. `adjusted_basis_cents` records the adjusted basis of the building at the start of the measuring period and must be zero or greater. `measuring_period_months` must be exactly 24 or exactly 60 — the two statutory windows a taxpayer may elect — and `measuring_period_start_date` gives the inclusive first date of that window. From these the server derives `measuring_period_end_date` as the start date advanced by that many calendar months on the same day of month, and this derived date is the exclusive upper bound of the window. The project opens with `certification_status` at `PRE_CERTIFICATION` and `compliance_status` at `NOT_MONITORED`, with `placed_in_service_date` null, and with the whole credit ledger — `qre_total_final_cents`, `qre_total_provisional_cents`, `nonqualified_total_cents`, `classified_cost_total_cents`, `developer_fee_disallowed_cents`, `annual_credit_cents`, `total_claimable_credit_cents`, `credit_claimed_to_date_cents`, `credit_recaptured_cents`, `credit_unclaimed_cents`, `recapture_balance_cents`, `max_investor_risk_score` — at zero, with `substantial_test_status` at `NOT_MET` and `frozen_at_certification` false. Its `substantial_test_threshold_cents` is derived immediately.

One building may not be rehabilitated twice at once. A project whose measuring period overlaps the measuring period of any other project on the same property is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_MEASURING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, applying the half-open overlap test of §2.11. Only projects whose certification dimension is not `DECERTIFIED` are considered when looking for a conflict, so an abandoned, denied rehabilitation does not block a fresh attempt. Periods that merely abut — one ending on the day the next begins — are accepted.

Until the completed work is certified, the frame can still be revised. A basis-and-period revision by the owning `Developer` may change `adjusted_basis_cents`, `measuring_period_start_date`, `measuring_period_months`, any combination of them, or none of them at all — a request carrying only `expected_project_version` is accepted as a no-op revision — and must carry `expected_project_version` as described in §2.8. Such a revision is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE` once the project's certification dimension has reached `PART3_CERTIFIED` or `DECERTIFIED` — the basis and the window freeze at certification of completed work — and it is subject to the same overlap check as project creation, the project being revised naturally excluded from that comparison, again reporting `ERR_PROPERTY_MEASURING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. On every accepted revision — whether it moved the window, touched only the basis, or changed nothing — the consequences ripple in a fixed order: `measuring_period_end_date` is recomputed, then every cost line item of the project has its `in_measuring_period` flag re-evaluated against the new half-open window, with only those items whose flag actually turns over being mutated and having their `version` bumped, then the qualified/non-qualified segregation is recomputed, then the substantial rehabilitation test, and only then the ratable credit schedule. The project's `version` increments by one and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

### 3.3. Part-by-Part Applications: Drafting, Superseding and Entering Review

Certification proceeds in three parts, and each part is a separate application row against the project. A `Developer` drafts one by naming its `project_id`, its `part_number` — exactly 1, 2, or 3 — and a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters describing the significance of the building for Part 1, the rehabilitation work for Part 2, or the completion of that work for Part 3. Creation also carries `expected_project_version` per §2.8. A new application begins in `DRAFT` with `review_round` at 1, `shpo_reviewer_id` and `nps_reviewer_id` null, `submitted_at`, `review_window_end` and `decision_at` null, `shpo_recommendation` and `determination` both `NONE`, and both `unresolved_condition_count` and `unresolved_violation_count` at zero. Creation increments the project's `version` by one.

Parts must be applied for in order. Part 2 requires the project's Part 1 application to have reached `CONDITIONAL` or `APPROVED`, and Part 3 requires the same of Part 2; Part 1 has no predecessor. A request that jumps the queue is refused with `ERR_PART_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`. Creation is deliberately permitted on a merely `CONDITIONAL` predecessor so that the developer can prepare the next part while conditions are being cleared by amendment — but that next part cannot enter review until the building itself is certified, which only an `APPROVED` Part 1 produces. Part 3 additionally requires the project's `substantial_test_status` to read `MET` at creation time; otherwise the request for certification of completed work is refused with `ERR_SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_NOT_MET`. No part application of any number may be created on a project whose certification dimension has reached the terminal `DECERTIFIED`, which is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`.

A part may be re-applied for, and doing so supersedes the earlier attempt rather than duplicating it. When an application already exists for the same project and the same `part_number` and its status is any non-terminal value — `DRAFT`, `SHPO_REVIEW`, `NPS_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONAL`, or `REVISION_REQUIRED` — that row transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, its `version` increments, its reviewer occupation interval is released so it no longer counts against that reviewer's capacity, and the new row records the superseded row's `id` in `supersedes_application_id`. When no such row existed, `supersedes_application_id` stays null. `APPROVED` and `SUPERSEDED` rows are never superseded: once a part is `APPROVED` it is closed, and a further application for it is refused with `ERR_PART_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`.

Submission moves an application from `DRAFT` to `SHPO_REVIEW` and is the point at which the state preservation office reviewer is chosen: the developer supplies `shpo_reviewer_id`, which is written onto the application. The named reviewer must exist, must have `agency` of `SHPO`, and must have `credential_status` of `ACTIVE`; a suspended or wrong-agency reviewer is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Submission stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock and derives `review_window_end` as that instant advanced by thirty days, which together bound the reviewer's occupation interval as a closed date interval. For a Part 2 or Part 3 application, the property's `evaluation_status` must already read `CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`, else `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`; Part 1 submissions are exempt from that particular precondition, since they are what produce the certification. The exemption is that narrow: the `DISTRICT_NONCONTRIBUTING` bar of §3.1 is a separate check, it is tested **before** the `evaluation_status` one, and it applies to a submission of any part number — a Part 1 submission on a non-contributing building in a district is refused with the same `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_CERTIFIED_HISTORIC`. A Part 3 submission additionally requires the project to have a non-null `placed_in_service_date`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`. Submission increments the application's `version` by one.

Reviewer load is capped by overlapping occupation. Before a submission is accepted, the system counts the *other* applications assigned to the same `shpo_reviewer_id` whose status is `SHPO_REVIEW`, `NPS_REVIEW`, or `CONDITIONAL` and whose closed occupation interval — from the date of their `submitted_at` through the date of their `review_window_end` — overlaps the prospective closed interval of this submission, which runs from the current logical clock date through that date advanced by thirty days. Overlap here uses the closed-interval convention of §2.11, so windows sharing only one endpoint date do overlap. A reviewer may hold at most two such overlapping windows: when the count of conflicting applications is two or more, the submission is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CAPACITY`. An application leaves the count as soon as its status leaves that set — when the recommendation forwards it, when it is superseded, or when it terminates.

### 3.4. Recommendation, Findings and the Binding Determination

The office holding the application recommends first. A `ShpoReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the application's `shpo_reviewer_id` and whose `credential_status` is still `ACTIVE` records a `recommendation` of either `RECOMMEND_APPROVAL` or `RECOMMEND_DENIAL` into `shpo_recommendation`, optionally attaching a `review_note`, and the application advances `SHPO_REVIEW → NPS_REVIEW`. The `version` increments by one. Two of those conditions are checked in different bands and therefore in a fixed order: the caller's `X-User-ID` must first resolve, in band 6, to a Reviewer record whose `agency` is `SHPO` and whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` — an unregistered, wrong-agency or `SUSPENDED` acting reviewer is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — while the comparison of `X-User-ID` against the application's `shpo_reviewer_id` is made only **after** the status check, so an `ACTIVE` `SHPO` reviewer who was never assigned this application receives `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE` when the application is not in `SHPO_REVIEW`, and `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` only when it is. Because the status has left `SHPO_REVIEW`, this also releases part of the reviewer's occupation load — although an application in `NPS_REVIEW` still occupies a window, so the release becomes effective only when the application leaves that set entirely.

Either agency's reviewer may raise findings against an application while it is under review — that is, while its status is `SHPO_REVIEW`, `NPS_REVIEW`, or `CONDITIONAL`; a finding against an application in any other status is refused with `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. A finding cites `standard_number`, an integer from 1 through 10 inclusive identifying which of the ten Secretary's Standards for Rehabilitation it invokes, carries a `severity`, and carries a `description` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters recording the deviation. Severity is graded: `ADVISORY` is purely informational and blocks nothing; `CONDITION` must be cleared by a concurred amendment before the application can be approved; `VIOLATION` blocks approval outright and can never be waived. A new finding starts with `resolution_state` of `UNRESOLVED` and records no author: raising one is authorized by the declared `X-User-Role` alone, the caller's `X-User-ID` never being resolved to a Reviewer record, so neither the raiser's `agency` nor its `credential_status` is examined on this operation. A `CONDITION` finding raises the application's `unresolved_condition_count` by one and a `VIOLATION` finding raises its `unresolved_violation_count` by one; the application's `version` increments in every case, an `ADVISORY` finding that moves neither counter included. A `VIOLATION` finding carries one further consequence that no other severity carries: when the project's compliance dimension has already left `NOT_MONITORED`, the new finding also re-scores every `ADMITTED` investor interest of the project under the `IN_SERVICE` variant of §3.7 and refreshes the project's `max_investor_risk_score`, bumping the `version` of each interest whose score actually moved and the project's own `version` when any of those scores or that maximum changed.

The binding determination is issued by an `NpsReviewer` on an application in `NPS_REVIEW`. The caller must resolve to a Reviewer whose `agency` is `NPS` and whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, and must not be the reviewer who recommended — the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1. The body carries `determination`, optionally `waived_finding_ids` naming findings the reviewer chooses to excuse, and optionally a `review_note` — which, like the recommendation's `review_note`, is validated for length and then discarded, appearing in no representation. Every id in `waived_finding_ids` is resolved before the branch is chosen; each named finding moves to `resolution_state` of `WAIVED` and decrements whichever unresolved counter its severity feeds. A named finding is not required to belong to the application being determined: a waiver of a finding raised against some other application is honoured all the same, and that other application's two unresolved counters are recomputed and its `version` bumped as part of this cascade. A finding that already reads `WAIVED` is left untouched and its `version` does not move, so naming it again is inert. A waiver may never be applied to a `VIOLATION`-severity finding: an attempt to waive one is refused with `ERR_PRESERVATION_STANDARD_VIOLATION`, and because that refusal is tested before the branch is chosen it fires whatever the declared `determination` is — on a `DENIED` determination as much as on an `APPROVED` one. All three branches stamp `determination`, set `nps_reviewer_id` to the caller's `X-User-ID`, set `decision_at` from the logical clock, and increment the application's `version` by one.

A determination of `APPROVED` advances the application `NPS_REVIEW → APPROVED`. It requires, after the waivers have been applied, that `unresolved_violation_count` be zero — otherwise `ERR_PRESERVATION_STANDARD_VIOLATION` — and that `unresolved_condition_count` also be zero; an `APPROVED` determination attempted while unresolved conditions remain is refused with `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, since `CONDITIONAL` is the declared outcome in that situation; when both counters are non-zero it is the violation that is reported, these two checks being evaluated in the order stated here rather than in the band order of §2.9. For a Part 3 application, approval further requires the project's `substantial_test_status` to read `MET`, else `ERR_SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_NOT_MET`. Approval then cascades outward in hops. For a Part 1 application, the first hop sets the property's `evaluation_status` to `CERTIFIED_HISTORIC` and stamps its `certified_at` from the logical clock, bumping the property's `version`. The second hop advances the project's certification dimension according to the part number: `PRE_CERTIFICATION → PART1_CERTIFIED` for Part 1, `PART1_CERTIFIED → PART2_APPROVED` for Part 2, and `PART2_APPROVED → PART3_CERTIFIED` for Part 3, bumping the project's `version`. For Part 3 only, a third hop sets `frozen_at_certification` to true, which permanently stops recomputation of `annual_credit_cents` and `total_claimable_credit_cents`, and drives every cost line item of the project from `QRE` to `LOCKED_QRE` and from `NON_QRE` to `LOCKED_NON_QRE`, bumping each of those line items' `version`. Each hop is guarded on its own terms. The first fires on every Part 1 approval whatever the property's current `evaluation_status`, including one an earlier denial had already driven to `NON_CERTIFIED`. The second fires only while the project's certification dimension is actually sitting at the origin state its part number names; when it is not — as on the approval of a resubmitted Part 1 application whose earlier denial had already carried the project to the terminal `DECERTIFIED` — the advance does not happen, the third hop that rides on it does not happen either, so nothing freezes and no cost item locks, and the project's `version` is left unchanged. The compliance dimension is untouched by all of this: it has already left `NOT_MONITORED` at the placed-in-service declaration, which Part 3 approval presupposes.

A determination of `CONDITIONAL` advances the application `NPS_REVIEW → CONDITIONAL`. It requires, after the waivers, that `unresolved_violation_count` be zero and that `unresolved_condition_count` be at least one; a `CONDITIONAL` determination on an application with no unresolved conditions is refused with `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, since `APPROVED` is the declared outcome in that situation. The project's certification dimension is expressly **not** advanced, and neither the property nor any cost line item is touched — the only row mutated is the application itself. `CONDITIONAL` parks the application until every `CONDITION` finding against it has been cleared by a concurred amendment, at which point the application completes without a further reviewer action, as described in §3.5.

A determination of `DENIED` advances the application `NPS_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUIRED`, returning it to the developer for revision. For a Part 1 application on a project whose certification dimension still reads `PRE_CERTIFICATION`, the denial also cascades: the property's `evaluation_status` becomes `NON_CERTIFIED` and the project's certification dimension moves `PRE_CERTIFICATION → DECERTIFIED`, which is terminal, with both of those rows' `version` incrementing. A denial of a Part 2 or Part 3 application, or of a Part 1 application on a project that has already moved past `PRE_CERTIFICATION`, mutates only the application.

The revision loop reuses the same row rather than creating a new one. The owning `Developer` resubmits an application in `REVISION_REQUIRED` with a replacement `narrative`, which advances it `REVISION_REQUIRED → SHPO_REVIEW` and hands it back to the reviewer already recorded in `shpo_reviewer_id` — who must still hold `credential_status` of `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A resubmission runs the same overlapping-window capacity check as a first submission and is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CAPACITY` on the same terms. Beyond that credential check and that capacity check it re-tests none of a first submission's other preconditions: neither the property's `listing_status` nor its `evaluation_status` is examined again, a Part 3 resubmission does not re-check for a `placed_in_service_date`, and the project's certification dimension is not consulted at all — so an application sitting in `REVISION_REQUIRED` may be resubmitted even on a project whose Part 1 denial already drove it to `DECERTIFIED`. The findings already raised against the row carry over untouched, and so do both unresolved counters: only the fields enumerated next are reset. On success, `review_round` increases by one, `shpo_recommendation` resets to `NONE`, `determination` resets to `NONE`, `nps_reviewer_id` resets to null, `decision_at` resets to null, `submitted_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock, `review_window_end` is re-derived as thirty days after it, and the `version` increments. The loop is bounded: the increment is applied first, and a resubmission whose resulting `review_round` would exceed three is refused with `ERR_PART_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, so an application passes through at most three review rounds in total.

### 3.5. Construction Changes: Amendments and Two-Agency Concurrence

Rehabilitations change in the field, and a change to approved scope needs reviewer concurrence. A `Developer` drafts an amendment naming the `project_id` whose scope is changing and the `part_application_id` of the Part 2 application whose approved scope it alters. It describes the change through `scope_category`, one of `WINDOW_REPLACEMENT`, `MASONRY_TREATMENT`, `INTERIOR_PARTITION`, `ROOF_ALTERATION`, `SYSTEMS_UPGRADE`, or `ADDITION`, through a `change_description` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters, and through `cost_delta_cents`, a signed integer that may be positive or negative according to whether the change adds or removes cost. An amendment may also be aimed at an outstanding finding by supplying `addresses_finding_id`; omitting it makes the amendment a pure scope change that clears nothing. A finding raised against a different application than the one being amended is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_FINDING_MISMATCH` — an amendment may only clear a finding raised against the very application whose scope it amends. That finding-to-application relation is the only cross-reference the draft actually tests: the named `part_application_id` is not required to belong to the named `project_id` — ownership is checked against the named project alone — and neither the application's `part_number` nor its status is examined until submission. The amendment begins in `DRAFT` with `shpo_decision` and `nps_decision` both `NONE` and `submitted_at` null, and drafting one mutates no other row: the project's `version` and the amended application's are both left exactly as they were.

Whether the change needs the federal agency as well as the state office is decided by the server at draft time and recorded in `requires_nps_concurrence`. It is true when any one of three conditions holds: the `scope_category` is `ADDITION` or `ROOF_ALTERATION`; or the magnitude of `cost_delta_cents`, ignoring its sign, is at least fifty thousand dollars' worth of cents; or the finding named by `addresses_finding_id` has `severity` of `VIOLATION`. Otherwise it is false, and the state office alone can dispose of the change.

Submission by the owning `Developer` moves the amendment `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, incrementing its `version`. The application it amends must be a Part 2 application whose status is `CONDITIONAL` or `APPROVED`; an amendment against anything else is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_INVALID_STATE`, as is a submission of an amendment that is no longer in `DRAFT`.

Concurrence is a fork-join. A reviewer records a `decision` of either `CONCUR` or `OBJECT`, optionally attaching a `concurrence_note` — validated for length and then discarded, appearing in no representation — and the declared `X-User-Role` selects which branch the decision lands on and which of `shpo_decision` or `nps_decision` it writes; the caller's `X-User-ID` must resolve, in band 6, to a Reviewer record whose `agency` is the one that role names and whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, an unregistered, wrong-agency or `SUSPENDED` acting reviewer being refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. A second concurrence by an agency that has already decided is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

- When `requires_nps_concurrence` is false, a `CONCUR` from an `ACTIVE` `SHPO` reviewer joins immediately: the amendment moves `SUBMITTED → CONCURRED` and `shpo_decision` becomes `CONCUR`.
- When `requires_nps_concurrence` is true, that same `CONCUR` from an `ACTIVE` `SHPO` reviewer only advances the amendment `SUBMITTED → SHPO_CONCURRED` and sets `shpo_decision` to `CONCUR`. No finding is resolved yet, because the join is incomplete; the amendment's `version` increments and nothing else is touched.
- From `SHPO_CONCURRED`, a `CONCUR` from an `ACTIVE` `NPS` reviewer completes the join: the amendment moves `SHPO_CONCURRED → CONCURRED` and `nps_decision` becomes `CONCUR`. This branch carries one extra precondition the state-office branch does not: the amended application must have `unresolved_violation_count` of zero, else `ERR_PRESERVATION_STANDARD_VIOLATION`.

Both `CONCUR` branches that reach `CONCURRED` fire the same four-hop resolution cascade, and both make it conditional — when `addresses_finding_id` is present — on the named finding still having `resolution_state` of `UNRESOLVED` and belonging to the same application the amendment amends. Those two conditions gate the cascade, not the decision: when either fails, exactly as when `addresses_finding_id` is absent altogether, the amendment still reaches `CONCURRED` and no error is returned — the cascade simply does not fire and no row other than the amendment moves. The first hop sets that finding's `resolution_state` to `RESOLVED`, bumping the finding's `version`. The second hop reduces by one whichever of the owning application's two unresolved counters the finding's `severity` feeds — `unresolved_condition_count` for a `CONDITION`, `unresolved_violation_count` for a `VIOLATION`, and neither counter for an `ADVISORY`, which feeds none — and bumps the application's `version` in every one of those cases. The third hop fires only when both of the application's unresolved counters are then zero and the application's status is `CONDITIONAL`: the application then completes on its own, moving `CONDITIONAL → APPROVED` with its `determination` set to `APPROVED` and its `decision_at` stamped from the logical clock — no reviewer action is needed for this step. The fourth hop follows the third: the project's certification dimension advances according to the completed application's `part_number`, exactly as an ordinary approval would advance it, bumping the project's `version`. When the third hop does not fire, the fourth does not either, and the project's `version` is left alone.

An `OBJECT` decision short-circuits the fork from whichever side it arrives on, and its side-effect set is deliberately different. An `ACTIVE` `SHPO` reviewer objecting moves the amendment `SUBMITTED → OBJECTED` and writes `OBJECT` into `shpo_decision`; an `ACTIVE` `NPS` reviewer objecting from `SHPO_CONCURRED` moves it `SHPO_CONCURRED → OBJECTED` and writes `OBJECT` into `nps_decision`. In both cases the addressed finding is not resolved and no counter moves. If that finding is itself still `UNRESOLVED`, has `severity` of `CONDITION`, and its application is sitting in `CONDITIONAL`, the objection sends the application back for revision: it moves `CONDITIONAL → REVISION_REQUIRED` with its `determination` set to `DENIED` and its `decision_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` increments. Both `OBJECTED` states are terminal for the amendment.

### 3.6. Cost Segregation, the Substantial Rehabilitation Test and the Credit Schedule

Every invoice touching the rehabilitation is recorded as a cost line item by the owning `Developer`: the `project_id` it belongs to, the `vendor_name` on the invoice (1 to 120 non-blank characters), the `category` driving statutory eligibility, the `amount_cents` invoiced (strictly greater than zero), and the `incurred_date` on which the cost was incurred. Categories are `HARD_CONSTRUCTION`, `ARCHITECTURAL_ENGINEERING`, `DEVELOPER_FEE`, `CONSTRUCTION_INTEREST`, `ACQUISITION`, `ENLARGEMENT`, `SITE_WORK`, `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, `FFE`, and `LANDSCAPING`. A line item may be recorded unclassified, in which case its `classification` is `PENDING_CLASSIFICATION`, or classified immediately by supplying the optional `classification` field with either `QRE` or `NON_QRE`. The server also derives `in_measuring_period`, which is true exactly when the `incurred_date` falls on or after the project's `measuring_period_start_date` and strictly before its `measuring_period_end_date`, and initialises `exclusion_reason` to `NONE` and `reclassification_count` to zero — at `NONE` even when the item is recorded straight onto the non-qualified side, no `exclusion_reason` being accepted from the client on this operation at all. Recording a line item, classified or not, then recomputes the project's segregation, its substantial-rehabilitation test and — unless the schedule is already frozen — its credit schedule, in the normative order of §2.7, and increments the project's `version`.

Reclassification is a first-class operation, and each successful one raises `reclassification_count` by one. A `Developer` may cycle an item `PENDING_CLASSIFICATION → QRE`, `QRE → NON_QRE`, and `NON_QRE → QRE` as the accountants argue, supplying the target `classification` and optionally an `exclusion_reason`; a request that merely re-declares the classification the item already holds is accepted on exactly the same terms as a real move, still raising `reclassification_count` and still bumping the versions below. Moving an item onto the qualified side has two hard preconditions: its `category` must not be one of the statutorily excluded categories `ACQUISITION`, `ENLARGEMENT`, `SITE_WORK`, `PERSONAL_PROPERTY`, `FFE`, or `LANDSCAPING` — those can never be qualified rehabilitation expenditures, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_NONQUALIFIED_CATEGORY` — and its `in_measuring_period` must be true, else `ERR_OUTSIDE_MEASURING_PERIOD`. Both checks apply equally when a `classification` of `QRE` is supplied at creation time. An item that fails either check may still be recorded and kept on the non-qualified side. Moving an item to `QRE` sets `exclusion_reason` back to `NONE`, whatever `exclusion_reason` the request may have supplied alongside it; moving it to `NON_QRE` sets `exclusion_reason` to the supplied value when one is given — taken as given, `NONE` included — and otherwise derives it in a fixed precedence: `NONQUALIFIED_CATEGORY` when the `category` is one of the statutorily excluded ones, else `OUTSIDE_MEASURING_PERIOD` when `in_measuring_period` is false, else `NONE`. The category reason therefore wins when both would apply, and an item moved to `NON_QRE` by pure accounting choice — eligible `category`, inside the window, no reason supplied — carries `exclusion_reason` of `NONE`. The other two members of the enumeration are never derived: `REVIEWER_DISALLOWED` is a default only for the audit disallowance of a locked item described below, and `DEVELOPER_FEE_CAP` — the disallowed fee slice being an aggregate of the project rather than a property of any one row — only ever appears because a client supplied it. Every classification change increments both the line item's `version` and the project's `version`, and, absent the substantial-test failure cascade described below, mutates no other row.

The segregation itself is an aggregation over the project's line items, and it runs in **two variants** that differ in exactly one respect: how unclassified items are treated. Only items whose `in_measuring_period` is true participate at all. Under the `FINAL` variant, the counted qualified set is the items whose `classification` is `QRE` or `LOCKED_QRE`. Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, the counted qualified set is those same items plus the items whose `classification` is `PENDING_CLASSIFICATION` and whose `category` is one of the four QRE-eligible categories `HARD_CONSTRUCTION`, `ARCHITECTURAL_ENGINEERING`, `DEVELOPER_FEE`, or `CONSTRUCTION_INTEREST`; the provisional figure is therefore an upper bound on the final one, and the final figure can only be less than or equal to it.

Within whichever counted set the variant selects, the arithmetic is the same. The **hard qualified total** is the sum of `amount_cents` over the counted items whose `category` is not `DEVELOPER_FEE`. The **fee claimed** is the sum of `amount_cents` over the counted items whose `category` is `DEVELOPER_FEE`. The developer fee is then capped at fifteen percent of the hard qualified total, and that cap is truncated downward to a whole cent rather than rounded. The **fee allowed** is the lesser of the fee claimed and the cap, and the **fee disallowed** is the fee claimed minus the fee allowed. The qualified total for the variant is the hard qualified total plus the fee allowed. The non-qualified total is the sum of `amount_cents` over the in-period items whose `classification` is `NON_QRE` or `LOCKED_NON_QRE`, plus the fee disallowed — the excess fee is *moved* to the non-qualified side, never dropped. The classified cost total is the sum of `amount_cents` over the in-period items whose `classification` is any of `QRE`, `NON_QRE`, `LOCKED_QRE`, or `LOCKED_NON_QRE`.

Five project fields hold the results. `qre_total_final_cents` holds the qualified total under the `FINAL` variant, `qre_total_provisional_cents` holds it under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, `nonqualified_total_cents` holds the non-qualified total under the `FINAL` variant, `developer_fee_disallowed_cents` holds the fee disallowed under the `FINAL` variant, and `classified_cost_total_cents` holds the classified cost total. These fields obey a conservation invariant that must hold after every classification history whatsoever: `qre_total_final_cents` plus `nonqualified_total_cents` equals `classified_cost_total_cents`. It holds precisely because the disallowed fee slice is relocated between the two sides rather than discarded, which is also why cycling an item `QRE → NON_QRE → QRE` restores `classified_cost_total_cents` to exactly its former value.

The segregation is available as a computed read on the project, which recomputes it on demand without mutating anything. The requested `variant` may be `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`; omitting it, or supplying it empty, defaults to `PROVISIONAL`, while any other value is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` schema violation reported before the path's project id is even resolved. The response reports `variant_applied`, `qre_total_cents`, `hard_qre_cents`, `developer_fee_claimed_cents`, `developer_fee_allowed_cents`, `developer_fee_disallowed_cents`, `nonqualified_total_cents`, `classified_cost_total_cents`, `substantial_test_threshold_cents`, `substantial_test_status_under_variant` — the test outcome computed against the requested variant's qualified total rather than the stored `FINAL` one — plus `in_period_item_count`, the number of in-period line items, and `pending_classification_item_count`, the number of those still awaiting classification. Every figure in that payload is computed fresh under the requested variant rather than read off the project's stored fields, so `developer_fee_claimed_cents`, `developer_fee_allowed_cents`, `developer_fee_disallowed_cents` and `nonqualified_total_cents` shift with the variant as well; `substantial_test_threshold_cents`, `classified_cost_total_cents` and `in_period_item_count` read the same either way. The `PROVISIONAL` variant is what a developer watches while preparing Part 3; the `FINAL` variant is what the certification and the credit actually run on.

Whether the rehabilitation is "substantial" is a single threshold comparison, recomputed immediately after every recomputation of the segregation and after any revision of the adjusted basis or the measuring period. The threshold, held in `substantial_test_threshold_cents`, is the greater of the project's `adjusted_basis_cents` and a statutory floor of five thousand dollars expressed in cents. `substantial_test_status` reads `MET` when `qre_total_final_cents` is greater than or equal to that threshold and `NOT_MET` otherwise; the comparison is inclusive, so an amount landing exactly on the threshold is `MET`.

The credit follows from the final qualified total, and its evaluation order is normative. When `substantial_test_status` is `NOT_MET`, both `annual_credit_cents` and `total_claimable_credit_cents` are zero. Otherwise `annual_credit_cents` is four percent of `qre_total_final_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `total_claimable_credit_cents` is that **already-rounded** per-year amount multiplied by five, the number of ratable years. Rounding after the multiplication instead of before it yields a different total for most inputs and is wrong. `credit_unclaimed_cents` is then `total_claimable_credit_cents` less `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` less `credit_recaptured_cents`. This schedule is recomputed after every substantial-test recomputation for as long as `frozen_at_certification` is false; once Part 3 certification sets that flag to true, the schedule is never recomputed again — the freeze covers `annual_credit_cents` and `total_claimable_credit_cents` alone, while `credit_unclaimed_cents` goes on being re-derived from them on every recomputation and after every movement of the ledger — and a later change in qualified expenditure leaves `annual_credit_cents` and `total_claimable_credit_cents` untouched and is expressed through recapture instead.

Costs lock when the completed work is certified. On Part 3 certification every `QRE` item becomes `LOCKED_QRE` and every `NON_QRE` item becomes `LOCKED_NON_QRE`, and a `Developer` who then attempts to reclassify one is refused with `ERR_COST_ITEM_LOCKED`. That locking is a single hop of the certification and is never re-applied afterwards: a line item recorded after Part 3 certification opens unlocked, and the owning `Developer` may classify it exactly as before. Only a `ComplianceMonitor` can move a locked item, and only in one direction: an audit disallowance takes it `LOCKED_QRE → LOCKED_NON_QRE`, raising `reclassification_count`, setting `exclusion_reason` to `REVIEWER_DISALLOWED` unless the monitor supplied an `exclusion_reason` of its own — which is then taken as given — and bumping both the item's and the project's `version`. A monitor may aim the same disallowance at an item that already reads `LOCKED_NON_QRE`: the classification does not move, but the request is accepted on those same terms, rewriting `exclusion_reason` and raising `reclassification_count` and both versions. A request to move a locked item back onto the qualified side is refused with `ERR_COST_ITEM_LOCKED`. The disallowance recomputes `qre_total_final_cents`, `nonqualified_total_cents` and `classified_cost_total_cents` while `annual_credit_cents` stays frozen — and if the recomputation flips `substantial_test_status` from `MET` to `NOT_MET` on a project whose certification dimension is `PART3_CERTIFIED`, the system raises a recapture event of its own accord, as described in §3.8. That trigger is a property of the recomputation rather than of the actor: any classification of any cost line item of a `PART3_CERTIFIED` project that flips the stored `substantial_test_status` from `MET` to `NOT_MET` raises it, a `Developer`'s reclassification of a still-unlocked item recorded after certification included.

### 3.7. The Investor Structure and Its Disqualification Risk

The credit is rarely used by the taxpayer that earns it, so every project admits investor parties under the structure it elected at opening. An interest is admitted by either an `InvestorAdmin` or a `Developer`, naming the `project_id`, the `kind` of interest, the `party_legal_name` of the investing party (1 to 120 non-blank characters), the `party_tax_status` of that party, the `capital_committed_cents` it commits (strictly greater than zero), and the `credit_allocation_pct` of the project credit allocated to it. A `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` is the master tenant that receives the credit by pass-through election; a `DIRECT_PARTNER` is a partner in a single-tier pass-through. Tax status is one of `TAXABLE_C_CORP`, `PARTNERSHIP`, `TAX_EXEMPT_ORG`, or `GOVERNMENTAL_UNIT`. `lease_term_years` states the master-lease term in whole years and must fall between 1 and 99 inclusive when supplied; it is required when the `kind` is `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` and must be omitted otherwise. Admission is the one `InvestorAdmin` write that does not resolve the caller's `X-User-ID` to an interest of its own — necessarily so, since the first interest of a project is admitted before any interest exists — so no `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED` can arise here, and an acting `Developer`'s ownership of the named project is the only actor check the operation applies.

Structure coherence is checked against the project's `structure_type`. A `MASTER_LEASE` project admits only `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` interests — exactly one of them — and requires `lease_term_years` on it; a `SINGLE_TIER` project admits only `DIRECT_PARTNER` interests and rejects `lease_term_years` outright. A mismatched `kind` — a `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` offered to a `SINGLE_TIER` project, or a `DIRECT_PARTNER` offered to a `MASTER_LEASE` project — a missing or forbidden lease term, or a second master-lease lessee is refused with `ERR_STRUCTURE_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The lessee slot is occupied by `kind` alone, whatever the sitting lessee's `status`: a lessee that has since been driven to `DISQUALIFIED` does not free it, and a replacement lessee is refused with that same error. A master-lease lessee whose `party_tax_status` is `TAX_EXEMPT_ORG` or `GOVERNMENTAL_UNIT` disqualifies the credit outright and is refused with `ERR_DISQUALIFIED_PARTY`. Those two checks and the risk check below are evaluated in the order just given — structure coherence, then the party's tax status, then the score — so a tax-exempt master-lease lessee offered to a `SINGLE_TIER` project reports `ERR_STRUCTURE_TYPE_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_DISQUALIFIED_PARTY`. Allocation is also policed in aggregate, because each interest can be individually valid while the portfolio is not: if admitting the interest would push the sum of `credit_allocation_pct` across the project's `ADMITTED` interests above one hundred percent, the admission is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`.

A successfully admitted interest has `status` of `ADMITTED`, `election_at` null, and `pass_through_election` of `PENDING` when its `kind` is `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` when it is `DIRECT_PARTNER`; a `DIRECT_PARTNER`'s representation still carries `lease_term_years`, holding null. Admission also refreshes the project's `max_investor_risk_score` and increments the project's `version`.

Every interest carries a `disqualification_risk_score`, an integer from 0 to 100, together with `risk_variant_applied` naming which of the two scoring variants produced it. The score is assembled from four components. Every interest starts from a base risk of 10 points. Its `party_tax_status` adds points on a fixed scale: nothing for `TAXABLE_C_CORP`, 10 points for `PARTNERSHIP`, 35 points for `GOVERNMENTAL_UNIT`, and 45 points for `TAX_EXEMPT_ORG`. The lease-term component applies only to a `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` that has a `lease_term_years` value, and is otherwise zero: a safe-harbour term is first computed as eighty percent of the building's thirty-nine-year useful life, truncated downward to a whole number of years; a lease term at or beyond that safe harbour scores the full lease-term ceiling of 30 points, while a shorter term scores 30 points scaled by the ratio of `lease_term_years` to the safe-harbour term, rounded half up to a whole point. Finally, an interest whose `pass_through_election` is still `PENDING` adds 12 points, and adds nothing once it is `ELECTED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. The sum of these four components is the base score.

The two variants diverge from there. The `ADMISSION` variant, applied when the interest is created and again when the pass-through election is made, is the base score capped at 100 and nothing more — covenant and finding history is ignored because none can exist before the building is in service. The `IN_SERVICE` variant, applied to every `ADMITTED` interest of a project whose compliance dimension has left `NOT_MONITORED`, adds a project-level adder on top of the base score: 20 points for each `BREACHED` covenant of the project, plus 10 points for each unresolved preservation violation, counting the total of `unresolved_violation_count` over the project's applications whose status is not `SUPERSEDED`. That adder is capped at 60 points **before** it is added to the base score, and the sum is then capped at 100. The same party attributes can therefore score higher after the building is in service than they did at admission. An interest admitted onto a project whose monitoring has already begun is nonetheless scored under the `ADMISSION` variant, and keeps that `risk_variant_applied` until some later cascade re-scores it, so a project's stored scores may mix the two variants. The project's `max_investor_risk_score` is the maximum `disqualification_risk_score` across its `ADMITTED` interests, whichever variant each of them was last scored under, and zero when the project has no `ADMITTED` interest at all.

A score of 70 or more is disqualifying. An admission whose `ADMISSION`-variant score reaches 70 is refused with `ERR_DISQUALIFICATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, and the same error blocks the filing of a credit claim when the project's standing `max_investor_risk_score` has reached 70 by the time of filing.

The pass-through election is what lets a master tenant actually take the credit, and only an `InvestorAdmin` may make it. Election moves `pass_through_election` from `PENDING` to `ELECTED`, stamps `election_at` from the logical clock, re-scores the interest under the `ADMISSION` variant — which drops the 12-point pending-election penalty and can therefore only lower the score — refreshes the project's `max_investor_risk_score`, and increments both the interest's and the project's `version`. An election on an interest whose `pass_through_election` is already `ELECTED`, or on an interest of a `SINGLE_TIER` project where the field reads `NOT_APPLICABLE`, is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An election attempted by an `InvestorAdmin` whose own interest is `DISQUALIFIED` or belongs to a different project is refused with `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED`, and so is one whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no investor interest at all.

An interest can also lose its standing. When a recapture event of `LEASE_TO_DISQUALIFIED` or `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` type is reported against the project, every `ADMITTED` interest whose `kind` is `MASTER_LEASE_LESSEE` moves `ADMITTED → DISQUALIFIED`, its `version` increments, and the project's `max_investor_risk_score` is refreshed over the interests that remain `ADMITTED`. A `DISQUALIFIED` interest can no longer open or file credit claims, which surfaces as `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED`.

### 3.8. Placed in Service, Covenant Monitoring and Recapture

Declaring the building placed in service is the pivot of the whole timeline: it starts the five-year monitoring clock and opens the claiming window. The owning `Developer` declares it by supplying `placed_in_service_date` — any well-formed calendar date is accepted, whether it precedes or follows the logical clock date and whatever its relation to the measuring period — and the declaration is legal only once the project's certification dimension has reached `PART2_APPROVED` or `PART3_CERTIFIED` — a building cannot be placed in service before its rehabilitation plan has cleared Part 2 review — otherwise it is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. It may be declared exactly once: a second declaration, or a declaration on a project that is `PART3_CERTIFIED` or `DECERTIFIED` and therefore no longer editable, is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`. On success the compliance dimension moves `NOT_MONITORED → IN_SERVICE_MONITORING`, every `ADMITTED` investor interest is re-scored under the `IN_SERVICE` variant, `max_investor_risk_score` is refreshed from those scores, and the project's `version` increments. The certification dimension is not touched.

Covenants are the instruments through which the monitoring period is actually policed. Either a `ComplianceMonitor` or a `Developer` creates one on a project that already has a `placed_in_service_date` — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE` — naming the `project_id`, the `covenant_type` (`USE_RESTRICTION`, `OWNERSHIP_HOLD`, `MAINTENANCE_STANDARD`, `INSURANCE_COVERAGE`, or `REPORTING`), the `monitoring_frequency` of either `ANNUAL` or `SEMIANNUAL`, and the inclusive `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` of the monitoring window, where the end must fall strictly after the start. A new covenant sits in `PENDING_ATTESTATION` with `attestation_count` and `exception_count` at zero and `last_attested_period_end` and `cure_deadline_date` null. Creating one mutates no other row: the project's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were.

Attestations drive the covenant cycle. The attesting party — the owning `Developer` or a `ComplianceMonitor` — supplies the `period_end_date` being attested to and an `outcome` of either `SATISFIED` or `EXCEPTION`. Every accepted attestation raises `attestation_count` by one and writes `period_end_date` into `last_attested_period_end`; the `period_end_date` must be on or before the current logical clock date, and, when a previous attestation exists, strictly after `last_attested_period_end`, with an incoherent period rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The covenant's `version` increments on every accepted attestation, and an attestation not declared from the covenant's current status is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`.

A `SATISFIED` outcome on a covenant in `PENDING_ATTESTATION` or `CURED` moves it to `COMPLIANT`. That origin set is exhaustive: a `SATISFIED` attestation declared on a covenant already reading `COMPLIANT` is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`, even though an `EXCEPTION` from that same status is accepted. When that attestation's `period_end_date` reaches or passes the project's `placed_in_service_date` advanced by five years of 365 days each, and every covenant of the project is then `COMPLIANT`, and `recapture_balance_cents` is zero, the attestation also closes the monitoring period: the compliance dimension moves `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING → COMPLIANT_CLOSED`, which is absorbing. A `SATISFIED` outcome on a covenant in `CURED` for a later period than the one already attested closes the covenant cycle `BREACHED → CURED → COMPLIANT`.

An `EXCEPTION` outcome breaches the covenant. From `PENDING_ATTESTATION`, `COMPLIANT`, or `CURED`, the covenant moves to `BREACHED`; `exception_count` rises by one alongside `attestation_count`, and `cure_deadline_date` is set to `window_end_date` advanced by ninety days. The breach then cascades in three hops: the project's compliance dimension moves `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING → CURE_PENDING`; every `ADMITTED` investor interest of the project has its `disqualification_risk_score` recomputed under the `IN_SERVICE` variant, the `version` of each interest whose score or `risk_variant_applied` actually changes incrementing while an interest that already carries that score under that variant is not mutated at all; and the project's `max_investor_risk_score` is refreshed from those scores. The posture hop fires only from `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING`: a breach recorded on a project already sitting in `CURE_PENDING`, or in one of the two absorbing postures, leaves the dimension exactly where it is and still runs the re-scoring hops. The project's `version` increments when that cascade moved the posture, some interest's score or the maximum, and is left alone when it moved none of them.

Curing is time-boxed. A `SATISFIED` attestation on a `BREACHED` covenant moves it `BREACHED → CURED`, but only when the attested `period_end_date` falls on or before `cure_deadline_date`; a cure attestation arriving after that deadline is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_INVALID_STATE`. A successful cure re-scores every `ADMITTED` interest under the `IN_SERVICE` variant first, on the same terms as the breach cascade, and then, when no other covenant of the project is `BREACHED` and `recapture_balance_cents` is zero, it returns the project's compliance dimension `CURE_PENDING → IN_SERVICE_MONITORING` and settles the outstanding recapture events. The project's `version` increments when either of those two steps changed something — a score, the maximum, or the posture — and is left alone when neither did.

Recapture events are reported by a `ComplianceMonitor` against a project that has a `placed_in_service_date`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`. The report names the `project_id`, the `event_type`, and the `event_date` on which the trigger occurred. Client-reportable types are `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER`, `CESSATION_OF_USE`, `LEASE_TO_DISQUALIFIED`, and `COVENANT_BREACH`; the fifth type, `SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_FAILURE`, is raised only by the system, and a client that supplies it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The event must fall inside the compliance period: its `event_date` must be on or after the project's `placed_in_service_date`, and its `full_years_elapsed` must be strictly less than five, else `ERR_RECAPTURE_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`. Those two are the whole of the calendar test — the `event_date` is never compared to the logical clock, so an event dated ahead of the clock is accepted as readily as one behind it, and the earlier-than-placed-in-service case is refused before any assessment arithmetic is attempted. Because `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` means the five-year period has run its course, a project in that state accepts no further recapture event and reports the same error. The other absorbing posture is no such bar: an event reported against a project already `RECAPTURED` is assessed normally and still raises `recapture_balance_cents`, while the dimension itself stays put.

The assessment itself runs before anything is persisted. `full_years_elapsed` is the number of days from the project's `placed_in_service_date` to the event's `event_date`, divided by 365 and truncated downward to a whole number. For an event of type `SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_FAILURE`, `recapture_pct` is exactly 1.00 — a failed substantial rehabilitation test claws back the entire credit claimed to date however long the building has been in service. For every other type, `recapture_pct` steps down with time: it is the number of compliance years remaining, namely five less `full_years_elapsed`, multiplied by twenty percent. `recapture_amount_cents` is then the project's `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` multiplied by `recapture_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. The event is persisted with `status` of `ASSESSED` and `source` of `REPORTED` for a monitor's report, or `SYSTEM_DERIVED` for one the system raises itself, and the amount is added to the project's `recapture_balance_cents`.

What the assessment does to the project depends on the type. An `OWNERSHIP_TRANSFER` event drives the compliance dimension to the absorbing `RECAPTURED` when that dimension currently reads `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING` or `CURE_PENDING` — those are the only two origins the hop fires from — and disqualifies every `ADMITTED` master-lease lessee; no credit claim may afterwards be created or filed on that project, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`. Any other type, assessed with a positive `recapture_amount_cents`, moves the dimension `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING → CURE_PENDING` and re-scores every `ADMITTED` interest under the `IN_SERVICE` variant, refreshing `max_investor_risk_score`; when the dimension already reads `CURE_PENDING` the move is a no-op but the re-scoring still runs, and when the assessed amount comes to zero — the project having claimed nothing yet — neither the move nor the re-scoring happens and only `max_investor_risk_score` is refreshed. A `LEASE_TO_DISQUALIFIED` event additionally disqualifies the master-lease lessee as described in §3.7, and does so *before* that re-scoring, so the interest it has just removed from `ADMITTED` is not itself re-scored. In every case the project's `version` increments.

The system raises its own recapture event in exactly one situation: the recording or classification of a cost line item whose recomputation flips the project's stored `substantial_test_status` from `MET` to `NOT_MET` while its certification dimension reads `PART3_CERTIFIED`. An auditor's disallowance of a locked cost is the ordinary case, but as §3.6 records the trigger belongs to the recomputation rather than to the actor. That event carries `event_type` of `SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_FAILURE` and `source` of `SYSTEM_DERIVED` and takes the current logical clock date as its `event_date`, without the compliance-period bounds check a reported event must pass: its `full_years_elapsed` is simply the floored 365-day count from `placed_in_service_date` to that date, which reads negative when the building was declared placed in service on a date the clock has not yet reached. It assesses the whole of `credit_claimed_to_date_cents`, raises `recapture_balance_cents` by that amount, moves the compliance dimension `IN_SERVICE_MONITORING → CURE_PENDING` whatever the assessed amount comes to and re-scores every `ADMITTED` interest under the `IN_SERVICE` variant when it does — any other posture, an absorbing one or a `CURE_PENDING` already in force, being left exactly where it is — moves every `FILED` claim of the project to `ADJUSTED`, and re-derives `credit_unclaimed_cents`. An assessed event leaves `ASSESSED` only one way: it moves `ASSESSED → SETTLED`, incrementing its `version`, once the project's `recapture_balance_cents` has been driven back to zero by claim offsets.

### 3.9. Claiming the Credit and Settling Recapture

The credit is claimed one tax year at a time, and only an `InvestorAdmin` may claim it. A claim is opened against a `project_id` for a given `tax_year`, an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive. Opening a claim requires the project's certification dimension to read `PART3_CERTIFIED` — credits cannot be claimed before the completed work is certified, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and requires the project to have a `placed_in_service_date`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_PLACED_IN_SERVICE`. The acting interest must belong to the same project and still be `ADMITTED`, else `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED` — an actor-identity check, so per §2.9 it is applied before either of the two state checks just named, and an unadmitted caller opening a claim on an uncertified project is told `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED`. The `tax_year` must fall inside the ratable claiming window, which runs from the calendar year of `placed_in_service_date` through that year plus four, inclusive — five years in all; a year outside it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. Only one claim may exist per project per tax year regardless of that claim's status, so a duplicate is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_DUPLICATE`.

A new claim snapshots the project's per-year credit: `gross_credit_cents` is set to the project's `annual_credit_cents` as it stands at claim creation, and it is deliberately **not** re-read at filing time. `claim_sequence` is the claim's one-based position among the project's claims ordered by `tax_year` ascending. It is computed once, from the claims that already exist at that moment, and no claim is ever renumbered afterwards: a claim opened for an earlier `tax_year` than one already on the project counts only the years below its own, so two claims of a project can end up carrying the same `claim_sequence`. The claim opens with `status` of `DRAFT`, `recapture_offset_cents` and `net_claim_cents` at zero, and `filed_at` and `filed_by_user_id` null, and opening it mutates no other row — the project's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were.

Filing is gated on five things beyond the claim's own state. The claim must still be `DRAFT`: a `FILED` or `ADJUSTED` claim cannot be filed again and is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_CLAIM_ALREADY_FILED`. The project's certification dimension must read `PART3_CERTIFIED` (`ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CERTIFIED`), and its compliance dimension must not read `RECAPTURED` (`ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`). The acting interest must belong to the project and be `ADMITTED` (`ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ADMITTED`) — again an actor-identity check, and so applied before every state check listed above it here. The project's `max_investor_risk_score` must be below 70 (`ERR_DISQUALIFICATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`). Only then, on a project whose `structure_type` is `MASTER_LEASE`, the master-lease lessee's `pass_through_election` must read `ELECTED`, because without the election the credit stays with the lessor and the lessee has nothing to claim; the shortfall is refused with `ERR_PASS_THROUGH_ELECTION_REQUIRED` — and because the standing-risk check is evaluated first, a filing that fails both reports `ERR_DISQUALIFICATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. And last of all, the fiscal check: the project's `substantial_test_status` must still read `MET`, else `ERR_SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_NOT_MET`.

Settlement then proceeds in a strictly pinned order, and the order is the specification. **First**, the gross figure is taken from the claim's stored `gross_credit_cents`. **Second**, `recapture_offset_cents` is set to the lesser of that gross figure and the project's currently outstanding `recapture_balance_cents` — the outstanding recapture obligation is satisfied out of this claim first, capped by what the claim is worth. **Third**, `net_claim_cents` is the gross figure less that offset: it is what survives the offset. **Fourth**, and only now, the project ledger is mutated: `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` increases by `net_claim_cents`, `credit_recaptured_cents` increases by `recapture_offset_cents`, `recapture_balance_cents` decreases by `recapture_offset_cents`, and `credit_unclaimed_cents` is recomputed as `total_claimable_credit_cents` less `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` less `credit_recaptured_cents`. **Fifth**, only after the balance has been reduced, every recapture event of the project whose `status` is `ASSESSED` moves to `SETTLED` if `recapture_balance_cents` has reached zero. **Sixth**, only after those two steps, the compliance posture is re-derived: when `recapture_balance_cents` is zero, no covenant of the project is `BREACHED`, and the dimension currently reads `CURE_PENDING`, it returns `CURE_PENDING → IN_SERVICE_MONITORING`. Computing the offset after mutating the ledger, rather than before, double-counts the offset and breaks the accounting.

Filing also stamps the audit trail: `status` moves `DRAFT → FILED`, `filed_at` is taken from the logical clock, and `filed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. This last field is an audit record only and is not treated as a foreign key. Both the claim's and the project's `version` increment. Across the whole ledger, one invariant must hold after every filing: `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` plus `credit_recaptured_cents` plus `credit_unclaimed_cents` equals `total_claimable_credit_cents`. Consistently with that, `credit_claimed_to_date_cents` is always the sum of `net_claim_cents` over the project's claims whose `status` is `FILED` or `ADJUSTED`, and `credit_recaptured_cents` is always the cumulative recapture actually applied against filed claims.

A filed claim can still be disturbed by a later failure. When a `SUBSTANTIAL_TEST_FAILURE` event is assessed against the project, every `FILED` claim moves `FILED → ADJUSTED` and its `version` increments, but its `net_claim_cents` is deliberately left unchanged — the obligation is carried on the project's `recapture_balance_cents` and worked off against future claims rather than rewritten into the history of past ones. `ADJUSTED` is terminal: such a claim can never be filed again.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that mint a new resource return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource — including state transitions, classifications, attestations, and the logical-clock control — return `200 OK`. Read operations return `200 OK`.
